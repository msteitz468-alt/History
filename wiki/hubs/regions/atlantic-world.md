> The ocean system created after 1492 — binding Europe, Africa, and the Americas through trade,
> conquest, slavery, and revolution into a single, violently integrated world. The defining

## Debates (→ [[hubs/historiography-hub|Historiography Hub]])
- The [[williams-thesis-abolition-debate|Williams thesis]] (slavery & capitalism) ·
  [[impact-of-atlantic-slave-trade-on-africa|the slave trade's impact on Africa]] ·
  [[causes-of-latin-american-underdevelopment|Latin American underdevelopment]] · [[black-legend|the Black Legend]].
- The utility and limits of **[[concepts/atlantic-history|Atlantic history]]** as an analytic construct (Greene & Morgan, 2009): coherence vs. multiplicity of Atlantics; boundedness vs. global links; neo-imperial charge; interiors/continental perspectives; connections vs. internal histories & comparison. Part III of the volume develops competing/complementary frames in detail: continental approach (Wood; "facing east," 281–83; Turner/Richter; prenational U.S. history, 300–301; interiors and Native autonomy); hemispheric perspective (Greene; 307–12; approaches 308–10; Latin America contrasts 302–304; inclusionary/exclusionary colonies 304–305; New Philology/Lockhart/Schwartz); global/beyond Atlantic (Canny, Coclanis; 321; California School 345–47; world systems/Wallerstein 346–48; integration vs. endogenous growth 350–51; rice/silver/Pacific/Indian Ocean conjunctures; Atlantic as subsection of world history). Propositions for best practice (balance contrasts, focus leading sectors, ideas/religion, chronology). National Atlantics (Spanish closed bureaucratic silver system; Portuguese archipelago/South Atlantic symbiosis with creole institutions; British rapid integration + West Indies centrality + African majority migrants; French Caribbean economic core + Haitian Revolution as multidirectional event; Dutch brief imperial moment shifting to transit trade + universalist geography) demonstrate contemporaries operated in competitive national spheres while the frame enables comparison.
- Part II Old Worlds evidence (Morgan on Africa; Phillips on Europe; Indigenous synthesis): African agency high (sellers' market, improved terms of trade, control of access); integration limited/uneven (coastal ports vs. interiors; regional extremes Upper Guinea vs. West-Central); economic effects modest on Africa with major social/ internal slavery changes; European politics and 16th/18th-c. booms tied to silver, commodities, rivalries, migration; Indigenous coastal crises vs. interior resilience. Reinforces need for continental/hemispheric complements and bounded early-modern framing.

## Leading sectors and webs (Greene & Morgan)
- Maritime world, island hubs (Azores, Madeira, Canaries, Cape Verdes, Caribbean, Bermuda), borderlands/middle grounds as vectors.
- Pan-Atlantic commodity chains, religious/scientific networks, diasporas (e.g., "Portuguese Nation"), and "webs of association" linking people, objects, beliefs.
- Common colonial project of expropriation with variations by indigenous capacity for resistance, endowments, and colonizer resources.
