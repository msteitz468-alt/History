## The arc

### Revelation & the early caliphates (610–750)
[[muhammad-and-the-rise-of-islam|Muhammad and the rise of Islam]] → the [[rashidun-caliphate|Rashidun]]
conquests → the [[umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad Caliphate]] and its westward thrust
([[umayyad-conquest-of-hispania-711|conquest of Hispania, 711]]).

### The classical age (750–1258)
The Abbasid era centred on [[baghdad|Baghdad]] — the translation movement and the height of Islamic
science. In the west, [[al-andalus|al-Andalus]] and the [[caliphate-of-cordoba|Caliphate of Córdoba]];
in Egypt and North Africa, the Shia [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimids]].

### Turks, Crusaders, and Mongols (1037–1300)
The [[seljuk-empire|Seljuk Empire]] reshapes the east; [[saladin|Saladin]] confronts the
[[crusades|Crusaders]]; the [[mamluk-sultanate|Mamluk Sultanate]] of Egypt halts the Mongols and
becomes the heart of the post-Abbasid Sunni world.

### Spread to new worlds
[[islamization-of-africa|Islamization of Africa]] (and the [[sokoto-caliphate|Sokoto Caliphate]],
[[west-african-jihads|West African jihads]]); [[islamization-of-southeast-asia|Islamization of Southeast Asia]].

### The gunpowder empires (1300–1800)
The [[ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]] (its [[ottoman-law-kanun|kanun]] law) and the
[[mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]] of India ([[muhammad-bin-tughluq|earlier Delhi Sultanate]] precursors) —
alongside Safavid Iran — rule most of the Islamic world at its early-modern height.

### The modern reckoning
[[muhammad-ali-egypt|Muhammad Ali's Egypt]] and reform; the [[fall-of-the-ottoman-empire-1922|fall of the Ottoman Empire]];
[[muhammad-ali-jinnah|Jinnah]] and the partition of India; the rise of [[political-islam|political Islam]].
