## 1. Formation and Early Influences

**R01-CorsicanIdentityFamily**  
Napoleone di Buonaparte (signed until manhood) born Ajaccio 15 Aug 1769 on tapestry after Letizia felt labour pains returning from church. Family minor Corsican gentry of Florentine origin (land, mill, sheep, vineyard, servants); social penumbra between bourgeoisie and minor nobility. Carlo (tall, handsome, Enlightenment-influenced) and Letizia (strong-willed, uneducated, “head of a man on the body of a woman”) had eight surviving children. After Ponte Nuovo (May 1769), Carlo refused exile with Paoli and took oath to Louis XV (retained assessor/forestry posts; later represented Corsican nobility in Paris). Family benefited; no evidence Napoleon despised father (named son Charles). “To my mother, I owe my fortune and all I’ve done that’s worthwhile.”  
**Grounding quote:** “‘She was on her way home from church when she felt labour pains,’ he would later say of his mother, Letizia, ‘and had only time to get into the house, when I was born, not on a bed, but on a heap of tapestry.’”  
**Locator:** Corsica chapter (R01).

**R01-EducationBrienneEcole**  
Sent to France age 9 (Autun 1779 for French; Brienne Royal Military School ~1779–84; École Royale Militaire Paris 1784–85). Excelled in mathematics (key for artillery); voracious reader (Plutarch, Caesar, Arrian on Alexander, Polybius, Voltaire, Rousseau, Ossian, Corneille, Racine). Heroes: Paoli (living), Alexander, Caesar, Charles XII. Thick Corsican accent + poverty + pride in homeland drew teasing → reinforced nationalism. Father died 1785 (stomach issues); Napoleon assumed family headship. “History could become for a young man the school of morality and virtue...”  
**Grounding quote:** “I was the poorest of my classmates... I was proud, I was careful not to show it... I didn’t know how to smile or play like the others.”  
**Locator:** Corsica chapter (R01).

**R01-EarlyWritingsNationalism**  
~60 pre-26 writings (essays, novellas, pamphlets, history of Corsica). Fierce early nationalism: “The Corsicans, following all the laws of justice, have been able to shake off the yoke of the Genoese, and may do the same with that of the French. Amen.” (1786 resistance essay). “On Suicide” (1786): romantic melancholy + “My fellow-countrymen are weighed down with chains... A good patriot ought to die when his fatherland has ceased to exist.” Shifted post-Revolution to French/Jacobin alignment after Paoli rejected Bonapartes. Clisson et Eugénie (1795 novella, heavily autobiographical): foreshadowed Josephine drama.  
**Grounding quote:** “I was born when the country was perishing. Thirty thousand Frenchmen vomited onto our coasts, drowning the thrones of liberty in seas of blood...”  
**Locator:** Revolution/Corsica chapters (R01).

**R01-CompartmentalizationHumor** (foreshadowed in formation)  
Napoleon likened mind to “drawers in a cupboard.” On eve of battle could dictate girls’ school rules or Comédie-Française regulations. “A more unexpected aspect... was his fine sense of humour. All too often historians have taken seriously remarks that were clearly intended as humorous. Napoleon was constantly joking to his family and entourage, even in the most dire situations.”  
**Grounding quote:** (compartmentalization) “Napoleon was able to compartmentalize his life to quite a remarkable degree... On the eve of battle... he could dictate his thoughts on the establishment of a girls’ school... and shortly after having captured Moscow he set down the regulations governing the Comédie-Française.”  
**Locator:** Introduction (R01).

Josephine relationship began via Directory circles; overwhelming crush on his side, her early infidelity (Hippolyte Charles) revealed in Egypt devastated him (“I have a great, great deal of domestic sorrow”). Contingent (Corsican outsider + artillery + Revolutionary merit openings) met structural (post-1789 openings, nation-in-arms). Formed the soldier-statesman who would identify himself with the Revolution’s best elements. 13 Vendémiaire (1795) “whiff of grapeshot” ended mob power and launched high command. (R01 synthesis.)

## 2. Rise / Path to Power

**R01-RevolutionToulonParis + Vendemiaire**  
Supported early Revolution (Friends of the Constitution; anti-clerical; Corsican National Guard lt-col 1792). Key: Toulon (artillery role; promoted brig-gen age 24). Paris 1792 witnessed Tuileries/Sept Massacres. 13 Vendémiaire Year IV (5 Oct 1795): Barras appointed second-in-command; seized Sablons cannon via Murat; deployed grapeshot vs. Sections (~300 rebels killed vs. ~6 government). Promoted gén. de division, then Army of Interior commander. Met Josephine (Vicomtesse de Beauharnais; affair with Barras/Hoche/Charles); civil marriage 9 Mar 1796.  
**Grounding quote:** “The ‘whiff of grapeshot’... meant that the Paris mob played no further part in French politics for the next three decades.”  
**Locator:** Desire chapter (R01).

**R01-ItalyRiseLodiMilan**  
Command Army of Italy (Mar 1796). Rapid victories (Montenotte etc.); Lodi bridge (10 May): personal leadership, “darling child of victory.” Entered Milan 15 May triumphantly; created Lombard Republic, “contributions,” reforms (tariffs, ghettos, feudalism), propaganda.  
**Grounding quote:** “Soldiers, you have rushed like a torrent from the top of the Apennines... You will then return to your homes... ‘He belonged to the Army of Italy.’”  
**Locator:** Italy chapter (R01).

**R01-EgyptSyriaInfidelity + Brumaire**  
Egypt 1798 (strike Britain/India; savants component). Pyramids (21 Jul): “From the top of those pyramids, forty centuries are contemplating you.” Junot revealed Josephine/Hippolyte Charles affair at Wardan ~19 Jul 1798 → devastated; no letters to her ~2 years; mistress in Cairo. Syria/Acre. Returned 1799. 18–19 Brumaire (9–10 Nov): allied with Sieyès/Talleyrand/Fouché/Lucien. Elders moved legislature to St-Cloud; Napoleon appointed district commander. Day 2: Elders speech; Five Hundred hostile (“Cromwell!”, “Tyrant!”, “Hors la loi!”); Lucien’s theatrical sword-on-Napoleon oath swayed guards; Murat/grenadiers cleared Orangery. Provisional Consulate (Sieyès, Ducos, Napoleon); no popular resistance.  
**Grounding quote:** (Five Hundred) “‘Down with the tyrant!’... ‘Cromwell!’, ‘Tyrant!’, ‘Down with the dictator!’, ‘Hors la loi!’”  
**Locator:** Brumaire chapter (R01).

**R02-ConsulateInstitutions + Plebiscite + Marengo**  
Year VIII Constitution (25 Dec 1799): real power to First Consul (10 years; broad appointment/treaty powers; live at Tuileries). Senate amendable via sénatus-consultes; Tribunate/ Legislative Body discussion/vote only. Lucien falsified plebiscite (~900k phantom votes added; true ~1.55M yes). Conseil d’État (technocrats) core deliberative body. Brigandage smashed (gendarmerie + special tribunals). “Between 17 and 18 Brumaire I have erected a wall of brass...”  
**Grounding quote:** “I will resign... War, like government, is a matter of tact.” (Italy command split threat, post-Lodi).  
**Locator:** Constitution/Brumaire (R02).

Marengo (14 Jun 1800): Army of Reserve crossed Great St Bernard; entered Milan 2 Jun. Near-disaster (scattered forces); Desaix + Kellermann turned it ~5pm. “We have gone back far enough today... remember it is my custom to bivouac on the field of battle!” Propaganda: invented Desaix last words + trap narrative. “You see, there were two battles... I lost the first; I gained the second.”  
**Grounding quote:** (bulletin/propaganda) “I have made 60,000 prisoners...” (exaggerated).  
**Locator:** Marengo chapter (R02).

**R02-Concordat + CivilCode + Amiens**  
Concordat (signed Jul 1801, published Apr 1802): state recognition of Catholicism + state control (joint bishop nominations, report subversives, prayers for Republic; irrevocable land sales). Organic Articles (unilateral) protected Protestants/Jews. Popular rurally.  
**Grounding quote:** “The clergy is a power that is never quiet... you must be its master.” “In religion, I do not see the mystery of the Incarnation, but the mystery of the social order...”  
**Locator:** Lawgiver chapter (R02).

Civil Code (launched 1801; law 1804): 2,281 articles after 107 sessions (Napoleon chaired 55). Equality before law + patriarchal bias (Art. 213: “A husband owes protection... a wife obedience”; divorce restricted; women barred from contracts etc.). “Women should not be looked upon as equals of men. They are, in fact, only machines for making babies.” Extended widely.  
**Locator:** Lawgiver chapter (R02).

Amiens (25 Mar 1802): Britain restored most conquests (retained Trinidad/Ceylon; Malta to Knights). Celebrations. Early breakdown: British prevarication on Malta; French Piedmont/Swiss annexations + Sébastiani report (Egypt retakable). “We do not seem to be at peace, but only in truce...” (R02).

**R02-SaintDomingue + EarlyStyle**  
Leclerc expedition (1801–03): restore order/slavery. Brutal (gas chambers, massacres); yellow fever devastated French. Toussaint surrendered then kidnapped; died Fort de Joux. “The Saint-Domingue business was a great piece of folly... I ought to have treated with the black leaders...” Ended western empire dreams (Louisiana sale).  
**Locator:** Saint-Domingue section (R02).

Early micromanagement/humor: relentless detail (Conseil 8–10hr sessions; 55 Code plenaries). “Napoleon was able to compartmentalize... drawers in a cupboard.” Humor: “I’m sorry you are angry with me” (to Decrès); wounded-horse quip at Marengo. Josephine letters: affectionate post-Marengo (“I hope to be in the arms...”). (R02).

**R01/R02 Synthesis:** Sequence of opportunistic alignments (Paoli → Jacobin/Convention → Barras/Directory → Sieyès) via artillery skill, Vendémiaire decisiveness, Italian command → Egypt prestige → Brumaire seizure → Consulate centralization. Early Italy/Egypt show proto-modernizing + personal vulnerability. Brumaire enabled stability after Directory chaos. (R01/R02 synthesis.)

## 3. Major Phases of Career

**Consulate (1799–1804) — R02:** Legal/administrative revolution (Code, Concordat, prefects, Banque de France, lycées, Conseil d’État as core technocrat body); Marengo recovery + propaganda (Desaix/Kellermann turned near-disaster; invented trap narrative + Desaix last words); Amiens peace (25 Mar 1802; Britain restored most conquests) and early breakdown (Malta prevarication, Piedmont/Swiss annexations, Sébastiani report); Saint-Domingue catastrophe (Leclerc; brutal suppression + yellow fever; 30k+ French + up to 350k islanders dead; “The Saint-Domingue business was a great piece of folly... I ought to have treated with the black leaders”; ended western empire dreams/Louisiana sale); plebiscite manipulation (Lucien added ~900k phantom votes). “The Revolution is established on the principles which began it. It is finished.” “Between 17 and 18 Brumaire I have erected a wall of brass...” (R02).

**Empire to Zenith (1804–1809) — R03:** Austerlitz (Dec 1805; Pratzen heights luring + center smash + “sun of Austerlitz”; Davout held with 10k vs. 36k; French ~8k casualties vs. Allied 16k+ killed/wounded + 20k captured + 186 guns; “I have made 60,000 prisoners...” exaggerated); Jena/Auerstädt (Oct 1806; corps pivot; Davout independent double envelopment 30k vs. 52k; Bernadotte absent); Eylau (Feb 1807; pyrrhic “What a massacre! And without any result!”; Augereau’s division destroyed in blizzard; Murat’s great cavalry charge; ~21–23k French vs. Russian ~18–20k + Prussians); Friedland (14 Jun 1807; Lannes fixes + Ney surge + Sénarmont artillery charge; “the Russian left was utterly destroyed”); Tilsit (June–July 1807; raft summit with Alexander; harsh on Prussia, lenient on Russia; “I’m very well satisfied with him...”). Continental System (Berlin Decrees 21 Nov 1806): “The British Isles are in a state of blockade... I will conquer the sea through the power of the land.” Widespread smuggling + European resentment; “the only means of striking a blow to England...” but “dangerous downturn” for Empire. Family thrones (Joseph Naples, Louis Holland, Jérôme Westphalia; “My brothers have done me a great deal of harm”; “installed largely inadequate brothers”). Wagram (July 1809; Lobau crossing mastery; Macdonald’s square; Grand Battery 112 guns; ~30k French casualties; “I’ve crushed them”). Coronation (2 Dec 1804; self-crowning with Charlemagne replica + laurel; Josephine crowned; eagle/bee symbols). Merit nobility (Légion d’Honneur “baubles” + new counts/barons from service). “I am the Revolution.” (R03).

**Overreach and Collapse (1810–1815) — R04/R05:** Iberian “ulcer” (Bayonne trap; Bailén; “Spanish ulcer”; Saragossa “Guerra al cuchillo”; “I committed a great mistake in putting that fool of a Joseph on the Spanish throne”). Russia 1812 (R04): typhus epidemic (6,000 ill/day first week, >80k dead/sick by late July with ≥50k from typhus; 1/5 of central army lost pre-battle); Vitebsk “Here I stop!” then press to Smolensk (“Why stop here for eight months when twenty days might suffice...”); Borodino (Guard refusal: “I do not wish to see it destroyed... The success of the day was assured”); Moscow entry/fire (“the beautiful and superb city of Moscow no longer exists; Rostopchin had it burnt”; letter to Alexander: “The fate of all civilized nations is concerned...”); Maloyaroslavets council/route choice (Davout southern vs. majority northern; Napoleon “I will decide”; later: “No; I was the master, and mine was the fault”); Berezina “miraculous crossing” at −33°C (Éblé’s trestles; ~50k crossed but 15k+ stragglers abandoned; “the men went into the water up to their shoulders... Some dropped dead”; “Food, food, food...”). Leipzig (Oct 1813): “Battle of the Nations”; Saxon/Württemberger desertions; premature Elster bridge blow; ~47k French casualties + 38k captured + 325 guns; retreat to Rhine with ~80k effectives from >200k. 1814 campaign: brilliant interior lines (Champaubert, Montmirail, Château-Thierry, Vauchamps) but Paris surrender + marshals’ pressure forced abdication (11 Apr 1814). Elba (May 1814–Feb 1815): active micro-governance + reading + monitoring Bourbon errors. 1815 return (R05): 26 Feb departure on *L’Inconstant* (1,142 men); landed Golfe-Juan 1 Mar; Alpine route; Laffrey (“What, you old rascal, were you about to fire on your Emperor?”); Grenoble entry (“Paris or death!”); Paris 20 Mar. Waterloo (18 Jun): Ligny/Quatre Bras misdirection; Grouchy detachment (33k with imprecise “follow up those Prussians... by your left”); mud delay (Drouot advised wait); Ney cavalry charges + late/partial Guard assault (“La Garde recule!”); “There is but a step from the sublime to the ridiculous.” Second abdication (22 Jun); Rochefort; surrendered to Maitland on *Bellerophon* (“I come, like Themistocles, to seat myself at the hearth of the British people”). (R04/R05).

**Captivity and legend-making (1815–1821) — R05/R06:** St Helena (Oct 1815); Hudson Lowe restrictions; dictated *Mémorial* (12 hrs/day, “Arcole, Rivoli, the Pyramids, Marengo, Austerlitz, Jena, Friedland – these are granite”); “crown of thorns” greater than imperial one. Health decline (swellings, vomiting, depression); autopsy 6 May 1821: stomach “ulcerated all over like a honeycomb” at pylorus. “How Napoleon could have existed for any time with such an organ was wonderful.” Companions’ dispersal (Bertrand, Montholon, Las Cases, Marchand, Antommarchi); death mask dispute (properly Burton’s). Remains returned to Les Invalides 1840. Myth outlasted empire; institutions prevented full Ancien Régime restoration. (R05/R06).

## 5. Intellectual / Policy / Military Style and Methods

**R01/R03 Compartmentalization + Micromanagement + Humor**  
Napoleon likened mind to “drawers in a cupboard.” On eve of battle could dictate girls’ school rules or Comédie-Française regulations while aides arrived with orders. “Napoleon was able to compartmentalize his life to quite a remarkable degree... On the eve of battle... he could dictate his thoughts on the establishment of a girls’ school... and shortly after having captured Moscow he set down the regulations governing the Comédie-Française.” Relentless micromanagement (22+ letters/day on minutiae: boots, bean types, bell-ropes, prostitutes in theatres, obscure priests’ sermons). “One can get a lot of work done in little time.” Naps (10 min even on campaign). Multiple simultaneous: baths with reading, carriage novels. “Work should be a way to relax.”  
**Grounding quote:** (humor) “A more unexpected aspect of Napoleon’s personality... was his fine sense of humour. All too often historians have taken seriously remarks that were clearly intended as humorous. Napoleon was constantly joking to his family and entourage, even in the most dire situations.”  
**Locator:** Introduction + R03 (R01/R03).  
**Grounding quote:** (micromanagement) “On September 20 alone he wrote thirty-six letters, his record for 1806.” “Napoleon slept in his carriage in the middle of our camp.”  
**Locator:** R03.

Corps delegation (Berthier staff work; “I have destroyed the Austrian army simply by marches”). “Trusting to his marshals’ experience and competence, Napoleon was generally content to leave logistics and battlefield tactics to them, so long as they delivered...” “I tried to converge all our forces on the point I wanted to attack. I massed them there.” Meritocracy (Légion d’Honneur “baubles” + new counts/barons from service) vs. family thrones (“My brothers have done me a great deal of harm”). “The population of Germany anxiously awaits... those who are not of noble birth but who are talented, have an equal right...” Propaganda (bulletins exaggerated; “lie like a bulletin”). “The Emperor has invented a new method of making war; he makes use only of our legs and our bayonets.” Preference for central position, fixing + envelopment (Ulm, Jena). “I will conquer the sea through the power of the land.” Capacity to separate political/military in crisis. Humor constant (even in extremis): “I’m sorry you are angry with me” (to Decrès); wounded-horse quip at Marengo (“You expect me to lend you my horses...?”); “It’s a saintly odour which is going to purify your old sins” (to Fourcroy re: Consalvi perfume). “Vive la paix!” “Pain et paix!” “Well, that suits me very well; let him be a king, if that’s all he wants!” (Württemberg). (R01/R03).

Religious utilitarianism: “In religion, I do not see the mystery of the Incarnation, but the mystery of the social order...” Flexible (Egypt: “BONAPARTE: Glory to Allah!... I am one of his friends...” fabricated). “If I ruled a people of Jews, I would rebuild the Temple.” (R02).

**R03 Delegation + Meritocracy vs. Family**  
“I tried to converge all our forces on the point I wanted to attack. I massed them there.” “Individual corps commanders... would be given a place to go to and a date... and would be expected to do the rest themselves.” Failures when not used properly (Aspern, Leipzig, Waterloo). “His corps became the standard unit adopted by every European army by 1812.” Contrast with family thrones: “installed largely inadequate brothers... stoked up problems.” “I have no shortage of thrones to give them.” “Be a constitutional king.” (R03).

**R03 Propaganda + Humor**  
“Napoleon’s bulletins were exciting to read, even as fiction.” Exaggerations routine (“I have made 60,000 prisoners...”). “Soldiers of the Grande Armée... You are the finest warriors in the world.” Order of Day: “The Russians and Austrians as mere ‘mignons’ of the British.” “The Emperor will give that battalion another standard after it has taken one from the enemy.” “Vive l’Empereur!” cheers; personal addresses (“Papa” jokes in Poland); “I am wonderfully well” post-battle. “He loved the soldiers who took the liberty of talking to him, and always laughed with them.” (R03). Humor even in crisis (R03): “Papa, Chleba? Nie ma” (reply in Polish; column laughs). Soldier: “Have you bumped your head...?” Napoleon: “Four more days of patience...” “Vive la paix!” “Pain et paix!” “Well, that suits me very well; let him be a king, if that’s all he wants!” (Württemberg). “It’s a good job you’re not any taller!” (helmet cannonball). “I am wonderfully well” after Jena/Eylau. “They will dine less comfortably than I will lunch.” “You see before you the unfortunate Mack!” (R03).

**R05 Campaign Style + Self-Appraisal**  
“After a victory there are no enemies, only men.” “There is but a step from the sublime to the ridiculous” (to Caulaincourt on return; also Pradt). “I made a great error, but I have the means to repair it.” “My health is good” repeated tic. (R05). (R01/R03/R05 synthesis.)

## 6. Character, Relationships, and Personal Life

**R01/R02 Josephine + Family + Early Temper**  
Josephine: overwhelming initial crush (her side less so); early infidelity (Hippolyte Charles) revealed in Egypt devastated him (“I have a great, great deal of domestic sorrow as now the veil has been completely lifted. Only you [Joseph] remain there for me on this earth...”); took mistress in Cairo partly for cuckoldry optics; no letters to her ~2 years. Reconciliation and a decade of contentment despite mistresses; dynastic divorce (1809) left her desolate but friendly relations continued (“She was the wife who would have gone with me to Elba”). Family: integrated Beauharnais (Eugène, Hortense); promoted siblings to thrones (mixed success). Early temper: violent youthful writings; family promoter (risk later). (R01/R02).

**R03 Marshals + Humor + Micromanagement under Pressure**  
Marshals: loyalty mixed with ambition/betrayal (Marmont “ragusard”; Ney’s 1815 defection then execution). Volcanic temper (hat thrown at Metternich; “Troops? Where would you like me to find them?” at Waterloo) but rapid recovery. Self-control cultivated (“it’s taken me years… Without all this self-control, do you think I could have done all I’ve done?”). Letters to Josephine affectionate yet detached; constant joking even in dire situations. “When I receive the monthly reports… I take greater pleasure reading them than a young lady does in reading a novel.” “My health is good” repeated to Marie Louise amid catastrophe. Malet plot shocked him because no one thought of the King of Rome. “I have more than four hundred million… and would gladly have given the whole for the ransom of my faithful companion-in-arms” (Ney). (R03).

**R03/R04 Self-Appraisal + Iron Control**  
R03: “I am wonderfully well” post-battle tic. “The Emperor is wonderfully well” in bulletins while army suffered. R04 Russia 1812 under pressure (verbatim from range_04_denouement_early.txt): “There is but a step from the sublime to the ridiculous” (to Caulaincourt on return journey; also Pradt). “I made a great error, but I have the means to repair it” (18 Dec Paris, on Moscow stay). On route: “No; I was the master, and mine was the fault.” On Moscow arsonists: “These are indeed Scythians!” “Don’t believe that the ones who burnt Moscow are people to make peace a few days later.” On wounded at Maloyaroslavets: “shaken”. On Berezina crossing (watching curses): “his outward appearance seemed indifferent and unconcerned over the wretchedness of his soldiers… he was still able to listen to them unmoved.” Letter after Berezina: “I have just traversed the Berezina… The cold is intense, and the army very tired.” Bulletin 29: blamed weather exclusively while ending “The health of His Majesty has never been better.” On Ney survival: “I have more than four hundred million in the cellars of the Tuileries, and would gladly have given the whole for the ransom of my faithful companion-in-arms.” After victory: “After a victory there are no enemies, only men.” These show candid self-appraisal + iron self-control amid catastrophe. Health tic: “My health is good.” (R03/R04).

**R05 1815 + Final Self-Image**  
1815 return: “On my march… I was an adventurer; in Grenoble I once again became a sovereign.” Waterloo: “La Garde recule!” + “There is but a step from the sublime to the ridiculous.” Surrender letter: “I come, like Themistocles, to seat myself at the hearth of the British people.” (R05).

**R06 Companions + Legend**  
Companions’ dispersal (Bertrand, Montholon, Las Cases, Marchand, Antommarchi); death mask dispute (properly Burton’s). “Thus the imperial epic that had started in Ajaccio in 1769 fizzled out in Chislehurst, Kent, 104 years later.” (R06). (R01–R06 synthesis.)

## 7. Death, Immediate Succession/Aftermath

**R05 Second Abdication + Surrender + Exile Setup**  
Second abdication (22 Jun 1815) after Waterloo; fled to Rochefort; surrendered to Maitland on *Bellerophon* (“I come, like Themistocles, to seat myself at the hearth of the British people”). Letter to Prince Regent (midnight 14 Jul): “‘Your Royal Highness, Exposed to the factions which divide my country and the enmity of the European Powers, I have ended my political career, and I come, like Themistocles, to seat myself at the hearth of the British people. I put myself under the protection of its laws...’” First words on boarding: “‘I come on board your ship to place myself under the protection of the laws of England.’” British cabinet chose St Helena for remoteness (Liverpool to Castlereagh, 20 Jul). Napoleon’s entourage (Bertrand, Montholon, Las Cases, Gourgaud + servants) accompanied; swords and excess gold confiscated. Voyage and initial island conditions (Longwood, The Briars) established the physical and psychological parameters of captivity. (R05).

**R06 Death + Immediate Aftermath**  
Napoleon died at Longwood House on St Helena after ~5.5 years captivity (May 1821). Companions quickly dispersed. Bertrand returned to Paris (Napoleon/Josephine’s rue Chantereine house); died Jan 1844. Montholon shared captivity of Napoleon III at Ham (1840–46, same 6 years as uncle); died Paris Aug 1853. Albine de Montholon separated from husband, affair with Basil Jackson in Brussels; died Mar 1848 at a ball in her honour by grandchildren. Las Cases published *Le Mémorial* (4 vols 1823; constantly revised); elected deputy 1831; died 1842. Marchand wrote memoirs in comfort in Auxerre. Antommarchi published *The Last Moments of Napoleon* (1825); tried to sell copies of death mask in 1833 but sued over authorship (properly Dr Francis Burton’s). On tombstone: “An Italian doctor at the service of the Emperor and the poor.” Hudson Lowe left St Helena; commanded in Ceylon 1825–30; not appointed governor; died aged 74 Jan 1844. Dr Vignali murdered at his house on Corsica Jun 1836. Pauline claimed to have been about to travel when news arrived. Marie Louise contracted morganatic marriage with Neipperg four months after Napoleon’s death. Napoleon II tutored by Marmont (tried to poison mind against father); joined Austrian army; died TB at Schönbrunn 22 Jul 1832, age 21; death mask in Museo Napoleonica, Rome; remains sent to Les Invalides by Hitler 1940. (R06).

**R06 Visuals + Legend Seeds**  
Longwood (5.5 years, death site); obese, balding Napoleon; dead on iron campaign bed (Royal Navy captain sketch). (R06).

Myth outlasted empire; institutions prevented full Ancien Régime restoration. (R05/R06 synthesis.)

## 8. Long-term Legacy and Impact

**R06 Institutions as True Victories + Failure of Restoration**  
“Thanks in large part to the legal and political reforms undertaken by Napoleon, the Bourbons were unable to reintroduce the old ways upon their second Restoration in 1815.” Louis XVIII spent reign finding middle course between reactionary Comte d’Artois (roll back concessions) and liberal constitutionalists. Died 1824; d’Artois became Charles X; overthrown July Revolution 1830. “The hopes of the unreconstructed royalists died with Charles.” Family/imperial continuity: Jérôme returned 1847, gov. Les Invalides 1850, Senate president, died 1860. Charles-Louis-Napoléon elected president 1848 (9.9M votes), coup 1851, Emperor Napoleon III 1852, overthrown after Franco-Prussian 1870–71, died exile 1873 (Chislehurst). “Thus the imperial epic that had started in Ajaccio in 1769 fizzled out in Chislehurst, Kent, 104 years later.” To the end wore the wedding ring his uncle gave Josephine. Many marshals/ministers continued under Bourbons/Louis-Philippe (Soult reforming war minister + Victoria’s coronation; Molé prime minister; etc.). Code/admin legacy endured (prefectoral system, legal code). Counter-examples: many in exile/papal protection; Napoleon II Austrian tutelage + early TB death 22 Jul 1832 age 21. “The imperial epic... fizzled out.” Opponents varied: Wellington “Now I may say I am the most successful general alive”; Talleyrand rebuked aide calling death an “event” (“merely a ‘news item’”). (R06).

**R06 Broader Impact**  
Civil Code (and derivatives) basis of law in France, Belgium, Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Latin America, etc. — “the greatest codification of laws since the fall of the Roman Empire.” Prefects, Banque de France, Conseil d’État, lycées, Légion d’Honneur, rational administration, abolition of feudalism, meritocracy survived 1815/1830. Unintended: provoked German, Spanish, Russian nationalisms defining 19th c. Redrew map (end Holy Roman Empire, Confederation of the Rhine, Grand Duchy of Warsaw). Model of modernizing soldier-statesman who “commence[d] its history” after “romance of the Revolution.” Archetype Great Man vs. structural. “I am the Revolution” egotistical but substantively correct on survivals codified/exported. (R06 synthesis with earlier.)

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

**R06 + Source Critique**  
Pre-2004 biographies relied on Napoleon III’s censored two-thirds edition. Las Cases *Mémorial* (dictated St Helena) deliberate self-mythologizing (“the historian… must persuade”); constantly re-issued revised editions; elected deputy 1831; died 1842. Many memoirs compromised by pensions/jobs under Bourbons (Rémusat changed after 1815; Constant ghosted by multiple writers; Laure d’Abrantès opium addict with Balzac ghosting; Fouché’s by Beauchamp; Bourrienne vicious, died lunatic asylum Caen Feb 1834; Barras “so bileful as to be of little historical value”). Antommarchi published *Last Moments* 1825; tried to sell death mask copies 1833 but sued (properly Dr Francis Burton’s). On tombstone: “An Italian doctor at the service of the Emperor and the poor.” Chateaubriand posthumous *Mémoires d’Outre-Tombe* castigated as tyrant, especially Enghien. Marmont memoirs “like a sharpshooter who hides behind his own tombstone to pick off people who cannot reply.” Post-1940 Hitler analogies distorted; author rejects Geyl-style parallels and “Napoleon Complex” tropes.

**R06 + Roberts Method**  
Roberts (2014) leverages full Fondation correspondence (>33k letters; far more complete/less bowdlerized than 19th-c. compendium) + 53 battlefield visits (often with John Lee) for re-evaluation: charm/humor/self-appraisal from letters; institutions > conquests; Continental System as key error; Russia not inevitable catastrophe. “Every one of them published since 1857 relied upon the correspondence that Napoleon III published... shamefully bowdlerized... In all the compendium included only two-thirds of his total output.” “All too often historians have taken at face value the biographies written by people around Napoleon, whereas many of them were deeply compromised...” “The Comte de Montholon... ghosted by the novelist Alexandre Dumas.” “The Correspondance Générale, published by the Fondation Napoléon since 2004, will comprise all the 33,000 letters signed by Napoleon. It is a publishing undertaking as monumental as it is invaluable, a landmark in Napoleonic scholarship.” Complements (does not duplicate) Chandler (campaigns), Muir (soldier experience), Rothenberg (army), Epstein (1809 modernity). “More books have been written with Napoleon in the title than there have been days since his death.” Collection now strong on personal/institutional layer; operational/economic gaps for cross-reference. “The teeth of envy are powerless here.” (R06 + source).

**Notes + Bibliography (R06):** Full citations to new correspondence (CG vols); battlefield/archive notes. “The bibliography of works on Napoleon is notoriously immense. More books have been published with his name in the title than there have been days since his death.” Complete list at www.andrew-roberts.net; only quoted in printed bib. Plates/legend uses visual sources. (R06).
