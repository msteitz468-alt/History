## 1. Formation and Early Influences

[[actors/musk-elon|Elon Reeve Musk]] was born 28 June 1971 in Pretoria, South Africa, to [[actors/musk-errol|Errol Musk]], an engineer and entrepreneur with a volatile authoritarian streak, and [[actors/musk-maye|Maye Musk]] (née Haldeman), a model and dietitian. The dominant fact of his childhood, repeated across Isaacson’s interviews with Musk, his siblings, and mother, is pain and the learned response of emotional shutdown and risk-seeking.

At twelve he was sent to veldskool, a wilderness survival camp “paramilitary Lord of the Flies.” Children were given tiny rations and “allowed—indeed encouraged—to fight over them.” “Bullying was considered a virtue,” [[actors/musk-kimbal|Kimbal Musk]] recalled. Elon, small and socially awkward, “got beaten up twice” and lost ten pounds. On the second trip, bigger and trained in judo, he learned: “if someone bullied me, I could punch them very hard in the nose.” 

South Africa in the 1980s was violent; Elon and Kimbal once waded through blood next to a corpse with a knife in its brain after an anti-apartheid concert. At six, his favorite German Shepherd “took a massive bite out of his back”; the family later “damn well shot the dog dead.” The deepest wound was paternal. After a school beating that left his face “such a swollen ball of flesh that you could barely see his eyes,” [[actors/musk-errol|Errol Musk]] sided with the attacker and then berated his son for an hour: “I had to stand... as he yelled at me and called me an idiot and told me that I was just worthless.” Kimbal called it “the worst memory of his life.” Errol had a “Jekyll-and-Hyde nature”; tirades could last “an hour or more.” “It was mental torture,” Elon said.

[[actors/musk-errol|Errol]] proudly described exercising “an extremely stern streetwise autocracy” and claimed “Elon would later apply that same stern autocracy to himself and others.” [[actors/musk-justine|Justine Musk]] later diagnosed the adaptation: “With a childhood like his... you have to shut yourself down emotionally in some ways... He learned to shut down fear.” Musk himself: “Adversity shaped me. My pain threshold became very high.”

His maternal grandfather Joshua Haldeman supplied a counter-model of adventurous risk. A chiropractor, cowboy, rodeo performer, and Social Credit activist (with anti-Semitic tinges), he and wife Winnifred (“The Flying Haldemans”) flew their twin daughters Maye and Kaye into the Kalahari searching for a lost city. Maye absorbed “I know that I can take a risk as long as I’m prepared.” Joshua died when Elon was three.

Elon’s sensory system “turns off” when he computes hard problems. He was lonely, called people “stupid,” read encyclopedias and science fiction that pulled him out of adolescent existential depression (Heinlein, Asimov, especially *The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy*—“we need to extend the scope of consciousness”). He built Blastar at thirteen and sold it for $500. At seventeen he left South Africa permanently after a final dinner with Errol (“You’ll be back... You’ll never be successful”), flying to Canada with $4,000 and a list of relatives. He worked a Saskatchewan wheat farm, cleaned boilers in Vancouver in a hazmat suit, interned at a Microsoft Toronto office, then chose Queen’s over Waterloo for social reasons.

At Penn he majored in physics and business, partied hard with Adeo Ressi in West Philadelphia, interned at Pinnacle (supercapacitors) and Rocket Science Games. The summer of 1994 crystallized the three missions: internet, sustainable energy, space. Stanford PhD material science was abandoned after two days: “The internet was not going to wait.”

## 2. Rise / Path to Power

Zip2 (1995) was the first vehicle: [[actors/musk-elon|Elon]] and [[actors/musk-kimbal|Kimbal]] in a tiny Palo Alto office, sleeping at the YMCA, eating Jack in the Box, building the first clickable online map+directory for newspapers. Elon was a brutal coder-reviewer who rewrote others’ work at night and fought his brother physically in the office. Mohr Davidow pushed him aside for a professional CEO; Elon rebelled and was removed as chair. Compaq bought Zip2 in 1999 for $307 million; Elon netted ~$22 million at 27.

X.com (1999) followed with $12 million of his money—an all-in-one financial platform vision. Brutal schedule (Thanksgiving launch), arm-wrestling [[actors/levchin-max|Max Levchin]] over tech stack, merger with Confinity/PayPal after a McLaren crash with [[actors/thiel-peter|Peter Thiel]] aboard (“At least it showed Peter I was unafraid of risks”). A few weeks after the merger, while Musk honeymooned in Australia, Thiel, Levchin, Hoffman, and Sacks executed the “coup.” The board removed him. Musk later reflected both “heinous” and “it was good I got couped.”

The $250 million PayPal payout (eBay sale 2002) funded the next bets. A post-ouster malaria near-death in South Africa and a Russian rocket-buying trip that ended in taunts (“little boy, you don’t have the money?”) produced the first-principles pivot: “I think we can build this rocket ourselves.” Idiot index calculation showed rockets were absurdly overpriced relative to materials. SpaceX incorporated May 2002; Tesla investment/leadership followed 2004.

## 3. Major Phases of Career

**Phase 1: 2002–2008 Founding & Near-Death.** SpaceX in Hawthorne with Tom Mueller as first hire; Falcon 1 developed with maniacal urgency and in-house manufacturing (70%+). Tesla under Eberhard/Straubel with Musk as chair pushing vertical integration and design purity (doors, carbon fiber, lights). Three Falcon 1 failures (2006–2008) and simultaneous Tesla cash crisis (supply chain hell, costs exploding) brought both companies to the brink. Divorce from Justine. Christmas Eve 2008 rescue round + Founders Fund lifeline + fourth launch success 28 Sep 2008 (“Fourth time’s a charm!”) + Daimler rescue saved both. “I couldn’t bring myself to decide that one was going to die.”
