# [Person Name] — Detailed Biography

> **Summary & network:** [[actors/[person-slug]|[Full actor page]]] · **Biography hub:** [[hubs/biographies/biographies-hub|Biographies Hub]]

A graduate-level analytic study of the individual's formation, the mechanics of key decisions, character in context, primary-source foundations, and structural vs. contingent legacy — at substantially greater depth than the standard `actors/` page.

## 1. Formation and Early Influences

Family, education, cultural/political/military milieu, key mentors or traumas, and the specific conditions that equipped (or deformed) this person for what followed.

*(Emphasize what was contingent vs. structural in the making of the individual.)*

## 2. Rise / Path to Power

The sequence of opportunities, alliances, conflicts, and self-presentations that brought the person from relative obscurity (or inherited position) to the center of events. Identify the decisive moments and the resources (personal, institutional, ideological) they mobilized.

## 3. Major Phases of Career

Break the active life into coherent phases. For each:

- Strategic/political/military/intellectual context.
- Primary objectives and constraints.
- Key actions and turning points.
- Immediate results.

*(Use subheadings. Link heavily to events/ and processes/ pages.)*

## 4. Signature Decisions and Their Mechanics

| Decision / Moment | Context & Pressures | Choice Made | Intended Consequence | Actual Outcome | Counterfactual Note | Key Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [e.g. 1740 invasion of Silesia] | ... | ... | ... | ... | Without this, ... | Duffy Ch. X; ... |

4–8 rows. For each major decision, analyse information available at the time, alternatives considered or available, personal style or doctrine applied, and why the outcome differed from (or matched) intent.

## 5. Intellectual / Policy / Military / Religious Style and Methods

What was distinctive in how this person thought and acted? Doctrines, preferred instruments, rhetorical or administrative techniques, attitude toward risk, innovation vs. inheritance, fusion or preservation of traditions.

*(Where relevant, contrast with contemporaries or predecessors.)*

## 6. Character, Relationships, and Personal Life

Historiographically careful portrait: what contemporary and later sources claim about temperament, virtues, vices, private life, and key relationships (family, patrons, rivals, inner circle). Distinguish contemporary polemic or hagiography from evidence.

## 7. Death, Immediate Succession / Aftermath

Circumstances of death (or exit from power). What happened to the structures, projects, or followers immediately afterward? How much of the person's work survived the transition?

## 8. Long-term Legacy and Impact

Structural consequences (intended and unintended) across the relevant scales. How later actors, institutions, or cultures used, mythologized, or reacted against this life. Enduring changes in politics, culture, warfare, religion, etc.

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

- **Primary sources** — quote key passages verbatim; analyse each author's bias, access, and distance from events. Note what is lost or distorted.
- **Conflicting accounts** — reconcile or flag (motives, numbers, sequence, character judgments).
- **Major scholarly lives and debates** — the live interpretive positions (great-man vs. structural, myth-making, national or civilizational appropriation, recent revisionism).
- **Methodological notes** — how biography as a genre has been applied here (psychohistory cautions, prosopography, etc.).
