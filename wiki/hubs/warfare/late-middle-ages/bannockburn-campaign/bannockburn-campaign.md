# Bannockburn Campaign (1314)

War/campaign hub for the decisive 1314 phase of the Wars of Scottish Independence, culminating in the Battle of Bannockburn.

**Key battle analysis:**
- [[hubs/warfare/late-middle-ages/bannockburn-campaign/battle-of-bannockburn-1314|Battle of Bannockburn — Tactical Analysis]]

**Narrative and full sources:** See [[events/battle-of-bannockburn-1314]].

Material drawn especially from DeVries (detailed tactical reconstruction of schiltrons, terrain engineering, infantry discipline, English response) and cross-referenced with NCMH, comparisons to Courtrai/Morgarten, and later English adoption of dismounted tactics.

**Related:**
- Precursor: William Wallace and Stirling Bridge (1297).
- Consequence: secured de facto Scottish independence (Treaty of Edinburgh 1328); influenced English tactics at Dupplin Moor/Halidon Hill and in the Hundred Years War (dismounted men-at-arms + archery).
