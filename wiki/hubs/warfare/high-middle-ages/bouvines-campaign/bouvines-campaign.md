## Campaign Context from France (Ch. 13)
John's 1214 strategy repeated Henry I's 1124 approach (eastern imperial diversion while operating in Poitou/Anjou). John landed at La Rochelle 15 Feb, tied down Prince Louis; William Longsword brought coalition to Valenciennes ~23 July. Philip marched to Tournai 26 July (~1,400 knights, 5–6k foot). Allies (~same knights, ~7.5k foot) at Mortagne 12km south; Otto IV brought few troops, leaving no dominant central figure. Philip chose retreat west toward Lille; allies attempted rapid cross-country intercept at Bouvines bridge over Marque. Decisive: Philip committed rearguard cavalry SE as screen under Guérin (front ~1km); French formed line behind it. Allies deployed from march column under screen of their own cavalry. Guérin launched French units sequentially, wearing down allied horse; Otto's disordered main charge produced giant mêlée. When Guérin broke the enemy cavalry screen, allied army collapsed. "Bouvines was a victory for cavalry... on the French side their discipline and order were remarkable." "good leadership was the decisive factor. Philip was firmly in charge... By contrast, Otto fought bravely, but he never seems to have been in control." Infantry on allied side poorly handled; Renaud of Boulogne's mercenaries showed what disciplined foot could do. Consequences: Capetian consolidation of Normandy/Anjou; Otto IV's fall; road to Magna Carta. (Grounded in France Ch. 13, range 04.)
