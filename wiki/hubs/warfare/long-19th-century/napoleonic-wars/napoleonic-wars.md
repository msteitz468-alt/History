# Napoleonic Wars — Campaign & Operational Analysis

> **Narrative:** [[napoleonic-wars|The Napoleonic Wars (event page)]] · **Warfare hub:** [[hubs/warfare/warfare-hub|Warfare & Strategy]] · **Period:** [[long-19th-century|Long 19th Century]]

The wars in which **[[napoleon|Napoleon]]** made battle **decisive** again, then over-reached and was
destroyed. This hub follows Doughty & Gruber's arc across chapters 6–9: the **[[nation-in-arms|nation
in arms]]** of the [[french-revolution-1789|Revolution]] forging mass armies; the **[[napoleonic-warfare|
Napoleonic system]]** (the corps, dispersed movement, concentration for annihilation, pursuit) at its
zenith; and then its **limits and collapse**.

## The operational arc

1. **Decisive dominance (1805–1807):** [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/napoleonic-wars/battle-of-austerlitz|Austerlitz]] and [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/napoleonic-wars/battle-of-jena-auerstedt|Jena–Auerstedt]] — the system at its most lethal.
2. **The limits (1808–1812):** the Spanish *guerrilla* ("the Spanish ulcer") and the catastrophic [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/napoleonic-wars/russian-campaign-1812|invasion of Russia / Borodino]] — strategic over-reach beyond the system's logistical and political limits.
3. **Breaking the power (1813–1815):** coalition mass and adaptation at [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/napoleonic-wars/battle-of-leipzig|Leipzig]] (the Battle of the Nations) and the final defeat at [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/napoleonic-wars/battle-of-waterloo|Waterloo]].
