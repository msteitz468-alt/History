# Battle of Fredericksburg — Tactical Analysis

> **Narrative & full sources:** [[events/battle-of-fredericksburg-1862|Full event page]] · **War hub:** [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/american-civil-war/american-civil-war|American Civil War War Hub]] · **Warfare hub:** [[hubs/warfare/warfare-hub|Warfare & Strategy]]

Staff-college analysis grounded in Range 2 captions and maps of the source.

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**Union (Burnside)** — Cross Rappahannock, seize Fredericksburg, drive on Richmond or destroy Lee's army before winter set in. Political pressure for action after Antietam.

**Confederate (Lee)** — Defend northern Virginia; use the Rappahannock as an obstacle and the heights behind Fredericksburg for a strong defensive position.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

- **Ground:** Wide Rappahannock River required pontoon bridges for crossing (vulnerable); open plain in front of Marye's Heights / Sunken Road line provided killing ground for defenders. Town of Fredericksburg itself an obstacle to deployment.
- **Weather:** December; cold, with the "Mud March" following the battle (source image 129: Giovanni Ponticelli painting of the Mud March).
- **Intelligence:** Lee had time to concentrate and entrench after Burnside's slow approach and bridge delays.

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

Union forces crossed in two main efforts (Franklin on left, Sumner/Hooker center/right). Confederates: Longstreet's corps on the heights dominating the town approaches; Jackson on the right flank.

(Full OOB per source campaign materials and referenced OR.)

*Deployment:* Union bridgeheads and assault columns advancing across the plain into prepared Confederate artillery and infantry fire from the ridge line.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Phase 1 — Crossing (11-12 Dec):** Delays building/using pontoons under fire; town occupied.
2. **Phase 2 — Main assaults 13 Dec:** Morning and repeated attacks, especially against the stone wall and Sunken Road at the base of Marye's Heights. "The stampede of the Eleventh Corps" context (related image).
3. **Phase 3 — Flank efforts and close:** Franklin's limited success on the left; overall repulse.
4. **Phase 4 — Withdrawal:** Burnside pulls back; subsequent Mud March.

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Frontal assault against entrenched defenders on reverse slope or commanding ground; artillery dominance from heights.

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

Decisive Confederate victory with disproportionate Union losses from repeated assaults.

Source explicitly: Confederate victory under Lee. 11–15 December 1862.

## 6. Critical Decisions — What Went Right and Wrong

**Union:** Poor choice of frontal attacks against known strong position; lack of adequate reconnaissance or flanking maneuver; command friction (Franklin vs. Burnside).

**Confederate:** Excellent selection and fortification of ground; patient defense; concentration.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

- **Objective** — Union objective clear but unattainable with the means and plan employed.
- **Offensive** — Violated by attacking strength without adequate preparation or mass.
- **Mass** — Union failed to concentrate combat power at a decisive point.
- **Economy of Force** — Confederates excelled by holding with minimal force on some sectors.
- **Maneuver** — Union crossing achieved but not exploited into maneuver room.
- **Unity of Command** — Burnside's direction of multiple corps across the river was strained.
- **Security** — Defenders had excellent security on the heights.
- **Surprise** — None for the defender.
- **Simplicity** — Union plan devolved into costly direct assaults.

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Classic example of the futility of frontal attacks against prepared positions in the rifled-musket era (prefiguring WWI trench warfare lessons). Emphasizes the importance of combined arms synchronization and avoiding "forlorn hope" assaults without suppressing fire or flanking. Taught as a case of defensive advantage of terrain and time.

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

- **Primary sources** — OR Series I (implied in source's campaign OOB and maps). Contemporary images: Kurz & Allison art of the crossing; Brady burial scene; Ponticelli Mud March.
- **Conflicting accounts** — Map situations provide precise timing (3:30 PM to dark) for reconstruction.
- **Modern scholarship** — West Point History (2014) places it as the capstone of the 1862 Eastern failures for the Union before Chancellorsville.
