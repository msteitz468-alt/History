## Major Phases and Theaters (from West Point History)

- **Origins and Borderlands (1860–early 1862)**: Secession, Fort Sumter, mobilization, First Bull Run, Shiloh, contest for Kentucky/Tennessee/Missouri.
- **War in the East 1861–1862**: Peninsula Campaign, Second Bull Run, Antietam, Fredericksburg.
- **Lee’s War in the East 1862–1863**: Chancellorsville, Gettysburg.
- **Grant’s War in the West**: Forts Henry & Donelson, Shiloh, Vicksburg, Chattanooga.
- **Coordinated Strategy and Hard War 1864–1865**: Overland Campaign, Atlanta, March to the Sea, Petersburg, Appomattox.
- **End and Reconstruction**: Surrenders (Lee then Johnston at Bennett Place), demobilization, military aspects of Reconstruction (occupation, Freedmen’s Bureau, counter-Klan/White League ops, Grant-Johnson tensions).

## Key Commanders
- Union: Abraham Lincoln, Ulysses S. Grant, William T. Sherman, George B. McClellan, Henry W. Halleck, George G. Meade.
- Confederate: Jefferson Davis, Robert E. Lee, Thomas J. “Stonewall” Jackson, James Longstreet, Braxton Bragg, Joseph E. Johnston.

**Intellectual, doctrinal, and organizational origins of modern (positional/attritional) warfare:** [[sources/hagerman-american-civil-war-origins-modern-warfare-1988|The American Civil War and the Origins of Modern Warfare (Edward Hagerman, 1988)]] supplies the missing causal spine. Prewar doctrine (Dennis Hart Mahan at West Point: active defense, rifle + spade, entrenchment preference) + rifled musket firepower + industrial logistics (rail + animal transport) + command adaptations (McClellan’s professionalizing staff/signals/engineering/logistics reforms and their 1863 maturation; Lee’s slow conversion from élan to systematic entrenchment; Western parallel via Halleck’s entrenching advances and Vicksburg siege) produced routine offensive entrenchment and continuous positional/attritional campaigning by 1864. Overland = threshold of "new era of entrenched positional warfare"; Atlanta/March = organizational exploitation of the new conditions for strategic exhaustion. Engineering organization declined in elite status as its techniques became universal. Explicit WWI parallels drawn.
