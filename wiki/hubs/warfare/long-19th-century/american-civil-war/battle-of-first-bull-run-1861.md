A staff-college-level analysis of objectives, terrain, order of battle, the phased conduct of the engagement, the critical decisions, and the lessons — at greater depth than the standard `events/` page.

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**United States (McDowell)** — primary objective: defeat Confederate forces near Manassas to clear road to Richmond and deliver "single grand victory" expected to end war quickly; public/media pressure "On to Richmond"; bypass slow Anaconda. Political constraint: Lincoln insisted despite McDowell warning troops "green."

**Confederate States (Beauregard/Johnston)** — primary objective: defend advanced position, repel invasion of Virginia; use interior lines/rail for concentration; avoid decisive loss early while building strength.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

- **Ground:** Bull Run creek; Matthews Hill to Henry House Hill; dense woods later noted in VA theater generally limited visibility/artillery/cavalry; "forest which... required the axmen to precede the artillery."
- **Weather/season:** July; no specific adverse noted in range for this fight.
- **Intelligence:** Confederates surprised; Union plan to fix right/center while flanking left at Sudley Springs; separate Union force in Shenandoah failed to pin Johnston reinforcements.

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

| Element | United States | Confederate States |
|---|---|---|
| Total | 35,000 | 25,000 (initial) |
| Infantry | Main columns | Defending positions |
| Cavalry | Limited | Limited |
| Missile / other | Artillery (11 guns at hill) | Artillery (13 guns at hill) |
| Command arrangement | McDowell divided into three parts | Beauregard/Johnston; later Johnston rail arrival |

*Deployment:* Union center (8,000) and left (main 12,000) to turn left at Sudley; right to fix. Confederates initially thin on left; rushed to Matthews Hill then Henry House Hill reverse slope.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Phase 1 — Approach and initial contact:** Union columns collide internally; left delayed crossing until 9:30 a.m.; Confederates surprised, rush troops (900→2,800 at Matthews Hill); Sherman flank drives them to Henry House Hill ~11:30.
2. **Phase 2 — Main engagement on Henry House Hill:** McDowell artillery bombardment (13 vs 11 guns); piecemeal attacks ("no more than two attacked at the same time" of 15 regiments). 8,500 rail reinforcements arrive. Jackson "stood 'like a stone wall' for several hours on the reverse slope."
3. **Phase 3 — Turning point:** 3:00 p.m., 33rd Virginia (blue uniforms) attacks artillery, mistaken for Federals, guns captured. Federal offensive collapses.
4. **Phase 4 — Rout and withdrawal:** "Confusion turned to chaos, and retreat to rout"; bridge jammed; spectators flee. No effective Confederate pursuit (exhaustion/disorganization).

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Flank maneuver vs. defensive stand + rail concentration; piecemeal commitment; uniform confusion.

- Strategic result: Confederates achieved local victory and demonstrated capability; Union plan failed on coordination.
- Immediate: Media/political panic; Crittenden Resolutions. Longer: showed material advantage insufficient alone; war would be longer.

## 6. Critical Decisions — What Went Right and Wrong

**Confederate success factors:** Rail agility to concentrate; defensive position on reverse slope; Jackson stand; exploitation of uniform confusion.
**Union shortcomings:** Internal column collision delayed flank; piecemeal attacks; failure to pin Shenandoah forces; poor coordination of 15 regiments (only 2 attacking at once); no exploitation of initial surprise.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

- **Objective** — Union aimed at decisive Richmond-opening victory but execution diluted it.
- **Offensive** — Initial Union flank honored offensive; broke down into unsupported attacks.
- **Mass** — Violated by both: Union piecemeal (only 2 regiments); Confederates initially thin but concentrated via rail.
- **Economy of Force** — Union fixed forces not effectively used while main effort delayed.
- **Maneuver** — Union planned effective turning movement at Sudley but coordination failed.
- **Unity of Command** — Violated: divided columns, no effective higher coordination; separate Shenandoah force failed mission.
- **Security** — Confederates surprised initially; Union civilians/spectators present.
- **Surprise** — Confederates achieved with rail arrival and uniform ruse; Union lost initiative.
- **Simplicity** — Union plan complex (3 parts); broke on execution.

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Piecemeal commitment and coordination failures in wooded terrain highlight enduring challenges of C2 at scale; value of interior lines/rail for rapid concentration; danger of over-reliance on single "decisive battle" without follow-through; uniform/identification friction in fluid fights.

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

- Primary: Range descriptions from Neely chapter (ch01oa.html etc.) emphasize plan vs. execution gap and media role. "The U.S. Army’s panicked flight made it clear..."

- Modern scholarship: Range (as West Point text) stresses operational agility + defensive advantage; coordination as "most difficult task."
