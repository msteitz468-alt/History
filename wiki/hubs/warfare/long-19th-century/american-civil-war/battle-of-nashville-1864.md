A staff-college-level analysis of objectives, terrain, order of battle, the phased conduct of the engagement, the critical decisions, and the lessons — at greater depth than the standard `events/` page.

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**United States (Thomas)** — primary objective: Destroy Hood’s army; end the threat in the West. Thomas waited for reinforcements and good conditions.

**Confederate States (Hood)** — primary objective: Defend or counterattack at Nashville; somehow reverse the losses from Atlanta and Franklin.

Hood took up a defensive position south of Nashville; Thomas attacked after weather cleared.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

- **Ground:** Hills and open ground south of Nashville; Confederate lines on ridges.

- **Weather / season:** Mid-December 1864; ice and snow delayed Thomas.

- **Intelligence:** Thomas had superior forces and scouted well.

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

| Element | United States | Confederate States |
|---|---|---|
| Total | ~55,000 | ~25,000 |
| Infantry | Multiple corps | Remnants of Army of Tennessee |
| Cavalry | Large | Forrest |
| Missile / other | Artillery | Artillery |
| Command arrangement | Thomas | Hood |

*Deployment (described as a map would show it):* Union attacked Confederate left and center on Dec 15; broke the line. On Dec 16, further attacks shattered the army.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Phase 1 — Dec 15:** Thomas attacked the Confederate left (Stewart’s corps); flanking and frontal assaults broke the line. Confederates fell back to new line.

2. **Phase 2 — Dec 16:** Renewed attacks on the new line; Confederate right and center collapsed under pressure. Hood’s army routed in the “Battle of Nashville.”

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Coordinated attacks on weakened Confederate lines; exploitation of the breakthrough.

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

| | United States | Confederate States |
|---|---|---|
| Killed | ~400+ | ~1,500+ |
| Captured / wounded | ~2,600 total | ~4,500+ total (plus thousands captured later) |
| Source(s) | OR, West Point text | OR, West Point text |

- Strategic result: Hood’s army ceased to exist as a fighting force; retreated in disorder to Mississippi.

- Immediate follow-on: End of major Confederate operations in the West.

## 6. Critical Decisions — What Went Right and Wrong

**Union success factors:** Thomas’s patience and preparation; superior numbers and coordination; exploitation.

**Union shortcomings:** Delay due to weather (criticized by Grant).

**Confederate success factors:** None.

**Confederate shortcomings:** Hood’s army was too weak after Franklin; poor position and leadership.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

- **Objective** — Thomas achieved the objective of destroying the enemy army.

- **Offensive** — Well-planned and executed.

- **Mass** — Achieved at the point of attack.

- **Economy of Force** — Good.

- **Maneuver** — Flanking attacks key.

- **Unity of Command** — Thomas had it.

- **Security** — Good.

- **Surprise** — Achieved on Dec 15.

- **Simplicity** — Clear plan.

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Example of a commander waiting for the right conditions (Thomas). Destruction of an army through successive defeats (Atlanta, Franklin, Nashville). Parallels to operational-level destruction of enemy forces.

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

- **Primary sources** — Thomas’s reports; accounts of the rout.

- **Conflicting accounts** — Grant pressured Thomas to attack sooner.

- **Modern scholarship** — Thomas vindicated; one of the most complete Union victories.
