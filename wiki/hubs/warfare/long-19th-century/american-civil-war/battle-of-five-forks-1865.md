A staff-college-level analysis of objectives, terrain, order of battle, the phased conduct of the engagement, the critical decisions, and the lessons — at greater depth than the standard `events/` page.

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**United States (Sheridan/Warren)** — primary objective: Turn Lee’s right flank at Five Forks; cut the South Side Railroad; force evacuation of Petersburg/Richmond.

**Confederate States (Pickett)** — primary objective: Hold the key crossroads and railroad; protect the flank of the Petersburg lines.

Sheridan’s cavalry and Warren’s infantry attacked Pickett’s isolated command.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

- **Ground:** Open fields and woods at the Five Forks intersection; roads key for movement.

- **Weather / season:** Early April 1865; good for operations.

- **Intelligence:** Sheridan had good cavalry reconnaissance; Pickett was surprised.

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

| Element | United States | Confederate States |
|---|---|---|
| Total | ~20,000+ | ~10,000-12,000 |
| Infantry | V Corps | Pickett’s infantry |
| Cavalry | Sheridan’s | Limited |
| Missile / other | Artillery | Artillery |
| Command arrangement | Sheridan overall; Warren V Corps | Pickett |

*Deployment (described as a map would show it):* Confederates at the crossroads; Union attacked from multiple directions.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Phase 1 — Cavalry action:** Sheridan’s cavalry engaged and fixed the Confederates.

2. **Phase 2 — Infantry assault:** Warren’s V Corps attacked the flank and rear.

3. **Phase 3 — Collapse:** Pickett’s command was rolled up and routed; many captured. Pickett was absent at a shad bake.

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Combined cavalry and infantry attack; exploitation of weak flank.

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

| | United States | Confederate States |
|---|---|---|
| Killed | ~100+ | ~500+ |
| Captured / wounded | ~900 total | ~2,500+ total |
| Source(s) | OR, West Point text | OR, West Point text |

- Strategic result: Lee’s right flank collapsed; he ordered evacuation of Petersburg that night.

- Immediate follow-on: Fall of Petersburg and Richmond; pursuit to Appomattox.

## 6. Critical Decisions — What Went Right and Wrong

**Union success factors:** Sheridan’s aggressive leadership; Warren’s infantry support; surprise on the flank.

**Union shortcomings:** Coordination issues between cavalry and infantry (Warren later relieved).

**Confederate success factors:** None.

**Confederate shortcomings:** Pickett’s absence; isolated position; poor security.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

- **Objective** — Union achieved the objective of turning the flank.

- **Offensive** — Well executed.

- **Mass** — Concentrated at the decisive point.

- **Economy of Force** — Good.

- **Maneuver** — Flank attack decisive.

- **Unity of Command** — Sheridan provided unity.

- **Security** — Confederates violated.

- **Surprise** — Achieved.

- **Simplicity** — Clear plan.

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Example of cavalry and infantry combined arms to turn a flank. Sheridan’s role as a dynamic commander. Lessons in decentralized execution and the importance of key terrain (the forks and railroad).

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

- **Primary sources** — Sheridan’s reports; accounts of the shad bake controversy.

- **Conflicting accounts** — Warren’s relief became controversial.

- **Modern scholarship** — Seen as the beginning of the end for Lee.
