# Battle of Chickamauga — Tactical Analysis

> **Narrative & full sources:** [[events/battle-of-chickamauga-1863|Full event page]] · **War hub:** [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/american-civil-war/american-civil-war|American Civil War War Hub]] · **Warfare hub:** [[hubs/warfare/warfare-hub|Warfare & Strategy]]

Grounded in Range 2 of source (OOB and campaign maps).

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**Union (Rosecrans)** — Advance to capture Chattanooga (key rail/logistics hub); maneuver Bragg out of position.

**Confederate (Bragg)** — Concentrate against isolated Union columns; counterattack to retake initiative in the West and possibly destroy Rosecrans' army.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

- **Ground:** Wooded, broken terrain along Chickamauga Creek with limited fields of fire; McLemore's Cove area.
- **Intelligence:** Bragg successfully concentrated reinforcements (incl. Longstreet) undetected in time; Rosecrans had dispersed corps.

Source maps: "Situation at Dusk, 12 September 1863" (counterattack setup); "Situation at Dawn, 19 September 1863"; "Situation at 1130 Hours, 20 September 1863" (breakthrough phase).

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

"Battle of Chickamauga—Order of Battle" (Rowan 2014, sources Civil War Trust and specific OR vols/parts).

Union deployed in line facing east; Confederates attacked from the south/east with echelon or successive attacks.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Phase 1 — 19 Sep:** Heavy fighting develops as Union columns concentrate; seesaw actions.
2. **Phase 2 — 20 Sep morning:** Bragg renews attacks.
3. **Phase 3 — Breakthrough:** Gap opens in Union line (command/positioning error); Longstreet's wing exploits; much of Union army routed.
4. **Phase 4 — Thomas holds:** George H. Thomas organizes defense on Snodgrass Hill / Horseshoe Ridge; prevents total disaster. Union retreats to Chattanooga.

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Exploitation of a gap in line; defensive stand by isolated commands.

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

Confederate victory on the field but Union army preserved. Led directly to siege of Chattanooga (source covers next).

## 6-8. Critical Decisions, Principles, Modern Parallels

(Condensed per range grounding: Bragg's concentration and attack plan succeeded tactically; Rosecrans' dispersion and reaction to breakthrough failed to prevent gap. Principles: Mass achieved by attackers at point of penetration; Security failed for Union line. Modern: Gap exploitation and "meeting engagement" in restricted terrain; importance of reserve positioning.)
