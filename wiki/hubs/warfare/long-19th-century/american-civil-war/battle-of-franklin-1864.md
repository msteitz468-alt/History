A staff-college-level analysis of objectives, terrain, order of battle, the phased conduct of the engagement, the critical decisions, and the lessons — at greater depth than the standard `events/` page.

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**United States (Schofield)** — primary objective: Retreat to Nashville and link with Thomas; avoid battle if possible but defend if necessary.

**Confederate States (Hood)** — primary objective: Destroy Schofield’s army before it reached Nashville; restore offensive momentum after Atlanta loss.

Hood launched a desperate frontal assault on entrenched Union positions.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

- **Ground:** Open fields in front of Union breastworks at Franklin; Carter house and other structures; no cover for attackers.

- **Weather / season:** Late November 1864; clear.

- **Intelligence:** Hood knew the position but attacked anyway; Union had strong defenses.

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

| Element | United States | Confederate States |
|---|---|---|
| Total | ~30,000 | ~20,000+ in assault waves |
| Infantry | IV and XXIII Corps entrenched | Army of Tennessee in multiple lines |
| Cavalry | Limited | Forrest |
| Missile / other | Artillery | Artillery |
| Command arrangement | Schofield | Hood |

*Deployment (described as a map would show it):* Union behind strong earthworks; Confederates advanced across open ground in waves.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Phase 1 — Approach:** Confederates advanced in the late afternoon.

2. **Phase 2 — Assault:** Massive frontal attacks against prepared positions. Some breakthroughs but repulsed with heavy loss. Fighting around the Carter house was intense.

3. **Phase 3 — Night:** Attacks continued into the night; Union held.

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Frontal assault on entrenched positions with rifled muskets and artillery; no effective flanking.

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

| Killed | ~200+ | ~1,750+ |
| Captured / wounded | ~2,300 total | ~5,000+ total |

- Strategic result: Decisive Union victory; Hood’s army lost irreplaceable manpower and leadership (6 generals killed or wounded).

- Immediate follow-on: Nashville.

## 6. Critical Decisions — What Went Right and Wrong

**Union success factors:** Strong fortifications; disciplined fire; leadership (Schofield, Cox).

**Union shortcomings:** None major.

**Confederate success factors:** None; courage of the assault.

**Confederate shortcomings:** Hood’s decision to attack frontally without adequate preparation or flanking; poor intelligence or disregard for it.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

- **Objective** — Hood’s objective unrealistic given the defenses.

- **Offensive** — Blind offensive without supporting maneuver.

- **Mass** — Mass dissipated by defensive fire.

- **Economy of Force** — Violated by both sides in different ways.

- **Maneuver** — No effective maneuver by Confederates.

- **Unity of Command** — Hood’s command led to disaster.

- **Security** — Poor.

- **Surprise** — None for the defender.

- **Simplicity** — Simple but suicidal plan.

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Textbook example of the futility of frontal assaults on prepared positions. Parallels WWI assaults and lessons on the power of the defense with modern weapons. Hood’s decision is studied as an example of poor generalship driven by desperation.

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

- **Primary sources** — Eyewitness accounts of the slaughter; Hood’s reports blaming subordinates.

- **Conflicting accounts** — Hood vs. subordinates on the decision to attack.

- **Modern scholarship** — Seen as the death knell of the Army of Tennessee; Hood’s worst decision.
