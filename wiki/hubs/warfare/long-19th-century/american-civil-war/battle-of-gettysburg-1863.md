# Battle of Gettysburg — Tactical Analysis

> **Narrative & full sources:** [[events/battle-of-gettysburg-1863|Full event page]] · **War hub:** [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/american-civil-war/american-civil-war|American Civil War War Hub]] · **Warfare hub:** [[hubs/warfare/warfare-hub|Warfare & Strategy]]

Grounded in source Range 2 map situations, OOB, and visual record (Walker, Gardner photos of aftermath).

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**Union (Meade)** — Defend northern soil, concentrate the Army of the Potomac rapidly, force Lee to fight on ground of Union choosing or withdraw.

**Confederate (Lee)** — Invade Pennsylvania to draw Union forces north, forage, win a victory that might end the war or gain foreign recognition. Stuart's absence deprived Lee of cavalry screen/intel.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

- **Ground:** Key features per source imagery: Devil's Den (rocky, defensive), Wheat Field (open killing ground), Little Round Top (flank anchor), Cemetery Ridge (main Union line), Seminary Ridge (Confederate artillery positions for final assault). Fishhook-shaped Union line provided interior lines and enfilade opportunities.
- **Weather:** Early July; hot conditions for assaults.
- **Intelligence:** Stuart's ride around the army left Lee "blind"; Union had better knowledge of converging roads at Gettysburg.

Source maps: "Gettysburg Campaign, Situation 9 June and 17 June 1863"; "24 June 1863". Battle maps for 1 July (1430h, 1800h), 3 July (1430h and Pickett's Charge).

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

Union: Meade in overall command; strong defensive deployment on ridges with artillery reserves.

Confederate: Lee; Longstreet's corps for the 2-3 July assaults; Pickett's fresh division for the grand charge.

"Battle of Gettysburg—Order of Battle" (Rowan 2014, sourced to Laino *Gettysburg Campaign Atlas*, 429–457).

*Deployment:* Union on high ground Cemetery Ridge / Round Tops; Confederates on Seminary Ridge opposite, with echelon attacks on 2 July and massed artillery/infantry assault on 3 July.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Phase 1 — 1 July (meeting engagement):** Initial clash west of town; Union I and XI Corps driven back through Gettysburg to the ridge line. "Gettysburg, The First Day" (Walker painting in source).
2. **Phase 2 — 2 July (flank assaults):** Ewell on left, Longstreet on right. Fighting at Devil's Den ("Alexander Gardner photo: Dead Confederate soldiers in the 'slaughter pen'"), Wheat Field ("Timothy H. O’Sullivan ... wheatfield"), Little Round Top. Close calls on Union left.
3. **Phase 3 — 3 July (Pickett's Charge):** Massive Confederate infantry assault across open ground against the Union center after artillery bombardment. Repulsed with heavy losses. "General Pickett taking the order to charge from General Longstreet"; "Pickett’s Charge" (Sainton etching).
4. **Phase 4 — Close and retreat:** Cavalry actions (Stuart); Lee withdraws.

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Defensive use of ridges and reverse slopes; failure of the grand assault due to artillery and infantry fire on open ground; echelon vs. coordinated attack issues.

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

Union victory. Lee forced to retreat. Source images document aftermath (Gardner dead at Rose Woods, etc.).

## 6. Critical Decisions

**Union:** Meade's rapid concentration and choice to stand on the ridge; correct use of reserves on 2 July.

**Confederate:** Decision to assault on 3 July despite Longstreet's objections and terrain; over-reliance on the charge after Stuart's absence compromised reconnaissance.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

- **Objective** — Lee's objective (decisive victory in the North) was ambitious.
- **Offensive** — The 3 July assault was offensive to a fault.
- **Mass** — Attempted on 3 July but broken by fire before reaching the objective.
- **Economy of Force** — Union used interior lines effectively.
- **Maneuver** — Limited on 3 July by open terrain and Union position.
- **Unity of Command** — Meade coordinated well under pressure.
- **Security** — Union line was well anchored on the Round Tops.
- **Surprise** — Limited after first day.
- **Simplicity** — Pickett's Charge plan was simple but execution under fire failed.

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Gettysburg is the classic U.S. case study for the "decisive battle" concept and the limits of the frontal assault against a prepared defender with artillery support. Pickett's Charge is taught as the dangers of "culmination" and attacking without adequate suppression or maneuver. Parallels to later battles (e.g. Cold Harbor in same source). Emphasizes cavalry's role in screening (Stuart's absence).
