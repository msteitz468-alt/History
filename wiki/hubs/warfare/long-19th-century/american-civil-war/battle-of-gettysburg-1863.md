# Battle of Gettysburg — Tactical Analysis

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**Confederate (Lee)** — After Chancellorsville, the Army of Northern Virginia held high confidence. "The universal feeling in the army was one of profound contempt for an enemy whom they have beaten so constantly." Lee sought a compensating eastern victory to offset the certain loss of Vicksburg. He rejected detaching forces west ("it becomes a question between Virginia and the Mississippi") and secured Davis's approval for invasion of Pennsylvania to draw the Federals north, live off the country, and win "a victory of real consequence won in the Northern heartland." Pre-campaign, Lee and Longstreet agreed: "We should assume the aggressive" while forcing the enemy to attack on ground of Confederate choosing. Subsistence shortages on the Rappahannock ("his men ... were hungry. They had been hungry there since the first of the year") made remaining in Virginia untenable.

**Union (Meade)** — The Army of the Potomac had absorbed Fredericksburg and Chancellorsville but showed remarkable resilience ("This Army of the Potomac is truly a wonderful army ... They have something of the English bull-dog in them"). Officer corps plotted against Hooker; rank-and-file held. Massive mustering-out of two-year and nine-month regiments (30,500 men, >27% of infantry) plus Chancellorsville losses shrank the army. Lincoln directed Hooker (then Meade) to operate on the "inside track," shortening lines while Lee lengthened his, and to fight when opportunity offered. Meade assumed command 28 June; his task was rapid concentration to protect Washington/Baltimore while seeking to force Lee to attack on favorable ground.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

**Cartographic support from Battles Map by Map (DK/Smithsonian):** Detailed multi-day map of the Gettysburg battlefield with phases, key terrain, and force movements.

![Gettysburg (1863) — Battle map showing Day 1-3 actions, deployments, and key features like Little Round Top and Cemetery Ridge (Battles Map by Map, p. 186)](assets/atlases/battles-map-by-map/bmb-p186-gettysburg.png)

*Map from [[sources/battles-map-by-map-2021|Battles Map by Map (DK/Smithsonian, 2021)]]. Provides excellent visual context for the three-day battle.*

**Ground:** Gettysburg sat at the convergence of ten roads. West of town: successive north-south ridgelines (Herr's, McPherson's/Oak, Seminary). South: the fishhook — Culp's Hill, Cemetery Hill and Ridge, Little Round Top and Round Top. "The most westerly ... Herr's Ridge. Immediately south ... rugged Culp's Hill and Cemetery Hill ... forming Cemetery Ridge ... Little Round Top and the taller Round Top." Broken ground at Devil's Den (boulders, "Slaughter Pen"), Wheat Field, Peach Orchard, and Plum Run valley favored cover but fragmented formations.

**Weather/Season:** Early July heat; assaults conducted under punishing conditions.

**Intelligence:** Stuart's ride (launched 25 June with orders to pass around the army, "feel the right of Ewell's troops, collecting information, provisions") removed Lee's "eye of the army." Stuart encountered unexpected Federal infantry, detoured, crossed the Potomac after 74 hours, and remained out of contact. On 28 June Harrison (Longstreet's scout) revealed the Army of the Potomac had crossed three days earlier and was near Frederick. Lee, "astonished," canceled the Harrisburg move and ordered Ewell south "in the direction of Gettysburg." Buford occupied Gettysburg 30 June, screened west, and reported Hill massed at Cashtown; he predicted a heavy morning attack "skirmishers three deep." Federal cavalry and Bureau of Military Information provided accurate identifications; Confederates operated blind.

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

**Union (Meade, ~95k present for duty; ~88k on field effective infantry/artillery):** Concentrated rapidly on interior lines. Left wing (Reynolds) pushed to Gettysburg as covering force while main body closed. Key: I Corps (Reynolds k / Doubleday / Newton), II Corps (Hancock w), III Corps (Sickles), V (Sykes), VI (Sedgwick), XI (Howard), XII (Slocum). Artillery chief Hunt conserved and positioned for the defense. Defensive "fishhook" on high ground with interior lines.

**Confederate (Lee, ~75k; three corps):** Dispersed during march. I Corps (Longstreet — McLaws, Hood, Pickett absent until late), II (Ewell — Early, Johnson, Rodes), III (Hill — Heth, Pender, Anderson). Stuart absent. Lee intended concentration east of South Mountain after Harrison report.

**Deployment 1 July:** Federals on McPherson's then Seminary Ridge (I Corps), XI Corps north. Confederates advanced on Chambersburg Pike (Heth) and from north (Rodes/Ewell).

**2–3 July:** Union on Cemetery Ridge fishhook (Round Tops to Culp's Hill). Confederates on Seminary Ridge / opposite. Longstreet's echelon (McLaws/Hood/Anderson) against Federal left; Ewell against right. Pickett's fresh division for 3 July grand assault.

**Key OOB excerpts (from Sears appendix, OR-based with primary mods; (k)=killed, (mw)=mortally wounded, (w)=wounded, (c)=captured):**

*Union I Corps (heavy losses 1 July):* Reynolds (k), Wadsworth (First Div), Meredith (w) Iron Brigade (2nd WI, 6th WI, 7th WI, 19th IN, 24th MI), Cutler, etc.

*Union II Corps (center 3 July):* Hancock (w), Gibbon (w), Webb (w) — 69th PA, 71st/72nd/106th PA, 1st MN, etc.; Cushing battery (Cushing k).

*Confederate Pickett's Division (3 July):* Pickett; Garnett (k), Kemper (w-c), Armistead (mw) — 8th/18th/19th/28th/38th/56th/57th VA etc.

Full detailed tabulation in [[sources/sears-gettysburg-2003]] "The Armies at Gettysburg."

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

**Phase 1 — 1 July (Meeting Engagement, ~7:00 A.M.–4:30 P.M.):** First shot ~7:30 A.M. (8th Illinois vs. Heth advance at Marsh Creek). Buford's dismounted troopers delayed on Herr's then McPherson's ridges. Reynolds committed I Corps ("fight them all through the town"). Iron Brigade shattered Archer (Archer captured); 6th Wisconsin + supports trapped Davis in railroad cut. Heth repulsed by noon. Ewell (Rodes on Oak Hill) attacked despite Lee's explicit "I do not wish to bring on a general engagement today." O'Neal and especially Iverson's brigade destroyed in uncoordinated assaults. XI Corps (Barlow advanced too far) collapsed at Blocher's Knoll/Early's flank attack. I Corps fell back from Seminary under Pender. Hancock arrived, assumed command, selected Cemetery Hill/Culp's Hill: "This position should be held at all hazards." ~4:00–5:25 P.M. Union held the high ground; ~10,000+ Federal casualties but position secured.

**Phase 2 — 2 July (Flank Assaults, afternoon–dusk):** Lee ordered Longstreet to strike the presumed exposed left while Ewell demonstrated. Sickles (unauthorized) advanced III Corps to Peach Orchard salient. Longstreet's echelon (McLaws perpendicular per Lee's direct order, Hood shifted right) struck across broken ground. Devil's Den/Plum Run: boulders fragmented formations; Smith's battery lost; Hood wounded early. Little Round Top: Warren discovered empty; Vincent seized responsibility ("Hold this ground at all costs"); Chamberlain's 20th Maine refused and bayonet-charged Oates' 15th Alabama in a close call. Wheat Field: Kershaw blunder + Barnes withdrawal + Caldwell's counter (Brooke's bayonet charge) produced mutual slaughter. Peach Orchard: Barksdale's charge overran the salient. Ewell converted to attack at dusk; Johnson lodgment on Culp's Hill halted by Greene's pre-built breastworks/traverse + reinforcements; Early/Hays reached wall on Cemetery Hill but unsupported. Longstreet committed all available (no Pickett reserve); Federals shifted V, II, and arriving VI Corps. Gains not consolidated.

**Phase 3 — 3 July (Artillery + Grand Charge, morning–afternoon):** Lee persisted with plan despite Longstreet's objections and lack of reconnaissance. Culp's Hill morning renewal: Johnson assaults (Steuart etc.) against works futile; ~2,000 Confederate casualties for no gain. Afternoon: 163-gun bombardment (faulty fuzes, overshoot, Ewell's guns inactive, Pendleton mismanagement) largely ineffective. Hunt conserved, replied sparingly, then massed canister. Pickett (fresh), Pettigrew, Trimble advanced ~13,000 men. Oblique march + fences + enfilade (McGilvery + Stannard's Vermonters) + massed infantry fire at wall shattered the assault. Armistead penetrated the Angle ("Come on, boys, we must give them the cold steel") with ~100 but isolated and destroyed (Armistead mw). Left wing (Pettigrew) broke earlier from flank fire and command loss. ~6,600 Confederate casualties from the charge (1,190 dead, 3,350 prisoners). Stuart's cavalry action east (vs. Gregg/Custer) tactically inconclusive.

**Phase 4 — Retreat (4–14 July):** Lee withdrew through rain; Imboden's 17-mile train of 8–8,500 wounded ("cries ... awful"). Meade's 4 July council declined immediate offensive or aggressive pursuit ("until we see what they are doing"). Lee crossed Potomac 13–14 July. Lincoln: "We had them within our grasp."

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Prepared defensive works + interior lines + artillery enfilade vs. echelon assault over broken ground without adequate suppression or reserves. Intelligence failure (Stuart) and command friction (Longstreet) vs. rapid concentration and depth (Meade/Hancock/Hunt).

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

Union victory; Lee forced to retreat from Pennsylvania. "High water mark" of the Confederacy in the East.

**Casualties (Sears Epilogue + appendix):**
- Battle only: Union 22,813 (3,149 dead); Confederate 22,625 (4,536 dead).
- Campaign total: Union ~30,100; Confederate ~27,125.
- Permanently off Confederate rolls from Gettysburg: ~17,250 (incl. 6,802 wounded captured).
- Pickett's Charge alone: ~6,600 Confederate (half of 13,000 engaged); Union ~2,300 in repulse.
- Officer toll: 19 of Lee's 46 div/brig infantry commanders casualties; Longstreet's corps especially gutted (Barksdale, Garnett, Armistead dead; Hood, Kemper wounded/captured).

Full per-unit OOB/casualty tabulation in the source appendix.

## 6. Critical Decisions

**Confederate:**
- Lee: approve invasion despite Vicksburg and logistics; accept Stuart's ride; reject Longstreet's flank proposal on 3 July; persist with frontal assault after inadequate bombardment and reconnaissance.
- Heth: press into Gettysburg 1 July despite orders.
- Ewell: convert demonstration to attack at dusk 2 July without full coordination.
- Stuart: execute the ride that left the army blind at the decisive moment.
- Longstreet: faithful execution despite objections, but echelon disrupted by terrain/Sickles.

**Union:**
- Reynolds: commit I Corps immediately to contest high ground.
- Hancock: select and fortify the fishhook position.
- Sickles: unauthorized advance to salient (altered geometry but created exploitable target).
- Warren/Vincent/Chamberlain: seize and hold Little Round Top.
- Hunt/Meade: conserve artillery, mass reserves, rapid reinforcement of center.
- Buford/Greene: defensive use of dismounted cavalry and engineered works.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

- **Objective** — Lee sought decisive victory in the North to offset Vicksburg and possibly end the war. Ambitious and unachieved.
- **Offensive** — 3 July charge "offensive to a fault"; echelon 2 July pressed against a stronger position after intelligence failure.
- **Mass** — Attempted on 3 July but broken before the objective by enfilade artillery + massed infantry fire at short range. Union used depth and rapid shifting of V/II/VI Corps.
- **Economy of Force** — Union interior lines and reserves (Hancock, Hunt conservation) allowed holding while Confederates dissipated strength piecemeal without reserves (Pickett absent 2 July).
- **Maneuver** — Stuart's ride achieved little positive; Confederate echelon and oblique march exposed flanks. Union rapid concentration on shorter lines succeeded.
- **Unity of Command** — Lee exercised personal control on 2 July (McLaws perpendicular) but overall coordination failed (Longstreet/Ewell/Hill/Pendleton). Meade coordinated under pressure; Hancock assumed on field.
- **Security** — Union anchored on Round Tops and Culp's Hill with works; Confederate right (Stuart) and reconnaissance fatally weak.
- **Surprise** — Limited after 1 July; 3 July charge telegraphed by bombardment.
- **Simplicity** — Pickett's Charge plan simple but execution collapsed under fire, command loss, and absent supports. Union defense relied on clear terrain advantage and pre-positioned depth.

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Classic case study in the limits of the "decisive battle" and frontal infantry assault against a prepared defender with artillery and works (parallels Cold Harbor, later WWI). Demonstrates:
- Catastrophic effect of intelligence/C3 failure (Stuart's absence = "total eclipse"; Harrison report came three days late).
- Value of defensive works, traverses, and interior lines (Culp's Hill, Cemetery Ridge fishhook turned numbers against the attacker).
- Culmination and overextension: Lee after Chancellorsville and long march.
- Decentralized initiative (Warren, Vincent, Chamberlain, Greene) vs. rigid echelon.
- Logistics and morale under sustained pressure (Imboden train; Union bull-dog resilience).
- Post-battle pursuit: councils of war and the gap between political expectation ("within our grasp") and operational reality.

Staff colleges study Gettysburg for reconnaissance, concentration under uncertainty, the offense-defense balance, and the human/terrain factors that decide whether massed infantry can close.
