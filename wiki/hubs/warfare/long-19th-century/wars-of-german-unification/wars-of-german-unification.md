# Wars of German Unification — Campaign & Operational Analysis

> **Narrative:** [[german-unification|German Unification (event page)]] · also [[franco-prussian-war-1870]] · **Warfare hub:** [[hubs/warfare/warfare-hub|Warfare & Strategy]] · **Period:** [[long-19th-century|Long 19th Century]]

The wars in which **[[moltke-helmuth|Moltke]]'s Prussian system** — the political work of
**[[otto-von-bismarck|Bismarck]]** — closed Doughty & Gruber's volume and inaugurated modern
industrialized warfare. This hub treats the operational method that produced swift, decisive victory
over Austria (1866) and France (1870).

## Why it matters militarily

Moltke fused the **railway** (for rapid mobilization and concentration from dispersed bases), the
**telegraph** (for strategic direction of separated armies), the **breech-loading rifle and rifled
artillery**, and a **professional General Staff** practicing **mission command (Auftragstaktik)**. The
method — **"march divided, strike united,"** converging separate armies onto the battlefield for a
**concentric envelopment** — delivered the decisive cauldron battles that the
[[napoleonic-warfare|Napoleonic system]] had sought but industrial means could now reliably achieve.
