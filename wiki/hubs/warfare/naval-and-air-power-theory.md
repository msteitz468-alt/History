# Naval & Air Power Theory

The land-warfare canon — Jomini, Clausewitz — has its counterpart in two younger bodies of
strategic thought: the theory of **sea power** (crystallized 1890–1911) and the theory of
**air power** (1921–1930). This sub-hub of [[hubs/warfare/strategy|Strategy]] collects the
four theorists who first organized each into a structured doctrine, drawn from
*[[sources/jablonsky-roots-of-strategy-4-1999|Roots of Strategy, Book 4]]* (ed. Jablonsky,
1999).

The defining contrast — Jablonsky's organizing thesis — is between **continuity and change**.
The naval theorists mined **history** for principles they believed survived every change of
technology; the air theorists declared the airplane had "broken the evolutionary continuity
of the character of war" and that history taught "less than nothing." The first pair tied
strategy upward to policy and **grand strategy**; the second subsumed policy in the imperative
for **[[total-war|total war]]**.

## The naval theorists

### [[mahan-alfred-thayer|Alfred Thayer Mahan]] (1840–1914) — *The Influence of Sea Power Upon History* (1890)
The most influential of the four. **[[sea-power]]** = the historically demonstrated dominance
of states that controlled the sea, resting on the triad of **navy + overseas trade + colonies**.
Operationally, command of the sea is won by a **concentrated battle fleet** ("never divided")
in **decisive battle**; commerce-raiding is "indecisive, wasteful." His **six elements of sea
power** (geographical position, physical conformation, extent of territory, population,
national character, character of government) frame *why* some nations are maritime powers.
Armed the navalism of Theodore Roosevelt and the Anglo-German naval race.

### [[corbett-julian|Julian S. Corbett]] (1854–1922) — *Some Principles of Maritime Strategy* (1911)
The more Clausewitzian and flexible naval theorist. **[[maritime-strategy]]** (his word,
deliberately broader than "naval") insists "it is almost impossible that a war can be decided
by naval action alone" — sea power matters for its **influence on operations ashore**. Command
of the sea is "**nothing but the control of maritime communications**," frequently *local* and
*temporary*; decisive battle is one option, and a **fleet in being** may suffice. Ties sea
power to the British tradition of **[[limited-war|limited war]]** and **combined operations**
("the army is a projectile to be fired by the navy").

## The air theorists

### [[douhet-giulio|Giulio Douhet]] (1869–1930) — *The Command of the Air* (1921/1927)
The radical. **[[command-of-the-air]]** — "to be in a position to prevent the enemy from
flying while retaining the ability to fly oneself" — is the prerequisite of victory and can be
won only by **large-scale [[strategic-bombardment]]** of enemy "vital centers" and population.
"Aerial warfare admits of no defense, only offense." Demands an **independent air force** of
**battleplanes**, an **air fleet in being** ready to strike before mobilization, and accepts
aerochemical attack on cities. Shaped the Luftwaffe and interwar bombing doctrine.

### [[mitchell-william|William "Billy" Mitchell]] (1879–1936) — *Winged Defense* (1925)
The American agitator. Shares Douhet's call for a **unified, independent air arm** and the
belief that command of the air is decisive ("nothing can stop the attack of aircraft except
other aircraft"), but — writing for an ocean-buffered United States — frames **[[air-power]]**
as continental *defense*, retains **pursuit/interception**, and proved his case by **bombing
battleships** (the *Ostfriesland*, 1921). Court-martialled in 1925; his impact was largely
domestic and posthumous, vindicated by the U.S. air arm of WWII.

## Cross-cutting doctrines

- **[[strategic-bombardment]]** — the doctrine that links Douhet and Mitchell and reaches
  forward to the [[strategic-bombing-campaign|Combined Bomber Offensive]] and the
  [[hubs/warfare/world-wars-era/second-world-war/strategic-bombing-japan-1944-1945|firebombing of Japan]]; its central wager — that bombing civilian morale and "vital centers" can be
  independently decisive — was the most-tested and most-contested claim in the volume.
- **[[total-war]]** — the air theorists' frame; the naval theorists' counter-frame is
  **[[limited-war]]**.
