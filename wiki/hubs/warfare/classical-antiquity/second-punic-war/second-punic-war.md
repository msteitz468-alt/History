# Second Punic War (218–201 BCE)

**Narrative overview**: [[events/second-punic-war-218-201bce]]

This page is the hub for detailed tactical and operational analyses of the major
battles and campaigns of the Second Punic War — the Hannibalic War, the best-recorded
conflict of antiquity and the wiki's deepest warfare cluster.

## Strategic shape of the war

[[goldsworthy-the-fall-of-carthage-2000|Goldsworthy]] reads the war as a contest Rome
should have lost on the battlefield and won on the strength of its institutions:

1. **Hannibal's offensive in Italy (218–216).** The march from Spain over the Alps,
   then three escalating annihilation victories —
   [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/second-punic-war/battle-of-the-trebia-218bce|Trebia (218)]],
   [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/second-punic-war/battle-of-lake-trasimene-217bce|Lake Trasimene (217)]],
   and [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/second-punic-war/battle-of-cannae|Cannae (216)]] —
   that should have ended any Hellenistic war but did not break Rome's will or its
   alliance system.
2. **Attrition and the Fabian answer (216–207).** Rome adopted [[fabian-strategy|Fabius Maximus' strategy]]
   of shadowing and refusing battle, contained defections in southern Italy, and bled
   Hannibal of the means to exploit his victories. The Carthaginian bid to reinforce
   him failed when his brother Hasdrubal was destroyed at the
   [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/second-punic-war/battle-of-the-metaurus-207bce|Metaurus (207)]].
3. **Scipio shifts the war to Spain and Africa (209–202).** The young
   [[scipio-africanus|Scipio]] took the Punic base of
   [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/second-punic-war/battle-of-new-carthage-209bce|New Carthage (209)]]
   by *coup de main*, broke Carthaginian Spain at
   [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/second-punic-war/battle-of-ilipa-206bce|Ilipa (206)]],
   invaded Africa, and finally beat Hannibal himself at
   [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/second-punic-war/battle-of-zama-202bce|Zama (202)]].

## The decisive theme

The war featured Hannibal's invasion of Italy, a series of devastating tactical
victories, and Rome's eventual strategic victory through **attrition (the Fabian
strategy), the resilience of the Italian alliance, and the counter-invasion of
Africa**. Hannibal won every great battle in Italy and still lost the war — the
central illustration of Rome's "deep-seated determination" and of the gap between
tactical annihilation and political victory.
