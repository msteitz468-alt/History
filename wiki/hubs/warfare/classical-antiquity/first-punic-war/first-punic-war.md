# First Punic War (264–241 BCE)

**Narrative overview:** [[events/first-punic-war-264-241bce]]

Hub for staff-college-grade analyses of the battles and campaigns of the first war
between Rome and Carthage — the **greatest naval conflict of antiquity** and the war
in which Rome, an Italian land power with no navy in 264, built fleets of 300+
quinqueremes, lost more ships and men to storms and battle than perhaps any
pre-modern state, and yet out-lasted Carthage to win command of Sicily and the sea.

## Strategic shape of the war

A struggle for **Sicily** that escalated into a naval war of attrition across the
western Mediterranean. Three phases:

1. **The land war in Sicily (264–256).** Rome's intervention at Messana drew in
   Syracuse (soon a Roman ally under Hiero II of Syracuse) and then Carthage. The
   storming of **Agrigentum (262)** showed Rome could beat a Punic field army but not
   quickly clear the fortified ports without sea power.
2. **The naval war and the African gamble (260–255).** Rome built a fleet, used the
   **corvus** boarding-bridge to turn sea fights into land fights, won at **Mylae
   (260)** and the colossal **[[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/first-punic-war/battle-of-ecnomus-256bce|Ecnomus (256)]]**,
   then invaded Africa under **[[atilius-regulus|Regulus]]** — only to be annihilated
   at the **[[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/first-punic-war/battle-of-the-bagradas-255bce|Bagradas / Tunis (255)]]**
   by the Spartan mercenary **[[xanthippus|Xanthippus]]**, and to lose the rescuing
   fleet to a storm.
3. **Stalemate and decision (254–241).** A grinding contest of sieges (Lilybaeum,
   Drepana — where Rome lost a fleet at Drepana in 249 and another to storms) and
   raids, with **[[hamilcar-barca|Hamilcar Barca]]** holding out on Sicily undefeated
   in the field. Rome, near bankruptcy, built one last privately-funded fleet and won
   the decisive **[[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/first-punic-war/battle-of-the-aegates-islands-241bce|Aegates Islands (241)]]**,
   cutting Hamilcar's supply line and forcing Carthage to terms.

## Battle & campaign analyses

- [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/first-punic-war/battle-of-ecnomus-256bce|Battle of Ecnomus (256 BCE)]] — the largest naval battle of antiquity by numbers engaged.
- [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/first-punic-war/battle-of-the-bagradas-255bce|Battle of the Bagradas / Tunis (255 BCE)]] — Xanthippus destroys Regulus' African expedition.
- [[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/first-punic-war/battle-of-the-aegates-islands-241bce|Battle of the Aegates Islands (241 BCE)]] — the decisive naval victory that ended the war.

## Why Rome won

Per [[goldsworthy-the-fall-of-carthage-2000|Goldsworthy]]: not tactical brilliance but
**institutional endurance** — Rome's manpower reserves and willingness to rebuild
fleet after fleet (losing perhaps 600+ ships and over 100,000 men, mostly to storms)
out-lasted a Carthage that fought the war as a limited commitment and would not match
Rome's total mobilization. The war set the template — and the grievances (the
indemnity; the seizure of Sardinia in 237) — for the
[[hubs/warfare/classical-antiquity/second-punic-war/second-punic-war|Second Punic War]].
