# American Revolutionary War — Campaign & Operational Analysis

> **Narrative & political/diplomatic story:** [[american-revolution|The American Revolution (event page)]] · **Warfare hub:** [[hubs/warfare/warfare-hub|Warfare & Strategy]] · **Period:** [[age-of-expansion|Age of Expansion]]

This is the **campaign hub** for the military conduct of the War of Independence: the
strategy, operations, and logistics by which an initially disorganized
[[continental-army|Continental Army]] — backed by state militias and, decisively, by a
French alliance — defeated the world's foremost military and naval power. The broader
political, ideological, and diplomatic narrative lives on the **[[american-revolution]]**
event page; this hub owns the *war*. Battle-level staff analyses are filed beneath this page.

The wiki's source for this analysis is the U.S. Military Academy's cadet textbook,
*[[west-point-history-american-revolution-2017|The West Point History of the American Revolution]]* (2017).

## Strategic Framework

The war turned on an asymmetry the British never solved. Britain had to **reconquer and
hold** a vast, populous territory 3,000 miles across an ocean; the Americans had only to
**not lose their army** and outlast British political will. The *West Point History* is at
pains to stress that [[washington-george|Washington]] was **not by temperament a
[[fabian-strategy|Fabian]]** — "far from … seek[ing] to preserve his own army while wearing
down the enemy," he repeatedly gambled on decisive battle, believing the war "would have to
be won quickly, or not at all." But the catastrophes of 1776 forced a reluctant **strategy of
posts** — preserving the Continental Army as the embodiment of the cause, avoiding a general
action "unless compelled" — which was Fabian *in effect* even as Washington chafed at its
passivity, while he sought the foreign intervention that could offset British sea power.
Saratoga (1777)
secured the **French alliance**; French money, troops, and above all the **fleet** made the
trap at [[siege-of-yorktown-1781|Yorktown]] possible. The British, by contrast, never
reconciled their political aim (restoring loyal allegiance) with their military means
(coercion that alienated the population), and pursued a succession of strategies — New
England, the Hudson, Pennsylvania, and finally the **southern strategy** — without unifying
command or sustaining any one to a decision.

## Theaters and Phases

1. **New England & the siege of Boston (1775–76).** [[battles-of-lexington-and-concord-1775|Lexington & Concord]], [[battle-of-bunker-hill-1775|Bunker Hill]], the [[siege-of-boston-1775|siege of Boston]]; the war begins and the Continental Army is created.
2. **The middle states (1776–78).** The disastrous New York campaign ([[battle-of-long-island-1776|Long Island]]); the recovery at [[battle-of-trenton-1776|Trenton]] and [[battle-of-princeton-1777|Princeton]]; Howe's Pennsylvania campaign ([[battle-of-brandywine-1777|Brandywine]], [[battle-of-germantown-1777|Germantown]]); [[valley-forge-1777|Valley Forge]] and the professionalization of the army under [[steuben-friedrich|Steuben]]; [[battle-of-monmouth-1778|Monmouth]].
3. **The northern turning point (1777).** [[battles-of-saratoga-1777|Saratoga]] — Burgoyne's surrender, and the diplomatic earthquake that brought France into the war.
4. **The southern war (1778–81).** The British [[siege-of-savannah-1779|move south]]; the [[siege-of-charleston-1780|fall of Charleston]] and [[battle-of-camden-1780|Camden]]; the partisan war and [[battle-of-kings-mountain-1780|Kings Mountain]]; Greene's campaign of attrition with [[battle-of-cowpens-1781|Cowpens]] and [[battle-of-guilford-courthouse-1781|Guilford Courthouse]].
5. **Decision in Virginia (1781).** Cornwallis to the Chesapeake; the Franco-American convergence and the [[siege-of-yorktown-1781|siege of Yorktown]].

**New England (1775–76)**
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battles-of-lexington-and-concord|Lexington & Concord (1775)]] — a failed raid become a fighting retreat; irregular harassment of a column.

**The middle states (1776–78)**
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-long-island|Long Island (1776)]] — Howe's turning movement through an open flank.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-trenton|Trenton (1776)]] — the surprise raid that saved the Revolution.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-princeton|Princeton (1777)]] — escaping a trap by a night flank march.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-brandywine|Brandywine (1777)]] — a second envelopment via an unguarded flank.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-germantown|Germantown (1777)]] — bold attack wrecked by plan complexity and fog.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-monmouth|Monmouth (1778)]] — the Valley Forge army proven; command-and-control retrieved a broken attack.

**The northern turning point (1777)**
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battles-of-saratoga|Saratoga (1777)]] — culmination and the destruction of an over-extended army; brought in France.

**The southern war (1779–81)**
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/siege-of-savannah|Savannah (1779)]] — failed Franco-American coordination; the negative rehearsal for Yorktown.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/siege-of-charleston|Charleston (1780)]] — the peril of the besieged force; the largest American surrender.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-camden|Camden (1780)]] — how *not* to use militia; the great negative lesson.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-kings-mountain|Kings Mountain (1780)]] — irregular/civil-war battle; marksmanship vs. an exposed position.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-cowpens|Cowpens (1781)]] — Morgan's double envelopment, the tactical gem of the war.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-guilford-courthouse|Guilford Courthouse (1781)]] — the Pyrrhic victory that broke Cornwallis's army.

- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-the-chesapeake|The Chesapeake (1781)]] — the fleet action that decided the war: sea control as the decisive objective.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/siege-of-yorktown|Siege of Yorktown (1781)]] — the war-ending combined-arms, coalition siege.

## Why the British Failed

Conway's analysis (chapter 5) frames the British defeat as strategic and political, not a
catalogue of battlefield losses — Britain won most major engagements. The decisive factors:
the **logistical tyranny of distance** and dependence on seaborne supply; the failure to
**translate military victory into restored political allegiance**; the **manpower shortfall**
that forced reliance on Loyalists and Germans and on a southern strategy premised on
Loyalist support that never materialized at scale; **divided and hesitant command**; and the
**French naval and military intervention** that, by 1781, stripped Britain of the local sea
control on which its whole expeditionary posture rested.
