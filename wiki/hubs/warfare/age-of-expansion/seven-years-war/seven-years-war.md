# Seven Years' War (European theater) — Campaign & Operational Analysis

> **Narrative & global war:** [[seven-years-war-1756|The Seven Years' War (event page)]] · **Warfare hub:** [[hubs/warfare/warfare-hub|Warfare & Strategy]] · **Period:** [[age-of-expansion|Age of Expansion]]

The European theater of the first "world war," in which **[[frederick-the-great|Frederick the Great]]**
held Prussia against the combined weight of Austria, France, and Russia. For Doughty & Gruber it is the
**apex of [[limited-war|limited-war]] operational art** — the most aggressive pursuit of decisive
battle the dynastic age produced, by a commander forced by strategic encirclement to seek quick
decisions on interior lines.

## Why it matters militarily

Frederick's method — **rigorous drill and fire discipline, the oblique order of attack (refusing one
wing while massing overwhelming force against the enemy's flank), and the use of interior lines to beat
coalitions in detail** — is displayed in the autumn of 1757, when within a month he won
**Rossbach** (against the French) and **Leuthen** (against the Austrians), saving Prussia.

## Battle Analyses (this hub)

- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/seven-years-war/battle-of-rossbach|Rossbach (1757)]] — Frederick destroys a larger Franco-Imperial army by mobility and surprise.
- [[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/seven-years-war/battle-of-leuthen|Leuthen (1757)]] — the oblique order in its perfected form against the Austrians.
