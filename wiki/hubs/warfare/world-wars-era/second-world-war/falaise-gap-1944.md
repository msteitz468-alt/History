Staff-college-level analysis of the climactic encirclement battle of the Normandy campaign. The "Falaise Gap" (or Pocket) remains one of the most controversial episodes of the war in the West.

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**Allies:** Complete the destruction of the German forces trapped in Normandy by linking the Canadian drive south from Falaise with the American drive north from Argentan, sealing the escape routes to the east.

**Germans:** Extricate as many troops as possible from the collapsing salient to fight another day on the Seine or in Germany.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

The narrow corridor between Falaise and Argentan; roads jammed with German columns; Allied air supremacy turned the pocket and escape routes into killing grounds.

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

Canadian/Polish forces from the north; US XV Corps and later additional divisions from the south; the gap between the two forces the critical variable.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Development of the pocket:** German forces ordered to hold or withdraw too late.
2. **Closing attempts:** Canadian attacks toward Falaise/Trun; US forces reach Argentan and are halted (controversy over halt order).
3. **The gap remains open:** Significant forces escape 16–19 August.
4. **Final closure at Chambois:** Polish 1st Armoured links with US forces; pocket liquidated.

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

Tens of thousands of Germans captured; the bulk of the Seventh Army and much of Fifth Panzer Army destroyed or captured. Large numbers escaped (estimates vary; D'Este discusses the figures and the controversy). Enormous materiel loss.

## 6. Critical Decisions

The most disputed: Bradley's decision to halt US forces at Argentan; Montgomery's coordination and priority; Patton's repeated requests to close the gap from the south; the speed of German withdrawal under pressure.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

(Details from D'Este: Offensive and Pursuit opportunities partially squandered by hesitation and command friction; Unity of Command strained at the army group boundary; Surprise and Security for the escaping Germans limited by Allied air.)

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Classic study of the difficulties of completing an encirclement when multiple national chains of command and competing priorities are involved. Lessons on exploitation, the value of air interdiction of LOCs, and the dangers of "gaps" in pursuit.

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

D'Este devotes an entire chapter to the controversy with primary evidence from all participants. Bradley defended the halt; Patton was furious. Montgomery's account and the British official history have been criticized. German sources emphasize the determination of the troops to break out and the role of rearguards. The episode remains a live point of contention in Normandy historiography.
