Staff-college-level analysis of the multi-pronged Allied offensive that isolated and bypassed the great Japanese base at Rabaul without a direct costly assault.

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**Allies:** Close pincers on Rabaul; neutralize its air/sea threat to Allied advance; open path to Philippines and Central Pacific link-up. "Cartwheel called for no fewer than thirteen separate landings."

**Japan:** Hold Rabaul as linchpin of southern perimeter; use for air/sea projection.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

- Complex island chains, jungle, limited infrastructure.
- Air superiority key to isolating Rabaul.
- Intelligence: Allies chose bypass when direct assault unnecessary.

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

| Element | Allies | Japan |
|---|---|---|
| Total | Multiple US/Aus/NZ divisions + air/sea | 8th Area Army + Rabaul garrison |
| Key ops | 13 landings (pincers) | Defensive garrisons |

*Deployment:* Dual advance: MacArthur along New Guinea coast; Halsey through Solomons. Air blockade of Rabaul.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Phase 1 — Advance on Rabaul (1943):** Series of landings closing pincers.
2. **Phase 2 — Decision to Bypass:** "The pincers were closing on Rabaul, but they never did close completely... the U.S. Joint Chiefs decided to bypass the fortress altogether."
3. **Phase 3 — Neutralization:** Continuous air and sea pressure; Rabaul starved of supplies/reinforcements.
4. **Phase 4 — Isolation Complete:** Rabaul out of the war without major ground battle.

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Bypass and neutralize vs direct assault; airpower + sea control to isolate.

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

- Major savings in Allied casualties vs hypothetical direct attack on Rabaul.
- Japanese forces at Rabaul effectively imprisoned.

## 6. Critical Decisions — What Went Right and Wrong

**Allied right:** Flexibility to bypass after initial plan; use of air/sea to neutralize without ground cost.

**Japanese:** Inability to contest air/sea control or relieve isolated garrison.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

- **Objective:** Allies achieved neutralization with economy.
- **Offensive:** Maintained momentum via multiple ops.
- **Mass:** Applied selectively at chosen points.
- **Economy of Force:** Bypass maximized.
- **Maneuver:** Dual advance + air.
- **Unity of Command:** MacArthur/Halsey coordination (inter-theater).
- **Security/Surprise:** Varied per landing.
- **Simplicity:** Overall plan complex but execution adaptive.

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Cartwheel is the canonical example of the "bypass and neutralize" operational art in island warfare and expeditionary campaigns. Parallels modern concepts of shaping operations, A2/AD defeat through isolation, and economy in choosing decisive points. Directly influenced later decisions (e.g., not storming every Japanese stronghold).

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

Citino highlights the shift from Guadalcanal/Tarawa lessons to operational maturity: Rabaul "never did close completely." West Point emphasizes the strategic payoff of not repeating high-cost fights when air/sea dominance allows isolation. Japanese sources describe slow strangulation.
