A staff-college-level analysis of objectives, terrain, order of battle, the phased conduct of the engagement, the critical decisions, and the lessons — at greater depth than the standard `events/` page.

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives
**Axis (Rommel/Arnim):** Exploit interior lines to mass against first arriving Allied army (US II Corps southern flank of First Army) before British Eighth (Montgomery) links up from east; destroy or delay to retain Tunisia. Constraints: stretched logistics after Torch, air inferiority.

**Allies (Fredendall/Anderson):** Secure Tunisia after Torch (Nov 1942); link Eighth Army; clear North Africa per Casablanca. US inexperienced in high-intensity combined arms.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence
- **Ground:** Mountain passes (Faïd, Kasserine, Sbiba, Thala); Western Dorsal as last defensible line; exposed US positions at Sidi bou-Zid/Gafsa.
- **Weather/season:** February — favorable for mobile ops initially.
- **Intelligence:** Germans knew US dispositions/weakness; US surprised by speed/scale of Panzer thrusts (Tiger tanks).

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment
| Element | Axis | Allies |
|---|---|---|
| Total | Multiple Panzer divs + Italian (200+ tanks incl. Tigers) | US II Corps (inf/eng/arty + 1st Armd elements); later British/US reinforcements |
| Infantry | 15th/21st Panzer + Centauro elements | 168th Inf Regt + Task Force Stark + 1st Armored |
| Cavalry | Limited | Limited |
| Missile / other | 88mm AA/AT guns, artillery | US artillery + French arty + naval gunfire support (later) |
| Command arrangement | Rommel (overall mobile ops); Arnim (Fifth Pz Army) | Fredendall (II Corps); Anderson (First Army) |

*Deployment (described as a map would show it):* Axis: concentrated Panzer forces from Faïd Pass area for thrust through Kasserine. Allies: dispersed US positions at Sidi bou-Zid/Gafsa; Task Force Stark holding Kasserine Pass heights with inf/engineers/French arty.

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases
1. **Phase 1 — Opening (Feb 14–16, Frühlingswind/Morgenluft):** Powerful Panzer drive from Faïd Pass overwhelms US at Sidi bou-Zid; encircles 168th Regt; Gafsa overrun. "It was a Panzer attack in the grand style..."
2. **Phase 2 — Main (Feb 19–20, Kasserine Pass):** Afrika Korps attacks pass; Task Force Stark (inf/engineers/French arty) fixed frontally + flanked on heights; 88mm fire destroys counterattacks. US defenses collapse; 200 tanks wrecked. "Survivors streamed away in confusion..."
3. **Phase 3 — Stiffening (Feb 20–22):** US defenses hold at Sbiba/Thala/Tebéssa (British 26th Armd + 9th Inf arty from 800 miles; 16th Inf + 1st Armd counter). Rommel's three thrusts fail.
4. **Phase 4 — Withdrawal (Feb 23):** Rommel orders retreat; supplies critical.

**Key tactical mechanisms:** Interior lines for concentration; combined arms Panzer thrusts; US artillery and naval gunfire as decisive in defense; logistics as limiting factor.

- Strategic result: Axis failed to deliver knockout; Allies preserved position and learned.
- "Kasserine was a strategic victory for the Allies."

## 6. Critical Decisions — What Went Right and Wrong
**Axis success factors (initial):** Concentration, surprise, armor/tiger edge, combined arms.
**Shortcomings:** Overextension, fuel/ammo exhaustion, Montgomery closing.
**Allied:** Initial command failure (Fredendall in bunker); later artillery/firepower + leadership stiffening.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)
- **Objective:** Axis honoured (smash First Army flank) initially but violated by overreach (logistics).
- **Offensive:** Axis strong early; Allies shift to defence then counter.
- **Mass:** Axis local mass succeeded at Sidi bou-Zid/Kasserine but Allies massed arty later.
- **Economy of Force:** US initially dispersed/vulnerable.
- **Maneuver:** Germans used mobility well; US improved with reserves.
- **Unity of Command:** Poor US (Fredendall lost control); later 18th Army Group.
- **Security:** US surprised; intelligence failure.
- **Surprise:** Axis achieved.
- **Simplicity:** Initial German plan direct; later multi-thrust complicated.

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels
Interior lines exploitation; value of artillery/fire support in defense; combined-arms maturation; lessons on inexperienced troops vs. veterans; logistics as decisive in mobile ops (parallels to later campaigns).

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources
- Primary: Rommel quote ("The Americans... had fought brilliantly"). Citino narrative emphasizes US "pulling itself together" (logistics, firepower, field-grade leadership) vs. initial amateurishness. British "Alice" slur noted.
- Conflicting: Tactical vs. strategic assessment of Kasserine.
- Modern: West Point operational focus on leadership/logistics.
