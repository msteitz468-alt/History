## Major Phases and Theaters (from West Point History Vol. 1 focus)

- **Road to War / Prelude**: Interwar period, Versailles, rearmament, rise of Axis (Waddell/Overy contributions).
- **Early German Campaigns / Blitzkrieg (1939-1941)**: Poland, Scandinavia, Low Countries, Fall of France, Battle of Britain (Citino/Overy).
- **Barbarossa and Eastern Front 1941–1945**: German invasion of USSR through Soviet strategic offensives (Megargee + Glantz/House). See dedicated analysis [[hubs/warfare/world-wars-era/second-world-war/operation-barbarossa-1941]] and Glantz/House operational synthesis (three periods, Red Army adaptation, maskirovka, force data).
- **Pacific to Midway (1941-1942)**: Pearl Harbor, Japanese expansion (Malaya/Singapore 8 Dec 1941–15 Feb 1942; Philippines; Java/NEI), Coral Sea (May), Midway (June) (Drea). Two full hub analyses now complete from Range 4.
- **Economies and Peoples at War**: Home fronts, production, resources (Overy).

**From West Point Vol. 1 Range 5 (Overy + Rogers "War at Midpoint") — synthesis for the hub:**
- US "superabundance": only power supplying guns + butter at scale. GNP +87%; Ford > Italy's total output by 1943; ship production 112 vs Japan 21 (1942), 206 vs 19 (1943). War Production Board coordinated.
- Lend-Lease: $10.7B USSR (food + trucks/rail critical); Stalin admitted dependence. Britain $30B (protected consumption). "vital."
- Soviet revival: collapse then "guns over butter"; coercive Gosplan + gulag labor; 1942-45 exceeded German major weapons output despite smaller base.
- German: pre-Speer inefficiency/confusion; Speer 1942+ rationalization + forced labor reached 1944 peak ("realization of... inefficiently mobilized war potential"). Efficiency gap stark vs Soviet/US.
- Home fronts: Soviet coercion + patriotism ("Great Patriotic War"); German continued coercion; US/UK moral commitment + fewer sacrifices.
- Midpoint 1942: Axis tide (Stalingrad/Caucasus, U-boats, early Pacific) but US industrial power + Pearl Harbor (Hitler hoped diverted US) + Churchill "we had won after all" (Allies "twice or thrice" Axis). Key: could UK/USSR hold until US fully mobilized? Roosevelt/Churchill grasped scale; enabled multi-theater (aid, Pacific, bombing, Overlord prep). "three turning points"; midpoint 281-90.
- Ties directly to operational leaders (prior ranges): production/logistics enabled (or failed to enable) their campaigns. Cross to war-economies-wwii and home-fronts-wwii processes. (Verbatim from Range 5; complements Murray.)
- **Conclusion at Midpoint**: Situation mid-1942, transition points (Rogers).

(Volume 1 covers up to ~mid-1942; Volume 2 covers 1942 turning points through 1945 victory, occupation, and assessment. Expand with Vol. 2 source.)

Overextension, radio-enabled centralization (killing subordinate independence / Auftragstaktik), material walls at culmination, and Soviet adaptation (retreat + new armies + consecutive operations) rendered classic encirclement no longer viable. Twin disasters (El Alamein + Stalingrad/Uranus) within weeks. ## Operational Synthesis: Murray & Millett, *A War to Be Won* (2000)
This gold-standard operational history (synthesis across theaters with emphasis on combined arms, logistics, command adaptation, and "why the Allies won") enriches the hub's narrative without duplicating West Point tactical analyses. Key claims (grounded verbatim in source; see full source page for ranges/quotes/locs):

- **Why Allies won (core assessment)**: Not moral inevitability alone ("moral righteousness alone does not win battles"); required fighting and winning on battlefield through superior resources, coalition coordination, adaptation, and combined-arms (air-ground-sea integration). Axis early skill (German decentralized C2/combined arms; Japanese initial naval/air edge) undone by overextension, poor grand strategy, and ideological rigidity (Nazi racial war rallied Soviets; Japanese occupation fueled resistance). Production shift decisive post-1942; US "arsenal of democracy" + Soviet manpower + British sea/air power + Allied learning curve (e.g., USAAF adaptation in bomber offensive; Pacific island-hopping refinement).
- **Logistics and coalition as decisive**: "Logistical superiority was crucial... America’s role as the 'arsenal of democracy' was the single most important contribution"; Mulberries, Red Ball Express, Lend-Lease trucks enabled exploitation where Axis (esp. German foraging/rail problems in USSR) failed. Coalition frictions real (Montgomery vs. Patton priorities; King Pacific focus delaying Atlantic fixes) but subordinated to victory; "unprecedented cooperation" per West Point cross-ref.
- **Campaign highlights (cross to existing analyses)**: Barbarossa (logistics collapse by Aug 1941, ideological "Vernichtungskrieg" stiffened resistance, Soviet mobilization + winter adaptation turned tide despite Stalin errors); Atlantic vs. Bomber Offensive trade-offs (U-boat "happy time" 1942 ended by convoy + escorts + air; Bomber Command indirect effects via flak diversion + V-weapon diversion; USAAF long-range fighter + oil/transport campaigns broke German air power/economy by 1944); Pacific (submarine commerce raiding + carrier battles + island-hopping strangled Japan; Iwo/Okinawa as air enablers for LeMay firebombing + atomic); Overlord/Bulge (logistics crisis post-Normandy; exploitation failures at Falaise/Market Garden allowed German escape; Bulge as last-gasp with fuel shortages vs. Allied air response once weather cleared); final phases (Soviet Bagration contrast to Allied caution; atomic + Soviet entry forcing surrender despite Japanese home-front collapse).
- **Home fronts and total war (ch19 + aftermath)**: Civilian deaths outnumbered combat (2:1+); bombing (Europe ~1.5-2M, Japan 240-300k dead + 8M+ homeless) + atrocities (Holocaust 6M Jews + 12M+ others; Japanese "don’t let them live, don’t let them die"; Soviet Gulag/mobilization) defined era. Women/labor shifts (Britain/US "total" mobilization; Germany slave labor + 6.5M Party; Soviet 11M+ mobilized + 400k women combat); postwar DPs (16M+), reconstruction (Marshall Plan), trials (Nuremberg/IMTFE) shaped Cold War order.
- **Assessment**: "The war was won by fighting"; adaptation key ("all military organizations get the next war wrong... more effective are those that recognize... and adapt"); friction/logistics/command often decisive over technology alone. Ties to Luttwak paradoxical logic (reversals in success) and Military Effectiveness levels (political/strategic primacy).
