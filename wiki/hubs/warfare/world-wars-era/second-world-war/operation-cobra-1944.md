Staff-college-level analysis of the American breakout operation that finally shattered the Normandy stalemate and transformed the campaign from attrition to pursuit.

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**US (Bradley):** Punch through the German defenses west of St. Lô using concentrated force and overwhelming air support, then rapidly exploit with armoured and mechanized forces to seize Avranches and open the door for a drive into Brittany and east behind the German main forces.

**German:** Contain the American sector with minimal forces while the panzer mass faced the British at Caen; prevent any breakout that would unhinge the entire front.

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

Bocage (hedgerow) country made initial penetration extremely costly and slow. The decision to use heavy bombers in close support was high-risk. Good intelligence on German weakness in the sector after attrition.

## 3. Order of Battle and Deployment

Concentrated US VII Corps assault with infantry to open corridors, armour to exploit. Massive preliminary air bombardment (with tragic friendly casualties on 24–25 July).

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Preparation and failed first attempt** (24 July): weather and short bombing.
2. **Main bombardment and infantry assault** (25 July): VII Corps fights through the crust.
3. **Armour exploitation and collapse** (26–27 July): German front disintegrates.
4. **Drive to Avranches and activation of Third Army**.

## 5. Outcome and Casualties

Complete operational success. The German left wing was shattered. Avranches fell; the gateway to the interior of France opened. German forces were left in an untenable salient.

## 6. Critical Decisions

Bradley's concentration and persistence with the air plan despite risks; Collins' handling of the exploitation; Patton's subsequent aggressive use of the corridor.

## 7. Lessons Learned — The Principles of War (FM 3-0)

(Full detail from D'Este and Citino to be integrated: Mass and Surprise achieved through air and concentration; Maneuver and Offensive restored after weeks of frustration; Economy of Force in the British sector enabling the American effort.)

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

Textbook example of set-piece breach followed by rapid exploitation in restricted terrain. Influential for later thinking on air-ground integration, "breakout" operations, and the transition from attrition to maneuver.

## 9. Historiography and Primary Sources

D'Este provides detailed treatment of the planning debates and the execution. Bradley's own account emphasizes the decision-making. German records show the sudden collapse once the crust was broken.
