# Battle of Leyte Gulf — Tactical Analysis

> **Narrative & full sources:** [[events/leyte-gulf-1944|Full event page]] (or [[events/world-war-ii-1939-1945]]) · **War hub:** [[hubs/warfare/world-wars-era/second-world-war/second-world-war|World War II — War Hub]] · **Warfare hub:** [[hubs/warfare/warfare-hub|Warfare & Strategy]] · **Source:** [[sources/rogers-seidule-west-point-history-world-war-ii-v2-2016|The West Point History of World War II, Volume 2 (2016)]] (Range 2 / Overy Ch. 2 + Love Ch. 5 sea/air material exclusively)

Staff-college-level analysis of the largest naval battle of WWII as the culminating sea/air power clash in the Pacific. Focus: Japanese complex "decisive battle" plan vs. US carrier/escort carrier flexibility, joint air-sea integration, desperate surface actions (Samar), and kamikaze debut. Grounded exclusively in range_2 text. Reciprocal links required to main event page, war hub, and [[strategic-bombing-campaign]] (air component enabling).

## 1. Strategic Context and Objectives

**Japan (Toyoda / Kurita et al.)** — Primary objective: destroy or cripple the US Pacific Fleet (esp. fast carriers) to prevent loss of the Philippines and the resulting cutoff of Borneo oil to the Combined Fleet and Home Islands. Last realistic chance for a "decisive battle" after failures at Midway and Philippine Sea. Complex multi-force plan to lure Halsey north with Ozawa's carriers while Center Force (Kurita) transits San Bernardino Strait to attack the Leyte invasion fleet / Taffy escort carriers. Secondary: use Southern Force through Surigao Strait for pincer.

**United States (Halsey / Kinkaid)** — Primary: protect the Leyte landings (MacArthur's return to Philippines), secure airfields, and destroy the Japanese fleet if it sortied. Halsey (Third Fleet) responsible for fleet engagement; Kinkaid (Seventh) for close support of amphibious force with old BBs, escort carriers (Taffys), and air. "Unity of command" issues arose due to separate chains (Nimitz vs MacArthur). Range: "Nimitz made it clear that Halsey’s primary mission was to meet the Japanese fleet should it seek an engagement."

*(Set inside broader Ch.2/5: Philippines recovery opens path for B-29s and cuts Japanese sea lines; ties directly to strategic bombing and submarine campaigns already strangling Japan.)*

## 2. Terrain, Weather, and Intelligence

- **Sea/terrain:** Complex archipelago waters (Sibuyan Sea, narrow straits like San Bernardino and Surigao); shoals, islands limited maneuver; Leyte Gulf landing beaches vulnerable to surface bombardment if fleet broke through.
- **Weather:** Squalls and rain provided critical cover for Taffy 3 carriers during Samar action ("carriers dodged shells under squalls and smokescreens").
- **Intelligence:** US radar on escort carriers and patrol planes provided early warning ("a patrol plane sighted Kurita’s formation... just seventeen miles"); Japanese exaggerated prior claims and poor recon; Spruance had forewarning in related Philippine Sea action. Submarines provided contact reports.

*Deployment notes (from range): Kurita's powerful Center Force approached Taffy 3 from west-northwest at ~17 miles; Taffy 3 slow escort CVEs turned south; screening DDs/DEs forward for torpedo runs/smoke. Japanese Southern Force met Oldendorf's battle line in Surigao.*

## 4. Course of the Battle — Phases

1. **Phase 1 — Approach and opening actions (23-24 Oct):** Japanese forces sortie per Toyoda plan. US carrier strikes (Halsey) and subs attrit Center Force (Musashi sunk etc. in Sibuyan Sea implied). Southern Force enters Surigao.

2. **Phase 2 — Surigao Strait (night 24-25 Oct):** Oldendorf's old battleships cross the T and destroy Nishimura/Shima force with radar-directed gunfire and torpedoes. Decisive night action.

3. **Phase 3 — Battle off Samar (morning 25 Oct):** Kurita Center Force emerges through San Bernardino unexpectedly (Halsey drawn north by Ozawa decoy). Taffy 3 (Sprague) surprised at close range ("just seventeen miles"). Carriers launch air strikes (mostly HE ordnance); desperate smoke screen + torpedo counterattacks by outclassed DDs/DEs (Samuel B. Roberts, Hoel, Johnston inflict damage on heavy cruisers incl. Kumano). "it did not appear that any of our ships could survive another five minutes of the heavy-caliber fire being received." Kurita, mistaking CVEs for fleet carriers and under pressure, retires at 9:15 a.m. without explanation.

4. **Phase 4 — Kamikaze and mopping / aftermath (25-26 Oct):** Special Attack Unit kamikazes hit Taffy groups (St. Lo sunk by crash; others damaged). 55 suicide planes account for 1 CVE +5 vessels sunk, 33 damaged. Japanese fleet remnants withdraw. Leyte landings proceed; Combined Fleet power projection ends.

**Key tactical mechanisms:** US use of radar, air spotting, smoke, and aggressive small-ship torpedo tactics vs superior Japanese surface firepower; Japanese over-complex plan and loss of nerve at decisive moment; integration of carrier air (even from "baby flattops") with surface defense.

| Captured / wounded | N/A | Significant on damaged ships; kamikaze toll |
| Source(s) | Range 2 (Love/Overy material): explicit ship losses and "costly victory" | Range 2: "The Battle off Samar had been a costly victory for the Americans..." |

- Strategic result: US achieved objective (protected landings); Japan failed "decisive battle" and lost sea control.
- Immediate follow-on: Secure Leyte; Japanese forces isolated; path cleared for Luzon, Iwo, Okinawa, and intensified strategic bombing + sub blockade.
- Longer-term: End of Japanese naval air/sea power as factor; accelerated collapse.

## 6. Critical Decisions — What Went Right and Wrong

- **Japan (Toyoda/Kurita):** Complex plan relied on perfect coordination and US error (Halsey took bait). Right: used carriers as decoy. Wrong: Kurita's retreat at cusp of victory ("a decision he never satisfactorily explained"); failure to press despite damage inflicted on Taffys; "Breast-beating about a 'decisive battle' masked a lack of moral courage."
- **US (Halsey/Sprague):** Right: aggressive pursuit of decoy drew main threat away temporarily; Sprague's immediate smoke + counterattack bought time ("fifteen minutes of cover"). Wrong: command split risked disaster (Halsey north while Kurita threatened beachhead); Taffy aircraft armed for ground support not anti-ship.
- Turning point: Kurita's 9:15 a.m. order to retire despite local superiority.

## 7. Lessons (Principles of War — FM 3-0)

All nine addressed with range grounding (honoured / violated):

- **Objective:** Japan violated (sought "decisive battle" that could not be won given material disparity). US honoured (protect landings + destroy fleet).
- **Offensive:** US (Sprague's DDs) honoured in desperate counter despite odds. Japan violated by retiring.
- **Mass:** Japan had local mass at Samar but failed to exploit. US used massed air + coordinated small ship attacks effectively.
- **Economy of Force:** US Taffy 3 (never intended for fleet action) forced to fight capital ships — violation of intended role.
- **Maneuver:** US smoke/squall + torpedo runs exemplify. Japanese plan relied on multiple axes but coordination failed.
- **Unity of Command:** Violated by separate Halsey/Kinkaid chains (noted as engendering "considerable controversy").
- **Security:** US surprised at Samar (Halsey drawn north); Japanese secrecy for approach.
- **Surprise:** Japan achieved initial (Kurita emergence); US radar/patrols turned it.
- **Simplicity:** Japan violated (overly complex Toyoda plan with multiple forces). US simpler (meet fleet, cover landings).

## 8. Modern Doctrine Parallels

- Carrier vs surface: Precursor to modern anti-access/area denial (A2/AD) and distributed maritime ops; escort carriers + air + small combatants defeating heavier surface threat mirrors today's integrated air/missile defense + surface action groups.
- Decoy operations and C2 friction: Ozawa decoy parallels modern deception; command split issues echo need for unified maritime command.
- Kamikaze / asymmetric: Early suicide tactics parallel modern swarm/drone threats; defensive CAP, radar pickets, and improved AA/firefighting (as on Franklin/Yorktown) relevant to counter-UAS and damage control.
- Logistics/sea control: Destruction of Japanese shipping (subs + this battle) underscores sea control as prerequisite for sustained ops; directly enables later strategic bombing (B-29s) and invasion prep.
- Principles application: Modern FM 3-0 / JP 3-0 still stress unity of command, mass at decisive point, and taking calculated risks (Sprague's "do what damage we could").
