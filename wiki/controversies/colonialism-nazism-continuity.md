# From Windhoek to Auschwitz? The Colonialism–Nazism Continuity Debate

Did Imperial Germany's colonial violence — above all the
[[herero-nama-genocide-1904|Herero and Nama genocide]] — stand in a
causal line of continuity to [[nazi-germany|Nazi Germany's]] war of
annihilation and [[the-holocaust|the Holocaust]]?

## Positions

### Strong continuity
Jürgen Zimmerer ("Annihilation in Africa," 2005) and Benjamin Madley
("From Africa to Auschwitz," 2005) argue the German war of annihilation
against the Herero and Nama — official extermination intent, desert
death drives, concentration camps, 65,000 of 80,000 Herero dead —
provided precedent and impulse for Nazi extermination policy.

### Skeptical (Evans's position, attributed)
Evans (2015, chs. 1 and 23) finds the strong claims "fail to convince
because there is no evidence of a direct connection." Personnel
continuity was minimal (the pre-1914 colonial administration was tiny;
Nazi-era administrative expertise came overwhelmingly from the domestic
civil service). The Nazis had many other models of racial
discrimination to draw on — the US (Native Americans "nationals" but
not citizens until 1924), British colonies, South Africa — and Hitler's
own imperial reference point was British India ("The Russian space is
our India"), not German Africa. Crucially, insofar as there is a
parallel it runs to Nazi policy toward the *Slavs* — a colonial war in
Eastern Europe — not toward the Jews, who were constructed as a global
enemy, not a colonial obstacle (see [[holocaust-uniqueness]]). Robert
Gerwarth ("Hannah Arendt's Ghosts," 2009) argues the case against
direct linkage.

### Middle positions
Mark Mazower (*Hitler's Empire*, 2008) — reviewed in ch. 23 — is "on
slightly safer ground" (Evans) in noting that German race laws in
colonies like Namibia provided a basis for the anti-Slav race
regulations of occupied Europe (e.g. sexual-relations bans on Polish
workers in the Reich), while overstating colonial administrative
continuity. Shelley Baranowski (*Nazi Empire*, 2011) treats Wilhelmine
imperial ambition and racial thinking as feeding Nazi imperialism
without determining the Holocaust. The differences Evans stresses on
Mazower's behalf: interwar empires held out eventual self-government
and built indigenous elites; the Nazi empire, applied inside Europe,
promised conquered peoples only extinction.

### Further evidence catalogued by Evans (chs. 1–2)

For continuity: the *Rassenschande* legal vocabulary originating in the
1905/1907 colonial intermarriage bans and resurfacing in the Nuremberg
Laws; the descent-based 1913 Citizenship Law drawing on colonial racial
doctrine; Herero identification tags; the Eugen Fischer pipeline (Rehoboth
craniometry → Kaiser Wilhelm Institute → training Mengele); Baranowski's
argument that the WWI eastern occupation (Brest-Litovsk: >1 million sq.
miles under "ruthless military dictatorship") was the true bridge between
overseas colonialism and Lebensraum. Against: Trotha's war "was an
exception in German colonial history" owing more to his own military and
racial doctrines; colonial genocide was a local commander's escalation
that Berlin and the governor opposed and domestic politics loudly
condemned, whereas Nazi genocide was centrally directed and met almost no
internal opposition; Nazi plans for a *restored* African empire in the
1930s envisaged conventional colonial development, not extermination —
Africans were never cast as a world-threat. Evans's preferred intermediate
cause is the First World War's brutalization of European politics, not
colonialism directly. His summary: colonial experience "infused the
ideology of National Socialism" but did not blueprint the Holocaust,
whose *Weltfeind* conception of the Jews "had no colonial precedent."
Named literature: Bley (1968) foundational; Conrad (2012) synthesis
("a model of its kind" — Evans); Baranowski (2010) "skilful and carefully
nuanced"; Zimmerer and Madley for; Gerwarth against.

## Related points of fact

Hitler's Lebensraum program was explicitly analogized by Hitler himself
to New-World settler colonialism (table-talk parallels between
Generalplan Ost and the fate of indigenous Australians and North
Americans) — the emic colonial framing is not in dispute; the causal
chain from Windhoek specifically is.
