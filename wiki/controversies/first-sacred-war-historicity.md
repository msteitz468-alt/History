# Historicity of the First Sacred War

Whether an early sixth-century **Sacred War** for control of Delphi occurred as later tradition claims. Event page: [[events/first-sacred-war-c590bce]]. Principal modern treatment for this wiki: [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] Ch.12.

## Positions

### Traditional historicist
Amphictyonic destruction of Cirrha, re-founding/reorganization of Pythian Games (582), participation of Solon or Alcmaeon and Cleisthenes of Sicyon; ten-year war 594–585 per Aristotle/Callisthenes.

### Minimal core
Something may underlie Pythian reorganization and amphictyonic control; personnel and stratagems (river poison, famous leaders) are unreliable embroidery.

### Hall’s source-critical agnosticism / developed-narrative rejection
Almost all literary evidence clusters in the fourth century; Herodotus is silent; divergences rule out a single early authoritative source; Aeschines has strong intentional motives. Any historical kernel is “so minute as to be practically insignificant.”

## Hall’s arguments in detail

**Temporal proximity fails.** “Virtually none of the literary evidence for the First Sacred War predates the fourth century, with much of it clustering in the 340s and 330s – precisely the period in which the Third and Fourth Sacred Wars were being fought.” Fullest sources include Aeschines, Aristotle/Callisthenes’ work on Pythian victors, and Antipatros. Only possible earlier allusions are interpolated *Hymn to Apollo* lines and the *Shield of Heracles* Cycnus episode.

**Consistency fails.** Sources diverge on Solon versus Alcmaeon as Athenian leader and on who poisoned the river. Chronological squeeze makes Solon, Alcmaeon, and Cleisthenes of Sicyon hard to place together in 594–585. The ten-year length may mirror the Third Sacred War; the name Eurylokhos recurs in Philip’s army.

**Intentionality.** Aeschines’ *Against Ctesiphon* instrumentalizes the war to convict Demosthenes of impiety regarding Amphissa: “Aeschines does not, then, mention the First Sacred War out of pure antiquarian interest. It is important for establishing a sacred law that Demosthenes is alleged to have violated.”

**Agnosticism, not pure denial.** Inventing a Sacred War from nothing was risky; famous names accreted to an earlier tradition explaining (1) the ban on cultivating sacred land and (2) amphictyonic (not local) administration of Delphi already before 480. Amphictyonic control likely involved multi-year hostilities against locals, but that “kernel” is practically insignificant relative to the full traditional war.

## Shared factual ground

Delphi became a major inter-polis sanctuary; an amphictyony administered it by the Classical period; Pythian Games were (re)organized in the early sixth century in traditional dating; later Sacred Wars are better attested. None of these secure points by themselves prove the full First Sacred War narrative.
