## The Question

How did the **Austronesian** languages — today spoken from Madagascar to Easter
Island — and the Neolithic material culture associated with them spread across
island Southeast Asia and the Pacific? Was it driven by a **migration of farming
populations** out of China/Taiwan carrying rice and pigs, or by **indigenous
islanders** with deep local roots adopting and transmitting culture through
maritime social networks? This is the Pacific counterpart of the
[[demic-vs-cultural-diffusion]] debate and is treated across CWH Vol. II Chs. 2,
3, and 16.

## Positions

**1. The "express-train" / farming-language dispersal (Peter Bellwood; Diamond &
Bellwood 2003).** Proto-Austronesian-speaking **Neolithic farmers** expanded from
**China → Taiwan → the Philippines → ISEA → the Pacific** (~3000–1500 BCE),
carrying a farming package (rice, pigs) and largely bypassing already-occupied New
Guinea. The signature case of the "farmers and their languages" hypothesis,
supported by the distribution of Austronesian languages and Neolithic material
culture (and, in the Remote Pacific, very low genetic diversity from recent
founder effects — the mtDNA "Polynesian motif").

**2. Deep-island ancestry + social networks (genetics-led critique).** Detailed
mtDNA, Y-chromosome, and autosomal work shows the express-train "is not holding
up": most Pacific lineages (including those ancestral to the Polynesian motif)
have **deep insular ancestry**, shaped by **post-glacial sea-level rise**, with
**eastern Indonesia / Near Oceania (the New Guinea region)** as a major proximal
source — not Taiwan. Taiwan may even be a *recipient* of southern lineages.
Domestic **pigs did not travel with rice** across ISEA (and some island pigs were
domesticated separately). The spread of Austronesian languages and Neolithic
industries may owe more to **social networks and spheres of interaction** than to
mass farmer migration.

**3. Intermediate / entanglement (Barton).** Rice was **adopted into indigenous
vegecultural systems** rather than arriving as a farming "package"; pre-Austronesian
landscape management (clearance/burning in Sarawak by ~6,500 years ago; Kuk swamp
cultivation from ~10,000 years ago) shows the islands were not a blank slate.
Barton urges abandoning the rigid pottery/Neolithic/agriculture categories
altogether and studying the "diversity for what it is."

## Resolution Status

**Open.** Linguistics and some archaeology still support a real Taiwan-origin
dispersal of Austronesian *languages*; genetics shows the *people* are mostly of
deep island descent and the *domesticates* spread incompletely and unevenly. The
reconciling view — language and some material culture spreading via voyaging
networks **without** wholesale population replacement or a tight crop package — is
gaining ground but the demographic weight of the Taiwan expansion remains
contested.
