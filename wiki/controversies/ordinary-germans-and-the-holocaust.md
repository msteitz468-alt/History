# Ordinary Germans and the Holocaust

What did ordinary Germans know of the genocide, and did they approve, participate, or merely fail to resist? Distinct from the intentionalist/functionalist decision debate (see [[wannsee-conference-1942]]).

## Positions

- **"Willing executioners" (Goldhagen, 1996):** a longstanding German "eliminationist antisemitism" made ordinary Germans willing perpetrators. Widely criticized methodologically but commercially and publicly influential.
- **"Ordinary men" (Browning, 1992):** Reserve Police Battalion 101 shows situational pressures (conformity, obedience, brutalization), not ideology, turned ordinary middle-aged men into killers. **Evans (2008) pushes back for the eastern killing units specifically**: the Order Police battalions attached to the Einsatzgruppen were screened, volunteer, ideologically trained formations — "neither 'ordinary men' nor 'ordinary Germans.'"
- **Knowledge without approval (Evans, Kershaw, Bankier):** knowledge of mass murder was widespread from 1941–42 (soldiers on leave, officials, railway workers; Klemperer recorded Auschwitz as "the most dreadful concentration camp" by March 1942), and the postwar claim of ignorance fails. But the evidence — muted SD reporting, widely negative reactions to the yellow star and deportations, and above all the pervasive 1943–44 reading of Allied bombing as retribution for the Jews ("Have we not murdered thousands of Jews? . . . By doing all this we have shown the enemy what they can do to us if they win" — SD Stuttgart, November 1944) — points to private disapproval plus guilt plus inaction, not enthusiasm. Goebbels's antisemitic campaigns failed both to generate approval (1941–42) and to convert guilt into resolve (1943–44).
- **The measurement problem:** the clandestine SPD reports' "outer shell of loyalty" — terror made real opinion unrecoverable; all positions must argue through this filter.
