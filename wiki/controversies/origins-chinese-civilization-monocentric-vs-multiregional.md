# Origins of Chinese Civilisation: Central-Plains Core vs. Multi-Regional Development

Did Chinese civilisation radiate from a **single nuclear area** — the
[[central-plains-china|Central Plains]] (*Zhongyuan*) — or emerge from **many regional
traditions** developing in parallel and interacting? This long-running interpretive dispute
structures how the Chinese Neolithic is read, and [[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu (2004)]]
treats the shift between the two models as a defining episode in the discipline's history.

## Positions

**1. Monocentric / Central-Plains nuclear model.** The traditional view, reinforced by
**Chang's (1959) "Longshanoid horizon"**, held that cultural similarities across eastern China
reflected **expansion from a single nuclear area, the Central Plains** — "the cradle of
Chinese civilisation." It fit both the intellectual tradition and the early data, which showed
a complete Neolithic sequence in the Central Plains but not elsewhere.

**2. Multi-regional development (*quxi leixing*).** As discoveries accumulated in peripheral
regions, **Su Bingqi's** [[quxi-leixing|*quxi leixing*]] framework recast Chinese prehistory
as **six independently developing regional systems** interacting over time. Even Chang (1986)
revised the "Longshanoid horizon" into a neutral "**spatial integrating device crosscutting a
number of regional sequences**" rather than a single radiating source.

**3. Yan Wenming's flower-and-petals compromise.** A middle position: development was
multi-regional, but the **Central Plains played the leading role** (the flower) with
surrounding cultures as petals — preserving Central-Plains primacy without monocentrism.

## What Drove the Shift

Liu stresses the move from a *Zhongyuan*-centered to a multi-centered view was **not mere
political propaganda**: it crystallised over ~20 years through (a) voluminous new finds outside
the Central Plains, (b) recognition of distinct regional traditions, (c) growing confidence in
textual records, and (d) a changing, **multi-ethnic** concept of nationalism after 1950 (see
[[nationalism-and-archaeology]]). Liu's own settlement and mortuary data support the
multi-regional reading: Neolithic China had **many regional centers and no single artistic
horizon** (unlike the Olmec/Chavín), with distinct trajectories in the
[[dawenkou-culture|Dawenkou]]/Shandong east, the [[liangzhu-culture|Liangzhu]] southeast, and
the [[central-plains-china|Central Plains]].

## Resolution Status

**Partially resolved.** The strong monocentric model is **abandoned**; multi-regional
development is the consensus. A residual debate continues over **how much primacy** the Central
Plains deserves — it is, after all, where the first state ([[erlitou-culture|Erlitou]])
emerged, which keeps Yan's flower-and-petals position alive. The related question of when the
**first state** appears, and whether it equals the Xia, is filed separately at
[[xia-dynasty-historicity]].
