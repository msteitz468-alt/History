# Was the First Intermediate Period a "Dark Age"?

A dispute over how to characterize the
[[processes/first-intermediate-period|First Intermediate Period]] (c.2160–2055 BCE) — the
interval of political fragmentation between the [[actors/old-kingdom-egypt|Old]] and
[[actors/middle-kingdom-egypt|Middle Kingdoms]]. It is an **interpretive** dispute about the
meaning of the evidence, not a factual disagreement about events.

## Position A — the traditional "dark age"

The First Intermediate Period was "a period of chaos, decline, misery, and social and
political dissolution: a 'dark age' separating two epochs of glory and power." The evidence
cited is the collapse of monumental royal building after Pepy II and the lurid disorder of
the Middle Kingdom **"pessimistic literature"** — the *Admonitions of an Egyptian Sage*, the
*Prophecy of Neferti*, the *Complaints of Khakheperraseneb*, and the *Dialogue between a Man
Tired of Life and his ba* — in which social order is overturned and the land overrun by
foreigners. This reading long dominated handbooks and still shapes popular accounts.

## Position B — Seidlmayer's revisionism

Stephan Seidlmayer (in the [[sources/shaw-oxford-history-ancient-egypt-2000|Oxford History]])
argues the dark-age picture "largely reproduces — sometimes with surprising naivety — the
literary theme developed in a group of Middle Kingdom literary texts." Those texts are
**not eyewitness accounts**: "they do not actually claim to be set in the First Intermediate
Period; nor do they mention any historical particulars," and they functioned as **royal
propaganda** — "the black background against which the tight politics of law and order
implemented by Middle Kingdom kings can be justified."

The contemporary record points the other way. Texts of the period itself (the biographical
inscriptions, the tomb of **Ankhtifi**) show "vigour, self-confidence, and pride in one's
own achievement … crisis brilliantly overcome." Archaeology shows **provincial vitality**:
more and larger ordinary tombs, gold and stone vessels reaching modest households, rapid
**material-culture innovation** (the potter's-wheel bag-jars, wooden offering figures,
cartonnage masks, the scarab seal), and the spread of once-royal funerary culture (the origin
of the Coffin Texts; see [[processes/democratization-of-afterlife]]). On the famine claim,
"independent evidence confirming climatic change during the First Intermediate Period is
lacking," and Elephantine flood data suggest slightly **above-average** Niles. The period was
"not … an outright collapse of Egyptian society and culture as a whole" but "an important,
though temporary, shift in its centres of activity and dynamism."

## Resolution status

**Partially resolved** in favour of Position B among specialists: the revisionist reading is
now mainstream in Egyptology, while the older "dark age" framing persists in popular and
introductory accounts. The debate connects to the broader argument that "intermediate
periods" partly reflect the **patchier survival of records**, not simply collapse (see
[[concepts/egyptian-dynastic-chronology]]).
