## The Dispute

Where did the Afroasiatic language family — which includes Semitic, ancient
Egyptian, Berber, Cushitic, Chadic, and Omotic — originate? The family is unusual
in spanning both Africa and the Near East, and its homeland bears directly on the
history of early food production, the peopling of Egypt and the Levant, and the
origins of Semitic-speaking peoples (including the linguistic ancestors of the
ancient Hebrews and Arabs).

The dispute matters because the two positions imply opposite directions of
movement and very different stories about the relationship between language spread
and the spread of agriculture.

## Positions

### Position 1: African (Horn of Africa) Origin — Ehret
Christopher Ehret (CWH Vol. I, Ch. 15; *Reconstructing Proto-Afroasiatic*, 1995)
argues the homeland was unequivocally in **Africa, specifically the Horn of Africa
(Ethiopian highlands)**:
- **Greatest internal diversity is in the Horn**: the first and deepest divergence
  in the family separated the Omotic branch (entirely restricted to the southern
  Ethiopian highlands) from the Erythraic ("Red Sea") branch containing all other
  languages. The second divergence (into Cushitic and North Erythraic) also played
  out in the Horn. Linguistic principle: the homeland is normally where the deepest
  branches cluster.
- Only at the *third* stage did North Erythraic speakers (ancestors of Berber,
  Egyptian, Semitic, Chadic) spread northward out of the Horn.
- **The proto-Afrasians were wild-grain collectors, not farmers or herders** — the
  cultivation and herding lexicons developed only later and *separately* in each
  sub-branch. This decouples the family's spread from the diffusion of Near Eastern
  agriculture.
- **Genetic corroboration**: Y-chromosome evidence places movement of people from
  the Horn into Egypt and thence the Levant in the relevant time frame
  (~15,000 BCE onward); a minority intrusive population that intermarried with
  existing populations.
- Semitic is the *only* branch found outside Africa today; Ehret argues it was
  carried into the Levant by Mushabian communities out of Africa — no later
  population movement could account for Semitic in Asia, and earlier out-of-Africa
  movements were far too early.

### Position 2: Near Eastern / Levantine Origin
A competing tradition (associated with Alexandr Militarev, and earlier with some
readings of Igor Diakonoff) places the homeland in the Near East, often linking
the family's spread to the diffusion of agriculture from Southwest Asia
(the "farming/language dispersal hypothesis," Bellwood & Renfrew). On this view:
- The proto-Afroasiatics were farmers, and the family spread with agriculture.
- Militarev reconstructs a proto-Afrasian "farming lexicon" — but Ehret counters
  that none of these terms are actually reconstructable to a shared set of food-
  production roots; they are later, independent developments in each sub-branch.

A separate but related sub-dispute: the homeland of **proto-Semitic** specifically.
Some (Hetzron) locate it in the Levant; one view brings it from the Horn. Ehret
notes a subgroup of closely related Semitic languages is spoken in the Horn today,
but attributes this to an intrusive first-millennium-BCE settlement from South
Arabia, not an indigenous Horn origin of Semitic.

### Historiographical note (CAH 1970)

[[edwards-cah-v1p1-1970|CAH Vol. I, Part 1]] (Ch. IV, Albright & Lambdin) treats the
family under the older name **"Afro-Asian (Hamito-Semitic)"** and reflects a
mid-century Near-Eastern/philological orientation built outward from the
well-attested Semitic and Egyptian branches, before the Africanist (Ehret,
Diakonoff) reconstruction shifted weight toward a Horn-of-Africa homeland on the
strength of the deep Omotic/Cushitic branching. The terminological shift itself —
from "Hamito-Semitic" to "Afroasiatic/Afrasian" — tracks this realignment. The CAH
chapter is useful here as a marker of the earlier consensus rather than as support
for either modern position.

## State of the Evidence

| Evidence | Favors African origin | Favors Near Eastern origin |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution of deepest branches (Omotic, Cushitic in Horn) | Strong | Weak |
| Reconstructed subsistence lexicon | Wild-grain collecting (African) | Disputed "farming lexicon" (Militarev) |
| Y-chromosome / genetic movement | Horn → Egypt → Levant | — |
| Semitic as sole Asian branch | Late carriage out of Africa | Indigenous Asian origin of Semitic possible |

## Resolution Status

Partially resolved in favor of the African (Horn of Africa) homeland, which is the
majority position among historical linguists today, especially for the family as a
whole. The Near Eastern hypothesis retains some support, particularly when tied to
the agriculture-dispersal model, and the specific homeland of proto-Semitic remains
more genuinely open. CWH Vol. I gives the African-origin position presumptive
weight (Cambridge reference series; see CLAUDE.md source-handling).
