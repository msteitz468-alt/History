# Irish Historiography: the Revisionism Debate

The defining methodological controversy of modern Irish history, and the frame for
[[jackson-oxford-modern-irish-history-2014|this Handbook]]. It has two entangled axes: **how to narrate
the national past**, and **whether Ireland was a colony or a metropole**.

## Positions

- **The nationalist / traditional narrative:** Irish history as a long, heroic struggle for liberation
  from English/British oppression — the [[great-famine-ireland-1845|Famine]] as quasi-genocide, the
  [[easter-rising-1916|Rising]] and revolution as the nation's fulfilment.
- **The "revisionist" critique** (from the 1930s, intensifying from the 1970s amid [[the-troubles|the Troubles]]):
  a self-consciously dispassionate, archive-based history that questioned the nationalist teleology,
  stressed complexity and contingency, downplayed colonial victimhood, and was in turn accused of
  draining Irish history of moral seriousness and of implicitly serving an anti-republican politics.
- **Post-revisionism & the post-colonial turn:** a synthesis that re-admits the colonial dimension and
  the reality of suffering while keeping revisionism's evidentiary rigour — reframing Ireland's status
  (colony? metropole? both?) as itself the productive question (the "colonized and colonizers" theme).

## Status

**Open.** The revisionism controversy reshaped the entire discipline and remains the lens through which
Irish historians position themselves; the colony-vs-metropole question is now treated as genuinely
two-sided rather than settled.
