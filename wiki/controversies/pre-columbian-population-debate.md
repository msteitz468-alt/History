# Pre-Columbian Population Debate (North America focus)

Companion controversy page to the hemispheric overview at [[pre-columbian-american-population]]. This page tracks **North America–specific** estimate bands, pandemic-horizon disputes, and the CHNPA I.1 synthesis (Trigger & Swagerty ch. 6; Salisbury ch. 7), without selecting a single “correct” population figure.

## Why a separate page

Hemispheric debates (Berkeley School, Hispaniola case studies, Andes/Mesoamerica) do not automatically settle **north of Mexico** densities or the **geographic reach** of sixteenth-century epidemics. High-count methods often export Central Mexican depopulation ratios; low-count traditions under-weight complex agricultural societies; archaeology and ethnohistory increasingly force **regional** answers.

## Positions (strongest form each)

### 1. High-count / early-pandemic model

Henry Dobyns and related work: Americas total **90–200 million**; north of the Rio Grande (in frames that can include Caribbean) **10–18 million**. Assumes catastrophic virgin-soil die-off, often ahead of direct observation, across most of the continent. Stakes: magnifies complexity of precontact societies and scale of loss. Critics (Henige *Numbers from Nowhere*, and others) charge unfalsifiable multipliers.

### 2. Low-count anthropological tradition

James Mooney **1,152,950** and Alfred Kroeber **900,000** north of the Rio Grande — early twentieth-century baselines Trigger & Swagerty say scholars have **abandoned as too low**, yet the numbers remain historiographic anchors. Kroeber’s hemispheric **~8.4 million** (Mann’s framing) sits in the same tradition.

### 3. Measured middle / handbook consensus band

Douglas Ubelaker **1.89–2.17 million** (excl. Caribbean); Smithsonian *Handbook of North American Indians* **1.21–2.64 million** ~1500; Russell Thornton **seven million or more** north of Mexico. Salisbury (CHNPA I.1 ch. 7) pairs Thornton’s **~5+ million → 600,000** (lower-48, 1500→1800) with Ubelaker’s **1,894,350 → 1,051,688** (all North America) as a drastic decline under either figure.

### 4. Regionally variable pandemic horizon (Trigger & Swagerty synthesis)

- **Southeast and Texas:** major trauma **firm** from Ayllón, Narváez, [[de-soto-expedition-1539]] documents and archaeology (Cofitachequi uninhabited towns; Nodena/Toqua decline; Smith on chiefdom unraveling).
- **Beyond SE/TX (California, Southwest, northern Plains, Northeast as in Dobyns):** **plausible but unconfirmed**.
- **Lower Great Lakes:** reason to believe **no major European outbreaks before 1633** (Snow on Mohawk; Warrick on Huron-Petun stability to 1630s); implies lower precontact densities than Dobyns and limited transmission where populations were thinner.
- **Mortality where epidemics struck:** initial virgin-soil rates calculated **30–75%**; at least 17 major New World epidemics recorded 1520–1600.
- **Micmac band:** Snow preepidemic ~**12,000** vs Miller **50,000** — both retained.

### Shared factual ground (framed per side)

All serious positions accept: (a) post-1492 demographic decline in many regions was severe; (b) Eurasian crowd diseases were major killers where they hit; (c) documentary sources are sparse, late, and biased; (d) method (multipliers, carrying capacity, settlement archaeology) drives outcomes more than new “smoking gun” documents. They disagree on precontact totals, how early and how far pandemics ran, and the weight of warfare, labor regimes, and nutrition beside pathogens.

## Linked questions

- Did [[coronado-expedition-1540|Coronado]] see a disease-reconfigured Pueblo world (Upham) or primarily an Athapaskan-pressure landscape?
- How much of Mississippian chiefdom collapse is contact disease ([[virgin-soil-epidemics]]) vs pre-contact Vacant Quarter dynamics ([[mississippian-culture]])?
- Can localized events (Cartier’s ~10% Stadacona winter mortality; Karankawa half-loss) be scaled to continental models?

## Status

**Open.** No consensus on a single North American total; credible published bands still span roughly **~1–18 million** north of Mexico depending on author and inclusion rules. CHNPA I.1’s editorial stance (via Trigger & Swagerty) is that the sixteenth century was a major period of change, that SE/TX trauma is secure, and that Dobyns-style continent-wide early pandemics remain unproven — without crowning a middle figure as fact.
