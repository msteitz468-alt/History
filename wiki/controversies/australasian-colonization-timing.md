## The Dispute

When did modern humans first arrive in Sahul (the Pleistocene continent of
Australia + New Guinea + Tasmania)? The answer matters not just for Australian
prehistory but for the entire Out of Africa 2 chronology: an early arrival
in Sahul implies a much earlier dispersal from Africa than the dominant
genetic model suggests.

## Positions

### Position 1: Late Dispersal (~45,000–50,000 BP)
The dominant model through the late 20th century:
- *Homo sapiens* left Africa ~60,000 BP as a single main dispersal
- Earlier populations in the Levant (~125,000 BP) and possibly elsewhere
  were false starts that left no descendants
- Rapid coastal migration from Africa to Southeast Asia enabled Sahul
  colonization ~45,000–50,000 BP
- The Lake Mungo remains (~43,000 BP) and other firm dates fit this window
- Earlier thermoluminescence (TL) dates for Australian sites are measurement
  errors or contaminated samples

### Position 2: Early Dispersal (~65,000–120,000 BP)
Peter Hiscock (Chapter 18 of CWH Vol. 1) and others argue for earlier dates:
- Genetic research suggests Australian populations diverged from African
  populations as early as ~120,000 BP — consistent with slower mutation
  rates than often assumed
- TL dating at Arnhem Land rock shelters suggests ~67,000 BP (published 2012;
  updated to ~65,000 BP in 2017 Madjedbebe excavation)
- Denisovan DNA evidence: Aboriginal Australians and Papuans carry more
  Denisovan DNA than any other modern population; the absence of Denisovan
  genes in East Asian populations suggests Australia was colonized by an
  earlier wave that mixed with Denisovans in Central/South Asia before
  reaching Sahul, while a later wave colonized East Asia
- This model implies that instead of racing from Africa via the Near East
  to Sahul, the founding populations of Australia may have dispersed
  gradually across the Old World for tens of thousands of years before
  reaching Sahul

## Key Evidence

| Evidence | Supports late | Supports early |
|---|---|---|
| Lake Mungo skeletal remains (~43,000 BP) | Yes (consistent with late model) | Yes (minimum date, not maximum) |
| Arnhem Land TL dates (65,000–67,000 BP) | Disputed (possible measurement error) | Yes (if TL dates reliable) |
| Genetic divergence from Africa | Consistent with ~60,000 BP | Consistent with ~120,000 BP if mutation rates slower |
| Denisovan introgression | Unexplained in simple late model | Explained by early wave dispersal |
| Mitochondrial DNA (Lake Mungo) | Single founding wave, no major subsequent colonization | — |

## Resolution Status

Open. The 2017 Madjedbebe excavation paper (Clarkson et al., *Nature*) arguing
for ~65,000 BP remains contested. Some archaeologists accept the early dates;
others argue for contamination or TL calibration issues. The Denisovan evidence
adds a new layer of complexity that neither simple model fully explains.

The controversy is structurally linked to [[homo-sapiens-emergence-date]]:
if behavioral modernity emerged ~70,000 BP (late emergence model), then
Australia ~65,000 BP would require near-immediate dispersal from Africa —
very fast but theoretically possible. If behavioral modernity emerged
later (~50,000 BP), then Sahul ~65,000 BP would imply pre-modern humans
colonized Sahul — which conflicts with other evidence.

## Implications

The timing of Sahul colonization is one of the key benchmarks for the entire
Out of Africa 2 chronology. Resolution of this controversy would substantially
constrain models of the dispersal's pace, route, and the cognitive status of
the colonizing populations. See [[colonization-australasia-50kbp]] and
[[out-of-africa-2-60kbp]].

This dispute is **structurally parallel** to the
[[clovis-first-vs-pre-clovis|Americas colonization debate]] at the opposite end
of the global dispersal: both pit an "early/coastal" model against a
"late/interior" model, both turn on contested early dates and largely submerged
coastal evidence, and both are entangled with a megafaunal-extinction debate
(see [[pleistocene-megafaunal-extinction]]).
