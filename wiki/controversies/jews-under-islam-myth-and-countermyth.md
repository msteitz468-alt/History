# Jews under Islam: The Myth of the Interfaith Utopia and Its Countermyth

How should the historical experience of Jews under medieval Islam be
characterized — and measured against their experience under Latin Christendom?
The dispute is as much about the **politics of historiography** as about the
medieval evidence, because both dominant positions were shaped by modern
conflicts (Jewish emancipation; Zionism and the Arab–Israeli conflict).

Nearly all serious scholars agree on one baseline **factual** point: during the
"classical centuries" (through roughly the thirteenth century), Jews in the
Arab-Islamic world experienced **less violence, expulsion, and persecution** than
Jews in medieval northern Christendom. The controversy is over how to frame,
weight, and explain that fact.

## Position 1 — The Myth of the Interfaith Utopia ("Golden Age")

**Claim:** Medieval Islam, above all in al-Andalus, offered Jews a tolerant
haven and a golden age of integration and cultural achievement, in stark contrast
to Christian persecution.

**Origins and holders:** Nineteenth-century founders of *Wissenschaft des
Judentums* — Heinrich Graetz above all — who took a Lutheran Hebraist's phrase
for a *literary* golden age (940–1040) and applied it to the whole political and
social order of Muslim Spain. Later echoed by Rudolf Kayser, Eliyahu Ashtor, and
others. In the twentieth century **adopted by Arab anti-Zionist writing** (George
Antonius; Al-Azhar conference papers; Pan-Arab polemicists), which held that
Jewish–Muslim harmony reigned for centuries until Zionism destroyed it — implying
that removing the "Zionist threat" would restore the utopia.

**Function:** In its Jewish form, a lever to demand emancipation in "liberal"
Europe; in its Arab form, an argument against Israel.

## Position 2 — The Neo-Lachrymose Countermyth

**Claim:** Islam persecuted Jews innately and continuously from Muhammad onward;
the "Golden Age" was largely a fiction; the fate of Jews under Islam was at times
as doleful as under Christendom.

**Origins and holders:** Emerged after the 1967 Six-Day War, first among
non-specialist popularizers (Saul S. Friedman, Rose Lewis, Martin Gilbert; most
prominently **Bat Ye'or**, *The Dhimmi: Jews and Christians under Islam*) and in
oblique scholarly forms. Frequently invokes [[maimonides|Maimonides']] "Epistle
to Yemen" ("no nation has done more harm to Israel") as a prooftext.

**Function:** A dialectical reaction to (a) rampant modern Arab antisemitism and
(b) the Arab propagandistic use of the utopia myth; also enlisted in Israeli
domestic politics (e.g., WOJAC's "exchange of populations" argument).

## Position 3 — Structural Comparativism (Cohen)

**Claim:** Both myth and countermyth distort the past. The correct task is
neither to celebrate tolerance nor to catalogue persecution but to **explain
structurally** why outcomes differed. [[cohen-under-crescent-cross-1994|Mark
Cohen]] (building on Claude Cahen, S. D. Goitein, Bernard Lewis, H. H.
Ben-Sasson) argues that the difference is real but contingent on a *combination*
of structural factors — religious (non-theological vs. theological hostility),
legal (the [[dhimmi|dhimma]] as one settled status among several tolerated
minorities), economic (Jews diversified vs. isolated into the pariah moneylending
niche), and social (pluralistic hierarchy, "essential equality among inferiors").
"Tolerance," on this view, is an anachronistic yardstick: no premodern
monotheistic society in power treated toleration as a virtue.

## Shared Factual Ground, Framed per Side

- **Fact:** Jews fared better under classical Islam than under northern
  Christendom. *Utopians* read this as harmony; *neo-lachrymose* writers minimize
  it as relative and unstable; *Cohen* explains it structurally.
- **Fact:** Real persecutions occurred under Islam (Almohad forced conversions
  of the 1140s; the Granada massacre of 1066; sporadic pogroms). *Utopians*
  downplay these as "passing"; *neo-lachrymose* writers universalize them;
  *Cohen* treats them as real but exceptional against the classical baseline.
- **Fact:** After ~1200 conditions worsened for Jews in *both* civilizations.
