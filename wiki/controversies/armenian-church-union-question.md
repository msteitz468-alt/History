# The Armenian Church Union Question

Throughout the life of the **[[cilician-armenian-kingdom|Cilician Armenian Kingdom]]**, the
papacy made **union of the non-Chalcedonian Armenian Church with Rome** the price of
military aid and of royal legitimacy — beginning with the [[coronation-of-levon-i-1199|Act of
Union of 1198]] that preceded King Levon I's coronation. Union was repeatedly promised,
partially enacted, and never durably consummated. The controversy concerns **what these
union efforts meant and what they did**.

## The Doctrinal Background

The Armenian Church rejected the **[[council-of-chalcedon-451|Council of Chalcedon (451)]]**
and its "two natures in one person" definition, holding instead to a **Miaphysite**
Christology (one united nature of the Word Incarnate, in the Cyrillian tradition). The
Armenian delegates were absent from Chalcedon — fighting the Battle of **Avarayr (451)**
against Sassanian Persia — and the church formally rejected the Chalcedonian creed at the
**Synods of Dvin (506, 551)**. This separated the Armenian Church from both Byzantine
Orthodoxy and Rome and made the Church the guardian of Armenian national identity. (The
brief 12th-century **conciliation** achieved by **Nerses Shnorhali** with Emperor Manuel I —
clarifying that Armenian "one nature" meant a *united* nature, not Eutychian monophysitism —
showed the gap was bridgeable in principle but never closed in practice.)

## Positions

**1. Union as sincere rapprochement (the Roman/unionist view).** Latin and pro-Rome
Armenian clergy (e.g. the later **Unitor** movement, Dominican-influenced) treated union as
a genuine return to catholic communion; the [[council-of-florence-1439|Council of Florence
(1439)]] proclaimed the Armenian Church reunited with Rome "after 900 years."

**2. Union as political tactic.** [[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian]]
argues — following the Armenian Patriarch-historian Maghakia Ormanian — that Armenian union
offers were **devoid of real unionist intent**: a recurring instrument for "enlisting the
help of Rome for political survival." On this reading Levon I's commitment was "a political
decision accompanied with very little, if any, religious considerations," and the union
decreed at Florence "was ratified neither by the Armenian nor the Greek Church."

**3. The union question as a decisive internal cause of the fall.** Ghazarian's central
thesis: papal insistence on union, and Lusignan enforcement of Roman rites, split Armenian
society irreconcilably into **"unionists" and "traditionalists,"** producing royal murders,
ecclesiastical paralysis, and a loss of social cohesion that — alongside Mamluk pressure —
brought down the kingdom and drove the [[fall-of-cilician-armenia-1375|exodus]].

**4. The secular-primacy counter-position.** The kingdom fell chiefly to **[[mamluk-sultanate|Mamluk]]**
military power and the broader rise of Turkic Islamic states; the religious split, while
real, is given disproportionate causal weight in a partisan, church-centred narrative.
Ghazarian himself concedes "no overwhelming single reason" for the demise even as he
foregrounds the ecclesiastical one.

## Assessment Status

**Open.** The factual chronology (the union acts of 1198, the professions of 1288 and 1346,
Florence in 1439) is secure; the **interpretation** — sincerity of the offers, and the
causal weight of the union question in the kingdom's fall — is contested and, in the one
dedicated synthesis available here, presented from an explicitly **Armenian-Apostolic,
Miaphysite-sympathetic** standpoint. This page records the positions without adjudicating.
