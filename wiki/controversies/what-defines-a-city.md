# What Defines a City?

## The dispute

There is no scholarly consensus on what makes a settlement a *city* rather than
a large village or town — a problem that directly conditions claims about where
and when the first cities arose. The dispute is **interpretive** (what concept
should organize comparison), not merely factual. It is foregrounded in
[[yoffee-cwh-v3-2015|CWH Vol. III]]'s introduction and runs through every case
study. See also [[urbanism]] and [[urban-revolution]].

## Positions

**1. Trait-list / demographic (Childe, Wirth).**
A city is identifiable by a checklist of archaeologically visible features —
Childe's ten criteria, or Wirth's "large, dense, permanent, heterogeneous."
*Strength:* operational and comparable. *Critique:* essentializing; it imposes
the Mesopotamian template as the norm and can deny anomalous cities their status
(the "first city" problem — Emberling).

**2. Functional / relational (Weber, central-place theory, Simmel).**
A city is defined not by size but by what it *does* — administration, exchange,
redistribution, defense, ceremony — in a structured relation to a hinterland,
and by the new *kind of social life* (heterogeneity, anonymity, citizenship) it
sustains. *Strength:* captures cities of unusual form. *Critique:* harder to
operationalize archaeologically.

**3. Heterarchical / anti-essentialist (R. McIntosh, Yoffee).**
Reject any universal threshold; some settlements are large, dense, complex, and
fully urban yet **lack centralized hierarchy and kingship** — e.g.
[[places/jenne-jeno|Jenne-jeno]] and its clustered Middle Niger settlements.
A city is a historically specific solution, best approached comparatively. See
[[heterarchy]] and [[hierarchy-vs-heterarchy-early-cities]]. *Strength:* fits the
global diversity the volume documents. *Critique:* risks dissolving the category
entirely, making comparison incoherent.

## Why it matters

The definition chosen determines the answers to "what was the first city?",
"is X (Çatalhöyük, Jenne-jeno, Cahokia) really a city?", and whether
[[urbanization]] is one process or many. Yoffee's own move is to keep the
category but treat it comparatively, adding the experiential criterion that
"cities must be awesome."

## Resolution status

**Open.** The volume deliberately does not adjudicate; it treats the absence of a
fixed definition as the precondition for comparative research rather than a
failure to be resolved.
