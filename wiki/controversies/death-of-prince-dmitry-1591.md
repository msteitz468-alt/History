# Death of Prince Dmitry of Uglich (1591)

## Positions

### Position 1: Assassination Ordered by Boris Godunov
This position argues that the nine-and-a-half-year-old Prince Dmitry (younger half-brother of Tsar Theodore and the last remaining natural Rurikid heir) was murdered in Uglich on May 15, 1591, on the direct orders of the court favorite Boris Godunov, who sought to clear his own path to the Russian throne (L2170-2174).

### Position 2: Accidental Self-Inflicted Death
This position argues that Prince Dmitry died from an accidental self-inflicted knife wound during an epileptic fit, and that there was no assassination plot or involvement by Boris Godunov (L2174-2176).

## Supporting Arguments and Scholars

### Arguments for Assassination (Godunov Guilty)
* **Popular Rumors and Drama:** Immediately following Dmitry's death, the populace of Uglich rioted, accusing the child's guardians of murder and killing them (L2172-2174). 
* **Early Historiography:** This position was adopted by early 19th-century historian Nikolay Karamzin, who argued that Godunov's ambition led him to murder. This version was popularized in Russian culture through Alexander Pushkin's play and Modest Mussorgsky's opera *Boris Godunov* (L2147, L2174-2176).
* **Legal Precedent:** During the Time of Troubles, successive governments (including Tsar Basil Shuisky) endorsed the murder theory to delegitimize Boris Godunov and the subsequent False Dmitrys, leading to the prince's canonization in June 1606 (L2252, L2272-2274).

### Arguments for Accident (Godunov Innocent)
* **The Official Commission (1591):** Immediately following the death, an official investigating commission headed by Prince Basil Shuisky (the future Tsar) conducted interviews in Uglich. The commission concluded that the prince had been playing with a knife and accidentally cut his own throat during a sudden epileptic fit (L2174).
* **Platonov's Rationale:** Historian Sergey Platonov argued that Godunov had no logical motive: Dmitry was the son of Ivan IV's seventh marriage, which was canonically invalid (making his rights to the throne highly dubious); Tsar Theodore was still in his thirties and could have fathered a natural heir; and Godunov, a master of palace intrigue, would not have staged an assassination so clumsily as to point immediately to his own associates (L2174-2176).
* **Vernadsky's Investigation:** George Vernadsky reviewed the case and established that no first-hand evidence or testimony supporting an assassination exists in the historical record, concluding the death was indeed accidental (L2176).

## Resolution Status
The controversy is **resolved by consensus** among modern professional historians (following Platonov and Vernadsky), who agree that Prince Dmitry's death was accidental, and that accusations of murder arose later as political propaganda during the Time of Troubles (L2176). However, the assassination myth remains firmly entrenched in popular culture (L2176).
