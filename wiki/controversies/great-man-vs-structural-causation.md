# Great Man vs. Structural/Systemic Causation in History

A long-standing interpretive dispute about the relative weight of individual agency versus large-scale impersonal forces in driving historical change. Directly relevant to how the wiki treats actors, events, and processes.

## Positions and Exemplars
- **Great Man emphasis**: Thomas Carlyle ("history is the biography of great men"); some military history (decisive battles, commanders); many traditional political biographies.
- **Structural emphasis**: Fernand Braudel (longue durée), Marxist modes of production, demographic and environmental historians (McNeill, Diamond in some readings), institutional economists.
- **Synthetic**: Most working historians combine both — e.g., "structures made X possible/likely; individual Y seized or squandered the opening."

The wiki's link taxonomy (caused_by vs contributed_to vs preceded_by) and emphasis on both actor pages and processes are attempts to hold both dimensions without collapsing one into the other.
