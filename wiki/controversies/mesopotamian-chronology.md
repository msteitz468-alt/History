## The Dispute

By exactly how many years should the entire chronology of the Bronze Age Near East
be shifted? Because the absolute dates of Mesopotamia hang from a single
astronomical record, the discipline carries several parallel chronologies that
differ by multiples of the Venus cycle — the **high, middle, and low** chronologies
— and the choice cascades into every Bronze Age date, including those of Egypt,
Anatolia, the Levant and the Aegean that are cross-tied to it. It is a
**source-reliability/calibration** dispute rather than an interpretive one: the
sequence of reigns is agreed; only its absolute anchoring is in doubt.

## The Anchor and Why It Is Ambiguous

The fixed point is a series of **observations of the planet Venus** recorded during
the reign of **Ammiṣaduqa**, tenth king of the First Dynasty of Babylon (the "Venus
Tablet of Ammiṣaduqa"). Venus's appearances recur on a ~8-year pattern, so the
astronomical data fit **several** absolute placements separated by 56–64 years.
Combined with uncertainties in the Assyrian eponym record and the lengths of the
Kassite and early dynasties, this yields the competing schemes.

## Positions

### Middle chronology — the CAH choice
In [[edwards-cah-v1p1-1970|CAH Vol. I, Part 1]] (Ch. VI), **M. B. Rowton** adopts
the solution placing the **end of the First Dynasty of Babylon at 1595 BCE** (sack
of Babylon by the Hittite Mursili I), "the most probable on the evidence available
at the moment of writing," with Hammurabi reigning c. 1792–1750. This became the
long-standing textbook default and is the chronology embedded throughout the CAH —
so the wiki treats CAH Bronze Age dates as *middle-chronology* dates by convention.

### High chronology
Rowton names **1651** as the next solution up that "deserves serious
consideration"; on it Hammurabi falls ~1848–1806. Defended at various times on
astronomical and historical grounds.

### Low and ultra-low chronologies
Later scholarship (Gasche, Armstrong, Cole, Gurzadyan; and dendrochronological work)
has pushed for **low (1531)** or **ultra-low (~1499)** dates for the fall of
Babylon, citing Old Assyrian eponym data and tree-ring sequences. These compress
the "dark age" between the fall of Babylon and the Kassite consolidation.

## Where Things Stand

**Open.** No single solution commands consensus. The **middle chronology** remains
the most common convention in reference works (and is the CAH's), but the **low/
ultra-low** options have gained ground from dendrochronology and re-readings of the
Old Assyrian record, while high-precision radiocarbon has narrowed but not closed
the range. Because the CAH and much of the wiki's antiquity backbone are built on
the middle chronology, dates carried from those sources should be understood as
**convention, not certainty**, and conflicts with low-chronology sources flagged
here rather than silently reconciled.
