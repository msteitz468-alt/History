# The Late Antique Population of the Samtavro Cemetery

How should the non-Christian Late Antique burials at the [[samtavro-cemetery|Samtavro
cemetery]] at Mtskheta, Georgia (1st–6th c. CE) be interpreted? The dispute is a genuine
scholarly disagreement over reading ethnicity and religion from grave form and orientation.

## Position 1 — A Jewish Diaspora Cemetery

**Tamila Mgalobishvili and Iulon Gagoshidze** argue that the Late Antique cemetery was
primarily a **Jewish diaspora** burial ground. Their case rests on the predominance of
**non-Christian, east-facing** burials and on stone-cist tombs read as analogous to Jewish
ossuary practice, set against the well-documented ancient presence of Jews in Georgia and a
handful of Aramaic/Hebrew inscriptions from the region.

## Position 2 — General Religious and Ethnic Pluralism

**Antonio Sagona, Claudia Sagona and Aleksandra Michalewicz** (Kim et al. 2017, ch. 9)
reject that reading as too narrowly framed and insufficiently engaged with the site's
material diversity. They point to a genuinely mixed assemblage — Christian *ichthys* rings
alongside Graeco-Roman intaglios, solar/astral imagery, a possible Zoroastrian fire-room,
and steppe-derived practices (broken bronze mirrors, cranial modification) — arguing for a
**general religious pluralism** at a Caucasus frontier crossroads. They grant that *some*
burials may indeed be Jewish, but note the ossuary analogy is weak on formal grounds:
Levantine ossuaries were small stone boxes, whereas Samtavro's are large stone cists dug
into the earth.

## Shared Factual Ground, Framed per Side

Both sides accept the same data: a large, long-used cemetery; a Late Antique shift toward
stone-cist tombs; predominantly east-facing, largely non-Christian burials; and a
documented Jewish presence in Georgia. They differ on **how much of the population one
label can carry** — a single diaspora community, or a plural frontier society — and on
whether tomb form is a reliable ethnic/religious marker at all, a caution the Sagona team
extends even to the "Alanic" identification (M.P. Abramova; Barca 2012). See
[[samtavro-cemetery]] and [[alans]].
