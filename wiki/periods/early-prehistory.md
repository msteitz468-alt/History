## Overview

Early Prehistory covers the ~3 million years during which the *Homo* genus
spread from Africa across Eurasia, developed progressively more sophisticated
stone tool technologies, began managing fire, and gradually evolved toward the
anatomically modern form. It closes with the appearance of the first archaic
modern humans and Neanderthals — around 300,000 BP.

This is the period of *Homo habilis*, *Homo ergaster*, *Homo erectus*, and
*Homo heidelbergensis*. Cumulative cultural learning is visible in the
archaeological record but slow; major tool traditions persist for hundreds of
thousands of years with little change.

## Major Developments

**Homo habilis and the Oldowan industry (~2.6–1.7 million BP)**
The first stone tools (Oldowan) are associated with Homo habilis and found
from ~2.6 million BP (notably at Olduvai Gorge, Tanzania — identified by
Louis Leakey in the 1930s). These simple flaked cobbles enabled butchering
of large animals and access to meat and marrow. This dietary shift may have
driven increased brain size and opened new ecological niches in colder regions.

**Out of Africa 1 (~1.8 million BP)**
The first migration of the *Homo* genus out of Africa. See [[out-of-africa-1-1800kbp]].
The earliest dated evidence outside Africa is at Dmanisi, Georgia (1.77 million BP).
Homo remains appear in Java (~1.5 million BP) and China (~1.66 million BP).
European evidence: Atapuerca, Spain (~1.3 million BP). Range initially limited
to approximately 40°N latitude.

**Acheulean technology (~1.7–0.3 million BP)**
Hand axes and other bifacially worked tools appear, associated with
*Homo ergaster* and *Homo erectus*. More versatile than Oldowan tools;
distributed across Africa, Europe, and South/East Asia.

**Fire management**
The earliest firm evidence for controlled use of fire by hominins dates from
~250,000 years ago, though some archaeologists argue for evidence from
South Africa and Israel as early as 800,000 BP. If the later date is correct,
fire management begins in Late Prehistory; if the earlier date, it begins in
this period. The question remains open (see [[homo-sapiens-emergence-date]] for
the wider dating controversy context). See also: [[processes/fire-management]].

**Initial peopling of Europe (~1.3 million–600,000 BP)**
The first European phase (Hoffecker, Ch. 16) involved sporadic occupation of
southern Europe by earlier *Homo* with pebble/flake tools: Atapuerca, Spain
(jaw + teeth ~1.2–1.3 million BP; younger fossils ~0.8 million BP classified as
*Homo antecessor*, possibly unique to Europe); Ceprano, Italy (skullcap
~0.7–1.0 million BP); a handaxe at Estrecho del Quípar, Spain (~0.9 million BP).

**Homo heidelbergensis (~600,000–200,000 BP)**
A new large-brained species (cranial volume averaging >1,100 cc) migrating from
Africa into western Eurasia ~600,000 BP, probably via the Bosporus. *Heidelbergensis*
was the common ancestor of both Neanderthals and *Homo sapiens*, and had mastered
fire control. It manufactured the refined Acheulean handaxe/biface — production
requiring a preconceived design and hierarchically organized steps (not the
reductive earlier toolmaking). Known from Boxgrove, England (~500,000 BP, a "whole
landscape" of butchery sites; a massive tibia hinting cold adaptations had not yet
evolved) and Schöningen, Germany (~400,000 BP, with the earliest known wooden
spears — pine/spruce, carefully shaped from the hardest wood at the base of the
tree). It gave rise to the [[neanderthals|Neanderthals]] in Europe.

## Key Actors

- *Homo habilis* — first identifiable member of the *Homo* genus; Oldowan tools
- *Homo ergaster* / *Homo erectus* — first African and Eurasian dispersal; Acheulean tools
- *Homo heidelbergensis* — immediate ancestor of both Neanderthals and modern humans

## Geographic Scope

Africa (entire period). Eurasia from ~1.8 million BP. No evidence of hominin
presence in Australasia or the Americas during this period.

## Transition

Follows [[deep-prehistory]], beginning with the earliest recognized stone-tool
manufacture (~3.3 Mya) that defines the start of the archaeological record proper.

Closes with the evolution of *Homo sapiens* (anatomically) in East Africa and
Neanderthals in Europe from *Homo heidelbergensis* ancestors, around 300,000–
250,000 BP. This marks the opening of [[late-prehistory]].

## Historiography

**Source quality**: Fossil and stone tool evidence only. Increasingly
supplemented by ancient DNA (aDNA) analysis, which has transformed the field
since the 2010s — notably the sequencing of the Neanderthal genome (2010) and
the identification of the Denisovans (2010, from a Siberian cave).

**Dating methods**: Potassium-argon, argon-argon, uranium-series, electron
spin resonance, thermoluminescence, paleomagnetism. Confidence intervals
typically ±50,000 to ±100,000 years for this period.

**Major debates**: The taxonomy of Homo erectus vs. Homo ergaster (lumpers vs.
splitters); the routes of Out of Africa 1; whether early Eurasian populations
contributed to the *H. sapiens* lineage; the date and origin of fire control.

**Recent revisionism**: Ancient DNA analysis has revealed that non-African
modern humans carry ~1–4% Neanderthal DNA and that some populations carry
Denisovan DNA, suggesting gene flow was ongoing across much of this period —
complicating a clean "Out of Africa" model in favour of a "mostly out of
Africa with some introgression" model.
