# Late Bronze Age

## Overview

The **Late Bronze Age** (~1,550–1,200 BCE in the Near East) is the age of the
**"great powers" system** — a world in which a handful of **territorial empires**
(Egypt, Hatti, Mitanni, Babylonia, and later Assyria) dealt with one another as
formal equals, through resident embassies, dynastic marriage, gift-exchange and
written treaties, while a dense layer of **vassal city-states** filled the
Syro-Palestinian land-bridge between them. It is the world of the **chariot
aristocracy**, the cosmopolitan luxury trade, and the first true **international
order** — the system whose correspondence survives in the **[[amarna-letters|Amarna
letters]]**. It opens out of the [[middle-bronze-age|Middle Bronze Age]] with the
~1595 collapse of the Amorite kingdoms and closes catastrophically in the ~1200 BCE
**[[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]]**. It is anchored to
[[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (the opening, to c. 1380 BCE) and
**[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]]** (the Amarna climax through the
collapse, c. 1380–1000 BCE) — between them they now cover the period in full.

## Major Developments

- **The reshuffling of ~1600–1550 BCE** — the [[hittites|Hittite]] king
  **[[mursili-i|Mursili I]]** destroys [[iamkhad|Aleppo]] and
  **[[fall-of-babylon-1595bce|sacks Babylon]]**, ending the
  [[babylon-first-dynasty|First Dynasty]]; the **[[kassites|Kassites]]** take
  Babylonia (their ~four-century dynasty, the longest in its history); the
  [[hurrians|Hurrians]] coalesce into **[[mitanni|Mitanni]]** across the north; and
  in Egypt the [[hyksos|Hyksos]] are expelled. A few territorial empires replace the
  Amorite mosaic.
- **The Egyptian empire (Eighteenth Dynasty)** — the **[[new-kingdom-egypt|New
  Kingdom]]** rises as a "military monarchy": [[ahmose-i|Ahmose I]] expels the
  Hyksos and retakes Nubia; [[thutmose-iii|Thutmose III]] (after **Megiddo**) carries
  the empire to the **Euphrates**; [[hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] and
  [[amenhotep-iii|Amenhotep III]] mark its zenith and the onset of stagnation, while
  the **[[amun-priesthood|Amun priesthood]]** and the Karnak temple-economy grow.
- **The four-power balance in Syria** — **Egypt, [[mitanni|Mitanni]] and the
  [[hittites|Hittites]]** contest the Levant, with [[ugarit|Ugarit]],
  [[alalakh|Alalakh]], Qadesh, Qatna, Tunip and Amurru as the chief
  **[[canaanite-civilization|Canaanite]]** vassal states; an Egyptian–Mitannian
  rapprochement (marriage alliance) checks Hatti — the world the
  [[interpolity-law|international system]] / Amarna letters document.
- **The chariot and the *maryannu*** — the horse-drawn war-chariot and its
  aristocratic charioteer caste (the Indo-Aryan-named *maryannu* of Mitanni;
  **Kikkuli's** horse-manual) define LBA warfare and elite culture across the whole
  zone.
- **The Aegean: Minoan zenith and Mycenaean rise** — [[minoan-civilization|Minoan
  Crete]] reaches its **Late Palace** zenith ([[knossos|Knossos]], the frescoes, the
  Palace Style) before the **[[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean Greeks]]** take over
  [[knossos|Knossos]] (~1450) — proved by **[[aegean-scripts|Linear B]]**, the
  earliest Greek — and the palace civilization of the Greek mainland (Mycenae,
  Pylos, Tiryns) spreads across the Aegean.
- **The Hittite Middle Kingdom → Empire** — after the Telipinu reforms and a
  Mitannian eclipse, the [[hittites|Hittite]] dynasty (Hurrianized under Tudhaliya
  and Arnuwanda) recovers, producing **[[shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma I]]**, who
  shatters [[mitanni|Mitanni]] and makes Hatti a superpower.
- **The Amarna climax (CAH II.2)** — under [[akhenaten|Akhenaten]] the
  [[amarna-letters|Amarna correspondence]] documents the great-powers system at its
  height even as Egyptian control of Syria frays; [[shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma I]]
  exploits the moment to destroy Mitanni and plant Hittite power in north Syria — the
  [[zannanza-affair-1322bce|Zannanza affair]] (the Egyptian queen's request for a
  Hittite prince) marking the strange intimacy and hostility of the two courts.
- **The clash of empires (CAH II.2)** — the 19th-Dynasty pharaohs
  [[sethos-i|Sethos I]], [[ramesses-ii|Ramesses II]] and [[merneptah|Merneptah]]
  fight Hatti for Syria: the [[battle-of-qadesh-1274bce|battle of Qadesh]] (c. 1274)
  and the resulting [[egyptian-hittite-treaty-1259bce|Egyptian–Hittite peace]] (c.
  1259) — the **earliest surviving parity treaty between great powers** — while
  [[assyria-middle|Middle Assyria]] under [[tukulti-ninurta-i|Tukulti-Ninurta I]]
  rises behind Mitanni's ruin and [[elam|Middle Elamite Elam]] reaches its zenith.
- **The collapse (CAH II.2)** — c. 1200 the system shatters in the
  **[[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]]**: the [[hittites|Hittite
  Empire]] falls, [[ugarit|Ugarit]] and the [[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean
  palaces]] are destroyed, the [[sea-peoples|Sea Peoples]] are repelled by
  [[ramesses-iii|Ramesses III]] and settle the coast as the
  [[philistines|Philistines]], and the [[early-iron-age|Iron Age]] world of small
  kingdoms begins.
- **The highland Iron-Age threshold** — the
  [[persian-plateau-second-millennium|Persian-plateau]] grey-ware and painted-ware
  cultures run to ~1350–1150, when new (probably Iranian-speaking) movements open the
  highland Iron Age.

## Key Actors

- Empires/states: [[new-kingdom-egypt|New Kingdom Egypt]] · the [[hittites|Hittite
  kingdom]] · [[mitanni|Mitanni]] · [[kassites|Kassite Babylonia]] ·
  [[minoan-civilization|Minoan Crete]] · [[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean Greece]].
- Rulers: [[ahmose-i|Ahmose I]] · [[hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] ·
  [[thutmose-iii|Thutmose III]] · [[amenhotep-iii|Amenhotep III]] ·
  [[hattusili-i|Hattusili I]] & [[mursili-i|Mursili I]] (Old-Kingdom forerunners);
  Saustatar of Mitanni.
- The [[canaanite-civilization|Canaanite]] vassal city-states ([[ugarit|Ugarit]],
  [[alalakh|Alalakh]]); the copper island of [[cyprus|Cyprus]] (Alashiya).

## Geographic Scope

Historically active across the whole eastern Mediterranean and Near East: Egypt and
Nubia, the Levant and Syria, Anatolia (Hatti, Arzawa, [[troy|Troy/Wilusa]]), Upper
Mesopotamia (Mitanni) and Babylonia (the Kassites), the Iranian highlands, and the
Aegean (Crete, the mainland, Cyprus). It is the first period in which these regions
form a **single interacting system**, knit by diplomacy and the trade in copper, tin,
gold, ivory, textiles and Aegean goods.

## Transition

Opens out of the [[middle-bronze-age|Middle Bronze Age]] across the ~1600–1550
horizon (the Hittite destruction of Aleppo and Babylon, the Kassite and Mitannian
consolidations, the Hyksos expulsion). It closes in the
**[[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|~1200 BCE Bronze Age Collapse]]** — the wave of
destructions (the [[sea-peoples|Sea Peoples]]; the fall of [[hittites|Hatti]],
[[ugarit|Ugarit]] and the [[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean palaces]]) that ends
the great-powers system and opens the [[early-iron-age|Early Iron Age]]. Both the
Amarna-age climax and the collapse are now covered by
[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II.2]].

## Historiography

**Source quality.** Uniquely rich and multi-archival: the **Amarna letters** (the
diplomatic correspondence of the great kings, in Akkadian), the **Hittite** treaty
and annal archives of [[hattusha|Boğazköy]], the **Egyptian** monumental and
administrative record, the **[[ugarit|Ugaritic]] and [[alalakh|Alalakh]]** tablets,
and — for the Aegean — the **[[aegean-scripts|Linear B]]** archives ([[knossos|
Knossos]], Pylos), the only LBA texts in **Greek**. Much of the Aegean and all of the
Iranian record is **archaeological**, read from palaces, frescoes, tombs and pottery.

**Debates.** The **agency and date of the fall of [[knossos|Knossos]]** and the
Minoan–Mycenaean transition ([[fall-of-knossos]]); the **absolute chronology**, still
hung on the [[mesopotamian-chronology|disputed ~1595 sack of Babylon]]; the location
of **Arzawa/Wilusa** and the historicity of the [[troy|Troy]]/Mycenaean contact; and,
looming over the period's end, the **causes of the Bronze Age Collapse** (a standing
controversy). The period is also the locus of the **second-millennium Indo-European
question** — Hittite/Luwian, Indo-Aryan *maryannu*, Mycenaean Greek, and the highland
Iranian grey-ware cultures.
