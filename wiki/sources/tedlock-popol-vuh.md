# Popol Vuh (trans. Dennis Tedlock, 1985)

The K'iche' Maya creation epic — the Americas gap's **Maya-voice** primary source, the cosmological
counterpart to the conquest narratives. Tedlock's translation renders the full **Council Book**: the
creation of the world by Heart of Sky and the Plumed Serpent; the failed humans of mud and wood; the
saga of the **Hero Twins** Hunahpu and Xbalanque and their defeat of the underworld **Xibalba**; the
creation of true humanity from **maize**; and the **genealogies and migrations** of the K'iche' lineages
to the eve of conquest.

## Source Notes

The Popol Vuh is the rare case of a Mesoamerican civilization preserved in its **own sacred voice**
rather than through conquest accounts or external archaeology. Three points matter for the wiki. (1) Its
**continuity**: the Hero Twins and their Xibalba ballgame appear on **Classic Maya vase-painting**
centuries before the colonial K'iche' text, so the Popol Vuh documents a living Maya tradition spanning
~a millennium — direct evidence against the [[holmbergs-mistake|"timeless/static Indian"]] frame. (2) Its
**pan-Mesoamerican theology**: humans exist to **nurture and praise the gods** (the failed mud/wood
people are destroyed precisely because they cannot), the same **reciprocal debt** that drives
[[aztec-religion-and-human-sacrifice|Aztec sacrifice]] — and humanity is literally **made of
[[maize-domestication|maize]]**. (3) Its **political function**: the closing genealogies legitimate the
K'iche' ruling houses by descent from the first corn-men, a charter as much as a cosmogony. Tedlock's
daykeeper-informed reading makes the text's living meaning unusually accessible; its limits are those of
any colonial recension of an oral tradition.
