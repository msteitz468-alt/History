# The Hellenistic Age (Peter Thonemann, 2016)

Short Oxford University Press survey of the three centuries from the death of
[[alexander-the-great|Alexander]] (323 BCE) to the fall of Ptolemaic Egypt (30 BCE).
Thonemann aims to convey the **richness and geographic scale** of Hellenistic
civilization — from the Library and Museum of Alexandria to the Afghan colonial
frontier — rather than a continuous political chronicle. Complements
[[walbank-hellenistic-world|Walbank's]] standard overview and the
[[walbank-cah-v7-1-1984|CAH VII.1]] formative-century treatment.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Thonemann's short OUP survey is **thematic, not exhaustive**: six chapters that each take a person, place, or problem as a window on the age. Its value to the wiki is therefore complementary to denser frames already present ([[walbank-hellenistic-world]], [[walbank-cah-v7-1-1984]], Boardman OHCW Price/Lane Fox/Barnes).

Three contributions stand out. **(1) Historiography of the category.** Droysen's *Hellenismus* as modern invention, the fusion-vs-apartheid debate, the counter-lament that Hellenistic evidence is *not* poor (third-century political narrative is the hole), and the explicit constructivism about exclusions (Sicily, Parthia, Jews, Romans) sharpen [[hellenistic-historiography]] beyond Walbank's source catalogue. **(2) Case studies that become pages.** Demetrius for *basileia*, Eratosthenes + Museum for science, Priene for the ordinary *polis*, and four edge "encounters" (Aï Khanoum, Ashoka, Olbia, Villa of the Papyri) give the wiki concrete microhistories rather than only structural overviews. **(3) Demythology of Alexandria.** Deflating library-size and fire myths while keeping the Museum's real creative power prevents the wiki from recycling legendary figures.

Limits: third-century narrative remains thin (by design and by source); Roman wars are sketched; no independent treatment of Jewish, Parthian, or western Greek material beyond the exclusions argument. File as accessible Oxford synthesis, not Cambridge reference.
