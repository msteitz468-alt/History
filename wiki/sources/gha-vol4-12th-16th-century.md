# UNESCO General History of Africa, Vol. IV: Africa from the Twelfth to the Sixteenth Century

The fourth volume of the **UNESCO *General History of Africa*** (ed. **D. T. Niane**, 1984) is the volume of
**Africa's great medieval empires** — the centuries when **Mali** and **Songhay** dominated the western
Sudan, the **Almohads** unified the Maghrib, **Mamluk** Egypt led the Islamic world, **Solomonic Ethiopia**
flourished, the **Swahili** city-states and **Great Zimbabwe** thrived on Indian Ocean gold, and the first
states of the **Great Lakes** and **Central Africa** took shape. It is the **high-medieval reference for
[[sub-saharan-africa|Gap 2]]** and the richest GHA volume for sub-Saharan political history — finally giving
the depth the wiki's [[reader-africa-biography|Reader]]-based pages lacked.

## The volume's argument

Niane's introduction presents these centuries as **Africa's medieval golden age**: powerful, literate,
wealthy states fully integrated into the trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean economies, exporting the gold that
underpinned the medieval Mediterranean (Mansa Musa's 1324 hajj as its emblem), and developing their own
syntheses of Islam (or, in Ethiopia, Christianity) with indigenous tradition. The closing chapters
(Devisse) place Africa squarely **within intercontinental relations** — a continent that shaped, and was
shaped by, the wider Afro-Eurasian world on the eve of the European maritime intrusion.
