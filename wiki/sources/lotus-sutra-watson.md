# The Lotus Sutra (*Saddharma Pundarika*)

"One of the most important and influential of all the sutras of **[[mahayana-buddhism|Mahayana
Buddhism]]**" — the scripture that, through Kumarajiva's Chinese translation, became central to the
religious life of the entire **East Asian** world. The second of the 7 remaining available
Processing-List sources (Phase 6).

## The Text

The Lotus Sutra is presented as a great assembly at which **Shakyamuni Buddha** reveals his highest
teaching. Its doctrines became defining for the Mahayana:

- **The One Vehicle (*ekayana*).** The various paths the Buddha had taught (those of the *shravaka*,
  *pratyekabuddha* and bodhisattva — the "three vehicles") are declared to be **expedient means
  (*upaya*)**: in truth there is **one vehicle**, leading **all beings without exception to
  Buddhahood**. Even the *arhats* are assured they will become Buddhas.
- **The eternal Buddha.** The Buddha reveals that his attainment of enlightenment and his entry into
  *nirvana* are skillful appearances: his true life-span is **immeasurable**, the Buddha an ever-present,
  cosmic reality.
- **Universal salvation and devotion.** Anyone — including women, the wicked, and ordinary people — may
  reach Buddhahood; the sutra exalts faith, and the chapter on the bodhisattva **Avalokiteshvara**
  (Guanyin/Kannon), the "Universal Gateway," became a beloved devotional text in its own right.
- **Parables.** It teaches through famous parables — the **burning house** (the father luring his
  children out with promised carts), the **prodigal son**, the rain that falls equally on all plants —
  dramatizing skillful means and universal grace.

## Significance

Through **Kumarajiva's** translation (406 CE) the Lotus Sutra became one of the **most revered scriptures
of East Asian Buddhism**: the central text of the **Tiantai (Tendai)** school, the foundation of
**Nichiren** Buddhism in Japan, and a pervasive influence on East Asian art, literature and devotion. It
is the wiki's primary witness to **Mahayana** religious thought and to the transmission of Buddhism
across Asia.
