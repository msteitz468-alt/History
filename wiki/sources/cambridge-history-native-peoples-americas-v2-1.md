# The Cambridge History of the Native Peoples of the Americas, Volume II: Mesoamerica, Part 1

One of three volumes (North America, Mesoamerica, South America) in the Cambridge
History of the Native Peoples of the Americas. **Volume II**, edited by Richard E. W. Adams
(Mesoamerican archaeology) and Murdo J. MacLeod (colonial Latin American history), treats
the indigenous peoples of Mesoamerica across two physical parts. **Part 1** (this source)
covers native prehistoric cultures from Paleoindian peopling through the Aztec empire on
the eve of Spanish invasion. **Part 2** (now ingested as
[[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v2-2|CHNPA II.2]]) covers Mesoamerica
since the Spanish invasion.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Part 1 is the **prehistoric half** of Cambridge’s Mesoamerica volume: eleven specialist
chapters that reconstitute indigenous culture history from first peopling to the eve of
Spanish invasion, deliberately postponing colonial transformation to Part 2. Adams’s
introduction fixes the volume’s analytical vocabulary — Mesoamerica as a **co-tradition /
diffusion sphere**, stage chronology (Paleoindian–Archaic–Preclassic/Formative–Classic–
Postclassic), and the anthropological premise that patterned behavior leaves material
correlates. The body chapters then deliver regional sequences of unequal density: Zeitlin
& Zeitlin on deep time and agricultural origins; Grove and Diehl on Formative highlands
and the Gulf Olmec arc; Hammond and Sharer on lowland and southern Maya; Cowgill on the
Teotihuacan–Epiclassic–Tula macro-sequence; Gorenstein on **Greater Mesoamerica**
(West/Northwest Mexico and the Purépecha); Marcus & Flannery on the Oaxaca evolutionary
ladder; Sheets on southeastern **frontiers** (zones, not mere peripheries); Charlton on
the post-Tula city-state landscape and the Triple Alliance.

For the wiki, this source **thickens a thin but already-started Mesoamerican backbone**
(CWH IV/V, Mann, Berdan, Snow et al.) into a proper regional lattice: named places for
Olmec, Oaxaca, Epiclassic, Maya, and West Mexican centers; process pages for Preclassic,
Paleoindian–Archaic, highland–Pacific Maya, and SE frontiers; concept pages for culture-area
definition, periodization, writing, *altepetl*, and chinampas; and event pages for the fall
of Tula and Triple Alliance formation. Cross-volume tensions preserved as attributed
positions include **Olmec mother vs sister culture** (Grove’s highland skepticism vs
Diehl’s inventory), **Teotihuacan collapse dating** (page slug ~550 vs Adams/Cowgill ~650),
**Teotihuacan political form** (collective leadership vs softer Cowgill reading), and
**Hammond’s logistical model** of Classic Maya collapse alongside multicausal consensus.

It pairs with already-ingested [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|CHNPA III.1]]
(South America Part 1) as the second continental pillar of the Native Peoples series.
**Part 2** (colonial–modern Mesoamerica, same series, separate raw file) remains to be
ingested and will reverse the vantage from archaeology-first to ethnohistory-first.
