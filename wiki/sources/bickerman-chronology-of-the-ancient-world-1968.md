# Chronology of the Ancient World — E. J. Bickerman (1968; 2nd ed. 1980)

A concise technical handbook explaining the calendars and chronological systems used in the ancient world, with guidance on converting dates between them. It covers the day, month, year structures in various cultures, eras (Olympic, Seleucid, etc.), and practical methods for historians.

This is a **reference work**, not a narrative source. It is the go-to tool for "dating precision" in ancient periods, directly addressing one of the wiki's core needs (see Chronological Uncertainty Protocol and Bickerman entry in Outstanding Sources).

## Structure

- Preface and Introduction
- I. The Calendar: Day, lunar/solar months, Greek calendars (including Athenian), Egyptian, Roman (pre- and Julian), zodiac, week.
- II. Chronography: Relative chronology, naming years (eponymous, consular, etc.), eras (Olympic, Seleucid, etc.), indiction.
- III. Applied Chronology: Principles for reducing dates, chronography techniques, practical suggestions.
- Abbreviations, Notes, and Tables (Astronomical Canon, rising/setting of stars, Olympic years, Roman Julian calendar, lists of rulers).

The tables are particularly valuable for cross-cultural dating.

## Historiography / Collection Note

Bickerman's work is foundational and still widely cited for its clarity on ancient time-reckoning. It predates some modern revisions (e.g., in Assyrian or Egyptian chronology) but remains the standard starting point. Later scholarship builds on or corrects specific tables.

The Bibliotheca Alexandrina now includes this essential reference tool alongside its narrative sources for antiquity.
