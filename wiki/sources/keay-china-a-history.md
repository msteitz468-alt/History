# John Keay, *China: A History* (2009)

A clear narrative of China across its whole span, the **spine** for the wiki's Phase D China
build-out. Ingested single-pass to fill the worst gaps in the dynastic backbone.

## Keay's framing: the dynastic dynamic

Keay organizes Chinese history around its **dynastic succession** — "history literally told the
time; dates … ticked away the minutes, dynasties tolled the hours." Of the ~100 self-declared
dynasties, a few "legitimate" ones form the "great plateaux" of Chinese history. The **five
longest-lived** (each ~three to four centuries) are the backbone to memorize:

- **Han** (202 BC–AD 220) — coeval with Rome;
- **Tang** (618–907) — coeval with the Arab expansion;
- **Song** (960–1279) — coeval with the Crusades;
- **Ming** (1368–1644) — coeval with the early Ottoman and Mughal empires;
- **Qing**/Manchu (1644–1912) — coeval with Europe's global expansion.

Bookending these, the short **Qin** (221–206 BC) under the **First Emperor** founded the
imperial system, and the Mongol **Yuan** (1279–1368) came closest to the Chinese boast of
ruling "All under Heaven." Keay stresses that China's history is "not altogether unlike that of
other nations … a difference of degree, not kind, of scale, not character."
