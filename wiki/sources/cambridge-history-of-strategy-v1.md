# The Cambridge History of Strategy, Volume I (2024)

The first volume of the **Cambridge History of Strategy** — a global, comparative survey of
**how polities have practised strategy** from antiquity to 1800, "transcending the
traditional Western focus and modern-state-based framework of strategic studies." Its payoff
for the wiki is conceptual: it supplies the framework on **[[strategy]]** and the
cross-civilizational findings on **[[strategic-practice-across-civilizations]]**.

## Argument

Strategy — the linking of political ends to means in a contest of wills — is a **global,
eternal phenomenon**, not a modern Western invention, detectable (via Kagan's definition)
wherever leaders set objectives, prioritise them, and allocate resources. The comparative
method reveals both **near-universal features** (the aspiration to universal monarchy,
peacetime deterrence, dynastic-marriage alliance) and deep **culture-specific variation**.
The volume's comparative conclusion ("Striving for and Resisting Universal Monarchy") draws
these threads together.
