# UNESCO General History of Africa, Vol. VI: Africa in the Nineteenth Century until the 1880s

The sixth volume of the **UNESCO *General History of Africa*** (ed. **J. F. Ade Ajayi**, 1989) covers the
**nineteenth century down to the eve of the Scramble** — a century of dramatic **African-driven**
transformation: the **Islamic jihads** and the rise of the **Sokoto Caliphate**, the **abolition** of the
Atlantic slave trade and the turn to "**legitimate commerce**," the **Mfecane** and the new southern African
states, **Muhammad Ali's** modernizing Egypt, the **Zanzibar** ivory-and-slave empire reaching deep into
East-Central Africa, **Ethiopian reunification**, and the **French conquest of Algeria**. It is the
**nineteenth-century reference for [[sub-saharan-africa|Gap 2]]**, bridging the early-modern states of GHA
Vol. V and the colonial conquest of Vol. VII.

## The volume's argument

Editor Ajayi's volume insists on **nineteenth-century Africa as an age of African initiative**, not merely a
passive "prelude to partition." Its through-lines are the **Islamic revolutions** that remade the West
African savanna, the **economic revolution** from slave-export to "legitimate" commodity trade, the
**state-building** of the Mfecane and the new East and Central African polities, and the **modernizing**
projects (Egypt, Ethiopia, Madagascar) — all cut short, but not caused, by the European conquest that
followed.
