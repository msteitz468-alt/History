# Orientalism (Edward W. Said)

The book that defined **[[orientalism|Orientalism]]** as a critical concept and founded **postcolonial
studies** — Edward Said's analysis of the Western discourse that constructed "the Orient" as the
European Other and underwrote imperial domination. Ninth source of **Gap 1**; a cornerstone for the
wiki's historiography of empire.

## The Work

Said examines British, French and later American writing about the Middle East and Asia — philology,
literature, travel, scholarship and administration — to argue that **"Orientalism" is a discourse**: a
structured Western way of knowing, representing and dominating the East that says more about the West's
will to power than about the societies it claims to describe. Knowledge and empire are inseparable; the
"Orient" is, in large part, a European invention serving European ends.

## Significance and Reception

*Orientalism* transformed the humanities — founding **postcolonial theory** and making the **politics of
representation** central to how historians read sources about non-European peoples. It is indispensable
to the wiki's pages on [[imperialism|imperialism]], colonial knowledge, and [[eurocentrism|Eurocentrism]].
It is also one of the most **debated** books of its era; the critiques (selectivity, a monolithic
"Orientalism," neglected agency and counter-traditions) belong on the [[orientalism]] page alongside its
argument.
