# The West Point Atlas of World War II

This is the cartographic companion to the *West Point History of World War II* (Rogers & Seidule, 2015–2016, two volumes) already ingested into the wiki. It supplies the standard situation and campaign maps used in professional military education for the European, Mediterranean, and Pacific theaters.

The primary value of this source is **visual**. Like the ACW volume of the same series, it consists primarily of full-page or double-page maps showing force positions at specific times, movement arrows, terrain overlays, and order-of-battle information. Text is minimal (captions, keys, occasional notes).

## Structure and Coverage (168 pages)

The atlas is organized by theater and chronology:
- Prelude and 1939 campaigns (Poland, etc.).
- 1940 Western campaign (Fall of France, Low Countries).
- Battle of Britain and air/sea.
- Barbarossa and Eastern Front 1941–45 (multiple plates for phases, key battles like Stalingrad, Kursk).
- North Africa / Mediterranean.
- Normandy invasion and breakout (Overlord, Cobra, Falaise).
- Later European campaigns (Bulge, advance into Germany).
- Pacific (Pearl Harbor context, Midway, island-hopping, Iwo/Okinawa, Japan homeland).
- Strategic air and naval.

Signature West Point style: clean, clear unit symbols, timed "Situation at..." labels, emphasis on command decisions and terrain effects.

## Historiography / Collection Note

These maps have been the teaching standard at West Point for generations. They prioritize clarity for cadets on operational concepts over micro-detail. Modern scholarship sometimes offers refined positions or timings, but the overall campaign pictures remain foundational and widely referenced.

The Bibliotheca Alexandrina now makes selected maps directly available alongside the narrative analyses.
