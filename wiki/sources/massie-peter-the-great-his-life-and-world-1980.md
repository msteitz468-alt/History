# Peter the Great: His Life and World (Robert K. Massie, 1980)

Robert K. Massie's major biography of Peter I (1672–1725, r. 1682/1696–1725), tsar and first Emperor of Russia. The book traces Peter's life from a contested childhood in Old Muscovy, through the regency of his half-sister Sophia and the strel'tsy revolts, his self-education in "games" and shipbuilding, the transformative Grand Embassy of 1697–98, the twenty-one-year Great Northern War against Charles XII of Sweden, the founding of St. Petersburg, sweeping institutional, cultural, and military westernizing reforms, the tragic confrontation with his son Alexis, and the creation of the Russian Empire (1721). Massie presents Peter as a force of nature — tall, tireless, curious, brutal when necessary, personally involved in everything — who dragged a traditional, inward-looking society into the European state system at immense human cost.

The biography is structured in five parts plus epilogue, balancing personal portraiture with the operational history of campaigns (especially Narva, Poltava) and the administrative revolution (colleges, Table of Ranks, Holy Synod, poll tax, new capital). It draws heavily on foreign observers who were astonished and often repelled by Peter's methods and appearance, while documenting the concrete results: a modern standing army and navy, a European-style bureaucracy, a new capital on the Baltic, and Russia's emergence as a great power.

Massie complements and humanizes the structural treatment in prior ingests (Cambridge History of Russia Vol. II; NCMH Vol. VI; Freeze). The rich primary texture and focus on decision mechanics justify both enrichment of the summary actor page and a high-resolution detailed biography page in the Biography Hub.
