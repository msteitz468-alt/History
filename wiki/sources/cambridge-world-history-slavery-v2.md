# The Cambridge World History of Slavery, Vol. 2: AD 500–AD 1420 (2021)

The medieval volume, edited by **Craig Perry, David Eltis, Stanley Engerman, and David
Richardson** — the most recent and most global of the four. It deliberately fills what its
editors call a gap in "general knowledge of the history of slavery between the fall of Rome and
the rise of the transatlantic plantation complexes," which they describe as "lacking precision."

## What it contributes

- **[[medieval-slavery]]** — a new process page for the c. 500–1420 millennium: the saqaliba
  ("Slav"→"slave") trade, the Black Sea–Mamluk Egypt circuit, military slavery, the western
  Indian Ocean, and the decline of chattel slavery into serfdom in north-west Europe.
- Enriches **[[slavery]]** and the [[slave-society]] concept (which it qualifies).
