# Strategy: A History (Lawrence Freedman, 2013)

The **backbone intellectual-history source** for the wiki's [[hubs/warfare/strategy|Strategy]]
sub-hub: a single-volume history of strategic *thought* from the cunning (*mētis*) of Odysseus
to the narrative turn of the late 20th century. Where *[[sources/jablonsky-roots-of-strategy-4-1999|Roots of Strategy, Book 4]]* gave the wiki four primary theorists of the sea and air domains,
Freedman supplies the **whole map of the field** — and, decisively, full chapters on the
land-warfare canon the wiki had been missing entirely: [[clausewitz-carl-von|Clausewitz]],
[[jomini-antoine-henri|Jomini]], [[delbruck-hans|Delbrück]], [[liddell-hart-basil|Liddell
Hart]], [[fuller-jfc|Fuller]], [[schelling-thomas|Schelling]], and [[boyd-john|Boyd]].

## Freedman's thesis

- **Strategy is "the art of creating power."** It "is about getting more out of a situation
  than the starting balance of power would suggest." A strategy is *more than a plan*: a plan
  assumes an orderly sequence to a goal; strategy is needed precisely because others — with
  opposing interests — will frustrate the plan ("everyone has a plan 'till they get punched in
  the mouth"). Strategy is therefore "**fluid and flexible, governed by the starting point and
  not the end point.**"
- **The underdog's art.** "For those who start as powerful, strategy should not be too
  difficult." The real tests of creativity are **underdog strategies** — winning by superior
  intelligence, deception, maneuver, and coalition rather than brute force. Freedman's
  recurring contrast: the exemplars are "**Odysseus but not Achilles, Sun Tzu and Liddell Hart
  but not Clausewitz and Jomini**" — guile over force, the *indirect* over the direct.
- **Coalition over the duel.** Strategy is wrongly imagined as a duel (a clash of two wills,
  the game-theory 2×2). "Combining with others often constitutes the most astute strategic
  move; for the same reason, preventing opponents from doing the same can be as valuable." The
  realm of strategy is "bargaining and persuasion as well as threats and pressure."
- **The narrative turn.** Across the military, political, and business spheres there has been
  "a degree of convergence around the idea that the best strategic practice may now consist in
  forming compelling accounts of how to turn a developing situation into a desirable outcome" —
  strategy as a **story told in the future tense from the perspective of a leading character**
  (the "strategic script"), as faith in central planning collapsed after the 1960s.
