# The Complete Works of Zhuangzi

The masterpiece of philosophical **[[daoism|Daoism]]** and one of the supreme works of Chinese
literature — the writings of **[[zhuangzi|Zhuang Zhou]]** and his school, whose theme can be summed up in
one word: **freedom**. The fourth and final source of this Phase-6 batch; Burton Watson's complete
translation (which subsumes the partial *Basic Writings* also on the Processing List).

## The Work

Thirty-three chapters of **parable, dialogue, paradox and fantasy** — the **Inner Chapters (1–7)** the
core of Zhuangzi's own thought, the **Outer** and **Miscellaneous** chapters the work of later hands.
Rather than argue systematically, Zhuangzi teaches through unforgettable stories: the giant **Peng bird**
and the cicada who cannot imagine its flight ("Free and Easy Wandering"); **Cook Ding** carving the ox by
following its natural lines; the **butterfly dream**; the skull who would not return to life; the
"useless" tree that survives because no carpenter wants it; the debate with the logician **Hui Shi** on
the happiness of fishes.

## Themes

- **Freedom through accord with the Dao** — release from the strivings, anxieties and conventions of the
  world by going along with the **natural way** (*wu wei*, *ziran*).
- **The equality of things** and the **relativity of all distinctions** (right/wrong, useful/useless,
  life/death) — perspectives, not absolutes; the sage roams beyond them.
- **Skepticism about language and knowledge** — the Dao cannot be caught in words; fixed judgments are
  traps.
- **Spontaneity, transformation and acceptance** — including the acceptance of death as part of the
  ceaseless flux.

## Significance

The *Zhuangzi* gave Daoism its **literary and mystical genius** and Chinese civilization a perennial
alternative to Confucian social order — nourishing **poetry, painting, landscape art**, the **private**
and contemplative life, and the Chinese reception of **Chan (Zen) Buddhism**. It is, with the *Laozi*,
the fountainhead of Daoist thought.
