# Trevor Bryce, *The Kingdom of the Hittites* (New Edition, OUP 2005)

The authoritative narrative history of the Anatolian kingdom of **Hatti** from its
Bronze Age origins to its disappearance in the [[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|collapse
of c. 1200 BCE]], and the standard English-language reference for the **political and
military** dimension of the Hittite world. Bryce, an Australian Hittitologist, builds
the account king-by-king from the cuneiform tablet archives of the capital
[[hattusha|Hattusa]] (Boğazköy) and the regional centres, letting "the people speak for
themselves" through translated primary passages. The New Edition rewrites sections of
every chapter in the light of finds made since 1996 — above all the **Bronze Tablet**
(the Kurunta treaty) and the **Tikunani letter** of [[hattusili-i|Hattusili I]].

This source overlays a dense, reign-by-reign political-military spine onto the wiki's
existing [[hittites|Hittite]] coverage (previously built from CAH and laid out
interpretively by [[sources/liverani-ancient-near-east-2014|Liverani]]). Where Liverani
read the Hittites structurally (mode of production, ideology, centre/periphery), Bryce
supplies the **events, persons, campaigns, treaties, and successions** — the narrative
scaffolding the wiki lacked.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Bryce's *Kingdom of the Hittites* enters a wiki whose Hittite coverage was already strong on
**structure** — the CAH-built spine and [[sources/liverani-ancient-near-east-2014|Liverani's]]
interpretive overlay (mode of production, ideology, the demographic/labour trap). What it
supplies is the dimension those left thin: the **continuous political-military narrative**,
reign by reign, person by person, grounded throughout in the Boğazköy tablets. Its single most
consequential contribution is filling the **Empire and Fall** of the [[hittites]] page, which
had explicitly deferred everything after c. 1380 BCE to a CAH cross-reference; the wiki now
carries the seven-reign arc from [[shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma I]] to [[suppiluliuma-ii|Šuppiluliuma II]]
and the first detailed Late-Bronze / ancient-Near-Eastern **biography hub**.

Three interpretive through-lines organise the volume and the pages built from it. First, the
**manufacture of legitimacy**: from Telipinu's edict through Šuppiluliuma's fratricide to
Hattusili III's *Apology*, Hittite kingship is chronically usurped and the central texts are
self-justifications to be read against the grain — a historiographical stance now flagged on
each relevant page. Second, **empire by treaty**: the new [[hittite-vassal-treaties]] concept
page captures the graded suzerain–vassal system and dynastic viceroyalty (at [[carchemish]]/
Aleppo) that let Hatti hold Syria cheaply, and explains both its reach and its fragility at every
succession. Third, **a soft collapse**: Bryce's endorsement of Seeher's *deliberate-abandonment*
reading of [[hattusha|Hattusa]], plus his deflationary "[[sea-peoples|disorganized array]]" and
internal-decline emphasis, aligns closely with Liverani's "nomads as effects" thesis on
[[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|the collapse]] — the two single-author syntheses now reinforce each
other there, differing mainly on the Sea Peoples' provenance (western Anatolia vs. the Balkans).

Where Bryce sits beside the existing spine he is overwhelmingly **corroborative and amplifying**
rather than contradictory: he gives the Hittite-archive face of [[battle-of-qadesh-1274bce|Qadesh]]
(a tactical draw, strategic Hittite win, complete with the deception and the discipline-breakdown
caveat), the mechanics and real motive of the [[egyptian-hittite-treaty-1259bce|Silver Treaty]]
(legitimacy over the Assyrian threat), and a testable political chronology for the
[[ahhiyawa-question|Ahhiyawa question]] (Wilusa = Troy; the erasure of Ahhiyawa from the Great
Kings list tied to the recovery of Milawata). All dates follow his **middle chronology** and are
explicitly provisional — the one standing tension with the wiki's older "high"-chronology
[[actors/shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma]] dating, which is noted on both that page and the biography.
