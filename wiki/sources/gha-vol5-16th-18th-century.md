# UNESCO General History of Africa, Vol. V: Africa from the Sixteenth to the Eighteenth Century

The fifth volume of the **UNESCO *General History of Africa*** (ed. **B. A. Ogot**, 1992) covers the
**early-modern centuries** when the **Atlantic slave trade** reshaped the continent, new **forest and
savanna states** rose (Asante, Oyo, Dahomey, Benin, Luba and Lunda), the **Ottomans** took North Africa and
the **Funj** the Sudan, the **Omani** displaced the Portuguese on the Swahili coast, and the **Great Lakes
kingdoms** (Buganda, Bunyoro, Rwanda) took shape. It is the **early-modern reference for
[[sub-saharan-africa|Gap 2]]**, deepening the wiki's slave-trade and state-level coverage between GHA Vol. IV
(the medieval empires) and Vol. VI (the nineteenth century).

## The volume's argument

Ogot's volume is organized around the **Atlantic slave trade and its consequences**. Its opening chapters
(Inikori, Harris) advance a strong thesis: that the **export slave trade** and the **emergence of the
Atlantic economic order** drained Africa of population and labour and laid foundations for its later
**underdevelopment**, while seeding the **African diaspora** of the Americas. The regional chapters then
show African societies **responding to the Atlantic and Saharan demand** — some states (Asante, Dahomey,
Oyo) rising as militarized trading powers, others (Kongo) hollowed out — on the eve of the nineteenth-century
European intrusion.
