# History: Fiction or Science? (Fomenko New Chronology, 2003 English edition)

## Overview

This 2003 Delamere Resources consolidated edition presents Anatoly T. Fomenko's "New Chronology" project in seven volumes. It is a sustained critique of traditional historical chronology combined with a proposed reconstruction that compresses and shifts most "ancient" and early medieval events into the 11th–17th centuries AD. The work is grounded in Fomenko's background as a professional mathematician specializing in geometry, topology, and dynamical systems; it applies statistical, astronomical, and parallel-analysis techniques originally developed for other domains to historical texts and king-lists.

The central thesis is that the chronology still used for ancient and medieval history (associated with Joseph Scaliger and Denis Pétau in the 16th–17th centuries) contains massive errors, duplications, and deliberate or accidental fabrications. "Ancient" history is argued to be largely a phantom projection of later (primarily medieval and early modern) events. The Bible, classical authors, Egyptian, Roman, Greek, and other "ancient" records are re-dated and re-localized accordingly. A major sub-thesis identifies medieval Russia with the "Horde" and links various "ancient" empires and figures through statistical parallels.

## Publication and Authorship

- Primary author: Anatoly Timofeevich Fomenko (b. 1945), Full Member of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Doctor of Physics and Mathematics, Professor and head of the Mathematics section at Moscow State University. Known for mathematical work on minimal surfaces (Plateau problem), integrable Hamiltonian systems, and topological classification.
- Co-authors for specific volumes: G. V. Nosovskiy, V. V. Kalashnikov.
- English edition: Delamere Resources Ltd, 2003 (ISBN 2-913621-05-8). Translated by Michael Jagger. Based on earlier Russian and some Western academic press editions of the empirico-statistical volumes (Kluwer 1994, CRC 1993, Edwin Mellen 1999).
- The volume presents itself as a systematic, unified reconstruction building on prior books, with added material and reduced repetition.

Fomenko's mathematical publications are listed separately in the front matter; the chronology project is presented as an application of quantitative methods to narrative sources.

## Main Methods

- Empirico-statistical analysis of narrative and dynastic material (volume/length correlations, name-frequency matrices, "double entry" detection in king lists).
- Astronomical retrocalculations (eclipses mentioned in sources; zodiac horoscopes, especially Egyptian temple zodiacs).
- Dynastic parallelism: detection of nearly identical sequences of rulers, events, and durations shifted by centuries.
- Critique of conventional dating methods (radiocarbon calibration issues, dendrochronological "floating" scales before ~10th century, archaeological circularity with Scaligerian dates).
- Textual analysis (consonantal scripts, "R/L" confusions, geographic inversions in medieval maps).

The work repeatedly contrasts its conclusions with the "consensual Scaligerian" framework and presents quantitative tables, graphs, and cross-references to earlier volumes.

## Key Theses (examples; attributed)

- Jesus Christ born ~1053 AD and crucified ~1086 AD.
- The Old Testament largely describes medieval events.
- The Apocalypse was written after 1486 AD.
- Much of "ancient" Rome, Greece, Egypt, and Biblical history is a duplicate or projection of 11th–16th century events (often Russian/Horde/Ataman related).
- Traditional ancient and early medieval chronology contains systematic shifts of 300–1800+ years.
- The Renaissance and "explosion" of classical learning are artifacts of chronological misplacement.

These are presented by the authors as the results of their methods; they are recorded here only as the content of the source.

## Historiography and Reception (per the source and context)

The source positions itself against "clerical chronologers" (Scaliger, Petavius) and earlier critics (Isaac Newton, N. A. Morozov). It claims support from statistical and natural-science methods and presents itself as correcting a "gigantic body of material" in prior volumes.

In the broader scholarly context (not adjudicated here): Fomenko's New Chronology is classified as pseudohistory or fringe chronology revisionism. It has not been accepted by professional historians, classicists, Egyptologists, or archaeologists, who cite contradictions with independent evidence (stratigraphy, calibrated C14, dendro, epigraphy, contemporary documents, genetics, etc.). It has attracted popular attention in Russia and some alternative-history circles.

The source is valuable for the wiki as:
- An example of quantitative/statistical approaches applied to historical texts.
- A detailed, sourced articulation of a radical revisionist position.
- Documentation of one modern reaction to perceived problems in traditional chronology and dating methods.
