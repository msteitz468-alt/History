# Alan Mikhail, *God's Shadow: The Ottoman Sultan Who Shaped the Modern World* (2020)

A popular, provocative biography of **[[selim-i|Selim I]]** ("the Grim," r. 1512–20) framed as
a corrective to the "rise of the West" narrative. Ingested single-pass for Phase C.

Mikhail's two intertwined arguments: (1) **Selim was the single most consequential Ottoman
sultan** — the fratricidal outsider who tripled the empire, made it majority-Muslim, and united
sultanate and caliphate ("there was the empire and world before Selim, and the empire and world
after Selim"); and (2) the **Ottoman Empire "made the modern world"** — its Mediterranean
dominance drove Europeans to the oceans, its threat catalyzed the Reformation, and the
anti-Islamic crusading ideology of the [[reconquista|Reconquista]] was carried by Columbus and
the conquistadors to the Americas. The biography (argument 1) is solid and the basis for
deepening [[selim-i]]; the sweeping global claims (argument 2) are recorded as a contested
thesis on [[ottoman-impact-on-european-expansion]].
