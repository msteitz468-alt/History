# The Aztecs (Frances F. Berdan, 2021)

The wiki's civilizational-depth source for **Mesoamerica**, completing — with D'Altroy's
[[daltroy-incas-2004|Incas]] for the Andes — the two great pre-Columbian empires for the Americas gap.
Berdan's eleven chapters move from the conquest "through Spanish eyes," to who the Aztecs were, building
the empire, making a living in country and city, markets and merchants, nobles and commoners, "to be a
proper Aztec," science and medicine, gods and sacrifice, the conquest from the indigenous side, and the
Aztec legacy.

## Pages

- **Expanded** [[aztec-empire]] — Berdan's account of the Mexica migration from Aztlan, the founding of
  Tenochtitlan (1325), the three strategies of ascent, the Triple Alliance (1428/30) and its six imperial
  rulers, the hegemonic 38-province tribute empire, the culture of warfare and "flowery wars," and the
  conquest from the indigenous vantage (the *tlamatinime* of 1524).
- **Created** [[aztec-religion-and-human-sacrifice]] — the Five Suns cosmology, the layered universe, the
  pantheon (Huitzilopochtli, Quetzalcoatl, Tlaloc, Tezcatlipoca), human sacrifice as reciprocal cosmic
  debt, the Templo Mayor/Coatepec, and the calendar of ceremonies (incl. the 52-year New Fire rite).
- **Created** [[aztec-economy]] — the commodity monies (cacao, *quachtli*, copper axes), the *tianquiztli*
  marketplace (Tlatelolco), and the *pochteca* long-distance merchants who doubled as state agents — the
  commercial counterpoint to the Inca's moneyless [[mita-and-andean-reciprocity|reciprocity economy]].
- **Enriched** [[tenochtitlan]] — the eagle-omen founding, the chinampas and aqueduct, the Templo Mayor,
  and the city as Triple Alliance capital and ritual stage.

## Source Notes

Berdan's distinctive contribution to the wiki is the **economy** (her specialism): the Aztec world emerges
as a fully **commercialised, money-using, market-and-merchant** society — the sharpest possible contrast
with D'Altroy's Inca, where exchange ran on labour-tribute and redistribution *without* markets or money.
Read together, the two civilizational-depth sources establish that the Americas hosted **two opposite
economic solutions** at imperial scale, both arrived at independently of the Old World — reinforcing the
hemispheric picture of Mann's [[pre-columbian-americas|*1491*]]. Berdan also models the
**source-critical, object-first** method (each chapter opening from a codex or artefact), and deliberately
gives the **indigenous** experience of the conquest its own chapter — setting up the conquest primaries
(**[[broken-spears|The Broken Spears]]** and **Bernal Díaz**) next in the Americas cluster. As a concise
synthesis it is light on the post-conquest colonial centuries (covered later by Burkholder & Johnson) and
on the contested **scale of human sacrifice**, which it treats judiciously (the cosmology is secure; the
largest Spanish-reported numbers are flagged as probable propaganda).
