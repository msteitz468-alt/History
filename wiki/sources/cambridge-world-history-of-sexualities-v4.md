# The Cambridge World History of Sexualities, Vol. 4: Modern Sexualities (2024)

The final volume carries the [[history-of-sexuality|history of sexuality]] into the
**modern world**, where sexuality was transformed by capitalism, the state, science,
mass media, and revolutionary social movements — and where it became a central axis of
political conflict.

## What it contributes

- **[[modern-sexuality]]** — the process page: the modern transformation of sexuality
  (medicalization, commodification, reproductive control, the World Wars, rights and
  backlash).
- **[[sexual-revolution]]** — the post-war liberalization of sexual norms.
- Enriches **[[history-of-sexuality]]** (the modern endpoint).

## Argument

The volume stresses that modern sexuality is inseparable from **capitalism and
consumerism** — sex became a commodity and commodities erotic ("parasexuality": desire
"deployed but contained" to sell goods) — and from the **modern state's** projects of
**eugenics, public health, and reproductive control**. It treats the late-20th-century
**[[sexual-revolution]]** with a critical eye, "challenging liberal assumptions about
agency, liberation, and progress," and closes on the contemporary politicization of
sexuality and its backlash.
