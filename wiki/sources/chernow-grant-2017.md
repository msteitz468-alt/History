# Grant (Ron Chernow, 2017)

Definitive modern biography of Ulysses S. Grant (1822–1885) by Ron Chernow. 2017. One of the most substantial single-volume lives, restoring Grant as the indispensable military leader who won the Civil War through persistent, coordinated grand strategy and as the president who made the most sustained effort of any 19th-century chief executive to secure civil and political rights for the freed slaves during Reconstruction.

Chernow frames the life with Grant's final heroic act: writing the *Personal Memoirs* while dying of throat cancer in 1885, aided by Mark Twain's publishing firm. The book systematically dismantles the Gilded Age caricature of Grant as a plodding butcher and habitual drunk, while giving full weight to his prewar failures, his alcoholism (a solitary binge pattern), political naiveté, and the scandals that marred his presidency. Its greatest contribution is the integrated treatment of Grant as the figure who linked the two acts of the Civil War/Reconstruction drama: victory in the field and the attempt to make black citizenship real.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Chernow’s 2017 biography integrates the prewar “lost years,” the military masterpiece at Vicksburg, the 1864 grand strategy of simultaneous advances, the generous Appomattox terms, and—most importantly—the sustained presidential effort to translate battlefield victory into enforceable black citizenship through the Klan Act, Justice Department prosecutions, the Fifteenth Amendment, and the 1875 Civil Rights Act. The work reframes Grant’s drinking as a chronic, manageable binge pattern rather than moral failing; documents his personal naiveté in business and politics (Grant & Ward, Black Friday, Babcock); and presents the final illness and *Memoirs* as the capstone of character. It aligns with and deepens prior ACW operational sources while supplying the missing high-resolution actor-level treatment of Grant as the figure who bridged military and Reconstruction phases. Collection note: the source fills a documented gap in post-1865 US political coverage.

## Collection Coverage Note

Chernow provides the richest single modern narrative synthesis of Grant's military and presidential careers available in the collection for the Long 19th Century US. The Bibliotheca Alexandrina is strong on the military history of the Civil War (Hess, Hagerman, Hattaway-Jones, Weigley, West Point atlases) but thinner on Grant's presidency and Reconstruction politics prior to this source. Chernow's use of the Grant Papers fills a critical gap for actor-level depth on the central figure who bridged battlefield victory and the attempt to realize the war's emancipatory promise. Note the broader collection bias against sustained post-1865 US political and social history outside military topics.
