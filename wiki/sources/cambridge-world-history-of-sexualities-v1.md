# The Cambridge World History of Sexualities, Vol. 1: General Overviews (2024)

The first volume of a four-volume global reference, ingested (per the user's request, one
volume at a time) to give the wiki a proper analytic treatment of the **history of
sexuality**. Volume 1 is the *meta* volume — the field's methods, key theorists, and
thematic building blocks.

## What it contributes

- **[[history-of-sexuality]]** — the concept page: the emergence of the field, the
  Freud/Foucault/queer-theory lineage, and above all the **social-constructionist vs.
  essentialist** debate (Foucault's thesis that "sexuality" is a modern construct).
- Enriches **[[gender-history]]** (the closely related analytic).

## Argument

The volume's organizing insight, descending from **Michel Foucault**, is that **"sexuality"
is not a timeless natural fact but a historically and culturally constructed category** —
so the historian's task is to recover the very different ways past societies organized
bodies, desires, acts, and identities, resisting the anachronistic projection of modern
labels (gay, straight, heterosexual) onto the past. Its thematic chapters then show how
sexuality intersects with women's history, race, class, religion, law, medicine, art, and
violence across world history.
