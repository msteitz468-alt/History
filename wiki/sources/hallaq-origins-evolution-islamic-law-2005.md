to his [[hallaq-sharia-2009|Sharīʿa]]. Because the Sharīʿa ingest already created the formative-period

## Volume Synthesis Note

A focused primary-history account of how Islamic law was made, deepening the formative-period backbone the
[[hallaq-sharia-2009|Sharīʿa]] ingest sketched. It traces the arc from [[pre-islamic-near-eastern-law|the
pre-Islamic legal substrate]] and [[quranic-law|Quranic legislation]], through the
[[islamic-legal-ethic|socialization of a legal ethic]], the [[proto-qadi|proto-qāḍīs]] and
[[early-islamic-judiciary|professionalizing judiciary]], the rise of [[prophetic-authority-in-law|Prophetic
authority]] and ḥadīth over [[ahl-al-ray|raʾy]], and the [[rise-of-legal-theory|formation of legal theory]]
(which Hallaq credits to [[ibn-surayj|Ibn Surayj]]'s circle rather than al-Shāfiʿī), to the
[[madhhab-formation|formation of the doctrinal schools]] and the [[great-synthesis-islamic-law|"great
synthesis"]] of traditionalism and rationalism. Its central analytical claim — that legal authority was
[[epistemic-authority-islamic-law|epistemic, not political]] — grounds Hallaq's larger account of Islamic
law as a non-state system, complementing the [[destruction-of-the-sharia-thesis|destruction thesis]] of the
Sharīʿa volume.
