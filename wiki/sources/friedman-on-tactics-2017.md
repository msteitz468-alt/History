# B. A. Friedman, *On Tactics: A Theory of Victory in Battle* (2017)

A concise, practitioner-focused theory that supplies the missing organizing structure for tactics. Friedman argues that while strategic theory is well-developed (Clausewitz et al.), tactics remains chaotic—reduced to lists of principles that are often contradictory or misapplied. He rectifies this by defining nine Tactical Tenets and five Tactical Concepts, grounded in history, and explicitly links tactics to strategy as its "base and servant."

This is a key Tier 3 source for doctrinal clarity in the [[hubs/warfare/warfare-hub|warfare hub]], particularly the tactics sub-hub, and for bridging [[hubs/warfare/strategy|strategy]] and battle analysis.

## Historiography / impact note
Friedman writes as a serving tactician frustrated by the gap between strategic theory and usable tactical thought. The work is deliberately short and accessible, avoiding the bloat of "principles of war" lists. It explicitly positions tactics within Clausewitz’s framework while insisting on its distinct but subordinate nature. Valuable for the wiki because it offers a clean, modern taxonomy that can structure battle analyses (apply the tenets to Cannae, Breitenfeld, Leuthen, etc.) and clarify the tactics–strategy link without technology determinism. Limitations: U.S./Western practitioner lens; limited non-Western examples; "timeless" claim will be tested against specific historical cases in the hub.

## Primary Claims from the Text (Grounded Extractions)

All claims are verbatim-grounded exclusively from the source text (2017 Naval Institute Press ed.). Organized by the book’s structure with locators (Preface, Chapter/section, Appendix). The work supplies a unified three-plane (physical/mental/moral) system of 9 Tactical Tenets whose cumulative effects target moral cohesion, explicitly subordinated to and linked with strategy.

### Theory Foundations & Purpose (Preface + Ch. 1–2)
- **Purpose of the work**: "Unlike strategy itself, there is no organizing structure such as that provided by Carl von Clausewitz’s *On War* (1976/1832). This work is an attempt to provide that structure or at least the beginning of one." Theory fulfills Clausewitz’s main task: "Theory will have fulfilled its main task it used to analyze the constituent elements of war, to distinguish precisely what at first sight seems fused, to explain in full the properties of the means employed and to show their probable effects, to define clearly the nature of the ends in view, and to illuminate all phases of warfare in a thorough critical inquiry."
- **Art vs. science**: "It is said that war is both an art and a science. The tactician knows he is an artist. The inherent probability and chance of warfare and the pervasive moral factors defy the scientist’s methods. ... the application of that science takes an artist’s mind."
- **Tactics as subset of strategy with interdependence**: "Due to the nature of tactics as simultaneously the base of and servant to strategy, it is impossible to construct or discuss a theory of tactics without strategy. Strategic ideas are thus interspersed throughout the text..."
- **The 9 Tenets system (core framework)**: "The intent of this tactical system is that the tactician arranges the physical means at his disposal in terms of maneuver, mass, firepower, and tempo to inflict mental effects in the mind of the opposing tactician and his units: deception, surprise, confusion, and shock. The accumulation of these mental effects will at some point overwhelm the enemy unit, at which point they will lose moral cohesion." "Victory in the narrow scope of this work... is usually achieved when the enemy force has lost its moral cohesion and fled..."

### Tactical Tenets
**Physical (Ch. 3–6)**  
- **Maneuver**: "Maneuver can be defined as attacking an enemy force from a position of comparative advantage." Forms include flanking, envelopment, turning movement, infiltration, swarming. "It depends on three other tactical tenets though: a higher tempo used to gain local superiority (mass) at that decisive point, with sufficient firepower..." Historical: Alexander, Napoleon (manoeuvre de derrière), Lee at Chancellorsville, infiltration/blitzkrieg, 2003 Iraq.
- **Mass**: "Mass is an advantageous concentration of combat power in space and/or time." Clausewitz: "the most common element in victory" but "at a vital or decisive point." "Most importantly, though, these tactical tenets are not mutually exclusive."
- **Firepower**: "Firepower is the ability to apply ranged weapons at an advantage against enemy forces." Rarely decisive alone; best combined ("combined arms"). "In combat, both sides seek to employ maneuver to mass firepower... victory will usually go to the side that can do it first and most often."
- **Tempo**: "Tempo is the ability to control the pace of combat to your advantage and the disadvantage of the enemy." Boyd OODA/friction exploitation. Sequential vs. cumulative (Wylie). "one slow, four quick."

**Mental (Ch. 7–10)**  
- **Deception**: "Deception is the manipulation of the enemy’s understanding of the situation in order to achieve an advantageous situation." Sun Tzu: cheng (normal/direct) + ch’i (extraordinary). "Any time the enemy is deceived, it produces mental shock..."
- **Surprise**: "Surprise in combat is the act of presenting your enemy with a situation or capability for which he is mentally unprepared." "surprise = 2,000:1 numerical success rate" in studies. Tet 1968 example.
- **Confusion**: "Confusion in combat is a state of mental overload or disarray that makes it difficult both to react to events and understand the situation." "Any methods that corrupt, disrupt, or overwhelm the enemy’s command-and-control system and sow confusion... are as potent a weapon as surprise and deception."
- **Shock**: "the shock effect is a state of psychological overload caused by the sudden, unexpected, or successive action of the enemy." "psychological result of 'speed and focus'." Bayonet charges, tanks, air/artillery barrages (Jünger WWI quote), Mohi Bridge 1241.

**Moral (Ch. 11)**  
- **Moral Cohesion**: "The moral element in warfare, then, is a combination of morale, cohesion, ethics and morality, and leadership. Combined, it can be termed 'moral cohesion.'... The important part for the tactician is simply the recognition of the moral forces at play in battle and their potential to trump physical and mental forces..." "In war there are the two factors—human beings and weapons. Ultimately, though, human beings are the decisive factor." Target of all other tenets: "The sum total of physical and mental effects can overcome an enemy’s moral capacity to endure and fight back."

### Tactical Concepts (Part II)
- **Culminating Point of Victory**: "the point where the offense becomes the defense... also a point of vulnerability." "This culminating point of victory is bound to recur in every future war in which the destruction of the enemy cannot be the military aim." (Clausewitz). "as a military force achieves victories it expends potential combat power..."
- **Offense, Defense, and Initiative**: "Defense has a passive purpose: preservation; and attack a positing one: conquest." "Time which is allowed to pass unused accumulates to the credit of the defender." Initiative not exclusive to offense.
- **Command and Control**: Mission-type orders ("what to do, but not how"); commander’s intent, main effort (Schwerpunkt), reserve. "The advantage which a commander thinks he can attain through continued personal intervention is largely illusory." (Moltke).
- **Environment and Geography**: Pervasive; "Know the ground, know the weather; your victory will then be total." (Sun Tzu). Affects all tenets (e.g., Chinese 1950 Korea infiltration via terrain + weather).
- **Linking Tactics with Strategy ("Crossing the Bridge")**: "Strategy is a two-way thoroughfare..." "A true tactical victory is won when the cumulative physical and mental means of one side shatters the moral cohesion of the other... This, however, should not be taken to mean that the shattering of moral cohesion is sufficient... the tactician will further seek through exploitation... for the purpose of the war." Must affect the enemy’s center of gravity ("the hub of all power and movement, on which everything depends").

### Strategy Dichotomies & Critiques (Appendices + Conclusion)
- **Clausewitz offense/defense & culminating point in practice**: Vietnam example—U.S. strategic offense without decisiveness led to culmination and withdrawal despite tactical successes.
- **Delbrück annihilation vs. exhaustion**: Annihilation seeks decisive battle; exhaustion uses economy of force, dispersion, ambuscades. Napoleon in Russia pursued annihilation against Russian exhaustion strategy and failed.
- **Wylie sequential vs. cumulative**: Sequential = interdependent steps (centralized); cumulative = disconnected actions (decentralized). Insurgencies often cumulative.
- **Strategic (not tactical) asymmetry / parallel strategies**: "The perceived asymmetry in tactics... is not the important characteristic. Rather, it is the asymmetry in the chosen strategy..." "What is of supreme importance in war is to attack the enemy’s strategy." (Sun Tzu). Washington/Greene in American Revolution; Mao.
- **Training & Education (App. E)**: "battles are not won on the battlefield, but rather in training." "tactics must always serve—or else they harm—strategy." U.S. system lacks formal closed feedback loop (collect-analyze-codify-train-execute); "The solution is to develop a training, doctrine, intelligence, and education system that functions in and of itself as a feedback loop."
- **Organization of Successful Militaries (App. F)**: Combined arms, adaptability, decentralized command (Auftragstaktik), clear-eyed assessment after defeat. 5 conclusions: 1. Combined arms ideal for mass + tempo. 2. Combine mass/firepower at fast tempo. 3. Adopt innovations (even enemy’s). 4. Honest self-assessment. 5. Decentralized command + highly trained/educated leaders.

### Glossary (Author’s Working Definitions)
- maneuver: Attacking an enemy force from a position of comparative advantage.
- mass: An advantageous concentration of combat power in space and/or time.
- firepower: The ability to apply ranged weapons at an advantage against enemy forces.
- tempo: The ability to control the pace of combat to your advantage and the disadvantage of the enemy.
- deception: The manipulation of the enemy’s understanding of the situation in order to achieve a situation more advantageous to yourself.
- surprise: The act of presenting your enemy with a situation or capability for which he is mentally unprepared.
- confusion: A state of mental overload or disarray that makes it difficult to react to events and understand the situation.
- shock: A state of psychological overload caused by the sudden or unexpected action of the enemy.
- moral cohesion: The state of connectives, community, and camaraderie in a military unit produced by the combination of the morale, effectiveness, ethics, professionalism, and dedication of its members and the ability of its leaders.
- center of gravity: The binding or animating force of a strategic actor, connected to its political aim or aims.
- strategy: The use of violent means in ways that achieve the policy end state over the long term.
- tactics: The use of military forces to achieve victory over opposing enemy forces over the short term.
- tenet: Tenet as used here means a main tactical concept that is not necessarily as strong as a principle or law.
