# The Bāburnāma (Memoirs of Babur, c. 1530; Beveridge trans.)

The first-person memoir of **[[babur|Babur]]**, founder of the [[mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]] —
the earliest true autobiography in Islamic literature and a primary source of extraordinary
vividness for Central Asia and India on the eve of the Mughal conquest.

## What it is

Babur wrote the *Bāburnāma* in his native **Chaghatai Turkish** (not Persian, the court language
his descendants would adopt). The text survives with gaps and is organised as annals of his life
in three theatres — **Fergana and Transoxiana** (the losing struggle for Timur's Samarkand against
the Uzbeks), **Kabul** (his base from 1504), and **Hindustan** (the conquest from 1526). Beveridge's
1921 English version, made from the Turki, remains the standard scholarly translation.

## Distinctive content

- **Candour.** Babur records his defeats, his drinking parties and his later renunciation of wine,
  his griefs and friendships, and moral judgements on himself and others — a startlingly personal
  voice for a sixteenth-century king.
- **Observation.** The memoir is full of precise **natural history and geography** — the fruits and
  melons of Bukhara and Samarkand, the wines of the Kabul valleys, birds, animals, revenues, and
  peoples — making it a major ethnographic and economic source.
- **The famous verdict on India.** Babur's ambivalent first impression of his new dominion is one
  of the most quoted passages in Indian historiography: *"Hindūstān is a country of few charms…
  there are no good horses, no good dogs, no grapes, musk-melons or first-rate fruits, no ice or
  cold water, no good bread or cooked food in the bāzārs, no hot-baths, no Colleges, no candles,
  torches or candlesticks."* He then balances it with India's real advantages — its vast
  population, gold, and wealth — explaining why he stayed.

## What it contributes

- Grounds the **[[babur]]** page in his own words (Fergana → Kabul → Panipat; the conquest of
  Hindustan; the verdict on India).
- A primary-source anchor for the founding of the [[mughal-empire|Mughal Empire]], complementing
  the dynastic narrative of the [[cambridge-history-of-india-v4|Cambridge History of India, Vol.
  IV]].
