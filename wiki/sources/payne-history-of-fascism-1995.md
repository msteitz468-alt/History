# Payne, *A History of Fascism, 1914–1945* (1995)

Stanley G. Payne's comprehensive narrative and comparative analysis of the
fascist era — the standard scholarly one-volume history against which the
contested/advocacy sources in the current batch
([[dsouza-big-lie-2017]], [[flynn-as-we-go-marching-1944]],
[[hayek-road-to-serfdom-1944]]) are to be balanced, and the narrative
companion to [[paxton-anatomy-of-fascism-2004]]'s process model. Part I
narrates every significant fascist and para-fascist movement from the fin de
siècle to 1945; Part II surveys interpretations and defends a "retrodictive
theory" of the conditions that produced fascism.

## Volume Assessment

The standard single-volume scholarly treatment: a complete narrative of
every fascist and para-fascist movement plus a disciplined comparative
apparatus. Its distinctive contributions to the wiki are (1) the
three-sector typology separating fascism from the radical and conservative
right — the structural rebuttal to one-axis spectrum arguments; (2) the
demonstration that the commonest fate of fascist movements was suppression
by the authoritarian right (the preemption paradox); (3) the retrodictive
theory of fascistogenic preconditions; and (4) carefully hedged
comparativism on the Nazi–Communist parallel. It balances the batch's
advocacy sources: where [[flynn-as-we-go-marching-1944|Flynn]] defines
fascism economically, Payne's corporatism is epiphenomenal ("comparatively
little direct economic power"); where [[hayek-road-to-serfdom-1944|Hayek]]
makes fascism a species of collectivism, Payne documents both the leftist
founding cadre *and* the rightist alliances and anti-Socialist violence.
Read with [[paxton-anatomy-of-fascism-2004]] (process model vs. Payne's
typology — Paxton's own verdict: "describes better than it explains") and
against [[dsouza-big-lie-2017]] and the Position-2 literature at
[[fascism-and-the-political-left]].
