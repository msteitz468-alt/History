# The Tale of Genji (Waley translation, 1925–33)

The wiki's ingest of the **central work of Japanese literature** — Murasaki Shikibu's
*Genji Monogatari* — the primary text that the cultural histories of
[[mason-caiger-history-of-japan|Mason]] and [[sansom-short-cultural-history-japan|Sansom]]
repeatedly invoke, now read directly. It is the supreme literary expression of
[[heian-court-culture|Heian court culture]].

## What it is

A long Heian court romance in **54 chapters**, following **Prince Genji** ("the Shining
One") and, after his death, his descendants, through a world of love, politics, exile, and
the pathos of transience. Its analysis as a literary work is at **[[tale-of-genji]]**; its
author at **[[murasaki-shikibu]]**. The famous opening (the *Kiritsubo* chapter) — the
emperor's doomed love for Genji's low-born mother, hounded to death by jealous rivals — sets
the tone of beauty shadowed by sorrow.

## Why it matters to the wiki

- It is the **primary witness** to the Heian aristocracy's inner life — manners, poetry,
  religion, and the aesthetic of ***mono no aware*** — that the [[heian-court-culture]] and
  [[heian-period]] pages describe at second hand.
- As the **world's first psychological novel** (Waley's claim), it is Japan's foremost
  contribution to world literature and a landmark in the global history of the form.
- It illuminates the [[murasaki-shikibu|extraordinary women's literature]] of the age and the
  [[fujiwara-clan|Fujiwara]] court of Michinaga in which it was written.
