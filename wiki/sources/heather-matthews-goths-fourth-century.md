# The Goths in the Fourth Century

A collection of the key contemporary documents on the **Goths** in the century before the catastrophe
of **[[battle-of-adrianople-378|Adrianople (378)]]** — their society, their conversion, and their
fateful entry into the Roman Empire. Edited with major interpretive introductions by **Peter Heather**
and **John Matthews**. The third source of this Phase-5 batch.

## The Texts

- **The Passion of St Saba** — the martyr-act of a Gothic Christian drowned by his own people in 372
  during the persecution of the chieftain **Athanaric**. A rare, vivid window onto **Gothic village
  society from within**: the *megistanes* (chiefs), the village assemblies, the pressure on Christians to
  eat sacrificial meat, and the texture of life among the Tervingi.
- **The Ulfila dossier** — the materials on **[[ulfila|Ulfila (Wulfila)]]**, the "apostle of the Goths,"
  centred on the **letter of Auxentius of Durostorum**, his pupil: Ulfila's career, his Homoian
  ("Arian") faith, and the conversion of the Goths.
- **The Gothic Bible materials** — a presentation of the **Gothic alphabet** Ulfila devised and the
  vocabulary of his Bible translation, the **earliest substantial Germanic text**.
- **Themistius, Orations** — the orator-statesman's speeches justifying the **Roman settlement of the
  Goths** and imperial policy under Valens.

## Significance

These sources document two developments of the first importance. First, the **conversion of the Goths
to Arian Christianity** through Ulfila — which made the Germanic successor peoples (Goths, Vandals,
Burgundians, Lombards) **Arians**, religiously divided from their Catholic Roman subjects for centuries.
Second, the **crisis of 376–378**: the Tervingi under **Fritigern**, pressed by the **Huns**, crossing
the Danube into the Empire, the mismanagement and famine that drove them to revolt, and the destruction
of the emperor **[[valentinian-and-valens|Valens]]** and his army at **Adrianople** — the event that
opened the late-Roman West to barbarian settlement. Heather and Matthews's volume is the documentary
backbone of the influential modern reassessment of Gothic ethnogenesis and the "barbarian migrations."
