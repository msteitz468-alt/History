# The Origins of the Second World War (A. J. P. Taylor)

A. J. P. Taylor's *The Origins of the Second World War* (1961) is the most **controversial** book ever
written on its subject — a revisionist account that deliberately broke with the post-Nuremberg consensus
that the war was the planned achievement of Hitler's will. Fifteenth source of **Gap 1 (Modern)**, and
the anchoring dissent on the **[[causes-of-the-second-world-war|WWII-origins controversy]]**.

## The Argument

Taylor treats the diplomacy of the 1930s as **ordinary great-power statecraft**, not as the unfolding of
a *Mein Kampf* blueprint. His provocations:

- **Hitler as opportunist.** Far from executing a master plan, Hitler was (Taylor argues) an
  **improviser** who exploited openings others created — "no more wicked than the other statesmen of his
  day," a claim that outraged readers.
- **Versailles and the system.** The war grew from the **unstable settlement of 1919** and the failure of
  the powers to either enforce or revise it — a structural, not purely intentional, origin.
- **Appeasement reconsidered.** Munich and appeasement were, in Taylor's telling, comprehensible
  *Realpolitik* rather than simple cowardice or betrayal.
- **War by miscalculation.** The actual outbreak in 1939 owed much to **blunder and accident** on all
  sides, not to a German timetable for world conquest.

## Significance and Reception

The book provoked a **furious scholarly backlash** (Trevor-Roper above all) for appearing to **normalize
Hitler** and to ignore the centrality of **Nazi racial ideology** and the project of a war of annihilation
in the East. Yet it permanently reshaped the field: it forced historians to take **structure,
contingency, and the diplomatic record** seriously alongside ideology, and it remains the indispensable
counter-pole in the debate. The wiki records it as the classic **revisionist** position, balanced by the
intentionalist mainstream, on the [[causes-of-the-second-world-war|controversy page]].
