# Payne, *Civil War in Europe, 1905–1949* (2011)

The first comprehensive account of the civil wars in Europe during the era of the world wars. Payne treats the initial confrontations before WWI, the concept of "European civil war," the impact of the world wars, the relation between revolution and civil war, and individual cases with special attention to Russia and Spain. Compares and contrasts with earlier internal conflicts, focusing on factors that made this era unusually violent and what brought it to an end. Major political, ideological, and social influences treated, with focus on violence against civilians.

## Note on Division of Labor

This source provides the essential comparative framework for the "era of civil wars" in Europe 1905–1949. It complements specialist monographs on individual conflicts (e.g., existing Spanish and Russian coverage) by supplying the big-picture analysis of why this period produced so many internal wars and what ended the pattern. Military details of specific campaigns belong in warfare hub or event pages; Payne excels at typology, foreign intervention, violence against civilians, and ideological drivers. ## Historiography Note (on source page)

Payne positions the work against both Marxist "class war" interpretations and liberal narratives that minimize internal violence. He insists on distinguishing full civil war (extended contest for state power) from mere political violence or insurrections. The book draws heavily on his own Spanish research and syntheses the literature on Russia and other cases. Strengths: clear typology, attention to foreign intervention as escalator, honest treatment of atrocities on all sides. Limitations: lighter on social/economic structures than some specialists; conservative skepticism toward revolutionary legitimacy. Excellent complement to more narrative or military-focused sources. Gaps in collection prior to this ingest were mainly in the comparative "European civil war" framing and coverage of non-Spanish/Russian cases (Finland, Baltics, Greece, Yugoslavia as WWII civil wars).

## Volume Synthesis Note (to be completed after extraction)

Payne argues that the first half of the twentieth century saw an unprecedented clustering of revolutionary and civil conflicts in Europe, driven by the interaction of rapid modernization, competing radical ideologies (communism, fascism, anarchism), the trauma of world war and defeat, and the breakdown of traditional institutions. The pattern ended after 1945 due to the exhaustion of revolutionary ideologies, economic stabilization, social democracy in the West, totalitarianism's monopoly on violence in the East, and the nuclear/Cold War environment that discouraged internal explosions on the old scale. The Spanish Civil War is treated as the most complex and "classic" case, combining all the era's features. The work challenges both the uniqueness of the Spanish case and any simple "European civil war" master narrative while providing the tools to analyze it.
