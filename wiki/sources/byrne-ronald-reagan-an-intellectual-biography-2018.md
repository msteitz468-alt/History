# Ronald Reagan: An Intellectual Biography — David T. Byrne (2018)

One-volume intellectual biography (Potomac Books / Univ. of Nebraska Press, 2018) arguing that Ronald Reagan (1911–2004) was a serious thinker whose cosmology of faith and freedom shaped both his domestic and foreign policy. Byrne rejects portrayals of Reagan as intellectually shallow (“amiable dunce”) and places him in the longer Western tradition: Christian (especially evangelical Protestant optimism and universalism), Enlightenment liberalism of freedom (appropriated for conservatism), and lessons of appeasement/WWII. Core thesis: Reagan reconciled the medieval “Kingdom of God” with a modern “Kingdom of Freedom” and made the spread of freedom— at home through limited government and abroad by confronting tyranny—the central moral and practical project of his career.

## Key Claims & Themes (to be expanded via subagents)

- Reagan as “idea man” and synthesizer: combined Christian universalism/Kingdom of God, Enlightenment freedom (highest political value), and WWII appeasement lesson into a coherent cosmology of actively spreading freedom globally and limiting government domestically. Freedom trumps equality, sometimes peace.
- Religious formation decisive: optimistic evangelicalism (no hell, human agency + providence, “Kingdom of Freedom” on earth); mother Nelle central; *That Printer of Udell* triggered baptism and lifelong role-modeling of Christian action in the world.
- Shift from liberal (FDR admirer, union president) to conservative driven by anti-communism (Chambers *Witness*), experience of Hollywood left, GE tours revealing government overreach; 1964 “A Time for Choosing” as foundational text.
- Domestic freedom: tax rate cuts (top marginal), welfare reform (later Clinton), skepticism of Social Security as currently structured, school choice/vouchers, states’ rights; government creates dependency; private/faith-based better for poverty.
- Cold War as moral struggle: “evil empire” rhetoric not rhetoric but core belief; SDI as moral/technological path to end nuclear terror and undermine Soviet economy; support for anti-communist resistance (Solidarity, mujahedeen, Contras) as duty to spread freedom, not mere containment.
- Reagan not original but significant synthesizer and actor: ideas derivative (Smith, Hayek, Friedman, Tocqueville, founding fathers, Chambers) yet applied with consistency and effect; changed trajectory of American conservatism and contributed to end of Cold War.
- Inconsistencies and limits acknowledged (deficits grew; some policies deviated from pre-presidential writings) but do not negate intellectual seriousness.
