# CAH Vol. XIII — The Late Empire, A.D. 337–425 (Cameron & Garnsey eds., 1998)

The direct sequel to [[bowman-cah-v12-2005|CAH XII]], the **first of the two volumes added to the new
edition** to carry the series into full **Late Antiquity**. Where CAH XII narrated the third-century
crisis and the Diocletianic–Constantinian refounding, CAH XIII covers the **long fourth century** — from
the death of [[constantine-the-great|Constantine]] (337) to c. 425 — the period in which the now-Christian
empire reached a cultural zenith even as its western half began to fragment. Its spine: the **dynastic
narrative** (the sons of Constantine; [[julian|Julian's]] pagan reaction; the Pannonian emperors; the
**Gothic war and Adrianople**; [[theodosius-i|Theodosius I]] and the final **division of 395**; the German
onslaught, **Alaric**, and the **sack of Rome in 410**); the mature **late-Roman state** (bureaucracy,
senate, army, and the **church as a public institution**); the **economy and society** of the period
(deliberately *against* the "decline" model); the transformed **barbarian world** (ethnogenesis, Goths,
Huns, the first settlements); the **religious revolution** (the attrition of polytheism, the Arian
controversy, asceticism, Christianization); and the **literary and artistic culture** (Greek, Latin,
Syriac and Coptic). The interpretive frame throughout is the **Jones/Brown transformation** of the field:
late antiquity as a vigorous, creative age of transition, not simply Rome's "decline and fall."

### Notes on cross-volume continuity
- Picks up directly from [[bowman-cah-v12-2005|CAH XII]] at the death of [[constantine-the-great|Constantine]]
  (337); carries the [[late-roman-army]], [[late-roman-administration]], [[late-roman-cities]],
  [[late-roman-economy]], and [[late-roman-coinage]] pages forward into their mature fourth-century form.
- Continues the **non-Roman world** pages into the fourth century: the [[sassanid-empire|Sassanids]]
  (Shapur II's wars, the treaty of 363), [[armenia|Armenia]], the [[arab-desert-peoples|Arabs]], and the
  [[germanic-peoples|Germanic peoples]] — now with the decisive **Hunnic** irruption and the Gothic
  settlement.
- Continues the **religious** story: [[late-polytheism]] into its attrition and Julian's failed revival;
  [[third-century-christianity]] forward through the **Arian controversy**, the rise of monasticism, and
  the Christianization of public life; [[christianization-of-the-roman-empire|Christianization]] becomes
  central.
- Major creations expected: the **dynasty of Constantine** (Constantius II, Constans) and **Julian**; the
  **Pannonian emperors** (Valentinian I, Valens); the **battle of Adrianople (378)**; **Theodosius I** and
  the **division of 395**; **Alaric** and the **sack of Rome (410)**; the **Huns** and the Gothic
  *ethnogenesis*; **Ambrose of Milan**; the **Arian controversy** and the Council of Constantinople (381);
  **monasticism/asceticism** (Antony, Pachomius); and the late-antique literary/artistic culture (Syriac,
  Coptic, Nag Hammadi).
