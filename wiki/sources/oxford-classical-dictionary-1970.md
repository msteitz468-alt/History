# The Oxford Classical Dictionary (2nd Edition, 1970) — N. G. L. Hammond and H. H. Scullard (eds.)

The standard English-language one-volume dictionary of the classical (primarily Graeco-Roman) world. It provides concise, scholarly entries on a wide range of topics: historical figures, mythological characters, cities and regions, political and social institutions, literature, philosophy, religion, art, architecture, and daily life.

This is a **reference work**, not a continuous narrative history. Its value to the wiki is as a factual lookup tool for the ancient Mediterranean, helping to standardize names, dates, identifications, and basic descriptions in period, event, actor, place, and concept pages for Classical Antiquity and adjacent periods.

## Structure and Coverage

The work is arranged alphabetically (A–Z entries, with cross-references). It covers:

- Greek world: Archaic to Hellenistic periods (city-states, philosophy, tragedy, democracy, Sparta, Athens, Alexander, etc.)
- Roman world: Republic to early Empire (institutions, law, military, literature, emperors, provinces)
- Mythology, religion, and cult
- Material culture and archaeology (limited)
- Some entries on neighboring cultures (Persia, Carthage, Etruscans, etc.) as they interact with the classical world

No "Section Plan" in the narrative sense; it is a dictionary. Major thematic clusters include:

- Persons (rulers, philosophers, poets, generals)
- Places (poleis, regions, battle sites)
- Institutions (polis, republic, senate, assemblies)
- Concepts (democracy, philosophy schools, stoicism, epicureanism)
- Texts and authors (Homer, Herodotus, Thucydides, Virgil, Tacitus, etc.)

## Historiography / Collection Note

The 2nd edition (1970) reflects mid-20th-century scholarship. Later editions (3rd 1996, 4th 2012) incorporate major updates from new evidence and approaches; this version should be used with awareness of its date. It remains a convenient starting point and is widely cited.
