# The Vietnam War: A Military History (Wawro, 2024)

Geoffrey Wawro's *The Vietnam War: A Military History* is a 2024 single-volume operational and strategic narrative that traces the entire military course of the American war in Vietnam from the Kennedy/Johnson escalation through the final North Vietnamese "Ho Chi Minh Campaign" and the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975. As a professional military historian, Wawro focuses on the mechanics of operations, the impact of technology (especially airmobility and airpower), command decisions at multiple levels, and the persistent gap between tactical/operational success and strategic/political outcomes.

The book is organized in 26 chapters plus Introduction and Conclusion, with chapter titles often drawn from contemporary slogans or turning points ("Parody of High Strategy," "Ia Drang," "Tet," "Khe Sanh," "Hamburger Hill," "The Fall"). It includes dedicated maps for the major campaigns (Cedar Falls/Junction City, Tet, Lam Son 719, Easter Offensive, final offensives) and treats both the "big war" of main-force units and the political constraints that shaped US and South Vietnamese choices.

## What it contributes

- Detailed operational accounts of signature engagements suitable for high-resolution warfare-hub analysis: the Ia Drang campaign (first major test of the 1st Cavalry Division airmobile concept), the Tet Offensive (scope, urban fighting at Hue, intelligence failures), the siege of Khe Sanh, Hamburger Hill (May 1969), the Cambodian incursion, and the 1975 final conventional offensives.
- Emphasis on the political origins and constraints: Vietnam as "a war of choice" driven by domestic fear of appearing weak (Kennedy, Johnson, Nixon), incrementalism, and the mismatch between military means and achievable political ends.
- Attention to ARVN performance and Vietnamization tests (including context for Lam Son 719 and An Loc from other sources).
- Integration of airpower, logistics, and technology as force multipliers and their limits in a hybrid environment.
- Critical perspective on the attrition strategy's costs and the "search and avoid" dynamics of the later war without flattening into simple myth or revision.
- Strong complement to the prior Vietnam strategy/operational ingests (Summers, Daddis, Willbanks, Krepinevich, Nagl) by supplying granular battle and campaign narrative grounded in primary records.

## Range 01 Extraction Block (front + Intro + Ch. 1–4)
(Exclusive /tmp/wawro-ranges/range_01_1-3500.txt)
- "Vietnam was a war of choice. Understanding it requires a reckoning with this stubborn fact. ... What motivated the United States to go to war and stay there was a fear of appearing weak." (Introduction) → caused_by political calculations across JFK/LBJ/Nixon.
- Graduated pressure + search-and-destroy produced massive firepower but no hold; VC local guerrillas decisive for intel/mobility. "If we are going to beat the enemy in the jungle we must take full advantage of our supporting firepower and avoid man-to-man engagements." (Ch. 4)
- Tonkin Gulf deception: "For all I know, our Navy was shooting at whales... LBJ... informed the lawmakers that North Vietnamese attacks had been confirmed." (Ch. 2) → contributed_to escalation authority.
- 1963 coup details: Diem/Nhu assassinated after Big Minh signal; "they had given their word and broken it." LBJ: "I am not going to lose Vietnam." (Ch. 1)
- Airmobile doctrine setup: "For the first time in the history of warfare, a large field army would rely on a fleet of thousands of helicopters..." (Ch. 4) Flag for hub Ia Drang.

## Range 02 Extraction Block (Ia Drang primary)
(Exclusive /tmp/wawro-ranges/range_02_3501-6500.txt)
- LZ X-Ray: Moore 1/7 Cav landed amid three NVA battalions; 33,108 artillery shells; kill ratio claimed 8:1 (79 US dead/121 WIA vs 634 confirmed NVA + est 1,215 WIA). NVA "hugging" tactics: "Once inside that envelope, the North Vietnamese could force the Americans to 'fight on their terms'." (Ch. 5)
- Albany ambush: 2/7 Cav column ambushed; 155 KIA + 124 WIA in 16 hrs. NVA commander: "Move inside their column, grab them by the belt, and avoid casualties from their artillery and air." Lessons "worth the cost of 100,000 men." (Ch. 5)
- Happy Valley futility precedent: "When the last Cav slick departed the valley, the hamlet chiefs ran for their lives and the Viet Cong moved back in." (Ch. 5)
- Flag for hub: OOB (1/7 Cav + supporting fires vs NVA regiments); phases (insertion, isolated platoon night defense with ring fires, morning assaults + napalm, Albany movement); principles (Mass/Offensive via fires honored; Security/Economy violated by close-in; Surprise initial but adapted against).

## Range 03 Extraction Block (Tet / Khe context + buildup)
(Exclusive /tmp/wawro-ranges/range_03_6501-9500.txt)
- Tet scope: 39/44 provincial capitals, 5 largest cities incl Saigon, 71 district capitals attacked in first days. "The Tet Offensive represented... 'the worst intelligence failure of the war.'" Westmoreland fixated on Khe Sanh ("Khe Sanh being the main event"); >50% US battalions shifted north. (Ch. 12-13)
- Khe Sanh as diversion: NVA ~20k+ troops (304th/325C) + heavy barrage (11k rounds ammo dump cook-off); intended to draw US away from cities. (Ch. 12)
- Westmoreland number manipulation: subtracted 56k "political" VC from OOB; claimed "nearly beaten" at Press Club Nov 21 while CIA reported 300k+ higher. (Ch. 12)
- Le Duan strategy: "Strategy of the General Offensive and General Uprising" to break US will psychologically despite not being strong enough to drive out all US troops. Giap: "We were not strong enough to drive out a half-million American troops, but that wasn’t our aim." (Ch. 12)

## Range 04 Extraction Block (Khe Sanh siege + Mini-Tet + Abrams)
(Exclusive /tmp/wawro-ranges/range_04_9501-12500.txt)
- Khe Sanh siege mechanics: Marines held plateau + hills (881S/861 etc.); bunkers with 5k sandbags; "Be Generous" arty (159k outgoing vs 11k incoming); Niagara B-52s 75k+ tons; sensors (Muscle Shoals); Lang Vei overrun by tanks Feb 7; Feb 29 assault repulsed by massed fires. US 205 KIA Marines; NVA est 5.5k–15k. Base later abandoned. (Ch. 15)
- "No regiment in history ever had such an overwhelming amount of firepower at their disposal." Weather nullified much air but technology prevailed.
- Mini-Tet May 1968: "It was not 'mini' at all... more Americans were killed than in any other week of the war." (2,169 US KIA in May). Saigon second wave destroyed 30k homes.
- Abrams transition: Abandoned pure search-and-destroy for "clear and hold"; "one war"; gave CAPs second look; measured by hamlets under control vs body count. Yet continuity of problems (Speedy Express praise by Abrams).

## Range 05 Extraction Block (Hamburger Hill + Cambodia)
(Exclusive /tmp/wawro-ranges/range_05_12501-15500.txt)
- Hamburger Hill (Hill 937 / Ap Bia, May 10-20 1969): 1,800 US + ARVN vs NVA 29th Regt bunkers; 11 assaults; 72 US KIA + 400 WIA; hill taken then abandoned. "Seventy-two more Americans had been killed and 400 wounded in eleven assaults on a height that was attacked with obsessive zeal and then promptly abandoned." Friendly fire incidents (5+). Triggered fragging/bounty hunting onset (96 incidents 1969, 37 officers killed). (Ch. 18)
- Cambodia incursion 1970: 31k US + ARVN into Fishhook/Parrot's Beak; COSVN relocated; 7k tons rice/20k weapons seized but "as if ... had never happened" pattern repeated; Khmer Rouge surged. Cooper-Church. ARVN test of Vietnamization: mixed/looting.
- ARVN/Vietnamization: "As soldiers the ARVN were useless... the Ruff-and-Puffs were outright dangerous." Phoenix mostly paper; commanders interested only in weapons/body count.

## Range 06 Extraction Block (Lam Son + Easter + Accords + decay)
(Exclusive /tmp/wawro-ranges/range_06_15501-18500.txt)
- Lam Son 719 (1971): "the heaviest defeat ever for Nixon & Company." ARVN abandoned 54 tanks + heavy equipment; "The ARVN did everything wrong." Demonstrated Vietnamization fraud.
- Easter Offensive 1972: ARVN collapses (Camp Carroll surrender, Quang Tri run); US air (advisers + B-52) decisive. Thieu sacked commanders.
- Paris Accords: "standstill" cease-fire leaving 160k NVA inside SVN. "Complete American withdrawal and the permanent emplacement of the North Vietnamese army assured the eventual destruction..."
- Post-Accords decay: Aid slashed ($1.6B → $700M); ammo 1.6 rds/inf/day; "well-equipped military corpse"; desertions spiked; "There are hardly any of us around anymore to prod the ARVN."

## Range 07 Extraction Block (The Fall + Conclusion)
(Exclusive /tmp/wawro-ranges/range_07_18501-23877.txt)
- Phuoc Long (Dec 1974–Jan 1975): NVA probe; city fell 2 days; only 850/5k ARVN escaped; no US reaction confirmed path.
- Ban Me Thuot: 3 NVA divs 5:1 surprise; Thieu ordered Highlands abandonment March 13 (40% territory). Pleiku/Kontum rout via Route 7B: 2/3 of corps disappeared; commanders fled first.
- Da Nang collapse: "rout of strategic proportions"; World Airways 727: 265 ARVN + 3 civilians; Hac Bao cut through crowd; fugitives fell from wheels.
- Xuan Loc: ARVN 18th (Dao) held 2 weeks vs 3 NVA divs; reduced 2/3 then retreated; "the most hated man in Vietnam" (re Thieu gravesite).
- FREQUENT WIND / surrender: Marines over wall; "It never came" for courtyard Vietnamese; tank at palace; Big Minh unconditional; Saigon → Ho Chi Minh City. 130k fled final period; boat people 50-70% mortality; reeducation camps 165k dead.
- Conclusion: "Vietnam was above all else a political war." "The mistake was to make their war America’s war... Collapse was inevitable." Will vs material; charts substituted for truth; "decent interval"; repressed lessons for later wars.
