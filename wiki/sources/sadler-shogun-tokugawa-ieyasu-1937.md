# Shogun: The Life and Times of Tokugawa Ieyasu (Sadler, 1937)

The classic biography of [[tokugawa-ieyasu|Tokugawa Ieyasu]], founder of the [[tokugawa-shogunate]] and
third of the "three unifiers" after [[oda-nobunaga]] and [[toyotomi-hideyoshi]]. Ingested as a DEEP read
deepening the wiki's [[edo-period]] / [[azuchi-momoyama-period]] coverage and filling the unification
narrative — the [[sengoku-period|Sengoku]] wars, the decisive [[battle-of-sekigahara]], the founding of
the shogunate, the [[siege-of-osaka|Osaka campaigns]] that destroyed the Toyotomi, and the institutions of
Tokugawa rule.

~**1,000–11,800** of `raw/_sadler-wrapped.txt`; later lines are appendices, notes, and index.

## Volume Synthesis Note

A DEEP biography filling the wiki's thin Japan narrative for the unification and founding of the Tokugawa
order. It tracks [[tokugawa-ieyasu|Ieyasu]] from the [[matsudaira-clan|Matsudaira]] hostage years and the
[[battle-of-okehazama-1560|break from the Imagawa]], through the [[battle-of-mikatagahara-1573|defeats]] and
victories of the [[sengoku-period|Sengoku wars]] under [[oda-nobunaga|Nobunaga]] and then
[[toyotomi-hideyoshi|Hideyoshi]] (the [[komaki-nagakute-campaign-1584|Komaki]] standoff, the
[[ieyasu-moves-to-edo-1590|move to the Kantō]]), to the decisive [[battle-of-sekigahara-1600|Sekigahara]]
(1600) and the founding of the [[tokugawa-shogunate|shogunate]] (1603). It captures his statecraft — the
[[building-of-edo-1604|building of Edo]], the foreign relations of [[adams-william|William Adams]] and the
[[red-seal-ships|red-seal trade]] — the [[siege-of-osaka-1614|destruction of the Toyotomi]] (1614–15), and
the institutional settlement ([[buke-shohatto|the military-house laws]], [[bakuhan-system|the bakuhan
system]], [[sankin-kotai|alternate attendance]]) that, sealed by his [[tosho-daigongen-deification-1617|
deification]], gave Japan its 260-year [[edo-period|Edo]] peace. Sadler's admiring narrative is flagged as
dated by the 2020 forewords; the wiki pages note where modern scholarship (e.g. the Nagashino "military
revolution" debate) revises it.
