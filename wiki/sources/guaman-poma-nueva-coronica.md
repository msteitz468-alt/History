# Nueva corónica y buen gobierno (Guamán Poma, c. 1615; Pease ed., Tomo I)

The great indigenous chronicle-and-petition of the colonial Andes — an Andean nobleman's
illustrated "letter to the king" denouncing Spanish misrule and proposing a reformed Christian
order. This page covers the source; the author and the work's argument are on
[[guaman-poma-de-ayala]].

## What it is

Written over decades and finished about 1615, the *Nueva corónica y buen gobierno* is two works in
one: a **"new chronicle"** narrating the history of the Andean world from a biblical creation through
the pre-Inca "ages," the Incas, and the Spanish invasion; and a treatise on **buen gobierno**
cataloguing the abuses of colonial officials — *corregidores*, *encomenderos*, priests, miners,
and merchants — and prescribing remedies. It is famous for its nearly **400 full-page line
drawings**, which carry much of its argument and are a primary visual source in their own right. The
manuscript was sent (or meant to be sent) to Philip III; it never reached him and surfaced only in
1908 in the Royal Library of Copenhagen, where it remains.

## What it contributes

- **[[guaman-poma-de-ayala]]** — the author and the work's content, drawings, and argument (new
  actor page).
- Primary-indigenous enrichment of **[[encomienda]]** (the documented abuses) and the colonial
  Andes (the [[burkholder-johnson-colonial-latin-america|Burkholder–Johnson]] overview), and a
  companion indigenous voice to the Mesoamerican [[popol-vuh|Popol Vuh]].

## Reading note

The Pease critical introduction (read closely) supplies the biography and the interpretive frame;
the chronicle proper is in OCR'd Spanish. Treated as a situated primary source for a flagged gap
region — Guamán Poma's biases and purposes are foregrounded, not smoothed over.

(Primary source — Tomo I of the Biblioteca Ayacucho edition; the indigenous chronicle of the
colonial Andes.)
