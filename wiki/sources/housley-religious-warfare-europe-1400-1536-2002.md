# Norman Housley, *Religious Warfare in Europe 1400-1536* (2002)

Monograph examining how religious values and institutions shaped the practice and perception of organised violence in Europe from the Hussite wars through the early Reformation. Housley argues that "religious warfare" (distinct from but overlapping with crusade and just war) retained a powerful presence and capacity for "sanctification" well into the sixteenth century, driven by crusade traditions, sectarian apocalypticism, national/eschatological messianism, and frontier conditions. The study traces both the release of this potential and the emergence of explicit critiques.

This source enriches the wiki's treatment of late medieval/early modern ideological drivers of conflict, the transition from crusading to confessional warfare, and the interplay of religion, identity, and violence on multiple frontiers (Ottoman, internal heretics, "interior Turk").

## Claims Integrated (main-thread synthesis from all ranges; all attributed to source)

**Core definitions and framing (R01 + synthesis):**
- Religious warfare as distinct category (direct divine mandate, Deo auctore bella, sanctification of war not always bloodshed, demonisation/"turkification" of enemy). Taborites practiced "economy rather than a totality of violence" despite mandate. Produced: [[concepts/religious-warfare]]. See Ch. 1.1.

**Hussite crucible (R01 dominant):**
- 1419–1436 as "battleground for four types of religious war" (Prague defence, Taborite/Orebite urgency, chiliast purgative violence, crusading response) + Chelcicky rejection. Five crusades defeated (Žižka/Prokop + wagon forts/artillery decisive); "bellum cottidianum" especially brutal on both sides. 
- Chiliasm 1419–early 1421 (five cities, Tábor/Horeb, "wash his hands in the blood"; suppressed after Adamites). Tábor distinct: eschatological, brief communism, non-noble vanguard yet invoked "Czech nation"/"Law of God". Zizka central (national + religious fusion; "old Czechs" precedent; Kalich fortress). 
- Just-war justification (Jakoubek et al.): "second way" for secular/community defence of causa Dei (right intention, charity, unresponsive to peace, per NT/doctors) vs. superior "first way" (Christ/Apostles, suffering/spiritual arms). Manifestos fused "God’s Law" + patria/"most Christian kingdom"/OT elect. Victories (Vítkov, Vyšehrad, Žatec) as Deus vult miracles. 
- Settlement via negotiation (Bratislava 1429, Basle/Eger) after military success + "truth conquers"; Lipany 1434 broke radicals; Jihlava 1436. 
- Atrocities: crusader demonisation + burning Czechs; Taborite/chiliast "more cruel", fires, child killings, coercion ("bellum cottidianum"); both sides. (Lines flagged for graphic: 1870s, 1895+, 2110s, 2890 etc.)
- Produced/enriched: [[events/hussite-wars-1419]], [[actors/zizka-jan]], [[places/tabor]], [[places/bohemia]], [[concepts/religious-warfare]], [[actors/hus-jan]] (legacy).

**Commonwealth / Ottoman / national messianism / Dózsa (R02):**
- Ottoman resurgence (post-Ankara recovery) primary driver elevating crusade at councils; "without the Turks this probably would not have happened." Belgrade 1456 (Capistrano + peasant crucesignati) as Observant revival; post-victory noble-peasant friction. 
- Dózsa 1514 (Hungary): crusade preached (Bakócz/Leo X, ~50k), Observant preaching; diverted to noble attacks; Cegléd proclamation demonises nobles as "infideles"/Turks; "crusade-revolt" unique (orthodox beliefs, no chiliasm, crusade form retained). Miracle of falling crucifix at Pest overrides legate dispersal. Brutal suppression; "word 'crusade' banned in Hungary after 1514". Comparable to Pilgrimage of Grace (religion as channel) and Niklashausen (1476). 
- Castile: national/royal messianism via Reconquista + bula de la cruzada; Alonso of Cartagena at Basle claims precedence for "guerra divinal"; Joachimist Encubierto/New David/Last Emperor applied to Fernando, Charles V (Jerusalem recovery, one shepherd). Columbus synthesised.
- Luther: rejects crusade apparatus/indulgences as corrupt; Christians fight with Word/prayer; rulers defend lands (obedience); "no one is saved as a soldier but only as a Christian". Shift 1530-1 to sanction self-defence vs "murderous papists"; Hussite/Žižka analogy; "worse than the Turks" for Roman church. Zwingli: gospel defence + Swiss/Israelite patriotism.
- German Peasants’ War 1524-5: "Göttliches Recht"/sola scriptura; conservative Gemeinde roots; astonishing restraint vs. Dózsa/Tabor brutality; no major evangelical support (Luther denounced). Müntzer: chiliast, Dan. 2/7 exegesis, "sword of Gideon", post-Frankenhausen self-blame for self-interest.
- Münster 1534-5 precursors in range (agency debates); full treatment later. 
- Produced/enriched: [[events/dozsa-rebellion-1514]], [[actors/dozsa-gyorgy]], [[concepts/antemurale-christianitatis]], [[concepts/religious-warfare]], [[actors/muntzer-thomas]], [[events/munster-anabaptist-1534]] (precursors).

**Authority, symbols, Three Turks (R03):**
- Scripture (Daniel, Rev, OT) as detailed guide for timing/agency/vengeance (Müntzer, Hoffman/Rothmann, Tabor chiliasts, Bockelson "New David"). Messianic figures (Dózsa, Bockelson, Encubierto, Last Emperor prophecies for Charles V). Symbols: chalice, cross/nomen Jesu (Capistrano, Dózsa), antemurale, blood-brotherhood at Senj.
- Three Turks: (1) External (Ottomans: disciplined, fanatical, impaling, devsirme, Gog/Magog; some "Turkish chic" compartmentalised but fundamental hostility). (2) Internal ("worse than the Turks": Venice accused during Cambrai; Swiss/Eidgenossen; French in Italy; Hungarian nobles/Styria rebels; "acting like a Turk"). (3) Interior (Erasmus: fight vice in self not man; "kill the Turk, not the man"; "degenerate into Turks long before we convert"; More’s *Dialogue of Comfort* synthesises all three — external threat, Hungarian "Turks" (Zápolyai/apostates), interior apostasy/"midday devil").
- Senj uskoks: antemurale without formal privileges; "Jesus!" cry; St George; "would rather lose head than obey order not to harm infidels"; booty economy balanced by creed.
- Dózsa "princeps of the cruciferi"; Zizka supreme captain only (no prophetic kingship).
- Produced/enriched: [[concepts/internal-turk]], [[concepts/antemurale-christianitatis]], [[actors/more-thomas]], [[places/senj]], [[concepts/religious-warfare]].

**Critique + Perspectives (R04):**
- Critiques: Chartier (France 1422: covenant failure); Prague masters post-Most (God punishes wrong motives); Zizka’s Nemecky Brod penance + 1423 Ordinance (discipline for "fight for God"); Nicholas of Pelhrimov (apologia + strict Tabor reform decrees 1422-26: priests limited role, charity "kill the Turk not the man", no spoils/excursions, necessity not gain); Chelcicky (absolute rejection: Devil enters hearts; no fusion of secular sword + Christ’s love; prefer martyrdom); Erasmus (philosophia Christi irreconcilable; no NT instructions for Turks; "Turk within" > external; internalised combat in *Enchiridion*); Czech Brethren evolution (initial Chelcicky pacifism → qualified "killing God’s enemies caritative"; post-Mohács majority acceptance); Anabaptist spectrum (pacifist reaction to Münster: Marpeck, Menno, Hutter, Sattler — "our wagon fortress is Christ").
- Four patterns releasing potential (synthesis): (1) Crusade (persistent, filtered through national/chivalric/eschatological; Dózsa retained form; Iberian; Senj); (2) Sectarian apocalypticism (Tábor/Münster: purgative/celebratory extremes; Tábor evolved stable, Münster perished with backlash — "early Reformation’s Lipany"); (3) National messianism (Iberia, England/France, Switzerland, Savonarola; fused with reformed faith later); (4) Defence of reformed faith (Hussites vs. crusades; later confessional).
- Continuity: no period 1095–1648 free of religious warfare; 1400–1536 bridges Crusades/Wars of Religion (not mere borrowing); frontier/chivalric/subversive apocalyptic persisted alongside. 1536 markers (Pilgrimage of Grace, Erasmus/More deaths, Münster fall) end ferment generation. Schmalkaldic/French Wars: continuity (crosses, indulgences, demonisation) + change (confessional states).
- Produced/enriched: [[concepts/religious-warfare]], [[concepts/holy-war]], [[events/hussite-wars-1419]], [[events/munster-anabaptist-1534]], [[places/senj]], periods.

**Overall synthesis note (Housley):** Religious warfare "an important feature" with potential released by the four patterns; crusade "constant undercurrent"; critique emerged precisely from radical claims. No easy secularisation narrative.
