# Before France and Germany — Patrick J. Geary (1988)

**The standard English-language synthesis of the Merovingian world**, tracing the
Frankish kingdoms from the late Roman provincial world (1st century) to the eve of
the Carolingian takeover (c. 800). Geary's central thesis: the barbarian world,
and the Franks above all, was **a creation of Rome** — its peoples, institutions,
and self-understanding shaped from the start within the orbit of provincial Roman
late antiquity, so that "before France and Germany" there was a hybrid Romano-
barbarian world whose transformation, not whose conquest, is the real story.

## Argument and approach

- **Ethnogenesis over migration.** Peoples (Goths, Franks) are not ancient biological
  nations that migrated intact but **constructed political communities** crystallised
  around a core of tradition-bearers (a *Traditionskern*), warband, and successful
  leadership — assembled, dissolved, and reassembled within the Roman frame. This is
  the [[ethnogenesis]] model Geary popularised for English readers.
- **Rome made the barbarians.** Roman frontier policy, trade, subsidy, recruitment, and
  ethnography actively structured the *Germanic* world long before any conquest of Roman
  soil.
- **Transformation, not fall.** The provincial Roman world of late antiquity — itself
  alien to classical Rome — was gradually amalgamated with barbarian traditions; the
  principals are usually indistinguishable, and change is read in incidental detail, not
  great events.
