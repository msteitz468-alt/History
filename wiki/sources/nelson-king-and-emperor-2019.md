# King and Emperor: A New Life of Charlemagne (Janet L. Nelson, 2019)

Janet L. Nelson's *King and Emperor* is a chronological, evidence-first life of
[[charlemagne|Charles the Great]] (748–814) by the leading Anglophone Carolingianist.
Its governing argument is methodological: that the historical Charles can be reached
only by reading **forwards** through the contemporary record, against the grain of the
retrospective, Rome-oriented portrait fixed by [[einhard|Einhard]]'s *Vita Karoli*.
Nelson rejects teleology ("Father of Europe") and anachronism ("German"/"French"
Charlemagne) alike, and reconstructs a contingent, family-driven, restlessly itinerant
career out of charters, capitularies, conciliar acts, letters, annals and poems.

This ingest treats the book primarily as the source for the **Biography Hub** analytic
life ([[hubs/biographies/early-middle-ages/charlemagne|Charlemagne — analytic biography]])
and as enrichment for the existing dense Carolingian cluster (actor [[charlemagne]],

## Key Distinctive Claims (Nelson's revisionism)

- **Birthdate 2 April 748, not 742** (Werner/Becher): Einhard fabricated the "seventy-second year"
  on a Suetonian model; true lifespan ~66.
- The **14,000 solidi** offered to Desiderius were "a pair of wergilds … the price of two princes,
  7,000 solidi apiece" — to buy custody of Carloman's sons, not to redeem papal lands.
- The **Lombard conquest "evolved … en route for Pavia"** — unplanned; Pavia fell to epidemic,
  "without any shedding of blood."
- The fall of **Tassilo III** was "systematically and ruthlessly planned"; the *Annales regni
  Francorum* trial is a "retrospective construction," a "shadow-biography."
- **Verden's 4,500** is "suspiciously … tidy" — possibly a scribal extra zero; the *Capitulatio*
  may post-date (respond to) the revolt rather than cause it.
- The **coronation of 800 was co-planned** — "any argument to the contrary simply can't be
  sustained"; the title *Romanum gubernans imperium* was a "significant evasion."
- **Rehabilitation of the queens** — [[hildegard]] and [[fastrada]] as political agents; rejection
  of *Friedelehe*; Fastrada "retrospectively made a scapegoat."
- **Rebuttal of Ganshof's "*l'échec de Charlemagne*"** — the late capitularies and *missi*
  correspondence show a functioning, monitored government ("re-read your capitularies!").

## Volume Synthesis Note

*King and Emperor* enters the wiki as the source spine for the life of Charles and a sustained
methodological corrective to the Carolingian cluster it joins. Where [[sources/mckitterick-ncmh-v2-1995|NCMH II]]
(Fouracre) asks structurally whether the Carolingian state was *objectively* different from its
Merovingian predecessor — and answers cautiously that custom and continuity outweighed innovation —
Nelson works at the opposite resolution: the contingent, dated, itinerant decisions of one man,
reconstructed forward "in hope but also in ignorance" of outcomes. The two are complementary rather
than contradictory: Fouracre's "power on the ground still lay with counts and bishops in 814 as in
714" sits comfortably beside Nelson's portrait of a sociable, associative rulership that *worked
through* counts and bishops rather than over them.

The sharpest cross-volume tension is with the older institutional pessimism (Ganshof) still echoed
in parts of the wiki's [[nature-of-carolingian-government|government controversy]] page: Nelson's
reading of the 806–813 capitularies as evidence of a functioning imperial communications system, not
"decomposition," should be registered there as the current revisionist position. Her birthdate
correction (748) and her insistence on the women of the dynasty as agents also update the wiki's
[[charlemagne]] and family pages at the level of fact, not just interpretation. Finally, her refusal
of the "Father of Europe" teleology is itself a historiographical datum: it marks the distance
between the eighth-century evidence and the twentieth-century European-unity uses of "Charlemagne,"
a distinction the wiki should preserve.
