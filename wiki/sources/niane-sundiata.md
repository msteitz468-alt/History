# Sundiata: An Epic of Old Mali (D. T. Niane)

*Sundiata* is the **founding epic of the [[mali-empire|Mali Empire]]** — the Mande oral tradition,
transmitted for some seven centuries by professional bards, of how **[[sundiata-keita|Sundiata (Mari
Djata) Keita]]** overcame disability, exile, and the sorcerer-king of Sosso to forge the empire. This is
the standard text, set down in the 1950s by the Guinean historian **D. T. Niane** from the recitation of
the griot **Mamadou Kouyaté**, and published in French (1960) and English (1965). It is the second source
of **Gap 2** and the wiki's primary witness to medieval West African state-formation from the **inside**.

## The narrative

The griot Mamadou Kouyaté opens by asserting the authority of his caste — "I am a *griot*... we are vessels
of speech, the memory of mankind." He tells how **Maghan Kon Fatta**, king of the Mande at **Niani**, is
told by a soothsayer to marry an ugly woman who will bear a great son; he weds **Sogolon Kedjou**, "the
buffalo-woman." Their son **Sundiata** is born crippled and cannot walk until, humiliated, he rises at age
seven — bending an iron rod into a bow and uprooting a baobab. Driven into **exile** by a jealous co-wife,
Sundiata and his mother wander the Sahel (Mema, the courts of various kings), where he matures into a
hunter and leader.

Meanwhile **Soumaoro Kanté (Sumanguru)**, the cruel **sorcerer-king of Sosso**, conquers the Mande. Recalled
by his people, Sundiata returns at the head of an allied army. After learning Soumaoro's secret taboo
(revealed by Soumaoro's own niece), Sundiata defeats him at the decisive **Battle of Krina (Kirina)** — the
sorcerer's power broken by an arrow tipped with a white cock's spur. Sundiata then reduces the remaining
kingdoms (Diaghan, Kita, Sosso) and, at the great assembly of **Kurukan Fuga** (at Ka-ba), the assembled
clans give his empire its **constitution**, partitioning offices and lands among the lineages. Mali is
founded, with its capital at Niani.

## Why it matters

- The **only extended indigenous narrative** of Mali's origin, and the charter myth of **Mande identity**
  — the Keita, Konde, Traore, Kamara and Koroma clans and their bonds.
- A window into the **griot (*jeli*) institution**: history, praise-poetry, genealogy and political
  counsel held by a hereditary caste (see [[griot-oral-tradition]]).
- A test case for **oral tradition as historical evidence** (the methodological problem foregrounded on
  [[mali-empire]] by Conrad/CWH V): rich in social and cultural truth, unreliable for literal chronology.
