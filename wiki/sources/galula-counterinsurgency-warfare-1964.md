# Counterinsurgency Warfare: Theory and Practice

**Counterinsurgency Warfare: Theory and Practice** (Praeger, 1964; 2006 reprint with Nagl foreword) by David Galula is the single most influential short theoretical treatment of counterinsurgency from the counterinsurgent's perspective. A French officer with experience observing revolutionary warfare in China, serving as a UN observer in Greece, and fighting in Algeria, Galula wrote the book while a fellow at Harvard's Center for International Affairs.

The work systematically contrasts revolutionary (insurgent) warfare with conventional war, identifies the inherent asymmetries, derives four "laws" of counterinsurgency, deduces principles, and prescribes a concrete strategy and tactical sequence of operations. It remains the clearest articulation of population-centric, politically-driven counterinsurgency.

## Primary Claims

### Range 02 (lines 2001-3800: Ch. 3 Insurgency Doctrine + Ch. 4-5 Counterinsurgency in Cold/Hot Revolutionary War + Laws and Principles)

**Topic: Orthodox (Communist) insurgency pattern — the five-step progression to armed victory**

**Grounding quote:** "**The Third Step: Guerrilla Warfare** The insurgent may seize power merely by political play and subversion. If not, then an armed struggle is the logical continuation. ... When the insurgent seizes power after an armed struggle, his victory is complete, his authority absolute. ... Through the armed struggle, the party consolidates itself. ... The party assumes power with a tested, reliable military establishment..." (lines 22-50). "**The Fourth Step: Movement Warfare** Guerrilla warfare cannot win the decision against a resolute enemy. ... **The Fifth Step: Annihilation Campaign** As the over-all strength of the insurgent grows while his opponent’s decreases, a balance of forces is reached... a series of offensives aiming at the complete destruction of the enemy will constitute the last and final step." (lines 176-305).

**Key claim:** The orthodox pattern requires building a party, united front via subversion, then progressive escalation from guerrilla bases (survival + population complicity as the "fish swimming in the water") to regular forces and final annihilation; guerrilla operations primarily organize the population rather than attrit the enemy.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[concepts/guerrilla-warfare]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]] [[hubs/warfare/strategy]]

**Topic: Shortcut (bourgeois-nationalist) insurgency pattern**

**Grounding quote:** "**THE BOURGEOIS-NATIONALIST PATTERN: A SHORTCUT** ... **The First Step: Blind Terrorism** The purpose is to get publicity... by random terrorism, bombings, arson, assassinations, conducted in as spectacular a fashion as possible... **The Second Step: Selective Terrorism** This quickly follows the first. The aims are to isolate the counterinsurgent from the masses, to involve the population in the struggle, and to obtain as a minimum its passive complicity. ... From there on, this pattern rejoins the orthodox one... Illegal and violent at the outset, dangerous for the insurgent because terrorism may backfire, this pattern may save years of tedious organizational work." (lines 307-366).

**Key claim:** The shortcut bypasses prolonged party-building by starting with spectacular blind terrorism for publicity, followed immediately by selective terrorism against local officials and to force complicity and fundraising, then merging into the orthodox guerrilla phase; it is faster but breeds later bitterness and party weakness.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[concepts/guerrilla-warfare]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]]

**Topic: The First Law of Counterinsurgency**

**Grounding quote:** "**The First Law: The Support of the Population Is as Necessary for the Counterinsurgent as for the Insurgent** ... This can be achieved only with the support of the population. If it is relatively easy to disperse and to expel the insurgent forces from a given area by purely military action... it is impossible to prevent the return of the guerrilla units and the rebuilding of the political cells unless the population cooperates. The population, therefore, becomes the objective for the counterinsurgent as it was for his enemy. ... This is where the fight has to be conducted..." (lines 754-772).

**Key claim:** Support of the population is the decisive objective for the counterinsurgent exactly as for the insurgent; military clearing is possible but meaningless without population cooperation to prevent return and rebuilding of insurgent structures.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]] [[hubs/warfare/strategy]]

**Topic: The Second Law of Counterinsurgency**

**Grounding quote:** "**The Second Law: Support Is Gained Through an Active Minority** The original problem becomes now: how to obtain the support of the population—support not only in the form of sympathy and approval but also in active participation... The answer lies in the following proposition... In any situation, whatever the cause, there will be an active minority for the cause, a neutral majority, and an active minority against the cause. The technique of power consists in relying on the favorable minority in order to rally the neutral majority and to neutralize or eliminate the hostile minority. ... The strategic problem of the counterinsurgent may be defined now as follows: 'To find the favorable minority, to organize it in order to mobilize the population against the insurgent minority.' Every operation... must be geared to that end." (lines 776-818).

**Key claim:** Support (active participation, not mere sympathy) is obtained by finding, organizing, and relying on the active minority favorable to the counterinsurgent to rally neutrals and neutralize opponents; every military/political/social/psychological action must serve this end. A competing counter-cause is required.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]] [[hubs/warfare/strategy]]

**Topic: The Third Law of Counterinsurgency**

**Grounding quote:** "**The Third Law: Support from the Population Is Conditional** Once the insurgent has established his hold over the population, the minority that was hostile to him becomes invisible. ... The minority hostile to the insurgent will not and cannot emerge as long as the threat has not been lifted to a reasonable extent. ... Four deductions can be made from this law. Effective political action on the population must be preceded by military and police operations against the guerrilla units and the insurgent political organizations. Political, social, economic, and other reforms... are inoperative when offered while the insurgent still controls the population. ... The counterinsurgent needs a convincing success as early as possible..." (lines 845-875).

**Key claim:** Hostile elements remain invisible and neutrals uncommitted until the insurgent threat is lifted and the counterinsurgent demonstrates will/means/ability to win; military/police action must precede meaningful political action and reforms.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]] [[hubs/warfare/strategy]]

**Topic: The Fourth Law of Counterinsurgency and derived strategy**

**Grounding quote:** "**The Fourth Law: Intensity of Efforts and Vastness of Means Are Essential** The operations needed to relieve the population from the insurgent’s threat and to convince it that the counterinsurgent will ultimately win are necessarily of an intensive nature and of long duration. They require a large concentration of efforts, resources, and personnel. This means that the efforts cannot be diluted all over the country but must be applied successively area by area. **STRATEGY OF THE COUNTERINSURGENCY** Translated into a general strategy... **In a Selected Area** 1. Concentrate enough armed forces to destroy or to expel the main body of armed insurgents. 2. Detach for the area sufficient troops... install these troops in the hamlets, villages, and towns... 3. Establish contact with the population, control its movements... 4. Destroy the local insurgent political organizations. 5. Set up, by means of elections, new provisional local authorities. ... 8. Win over or suppress the last insurgent remnants." (lines 883-923).

**Key claim:** Counterinsurgency requires intensive, prolonged, concentrated (not diluted) efforts applied successively area-by-area; the derived strategy is a strict sequential process in a selected area (clear main forces → deploy static troops among population → control/seal → destroy cells → new authorities → self-defense → national movement → mop-up).

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]] [[hubs/warfare/strategy]]

**Range summary:** This range establishes the two insurgent patterns with their vulnerabilities, demonstrates the fundamental mismatch of conventional and insurgent methods when adopted by the counterinsurgent, and derives the four laws (population support as objective; via active minority; conditional on demonstrated strength; requiring intensive concentrated successive effort) plus the explicit sequential strategy and supporting principles that must replace them. The four laws receive the heaviest verbatim treatment.

### Range 01 (lines 1-2000: Front matter, Foreword, Introduction, Ch. 1 Revolutionary War Nature + early Ch. 2)

**Topic: Asymmetry between insurgent and counterinsurgent (fly vs. lion)**

**Grounding quote:** "Revolutionary war, on the other hand, represents an exceptional case not only because, as we suspect, it has its special rules, different from those of the conventional war, but also because most of the rules applicable to one side do not work for the other. In a fight between a fly and a lion, the fly cannot deliver a knockout blow and the lion cannot fly. It is the same war for both camps in terms of space and time, yet there are two distinct warfares—the revolutionary’s and, shall we say, the counterrevolutionary’s." (lines ~618-628)

**Key claim:** Revolutionary war has fundamentally asymmetric rules; the insurgent's congenital weakness is offset by political advantages and fluidity, while the counterinsurgent's strength is offset by rigidity and the need to protect everything.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[concepts/guerrilla-warfare]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]]

**Topic: The population as the central objective**

**Grounding quote:** "Objective: The Population ... In the conventional war, the object is to destroy the enemy's military forces and occupy his country. In revolutionary war, the objective is the population." (from Ch. 1 structure + related)

**Key claim:** Unlike conventional war (enemy forces + territory), the objective in revolutionary war for both sides is the population; the insurgent needs its active support or at least passive complicity to survive and grow; the counterinsurgent needs it to deny that support and to obtain intelligence.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]]

**Topic: Revolutionary war is a political war, protracted, cheap for insurgent / costly for counterinsurgent**

**Grounding quote:** "Revolutionary War Is a Political War ... Revolutionary War Is a Protracted War ... Insurgency Is Cheap, Counterinsurgency Costly ... Fluidity of the Insurgent, Rigidity of the Counterinsurgent ... The Power of Ideology ... Propaganda—A One-Sided Weapon" (Ch. 1 headings and discussion)

**Key claim:** Revolutionary war is political at all levels; it is protracted by design (time works for the insurgent); it is cheap and low-risk for the insurgent (small units, no decisive battle required) while the counterinsurgent must protect everything at high cost and appears rigid.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]] [[hubs/warfare/strategy]]

**Range summary:** Range 01 establishes the foundational asymmetry and the population as the true objective, the political/protracted/cheap-for-insurgent character of revolutionary war, and the prerequisites (cause + counterinsurgent weakness + geography + outside support). These set up the later derivation of counterinsurgent-specific laws and the sequential operational template.

### Range 03 (lines 3801-5600: Ch. 6 From Strategy to Tactics + Ch. 7 The Operations — the six/eight steps + Concluding Remarks)

**Topic: The Third Step – contact with and control of the population (core objectives)**

**Grounding quote:** "**THE THIRD STEP: CONTACT WITH AND CONTROL OF THE POPULATION** Three main objectives are pursued in this step: 1. To re-establish the counterinsurgent’s authority over the population. 2. To isolate the population as much as possible, by physical means, from the guerrillas. 3. To gather the necessary intelligence leading to the next step—elimination of the insurgent political cells. This is the most critical step in the process because of its transitional character, moving from emphasis on military operations to emphasis on political ones..." (range lines 96-108)

**Key claim:** The Third Step is the most critical due to its transitional military-to-political character; it pursues re-establishment of authority, physical isolation of the population from guerrillas, and intelligence collection for destroying insurgent political cells.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]] [[hubs/warfare/strategy]]

**Topic: The Fourth Step – destruction of the insurgent political organization**

**Grounding quote:** "**THE FOURTH STEP: DESTRUCTION OF THE INSURGENT POLITICAL ORGANIZATION** The necessity for eradicating the insurgent political agents from the population is evident. ... The procedure would be: 1. To arrest simultaneously a large group of minor suspects. 2. On the basis of their disclosures, to arrest the cell members. ... Leniency seems in this case a good practical policy, but not blind leniency." (range lines 339-429 approx.)

**Key claim:** The Fourth Step is a rapid, decisive professional police "purge" using simultaneous mass arrest of minor suspects to identify and arrest cell members indirectly; leniency (full confession + active counterinsurgent participation) is practical to encourage disclosures.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]]

**Topic: The Fifth Step – local elections**

**Grounding quote:** "**THE FIFTH STEP: LOCAL ELECTIONS** Now begins the constructive part of the counterinsurgent program. ... A better approach would be to call for absolutely free elections for local provisional self-government, thus letting leaders emerge naturally from the population... Whatever the results of the elections, the counterinsurgent must accept them with the publicly announced proviso that these new local leaders are temporarily in office until definitive elections when peace has been restored all over the country." (range lines 442-501)

**Key claim:** After the purge, the Fifth Step begins constructive work by holding absolutely free local provisional elections to let natural leaders emerge; results must be accepted with the explicit proviso of provisional status.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]]

**Topic: The Sixth Step – testing the local leaders and building the political machine**

**Grounding quote:** "**THE SIXTH STEP: TESTING THE LOCAL LEADERS** The ultimate results of the counterinsurgent’s efforts in regard to the population depend on the effectiveness of the men who have just been elected. ... The first thing to do, therefore, is to test these new local leaders. The principle of the test is simple: They are given concrete tasks and they are judged on their ability to fulfill them. ... When in a part of the selected area, the situation has reached the stage where the population actively helps the counterinsurgent, it means that a breakthrough has been achieved..." (range lines 503-567)

**Key claim:** The Sixth Step tests elected leaders via concrete tasks; successful leaders are consolidated with regime assets while failures are eliminated with population consent; breakthrough signs include population acting as propagandists outside their area.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]] [[hubs/warfare/strategy]]

**Topic: The Seventh Step – organizing a party / building the political machine**

**Grounding quote:** "**THE SEVENTH STEP: ORGANIZING A PARTY** As the work proceeds in the area, tested leaders will finally appear in each village and town. They will eventually have to be grouped and organized within a national counterinsurgent political party. ... The counterinsurgent political party should select its members carefully, and rely more on quality than on quantity." (range lines 571-611)

**Key claim:** Tested local leaders must be organized into a national counterinsurgent political party because a party is the essential instrument of politics and provides a solid machine rooted in the population; quality over quantity.

**Wiki links:** [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]]

**Topic: Overall synthesis – the basic mechanism of counterinsurgency warfare**

**Grounding quote:** "CONCLUDING REMARKS Such is, in the author’s view, the basic mechanism of counterinsurgency warfare. Whether in the cold or in the hot revolutionary war, its essence can be summed up in a single sentence: Build (or rebuild) a political machine from the population upward. The idea is simple. ... Insurgencies in the recent past have stemmed from two major causes: (1) the rise of nationalism in colonial territories, and (2) Communist pressure..." (range lines 676-739)

**Key claim:** The essence of COIN (hot or cold revolutionary war) is to "Build (or rebuild) a political machine from the population upward"; victory is not guaranteed; insurgencies arise from nationalism + Communist pressure; even success yields only a respite if underlying issues persist.

**Wiki links:** [[actors/galula-david]] [[concepts/counterinsurgency]] [[sources/galula-counterinsurgency-warfare-1964]] [[periods/cold-war]]

**Range summary:** Range 03 covers the transition to population contact and control, the police purge of insurgent cells, elections to surface leaders, leader testing via tasks, party organization, final suppression of remaining guerrillas with popular participation, and Galula's concluding synthesis that COIN's basic mechanism is building a political machine upward from the population. The six (plus preparatory and mop-up) steps are presented as the concrete implementation of the four laws.

## Volume Synthesis Note

*(To be completed after agent reconciliation.)*

Galula's central contribution is to treat counterinsurgency as a distinct form of warfare with its own logic, not merely "small war" or conventional war with lower intensity. The four laws (support of the population is as necessary for the counterinsurgent as for the insurgent; support is gained through an active minority; support is conditional; intensity of effort and vastness of means are essential) plus the insistence that the objective is the population and that the war is fundamentally political drive the entire subsequent doctrine.

Key practical advance: a clear, sequential operational template that begins with military clearing but immediately transitions to population control, political organization, and the building of a competing political machine — rather than "search and destroy" alone or purely civic action. The book also stresses the need for unified civil-military command and for the counterinsurgent to adapt organizationally and psychologically to a protracted, asymmetric contest.

## Notes

Galula deliberately writes from the counterinsurgent's viewpoint. The book is deliberately short, prescriptive, and derived from experience + limited historical cases rather than exhaustive theory. Nagl's foreword (2006) situates it as foundational for post-9/11 U.S. doctrine.
