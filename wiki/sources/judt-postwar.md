# Postwar: A History of Europe Since 1945 (Tony Judt)

Tony Judt's *Postwar* (2005) is the standard history of **Europe since 1945**, narrated as a single story
embracing **both halves of the divided continent** — and read by Judt as the slow, costly recovery of a
Europe that had nearly destroyed itself, culminating in the settlement of **1989–91** and the rise of a
new European model. Seventeenth source of **Gap 1 (Modern)**, the European complement to
[[westad-cold-war|Westad's]] global Cold War and the [[cold-war|Cold War period page]].

## The Argument

Judt's organizing themes:

- **From ruin to recovery.** 1945 Europe was a continent of rubble, displaced persons and shattered
  legitimacy; the book traces the **post-war settlement**, Marshall aid, and the building of the
  **welfare-state social-democratic model** that defined Western European stability.
- **East and West as one story.** Judt refuses to treat the **Soviet bloc** as a separate subject: the
  communist experiment, its 1956 and 1968 crises, and its 1989 collapse are integral to Europe's history,
  not a footnote to it.
- **Memory and the Holocaust.** A famous **coda, "From the House of the Dead,"** argues that the
  **recovered memory of the Holocaust** became the moral foundation — the "entry ticket" — of the new
  Europe; post-war prosperity was built on a long deferral of that reckoning.
- **1989 and after.** The revolutions of 1989 and the Soviet collapse are the climax; Judt is
  clear-eyed about the difficulties of the post-communist transition and the limits of the European
  Union's promise.

## Significance

*Postwar* gives the wiki its fullest **European** history of the second half of the twentieth century,
anchoring the [[cold-war|Cold War]] and [[contemporary|Contemporary]] period pages on their European side,
and connecting the [[world-wars-era|war]] and the [[genocide|Holocaust]] to the continent's later politics
of memory. It pairs with [[westad-cold-war|Westad]] (the global frame) and
