# Sternhell, Sznajder & Asheri — *The Birth of Fascist Ideology* (Princeton UP, 1994)

Expanded English version of *Naissance de l'idéologie fasciste* (Paris, 1989).
Sternhell's culminating statement of the thesis developed across *Maurice
Barrès et le nationalisme français* (1972), *La droite révolutionnaire*
(1978), and *Ni droite ni gauche* (1983): fascism was not a parenthesis, an
anti-ideology, or a product of the Great War, but a coherent political
culture with an intellectual pedigree as respectable as liberalism's or
Marxism's — the synthesis of **organic tribal nationalism** with the
**antimaterialist revision of Marxism** carried out by Georges Sorel and his
school. The ideology was essentially complete before August 1914; the war
supplied only the sociological conditions for its political victory in Italy.

Key claim clusters, all grounded to the text: the two-pillar definition of
fascism and the fascism/Nazism firewall (Introduction); Sorel's full system —
myth, violence, pessimism, anti-Cartesianism, the Action française junction,
the *L'Indépendance* antisemitic campaigns including the blood-libel material
(Ch. 1); the New School, Cercle Proudhon, and "France as laboratory" thesis

## Volume Synthesis Note

Sternhell's book gives the wiki's fascism cluster its missing genealogical
layer. Where [[paxton-anatomy-of-fascism-2004|Paxton]] defines fascism by
what fascists did and [[payne-history-of-fascism-1995|Payne]] by typological
description, Sternhell insists the ideas came first and were complete before
1914: a synthesis of Sorelian antimaterialist Marxism-revision with organic
nationalism, incubated in France (Cercle Proudhon), realized in Italy through
the syndicalist migration from class to nation. The three now form a
productive triangle on [[generic-fascism]]: Paxton explicitly demotes
pre-1914 intellectual history that Sternhell makes causal; Payne's narrative
corroborates Sternhell's left-origins evidence while rejecting a one-axis
placement.

Cross-volume tensions worth flagging: (1) on the [[march-on-rome-1922]],
Paxton/Payne stress conservative calculation and squadrist leverage where
Sternhell stresses elite ideological "impregnation" — complementary but
differently weighted; (2) Sternhell's fascism/Nazism firewall (racism not a
necessary condition of fascism) is sharper than Payne's typology and cuts
against any unified generic model; (3) his materials feed both positions of
[[fascism-and-the-political-left]] while his own "neither left nor right"
synthesis refuses both. The book's normative frame is explicit — it ends by
reaffirming "the universalism and humanism of the Enlightenment" — and its
thesis remains contested by the De Felice/Gentile school, which the wiki
records rather than resolves.
