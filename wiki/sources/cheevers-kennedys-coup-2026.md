# Kennedy's Coup: A White House Plot, a Saigon Murder, and America's Descent into Vietnam (Jack Cheevers, 2026)

Journalistic history of the Kennedy administration's role in the November 1963 coup against South Vietnamese President Ngo Dinh Diem and his brother Ngo Dinh Nhu. Argues the coup was not merely tolerated but actively facilitated by key US officials and that its chaotic aftermath was a major driver of the subsequent Americanization of the Vietnam War under Johnson.

## Key Claims / Thesis (high level from Epilogue and framing)

- The anti-Diem coup was not an inevitable or purely South Vietnamese affair; the Kennedy administration (via Hilsman's unauthorized "green light," televised criticism, and aid cut) supplied critical accelerants and assurances to the plotting generals.
- Diem and Nhu were murdered on Nov 2, 1963 (order attributed to Big Minh); their deaths and the ensuing factional chaos destroyed the relatively stable (if flawed) SVN government and created the political vacuum that justified deeper US military intervention.
- JFK personally opposed large US ground troops and had indicated desire to wind down advisory presence after 1964 election, but his handling of the coup (lax control, sensitivity to US press, failure to protect Diem or restrain Lodge) made escalation more likely.
- Counterfactual: a surviving Diem regime might have resisted US combat troops in 1965, continued strategic hamlets (with fixes), and avoided the rapid descent into "free fall" that followed.

## Historiography Note (to be expanded on source page and event pages)

Cheevers draws on post-Cold War declassifications unavailable to earlier writers (Sheehan, Karnow, Halberstam). Positions the 1963 coup as a pivotal "what if" turning point more consequential than often credited in standard narratives. Cross-reference with Logevall (*Embers of War*), Goscha (*Vietnam: A New History*), and FRUS Vietnam volumes for documentary baseline. The book is explicitly critical of the Kennedy inner circle's decision-making.
