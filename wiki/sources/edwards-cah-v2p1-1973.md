# The Cambridge Ancient History, Vol. II, Part 1 (1973)

**History of the Middle East and the Aegean Region, c. 1800–1380 BC.**
Edited by I. E. S. Edwards, the late C. J. Gadd, N. G. L. Hammond and
E. Sollberger. Cambridge University Press, 3rd edition, 1973.

This is the second-edition CAH volume that bridges the gap left in the collection
between the already-ingested [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|Vol. I, Part 2]] (which
carried the Near East to c. 1750 BCE) and the surviving Vols. V–XIV. It covers the
mature [[middle-bronze-age|Middle Bronze Age]] and the opening of the
[[late-bronze-age|Late Bronze Age]] across the whole eastern Mediterranean world:
the Amorite kingdoms and Hammurabi, the Egyptian Second Intermediate Period and
the New Kingdom to Amenophis III, the rise of the Hittite kingdom, the Minoan
palaces and the first Mycenaeans. It is the natural continuation of the
[[old-assyrian-trade|Old Assyrian]], [[amorites|Amorite]] and
[[coming-of-the-greeks-1900bce|coming-of-the-Greeks]] threads opened in Vol. I.2.

The other missing CAH backbone volumes — II.2 (c. 1380–1000 BC), III.1–III.3
(10th–6th centuries BC) and IV (Persia/Greece to 479 BC) — remain unsourced; see
[[../../Outstanding Sources|Outstanding Sources]].

## Editors' Framing (from the Preface)

The editors set the volume's spine as four interlocking stories: (1) the **Amorite
ascendancy** in Western Asia and its greatest dynasty, that of **Hammurabi the
"lawgiver,"** soon displaced in Babylonia by the **Kassites** (the longest dynasty
in Mesopotamian history); (2) the rise of the **Hittites** in Anatolia, "the
beginning of the first Indo-European empire," which would eventually destroy
Amorite Babylon; (3) the **Hyksos** domination of Egypt — Asiatics who took the
Delta "without a battle" by gradual infiltration, aided by the horse-drawn chariot
and Egyptian weakness, and their expulsion by the warrior kings of the **Eighteenth
Dynasty**, whose empire reached the Euphrates and deep into Nubia until stagnation
set in under **Amenophis III**; and (4) in the Aegean, the **Minoan** palace
civilization of Crete and, on the mainland, the arrival of Indo-European
Greek-speakers in the Middle Bronze Age and the subsequent rise of **Mycenaean**
civilization, whose conquerors of Crete adapted the Minoan linear script into
**Linear B** — the earliest Greek texts.

and it interleaves regions chronologically. Per the Large-Volume Protocol for the
CAH, the chapters are grouped here into **six regionally/thematically coherent
