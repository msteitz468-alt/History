# The Hellenistic World (F. W. Walbank)

The classic modern overview of the **[[hellenistic-world|Hellenistic world]]** — the three centuries from
the death of Alexander (323 BCE) to the death of Cleopatra and the Roman annexation of Egypt (30 BCE) —
by the great historian F. W. Walbank. The sixth source of the Phase-9 batch.

## Coverage and Themes

Walbank surveys the whole Hellenistic system:

- **The kingdoms** — the **Ptolemaic**, **Seleucid**, **Antigonid** and lesser monarchies (Pergamon,
  Bactria) that the **Diadochi** carved from Alexander's empire, their courts, armies, and rivalries.
- **The polis in a world of kings** — how the Greek city adapted to (and was patronized and constrained
  by) the great monarchies; federalism and the leagues; ruler-cult.
- **Economy and society** — the new scale of trade, coinage and royal economies (above all the
  bureaucratic Ptolemaic state in Egypt), cities old and new, and the Greek diaspora.
- **The meeting of cultures** — the spread of Greek language and institutions and their interaction with
  Egyptian, Babylonian, Jewish and Iranian traditions, treated with **caution**: Walbank emphasizes the
  **limits of fusion** and the enduring divide between Greek/Macedonian rulers and indigenous subjects,
  against the older romantic synthesis.
- **The coming of Rome** — the absorption of the Hellenistic East into the Roman orbit, the climactic
  meeting of the two systems.

## Significance

For decades the **standard one-volume introduction** to the period, Walbank's book gives the wiki its
balanced modern frame for the Hellenistic age — the measured counterpoint to the older, idealizing
**[[jouguet-macedonian-imperialism|Jouguet]]** synthesis, and the natural complement to the wiki's
kingdom-by-kingdom coverage.
