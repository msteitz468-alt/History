# Gandhi Before India — Ramachandra Guha (2013)

**Full citation**: Guha, Ramachandra. *Gandhi Before India*. New Delhi: Penguin Books India, 2013. (US/UK editions: Alfred A. Knopf / Allen Lane, 2014.)

This is the first volume of Guha's two-volume biography of Mohandas K. Gandhi. It covers the formative years from birth in 1869 through his departure from South Africa in 1914. It is listed in the project's Processing List (Top 50 Biographies) as the definitive modern account of Gandhi's pre-India career and the invention of satyagraha.

## Overview and Thesis

Guha reconstructs Gandhi's "less known and sometimes forgotten years" (Porbandar, Rajkot, Bombay, London, Durban, Johannesburg) from contemporary records rather than later autobiography or hagiography. The central argument is that the first forty-five years — especially the two decades in South Africa — made the Mahatma. In South Africa Gandhi elaborated his ecumenism (religious, social, linguistic), developed the technique and philosophy of satyagraha as an "honourable instrument," forged a coalition across class, language and religion among the Indian diaspora, and learned to confront the modern state with disciplined, conscience-based non-violent resistance.

Key themes: Gandhi as middle-caste Bania adapting and transcending caste norms; the influence of vegetarianism, Theosophy, Tolstoy, and Ruskin; the creation of *Indian Opinion* and the Phoenix Settlement / Tolstoy Farm experiments; the shift from loyalist petitioning to mass satyagraha (1906–1914); and the absence of bitterness toward the "ruling race" combined with unyielding insistence on rights.

## Methodological Notes

- Primary reliance on the *Collected Works of Mahatma Gandhi* (early volumes) plus the under-used incoming correspondence to Gandhi.
- Extensive use of private papers of key associates (Gokhale, Polak, Kallenbach, Doke, Andrews).
- Government records (Natal, Transvaal, India Office, Viceroy) to reconstruct the view from the other side (Milner, Smuts).
- Explicit caution on retrospective sources including Gandhi's own *An Autobiography* (written 1920s); preference for letters, contemporary journalism, and official files written at the time.
- Strong attention to Indian diasporic diversity (Gujarati merchants, Tamil labourers, Chinese satyagrahis) and to the roles of women (Kasturba, Millie Polak, Sonja Schlesin, Tamil volunteers).

## Key Contributions to the Wiki

- The only full scholarly reconstruction of the South African laboratory where satyagraha was forged.
- Granular account of the 1906–1914 campaigns (Transvaal registration, 1913 strikes and marches) with precise causal sequences.
- Rich portraits of the inner circle (Polak, Kallenbach, Sonja Schlesin, Thambi Naidoo, Cachalia, Maganlal Gandhi) and adversaries (Smuts, Milner).
- Corrective on Gandhi's religious formation: deep but selective engagement with Christianity (Tolstoy, Sermon on the Mount) and pragmatic political engagement with Muslims; limited direct influence from the Koran.
- Demonstration of how South African experience produced both the method (satyagraha) and the moral style (absence of hatred, emphasis on self-suffering) later deployed in India.
