# Islamic Science and the Making of the European Renaissance (Saliba, 2007)

A revisionist history of **Islamic science** that overturns the "classical narrative." Ingested as a
DEEP pass filling the wiki's thin science layer — it enriches [[islamic-science-medieval]] and
[[the-translation-movement]] and supplies what they lack: Saliba's thesis on the **origins** of Islamic
science in the administrative needs of the early state; the sustained Muslim **mathematical critique of
Ptolemy** ([[islamic-astronomy]], the Maragha school, the [[tusi-couple|Ṭūsī couple]], [[ibn-al-shatir|Ibn
al-Shāṭir]]); and his signature argument that these techniques reached **Copernicus**, making Islamic
science constitutive of the European Renaissance.

Deployed-subagent strategy, **5 agents** mapped to the chapters, weighted toward the distinctive chapters
(origins thesis; the astronomical innovations; the Copernican connection). Body content: lines
~**343–12,900** of `raw/_saliba-wrapped.txt`; later lines are endnotes, bibliography, and index.
