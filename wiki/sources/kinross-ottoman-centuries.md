# Lord Kinross, *The Ottoman Centuries* (1977)

A classic narrative of the whole Ottoman span, ingested **targeted**. With the Ottoman spine
already built (Imber, Finkel, Mikhail), Kinross was mined for the **one clear gap** and for
narrative enrichment rather than re-ingested wholesale.

This ingest:
- **Created** [[mehmed-ii]] (the Conqueror / *Fatih*) — the sultan who took Constantinople and
  made the Ottomans an imperial power claiming the inheritance of Rome and Byzantium; a clear
  gap (he had been covered only within [[ottoman-empire]] and [[fall-of-constantinople-1453]]).
- **Deepened** [[fall-of-constantinople-1453]] and [[ottoman-empire]] with Kinross's narrative
  of Mehmed and the imperial transformation.

Kinross's framing is the traditional "rise and fall" arc (now qualified by the
[[ottoman-decline-thesis|revisionist critique]] of "decline"), but his narrative remains a
vivid source for the personalities and events of Ottoman history.
