# The Rise of Theodore Roosevelt (Edmund Morris, 1979)

**The first volume of Edmund Morris's trilogy on Theodore Roosevelt.** Covers TR's life from birth (27 October 1858) through his accession to the presidency on 14 September 1901 following the assassination of William McKinley. A landmark narrative biography that restores the human scale, contradictions, and sheer energy of the pre-presidential Roosevelt.

## Claims

### S01 Claims (Range 01 ONLY)
- **S01-01**: 1907 New Year’s Day White House Reception Framing — Prologue opens with TR receiving citizens at ~50 handshakes/minute in 1907, symbolizing his vitality and accessibility at height of power. Quote: “AT ELEVEN O’CLOCK PRECISELY the sound of trumpets echoes within the White House...” (ll. ~264+)
- **S01-04**: Birth of Theodore Roosevelt Jr. 27 Oct 1858 at 28 East 20th St, NYC; 8.5 lbs; grandmother calls “sweet... pretty,” mother says “terrapin.” 
- **S01-05**: Early asthma — father walks him at night and drives carriage for air; “sitting up in bed gasping.” 
- **S01-06/07**: Theodore Sr. (27 at birth) — vigorous importer, philanthropist (“troublesome conscience”), founder Children’s Aid; Mittie Bulloch (Georgia) — moonlight-white skin, “noir doré” hair.
- **S01-10**: 1871 paternal challenge: “You have the mind but you have not the body... You must make your body.”
- **S01-11**: 1872 spectacles open “an entirely new world”; transformative for observation/writing.
- **S01-14**: Theodore Sr. dies 9 Feb 1878; TR draws thick diary slash; lifelong idealization + grief.
- **S01-15/17**: Courtship of Alice Hathaway Lee (met 1878 at Chestnut Hill); persistent despite rejections; engagement Jan 1880.
- **S01-18/19**: Harvard magna cum laude 1880; rejects doctor’s sedentary advice; begins *Naval War of 1812* research at Astor Library while at Columbia Law.
- **S01-20**: First politics at Morton Hall (21st District Republican); learns machine from bottom; resolves to join “governing class.”
- Additional from Range 01: Harvard boxing (refuses to quit after foul), early natural history museum, Grand Tour, Civil War family division (hires substitute), early writing voice.

### S02 Claims (Range 02 ONLY)
- **S02-04/05**: Joe Murray backs TR over machine candidate for Assembly (1881); nominated, elected youngest member (age 23) by double margin; platform “strong Republican on State matters, but independent on local.”
- **S02-06**: *Naval War of 1812* published 1882; “last word,” adopted by Navy, ordered aboard ships; call for modern fleet.
- **S02-07/09**: Albany arrival as “Dude” (eyeglass, sideburns); maiden speech on deadlock; dubbed “Cyclone Assemblyman.”
- **S02-11/12**: Westbrook/Gould corruption resolution (1882) — names “sharks and swindlers”; passes initial vote but Judiciary whitewashes; TR condemns colleagues for “tainting themselves with the leper.”
- **S02-16**: Civil Service Reform bill passes (1883) with Cleveland support.
- **S02-17**: Dakota investment decision (Sept 1883) — writes $14k check impulsively after buffalo hunt; “I’ll buy those cattle.”
- **S02-19**: Returns to Albany post-14 Feb 1884 double tragedy (Alice + Mittie die same day); drives self relentlessly.
- **S02-20/21**: Utica (1884) tops delegate vote for Edmunds; Chicago fights for Lynch chair and against Blaine to end.
- **S02-23**: Post-Chicago: “I am going cattle-ranching in Dakota.”
- Additional: Five-Cent Bill confession of error (supports Cleveland veto); attempted sex blackmail trap; Matterhorn climb 1881; Garfield assassination news.

### S03 Claims (Range 03 ONLY)
- **S03-04/11/12**: Blaine support backlash; 1884 campaign speeches + Cleveland criticism; post-election self-assessment.
- **S03-07**: Mingusville bully confrontation — knocked out gunman with three blows; “Four Eyes” leaves town.
- **S03-08**: Range dispute with de Morès/Paddock; confronts Paddock directly: “I have come over to see when you want to begin the killing.”
- **S03-09**: Private memorial for Alice: “the light went from my life forever.”
- **S03-10/15**: Big game hunts (grizzly brain shot; near-drowning on Manitou); roundup endurance (100 mi/day, 40-hr stretches).
- **S03-13/25/26**: 1886–87 winter (“Blue Snow”) devastates range; “countless carcasses... rolling over... all four stiffened legs... point skyward”; no spring roundup.
- **S03-16**: Re-encounter/engagement to Edith Carow (Nov 1885, secret due to widowhood interval).
- **S03-18**: Horse-thief boat chase/capture (Finnegan gang) — 150+ miles downriver in subzero; “We took them absolutely by surprise.”
- **S03-20/21/23**: Dickinson 4th of July speech (Packard prophesies presidency); 1886 mayoral run (nominated by bosses); third place (Hewitt 90k, George 68k, TR 60k).
- **S03-24**: Honeymoon + European impressions (1886–87); meets Spring Rice.
- Additional: Elkhorn ranch founding; *Hunting Trips of a Ranchman* publication; horse-thief episode + literary work while guarding prisoners; return East after mayoral defeat.

### S04 Claims (Range 04 ONLY)
- **S04-01/02**: 1886–87 winter losses ~65% of TR herd; Medora declines into ghost town after de Morès closure.

### Volume Synthesis Note

Morris’s *The Rise of Theodore Roosevelt* (1979/2001) supplies the indispensable personal narrative spine for TR’s ascent from asthmatic Knickerbocker child (1858) through self-overcoming (father’s body challenge, spectacles, boxing), political apprenticeship (“Cyclone Assemblyman,” Westbrook fight, Civil Service bill), personal catastrophe (1884 double death), Western self-reinvention (Dakota, Elkhorn, boat chase, hunts), reform celebrity (Police Commissioner midnight patrols, Sunday law, Parker war), national office (Asst. Navy, Dewey orders, resignation), and war hero (Rough Riders hijack, Las Guásimas, “wolf rising” charge at Kettle Hill/San Juan 1 Jul 1898, round-robin). Key contributions: vivid reconstruction of the “dividing line” of combat; exhaustive primary sourcing (TR Papers, diaries, newspapers, trooper interviews, Lodge/Hanna/Platt correspondence); fair portrayal of contradictions (imperialist yet conservation founder; moralist reformer who courted machine; “bull luck” + relentless effort). The volume ends with the 13 Sep 1901 telegram on Mount Marcy and TR’s accession — the hinge to the presidency. It complements Cambridge series material on the era and earlier Lincoln/Grant bios by restoring agency, personality, and contingency to the emergence of the modern activist American executive and the U.S. turn to global power at century’s end. No major contradictions with prior wiki content; strengthens collection coverage for Long 19th Century north-america narrative.
