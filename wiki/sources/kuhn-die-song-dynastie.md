# Die Song-Dynastie (Kuhn, 1987)

A social and cultural history of the [[song-dynasty|Song]] (960–1279) arguing that it constituted a
**"new society"** — the China of the scholar-officials, examinations, commerce, printing, and
Neo-Confucianism that succeeded the aristocratic medieval order. Fills the Song gap in the wiki's
China backbone.

## Kuhn's thesis

Kuhn's title — *eine neue Gesellschaft im Spiegel ihrer Kultur* — states the argument: Song culture
is the **mirror of a transformed society**. The decisive change was social: the old **aristocracy**
(*Aristokratie*) that had dominated through the Tang was displaced by the **scholar-officials**
(*Beamten-Gelehrte*; Chinese *shi-da-fu*) — a class defined not by birth but by **classical
education and success in the examinations** (*Beamtenprüfungen*). The Song "embodied like no other
dynasty the **state of the scholar-officials**." From this flow the volume's themes: the
recruitment, origins, and functions of the official class; the commercial and urban revolution; the
flowering of printing, art, and technology; and the rise of **Neo-Confucianism** as the literati's
philosophy.
