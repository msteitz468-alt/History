## Volume Synthesis Note

Part 2 completes Volume I’s North America arc: regional colonial and settlement-pressure histories (Fowler Plains; Lamar & Truett Southwest/California; Fisher Northwest) feed a shared U.S./Canadian **reservation period** (Hoxie: allotment, boarding schools, IRA, termination, pan-Indian politics), while Ray’s Subarctic and Damas’s Arctic document **northern continuities**—fur trade and Arctic adaptation without the eastern agricultural-dispossession path—into modern claims politics (James Bay, Berger, ANCSA, Nunavut, Greenland Home Rule). Washburn’s mid-1990s “Renaissance” essay is the volume’s most personal chapter: strongest as a multi-domain comparative checklist (demography, self-determination policy, courts, arts, health) across the United States, Canada, and Greenland; weakest as activist microhistory (Alcatraz absent; Wounded Knee 1973 as type-example).

For the wiki, I.2 **closes the continental Native North America Cambridge set** begun in [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v1-1|I.1]], thickening Plains/SW/NW/Subarctic/Arctic and modern policy pages that Gwynne, Snow, and Mann only sampled. Cross-volume tensions kept attributed include Fisher’s cautious disease magnitude, Hoxie’s “altered but whole” reservation thesis, Ray’s rejection of Calvin Martin’s “war on animals,” and Washburn’s optimistic distinctiveness vs dependency critique. Canadian and Greenland coverage is a deliberate comparative strength relative to U.S.-only surveys.
