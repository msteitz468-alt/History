# The Chronicle of Pseudo-Joshua the Stylite

A vivid, near-contemporary Syriac chronicle of **upper Mesopotamia c. 494–506** — the natural disasters
that struck **Edessa** and the **[[anastasian-war-502-506|Anastasian War]]** between Rome and Sasanian
Persia — written within a year or two of the events. The sixth source of this Phase-5 batch; a
companion to the [[pseudo-dionysius-zuqnin-chronicle|Chronicle of Zuqnin]], which later absorbed it.

## Content

The chronicle falls into two interlocking halves:

- **The years of disaster at Edessa** — a remarkably circumstantial account of **famine, locusts and
  plague** in the late 490s: failed harvests, soaring grain prices, the flooding of the city with
  starving refugees, the deaths in the streets, and the relief measures of the governor and bishop. It
  is one of late antiquity's best windows onto **subsistence crisis and urban society**.
- **The [[anastasian-war-502-506|Anastasian War (502–506)]]** — the war provoked when the shah
  **Kavadh I**, refused subsidies by the emperor **Anastasius**, invaded Roman Mesopotamia: the sack of
  Theodosiopolis, the long siege and **fall of Amida (January 503)**, the costly and mismanaged Roman
  counter-offensives, and the truce of 506, after which Anastasius fortified **Dara** as a frontier
  bastion.

## Significance

Pseudo-Joshua is among the **finest contemporary sources for the late-antique Roman–Persian frontier**
and for the texture of provincial life on it: it combines precise military and diplomatic narrative with
unusually concrete economic and social detail, all from a writer on the spot. It is also a key monument
of early **Syriac historiography** and a principal source for the reign of Anastasius in the East.
