# The New Cambridge Medieval History, Vol. 7, c. 1415–c. 1500

Seventh and **final** volume of the NCMH, covering the fifteenth century (c. 1415–c. 1500).
Successor in the backbone to [[jones-ncmh-v6-2000|NCMH Vol. 6 (c. 1300–c. 1415)]]; the close of the
NCMH series and the bridge to the early-modern world.

## Editorial Framing

Christopher Allmand's preface deliberately rejects the old view of "the final century of the
traditional Middle Ages" as "a period of decay and decline." A generation of research has instead
revealed "an age of important, indeed dynamic, achievements — some of which are the outcome of
earlier advances, others seeing their fulfilment only in the century to come." The volume is
distinctively **comparative and pan-European**: it places **sixteen thematic chapters (Parts I–III)
before** the regional "states" chapters (Part IV), on the principle that "both men and politics are
part of a wider world picture which encompasses ideas, political, religious, cultural and artistic,
as well as economic and social change" — reversing the usual "politics first, culture second." It
covers more states and far more European-level themes than its predecessor, and reaches the
thresholds of the modern world: the **printing revolution**, **humanism and the Renaissance**, the
**Atlantic voyages of discovery**, the **fall of Constantinople (1453)** and the Ottoman ascendancy,
and the consolidation of the "new monarchies."
