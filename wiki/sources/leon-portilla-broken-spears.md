# The Broken Spears (Miguel León-Portilla, ed., 1959)

The classic compilation of the **Nahua account of the [[spanish-conquest-of-mexico-1519|conquest of
Mexico]]** — the Americas gap's indigenous-voice primary source. León-Portilla braided sixteenth-century
Nahuatl texts into a chronological narrative running from the **omens** through first contact, the
**Toxcatl massacre**, the **Night of Sorrows**, the **siege**, **Cuauhtemoc's surrender** (13 August
1521), and the **elegies** for the fallen city.

## Source Notes

*The Broken Spears* is significant less for new facts than for **perspective**: for ~450 years the
conquest was narrated from Spanish accounts; León-Portilla (1959) recovered the **defeated's own
telling**, which reframes the whole event. Three things stand out for the wiki. (1) The Nahua sources
themselves locate the decisive cause in **indigenous disunity** — the Tlaxcalans and others "joined right
in" against the Mexica — corroborating the modern verdict that Cortés won through **alliances and
[[virgin-soil-epidemics|disease]]**, not lone Spanish prowess. (2) The texts are strikingly **non-
moralising** about the Spaniards (Cortés is rarely a villain); the grief is reserved for **the city**.
(3) They are **collective, oral-tradition memories**, written down by Nahua scholars (with Sahagún) and
shaped by the survivors' need to **explain Motecuhzoma's failure** and dignify a glorious fall — so they
must be read as authentic *interpretation*, not neutral chronicle. Paired with
[[diaz-conquest-of-new-spain|Bernal Díaz]] (the conqueror's eyewitness memoir, next in Phase A), it makes
the conquest a genuinely **two-sided** history — and, as León-Portilla's readers have long noted, a
template for the conquest of the Americas at large.
