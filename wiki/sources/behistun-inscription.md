# Behistun Inscription (Old Persian version, with vocabulary and commentary)

The Behistun (Bisotun) Inscription is the monumental trilingual rock relief and text of **Darius I** (r. 522–486 BCE) carved on the cliff at Behistun in the Iranian Zagros. The Old Persian version (with Elamite and Babylonian parallels) is the longest and most important contemporary Achaemenid royal inscription. This source is the 19th-century scholarly edition/vocabulary of the Old Persian text, with etymological explanations, grammatical notes, and historical commentary keyed to the columns and lines of the inscription.

## Historical Content (Darius's Own Account)

The inscription opens with Darius's self-presentation and genealogy: "I am Darius the king, son of Hystaspes, an Achaemenid, a Persian, son of a Persian, an Aryan, of Aryan seed." It then recounts the crisis after the death of Cambyses: the Magian Gaumata seized the throne by pretending to be Bardiya (Smerdis), the son of Cyrus. Darius, with the help of six Persian nobles and the favor of Ahuramazda, killed Gaumata and restored order.

Darius lists nine rebellions in the first year of his reign (in Elam, Babylon, Media, Sagartia, Margiana, etc.), claiming to have fought 19 battles and captured 9 "liar-kings." He emphasizes that he "did not allow falsehood (drauga)" and that "by the favor of Ahuramazda" he brought the empire back under control. The text also records building activities, the provinces of the empire, and a prayer for the protection of Persia.

The edition in this source provides the vocabulary for key terms (e.g., GAUMATA, the name of the impostor; entries for the names of rebel leaders like Atrines, Nidintu-Bel, Fravartish, Skunkha the Scythian) and discusses the historical reliability by comparing with Greek historians.

## Volume Synthesis Note (main thread)

This source is a 19th-century scholarly vocabulary and commentary keyed to the Old Persian text of Darius I's trilingual rock inscription at Behistun (Bisotun/Bagastana), c. 520 BCE. The file preserves etymological analysis, grammatical notes, and extensive historical/geographical commentary drawn directly from the inscription's columns and lines (with cross-references to Persepolis, Nakhsh-i-Rustam, and Median/Babylonian versions).

**Core contribution:** The authentic first-person Achaemenid voice on the critical events of 522–521 BCE. Darius recounts his genealogy ("I am Darius, son of Hystaspes, an Achaemenid, a Persian, son of a Persian, an Aryan"), the usurpation by Gaumata the Magian (who impersonated Bardiya/Smerdis, son of Cyrus, after Cambyses' death), the restoration of the ayadana (places of worship) and religious observances that the usurper had destroyed, and the suppression of nine "liar-kings" in rebellions across the empire (Elam, Babylon, Media, Sagartia, Margiana/Thatagush, etc.) in a single year through 19 battles "by the favor of Ahuramazda." The text frames all opposition as drauga ("the Lie") and positions Darius as the restorer of arta (truth/order).

**Value vs. Greek sources:** The edition repeatedly contrasts the inscription with Herodotus (who has two Magi brothers and different details), Ctesias (who uses the Zend title Spendadates), and Justin/Trogus. The inscription is presented as corrective primary evidence, exposing Greek distortions while preserving the Magophonia feast and religious-reformer image of Darius.

**Empire geography and provinces:** Detailed onomastics and locations for Athura (Assyria, primitive orthography), Gadara/Gandhara (Indus banks, with later name migration to western Gandhara/Candahar via relic transport and Yue-chi incursions), Asagarta (between Media and Parthia, politically tied to Media; rebel Chitratakhma claimed Cyaxares descent), Khwarizm/'Uvarazmish (at Oxus mouth, ancient reverence), Thatagush/Sattagydia (Paropamisus/Hindu Kush, bounded by Arachosia, Gandara, Margush, Saka), Katapatuka (Cappadocia), 'Ufratauva (Euphrates, Arian etymology suggesting early Arian precedence in Mesopotamia), Arakadrish (mountain/range east of Persis, origin of Gaumata), Gudrush (Media, Phraortes' last battle), etc.

**Scythians/Saka:** Sakukha (Skunkha) uniquely depicted on the Behistun relief with the national high/pointed conical cap (Tigrakhuda = "pointed-hatted" or Tigris-dwelling western Saka on the upper Tigris; listed near Babylon/Assyria). Sakā tyaiy paradraya = "Scythians beyond the sea" (European/northern Scythians/Thracians of the Danube/Dniester/Don, subdued in Darius's Hellespont expedition). High caps link to Nimrud iconography (periods of Scythian control/alliance in Assyria). Darius's personal campaign against them is in the mutilated 5th column.

**Ideology and administration:** drauga ("the Lie") vs. truth (thrad/hamahyāyā thrada = "true in every thing" or performance/completion); arika (heretics/Magi, followers of proscribed sacrificial worship; their predominance paved the way for the Magian revolution; Darius counts not being one among virtues securing Ormazd's favor). Royal titulary: khshatram (crown/empire), khshayathiya (king), khshathrapa (satrap). Building/sculpture: tachakam ("sculpture"); hamatakhshiya ("I laboured"). Calendar months of ancient Arian/seasonal origin (Thigarčish/Thegarsis, Thuravahara, etc.), predating Zend Avesta forms. Bhagas/"superior intelligences" (Ormazd as chief) exercise influence on worldly matters; ayadana restored.

**Other:** Gobryas (Gaubruwa), son of Mardonius, one of the Seven conspirators; later led Susiana expedition (high noble status confirmed by Herodotus; family name alternation). Atrines (Atrina) led Susiana revolt (Arian name; native extraction implies early Arian colonization of Susiana by Persian monarchy era). Arakha (Armenian pretender as Nabochodrossor son of Nabonidus) in Babylon; exhibited on the triumphal tablet. Arian etymologies throughout (links to Sanskrit/Zend/Pehlevi); notes on Median translations adopting Persian terms as proper names.

**Limitations and bias:** Royal propaganda presenting all opponents as "liars" and Darius as divinely favored restorer of order. 19th-c. edition is philological (cautious on etymologies/grammar, e.g., doubts on exact renderings of thrada or arika) with extensive comparison to classical sources and later Iranian tradition. Value for empire structure, onomastics, and ideology is unmatched; geography sometimes conjectural (e.g., Arakadrish on eastern Kerman borders).

This source supplies the authentic Achaemenid voice that underpins the wiki's treatment of the early Persian empire, correcting and enriching Greek-centric narratives (esp. Herodotus on the false Smerdis). It directly grounds claims about Darius's consolidation, provincial organization, Scythian campaigns, and royal ideology in the king's own words.

**Accession & Gaumata (Range 1):**
- Gaumata the Magian dispossessed Cambyses of Persia and Media; suppressed the ayadana (places of worship) and religious observances (including sacred chanting); Darius overthrew him, restored what had been taken, and recorded the events in the first person at Behistun.
  - Quote examples: "Gaumdta hya Magush ddind Kabujiyam utd Pdrsani utd Mddam" (ll. 597–598); "Ayad(a)na tytl Gaumrita hya Magnsh viyaka, ... adam niya(?)j>:trayain, kiirahytl abicharish..." (ll. 1357–1369); "In nothing is the Bchistun Inscription more valuable than in the brief, but authentic account which it contains of tho Magian usurpation..." (ll. ~7095–7099); comparisons to Herodotus (two Magi, name Smerdis), Ctesias (Spendadates), Justin (different sequence).

- Atrines (Atrina) excited a revolt in Susiana immediately after Darius's accession; name of Arian etymology (not in Greek history); implies early Arian colonization of Susiana.
  - Quote: "The name of Atrina, which is borne in the inscriptions by the chief who oxcitod a revolt in Susiaua immediately after tho accession of Darius, is not to be found I believe iu Grecian history." (ll. 608–610).

- Arakha, an Armenian, threw Babylonia into revolt pretending to be Nabochodrossor son of Nabonidus; exhibited among captives on the Behistun triumphal tablet.
  - Quote: "Arakha was the name of an Armenian rcbol who throw Babylonia into rovolt, pretending to be Nabochodrossor, tho son of Nabonidus. He is exhibited at Behistun among the captive figures of the triumphal tablet." (ll. 1753–1756).

**Provinces & Geography (Range 2):**
- Athura = Assyria (primitive orthography; province north of Babylon; inflected as feminine noun; locative in ayā).
- Gadara/Gandhara = country along the Indus and tributaries from the mountains (VIIth satrapy; name later carried west to Candahar area via ~A.D. 400 relic migration + Yue-chi incursions; Chinese sources trace water-pot of Fo transport).
- Asagarta (Sagartia) = between Media and Parthia (politically dependent on Media); rebel Chitratakhma claimed descent from Cyaxares' family; suppression claimed as action "in Media."
- Khwarizm/'Uvarazmish = at mouth of the Oxus; extraordinary ancient reverence; indifferent orthography.
- Thatagush (Sattagydia) = Paropamisus (Hindu Kush) region, bounded by Arachosia, Gandara, Margush, Saka.
- 'Ufratauva (Euphrates) = Arian etymology suggesting Arian precedence before Semitic colonization in Shinar/Mesopotamia.
- Arakadrish = mountain/range east of Persis Proper (origin of Gaumata/Smerdis Magus); Darius removed the Magi's Ecbatana there.
- Gudrush = city in Media (near modern Qazvin/Casvin conjectured); site of Phraortes' (Fravartiš) last battle; he fled to Rhagae (Raga) and was brought to Darius at Ecbatana.

- Arian colonization inferences: Native names in Susiana revolt (Atrina, Upadarma) show Arian penetration by the time of the Persian monarchy.

**Ideology, Scythians, Building (Range 3):**
- drauga = "the Lie" (explicitly contrasted with truth/order; Darius urges "hachā daraugā darsham(a) patipayuvā" = "from the Lie practise restraint").
- arika = heretics/Magi (followers of proscribed sacrificial worship/Magian invocations; their predominance paved the way for the Magian revolution; Darius counts not being one among virtues securing Ormazd's favour).
- Sakukha (Skunkha) = Scythian leader depicted on Behistun relief with national high/pointed conical cap (Tigrakhuda = "pointed-hatted"/Tigris-dwelling western Saka on upper Tigris, listed near Babylon/Assyria). High caps link to Nimrud (periods of Scythian control/alliance in Assyria).

- tachakam = "sculpture"; hamatakhshiya = "I laboured" (building/sculpture activity; Behistun monument exemplifies royal carving programs).
- Gobryas (Gaubruwa), son of Mardonius, one of the seven conspirators; later led Susiana expedition (high noble; Herodotus confirms marriage to Darius's sister; family name alternation preserved in inscriptions).

- Royal titulary and structure: khshatram (crown/empire), khshayathiya (king), khshathrapa (satrap); provinces headed by Asagarta in eastern lists.
