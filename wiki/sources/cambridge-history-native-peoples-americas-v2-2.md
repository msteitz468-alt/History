## Volume Synthesis Note

Part 2 completes Cambridge’s Mesoamerica volume by reversing the vantage of Part 1:
from archaeological culture-history to **ethnohistory of native peoples under and after
Spanish rule**. MacLeod’s overview sets the controlling problem — how to write continuous
indigenous history when categories (“Indian”), sources, and demography are colonial
products — and inventories regional models (Nahuatl continuity; Yucatecan neglect;
highland Maya extraction; northern silver-mission-war frontiers). Nine regional chapters
then refuse a single narrative: Northwest mission mosaics (Deeds), Northeastern ethnocide
and Tlaxcalan colonization (Frye), West Mexico’s ranchero myth vs estate–Indian
bi-modality and the Mixtón War (van Young), Central Mexico’s *altepetl*/cabildo
remodeling (Cline) and post-Independence land/identity politics (Schryer), Gulf and
Oaxaca long-run ethnohistories (Deans-Smith; Romero Frizzi), and the two Maya arcs —
lowland conquest→Caste War (Jones) and highland survival through Liberal coffee labor
and late-twentieth-century violence (Lovell).

For the wiki, Part 2 **closes the colonial–modern Mesoamerica gap** left open by Part 1
and by thinner CWH/Berdan coverage. It supplies a full regional process lattice, major
events (Chichimeca War, Mixtón, Yucatán and Guatemala conquests, Caste War), and
frontier actors (Rarámuri, Yaqui, Mayo, Seri, Apache). Cross-links to Andean
[[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|CHNPA III]] and to existing colonial
process pages make Mesoamerica comparable rather than exceptional. Volume II as a whole
now runs from Paleoindian peopling to EZLN and Guatemalan peace negotiations — the
intended Cambridge span.
