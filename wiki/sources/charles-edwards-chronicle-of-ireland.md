# The Chronicle of Ireland

The reconstructed **common source of the Irish annals** — the single lost chronicle (closing in 911)
from which the surviving medieval Irish annal-collections descend — and thus the **chronological backbone
of early medieval Ireland** and of Gaelic Scotland. Substituted into this Phase-5 batch (for the corrupt
TTH 07) as a Translated Texts for Historians volume; reconstructed by **T. M. Charles-Edwards**.

## The Reconstruction

No medieval manuscript called "the Chronicle of Ireland" survives. What survive are later annal-sets —
the **Annals of Ulster**, the **Annals of Tigernach**, the **Chronicon Scotorum**, the **Annals of
Inisfallen**, and others — which agree closely down to **911** and then diverge. From this Charles-Edwards
infers a **shared archetype**, the "Chronicle of Ireland," and reconstructs it by collation. Its textual
history points to **[[iona|Iona]]** as the home of the early chronicle (to c. 740), after which the record
passed to the **Irish midlands** (the Clonmacnoise group of monasteries).

## Character and Content

The text is **annalistic**: brief year-by-year entries recording the **deaths of kings, abbots and
bishops**, **battles** and dynastic struggles among the Irish and Dál Riata, Viking **raids** from the
late eighth century, **plagues**, famines, eclipses and ecclesiastical affairs. The earliest sections
(fifth–sixth century, including the conventional dates for **St Patrick**) are thin and partly
retrospective; from the later seventh century the annals become a broadly **contemporary record** and
the most reliable framework we possess for the political and ecclesiastical history of early Christian
Ireland and Iona-centred Scotland.

## Significance

The Chronicle is the **foundation source for early medieval Ireland** — the skeleton on which all
narrative reconstruction of Irish kingship, the great churches, and the Viking impact is built — and a
major witness for **northern Britain** (Dál Riata, the Picts, Northumbria) through the lens of the
**Columban familia** of Iona. It complements **Bede** and **Adomnán's** *Life of Columba* and anchors
the chronology of the Insular world.
