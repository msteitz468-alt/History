# Early Modern Japan in Asia and the World (Howell ed., New CHJ Vol. II, 2023)

The current, thematic reference on **early modern Japan (c. 1580–1877)** — the New Cambridge
History of Japan's middle volume — read through the lenses of **global connection,
environment, science, status, the peripheries, and culture** rather than straight political
narrative. Its task here is to **deepen and refine** the early-modern spine the wiki built from
the older CHJ Vols 3–5, and to add discrete pages for genuinely new angles.

Howell's Introduction ("Genealogies of Japanese Early Modernity") is a historiographical
essay on the very concept of **"early modern" / *kinsei***: he argues that early modernity as
a periodization is, surprisingly, **older in East Asian than in European historiography**, and
that *kinsei* "has nothing to do with feudalism" — overturning the older CHJ Vol. 4 framing
(Japanese *kinsei* as "more feudal than modern" vs. Western "early modern" as "more modern than
feudal"). The volume thus treats Japanese early modernity on its own terms and within Asian and
global circuits.
