# Nations: The Long History and Deep Roots of Political Ethnicity and Nationalism (Gat & Yakobson, 2013)

The wiki's anchor source for the **antiquity-of-nations debate** and the strongest scholarly
statement of the **"deep roots"** position. It is the conceptual backbone for the
[[nationalism]], [[nation]], [[ethnicity]], and [[national-state]] pages, and for the
[[antiquity-of-nations]] controversy. Its companion is Gat's evolutionary
[[gat-war-in-human-civilization-2006|War in Human Civilization]] — *Nations* applies the same
kin–selection / cultural-marker logic to the political community.

## Thesis: nationalism is a form of political ethnicity

- **The great fault line.** Since the 1980s the field has split between **modernists** —
  who hold the nation "a creation of modernity," made possible only by print, capitalism,
  industrialization, urbanization, mass education, and the mobilizing state (Gellner,
  Anderson, Hobsbawm) — and **traditionalists** ("primordial" or "perennial"), who hold
  nationhood "older, existed before modernity… perhaps as far back as antiquity, and not only
  in Europe but throughout the world" (Anthony Smith, John Armstrong, Adrian Hastings).
- **Transcend, don't just take sides.** Gat is "closer to" the traditionalists but argues the
  debate must be "transcended." His move: accept **Gellner's own definition** of the nation —
  "a rough congruence between culture or ethnicity and state" — and observe that, so defined,
  "nations are not confined to modern times."
- **Ethnicity has always been political.** "By and large, nationalism is one particular form
  of a broader phenomenon, that of political ethnicity; and ethnicity has always been highly
  political, ever since the emergence of the state and even before." (Ethnicity = "a
  population of shared kinship (real or perceived) and culture.")
- **The reciprocal engine.** "Ethnicity made the state and the state made ethnicity, in a
  reciprocal and dialectical process." Where a state "roughly encompassed and remained largely
  confined to an entire generally distinct ethnic space… identified with a particular
  *Staatsvolk*, the result is known as a **national state**."
- **Empires were not ethnically blind.** The modernist picture of premodern empires as
  ethnically indifferent elite structures is "highly simplistic… nearly all of them were
  grounded in and relied upon a dominant ethnic nucleus."
- **The civic/ethnic dichotomy is overdrawn.** "There have been very few nations, if any,
  whose existence was divorced from ethnicity"; even civic nationalism "generates assimilation
  into the ethnonational community." A better distinction: ethnic nationalism emphasizes
  *descent + culture*; civic, *state territory + culture*.
- **What modernity did.** Gat fully grants modernity's transformation — "the tremendous growth
  of modern nationalism" via mass integration and the empowerment of the masses — but as
  *release and enhancement* of an old form, not creation *ex nihilo*. Nationalism is "the
  elephant in the room" that liberal and Marxist theory persistently failed to see.
