# The Ecclesiastical History of Evagrius Scholasticus

The **last of the classical Greek ecclesiastical histories**, by **[[evagrius-scholasticus|Evagrius
Scholasticus]]** of Antioch — the principal continuous Greek narrative of the Eastern Church, and much
of the Eastern empire, from the **Council of Ephesus (431) to the reign of Maurice (594)**. The seventh
source of this Phase-5 batch.

## The Work

Evagrius wrote in **six books**, explicitly **continuing** the chain of church historians from
**Eusebius** through Socrates, Sozomen and **Theodoret** (where he begins). His central thread is the
**Christological controversy** after **[[council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedon]]**: the schisms over the
Council, the *Henotikon* of Zeno, the Acacian schism with Rome, the reign of the Miaphysite-leaning
Anastasius, the Chalcedonian restoration under Justin I, and the religious policies of
**[[justinian|Justinian]]** and his successors down to Maurice. Unusually for the genre, he weaves in
substantial **secular history** — the wars with Persia, imperial accessions, and the disasters of his
own Antioch (the **plague**, which he witnessed and which killed members of his family across its
recurrences, and the great earthquakes).

## Significance

Evagrius is the **indispensable source** for the ecclesiastical history of the later fifth and sixth
centuries in the Greek East, and a substantial witness for its political and military history; with him
the **classical Greek tradition of church history closes**. His firmly **Chalcedonian** standpoint makes
him the natural counterweight to the **Miaphysite** Syriac tradition — **[[john-of-ephesus|John of
Ephesus]]** and the [[pseudo-dionysius-zuqnin-chronicle|Chronicle of Zuqnin]] — so that reading them
together reveals the doctrinal fracture of the late-antique East from both sides.
