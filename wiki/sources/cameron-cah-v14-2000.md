# CAH Vol. XIV — Late Antiquity: Empire and Successors, A.D. 425–600 (Cameron, Ward-Perkins & Whitby eds., 2000)

The **final volume of the Cambridge Ancient History**, the direct sequel to [[cameron-cah-v13-1998|CAH
XIII]], carrying the story from c. 425 to **c. 600** — the eve of the Arab conquests, and the editors'
chosen end of "antiquity" itself. Where CAH XIII showed the empire dividing and the West beginning to
fragment, CAH XIV narrates the **definitive fall of the western empire** (476) and the rise of the
**barbarian successor kingdoms**, alongside the **survival and dramatic resurgence of the East** —
above all under **Justinian** (the "reconquest" of Africa, Italy and part of Spain, the codification of
Roman law, the building of Hagia Sophia). Its interpretive frame is the mature scholarship of late
antiquity (Jones, Brown, and the new archaeology): a world still **Mediterranean-centred and recognizably
"ancient,"** yet **fragmenting into separate regional identities** — and, in the East, "spectacularly
prosperous" right up to the eve of its seventh-century transformation. The volume leans deliberately
**eastward**, since the eastern polity now dominated the political and mental world, and it ends where the
Persian and Arab wars of the seventh century would make change irreversible — the rise of Islam already on
its final horizon.

### Notes on cross-volume continuity
- Picks up directly from [[cameron-cah-v13-1998|CAH XIII]] after the early years of Theodosius II; carries
  forward the [[late-roman-army]], [[late-roman-emperorship|imperial government]], [[late-roman-cities]],
  [[late-roman-economy]], [[late-roman-church-institution]], [[late-antique-asceticism]], and
  [[christianization-of-the-roman-empire]] pages into their fifth–sixth-century maturity.
- Continues the **non-Roman world**: the [[huns|Huns]] (now **Attila**), the [[sassanid-empire|Sasanids]]
  (Khusro I), [[armenia|Armenia]], and the [[germanic-peoples|Germanic peoples]] — now as **settled
  successor kingdoms** — plus the genuinely new **[[arab-desert-peoples|Arabs]]** culminating in the rise
  of Islam, and the **Avars**.
- Major creations expected: the **fall of the western empire (476)** and **Odoacer**; **Theoderic** and
  **Ostrogothic Italy**; **Clovis** and **Frankish Gaul**; **Visigothic Spain**; **Vandal Africa**; the
  **Lombards**; **Justinian** (and **Theodora**, **Belisarius**, the **Nika riot**, the **reconquest**, the
  **Codex Justinianus**, the **plague of 541**); **Hagia Sophia**; the **Council of Chalcedon (451)** and
  the **Monophysite schism**; the **closing of the Athenian Academy (529)**; **Khusro I**; and **Muḥammad
  and the rise of Islam**.
