# Payne, *Fascism: Comparison and Definition* (1980)

Payne's explicit purpose (Preface): "not to provide another description of
fascism but to wrestle with some basic problems of definition and
comparison" — a systematic review "that endeavors to define characteristics
and make distinctions on a broadly comparative basis." Written at Madison
alongside George L. Mosse, with acknowledged debts to A. J. Gregor and
Juan J. Linz. This is the original 1980 statement of the typological
description of fascism and the fascist / radical-right / conservative-right
distinction later elaborated in [[payne-history-of-fascism-1995|*A History
of Fascism*]] (1995) and recorded at
[[payne-typology-of-authoritarian-nationalism]].

## Volume Synthesis Note

The book is the seed crystal of Payne's entire later apparatus: the Table 1
typological description (tripartite structure suggested by Linz, Bergen
1974), the Three Faces typology, the movements-not-regimes restriction, the
Europe-and-era boundedness thesis, and the retrodictive preconditions all
appear here fifteen years before *A History of Fascism* (1995). Its
polemical fronts are fourfold: against Nolte's six-point minimum (too
German, too negative, wrongly totalitarian); against Marxist agent theory
(Turner's evidence; the right authoritarians, not fascists, were the party
of big business); against totalitarianism theory (Italy never totalitarian;
Germany incomplete; full totalitarianism requires a socialist economy); and
against anti-modernization readings (fascism as Enlightenment byproduct,
Nazi racism "conceivable only in the twentieth century").

Cross-volume tensions worth watching: Payne's economic rehabilitation of
Fascist Italy (Maddison indices, IRI) and his "neither sanguinary nor
particularly repressive" verdict sit in tension with the Evans/Corner
material already on [[mussolini-benito]] (OVRA surveillance scale, 13,000
confino deportees, Ethiopian gas warfare) — the 1980 judgments predate the
post-1990s "cult of the Duce" and colonial-violence literatures. His
endorsement of Sternhell's France-first genealogy (1978/1980) predates his
later reservations about the full Sternhell thesis. The Ellul and Bracher
"authentic revolution" material converges with Griffin's modernism thesis
from the opposite direction. Payne's ch. 9 claim that fascism cannot recur
because "all competing ideological forces now share a common humanist
materialism" is the most dated element, written before the post-Cold-War
radical-right revival he himself addresses in 1995.
