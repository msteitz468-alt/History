# Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (Penguin, 2008)

Concluding volume of Richard J. Evans's trilogy (after [[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003|*The Coming of the Third Reich*]] and [[evans-third-reich-in-power-2005|*The Third Reich in Power*]]), covering Nazi Germany from the invasion of Poland in September 1939 to the regime's destruction in 1945 and its immediate aftermath. Evans's distinctive contribution is integration: military narrative interwoven with the racial war, the home front, the war economy, occupied Europe, cultural and scientific life, and German popular opinion, with the genocide of the Jews placed at the war's center rather than treated as a separate track.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Evans's concluding volume completes the trilogy's argument: the war and the genocide were one project, not parallel tracks. Its distinctive contributions to the wiki are (1) the **process-not-decision** account of the Final Solution's genesis — Hitler's public "prophecy" rhetoric setting parameters that Himmler and regional commanders competed to fulfill, with no single written order (see [[wannsee-conference-1942]]); (2) the **Poland thesis** — "almost everything that was to happen in the invasion of the Soviet Union from June 1941 onwards was already happening on a smaller scale in the invasion of Poland nearly two years before"; (3) the **T4 incubator** — personnel and gassing technique passing bodily from the euthanasia program to [[operation-reinhard-1942|Operation Reinhard]]; (4) the diarist chorus (Klemperer, Solmitz, Klukowski, Hosenfeld, Sierakowiak) that grounds his account of what Germans and Poles knew and felt; and (5) his three named turning points — the failure against Britain (1940), the failure before Moscow plus US entry (end 1941), and Stalingrad.

Cross-volume tensions worth tracking: Evans's rejection of the Browning "ordinary men" frame for the eastern killing units (filed at [[ordinary-germans-and-the-holocaust]]); his Prokhorovka demythologization (post-Soviet-archive figures on [[battle-of-kursk-1943]]); his contested judgment that the Roma persecution was not "genocide in the same way" as the Holocaust (attributed, with the counter-position, at [[porajmos-roma-genocide]]); his death-march framing as general repression rather than purely a Holocaust chapter ([[death-marches-1944]]); and his cautious verdicts on the Bethel bombing and Speer's gas-the-bunker story, both flagged as unresolved on the relevant pages. The volume closes with Bullock's Wren epitaph for Hitler's Europe: *Si monumentum requiris, circumspice*.
