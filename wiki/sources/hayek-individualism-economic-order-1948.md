# Hayek, *Individualism and Economic Order* (1948)

Twelve essays (1935–1948) collected by Hayek at Chicago, spanning moral
philosophy, methodology of the social sciences, the theory of knowledge and
competition, the socialist calculation debate, and postwar policy questions.
Hayek's preface presents them as facets of one central issue: how a society
of free individuals coordinates dispersed knowledge without central command.
Companion volume to [[hayek-road-to-serfdom-1944|*The Road to Serfdom*]]
(chapters I and VI are its scholarly supplements).

## Claims Integrated

- **Ch. I (Finlay Lecture, Dublin, 17 Dec 1945)** → [[true-and-false-individualism]]:
  the two-traditions genealogy (Locke–Hume–Smith–Burke–Tocqueville–Acton vs.
  Descartes–Rousseau–physiocrats), the German third strand, the equality
  argument, and the Tocqueville "new form of servitude" link to
  [[road-to-serfdom-thesis]].
- **Ch. II ("Economics and Knowledge", London Economic Club, Nov 1936)** and
  **Ch. IV ("The Use of Knowledge in Society", *AER* 1945)** →
  [[knowledge-problem]]: the division of knowledge, the price system as
  signaling machinery, the "marvel" passage, the Schumpeter/Barone-myth
  footnote.
- **Ch. III ("The Facts of the Social Sciences")**: subjectivist/interpretive
  philosophy of social-science facts ("in the social sciences the things are
  what people think they are"), Hayek's break with his Machian training —
  recorded on this page as the volume's methodological layer (no separate
  concept page; the anti-scientism argument belongs with the fuller
  "Scientism" essays if ever ingested).
- **Ch. V (Stafford Little Lecture, Princeton, May 1946)**: critique of
  "perfect competition" as assuming away the competitive process; "a much
  bigger gulf divides competition from no competition than perfect from
  imperfect competition."
- **Ch. VI (opening paper, founding Mont Pèlerin conference, April 1947)** →
  [[hayek-friedrich]]: the "competitive order" vs. "'free' enterprise" vs.
  "ordered competition" distinction and the full reform program (property/
  patent/trade-mark law, limited liability, trade-union law per Henry
  Simons, rule-bound money, progressive-taxation critique), framed by the
  Keynes "academic scribbler" quotation.
- **Ch. VII–IX (the calculation essays, 1935/1935/1940)** →
  [[socialist-calculation-debate]] (full narrative), [[mises-ludwig]],
  [[lange-oskar]]: pre-history (Gossen 1854, Cannan, Pierson–Kautsky 1902,
  Barone 1908), the 1919–22 planning moment (Neurath, Bauer, the
  Wissell–Moellendorff "Planwirtschaft" memorandum), the Mises–Weber–
  Brutzkus convergence of 1920–21, Hayek's taxonomy of socialist responses,
  and the 1940 reply to Lange/Dickinson culminating in the
  economics-politics "fuse into one" passage.
- **Ch. X (commodity reserve currency, *EJ* 1943)** and **Ch. XI (Ricardo
  effect, *Economica* 1942)**: noted here only — technical economics for the
  sibling Economics wiki; historically they place Hayek in the wartime
  international-monetary debate (contemporaneous with Bretton Woods
  planning) and in continuing Austrian capital-theory controversy.
- **Ch. XII (*New Commonwealth Quarterly*, Sept 1939)** →
  [[interstate-federalism]]: the interwar federalist movement (New
  Commonwealth Society, Robbins, Streit) and the argument that federation
  structurally forecloses planning ("less government all round"), including
  the USSR-incompatibility passage.
