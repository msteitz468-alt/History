# The Secret History of the Mongols — de Rachewiltz translation (shorter version)

## Source Summary

The *Secret History of the Mongols* (Mongqol-un niuča tobčiyan) is the earliest and most important literary and historical monument of the Mongol people. Compiled in the first half of the 13th century (colophon points to c. 1228 or 1240), it is the only genuine native account of the life and deeds of Činggis Qan (Temüjin, our Genghis Khan) and the unification of the Mongol tribes.

This edition is the accessible shorter version of Igor de Rachewiltz’s definitive three-volume scholarly translation and commentary (Brill, 2004–2013), prepared by John C. Street (2015). It provides a clear, readable English rendering of the Middle Mongolian text while preserving its blend of prose narrative, genealogy, and epic poetry.

The work is unique among steppe peoples: no other nomadic or semi-nomadic group produced a comparable literary masterpiece combining historical chronicle, legend, and poetic tradition. It is the richest source for pre-Classical and Middle Mongolian language and offers an unmediated glimpse into the mental world of the 13th-century Mongol elite.

The narrative is broadly chronological. Early chapters cover legendary tribal origins and Temüjin’s difficult youth. Middle chapters detail the unification wars, the 1206 quriltai, and reorganization of society and army. Later chapters narrate the great campaigns and the transition to the next generation. Index provides rich citation anchors for verification.

## Key Themes and Historiography

The *Secret History* presents Chinggis Khan not as a supernatural figure but as a man who rose through personal loyalty, merit, and ruthless pragmatism. Central themes:
- **Loyalty over kinship**: The *nökör* (sworn companions) system and breaking of old tribal structures in favor of decimal military-administrative units (10s, 100s, 1,000s, 10,000s).
- **Merit and charisma**: Promotion by ability and personal bond rather than birth; the quriltai as acclamation of universal rule.
- **Justice and terror**: The *jasaq* (later yasa) as emerging code; generosity to the loyal and merciless punishment of betrayal.
- **Worldview**: Blend of shamanistic, epic, and pragmatic elements; view of the steppe as a political arena to be unified.

As history it mixes fact, legend, and retrospective justification (written after the conquests had begun). Its value lies precisely in the “inside” perspective unavailable in Persian or Chinese sources. De Rachewiltz’s apparatus carefully distinguishes layers and provides philological and historical commentary.

## Collection Coverage Note

This is the single most important primary source for the early Mongol Empire in the Bibliotheca Alexandrina. It supplies the native Mongol voice for the rise of [[genghis-khan|Chinggis Khan]] and the formation of the [[mongol-empire|Mongol Empire]] — pages that previously relied on later Persian/Chinese accounts and modern syntheses (May, Morgan, Jackson, Cambridge volumes). 

The text is especially strong on:
- Tribal politics and the unification process.
- The personal and ideological foundations of Chinggisid legitimacy.
- The decimal army and *keshig* as institutions.
- Early campaigns (pre-1227).

Gaps remain for detailed battle narratives of later conquests and the post-1227 khanates (covered better in other sources). The poetic passages offer rare insight into pre-Buddhist Mongol oral culture.
