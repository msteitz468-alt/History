# Andrew Carnegie (Nasaw, 2006)

**Source page for David Nasaw, *Andrew Carnegie* (The Penguin Press, 2006; Penguin Books, 2007).**

The definitive modern biography of Andrew Carnegie. Nasaw presents Carnegie whole: the Scottish weaver's son who became the archetype of the Gilded Age industrialist and then the most systematic large-scale philanthropist of his generation. The book emphasizes Carnegie's agency in building the low-cost steel empire, the gap between his public writings and business practice (especially labor), the sincerity and long pre-history of his commitment to give the fortune away, and his later career as a transatlantic peace activist. It is the first biography to make full use of the new archival materials that became available in the late twentieth century.

## Methodological Notes

- Extensive use of public and private archives in the US, Scotland, England; unpublished Autobiography drafts; family papers; correspondence with heads of state and intellectuals.
- Critical reconstruction of Carnegie's self-mythologizing (Autobiography as partial and rearranged; earlier authorized lives by Hendrick and Wall constrained by family or sources).
- Business history detail on the shift to steel, vertical integration, cost leadership, and the 1901 sale.
- Full treatment of the "Gospel of Wealth" as both genuine conviction and self-justification, with Homestead as the central interpretive problem.
- Balanced but not neutral: Carnegie is portrayed as fascinating, contradictory, and consequential rather than heroic or villainous.

## Key Contributions

- The most complete account of Carnegie's Scottish childhood, the trauma of the weaver trade's collapse, emigration, and early Pittsburgh years (bobbin boy, telegraphy, rise under Scott).
- Definitive reconstruction of the insider railroad contracts (Scott/Thomson), early oil and iron fortunes, and the decision to go "all eggs in one basket" on Bessemer steel.
- Granular treatment of the Carnegie Steel years: Edgar Thomson design, recruitment of Frick and Schwab, Frick Coke integration, Mesabi ore deals, relentless modernization and cost-cutting.
- The fullest and most archivally grounded account of the 1892 Homestead Strike, Frick's role, Carnegie's knowledge and responsibility, and the long-term reputational consequences.
- Reconstruction of the intellectual and social life (Spencer, Arnold, Triumphant Democracy, transatlantic networking) and the evolution of the philanthropy from libraries and organs to the great endowments and the Carnegie Corporation.
- Carnegie's peace activism as a serious second (or third) career, including his relations with presidents and European leaders and his despair in 1914–1919.
