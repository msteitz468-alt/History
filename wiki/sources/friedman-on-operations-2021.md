# B. A. Friedman, *On Operations: Operational Art and Military Disciplines* (2021)

The companion to *On Tactics* (2017). Where the earlier book supplied an organizing structure for **tactics**, this volume addresses the operational level/art and the role of military staffs. Friedman argues that the “operational level of war” is a problematic modern construct that has severed the direct link between tactics and strategy. In its place he offers “operational art” as the practical work of staffs — expressed through six core disciplines — that supports and sustains tactical action in the service of strategy.

The book is explicitly Clausewitzian and practitioner-oriented, aimed at officers who will serve on or lead staffs.

## Historiography / impact note

Friedman writes as a serving/reserve Marine officer frustrated by the doctrinal separation of tactics from strategy that the “operational level” introduced. He seeks to restore a direct, Clausewitzian relationship in which operational art is what competent staffs do to make tactics effective at the strategic level. The book is short, clear, and deliberately provocative on the harm caused by treating the operational level as a distinct “level” rather than a staff function.

This is the natural doctrinal follow-on to the 2017 tactics volume. Together they provide a clean, modern taxonomy for the lower two-thirds of the strategy–operations–tactics spectrum that the warfare hub can use consistently.

Limitations: Explicitly Western-focused (acknowledged); case studies are illustrative rather than exhaustive archival studies; the critique of the operational level will be contested by those who see value in it.

## Primary Claims from the Text (Grounded Extractions)

All claims verbatim-grounded exclusively from the 2021 Naval Institute Press source. Organized by structure with locators (Preface, Introduction, Ch. X, case study name, Conclusion). The work completes the tactics–operations–strategy framework from *On Tactics* (2017) by arguing that the “operational level of war” is a flawed, post-Napoleonic construct that severs tactics from strategy, while “operational art” is the practical staff work (via six disciplines) that links them.

### Framing and Core Distinction (Preface + Introduction)
- **Clausewitzian foundation**: "This book, like my previous book, is Clausewitzian. ... Good footnotes to Clausewitz is all I hope these books to be." (Preface). "If there is any one takeaway from this book and On Tactics, it is that there is no one route to victory in war." (Preface).
- **Operational level vs. operational art**: "the invention of the operational level of war, a concept that is, this book argues, problematic at best and ruinous at worst. In contrast, operational art, a sister concept, is useful as a descriptor of what military staffs do to support and sustain tactics in the pursuit of strategy." "The military success usually attributed (wrongly) to the 'discovery' of an operational level of war should instead be credited to the rise of general staffs." (Introduction).
- **No logical space for a separate level**: Tactics (linear, battlefield victory) and strategy (nonlinear, use of victories for war’s purpose) leave "no logical space for an operational level of war." The level "amputates" the direct tactics–strategy dialectic. (Introduction).
- **Operational art definition**: "Operational art, then, is the planning, preparation, synchronization, and sustainment of tactics over a sustained period of time, a large geographic expanse, or both." It "comprises the disciplines required to place military forces in an advantageous position to employ tactics to achieve strategic effect" and "exhibits no logic of its own." (Introduction; Ch. 6).
- **Staff vs. commander focus**: *On Tactics* addressed commanders/tacticians; *On Operations* addresses "staff officers who support those commanders by performing the functions of operational art." (Introduction).

### Historical Development (Ch. 1–4)
- **Napoleonic Revolution origins**: Napoleonic-era complexity (scale, corps system, combined arms) required staffs. Napoleon used a "protostaff" (Berthier). "the military staff, its worth proven by Napoleon, would be the vehicle that would integrate all subsequent changes in the character of war." (Ch. 1).
- **German General Staff (Scharnhorst Model)**: Institutionalized to replicate Napoleonic success without relying on genius. Railroads, mission-type orders, professional pipeline. "The rise of operational art is intimately connected with the rise of general-staff systems." No explicit "operational level" in pre-1945 German thought. (Ch. 2).
- **Soviet operational thought**: Used "operational art" partly to discuss strategy safely under Marxism-Leninism (strategy reserved for Lenin/Stalin). Simultaneity for shock/paralysis. "Operational art is what military staffs do to support tactics and strategy." (Ch. 3).
- **American adoption and problems**: Mistranslated and conflated Soviet ideas; inserted as a "level" post-Vietnam. Led to "amputation of strategy from tactics" and directionless wars. "Conflation of the two concepts has prevented the U.S. military from applying operational art to its full potential." (Ch. 4).

### Theory of Operational Art and the Disciplines (Ch. 5–12 + taxonomy)
- **Civil-military critique**: The operational level creates a "politics-free zone" between tactics and strategy, shifting blame and undermining responsibility. Strategy remains inherently political. (Ch. 5).
- **Six disciplines of operational art** (staff functions that cut across domains/services): Administration (moral + bureaucratic; ties soldier to institution); Information (superiority, deception, shared consciousness); Operations (coordination of time/space/forces, simultaneity, sequencing); Fire Support (probable effect vs. close combat certainty); Logistics (foundation enabling tactics; LOCs, sustainment); Command and Control (centralized vs. decentralized trade-offs; modern staff systems). (Ch. 6–12).
- **Campaign taxonomy**: Offensive/Defensive × Persisting/Raiding × Annihilation/Cumulative (OPA, DPC, ORA, etc.; hybrids). Campaigns distinguished from battles; choice is political/strategic, not purely "operational." (Ch. 13–15).
- **Staff actualization**: Modern staffs realize operational art when they integrate the disciplines. U.S. Joint Staff lacks true general-staff career pipeline/education (contrast Scharnhorst Model). McChrystal "Team of Teams" (shared consciousness + empowered execution) and collaborative command as adaptations. (Ch. 16).
- **Force protection**: Treated as a note, not a full discipline. (Ch. 17).

### Case Studies (illustrating staff/operational art in action)
- **Austerlitz (1805)**: Protostaff (Berthier) + information superiority + deception enabled corps convergence, Ulm maneuver, and envelopment. (Case Study 1).
- **Königgrätz (1866)**: Prussian general staff (Moltke) vs. dysfunctional Austrian staff; exterior lines, rail mobilization, flexible orders produced decisive result despite Austrian advantages in some arms. (Case Study 2).
- **Atlantic Campaign (1914–18)**: Naval operational art; convoy coordination, codebreaking, global basing vs. German constraints. (Case Study 3).
- **Battle of Britain (1940)**: Air campaign; Dowding System (radar + centralized C2 + decentralized execution) vs. Luftwaffe command confusion and poor targeting of C2 nodes. Production and logistics decisive. (Case Study 4).
- **Operation Watchtower (Guadalcanal, 1942)**: Triphibious campaign; cross-domain permeation of the six disciplines (esp. information superiority via Coast Watchers/codebreaking; mutual siege logistics). U.S. staff coordination across services ultimately prevailed. (Case Study 5).

### Conclusion and Overall Argument
- The "operational level" is a tragic error (Soviet political workaround + Western mistranslation) that has produced directionless wars. Real advances came from competent general staffs.
- Operational art restores the Clausewitzian link: tactics (linear) in service of strategy (nonlinear/political) via staff disciplines.
- "The meaning of tactical events—be they battles or campaigns... cannot be found in and of themselves but only in the effect they produce for strategy."
- Success requires integrating all disciplines; no single "key" to victory.

## Consolidated Volume Synthesis Note

*On Operations* completes Friedman’s modern doctrinal project begun in *On Tactics*. It provides a clear, usable taxonomy and framework for operational art as staff disciplines that enable tactics to produce strategic effect, while sharply critiquing the post-Napoleonic "operational level of war" as conceptually incoherent and practically harmful. Historical chapters trace the rise of staff systems as the true response to complexity; the six disciplines and campaign taxonomy give concrete tools; the case studies (Austerlitz through Watchtower) demonstrate application across eras and domains. Together with the 2017 volume, it supplies the warfare hub with a coherent, practitioner-derived bridge between tactics and strategy grounded in Clausewitz but updated for contemporary staffs and multidomain operations.
