# The Times Atlas of World History (New Edition, 1999)

A major single-volume historical atlas providing maps, timelines, and supporting text covering human history from the origins of hominins to the late 20th century. Edited by Richard Overy for this edition (original conception by Geoffrey Barraclough).

This is a **reference work**, not a narrative history. Its primary value lies in its cartographic representation of political entities, cultural spreads, trade networks, environmental changes, and demographic patterns across time and space. It complements narrative sources by supplying the "where" dimension explicitly required by the wiki's network model (time + place + topic).

## Collection Coverage Note

This atlas is a key **geographic reference** (low-priority supplementary per sourcing roadmap). It fills gaps in the wiki's visual and spatial understanding, particularly for:

- Precise historical boundaries and their changes over time.
- Trade routes, migrations, and environmental contexts that link periods and regions.
- Chronological synchronization across civilizations.

It is not a source for detailed narrative claims or primary-source analysis (those come from the Cambridge series, specialist monographs, and translated texts). Textual content in the PDF is limited to captions, chronologies, and short essays; the core value is the maps themselves (retained in the filed source file).

## Historiography / Collection Note

One of the standard English-language historical atlases of its era. Strengths: clear, consistent cartography; good balance of political, economic, and cultural mapping; useful parallel chronologies. Limitations: interpretive choices in boundaries and emphasis; pre-digital scholarship (some revisions superseded by later archaeology/genetics); visual nature makes full text ingest impractical.

Filed as reference support for the wiki's 3D (time-place-topic) model rather than a primary narrative source. Complements the Cambridge World History volumes and specialist texts already ingested.
