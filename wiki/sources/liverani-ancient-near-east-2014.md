# The Ancient Near East: History, Society and Economy — Mario Liverani (2014)

**A single-author synthesis of three millennia of Near Eastern history (c. 3500–500
BCE), read through society, economy, and ideology.** First published in Italian in 1988
(*Antico Oriente*) and long the standard introduction in Italy, the work appeared in
English only in 2014, fully revised. Liverani's governing project is to **"normalise"**
the ancient Near East — to study it like any other period rather than as the biblical
"Holy Land," the Orientalist despotic "Other," or the Eurocentric "cradle of
civilisation" — and to correct the **documentary bias** by which surviving palace and
temple archives make an overwhelmingly rural, illiterate, scarcity-bound world look
opulent and urban. The analytic spine is economic-anthropological: **modes of
production**, redistribution and reciprocity, the **"great organisations"** (temple and
palace), and the recurring oscillation between palatial-urban and village-tribal
organisation across the three millennia.
