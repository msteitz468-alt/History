# King Leopold's Ghost (Adam Hochschild)

Adam Hochschild's *King Leopold's Ghost* (1998) is the work that returned the **Congo Free State** — the
private central-African empire of **Leopold II of Belgium**, run for rubber and ivory between 1885 and
1908 — to general historical memory. Tenth source of **Gap 1 (Modern)**, and the wiki's anchor for the
human cost of high [[imperialism|imperialism]] in [[central-africa|equatorial Africa]].

## The Argument

Hochschild reconstructs how Leopold, denied a colony by his own government, acquired a domain
**seventy-six times the size of Belgium** as his *personal* possession through the diplomacy of the
1884–85 Berlin Conference, disguising a forced-labour extraction regime behind the language of
philanthropy and anti-slavery. Under quotas enforced by the **Force Publique**, Congolese were compelled
to gather wild rubber; failure was met with hostage-taking, village burning, mutilation (the severed
hands that became the regime's signature atrocity), and mass death. Hochschild presents the demographic
catastrophe — a population decline he, following Jan Vansina, estimates at roughly **ten million** across
the period — as the product of killing, famine, overwork, flight, disease and a collapsed birth rate.

The book is equally a history of the **first great international human-rights campaign**: the Congo Reform
Association built by **E. D. Morel** (the shipping clerk who deduced the slavery from the trade figures),
the consular report of **Roger Casement**, and the testimony of missionaries and of African witnesses such
as **George Washington Williams** (who coined "crimes against humanity" for the Congo) and **William
Sheppard**. Sustained pressure forced Belgium to annex the territory from Leopold in **1908**.

## Significance

The book reframes the **Scramble for Africa** around its costs rather than its maps, supplies the wiki's
fullest case of imperial atrocity and of the **production of famine and demographic collapse** by colonial
extraction (alongside [[davis-late-victorian-holocausts|Davis]] for India), and documents the **origins of
modern humanitarian and human-rights activism**. It feeds the [[genocide]] debate (Leopold's Congo is a
standard borderline case — whether the demographic disaster meets the intent threshold is disputed) and
the historiography of [[imperialism|imperialism]].
