# Hans Delbrück, *History of the Art of War, Volume I: Warfare in Antiquity* (trans. 1975)

The foundational critical study that applied systematic "objective" (logistical, organizational, and tactical) analysis to the military history of antiquity. Delbrück begins the continuous narrative at the Persian Wars because only then do sources permit testing legend against military reality. The work traces the evolution of Western infantry systems (Greek phalanx → Macedonian sarissa phalanx with combined arms → Roman manipular legion → cohort legion) and provides detailed battle reconstructions for the major set-piece actions of the era.

This is the primary Tier 3 source for the classical antiquity wing of the warfare hub and the origin point for much subsequent operational historiography.

## Historiography / impact note (for source page and cross-ref)

Delbrück is the archetype of the "critical" school that treats ancient battle accounts as problematic until tested against what armies could actually do on the ground. His downward revisions of Persian and Greek numbers at Marathon and Plataea, his reconstruction of Cannae's mechanics, and his tracing of the phalanx → legion lineage remain reference points even where later archaeology or scholarship has modified details. Directly feeds the locked battle-analysis template standards (terrain, OOB feasibility, principles of war, source criticism).
