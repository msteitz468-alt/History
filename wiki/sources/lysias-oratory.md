# Lysias (Oratory of Classical Greece)

The surviving speeches of **[[lysias|Lysias]]**, the greatest Athenian **speechwriter** — a vivid
primary source for the law, society and politics of early-fourth-century Athens. The third source of the
Phase-9 batch, and the first of the canonical **Attic orators** ingested.

## The Speeches

About thirty-five speeches survive, overwhelmingly **forensic** (written for litigants in the law
courts). They range across Athenian life: inheritance and property suits, commercial and personal
disputes, charges of impiety and corruption, the **scrutiny (*dokimasia*) of officials**, and the
politics of the restored democracy. The headline piece is:

- **Against Eratosthenes** — Lysias's own prosecution of one of the **[[thirty-tyrants|Thirty]]** for the
  murder of his brother **Polemarchus**: a first-person account of the regime's seizure of metic property
  and its killings, and one of the most powerful surviving testimonies to the terror of 404/3 BCE.

Other notable speeches treat the defence of democracy, sycophancy, and the texture of household and
neighbourhood life that rarely surfaces in the narrative historians.

## Value

Because Lysias's clients are ordinary Athenians arguing before mass juries, the speeches are a **primary
source of the first order for Athenian society and law** — property, family, slavery, the **metic**
experience (which Lysias himself embodied), civic scrutiny, and the workings of the democratic courts —
and for the **political crisis of the Thirty and the restoration of 403**. They are also the classic
model of **plain Attic prose** and of *ēthopoiia* (fitting the speech to the speaker's character).
