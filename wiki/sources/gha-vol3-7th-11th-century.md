# UNESCO General History of Africa, Vol. III: Africa from the Seventh to the Eleventh Century

The third volume of the **UNESCO *General History of Africa*** (eds. **M. El Fasi & I. Hrbek**, 1988)
covers the pivotal centuries when **Islam came to Africa** and the **West African trading empires** rose on
the trans-Saharan gold trade. Spanning the Arab conquest of Egypt and the Maghrib, the Fatimids and
Almoravids, the empire of Ghana and the Chad and Guinea zones, the Christian kingdoms of Nubia and Ethiopia,
and the Bantu, East African and Malagasy worlds, it is the **early-medieval reference for
[[sub-saharan-africa|Gap 2]]** — the bridge between the ancient civilizations of GHA Vol. II and the great
medieval empires of Vol. IV.

## The volume's argument

Editor Hrbek's framing chapter places Africa **in world history**: the Arab conquests reintegrated North
Africa and Egypt into a vast Islamic world-economy, and — most consequentially "from the African
perspective" — **introduced Islam to trans-Saharan Africa**, chiefly through the **gold trade** that drew
the Maghrib's hunger for bullion across the desert to the **Western Sudan**. The volume treats this
Islamization not as conquest from outside but as a long, uneven process of **conversion through commerce**,
and foregrounds the **Berber** agency (the Almoravid movement) and the indigenous African states that
engaged Islam on their own terms.
