# FDR — Jean Edward Smith (2007)

One-volume narrative biography of Franklin Delano Roosevelt (1882–1945), 32nd President of the United States. Widely regarded at publication as the leading modern single-volume life, synthesizing FDR Library materials with participant accounts and prior scholarship into a readable yet detailed portrait of the man, his polio-driven personal transformation, his administrative methods, the New Deal response to the Depression, and his leadership of the United States through World War II and the forging of the postwar order.

## Key Claims & Themes (to be expanded via subagents)

- FDR as one of three presidents who “dominate American history” (Washington founded, Lincoln preserved, FDR rescued from collapse and led to victory in greatest war).
- Polio and especially repeated stays at Warm Springs exposed FDR directly to rural Southern poverty (“ill-housed, ill-clad, ill-nourished”), shaping the economic and social vision of the New Deal beyond mere personal overcoming.
- Administrative style: deliberate compartmentalization (“I’m a juggler. I never let my right hand know what my left hand is doing”), reliance on overlapping agencies and personal loyalists, hands-on diplomacy and military appointments (Marshall over seniority, Stimson/Knox for bipartisanship).
- Major institutional creations that became permanent: Social Security, federal deposit insurance, securities regulation, collective bargaining rights, rural electrification, minimum wage/hour laws.
- Wartime leadership: restored confidence, mobilized “arsenal of democracy,” managed coalition with Churchill and Stalin at high level; criticisms acknowledged on race, Jewish refugees, civil liberties, China policy, and de Gaulle.
- Explicit rejection of Pearl Harbor conspiracy theories; administration expected possible attack but not at Pearl Harbor.
- Death 12 April 1945 at Warm Springs; work on postwar order (UN, Bretton Woods) largely complete; Truman succession.

### Range 04 (lines 15501–20500: 1936 reelection through 1937 Court-packing context, contrasts to early New Deal)
This range provides dense material contrasting first-term mastery with 1937 missteps, while grounding second inaugural rhetoric, administrative style, personnel evolution, and coalition consolidation.

**Key grounded claims**:
- Second inaugural (Jan 20, 1937, rain-soaked): “The test of our progress is not whether we add more to the abundance of those who have much; it is whether we provide enough for those who have too little... I see one-third of a nation ill-housed, ill-clad, ill-nourished.”
