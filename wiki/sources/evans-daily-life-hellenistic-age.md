# Daily Life in the Hellenistic Age (James Allan Evans)

A social-history survey of **everyday life in the [[hellenistic-world|Hellenistic world]]**, from
Alexander to Cleopatra — the texture of ordinary existence beneath the wars of the kings. The eighth
source of the Phase-9 batch.

## Coverage

Evans reconstructs Hellenistic daily life across the major domains, drawing especially on the **papyri**
of Ptolemaic Egypt:

- **The city and the household** — the Greek *polis* transplanted across the East, urban life, housing,
  and the family.
- **Women** — their (somewhat expanded but still circumscribed) roles, marriage, and the prominence of
  royal women (the Ptolemaic queens).
- **Work, economy and slavery** — farming, crafts, trade, money, taxation, and the place of slave and
  free labour.
- **Religion and the mind** — the old civic cults, the new **mystery** and **savior** cults, **ruler
  cult**, **Tyche** (Fortune), astrology, and the Hellenistic philosophies (Stoicism, Epicureanism,
  Cynicism) as guides to living.
- **Education, entertainment, food and the body** — the gymnasium, theater and festivals, diet,
  medicine, and death.

## Significance

The book gives the wiki its **social-historical dimension** for the Hellenistic age — the lived
experience of the Greek diaspora and the peoples among whom they settled — complementing the political
narratives ([[walbank-hellenistic-world|Walbank]], [[jouguet-macedonian-imperialism|Jouguet]]) and the
kingdom-building studies ([[grainger-seleukos-nikator|Grainger]]). It is especially useful on
**Ptolemaic Egypt**, where the documentary record makes daily life unusually recoverable.
