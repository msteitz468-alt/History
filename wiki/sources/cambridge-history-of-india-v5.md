# The Cambridge History of India, Vol. V: British India, 1497–1858 (1929)

The narrative of the European companies and the British conquest of India, edited by H. H.
Dodwell — the classic (and frankly imperial-era) reference for how a trading company became the
ruler of the subcontinent, deepening the wiki's [[english-east-india-company|EIC]],
[[battle-of-plassey-1757|Plassey]], and [[british-raj]] pages.

## What it contributes

- **[[battle-of-buxar-1764]]** — the battle and the **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** grant of the
  *diwani*, which turned the Company from a Bengal kingmaker into the territorial sovereign of
  Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa — the true foundation of British India.
- Deepens [[english-east-india-company]], [[battle-of-plassey-1757]], and [[british-raj]].

## Reading note

A flagged weak region, so the conquest narrative is worth recovering; but this 1929 volume is the
most **ideologically dated** of the three CHI volumes ingested, and is used critically — mined for
chronology while its imperial framing is flagged, not adopted.
