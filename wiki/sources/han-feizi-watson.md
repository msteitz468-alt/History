# Han Feizi: Basic Writings

The classic statement of **[[legalism|Legalism]]**, by **[[han-feizi|Han Fei]]** — the philosophy of
law, technique and ruler-power that armed the **[[qin-dynasty|Qin]]** to unify China. The third source of
the Phase-6 East Asian Classics; Burton Watson's selection of the key essays.

## The Work

The essays gather Han Feizi's theory of the state into memorable, often ruthless arguments, illustrated
by historical anecdotes and fables (the most famous in Chinese literature — the man who "**waited by the
stump**" for another hare to run into it, mocking those who cling to the ways of the ancient kings). The
selection sets out the doctrine of **law (*fa*)**, **statecraft/technique (*shu*)** and **positional
power (*shi*)**; the **"two handles"** of reward and punishment; the matching of **performance to title**
as the test of officials; and the ruler's need to keep his desires hidden lest ministers manipulate him.
Famous chapters — "The Two Handles," "The Five Vermin," "Precautions within the Palace" — argue that a
state is strong only through **strict law and the suppression of the parasitic classes** (including
Confucian scholars and wandering swordsmen) who live off fine words.

## Significance

The *Han Feizi* is the **fullest and most rigorous primary statement of Chinese Legalist political
thought** and a founding text of the Chinese theory of the centralized state. Its principles, embraced by
the **First Emperor** and **Li Si**, shaped the **Qin** unification of 221 BCE and, beneath the later
Confucian veneer, the enduring institutions of imperial China. It stands as the great **realist,
amoral** counter-pole to the Confucian classics in this group.
