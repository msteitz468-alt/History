# Hitler 1936–1945: Nemesis (Ian Kershaw, 2000)

**Source page for Ian Kershaw, *Hitler 1936–1945: Nemesis* (2000).** Second and final volume of the major scholarly biography, picking up from the Rhineland remilitarization in 1936.

Kershaw explores how Hitler exercised the near-absolute power consolidated by 1936, how German elites and society became complicit in genocidal war and the Holocaust on an unprecedented scale, and how hubris led to nemesis — the self-destruction of the regime, the Wehrmacht, Prussia, the Junker class, and much of Germany and Europe. The volume ends with the Berlin bunker and reflections on Hitler's unique place as the 'quintessential hate-figure' of the twentieth century.

## Approach and Method

Continuation of the structuralist-informed biography from *Hubris*. Uses Weberian charismatic leadership to explain the personalized rule and 'working towards the Führer' dynamic that drove radicalization even (or especially) without constant direct orders. Heavy use of contemporary sources (Goebbels diaries central) and critical handling of post-war testimony. Goal: explain, not merely condemn; understand societal complicity and the mechanics of absolute power in a modern state.

## Range 01 Claims (lines 1–5700 of source text: Preface + 1936 to early 1938 / Anschluss aftermath and Czech turn)

**Framing (Preface)**
- 'By the time the first volume drew to a close with the remilitarization of the Rhineland in 1936, [Hitler] enjoyed the support of the overwhelming mass of the German people... his narcissistic self-glorification had swollen immeasurably... he thought himself infallible; his self-image had reached the stage of outright hubris.'
- 'The German people had shaped this personal hubris of the leader. They were about to enter into its full expression: the greatest gamble in the nation’s history... to acquire complete dominance of the European continent. They would have to live with the consequences.'
- Nemesis as goddess of retribution for hubris. Hitler's legacy 'uniquely... one of utter destruction' (contrasted with Napoleon). 'His place in history has certainly been secured — though in a way he had not anticipated: as the embodiment of modern political evil.'
- Task not moralizing but explanation of grip on ordinary people: 'millions of German citizens who were mostly ordinary human beings... would find so much of what Hitler stood for attractive – or would be prepared to fight to the bitter end...'
- Nemesis of Germany itself: country in ruins, divided, Prussia gone, eastern provinces lost, Junker estates swept away, Wehrmacht discredited.

**1936 Triumph and Internal Dynamics**
- Rhineland 7 March: 'After three years, I believe that, with the present day, the struggle for German equal rights can be regarded as closed.' 'we have no territorial claims to make in Europe.' 98.9% plebiscite support (29 March). 'Great are the successes which Providence has let me attain for our Fatherland in these three years.'
- Demonstrated western weakness: 'the plainest demonstration of the weakness of France and Great Britain'. 'Hitler had broken with impunity the treaties of Versailles and Locarno.'
- Mass adulation: 'Large sections of the population simply idolized him.' Underground socialist reports: 'What a fellow, Hitler. He had the courage to risk something.' 'The spirit of Versailles is hated by all Germans. Hitler has now torn up this accursed treaty...'
- 'Good times': unemployment 'practically wiped out'; living standards 'beginning to improve'; Strength Through Joy, people's radio. 'For many, even looking back long after the war, these were the "good times".'
- Sopade: 'That this new deed of Hitler is another milestone on the way to the hell’s jaws of destruction seems hardly to have entered the consciousness of anyone.'
- Opposition crushed; only conceivable danger from within regime. Army/business/conservative elites loyal (rearmament, profits, anti-Marxism).
- Church struggle compartmentalized; bishops endorsed regime against Bolshevism.

**Radicalization Mechanics ('working towards the Führer')**
- Twin fixed ideas unchanged since 1920s: 'destroying the Jews — for him, Germany’s mortal enemy; and, through their destruction, acquiring mastery over the entire continent of Europe — a platform for subsequent world dominance.'
- 'Hitler’s essential, unchanging, distant goals had inexorably become the driving-force of the entire Nazi regime, constituting the framework for the extraordinary energy and dynamism that permeated the entire system of rule.'
- 'The triumph of 1936... proved in this way not an end but a beginning... the ideological dynamism of the regime not only did not subside but intensified.'
- Four-Year Plan: Hitler's late-Aug 1936 memorandum (written statement rare). Political section pure Hitler (Bolshevism crisis; 'Germany will be lost' without first-class army). Economic: autarky drive, synthetics, 'cost is irrelevant'. 'The nation does not live for the economy...' Ultimate solution: 'living space'. 'In the next four years, the German army had to be made operational, the economy made ready for war.' Göring driving force; special commissioners; administrative anarchy.
- Spanish intervention 25 July 1936 (Bayreuth after Siegfried): 'If Spain really goes communist, France in her present situation will also be bolshevised in due course, and then Germany is finished.' 20+ Ju-52s + fighters sent; Legion Condor from Oct. Goebbels: 'We’re taking part a bit in Spain. Not clear. Who knows what it’s good for.' Later cabinet: 'Spain had become the decisive issue... Rearm. Money can play no role.'
- Olympics: propaganda triumph; antisemitism temporarily reined in; Hitler no handshake with Owens.
- Axis (Ciano Oct; Mussolini 'axis' speech Milan): common anti-Bolshevism.
- Anti-Comintern Pact (Japan 27 Nov 1936).
- Jewish policy: Olympic restraint temporary. By late 1937 aryanization rapid. Goebbels 30 Nov 1937 (after lunch with Hitler): 'The Jews must get out of Germany, yes out of the whole of Europe. That will still take some time. But it will and must happen. The Führer is firmly decided on it.'
- Hitler personality/routines 1937: formal distance; 'The masses need an idol'; closest to Goebbels/Speer; Eva Braun hidden/neglected; Morell from Christmas 1936 (stomach, eczema); 'probably did not have long to live'; 'solve the problems... (living-space) as soon as possible, so that this could still take place in his lifetime.'

**Hoßbach Meeting 5 Nov 1937**
- Long monologue: living space essential; by 1943–5 at latest (or earlier if France weak). 'Unalterable determination to solve the German problem of space by 1943–5 at the latest.' Attack Austria + Czechoslovakia simultaneously 'lightning fast' ('blitzartig schnell'). Britain/France written off Czechs.
- Military/Neurath reaction negative (risk of war with west). Hitler knew. 'It was perhaps out of pique that he more than once refused to read the memorandum.'
- Aligned with private comments to Goebbels.

**Blomberg–Fritsch Crisis Jan–Feb 1938**
- Blomberg married Gruhn (past as prostitute); Hitler/Göring witnesses. Scandal broke; Hitler 'stunned'. 'If a German Field-Marshal marries a whore, anything in the world is possible.' Göring failed to force annulment. Blomberg exiled.
- Fritsch file revived; confrontation with Schmidt; word of honour ignored; Hitler lost faith. Gürtner report damning. Resignation demanded.
- Outcome: Hitler takes direct Wehrmacht command (Keitel adviser); 12 generals + 51 posts purged; Ribbentrop for Neurath. 'In this Reich, everyone in any responsible position is a National Socialist.' Army swallowed it. 'The army... had suffered a devastating blow.'

**Anschluss (Feb–Mar 1938)**
- Berchtesgaden 12 Feb: Hitler to Schuschnigg: 'Perhaps I’ll appear some time overnight in Vienna; like a spring storm.' Ultimatum accepted under threat (Seyß-Inquart Interior with police).
- Schuschnigg plebiscite (9 Mar): Hitler 'in full swing'; 'in a wonderful fighting mood'. 'The hour has arrived.'
- Göring drove ultimatums by phone; 'master of the situation'. Schuschnigg resigned; Miklas yielded. Hitler ordered march. Mussolini acceptance: 'I will never forget you for this... do or die I shall stick by him, come what may.'
- Crossed at Braunau 12 Mar; Linz (tears; 'I have now fulfilled my mission'). Full Anschluss law 13 Mar (Stuckart). Vienna Heldenplatz 15 Mar: 'the entry of my homeland into the German Reich.' 'New mission' of Ostmark as 'bulwark' against 'storms of the east'.
- Repression: Himmler/Heydrich on 12 Mar; 10–20k arrests; Jews plundered, forced cleaning squads, 'Ju-da verr-rrecke!', suicides. 'Hades had opened its gates' (Zuckmayer).
- Goebbels immediately after: 'First comes now Czechia... And drastically (rigoros), at the next opportunity... The Führer is wonderful... A true genius.'
- Papen (Kershaw): 'Hitler had brought about the Anschluss by force; in spite of all warnings and prophecies, his own methods had proved the most direct and successful... Hitler became impervious to the advice of all those who wished him to exercise moderation.'

**Munich Agreement crisis and decision mechanics (Sept 1938):**

Chamberlain: 'That’s an ultimatum.' 'With great disappointment and deep regret I must register, Herr Reich Chancellor, that you have not supported in the slightest my efforts to maintain peace.' - 26 Sept Sportpalast speech: Hitler 'in the worst state of excitement I’ve ever seen him in'; tirades against Beneš ('slowly to exterminate Germandom'); 'We don’t want any Czechs at all'; 'I will smash the Czechs' (to Wilson next day); 'If France and England want to strike, let them go ahead. Terms essentially Godesberg modified with progressive occupation over 10 days.

## Range 04 Claims (lines 17101–22800 of source text)

**Focus (per task):** Decision mechanics in defeat (1941-42 winter crisis), total war shift (home front radicalization 1942), internal dynamics (generals vs. Hitler, Speer rise), Stalingrad / Blue planning and overstretch (Kursk precursors in offensive design). Verbatim quotes + mechanics only from this cache range.

**1. Winter crisis 1941-42 and 'stand-fast' order mechanics.**
After Soviet counter-offensive (5 Dec 1941 onward) shattered hopes for Moscow, Hitler overruled Army High Command and field commanders. 'There can be no question of a withdrawal. Only in some places has there been deep penetration by the enemy. Setting up rear positions is fantasy. The front is suffering from one thing only: the enemy has more soldiers.' (night 16-17 Dec order to Brauchitsch/Halder). 'The fanatical will to defend the ground on which the troops are standing must be injected into the troops with every possible means, even the toughest... Where this will is not fully present the front will begin to crumble without any prospect of stabilizing it once more in a prepared position.' Scorched-earth: 'Every piece of territory which is forced to be left to the enemy must be made unusable... Every place of inhabitation must be burnt down and destroyed without consideration for the population.' Direct interventions: Guderian audience 20 Dec (5 hours); Hitler forbade retreat, insisted on blasting foxholes in frozen earth as in Flanders. Guderian: 'ground conditions in Flanders and Russia in midwinter were scarcely comparable.' Hitler: 'Do you think Frederick the Great’s grenadiers were anxious to die? They wanted to live, too, but the king was right in asking them to sacrifice themselves. I believe that I, too, am entitled to ask any German soldier to lay down his life.' 'You should stand back more... Believe me, things appear clearer when examined at longer range.' Guderian/Bock contempt for cave-in; Guderian sacked 26 Dec on Kluge request. Similar orders to all Army Groups: 'defend the front to the last man.' Crisis stabilized by end Jan only after partial concessions to tactical withdrawals. Hitler: 'And I pulled it off! That we overcame this winter... is solely attributable to the bravery of the soldiers at the front and my firm will to hold out, cost what it may.' (Grounded verbatim: cache lines ~2470-2689, esp. 2484, 2490, 2582, 2590, 2620-2639, 2673-2689)

**2. Hitler assumes direct command of Army (19 Dec 1941).**
Brauchitsch (severely depressed, heart attack Nov, 'scarcely any longer even a postman') dismissed. Hitler took supreme command himself. 'It meant... that Hitler was now taking over direct responsibility for tactics, as well as grand strategy. No other head of a belligerent state — not even Stalin... — was so closely involved in the minutiae of military affairs.' Hitler: army command 'no more than a “little matter of operational command” that “anyone can do”.' Halder saw opportunity for greater influence (quickly dashed by Keitel). Deprived regime of future scapegoats for disasters. Sacking of top generals (Bock, Guderian, Rundstedt, Leeb, Hoepner, Strauß, Förster, Sponeck) followed in weeks. (Grounded: cache ~2520-2560, 2490-2500, 2643-2650)

**3. Winter 1941-42 as turning point and 'beginning of the end'.**
'Against what had seemed in the depths of the winter crisis almost insuperable odds, Germany was ready by spring to launch another offensive in the east. The war still had a long way to go.' 'By the end of 1941, Germany had failed to defeat the Soviet Union and was now embroiled in a long, enormously bitter and costly, war in the east. Britain had not only been uninterested in coming to terms, but was now fighting alongside the USA and... allied... with the Soviet Union. Not least, Germany was now at war with America. Whatever Hitler’s contempt, he knew no ways of defeating the USA... Hitler now had to place his hopes in the Japanese... He could no longer depend upon the power of German arms alone. Germany no longer held the initiative.' 'The winter of 1941–2 can nevertheless, in retrospect, be seen to be not merely a turning-point, but the beginning of the end.' (Grounded: cache ~2727-2755, 2732-2733)

**4. Total war shift and home-front radicalization (Feb-Apr 1942).**
Todt's death in air crash 8 Feb 1942 (after tense armaments meeting with Hitler) led to Speer's immediate appointment (within 6 hours) to all Todt offices. Speer 'could do within the widest limits practically what I wanted' after Hitler's public backing. Goebbels (19 Mar visit) pressed for radical measures against black market, privilege, 'saboteurs'; judiciary 'bourgeois elements'. Hitler agreed: prepare Reichstag to grant 'special plenipotentiary power' so 'evil-doers know that he is covered... by the people’s community'. 26 Apr Reichstag: Hitler given powers 'without being bound to existing legal precepts' to remove from office/punish anyone failing duty (no pension rights, no formal proceedings) in capacity as Führer, Supreme Commander, Head of Government, 'oberster Gerichtsherr', Party Leader. 'Hitler now was the law.' Used sensationalized Schlitt case (lenient sentence for wife-killing) to justify assault on judges: 'I will intervene in such cases and dismiss judges who visibly fail to recognize the demands of the hour.' Parallel winter clothing appeal (20 Dec 1941) and ration cuts exposed failures and required 'tough measures'. (Grounded: cache ~4459-4890, 4560-4570, 4660-4720, 4830-4860, 4690-4710)

**5. Stalingrad/Blue offensive decision mechanics and overstretch (Directive No.45, 23 July 1942).**
After Kerch/Kharkov successes and Bock dismissal (Voronezh delay), Directive No.45 (Hitler's, Halder's imprint absent) changed plan: simultaneous thrusts — Caucasus (Army Group A under List) and Stalingrad (weaker Army Group B under Weichs), including capture of the city. Earlier April directive: destroy forces near Stalingrad then Caucasus. 'The strategy was sheer lunacy.' Halder (12 July): Soviets avoiding encirclement, withdrawing south. Hitler: Red Army 'close to the end of its tether'; pressed speedy advance. Halder 15 Aug: 'Overall picture: have we extended the risk too far?' 23 July diary: 'This chronic tendency to underrate enemy capabilities is gradually assuming grotesque proportions and develops into a positive danger. The situation is getting more and more intolerable. There is no room for any serious work. This so-called leadership is characterized by a pathological reacting to the impressions of the moment and a total lack of any understanding of the command machinery and its possibilities.' Hitler (spring 1942): 'If I don’t get the oil of Maykop and Grozny, then I must finish this war.' Stalingrad commitment (30 Sept): 'You can be sure that nobody will get us away from this place again!' Move to Werwolf HQ (Vinnitsa) 16 July; logistics exposed flanks immediate problems. (Grounded: cache ~5536-5689, 5550-5560, 5600-5629, 5639-5674, 5015-5020)

**6. Internal dynamics and Hitler's contempt for professionals.**
Hitler: generals showed 'almost complete failure of leadership'; 'one general had come to him, wanting to retreat... I immediately dismissed the general'. 'I pulled it off!' Pattern of direct interventions and sackings (Brauchitsch, Bock, Guderian, Rundstedt etc.). 'My generals know nothing about the economic aspects of war.' (Guderian recall). Jodl (Aug 1941 context recurring): 'the Führer’s reasons are well thought out and cannot be pushed aside... His intuition has generally been right. You can’t deny that!' (Grounded: cache ~2673-2689, 880-890, 2540)

**7. Hitler on Stalingrad surrender (1 Feb 1943).**
'How can someone be so cowardly? I don’t understand it. So many people have to die. Then such a man goes and besmirches in the last minute the heroism of so many others.' (on Paulus). (Grounded: cache 4334-4336)

**8. 1942 outlook in Hitler's view.**
'H itler saw 1942 as difficult, but a far better situation developing in 1943. Foodstuffs and raw materials were now available from the occupied European parts of the Soviet Union... our victory can no longer be endangered.' But 'the last two weeks of October had had a highly sobering effect'; troops short ~625k men by spring; 'the situation in the east was “at the moment not very good”'. (Grounded: cache 2015-2016, 2320, 5030-5040)

**Liquidation of remainder of Czechoslovakia and Memel (Oct 1938–March 1939):**

**20 July 1944 assassination attempt and coup (Stauffenberg, Valkyrie, Wolf's Lair, Bendlerblock):**
- Stauffenberg background and conversion: 'when he turned irredeemably against Hitler in the late spring of 1942, it was under the influence of incontrovertible eye-witness reports of massacres of Ukrainian Jews by SS men. Hearing the reports, Stauffenberg concluded that Hitler must be removed.' 'the only way to deal with Hitler was to kill him.'
- Valkyrie transformation: recast existing plan to denounce 'an unscrupulous clique of non-combat Party leaders' which 'has tried to exploit the situation to stab the deeply committed front in the back'; 'The aim of 'Valkyrie' had been to protect the regime; it was now transformed into a strategy for removing it.'
- Access problems and failed earlier attempts: Bussche (wounded), Kleist, Breitenbuch (ADCs excluded); Stieff declined twice. 'Luck was still on Hitler's side.'
- 20 July mechanics (verbatim detail): Stauffenberg and Haeften armed one charge in toilet; 'There was no chance of setting the time-fuse for the second device'; second device left behind. 'Had the second device... been placed in Stauffenberg's bag along with the first, it would have been detonated by the explosion, more than doubling the effect. Almost certainly, in such an event, no one would have survived.'
- Placement and explosion: briefcase 'against the outside of the solid right-hand table-leg'. Hitler 'bent over the heavy oaken table, propped up on his elbow, chin in his hand'; 'flash of blue and yellow flame and an ear-splitting explosion'. 'Twenty-four persons had been in the briefing-hut.' Stenographer Berger killed; Brandt, Korten, Schmundt died later.
- Hitler survival verbatim: 'Hitler had, remarkably, survived with no more than superficial injuries.' 'Keitel helped Hitler, his uniform jacket torn, his black trousers and beneath them long white underwear in shreds, out of the building.' 'Linge, someone has tried to kill me.' 'When Linge, his valet, panic-stricken, rushed in, Hitler was composed, and with a grim smile on his face said: "Linge, someone has tried to kill me."'

'Leaderless and mindless' (Gisevius on the group awaiting Stauffenberg). 'A fine mess, this (Schöne Schweinerei, das)' (Witzleben to Stauffenberg).

**Hitler's reaction, purge, and radicalization:**
- 'Now I finally have the swine who have been sabotaging my work for years... Now I have proof: the entire General Staff is contaminated.' 'Now I know why all my great plans in Russia had to fail in recent years. It was all treason! But for those traitors, we would have won long ago.'

- Paranoia: 'he believed no one any more... In his intimate circle he now found no restraining influence.' Security: 'At military briefings, all personnel were from now on thoroughly searched for weapons and explosives.' Food and presents destroyed.

- Initial surge: 'strongest consternation, shock, deep outrage, and fury'. 'Thank God the Führer is alive.' Rallies; 'loyalty rallies'. Rise in faith among POWs and front letters.

- 'I do not wish to fall into the hands of enemies who, for the amusement of their whipped-up masses, will need a spectacle arranged by Jews.'
- Expels Göring and Himmler 'for the damage they had done through negotiating with the enemy “without my knowledge and against my wishes”'. Dönitz as head of state (Reich President, not Führer); Goebbels Chancellor; Bormann Party Minister; Schörner Commander of the Army; Karl Hanke Reichsführer-SS; 'I charge the leadership of the nation... with the meticulous observance of the race-laws and the merciless resistance to the universal poisoner of all peoples, international Jewry.'
- Goebbels coda (5.30a.m.): 'For the first time in my life, I must categorically refuse to obey an order of the Führer.' 'In the delirium of treachery... there have to be at least a few who stay unconditionally loyal to him even unto death.' 'rather at the Führer’s side to end a life which for me personally has no further value if it cannot be used in the service of the Führer and by his side.'

**Final preparations, Blondi test, farewells (29 April):**
- Blondi test (Prof. Haase, Tornow): 'forced open the dog’s jaws and crushed the prussic acid capsule with a pair of pliers. The dog slumped in an instant motionless to the ground.' Hitler 'glanced for a few seconds at the dead dog. Then, his face like a mask, he left without saying anything'.
- Couriers sent with Testaments (Zander, Johannmeier, Lorenz).
- Farewell to ~20–25 servants/guards: 'mentioned Himmler’s treachery and told them that he had decided to take his own life rather than be captured by the Russians and put on show like an exhibition in a museum.' 'released them from their oath'.
- Final briefing: Weidling 'the battle for Berlin would in all probability... be over that evening.' Hitler gave written permission for break-out in small numbers; 'rejected emphatically a capitulation of the capital.'
- Commission to Günsche: 'He did not want to be put on display in some waxworks in Moscow.' Günsche phoned Kempka for petrol.

**Suicide and cremation (30 April):**
- ~13:00 lunch with secretaries 'composed, giving no hint that his death was imminent.'
- ~15:20–15:30: farewell handshake to secretaries, Bormann, Goebbels, Burgdorf, Krebs, Axmann. 'Looking more stooped than ever, Hitler, dressed as usual in his uniform jacket and black trousers, appeared alongside Eva Braun, who was wearing a blue dress with white trimmings.' 'He held out his hand to each of them, muttered a few words, and, within a few minutes and without further formalities, returned to his study.'
- 'After waiting ten minutes or so, still without a sound from Hitler’s room, Linge took the initiative. He took Bormann with him and cautiously opened the door. In the cramped study, Hitler and Eva Braun sat alongside each other on the small sofa. Eva Braun was slumped to Hitler’s left. A strong whiff of bitter almonds – the distinctive smell of prussic acid – drifted up from her body. Hitler’s head drooped lifelessly. Blood dripped from a bullet-hole in his right temple. His 7.65mm Walther pistol lay by his foot.'
- Bodies wrapped in blankets, carried up: Linge + 3 SS guards for Hitler ('head covered by the blanket, his lower legs protruding'); Bormann then Kempka/Günsche for Eva. Laid 'side by side, Eva Braun to Hitler’s right, on a piece of flat, open, sandy ground only about three metres from the door down to the bunker.'
- Cremation: ~200 litres petrol poured. 'setting the funeral pyre alight with the matches Goebbels supplied proved difficult.' Linge 'managed to find some paper to make a torch. Bormann was finally able to get it burning'. 'Arms briefly raised in a final “Heil Hitler” salute, the tiny funeral party hurriedly departed underground'. 'the end of the leader whose presence had a mere few years earlier electrified millions was witnessed by not a single one even of his closest followers.'
- Remains: Karnau/Mansfeld saw 'two charcoaled, shrivelled, unrecognizable bodies (“zwei verkohlte, zusammensgeschrumpfte Leichen, die nicht mehr zu identifizieren waren”)'. Lindloff (with Reisser) 'carried out the order'. 'The bodies, he said, had been already thoroughly burnt (“schon verkohlt”) and were in a “shocking state (scheußlichem Zustand)”'.

**Soviet identification and aftermath:**
- 'Nine days later, they showed the dental technician Fritz Echtmann... a cigar-box containing part of a jaw-bone and two dental bridges. He was able to identify from his records one of the bridges as that of Hitler, the other as Eva Braun’s. The lower jaw-bone, too, was Hitler’s. It was almost certainly all that they were able to identify of the former Dictator of Germany. The earthly remains of Adolf Hitler, it appears, were contained in a cigar-box.'
- Krebs emissary 1 May: Soviets demand 'unconditional surrender' by 4p.m. 'This was the end.'
- Goebbels/Bormann telegrams to Dönitz (10:53a.m.): 'Testament in force.' Broadcast at 10:26p.m.: 'Hitler had died that afternoon' (actually previous day) 'in combat “at his post in the Reich Chancellery, while fighting to his last breath against Bolshevism”.' 'The Führer’s “heroic death”.' Weidling order to troops: 'On 30.4.45 the Führer took his own life and thereby abandoned those who had sworn him loyalty.'
- Goebbels family (evening 1 May): Kunz administered morphine ~20:40 to Helga, Hilde, Hellmut, Holde, Hedda, Heide (ages 12–4). Stumpfegger 'crushed a phial of prussic acid in the mouth of each'. Goebbels/Magda 'climbed the stairs to the Chancellery garden, and bit on the prussic acid capsules. An SS orderly fired two shots into the bodies to make sure.'
- Break-out late 1 May: groups via underground to Friedrichstraße; 'confusion took over.' 'Most, among them Otto Günsche and Heinz Linge, fell into Soviet hands'. 'Prominent among the latter were... Martin Bormann, and his doctor, Ludwig Stumpfegger. Both... had swallowed poison in the early hours of 2 May 1945 in Berlin’s Invalidenstraße.'
- Krebs, Burgdorf, Schädle also suicides in bunker.

**Dönitz regime and capitulation:**
- Dönitz (Flensburg): retained Keitel/Jodl/Speer; hoped partial capitulation to west only, common front vs Bolshevism. '1.8 million German soldiers... avoid Soviet captivity'.
- Reims: Jodl signed 2:41a.m. 7 May; 'complete ending of all German military engagements by the end of the following day.'
- Karlshorst (Berlin) 8/9 May: Keitel, Friedeburg, Stumpff (Luftwaffe) signed full document. 'The struggle lasting almost six years is accordingly at an end.'
- 'Hitler’s war was over. The reckoning was about to begin.'

**Other leaders' fates:**
- Himmler: 'crunched a phial of potassium cyanide in captivity at Lüneburg on 23 May'.
- Ley: 'strangled himself in the lavatory of his prison cell at Nuremberg on 24 October'.
- Göring: 'committed suicide – cheating the hangman... on the late evening of 15 October 1946' after Nuremberg conviction.
- Hanged 16 Oct 1946: Ribbentrop, Keitel, Jodl, Rosenberg, Frick, Seyß-Inquart, Sauckel, Kaltenbrunner, Frank, Streicher.
- Others: Speer, Dönitz, Funk, Neurath, Raeder, Schirach, Heß long sentences. Eichmann hanged 1962 (Israel). Höß, Stroop, Greiser, Forster hanged in Poland.

**Epilogue reflections (Kershaw):**
- Manchester Guardian (2 May 1945): 'Europe has never known such a calamity to her civilisation and nobody can say when she will begin to recover from its effects.'
- 'Never in history has such ruination – physical and moral – been associated with the name of one man.'
- 'Hitler was the main author of a war leaving over 50 million dead... Hitler was the chief inspiration of a genocide the like of which the world had never known... The Reich whose glory he had sought lay at the end wrecked... The arch-enemy, Bolshevism, stood in the Reich capital itself...'
- 'The German people he was prepared to see damned alongside him proved capable of surviving even a Hitler.'
