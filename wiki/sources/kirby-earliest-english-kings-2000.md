# The Earliest English Kings (D. P. Kirby, rev. edn 2000)

D. P. Kirby's *The Earliest English Kings* is the standard modern political narrative of
the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms from the late sixth century to the mid-ninth — from the
shadowy first overlords through the conversion-era kings of [[kingdom-of-kent|Kent]] and
[[kingdom-of-northumbria|Northumbria]], the seventh-century northern hegemonies of
[[edwin-of-northumbria|Eadwine]], [[oswald-of-northumbria|Oswald]] and
[[oswiu-of-northumbria|Oswiu]], the eighth-century [[mercia|Mercian]] supremacy of
[[æthelbald-of-mercia|Æthelbald]] and [[offa-of-mercia|Offa]], and the early
ninth-century rise of [[ecgberht-of-wessex|Ecgberht]] of
[[kingdom-of-wessex|Wessex]], closing with the coming of the Vikings.

Its distinctive contribution is **source criticism applied to chronology and to the
idea of overlordship**: Kirby dismantles the tidy "[[heptarchy|Heptarchy]]" and the
notion of a continuous "[[bretwalda|Bretwalda]]"-ship, shows how far Bede and the
*Anglo-Saxon Chronicle* impose retrospective order on a fragmentary record, and rebuilds
the political sequence kingdom by kingdom from charters, papal letters and the Irish and
Welsh annals.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Kirby's *The Earliest English Kings* gives the wiki its first dedicated political spine for
Anglo-Saxon England from the conversion period to the eve of the Viking settlement —
filling the gap between the late-Roman material ([[roman-britain]]) and the existing
late-Anglo-Saxon and Norman pages ([[kingdom-of-england-late-anglo-saxon]],
[[norman-conquest-of-england-1066]]). Where the regional period page
[[early-middle-ages-britain-ireland]] (built on Stafford's *Companion*) frames the
archipelago thematically and four-nations, Kirby supplies the kingdom-by-kingdom
political narrative and, above all, a rigorous **chronology**.

The volume's central methodological lesson, propagated across the pages it created, is
**source-critical scepticism about dates and about the very categories of overlordship**.
Kirby repeatedly corrects Bede and the [[anglo-saxon-chronicle|Chronicle]] against the
Irish annals, the Welsh material and indictional dating — pushing Eadwine's death to 634,
the Winwæd to 655/6, Aldfrith's death to 704, Æthelbald's to 757 (despite the Chronicle's
755), and Ellendun to 825 (not 823). He dismantles the tidy [[heptarchy|"Heptarchy"]] and
the notion of a transmissible [[bretwalda|"Bretwaldaship"]], reconstructing instead a
fluctuating hierarchy of tribute and military leadership best glimpsed through the
[[tribal-hidage|Tribal Hidage]].

Read end to end, the book traces a shifting centre of gravity: the early Kentish and West
Saxon overlordships; the seventh-century **northern Anglian hegemony** of Eadwine, Oswald
and Oswiu, checked by [[penda-of-mercia|Penda]] and ended by the Picts at
[[battle-of-nechtansmere-685|Nechtansmere]]; the eighth-century [[mercian-supremacy|Mercian
supremacy]] of Æthelbald and Offa; and the early-ninth-century West Saxon breakthrough at
[[battle-of-ellendun-825|Ellendun]] — the platform on which Alfred's house would later
build. This complements rather than conflicts with the existing [[mckitterick-ncmh-v2-1995|NCMH II]]
treatment (Keynes): both stress the distorting partisanship of the sources, but Kirby goes
further in actively re-dating the political sequence.
