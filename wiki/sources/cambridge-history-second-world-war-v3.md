# The Cambridge History of the Second World War, Vol. III: Total War (2015)

The economy-society-culture volume of the series, edited by Michael Geyer and Adam Tooze — the war
as the total mobilisation and destruction of whole societies. The most additive of the available
volumes for the wiki's analytical pages.

## What it contributes

- **[[the-holocaust]]** — a new event page (the genocide as the catastrophic core of the war),
  grounded in Vol. III's treatment of ideological war (*Weltanschauungskrieg*), displacement, and
  "destruction through labour," plus established scholarship (the dedicated ideology volume being
  absent). The analytical concept lives on [[genocide]].
- Deepens **[[war-economy]]** with Tooze's WWII economics (the Allies' material preponderance vs.
  Axis resource constraints) and **[[total-war]]**.

## Reading note

Deep-read of the economic chapters and the introduction (Geyer & Tooze on total war as the
consumption of society; ideological war and genocide); the memory chapters sampled. This is the
distinctive, additive scholarship of the available volumes.
