# Islam and Asia: A History (Chiara Formichi, 2020)

Cambridge University Press, *New Approaches to Asian History* series. A thematic
survey arguing that **Asia is central — not peripheral — to the history of Islam**.
Formichi (Associate Professor, Cornell) inverts the standard Middle-East-centric
narrative: the majority of the world's Muslims have always lived east of the Arab
heartland, and Asia's Muslim societies have been sites of production, not mere
reception, of Islamic thought, law, art, and politics. Each chapter opens with a
material object or vignette and develops a theme across a broad chronological span
rather than a linear regional narrative.

## Central Argument

- The Middle East's presumed dominance in "authentic" Islam is a modern
  historiographical artifact; demographically and intellectually, Asia (South,
  Southeast, Central, and East) has been the Muslim world's center of gravity for
  most of Islamic history.
- Islamization was **multidirectional and continuous** — a matter of ongoing
  "becoming Muslim" through networks (trade, Sufi orders, pilgrimage, scholarship)
  rather than a single moment of conquest or conversion.
- Local Asian conceptions of power, gender, science, and bureaucracy shaped Islamic
  practice as much as Islam reshaped them; "Islamized interactions" flowed back into
  the wider Muslim world.
