# Griffin, *The Nature of Fascism* (Pinter, 1991)

The book that reoriented Anglophone fascism studies: against definitional
nominalism and checklist typologies, Griffin locates fascism's identity in a
**mythic core** — "a palingenetic form of populist ultranationalism" — and
applies the ideal type across Italy, Germany, the abortive interwar
movements, non-European and post-war cases, and the psycho-historical and
socio-political conditions of fascism's success or failure.

## Volume Synthesis Note

With Griffin ingested, the wiki's definitional quadrilateral on
[[generic-fascism]] is complete: Paxton (behavioral-processual), Payne
(typological), Sternhell (ideological-genealogical), Griffin
(mythic-ideal-typical). Griffin's distinctive contributions: (1) the
Weberian reframing — all definitions are competing ideal types, "useful"
not "true," which dissolves the field's definitional anxiety; (2) the
palingenetic core as the most compact discriminator, with
[[para-fascism]] handling the boundary cases the others handle by
typology (Payne) or behavior (Paxton) — the three frameworks converge
almost perfectly on Franco, Salazar, Vichy, and Antonescu as non-fascist;
(3) the four-precondition retrodictive model, which parallels Payne's
retrodictive theory while adding the Bulgaria negative case and the
Italy/Germany/Finland/South-Africa list; (4) against Sternhell, the
reincorporation of Nazism into generic fascism, with racism as contingent
variant rather than generic separator — the sharpest live disagreement now
recorded on [[sternhell-thesis]] and [[palingenetic-ultranationalism]].

Cross-volume tensions: Griffin dates fascism's coherence to myth rather
than doctrine (vs. Sternhell), extends it past 1945 and outside Europe
(vs. Payne and Nolte), and reads regime failure as structurally inevitable
("a parenthesis... Mussolini or no Mussolini" — a partial rehabilitation of
Croce against Sternhell's anti-parenthesis polemic). His 1991 vantage
(post-communist prognosis, apartheid South Africa as live case, Cambodia/
Iraq as post-fascist dangers) is itself now a historiographical artifact
worth preserving. The remaining gap in the quadrilateral's literature is
Griffin's later *Modernism and Fascism* (2007, in raw/) and the Nazi
narrative backbone (Evans trilogy, in raw/).
