# A History of India, Volume One (Romila Thapar, 1966)

The standard academic narrative of the Indian subcontinent from the **Indus civilization to the
eve of the Mughals (1526)**, and the wiki's structural backbone for **Gap 3**. Thapar's organising
concerns are the **social and economic substructure** beneath the dynasties, the recurring tension
between **northern and peninsular (Tamil) India**, the long interplay of **orthodoxy and heterodoxy**
(Brahmanism vs. Buddhism/Jainism/Bhakti), and India's deep integration into **Eurasian trade and
cultural exchange** (the Roman trade, the Indianisation of Southeast Asia, the coming of Islam).

This volume mostly **enriches** the India pages already created from the Cambridge World History and
Asian-Classics primary texts (Mauryan empire, Ashoka, Chandragupta, Gupta empire, Kushans, Magadha,
Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, caste) and **creates** the large missing pieces — the Indus civilization,
the Vedic age, the Cholas and the southern kingdoms, the regional states and Rajputs, the Delhi
Sultanate, the Bhakti movement, and Vijayanagara.

## Section Plan

| Cycle | Chapters | Period | Key Topics |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundations & the Vedic age | 1–2 | Neolithic–Early Iron Age | Historiography of India; the **Indus/Harappan civilization**; the coming of **Aryan/Vedic culture**; **caste (varna)**; Vedic religion |
| 2. Magadha to the Mauryas | 3–4 | Archaic–Classical Antiquity | Republics & kingdoms; rise of **Magadha**, the Nandas, Persian/Greek contact; urbanisation; the **heterodox sects (Jainism, Buddhism)**; the **Mauryan empire**, **Ashoka & Dhamma** |
| 3. Between the empires | 5–6 | Classical–Late Antiquity | Post-Mauryan fragmentation (Shungas, **Indo-Greeks, Shakas, Kushanas, Satavahanas**, early South); the **mercantile boom** (guilds, **Roman trade**, China/SE Asia contacts); **Mahayana**, early Hinduism, Christianity |
| 4. The Classical pattern & the South | 7–9 | Late Antiquity–High Middle Ages | The **Guptas** & Harsha (the "classical" age); the southern kingdoms (**Chalukya/Pallava/Pandya**; Shankara; Tamil devotion); the **Cholas** (administration, temple, trade) |
| 5. Regional states & the coming of Islam | 10–11 | Early–High Middle Ages | Northern regional states (**Rashtrakutas/Pratiharas/Palas**; Arabs in Sind; **Rajputs**; **Mahmud of Ghazni**, Muhammad Ghuri); **"Indian feudalism"**; decline of Buddhism; the arrival of the **Sufis** |
| 6. The Delhi Sultanate & assimilation | 12–14 | High–Late Middle Ages | The **Delhi Sultanate** (Slave/Khalji/Tughluq/Sayyid/Lodi); the impact of Islam, the **Bhakti** movement, new languages; **Vijayanagara & the Bahmani** kingdoms. Then synthesis. |
