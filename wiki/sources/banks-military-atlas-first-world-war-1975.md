# A Military Atlas of the First World War (Arthur Banks)

This is a major cartographic reference for the First World War / Great War (1914–1918), featuring hundreds of detailed maps covering pre-war alignments, the 1914 mobile campaigns on all fronts, the development of the Western Front stalemate, Gallipoli, the Eastern Front, Italy, the Middle East, naval warfare, and later operations through 1918.

Primary value: **visual and spatial**. The maps provide clear, labeled depictions of force dispositions, advances, key terrain, and decision points at campaign and battle level. Text is limited to titles, keys, and the accompanying Alan Palmer commentary.

## Structure and Coverage (327 pages)

Organized by period and theater with detailed plates for nearly every significant operation:

- The Pre-War Situation (causes, alliances, crises 1905–1914, July 1914)
- War on the Western Front in 1914 (very high resolution: Liège, Namur, Mons, Le Cateau, Marne, Aisne, Antwerp, Yser, First Ypres, etc.)
- War on the Eastern Front in 1914 (Tannenberg, Masurian Lakes, Galicia, Lodz, Serbia)
- The Gallipoli Campaign (Dardanelles naval ops, landings, trench warfare, evacuation)
- The War in 1915, 1916 (Neuve Chapelle, Ypres, Artois, Loos, Verdun, Somme, etc.)
- Subsequent years: 1917–1918 offensives, other theaters (Italian Front, Middle East, Africa, naval war, air)
- Thematic overviews, fortifications, force organization comparisons, and situation snapshots at critical dates.

Signature style: clean line work, hatching for Entente vs Central Powers, arrows for movement, numbered keys explaining context. Page numbers in book often correspond directly to the map depicted. Maps are accompanied by concise Palmer commentary in the printed volume.

## Historiography / Collection Note

Banks' atlas has been a standard reference for students and historians since 1975 for its combination of breadth and cartographic clarity. It excels at showing the simultaneous global nature of the war and the specific operational problems on each front. Later scholarship (e.g. on opening moves or Gallipoli) may adjust details, but the maps remain reliable for understanding scale, geography, and sequence. The Bibliotheca Alexandrina extracts provide direct visual support for operational analysis without duplicating narrative.
