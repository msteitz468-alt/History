# Hartz (ed.), *The Founding of New Societies* (1964)

Hartz's generalization of the argument of
[[hartz-liberal-tradition-1955]] into a comparative theory of five
"fragment" societies created by European migration: the United States,
Latin America, South Africa, Canada, and Australia. Part One (Hartz) sets
out the general theory — extrication from Europe, atrophy of the
ideological future, interior unfolding of the inherited ethic, its burial
under substitute nationalisms, its extension to race, and its Cold-War-era
"return to revolution." Part Two applies it country by country through
essays by other hands (the framework page is [[fragment-thesis]]).

## ⚠ Extraction Incompleteness

The epub→text conversion on hand preserves **only the Preface and
Chapters One and Two** (the fragment mechanism; the feudal/liberal/radical
patterns). Chapter Three of Part One and the entirety of Part Two — the
five country studies, including Hartz's own Chapter Four on the United
States (his 1962 Commonwealth Lectures) — dropped out of the conversion.
The ingest therefore covers the theoretical core at full fidelity but
none of the country-specialist material. If a complete copy is later
obtained, Part Two warrants a supplementary pass (Morse's Latin America
and McRae's Canada essays are independently influential).

## Key Claims (Chapters One–Two)

**Ch. 1 — The Fragmentation of European Culture and Ideology.** The
fragment mechanism and the "contagion" model of European ideological
succession ("Marx fades because of the fading of Laud"); fragmentation as
mutual unintelligibility between Europe and fragment; ideology reborn as
nationalism (Americanism, the French-Canadian race, the Australian
Legend), articulated by the settlers' *children*, honest rather than
meretricious; nationalism's services — immigrant absorption
("Americanization"), the treason cry against enemies that "do not, and
cannot, exist"; the racial question as the fragment ethic extended to
relationships Europe never knew (Suárez beneath the encomienda, Calvin
beneath Dutch-South-African slavery; "liberal slavery" harsher in
practice than feudal); the return to revolution — fragment reaction
(re-flight/isolationism, treason cries, hysteria) and the predicted
dissolution of the fragment world by new generations.

**Ch. 2 — Fragmentation Patterns: Feudal, Liberal, and Radical.**
Determinism of the pattern ("choice is not the word to use").
*Feudal* (Latin America, French Canada): no Whiggery → no Jacobinism →
no normal socialism; Papineau's Patriotes and the crushed Rougists;
post-Independence caudillos as projections of a preliberal tradition, not
Bonapartes (contra García Calderón); Comte over Marx as the giveaway;
Catholic syndicates, Perón–Vargas authoritarian labor, Quebec clergy's
admiration for Salazar. *Liberal* (US, English Canada, Dutch South
Africa): Whiggery strategically doomed (nothing to reform, nothing to
lean on, no "leveler" cry); the Boer republics' spontaneous "Jacksonian
logic" (Orange Free State constitution); peasant→farmer,
proletarian→Gompers; Skidmore disciplined; "1848" yielding solid
democratic achievement. *Radical* (Australia, weaker British South
Africa): Exclusives vs. Emancipists and Wentworth's laughed-out
aristocracy; Labour's post-1890s mastery; caucus control and the "tall
stoop" distrust smothering doctrinaire socialism; other parties as
Hancock's "parties of resistance." *The Twentieth Century*: "the future
has returned" — delayed rendezvous with Babeuf; impatience and extremism
in feudal fragments (Castro, QLF); Enlightenment fragments as
"successfully functioning antiquities" whose real problem is
international incomprehension.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Read against the 1955 book, the 1964 volume converts an American
exceptionalism argument into a general comparative method: the United
States ceases to be unique and becomes one of three fragment types, its
Lockean unanimity now the *liberal* instance of a process that equally
produces Quebec's clerical traditionalism and Australia's radical
"mateship" conservatism. The deepest move is the symmetry claim — that a
**radical conservatism** (Australia enshrining Cobbett as passionately as
the US enshrines Locke) proves the traditionalizing power of
fragmentation "beyond any doubt at all." For the wiki, the book supplies
the missing comparative bridge between the American-history Hartz cluster
and the Australia, Canada, Latin America, and South Africa pages, and
stands as the mid-century predecessor that [[settler-colonialism]] theory
later displaced — ideology-of-origin explanation giving way to
land-and-elimination explanation, with almost no direct citation between
the two literatures.
