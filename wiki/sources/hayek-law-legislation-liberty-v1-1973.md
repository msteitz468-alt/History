# F. A. Hayek, *Law, Legislation and Liberty*, Volume 1: *Rules and Order* (1973)

First volume of Hayek's late trilogy (1973/1976/1979), his "new statement of the liberal principles of justice and political economy" — the sequel and self-correction to *The Constitution of Liberty* (1960). Vol. 1 argues that the trilogy's central defect-diagnosis of modern government is constitutional: the ideal of the rule of law failed because all powers were handed to a single omnicompetent legislature. Its positive core is the distinction between **cosmos** (grown, spontaneous order) and **taxis** (made order), and correspondingly between **nomos** (the law of liberty, grown rules of just conduct enforced by judges) and **thesis** (the law of legislation, rules of organization for government).
