# The Dutch Overseas Empire, 1600–1800 (Emmer & Gommans)

A comparative, two-ocean history of the **[[dutch-overseas-empire|Dutch overseas empire]]** — the global
commercial empire of the Dutch Republic across the Atlantic and Indian Oceans. The seventh and last of
the remaining available named Processing-List sources (Phase 12).

## The Argument

Emmer and Gommans treat the Dutch empire as a single, **two-ocean** phenomenon and set it in a
**comparative and indigenous** frame — asking how it measured against other European empires and how it
was shaped by the **Asian, African and American societies** the Dutch entered:

- **The Asian empire (VOC).** The **[[dutch-east-india-company|VOC]]** built a seaborne network from
  Batavia across the Indonesian archipelago to Ceylon, India, the Cape and Japan, displacing the
  Portuguese and enforcing **spice monopolies** through coercion and violence.
- **The Atlantic empire (WIC).** The West India Company warred on the Iberians, held Brazil and New
  Netherland for a time, and built the West African slaving posts and Caribbean/Guiana plantation
  colonies that made the Dutch major participants in the **Atlantic slave trade**.
- **The central paradox.** A republic celebrated for **religious tolerance** and commercial freedom that
  simultaneously ran **brutal monopolies and slavery** — a tension the authors place at the heart of the
  Dutch imperial experience.
- **Impact at home and abroad.** The empire's effects on the Dutch economy, society and the
  **[[merchant-republic|merchant republic]]**, and on the many overseas societies it touched.

## Significance

The book is the wiki's principal account of the **Dutch Golden Age empire** — the commercial and naval
power that, with the VOC and WIC, pioneered **company colonialism** and stood at the center of the
seventeenth-century world economy. It substantially deepens the wiki's thin **early-modern global**
coverage, joining the Reformation, military and commercial threads of this Phase-12 batch.
