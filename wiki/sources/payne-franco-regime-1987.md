# Payne, *The Franco Regime, 1936–1975* (Phoenix Press ed. 2000; orig. Univ. of Wisconsin Press, 1987)

Stanley G. Payne's 708-page political history of the Franco regime from its Civil War origins to the dictator's death — the seventh item of the fascism-studies batch, and the empirical companion to his typological work already ingested ([[sources/payne-history-of-fascism-1995|A History of Fascism (1995)]]). Payne's core framing: the regime "was not one but several" — a semi-fascist New State (1939–45), a National-Catholic corporative dictatorship (1945–59), and a developmentalist bureaucratic authoritarianism (1959–75) — with Franco's eclectic but stable personal beliefs (nationalism, unity, Catholicism, anti-party authoritarianism, politically directed economic development) as the continuity underneath. This is the key monographic evidence behind the [[concepts/para-fascism|para-fascism]] verdict on Franco already recorded from Griffin and Payne (1995).

## Volume Synthesis Note

Payne's overall argument is that the Franco regime is best understood as a *sequence* of regimes held together by one man's small, stable set of beliefs and his refusal ever to concede personal power ("from here only to the cemetery"). The book's analytical payoff comes at the two ends: the opening chapters embed the regime in a five-phase model of modern political development in which Spain's authoritarian interlude is typical, not exceptional (the Anglo-American path being the anomaly); the closing chapter delivers the comparative classification — authoritarian not totalitarian (Linz), semifascist 1937–45, closest to Antonescu's Romania, on a continuum between Mussolini and Salazar, with Bonapartism as the deepest genealogy of caudillaje.

For the wiki, the volume completes the fascism-studies batch's Spanish flank: where Payne (1995) and Griffin (1991) supplied the typological verdicts (semifascism, para-fascism), this book supplies the mechanics — the FET unification, the taming of Salvador Merino and Muñoz Grandes, the three-phase defascistization, the Hendaye price negotiations. Its two most cite-worthy empirical contributions are the repression accounting (Salas Larrazábal's registry-based ~70–72,000 executions per side, against both the inflated 200,000+ and the propaganda half-million; now on [[events/francoist-repression-1936]]) and the desarrollo statistics (on [[processes/spanish-economic-miracle]]).

Cross-volume tensions to watch: Payne's near-parity repression accounting, his assignment of "main responsibility" for 1936 to the Republican Left, and his counterfactual that a victorious Republic would have been no gentler are precisely the claims the post-2000 memoria-histórica school and Preston (*The Spanish Holocaust*, 2012, not yet ingested) reject; ingesting Preston or a Civil War-focused survey should trigger a controversy page on the scale and symmetry of the Spanish terrors. Payne's WWII verdict (Franco willing at his price; abstention initially Hitler's choice) likewise contradicts the older "skillful prudence" apologia — already flagged on [[events/hendaye-meeting-1940]].
