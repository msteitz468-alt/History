# The Storm Before the Storm: The Beginning of the End of the Roman Republic

## Overview
Mike Duncan's *The Storm Before the Storm* (2017) is a popular narrative history
of the two generations of Roman political history that preceded the civil wars
of Caesar and Pompey — roughly the destruction of Carthage and Numantia (146–133
BCE) through the death of Sulla (78 BCE). Its organizing argument is that the
Republic did not fall suddenly with Caesar but was hollowed out over the
preceding seventy years by the progressive erosion of the **mos maiorum** — the
unwritten customs and norms that had regulated aristocratic competition. Each
breach (the murder of [[tiberius-gracchus|Tiberius Gracchus]], the private
armies of [[marius|Marius]], the first march on Rome, the [[sullan-proscriptions-82bce|proscriptions]])
lowered the threshold for the next, until organized political violence and
personal armies became normal tools of Roman politics.

## Coverage
The book is a chronological narrative in thirteen chapters plus prologue,
covering (in order): the post-Carthage settlement and the Gracchi
([[tiberius-gracchus]], [[gaius-gracchus]]); the [[jugurthine-war-112-105bce|Jugurthine War]]
and the rise of [[marius|Gaius Marius]]; the [[cimbric-war-113-101bce|Cimbric War]]
and the Marian military reforms; the tribunates of Saturninus and Livius Drusus;
the [[social-war-91-88bce|Social War]]; the first [[first-mithridatic-war-89-85bce|Mithridatic War]];
Sulla's two marches on Rome, the [[sullan-civil-war-83-82bce|civil war of 83–82]],
the [[sullan-proscriptions-82bce|proscriptions]], and the Sullan constitutional
settlement and dictatorship.

## Key Attributed Facts and Theses

- **The mos maiorum thesis** (Duncan's frame): the Republic fell not through the erosion
  of written law but through the collapse of unwritten aristocratic norms — a ratchet of
  precedent-breaking running Tiberius Gracchus → Marius's client army → Sulla's march on
  Rome → the proscriptions. Quarantined to [[mos-maiorum]] as an interpretive frame, not
  asserted in wiki voice.
- **Precedent-setting facts** carried onto pages as attributed material: Scipio
  Aemilianus's ~4,000-man private client army for Numantia (134); the Marian recruitment
  of the *capite censi*; Marius's five consecutive consulships and title "Third Founder of
  Rome"; Sulla's two marches on Rome and the crossing of the Pomerium under arms; the
  Marian terror of 87 and the Sullan proscriptions of 82; the young Pompey and Crassus
  each raising private armies to join Sulla.
- **Duncan's own source-critical flags** (worth preserving): he rejects the traditional
  claim that Marius switched the legion from maniple to cohort ("not one shred of
  evidence"); calls Sulla's claimed 14-dead casualty ratio at Chaeronea "a bald-faced
  lie"; and notes Sulla's *Memoirs* shaped the entire later tradition.
