## Overview

Ian Tattersall's *The World from Beginnings to 4000 BCE* (Oxford University
Press, New Oxford World History, 2008) is a compact (~150 pp.) synthesis of human
evolution from the origin of the hominid family (~7 million years ago) through the
emergence of agriculture and the first complex societies (~4000 BCE). Unlike most
volumes in its series, it is essentially a work of paleoanthropology and
Paleolithic archaeology rather than conventional "history": its subject is the
biological and cognitive becoming of *Homo sapiens*, told through fossils and
stone tools rather than texts.

The book's analytic spine is Tattersall's insistence that human evolution was
**not** a linear progression from primitiveness to perfection but a "bushy,"
experimental radiation in which many hominid species coexisted, competed, and
mostly went extinct. He reads this through the lens of [[punctuated-equilibria]]
and species-as-individuals, and applies it to three great transitions: the
acquisition of upright bipedalism, the appearance of modern body proportions and
the dispersal from Africa, and the late, abrupt emergence of [[symbolic-cognition]].

## Key Claims Extracted

- **Bushy, non-linear hominid phylogeny.** From the earliest fossils the human
  family was a record of "evolutionary experimentation" — multiple coexisting
  species exploring the ways to be a hominid; *Homo sapiens* as the sole surviving
  hominid is an unusual, not a natural, state of affairs. See [[human-evolution]].
- **Punctuated equilibria and species-as-individuals.** Following Eldredge and
  Gould (1972), Tattersall reads the fossil record as long stasis punctuated by
  rapid speciation, with species (not just individuals) as evolutionary actors;
  evolutionary trends reflect species sorting/competition, not gradual within-
  lineage fine-tuning. See [[punctuated-equilibria]].
- **Bipedalism first, brains and tools later.** Upright posture, not the large
  brain or toolmaking, is the defining hominid trait; the Piltdown hoax long
  propped up the "brain-first" error. [[australopithecus-afarensis]] ("Lucy")
  walked upright with an ape-sized brain.
- **Body before behavior in the genus *Homo*.** Modern body proportions
  (Turkana Boy, [[homo-ergaster]], ~1.6 mya) appeared abruptly — plausibly via a
  regulatory-gene change — and it was this new "walking machine" anatomy, not
  better tools or bigger brains (cf. small-brained Dmanisi hominids), that
  enabled the first [[out-of-africa-1-1800kbp|exit from Africa]].
- **Out-of-Africa replacement, not multiregional continuity.** mtDNA ("African
  Eve," ~150–200 kya), a recent population bottleneck, and Neanderthal mtDNA
  distinctness support a recent African origin and replacement; Tattersall
  minimizes Neanderthal–sapiens interbreeding (a position later partly overturned
  by nuclear ancient DNA). See [[modern-human-origins-debate]].
- **Anatomy precedes behavioral/symbolic modernity.** Anatomically modern humans
  (Herto ~160 kya; Qafzeh ~92 kya) at first left an archaic, non-symbolic
  archaeological record; the symbolic "human capacity" emerged later (Blombos
  ochre ~75 kya; Cro-Magnon explosion ~40 kya), released culturally — most
  plausibly by [[syntactic-language-origins|language]] — from a neural substrate
  acquired earlier via **exaptation and emergence**. See [[symbolic-cognition]].
- **Multiple, independent agricultural origins.** Against diffusionism, agriculture
  arose independently in seven or eight epicenters after the Ice Age; settled life
  and plant domestication may have been prerequisites for animal domestication.
  See [[neolithic-revolution]], [[agricultural-origins]], [[domestication]].

## Reliability and Use

A trustworthy, authoritatively-authored overview whose chief value to the wiki is
(a) a clean species-by-species map of the hominid bush and (b) a sharply argued
theoretical frame (punctuated equilibria; exaptation/emergence of cognition;
anatomy-before-behavior). Its principal datedness is genetic: it predates the
2010 Neanderthal and Denisovan nuclear genomes, so its strong replacement-without-
interbreeding stance is now known to be too absolute (modern non-Africans carry
~1–2% Neanderthal ancestry). Its taxonomic "splitting" is itself a contested
position — "lumpers" recognize far fewer species. Both points are flagged on the
derived pages and in [[modern-human-origins-debate]].
