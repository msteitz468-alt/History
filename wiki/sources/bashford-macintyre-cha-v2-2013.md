# The Cambridge History of Australia, Volume 2: The Commonwealth of Australia

Volume 2 of *The Cambridge History of Australia* (Bashford & Macintyre, eds.,
2013) covers 1901 to the present. It opens on the first day of the twentieth
century with the inauguration of the [[commonwealth-of-australia|Commonwealth of
Australia]] and traces the fortunes of the new nation-state: the building of
national institutions to protect living standards (the "[[australian-settlement|
Australian Settlement]]"), the maintenance and eventual dismantling of the
[[white-australia-policy|White Australia policy]] and protective tariff regime,
the response to the two world wars and the Depression, and the late-century
turn to neoliberal reform, multiculturalism and engagement with Asia.

This source page anchors the Vol. 2 ingest. It closes the post-1901 Australia
gap, building on the deep-time and colonial backbone laid by Vol. 1.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Volume 2 carries the Australia backbone from Federation to the present, completing
the arc begun in [[bashford-macintyre-cha-v1-2013|Vol. 1]]. Its organising
argument is that the early [[commonwealth-of-australia|Commonwealth]] built a
distinctive regulatory order — protective tariff, wage arbitration, racially
restricted immigration, state paternalism and imperial dependence — that
contemporaries experienced as an egalitarian social democracy and that Paul Kelly
later named the "[[australian-settlement|Australian Settlement]]." Whether that
order was a single durable settlement or a continuously renegotiated exercise in
nation building is the volume's central historiographical tension, promoted here
to [[australian-settlement-debate]].

Part I shows the order stressed by four crises — World War 1, the 1930s
Depression, World War 2, and the 1970s collapse of protection — each extending
state capacity while leaving the two-party system and federal frame intact, until
the [[neoliberal-reform-australia|Hawke–Keating reforms]] dismantled the economic
pillars and the [[white-australia-policy]] gave way to
[[multiculturalism-australia]]. Part II's thematic chapters supply the texture:
secularisation, a concentrated media market, the [[australian-welfare-state]],
second-wave feminism, and above all the transformation of
[[indigenous-australia-20th-century|Indigenous policy]] from protection and
assimilation through self-determination to [[mabo-decision-1992|Mabo]] and the
apology.

Against the wiki's existing coverage, Vol. 2 reframes several already-ingested
themes: it gives the [[anzac-legend]] and [[australian-imperial-force|AIF]] their
twentieth-century afterlife in national memory; it connects
[[australia-asia-pacific-engagement|Asian engagement]] and
[[australian-defence-security|ANZUS]] to the slow unwinding of the
[[british-commonwealth|British]] tie; and it closes with the "history anxiety"
([[history-wars-australia]]) that makes Australian historiography itself
first-class subject matter — a fitting bookend to the frontier-violence debate
that Vol. 1 left open.
