# Reformations: The Early Modern World, 1450–1650 (Carlos M. N. Eire)

A magisterial survey of the **age of the Reformations** — the religious upheavals that remade Europe
between the late Middle Ages and the mid-seventeenth century — and of their role in the birth of the
**early modern world**. The fourth of the 7 remaining available Processing-List sources (the first of
the Phase-12 Early Modern titles), and a substantial addition to the wiki's thin early-modern coverage.

## The Argument

Eire's title is deliberately **plural**: there was not one Reformation but several intertwined ones.

- **The late-medieval background.** The explosion of 1517 was "long in coming" — prepared by reforming
  piety, humanism, the printing revolution, conciliarism, and widespread anticlericalism. **[[protestant-reformation-1517|Luther]]**
  lit elements already primed.
- **The Protestant Reformations.** **Lutheran**, **Reformed/Calvinist** (Zwingli, Calvin, Geneva), and
  the **English** settlement — each with its own theology, politics and geography — reshaped doctrine,
  worship, the church, and the relation of religion to the state.
- **The Radical Reformation.** Anabaptists, spiritualists and other dissenters who rejected both Rome and
  the magisterial reformers, often violently suppressed.
- **The [[catholic-reformation|Catholic Reformation]].** Not merely a "Counter-Reformation" reaction but
  a deep renewal — the **Council of Trent**, new orders (above all the **Jesuits**), reformed clergy and
  piety, and a global missionary drive.

## The Larger Thesis

Eire situates all this within the **making of modernity**: the Reformations shattered the unity of Latin
Christendom, entangled religion with the rising state and with confessional warfare (down to the Thirty
Years' War), and — paradoxically — set in motion a long **"desacralization" of the world**, a redrawing
of the boundary between sacred and secular that he sees as a deep root of later **secularity** and
**religious pluralism**. Belief and theology, not just politics and economics, are kept at the center of
the story.
