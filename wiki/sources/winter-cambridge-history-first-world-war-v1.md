# The Cambridge History of the First World War, Vol. I: Global War (2014)

The first volume of Jay Winter's three-volume reference, ingested to deepen the wiki's
**[[first-world-war-1914|First World War]]** coverage beyond origins and narrative — here,
the war as **total** and **global**.

## What it contributes

- **[[total-war]]** — the concept page: the war as the first total war (the mobilization of
  whole societies and economies for absolute aims; the erosion of the soldier/civilian line).
- Enriches **[[first-world-war-1914]]** (the global and colonial dimension).

## Argument

Winter's volume reframes the conflict from a European war into a **world event**: "the first
total war was truly a world event, in that no region of the planet, or nearly none, was
spared." It stresses the global mobilization of manpower (Chinese, Indian, Vietnamese, and
African labourers and soldiers — "an important part of the war for their huge human resource
contributions"), the spread of fighting to Africa, the Middle East, and the oceans, and the
way total war's logic — abolishing the distinction between soldier and civilian — produced
mass atrocity and the **Armenian genocide**. It is also careful: the actual battlefield (the
attrition of the Somme and Verdun, where only a fraction of divisions fought on a given day)
complicates a naive picture of "total" combat.
