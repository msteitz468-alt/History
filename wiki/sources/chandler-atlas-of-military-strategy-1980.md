# Atlas of Military Strategy: The Art, Theory and Practice of War 1618-1878 (David Chandler, 1980)

This is a focused cartographic and analytical atlas by David Chandler tracing the development of military strategy from the Thirty Years' War to the mid-19th century. It covers major campaigns with maps illustrating maneuver, concentration, decisive battle, and the application of strategic principles.

Primary value: maps and analysis of the "art, theory and practice of war" during the period when linear tactics, the rise of Prussia, and the Napoleonic revolution transformed European warfare. Strong for the warfare hub's strategy/tactics pages and for the transition from early modern to modern war.

## Structure and Coverage (220 pages)

Chronological and thematic coverage of:

- The Thirty Years' War and the birth of modern strategy
- The wars of Louis XIV and Marlborough
- Frederick the Great and the rise of Prussia
- The American and French Revolutionary Wars
- The Napoleonic Wars (detailed campaigns)
- Post-Napoleonic developments to 1878

Emphasis on strategic concepts illustrated by maps.

## Historiography / Collection Note

By David Chandler, author of the standard "Campaigns of Napoleon". The maps are designed to teach strategy and are among the clearest for the period. They reflect Chandler's emphasis on the operational level and the "military mind."
