## Volume Synthesis Note

This **targeted ingest** did exactly what it set out to do: it converted the wiki's remaining Southeast
Asian **forward-links into real pages**, giving Critical Gap 3 its discrete classical polities at last.
From Keith Taylor's "Early Kingdoms" (Ch. 3) came six actor pages — [[srivijaya]], [[majapahit]],
[[champa]], [[pagan-kingdom]], [[ayutthaya]], and [[dai-viet]] — and from Kenneth Hall's economic chapter
(Ch. 4), with the religion chapter (Ch. 5) in support, came the unifying analytical page,
[[mandala|the mandala / galactic polity]]. The [[southeast-asia]] anchor and the
[[indianization-of-southeast-asia]] process were enriched to tie them together.

The volume's organising insight — which now structures the wiki's SE Asia coverage — is the **typology of
the early state**: **inland agrarian temple-states** ([[khmer-empire|Angkor]], [[pagan-kingdom|Pagan]])
that mobilised great rice plains and built monuments, versus **maritime entrepôt powers**
([[srivijaya|Srivijaya]], [[champa|Champa]]) that lived off the Straits trade — with the **Tai successor**
[[ayutthaya|Ayutthaya]] and Javanese [[majapahit|Majapahit]] fusing the two, and **sinicized**
[[dai-viet|Dai Viet]] as the China-facing exception. Beneath all of them runs the [[mandala|mandala]]
logic (Wolters/Wheatley/Geertz): power as **status and radiance**, not bounded territory and bureaucracy —
which dovetails with Reid's [[debt-bondage-in-southeast-asia|manpower-not-land]] and
[[theatre-state|theatre-state]] readings and with the [[devaraja|devaraja]] of Coe's Angkor. Taylor's
recurrent **historiographical** theme is also captured: how unevenly these polities are knowable —
Dai Viet richly documented through a China-facing chronicle tradition, Champa and Srivijaya almost pure
twentieth-century reconstruction — and how that unevenness is itself historically meaningful.

**Scope and what remains.** Per the user's direction, the prehistory chapter and **Part Two (c. 1500–
1800)** were deliberately skipped as redundant with the wiki's existing prehistory pages and with the two
[[reid-southeast-asia-age-of-commerce-v2-1993|Reid]] volumes (which already cover the commercial age,
Islamization, gunpowder absolutism, and the VOC crisis). Volumes 2–4 (modern Southeast Asia) were not
taken. A few connective pages remain as forward-links rather than full articles — notably the
**Sailendra** dynasty (Borobudur), the **Lan Na/Lan Sang/Sukhothai** Tai states, and **Funan/Oc Eo**
(which has its own page from Coe). With this pass, **Critical Gap 3 (South & Southeast Asia) is
substantially closed**: India is solid prehistory→1947, mainland and maritime Southeast Asia both have
their classical polities, the commercial age, and the religious and political frameworks.
