# The Penguin Atlas of Ancient History — Colin McEvedy (1967)

A compact reference atlas covering human history from the Paleolithic through the early medieval period, with a focus on the Old World (Europe, Near East, Mediterranean, and adjacent regions). McEvedy's maps are deliberately simplified and diagrammatic, using consistent symbols to illustrate states, peoples, cities, migrations, and trade.

This is a **reference work**, not a narrative history. It is particularly useful for visualizing the spread of agriculture, the rise of the first civilizations (Mesopotamia, Egypt, Indus, China), the Bronze Age world, the classical Mediterranean, and the barbarian migrations.

## Structure and Coverage

The atlas consists primarily of full-page maps with facing pages of explanatory text and timelines. Coverage spans:

- Prehistory and the origins of agriculture and urbanism
- The ancient Near East (Sumer, Akkad, Babylon, Assyria, Hittites, etc.)
- Egypt and the Nile valley
- The Aegean (Minoans, Mycenaeans, classical Greece)
- The classical world (Persian Empire, Hellenistic world, Rome)
- The fringes (Celts, Scythians, steppe nomads, early India/China contacts)
- Transition to late antiquity and early Middle Ages

No detailed "Section Plan" like a narrative volume; it is organized chronologically by map plates.

## Collection Coverage Note

This is a key **cartographic reference** for prehistory through classical antiquity (low-priority supplementary per sourcing roadmap). The collection has strong narrative holdings for these periods via the Cambridge World History, Cambridge Ancient History, and primary sources. This atlas adds visual/spatial clarity that text alone cannot provide.

It is especially valuable for:
- Updating place pages with historical extents and routes.
- Cross-checking period pages (Neolithic through Classical Antiquity) for geographic scope.
- Supporting process pages (e.g., agricultural origins, urbanization, Indo-European migrations, Silk Road precursors).
- Visualizing the "eurasia", "near-east", "mediterranean-world", and "steppes" regions.

Textual content is limited; the maps are the core resource (retained in the filed PDF).

## Historiography / Collection Note

McEvedy's atlases are valued for clarity and accessibility but are interpretive and somewhat dated in details. They remain extremely useful teaching/reference tools for the broad outlines of ancient history.

The Bibliotheca Alexandrina now includes this as a visual companion to its strong holdings for early periods.
