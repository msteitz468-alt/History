## The work and its author

Sima Qian (c. 145–86 BCE) succeeded his father **Sima Tan** as *Taishi* ("Grand Historian") at
the court of **Emperor Wu of Han**, and completed the *Shi ji* in fulfillment of his father's
dying wish. He devised "an entirely new historiographical form," dividing 130 chapters into
**Basic Annals** (*benji*, by dynasty/reign), **Chronological Tables**, **Treatises** (rites,
music, astronomy, economics, etc.), **Hereditary Houses** (the feudal states), and
**Biographies** (*liezhuan*, of famous persons and foreign lands) — a structure that shaped two
millennia of Chinese "Standard Histories." Each chapter often closes with a personal verdict —
"**The Grand Historian remarks**" — Sima Qian's signature moral-historical judgment.

## On the Qin

Sima Qian traces Qin from "a tiny feudal domain on the far northwestern border" gradually
**"eating away in silkworm fashion" (*canshi*)** at its rivals until the king of Qin united
China in 221 BCE as [[qin-shi-huang|First Emperor]]. His "dramatic portrait of the First
Emperor … skilfully juxtaposes" the monarch's grandiose self-celebrating inscriptions with "grim
accounts of the cruelty, folly, and oppression" of his rule. Because the First Emperor had
**burned the records** of the other states, Sima Qian had few sources and leaned on the
*Intrigues of the Warring States* — material "closer to historical fiction than true history,"
but "highly effective as literature."
