# E.J. Brill's First Encyclopaedia of Islam, 1913–1936 (Houtsma et al.)

The original **Encyclopaedia of Islam** (EI1), published by E.J. Brill (Leiden) and Luzac & Co. (London) in fascicules 1913–1938, with the photomechanical reprint of the full set issued 1987 (ISBN 90-04-08265-4). Edited by M. Th. Houtsma (primary), with T. W. Arnold, R. Basset, and R. Hartmann. Subtitled "A Dictionary of the Geography, Ethnography and Biography of the Muhammadan Peoples."

This is the authoritative reference of its era for the Islamic world. Articles are signed by leading scholars of the day (e.g. Goldziher, Barthold, Huart, Becker, Hell, Streck). Coverage is strongest for the central Islamic lands (Near East, Persia, early Turkic and Arab history) from the rise of Islam through the medieval and early modern periods.

## Source Summary and Status

- **Format**: Alphabetical dictionary. Articles range from a few lines (minor poets, small localities) to multi-column treatments (major cities like Baghdad, dynasties, key doctrines, caliphs and rulers).
- **Extracted text available**: Primarily Volume II range **Baba Fighani–Dwin** (B–D letters) plus front matter and errata/corrections pages. The root PDF (59 MB) is the full multi-volume reprint; the accompanying .txt (163k lines) and variants in `raw/_islam-encyclopaedia-ocr/` are the current usable extraction.
- **Companion index**: The 1996 Index of Subjects (to EI2) is already ingested as a pure finding-aid (`encyclopaedia-of-islam-index-subjects-1996`). EI1 itself now has its own source record.
- **Not a narrative monograph**: Treat per reference-work precedent (Oxford Classical Dictionary, Times Atlas, Bickerman). No attempt to create a page per lemma. Selective high-value claim extraction and enrichment of existing pages.

## Major Developments / Actors / Concepts Covered (sample high-value)

From B–D range (grounded in legible passages):
- **Babek (Babak al-Khurrami)**: Khurramite revolt leader against Abbasids; detailed military narrative of Afshin campaign, capture 223 AH.
- **Babi / Babis**: 19th-c. movement from the Bab; persecutions, schism into Azalis / Baha'is; explicit political turn.
- **Baghdad**: Extensive history article — Iranian etymology, al-Mansur foundation (Madinat al-Salam as omen/Paradise reference), Round City plan, gates, later sieges (Amin, Buyids, Seljuks, Mongols), topography.
- **Badakhshan, Badajoz (Batalyaws), Bagrirmi (Bagirmi), Baha’ al-Dawla, Badr (battle and personages), Badis (Zirid), etc.**: Regional rulers, trade nodes, frontier history.
- Doctrinal: **Bada’** (Shi‘i concept of divine "change of mind"); detailed theological discussion with Qur’anic and hadith proofs.
- Many minor poets, scholars, tribes (Badila, etc.), and places useful for prosopography and toponyms.

## Historiography / Collection Coverage Note

EI1 is the classic pre-WWII synthesis. Its strengths (philological precision, citation of primary Arabic/Persian chronicles and geographies) remain valuable. Weaknesses typical of the period: limited social history, reliance on literary sources, occasional Eurocentric framing, and pre-modern archaeology for many sites.
