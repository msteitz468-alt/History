# The Chronicle of Pseudo-Zachariah Rhetor

A twelve-book Syriac chronicle — "Church and War in Late Antiquity" — compiled at **Amida c. 569**, a
major **Miaphysite** witness to the eastern Roman world from the mid-fifth to the mid-sixth century. The
ninth source of this Phase-5 batch, and a companion to the other Syriac chronicles already ingested.

## The Work

The compilation takes its name from the historian whose work forms its core: **Zachariah of Mytilene**
(Zachariah Scholasticus/Rhetor), a Miaphysite intellectual (later a Chalcedonian bishop) whose **lost
Greek Ecclesiastical History** survives chiefly in the Syriac adaptation embedded here (books 3–6),
covering c. **450–491** — the aftermath of **[[council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedon]]**, the *Henotikon*
of Zeno, and the Miaphysite struggle. To this an **anonymous compiler** at Amida added further books,
including a notable **account of the reign of Anastasius** (with the **[[anastasian-war-502-506|Anastasian
War]]** and the fall of Amida) and Justinianic-era material, carrying the story to c. 550.

## Significance

Pseudo-Zachariah is one of the four pillars of the **Syriac Miaphysite chronicle tradition** now in the
wiki — alongside **[[watt-pseudo-joshua-stylite|Pseudo-Joshua the Stylite]]**, **[[john-of-ephesus|John of
Ephesus]]** and the **[[pseudo-dionysius-zuqnin-chronicle|Chronicle of Zuqnin]]** — that together give
the **anti-Chalcedonian East** its own voice against the Greek, Chalcedonian sources (Procopius,
[[whitby-evagrius-ecclesiastical-history|Evagrius]]). It preserves the otherwise-lost Zachariah, is a key
source for the **Roman–Persian frontier** and the church politics of the age, and includes vivid
documentary and legendary material found nowhere else.
