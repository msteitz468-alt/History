# The Encyclopaedia of Islam (New Edition) — Index of Subjects

## Source Summary

The *Index of Subjects / Index des Matières* to **The Encyclopaedia of Islam, New (2nd)
Edition** (EI2) — compiled by **P.J. Bearman** and published by **E.J. Brill** (Leiden, 1996,
"third edition" of the index). It indexes EI2 **Volumes I–VIII** (alphabetically A through the
letter S, the latest being Volume VIII, June 1995) together with the **Supplement, fascicules
1–6**. The volume is **bilingual**, with parallel English and French apparatus.

This is a **reference finding-aid, not a historical source.** It is being filed for the wiki
as a source page only — there are no claims to extract, and the workflow's subagent
claim-extraction does not apply. It follows the same source-page-only treatment used for the
other pure reference works in the collection ([[oxford-classical-dictionary-1970|Oxford
Classical Dictionary]], [[bickerman-chronology-of-the-ancient-world-1968|Bickerman's
Chronology]], and the [[times-atlas-of-world-history-1999|Times Atlas]] / McEvedy atlases).

## What the Index Contains (structure)

1. **List of Entries** — an alphabetical English (and separately French) list that maps a plain
   subject term to either (a) the single EI article that treats it, given by its Arabic lemma,
   or (b) an arrow (→) pointing to a heading in the Index of Subjects proper. This was added in
   this edition precisely because EI articles appear under Arabic/Persian/Turkish headwords
   (e.g., *reed-pen* → article **Ḳalam**; *Algebra* → **MATHEMATICS**), which can defeat a
   non-Arabist searcher.
2. **Index of Subjects proper** — thematic groupings that collect every EI article bearing on a
   subject under one capitalised heading, often with sub-headings (e.g., **ADMINISTRATION**;
   **LAW.OTTOMAN**; **MILITARY.MAMLUK**, **MILITARY.OTTOMAN**; **MYSTICISM**;
   **WRITING.MATERIALS**). Countries, dynasties, and caliphates are indexed *in extenso* here.
3. A short **Guide to the Use of the Index of Subjects**.

## How It Will Be Used in the Wiki

- As a **disambiguation and look-up tool** for the Islamic-world material the wiki already holds
  from narrative sources (Hodgson, Lapidus, the GHA series, Ottoman/Indian-Ocean volumes, etc.):
  it tells you the EI lemma under which a topic is treated and which clusters of articles bear on
  a theme.
- As **scaffolding for a future EI ingestion**: the subject headings (ADMINISTRATION, ALCHEMY,
  LAW, MILITARY, MYSTICISM, WRITING, etc.) preview the EI's own thematic taxonomy and can guide
  section planning when the encyclopaedia proper is read.

No wiki content pages were created or updated from this index beyond this source record and the
standard bookkeeping; doing otherwise would produce empty stubs, which the schema forbids.

## Reliability and OCR Note

The EI2 itself is among the most authoritative reference works on the Islamic world; this index
is its official Brill companion. **However**, the file in the collection is a 2023 OCR scan with
heavy noise — garbled diacritics, transliteration errors, and corrupted front-matter — so it is
reliable for *locating* articles but must not be quoted as text. An identical copy is filed under
`raw/_islam-encyclopaedias/`.

## Relation to the Encyclopaedia of Islam proper

This indexes a work the wiki does **not yet hold**. On `Outstanding Sources.md` the *Encyclopaedia
of Islam* (Brill) remains an outstanding acquisition; ingesting this index does **not** close that
gap — it provides the finding-aid that will make the eventual ingestion of EI2 far more navigable.
As of this writing the Brill *Encyclopaedia of Islam* itself is **still undergoing OCR** (raw scans
in `raw/_islam-encyclopaedia-ocr/`, including *Brill's First Encyclopaedia of Islam, 1913–1936*) and
is not yet in an ingestible state; this Index of Subjects is the only part presently usable.
