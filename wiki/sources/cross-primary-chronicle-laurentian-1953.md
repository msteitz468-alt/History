# The Russian Primary Chronicle — Laurentian Text (Cross & Sherbowitz-Wetzor trans.)

## Source Summary

This is the standard scholarly English translation of the *Povest' vremennykh let* ("Tale of Bygone Years" or Primary Chronicle), the foundational annalistic history of Kievan Rus'. The present edition is based on the Laurentian Codex (copied 1377 by the monk Lawrence for Prince Dmitriy Konstantinovich of Suzdal). It covers the conventional origins of Rus' from 852 (Byzantine Era year 6360, accession of Emperor Michael) through the early twelfth century (to ca. 1117/1118), with appendices including the Testament of Vladimir Monomakh.

The work is a compilation, not a single-author composition. It draws on earlier oral traditions, lost annals, Byzantine chronicles, and contemporary records. The Sylvester colophon (1116) in the Laurentian text indicates a major redaction at the Kiev Caves (Crypt) Monastery. Traditionally attributed to the monk Nestor (author of lives of Boris and Gleb and Theodosius), modern scholarship (Shakhmatov and others) views it as a composite with multiple layers and later interpolations. It is strongly pro-Rurikid and Kievan in perspective.

The translation is by Samuel Hazzard Cross (first English rendering, 1930, revised posthumously) and Olgerd P. Sherbowitz-Wetzor, published by the Mediaeval Academy of America (based on the Karsky diplomatic edition of 1926). It includes a substantial scholarly Introduction on manuscripts, authorship, and editions; notes; and appendices.

The core chronicle text is annalistic (short entries under years, with longer narratives for major events). It is not a continuous narrative but a compilation with chronological problems (e.g., some dates adjusted to fit Byzantine Era calculations).

## Historiography in the Source

The scholarly Introduction (by Cross, revised) provides essential context:
- **Manuscripts**: Laurentian (1377, North-East Rus' copy) and Hypatian (15th c., South-Russian original) are the two main redactions of the early text. The Laurentian is notable for the Sylvester colophon and inclusion of Monomakh materials.
- **Composition and Authorship**: Not a homogeneous work by Nestor alone. It incorporates earlier annals, legends, and foreign sources. Diversity between the Nestorian *Life of Boris and Gleb* and the chronicle account of their deaths suggests Nestor was not the main compiler of the *Povest'*. Sylvester (Prior of St. Michael's) explicitly redacted in 1116.
- **Value and Limitations**: "The most primitive text" for the period 852–early 12th c. Valuable for political, military, and religious history of Kievan Rus', but earlier sections (pre-11th c.) are less reliable (oral traditions, retrospective dating, bias toward the Rurikids and Kiev). Includes important details on trade routes ("way from the Varangians to the Greeks"), conversion, law, and princely relations. Must be cross-checked with archaeology, Byzantine/Arabic sources, and other chronicles.
- **Editions and Translations**: Based on the 1926 Karsky diplomatic text (Laurentian). Notes on earlier editions (P.S.R.L. 1846, etc.) and Western translations (Müller, Paris, Léger, etc.). This is the first full English version.

The chronicle itself is a product of 11th–12th c. Kievan monastic culture, reflecting Christianization and the consolidation of Rurikid power. It emphasizes divine favor, princely legitimacy, and the superiority of Byzantine (Orthodox) Christianity.

## Claims Extracted (Lines 9001–12000 only; Laurentian Text 1094–1116 + Monomakh appendices)

**Political fragmentation and Polovtsian wars (1094–1096):**
- Svyatopolk and Vladimir summoned Oleg to join foray against Polovcians; Oleg came but "did not follow the same route"; they seized Polovcian cattle, horses, camels, slaves. Enmity: "You did not accompany us upon our attack against the pagans who have brought ruin upon Rus’. You have a son of Itlar’ at your court. Either kill him or deliver him up to us." Oleg paid no attention (ll. 9011–9022).
- Polovcians besieged Yur'ev all summer; Svyatopolk pacified them; citizens fled to Kiev; Svyatopolk "ordered that a town should be built on the hill of Vitichev" and settled refugees with Bishop Marinus; "The Polovcians then burned the deserted town of Yur'ev" (ll. 9023–9030).
- David left Novgorod for Smolensk; Novgorodians brought Mstislav (son of Vladimir) as prince. Izyaslav (son of Vladimir) took Murom; locusts covered land Aug 28 (ll. 9031–9041).
- 6604 (1096): Svyatopolk, Vladimir summon Oleg to Kiev "before the bishops and the priors and in the presence of the vassals of their fathers and of the citizens" to defend against pagans. Oleg: "it was not proper for him to submit to the judgment of a bishop, or priors, or of the common people." They march on Chernigov; Oleg flees to Starodub, siege 33 days; peace granted, Oleg to kiss cross and report to Kiev with David (ll. 9042–9069).
- Bonyak appeared before Kiev Sunday evening, "burned the prince’s palace at Berestovo." Tugorkan (Svyatopolk's father-in-law) besieged Pereyaslavl'; Svyatopolk and Vladimir crossed Dnieper undetected "for God protected them"; battle July 19: "Upon this day the Lord performed a great deliverance. The aliens were thus conquered... Their prince Tugorkan was killed" (ll. 9070–9096). Svyatopolk buried him at Berestovo "respecting him as his father-in-law even though he was a foe" (ll. 9097–9100).
- Bonyak raided Kiev again May 20? "the godless, mangy thief and bandit"; attacked Crypt Monastery after matins: "they planted two standards before the monastery gates... slew the brethren... set fire to the shrine of the Holy Virgin... blasphemed against God and our faith" (ll. 9101–9120). Burned Vladimir's red palace at Vydobychi. Chronicle interprets as divine chastisement (ll. 9127–9138).
- Polovcians' origins per chronicle: "They came forth from the desert of Yathrib in the northeast. Four races of them issued forth: Torkmens, Pechenegs, Torks, and Polovcians." Linked to Methodius of Patara on eight nations fleeing Gideon; Ishmaelites, unclean peoples shut by Alexander of Macedon (ll. 9139–9153).
- Gyuryata Rogovich of Novgorod story (heard 4 yrs prior): servant among Yugra (with Samoyedes); Yugra reported mountains to sea where "great cries and the sound of voices; those within are cutting their way out"; small opening, trade knives/axes for furs; "These are the peoples shut up by Alexander of Macedon" per Methodius (ll. 9154–9189).

**Oleg's campaign vs Izyaslav and Mstislav (1096):**
- Oleg broke promise, went to Murom (Izyaslav ruling); "Return to your own father’s district in Rostov, for this is my father’s territory here." Battle Sept 6: "Izyaslav, the son of Vladimir and the grandson of Vsevolod, was thus slain." Oleg took Murom, Suzdal (only Crypt Monastery and St. Demetrius survived burning), Rostov; exacted tribute (ll. 9190–9223).

## Claims Extracted (Lines 3000–6000 only; early annals: origins to Vladimir's baptism 988)

**Tribal origins, customs, and Khazar tribute (c. pre-852):**
- Slavic tribes listed (Polyanians, Derevlians, Novgorod Slavs, Polotians, Dregovichians, Severians, Buzhians/Volhynians); other tribute-paying (Chud’, Merya, Ves’, Muroma, Cheremis’, Mordva, etc.) belong to Japheth race in north. "For the Slavic race in Rus’ includes only the Polyanians..." (ll. 3000–3020).
- Customs contrast: "The Polyanians retained the mild and peaceful customs of their ancestors... The Derevlians, on the other hand, existed in bestial fashion, and lived like cattle. They killed one another, ate every impure thing, and there was no marriage among them, but instead they seized upon maidens by capture." Radimichians, Vyatichians, Severians similar forest customs; no law of God (ll. 3074–3116).
- Khazars demand tribute from Polyanians (in hills/forests); they pay "one sword per hearth". Khazar elders: "Evil is this tribute, prince. We have won it with a one-edged weapon called a sabre, but the weapon of these men is sharp on both edges... These men shall impose tribute upon us..." Outcome: "the Russes rule over the Khazars even to this day" (ll. 3170–3200).

**Rurik and Varangian calling (6360–6370 / 852–862):**
- 6360 (852): "the land of Rus’ was first named" at accession of Emperor Michael (15th indiction); Russes attacked Tsar’grad per Greek Chronicle. Detailed world chronology from Adam to Michael (ll. 3201–3239).
- 6367 (859): "The Varangians from beyond the sea imposed tribute upon the Chuds, the Slavs, the Merians, the Ves’, and the Krivichians. But the Khazars imposed it upon the Polyanians, the Severians, and the Vyatichians, and collected a white squirrel-skin from each hearth" (ll. 3246–3249).
- 6368–6370 (860–862): Tributaries "drove them back beyond the sea... set out to govern themselves. There was no law among them, but tribe rose against tribe." They invite Varangians: “Our land is great and rich, but there is no order in it. Come to rule and reign over us.” Rurik locates in Novgorod, Sineus at Beloozero, Truvor in Izborsk. "On account of these Varangians, the district of Novgorod became known as the land of Rus’. The present inhabitants of Novgorod are descended from the Varangian race, but aforetime they were Slavs." After deaths, Rurik sole rule; assigns cities (Polotsk, Rostov, Beloozero). Askold and Dir (not of Rurik kin) settle in Kiev, establish dominion over Polyanians (ll. 3250–3287).

**Askold/Dir and early raid on Constantinople (6371–6374 / 863–866):**
- Askold and Dir attack Greeks in 14th year of Michael. Russes in 200 boats make "great massacre of the Christians"; Emperor returns, with Patriarch Photius prays at Blachernae, carries Virgin’s vestment to sea; storm wrecks Russes: "few escaped such destruction" (ll. 3288–3305).

**Oleg’s succession and capture of Kiev (6388–6390 / 880–882):**
- Rurik on deathbed bequeaths realm to Oleg (of kin), entrusts young Igor (ll. 3308–3311).
- Oleg marches with Varangians, Chuds, Slavs, Merians, Krivichians; takes Smolensk and Lyubech with garrisons. At Kiev hills, hides warriors, tricks Askold and Dir as "stranger on his way to Greece on an errand for Oleg and for Igor’, the prince’s son". "You are not princes nor even of princely stock, but I am of princely birth." Igor shown as Rurik’s son. "They killed Askold and Dir, and after carrying them to the hill, they buried them there..." "Oleg set himself up as prince in Kiev, and declared that it should be the mother of Russian cities. The Varangians, Slavs, and others who accompanied him, were called Russes." Builds towns, imposes tribute; Novgorod pays 300 grivny/year to Varangians (ll. 3312–3342).

**Oleg’s tribal conquests and 907 raid/treaty on Greeks (6391–6415 / 883–907):**
- Conquers Derevlians (black marten tribute), Severians (light tribute, ends Khazar payment), Radimichians (shilling tribute). Wars Ulichians/Tivercians (ll. 3343–3356).
- 6412–6415 (904–907): Oleg attacks Greeks with 2000 ships (Varangians, Slavs, Chuds, Krivichians, Merians, Polyanians, Severians, Derevlians, Radimichians, Croats, Dulebians, Tivercians). "He commanded his warriors to make wheels which they attached to the ships, and when the wind was favorable, they spread the sails and bore down upon the city from the open country." Greeks terrified ("This is not Oleg, but St. Demetrius"); pay tribute 12 grivny per man (40 per ship). Detailed treaty: grain, supplies 6 months, baths; Russes swear by Perun and Volos; Oleg demands brocade sails for Russes, silk for Slavs; "The Russes hung their shields upon the gates as a sign of victory" (ll. 3478–3561).
- Full 6420 (912) treaty text with envoys (Karl, Ingjald etc.): peace, murder penalties (death or kin share), assault (5 lbs silver), theft (3x or death if resist), shipwreck aid, prisoner ransom (20 bezants), inheritance rules, war service voluntary, slave rules, etc. Russes swear by weapons/Perun/Volos; Greeks by cross. Envoys honored with gifts/relics (ll. 3566–3706).

**Oleg’s death (6420 / 912):**
- Magicians foretell death from beloved horse; Oleg never mounts it. After return, horse dead; "Soothsayers tell untruths... This horse is dead, but I am still alive." Stamps skull: "So I was supposed to receive my death from this skull?" Serpent emerges, bites foot; Oleg sickens and dies. Buried on Shchekovitsa hill. "It is remarkable what may be accomplished through witchcraft and enchantment." (ll. 3723–3749). Long digression on Apollonius of Tyana and magic (ll. 3751–3814).

**Igor’s reign, raids, and 945 treaty (6421–6453 / 913–945):**
- Igor succeeds; Derevlians resist. Attacks Derevlians, larger tribute. Pechenegs first enter Rus’ (915), make peace (ll. 3815–3824).
- Igor’s 941 raid: 10,000 vessels? Ravages Bithynia to Heraclea/Paphlagonia/Nicomedia; Greeks use "Greek fire" from ships: "the Russes cast themselves into the sea-water... the Greeks had in their possession the lightning from heaven". Igor collects new army with Varangians (ll. 3845–3902).
- 944: large coalition (Varangians, Russes, Polyanians, Slavs, Krivichians, Tivercians, Pechenegs); Emperor offers tribute + gifts; Igor accepts at Danube after retinue advises ("Who knows who will be victorious... Death lies in wait for us all"); Pechenegs ravage Bulgaria; returns to Kiev (ll. 3909–3938).
- 6453 (945) detailed renewal treaty with Romanus, Constantine, Stephen: long envoys list (Ivar for Igor, Vefast for Svyatoslav, Isgaut for Olga, many merchants); certificates for ships/agents; monthly allowances (Kiev first); no silk >50 bezants without stamp; ransom scales (10/8/5 bezants); Kherson protections; "If a Christian kill a Russ, or a Russ a Christian, he who has committed the murder shall be held by the relatives..."; military aid mutual. Sworn at Perun statue (pagans) and St. Elias church (Christians) "which is above the creek, in the vicinity of the Pasjincha square and the quarter of the Khazars. This was, in fact, a parish church, since many of the Varangians were Christians." (ll. 3939–4144).

**Igor’s death and Olga’s revenge (6453 / 945):**
- Igor seeks more tribute from Derevlians; retinue urges "The servants of Sveinald are adorned with weapons and fine raiment, but we are naked." Returns alone with few; Derevlians (Mal): "If a wolf come among the sheep, he will take away the whole flock one by one, unless he be killed." Kill Igor and company near Iskorosten; "his tomb is near the city of Iskorosten in Dereva even to this day" (ll. 4154–4173).
- Olga (with young Svyatoslav, tutor Asmund, commander Sveinald) receives Derevlian envoys (20) proposing marriage to Mal. She agrees outwardly: "Your proposal is pleasing to me"; tricks them into being carried in boat ("We will not ride on horses nor go on foot; carry us in our boat"), drops into prepared deep trench outside city, buries alive. "Olga bent over and inquired whether they found the honor to their taste. They answered that it was worse than the death of Igor’." (ll. 4174–4221).
- Second group of "distinguished men": invites to bath, locks and burns bathhouse. Third: funeral feast at Igor’s tomb ("prepare great quantities of mead... that I may weep over his grave"); Derevlians drunk, she orders massacre ("cut down five thousand of them"); returns to Kiev, prepares army (ll. 4222–4261).

**Olga’s campaigns, reforms, and baptism (6454–6463 / 946–955):**
- 6454 (946): with Svyatoslav, besieges Iskorosten; Svyatoslav’s spear barely clears horse’s ears. Demands 3 pigeons + 3 sparrows per house; ties sulphur/cloth, releases at night: "the dove-cotes, the coops, the porches, and the haymows were set on fire. There was not a house that was not consumed". Takes city, kills some elders, enslaves others, imposes heavy tribute (2 parts Kiev, 1 to Olga in Vishgorod). Establishes laws/tribute; "Her trading posts and hunting-preserves are there still" (ll. 4262–4336).
- 6455 (947): to Novgorod; establishes trading-posts and tribute along Msta and Luga; "Her hunting-grounds, boundary posts, towns, and trading-posts still exist throughout the whole region, while her sleighs stand in Pskov to this day." (ll. 4337–4345).
- Olga to Tsar’grad (Constantine son of Leo); Emperor marvels at her wisdom/fairness, offers co-reign; she demands he baptize her first. Baptized with Patriarch (named Helena after Empress). Patriarch: "Blessed art thou among the women of Rus’... The sons of Rus’ shall bless thee to the last generation." Emperor later proposes marriage; Olga: “How can you marry me, after yourself baptizing me and calling me your daughter? For among Christians that is unlawful.” Dismissed with gifts, still called daughter. Patriarch blesses return. Olga prays for pagan son/people (ll. 4346–4410).
- Urges Svyatoslav baptize repeatedly; he refuses: “How shall I alone accept another faith? My followers will laugh at that.” Olga: “If you are converted, all your subjects will perforce follow your example.” She prays night/day; "notwithstanding, Olga loved her son" (ll. 4411–4453).

**Svyatoslav’s campaigns and death (6464–6480 / 956–972):**
- Svyatoslav collects army; "Stepping light as a leopard... Upon his expeditions he carried with him neither wagons nor kettles, and boiled no meat, but cut off small strips of horseflesh, game, or beef, and ate it after roasting it on the coals. Nor did he have a tent, but he spread out a horse-blanket under him, and set his saddle under his head" (ll. 4454–4461).
- 6473 (965): vs Khazars; defeats, takes Bela Vezha; conquers Yasians and Kasogians (ll. 4466–4472).
- 6474 (966): conquers Vyatichians, makes tributaries (ll. 4473–4475).
- 6475 (967): to Danube vs Bulgars; conquers, 80 towns; resides in Pereyaslavets: "that is the centre of my realm, where all riches are concentrated; gold, silks, wine, and various fruits from Greece, silver and horses from Hungary and Bohemia, and from Rus’ furs, wax, honey, and slaves" (ll. 4476–4484).
- 6476 (968): Pechenegs besiege Kiev first time. Olga shuts in with grandsons Yaropolk, Oleg, Vladimir. "Is there no one that can reach the opposite shore and report... if we are not relieved on the morrow, we must perforce surrender". Youth swims Dnieper (with bridle, speaking their language); warns. Pretich leads boats at dawn with trumpets; Pechenegs think prince returned, flee. Olga/grandsons escape to boats. Pretich meets Pecheneg prince, swaps gifts to feign vanguard of larger force. Svyatoslav returns, "regretted what they had suffered", drives Pechenegs out (ll. 4485–4560).
- 6477 (969): Olga dies; Svyatoslav weeps. Long eulogy: "Olga was the precursor of the Christian land, even as the day-spring precedes the sun... She was the first from Rus’ to enter the kingdom of God" (ll. 4561–4625).

**Succession struggles and Vladimir’s rise (6478–6488 / 970–980):**
- Svyatoslav sets Yaropolk in Kiev, Oleg in Dereva; Novgorodians request prince; Vladimir (son of Malusha, Dobrynya’s nephew) sent (ll. 4626–4636).
- 6479 (971): Svyatoslav at Pereyaslavets vs Bulgarians/Greeks; "Here is where we fall! Let us fight bravely, brothers and companions!" Victorious; Greeks deceive with numbers. Accepts arms (not gold) from envoys. Detailed treaty 6479 (971) with Johannes Tzimiskes: "I desire to preserve peace and perfect amity... I will therefore contemplate no attack upon your territory... nor will I incite any other foe... particularly the district of Kherson"; oath "of Perun and Volos, the god of flocks"; "may we be accursed of the god in whom we believe" (ll. 4637–4766).
- Returns; ambushed by Pechenegs (Kurya) at Dnieper cataracts (6480/972); "Svyatoslav was killed. The nomads took his head, and made a cup out of his skull, overlaying it with gold, and they drank from it." 28 years reign (ll. 4777–4800).
- Yaropolk reign. Feud: Oleg kills Lyut (Sveinald’s son) hunting; Yaropolk attacks Dereva; Oleg pushed into moat at Vruchiy bridge, crushed, dies. Yaropolk seizes property, weeps (ll. 4802–4837).
- Vladimir flees Novgorod; returns with Varangians. Kills Rogvolod + sons in Polotsk; marries Rogned (who had said "I will not draw off the boots of a slave’s son, but I want Yaropolk"). Advances on Yaropolk; Blud betrays ("Be my friend; if I kill my brother, I will regard you as my father"). Yaropolk flees to Rodnya (famine); Blud lures him to Vladimir’s hall; "two Varangians stabbed him in the breast with their swords". Vladimir takes brother’s Greek wife (nun); Svyatopolk born in adultery (ll. 4844–4931).
- Varangians demand 2 grivny/man tribute for Kiev; Vladimir delays, selects good ones for cities, sends rest to Tsar’grad with warning "Do not keep many of them... Scatter them" (ll. 4932–4945).
- Vladimir sets idols on hill: "one of Perun, made of wood with a head of silver and a mustache of gold, and others of Khors, Dazh'bog, Stribog, Simar’gl, and Mokosh’. The people sacrificed to them... the land of Rus’ and this hill were defiled with blood." Appoints Dobrynya to Novgorod (idol on Volkhov). "Vladimir was overcome by lust for women... He had three hundred concubines at Vishgorod, three hundred at Belgorod, and two hundred at Berestovo... He was insatiable in vice. He even seduced married women and violated young girls, for he was a libertine like Solomon" (ll. 4946–4999).

**Pagan resistance and "test of the faiths" leading to 988 baptism (6489–6496 / 981–988):**
- 6491 (983): Vladimir conquers Lyakhs (Peremyshl’, Cherven), Vyatichians (twice), Yatvingians; returns, sacrifices to idols. Lot falls on Varangian’s son (Christian immigrant from Greece, house by future Holy Virgin Church site). Father refuses: "These are not gods, but only idols of wood. Today it is, and tomorrow it will rot away... I will not give up my son to devils." People kill both; "no one knows where they are buried". "For at this time the Russes were ignorant pagans. The devil rejoiced thereat, for he did not know that his ruin was approaching" (ll. 5029–5071).
- 6494 (986): Bulgars (Mohammedan) urge circumcision/no pork/wine/70 wives; Vladimir: "Drinking is the joy of the Russes. We cannot exist without that pleasure." Germans (Pope): fasting; "Depart hence; our fathers accepted no such principle." Jewish Khazars: circumcision/Sabbath; Vladimir: "How can you hope to teach others while you yourselves are cast out...?" Greek scholar: long narrative from creation (Satan fall, Adam/Eve, Cain/Abel, flood, tower, Abraham, Moses plagues/Red Sea, judges/kings, prophets of rejection of Jews/calling Gentiles, incarnation prophecies, life, crucifixion, resurrection, judgment). Exhibits canvas of Judgment; Vladimir sighs "Happy are they upon the right... I shall wait yet a little longer" (ll. 5113–5727).
- 6495 (987): boyars/elders advise test faiths with envoys. Bulgars: "disgraceful... no happiness... dreadful stench." Germans: "we beheld no glory". Greeks (Hagia Sophia, Emperor Basil/Constantine, Patriarch): "we knew not whether we were in heaven or on earth. For on earth there is no such splendor or such beauty... Every man, after tasting something sweet, is afterward unwilling to accept that which is bitter". Boyars: "If the Greek faith were evil, it would not have been adopted by your grandmother Olga who was wiser than all other men." Vladimir: decision his (ll. 5728–5795).
- 6496 (988): Vladimir with force against Kherson (Greek city); barricaded, bowshot harbor. Earthwork; inhabitant Anastasius arrows "There are springs behind you to the east... Dig down and cut them off." Vladimir vows baptism if succeeds; cuts pipes, thirst forces surrender. Demands unwedded sister Anna or "deal with your own city as I have with Kherson." Emperors: "It is not meet for Christians to give in marriage to pagans. If you are baptized, you shall have her...". Vladimir agrees (studied faith); Anna reluctant ("It is as if I were setting out into captivity; better were it for me to die at home"); brothers persuade for Rus’ repentance/Greece safety. Vladimir eye disease; Anna: baptize or no cure. Baptized in Church of St. Basil (Kherson square); "as the Bishop laid his hand upon him, he straightway received his sight." "I have now perceived the one true God." Many followers baptized. Marries Anna. "Those who do not know the truth say he was baptized in Kiev, while others assert this event took place in Vasil’ev". Takes Anna, Anastasius, Kherson priests, relics of St. Clement and Phoebus, sacred vessels/images.
