# The Cambridge History of India, Vol. III: Turks and Afghans (1928)

The dynastic narrative of the **Delhi Sultanate** and the regional Muslim kingdoms, c. 1200–1526,
edited by Wolseley Haig — the classic (if now dated) English-language reference for the political
history of medieval Muslim India. It deepens the wiki's summary [[delhi-sultanate|Delhi Sultanate]]
coverage with chapter-length treatments of each dynasty and ruler.

## Section Plan

| Cycle | Chapters | Theme | Key page operations |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I–VII | Arab Sind; Ghaznavid & Ghurid conquests; the Slave/Mamluk kings; the **Khalji** dynasty & first Deccan conquest; the **Tughluqs** | enrich [[delhi-sultanate]]; **create [[alauddin-khalji]], [[muhammad-bin-tughluq]]** |
| 2 | VIII–XII | later Tughluqs, Timur's invasion (1398), the Sayyids and **Lodis** | enrich [[delhi-sultanate]] |
| 3 | XIII–XXI | the independent kingdoms: Kashmir, Jaunpur, Malwa, Gujarat, Bengal, the **Bahmani** sultanate, **Vijayanagara** | enrich [[vijayanagara-bahmani]] (incl. the Bahmani sultanate) |

## What it contributes

- **[[alauddin-khalji]]** — the Sultanate's most powerful ruler (price controls, Mongol defence,
  the Deccan conquests under Malik Kafur).
- **[[muhammad-bin-tughluq]]** — the brilliant, disastrous experimenter (the Daulatabad capital
  transfer; the token currency).
- Deepens the dynastic narrative on [[delhi-sultanate]] and [[vijayanagara-bahmani]] (the latter
  enriched with the Bahmani sultanate's own history — Mahmud Gawan, the Persian/Deccani factions,
  the split into the five Deccan sultanates).

## Reading note

India is a flagged weak-coverage region, so the Sultanate's dynastic detail is worth recovering;
but this 1928 volume is read **critically** — its political narrative is mined for the new ruler
pages while its dated interpretive frame is flagged, not adopted.

(Vol. III of the original Cambridge History of India.)
