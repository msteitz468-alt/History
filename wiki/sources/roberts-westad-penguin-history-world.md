# The New Penguin History of the World (Roberts & Westad)

The classic single-volume **narrative of world history** from human origins to the early twenty-first
century — J. M. Roberts's synthesis, revised by **Odd Arne Westad** to strengthen its modern and global
coverage. The first source ingested under **Outstanding Sources Gap 1 (Modern, 1750–present)**, where it
serves as a **synthetic spine** for the wiki's previously thin post-1650 leg.

## Approach

Roberts set out to show "the major historical **processes** which affected the largest numbers of human
beings," emphasizing the **comparative scale and relations** of developments rather than encyclopedic
country-by-country detail. A central theme is the tension between **historical inertia** (the deep weight
of the past) and humanity's growing **power as a change-maker** — and the acceleration of that power in
the last few centuries. Westad's revisions extend and rebalance the account toward a less Eurocentric,
more genuinely **global** modern history (decolonization, Asia's rise, the post-Cold-War world).

## Value to the Wiki

As a tertiary overview it is most useful as a **framing and orientation** source across the whole
temporal framework, and especially as a **narrative backbone for the modern periods** — the
[[long-19th-century|Long 19th Century]], the [[world-wars-era|World Wars Era]], the [[cold-war|Cold War]],
and the [[contemporary|Contemporary]] period — which the specialist Gap-1 sources (Hobsbawm, Westad's own
*Cold War*, Judt, the war-origins debates) then deepen.
