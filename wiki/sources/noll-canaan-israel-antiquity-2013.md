# Canaan and Israel in Antiquity (Noll, 2nd ed. 2013)

K. L. Noll's *Canaan and Israel in Antiquity* is a methodologically self-conscious
textbook covering the eastern Mediterranean littoral from the lithic eras through the
Persian period, with the [[kingdom-of-israel|kingdom of Israel]] and
[[kingdom-of-judah|kingdom of Judah]] as its focus. Every chapter foregrounds **method** —
what counts as history, how ancient writers constructed a past, how archaeology, epigraphy,
historical linguistics, and radiocarbon dating are weighed — rather than delivering a
seamless narrative. It is, in effect, a working historian's guide to *why* the standard
biblical story cannot be taken at face value, paired with the modest reconstruction that
the material record will actually support.

Noll occupies a **moderate-skeptic** position. He treats the Hebrew Bible as a late
anthology — composed no earlier than Iron Age II and largely in the Neo-Babylonian,
Persian, and Greco-Roman periods — whose authors had "forgotten that Egypt once ruled
their land" and had no access to sources for the Bronze Age. This places him closer to the
[[the-exodus|"minimalist" / revisionist]] reading of the text than to
[[sources/dever-beyond-the-texts-2017|Dever's centrism]], but his purpose is pedagogical
and his tone non-polemical; he repeatedly says he is "less interested in your agreement
with me than in presenting the methods by which historical judgments are made."

## Two signature frameworks

- **Israel as a subset of Canaan.** "Canaanite" was never an ethnic designation or a means
  of personal identity; in the ancient texts Canaan was a **geographic** term for, roughly,
  the eastern shore of the Mediterranean. The Merneptah stela lists Israel as one of the
  peoples *in Canaan*; Ezekiel 16.3 calls Jerusalem Canaanite by origin. "Almost all
  researchers today speak of a single, relatively homogenous 'Canaanite cultural sphere',
  of which Israel was a part." The biblical wall of difference between Canaanite and
  Israelite is a late religious construction, not an ancient ethnic reality. See
  [[canaan|Canaan]].
- **Divine patronage as the common religion of the ANE.** The patron–client political
  structure (a warlord-king as patron, his people as clients) was projected onto a
  three-tier divine hierarchy: a **patron god** chosen the human patron, who ruled the
  clients. This produced **henotheism** (one supreme god over many lesser gods), occasionally
  hardened into **monolatry** ("worship of only one"). Biblical "monotheism" is, on Noll's
  reading, henotheism/monolatry: angels, demons, and minor gods abound, and a non-believer
  in this culture said "there are no gods" (plural). See
  [[divine-patronage-ancient-near-east|Divine Patronage]] and
  [[yahwism-and-israelite-religion|Yahwism and Israelite Religion]].

## Volume Synthesis Note

Read alongside the just-ingested [[sources/dever-beyond-the-texts-2017|Dever, *Beyond the
Texts* (2017)]], Noll sharpens the wiki's map of the live debate over early Israel. Both men
are professional skeptics of the biblical narrative and both downsize the tenth-century
polity, but they sit at different points on the spectrum: Dever insists archaeology vindicates
a real (if modest) United Monarchy and savages the minimalists; Noll treats the biblical text
as a late artifact with no Bronze Age memory, leans toward the Low/Middle Chronology, and
declines to defend a "United Monarchy" as usually defined. The wiki now holds the centrist
(Dever) and moderate-skeptic (Noll) readings side by side on the
[[controversies/historicity-of-the-united-monarchy|United Monarchy]] and
[[controversies/emergence-of-early-israel|emergence]] controversy pages.

Noll's most distinctive contribution to the wiki, however, is **religious**: the
[[divine-patronage-ancient-near-east|divine-patronage]] framework gives a single analytical
model for ANE state religion as a whole, and the [[yahwism-and-israelite-religion|Yahwism]]
page records the El→Baal→Yahweh development and the henotheism→monolatry→monotheism trajectory
that the biblical-archaeology and kingdom pages had only gestured at. The
[[canaan|Canaan]] page, finally, supplies the place entry that the Dever ingest had to delink
for lack of a target — closing a structural gap in the Levant coverage.
