# The Penguin Atlas of Medieval History — Colin McEvedy (1961)

A compact, influential reference atlas focusing on the political and demographic history of Europe and the Mediterranean from the end of the Roman Empire to the late 15th century. McEvedy's maps are deliberately simplified and diagrammatic, using consistent symbols to show states, peoples, cities, trade routes, and ecclesiastical divisions.

This is a **reference work**, not a narrative history. It is particularly useful for visualizing the extreme political fragmentation of the early medieval period, the gradual consolidation in the High Middle Ages, the impact of the Crusades, the Mongol incursions, and the rise of the Ottoman Empire and the Italian city-states.

## Structure and Coverage

The atlas consists primarily of full-page maps with facing pages of explanatory text and timelines. Coverage is strongest for:

- The successor states to the Western Roman Empire (5th–8th centuries)
- The Carolingian and Ottonian empires
- The Byzantine Empire and its relations with the West and Islam
- The rise of the Islamic caliphates and their fragmentation
- The Crusader states and the Latin East
- The emergence of the major medieval kingdoms (England, France, Germany/Holy Roman Empire, Spain, Scandinavia, Eastern Europe)
- Ecclesiastical geography (dioceses, monastic orders)
- Trade routes and urban centers

No detailed "Section Plan" like a narrative volume; it is organized chronologically by map plates (roughly 100 pages of maps + text).

## Collection Coverage Note

This is a key **cartographic reference** for the medieval period (low-priority supplementary per sourcing roadmap). The collection already has strong narrative coverage for the Middle Ages via the New Cambridge Medieval History and related series. This atlas adds visual clarity to spatial relationships, boundaries, and the "shape" of medieval Europe that is hard to convey in text alone.

It is especially valuable for:
- Updating or enriching place pages with historical extents and routes.
- Cross-checking period pages (Early, High, and Late Middle Ages) for geographic scope.
- Supporting process pages (e.g., spread of Christianity, feudalization, crusades, urban revival).
- Visualizing the "mediterranean-world" and "western-europe" regions.

Textual content in the PDF is limited and the OCR is poor; the maps themselves are the core resource (retained in the filed PDF).

## Historiography / Collection Note

McEvedy's atlases (including this one and the companion Penguin Atlas of Ancient History) are beloved for their clarity and wit but criticized for over-simplification and occasional outdated interpretations (1961 publication date). They remain extremely useful as teaching/reference tools for the broad outlines of medieval political history.

The Bibliotheca Alexandrina now includes this as a visual companion to the stronger narrative holdings for the medieval Mediterranean and Europe.
