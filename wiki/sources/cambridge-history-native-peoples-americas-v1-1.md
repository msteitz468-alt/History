# The Cambridge History of the Native Peoples of the Americas, Volume I: North America, Part 1

One of three volumes (North America, Mesoamerica, South America) in the Cambridge
History of the Native Peoples of the Americas. **Volume I**, edited by Bruce G. Trigger
(McGill) and Wilcomb E. Washburn (Smithsonian), treats the indigenous peoples of
North America (including Greenland in the full volume plan) across two physical parts.
**Part 1** (this source) covers: Native and Euro-American historiographies; Paleoindian
through Archaic hunter-gatherers; indigenous agricultural development in the East and
Southwest; Mississippian agricultural chiefdoms; sixteenth-century contact; eastern
Native–settler relations 1600–1783; and eastern dispossession/Removal 1780–1880.
**Part 2** (separate raw file) covers the Plains, Southwest/California, Northwest Coast,
reservation period 1880–1960, Northern Interior, Arctic, and the Native American
Renaissance 1960–1995.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Part 1 is the **eastern-and-deep-time half** of Cambridge’s North America volume: eight specialist
chapters that move from how Native and Euro-American peoples have written history, through Paleoindian–
Archaic differentiation and dual agricultural trajectories (East seed-crop complex; Southwest Hohokam–
Mogollon–Ancestral Pueblo), to Mississippian chiefdoms, the long sixteenth century of intermittent
contact, the colonial Eastern Woodlands 1600–1783, and federal dispossession/Removal to 1880. The
co-editors’ preface rejects both unilinear evolutionism and extreme relativism: alternative histories
are valued, but interpretations remain testable against accumulating evidence. Nabokov makes
**indigenous historical consciousness** first-class content; Washburn & Trigger map the shifting
Euro-American image tradition (including the noble savage) that ethnohistory later dismantled.

For the wiki, this source **opens the North American colonial and Removal lattice** that Snow’s
archaeology survey and Mann’s *1491* could only sketch, while thickening the pre-contact spine already
started by Snow–Gonlin–Siegel. Cross-volume tensions preserved as attributed positions include
**Clovis-first vs pre-Clovis** (Snow’s 1996 detection inventory), **chiefdom vs state at Cahokia**
(Smith’s complex-chiefdom operationalization vs higher-order readings), **sixteenth-century epidemic
scale** (Trigger & Swagerty’s regional unevenness vs high-counter Dobyns), and **Removal policy intent**
(Green’s “not designed to exterminate” framing recorded alongside full atrocity documentation of
stockades, forced marches, and war).

It pairs with already-ingested [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v2-1|CHNPA II.1]] /
[[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v2-2|II.2]] and [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|III.1]] /
[[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-2|III.2]] as the third continental pillar of the series.
**Part 2** (Plains, Southwest/California colonial, Northwest, reservation era, Northern Interior, Arctic,
1960–1995) remains to be ingested and will reverse the Part 1 eastern focus toward the rest of the
continent and the modern era.
