# Atlas of Military History: The World of Warfare from Antiquity to the Present Day (Aaron Ralby, 2015)

This is a broad illustrated atlas covering military history and warfare from antiquity (including pre-Columbian Americas, ancient Near East, Greece, Rome) through the medieval period, early modern, Age of Expansion, Long 19th Century, the World Wars, Cold War, and into the contemporary era. It emphasizes maps of campaigns, battles, empires, and strategic situations, accompanied by captions and short contextual text.

Primary value for the wiki: cartographic and visual enrichment. The maps provide clear depictions of theaters, movements, battles, empires, and strategic context across a very wide chronological and geographic range. Text is primarily captions and overviews.

## Structure and Coverage (328 pages)

Broad chronological survey with maps for major conflicts and eras:

- Ancient civilizations and early empires (Olmec, Maya, Moche, Inca, Aztec; ancient Near East, Greece, Rome/Punic Wars)
- Medieval and crusading era campaigns
- Early modern and Age of Expansion (including American Revolutionary War, Wars of Independence)
- 19th century (American Civil War, etc.)
- World Wars (WWI and WWII detailed theaters)
- Later 20th/21st century (Korea, Vietnam, modern conflicts, 9/11 context)
- Supporting sections on weapons, tactics evolution, and global overviews.

The atlas is designed as a visual journey through the history of warfare, highlighting how battles and leaders shaped the political world.

## Historiography / Collection Note

A well-produced popular/reference atlas with attractive, readable modern maps. Not a primary academic monograph but effective for quick visual reference and teaching the broad sweep of military history. Maps are interpretive reconstructions based on historical sources; generally reliable for major movements, locations, and scale, though users should cross-reference specialized studies for disputed details. Excellent for enriching the wiki's visual layer across many periods.
