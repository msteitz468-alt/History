# Caroline Finkel, *Osman's Dream: The History of the Ottoman Empire* (2007)

The leading one-volume **narrative** of the Ottoman Empire across its whole span (c. 1300–1922,
with an epilogue to 1927). Its task here is to provide the **narrative spine** for the wiki's
Ottoman coverage — and above all to carry the story **beyond 1650**, where the structural
[[imber-ottoman-empire-1300-1650|Imber]] account stops, through the seventeenth-century
transformation, the eighteenth-century retreat, the nineteenth-century reforms, and the
empire's dissolution.

## Finkel's thesis

Finkel's **"new narrative"** is an explicit corrective to the "old narrative" of Western
polemic. She rejects the "over-simplified notion that the Ottoman Empire rose, declined, and
fell," and the recycled "soundbites" — "Oriental despotism," "the Sick Man of Europe," "what
went wrong?" — that froze the Ottomans into "a theatre of the absurd … salacious sultans, evil
pashas, hapless harem women." She insists on treating Ottoman history **by the same standards
as other histories**, stressing process and change over timeless exotic stereotype, and on the
**continuities between the empire and the Turkish Republic** (extending the account to
Atatürk's 1927 speech). She also deliberately weaves "institutions" (janissaries, harem,
religion) into the narrative rather than freezing them in separate chapters — the opposite of
[[imber-ottoman-empire-1300-1650|Imber's]] structural method, making the two volumes
complementary.
