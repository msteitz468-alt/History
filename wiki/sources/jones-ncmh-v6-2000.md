# The New Cambridge Medieval History, Vol. 6, c. 1300–c. 1415

Sixth volume of the NCMH, covering the **long fourteenth century** (c. 1300–c. 1415).
Successor in the backbone to [[abulafia-ncmh-v5-2000|NCMH Vol. 5 (c. 1198–c. 1300)]]; precedes
NCMH Vol. 7 (c. 1415–c. 1500). It replaces the old *Cambridge Medieval History* Vol. VII,
"Decline of the Empire and Papacy."

## Editorial Framing

Michael Jones's introduction frames the fourteenth century as **the "apocalyptic" century** — "this
shipwreck of a century which is going from bad to worse" (Filippo Villani): the **Black Death**,
famine, and incessant **war** brought "to an end three centuries of economic growth and cultural
expansion in Christian Europe." Yet it was not simply an age of decline: the volume stresses
**important developments in government**, **changes in religious and intellectual life** giving
"greater weight to the voice of the laity," and **new cultural and artistic patterns**, "not least
with the rise of vernacular literature." Against the old CMH's pope-and-emperor narrative, this
volume is **more comprehensive territorially** — giving real attention to eastern and northern
Europe, Byzantium, the Balkans and the rise of the Ottomans — and devotes a full third to
**analytical "general themes"** rather than political narrative. Jones repeatedly invokes Albert
Rigaudière's caution that "western Europe in the fourteenth century was as diverse as the states of
which it was composed."
