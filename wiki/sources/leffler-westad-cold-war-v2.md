# The Cambridge History of the Cold War, Vol. II: Crises and Détente (2010)

The second volume covers the Cold War's **most dangerous crisis and its partial relaxation
(c. 1962–1975)** — from the Cuban Missile Crisis to the high tide of détente, with the
conflict's centre of gravity shifting into the decolonizing **Third World** (Vietnam, the
Middle East, Cuba, Africa). Ingested to build out the wiki's [[cold-war|Cold War]] structure.

## What it contributes

- **[[cuban-missile-crisis-1962]]** — humanity's closest brush with nuclear war.
- **[[vietnam-war]]** — the Indochina wars (1945–75), the great hot war of the era.
- **[[sino-soviet-split]]** — the fracture of the Communist world into rival poles.
- **[[detente]]** — the relaxation of superpower tensions (Ostpolitik, SALT, Nixon–Brezhnev).
- Enriches [[cold-war]] and [[nuclear-arms-race]].
