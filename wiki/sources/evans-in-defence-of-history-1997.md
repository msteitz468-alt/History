# Evans, *In Defence of History* (Granta, 1997)

Richard J. Evans's defence of the possibility of historical knowledge against
postmodernist skepticism, framed as an update and replacement of the two books that
had dominated historical methodology teaching for three decades: E. H. Carr's *What
is History?* (1961) and Geoffrey Elton's *The Practice of History* (1967). Evans
steers between Carr's relativism and Elton's empiricist objectivism, arguing that
history is a legitimately knowledge-producing discipline whose conclusions are
"tenable though always less than final."

**Key claims carried into the wiki:**
- Ranke's *wie es eigentlich gewesen* is better read "how it essentially was"; the
  Anglo-American misreading licensed a naive empiricism Ranke did not hold.
- Facts precede interpretation conceptually; interpretation precedes evidence — the
  fact/evidence distinction resolves the Carr–Elton standoff (Stalybridge Wakes
  demonstration: contemporary papers record no gingerbread-seller killing).
- History is science only in the weak *Wissenschaft* sense; predictive/covering-law
  programs (Toynbee, Kennedy, cliometrics) failed on the evidence.
- Causal hierarchy survives the anti-causal and postmodernist turns; contingency is
  real but operates within structural corridors (Hitler-1928 counterfactual).
- The Abraham affair shows interpretations can be falsified by evidence — and shows
  professional sanction can be disproportionate (Stone 1951 parallel).
- Strong postmodernist relativism self-refutes (Boghossian) and fails the Holocaust-
  denial limit case (Lipstadt; White's retreat in Friedländer's volume); its methods
  (reflexivity, language, microhistory, narrative revival) are worth keeping.
- Objectivity ≠ neutrality (Haskell contra Novick): detachment and self-discipline,
  compatible with political commitment that does not distort evidence.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Evans's book functions for the wiki as the methodological keystone for its
historiography sections: it supplies the vocabulary (fact vs. evidence, necessary/
sufficient/absolute/relative causes, objectivity-as-detachment) that the CLAUDE.md
link taxonomy and historiography protocol already presuppose. Its argument runs from
the discipline's Rankean founding (ch. 1), through the failed science ideal (ch. 2),
the fact/evidence distinction (ch. 3), the defence of source criticism against the
linguistic turn (ch. 4), causal hierarchy (ch. 5), the society/individual balance
(ch. 6), knowledge/power and identity-epistemology (ch. 7), to the Haskell-inflected
defence of objectivity (ch. 8). Tension to watch: Evans is a participant in the
controversy he narrates — his readings of Jenkins, Ankersmit, and Purkiss were
disputed (2000 afterword exchange), and his portrait of Carr as "essentially a
Stalinist" is polemical. Where wiki pages built on this source characterize
postmodernist positions, a postmodernist-side source (e.g. Jenkins, *Re-thinking
History*; Munslow) would balance the record. Complements the already-ingested Evans
Third Reich trilogy: the 1928-counterfactual and Abraham material connect directly to
[[weimar-republic]] and [[causes-of-the-second-world-war]].
