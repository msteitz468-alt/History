# Three Political Voices from the Age of Justinian

Three Greek texts that voice the **political ideas and imperial ideology** of the reign of
**[[justinian|Justinian]]** — on the ideal ruler, the ideal constitution, and the sacred majesty of the
empire embodied in its greatest building. The seventh source of this Phase-5 batch. They illuminate
**[[byzantine-imperial-ideology|Byzantine imperial ideology]]**.

## The Three Texts

- **Agapetus, *Advice to the Emperor Justinian* (the *Ekthesis*)** — by a deacon of Hagia Sophia, the
  most famous **Byzantine "mirror of princes":** seventy-two short, acrostically arranged chapters
  counselling the emperor. Its themes define the ideal: the emperor is **God's image and vicegerent** on
  earth, supreme in dignity yet **mortal and accountable**, who must rule with **piety, justice,
  philanthropy (*philanthrōpia*) and self-mastery**, mindful that he is "in his body equal to every
  man." Wildly influential, it was translated and imitated across medieval Europe and Russia for a
  millennium.
- **The *Dialogue on Political Science*** — an anonymous, fragmentary philosophical dialogue on the
  **best constitution**, drawing deeply on **Plato** and **Cicero** (Scipio's dream). What survives
  argues for an idealized, lawful imperial polity — a notable, almost "constitutionalist" reflection on
  how supreme power should be limited and well-ordered.
- **Paul the Silentiary, *Description of Hagia Sophia*** — a virtuoso verse **ekphrasis**, recited at
  the **rededication of [[hagia-sophia|Hagia Sophia]] in 563** after the repair of its fallen dome: a
  dazzling description of the church's light, marbles and vaults that is at once art-historical evidence
  and a celebration of **Justinian's piety and imperial glory**.

## Significance

Together the three works open a window onto **how the sixth-century Roman East imagined power**: the
Christian emperor as God's deputy bound by virtue (Agapetus), the philosophical ideal of a well-tempered
state (the Dialogue), and the fusion of empire, Orthodoxy and monumental splendour (Paul). They are
foundational documents of **[[byzantine-imperial-ideology|Byzantine political theology]]** and of the
self-image of Justinian's renovated empire.
