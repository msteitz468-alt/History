# Einstein: His Life and Universe (Walter Isaacson, 2007)

**Source page for Walter Isaacson, *Einstein: His Life and Universe* (Simon & Schuster, 2007).**

Isaacson’s biography is a comprehensive, accessible life that treats Einstein’s scientific imagination, personal nonconformism, and political/moral evolution as intertwined. It draws on the opening of the Einstein archives and the early volumes of the Einstein Papers Project to show how private character (rebellious curiosity, visual thought experiments, aversion to authority, cosmic religious awe) produced the 1905 miracle papers, the long road to general relativity, resistance to quantum mechanics’ probabilistic turn, and the later public role as refugee, atomic-age conscience, and advocate for world federalism.

The book balances celebration of Einstein’s achievements with honest treatment of his human limitations (strained marriages, distant fathering, complicated relationships). It is particularly strong on the “how” of the great discoveries via thought experiments and the interplay between personal freedom and creativity.

## Approach and Method

Isaacson had privileged access to newly transcribed and translated correspondence. He supplemented this with extensive interviews and readings by leading historians of physics and practicing physicists who vetted the scientific explanations for accuracy and clarity. The structure is largely chronological with thematic chapters (e.g., “Einstein’s God,” “The Bomb,” “One-Worlder”). Emphasis is on character as the driver of both scientific style and political positions: the “reverential rebel” who sought invariants and harmony while rejecting dogma and nationalism.

## Range 01 Extraction (lines 1–3400): Front Matter through 1905 Papers, Personal Formation, Patent Office, Early GR Path, and 1919 Confirmation Announcement (ca. 1879–1919)

**Grounded claims extracted exclusively from this range of Isaacson (with verbatim quotes and context). All links use established or properly kebab-cased names only. Contributed to detailed biography and new pages below.**

- **Childhood and formation (Ulm/Munich/Aarau/Zurich)**: Born 1879 Ulm; compass moment ~age 4–5 (“Something deeply hidden had to be behind things”); slow speech, echolalia, visual thinking; rebellion vs. Munich gymnasium military tone and “Zwangs”; father’s business failures; Aarau (Pestalozzi visual methods, first light-beam rider thought experiment: “If a person could run after a light wave with the same speed as light, you would have a wave arrangement which could be completely independent of time”); Winteler family (Marie girlfriend; ties to Besso/Maja); renounced German citizenship 1896 (“no religious denomination”); Zurich Polytechnic (Weber antagonism: “You’re a very clever boy... you’ll never let yourself be told anything”; Pernet lab explosion; Grossmann notes; self-study Maxwell, Boltzmann, Föppl, Poincaré, Hume, Mach).
- **Mileva Mari relationship**: Intense letters mixing romance (“Dollie,” “Johnnie”) and physics (“We understand each other’s dark souls so well”; electrodynamics of moving bodies critique); Lieserl (1902, Novi Sad; fate uncertain post-1903 scarlet fever; never publicly acknowledged); marriage 1903 (Solovine/Habicht witnesses); Hans Albert 1904.
- **Bern patent office (1902–1909)**: Third-class examiner; Haller credo “remain critically vigilant”; clock-tower and synchronization patents; “I was able to do a full day’s work in only two or three hours”; “that worldly cloister where I hatched my most beautiful ideas.”
- **Olympia Academy**: Solovine, Habicht; Hume, Mach, Poincaré, Spinoza; “Our cheerful ‘Academy,’ which was less childish than those respectable ones which I later got to know at close quarters.”
- **1905 miracle year**: Habicht letter (late May): “I promise you four papers... The first deals with radiation and the energy properties of light and is very revolutionary... The fourth paper is... an electrodynamics of moving bodies which employs a modification of the theory of space and time.” Light quanta (March): “the energy is not continuously distributed... but consists of a finite number of energy quanta”; explained Lenard photoelectric via E = hν. Brownian (May): random molecular collisions produce observable walks; “Let us hope that a researcher will soon succeed...” Thesis (April): molecular sizes via viscosity. Special relativity (June 30): two postulates (relativity + constant c); simultaneity relative (train/embankment lightning; “Our judgments in which time plays a part are always judgments of simultaneous events”); ether “superfluous”; credited Besso; E=mc² addendum. “An analysis of the concept of time was my solution.”
- **Early path to GR (“happiest thought”)**: 1907 while writing yearbook article; falling man/elevator: “If a person falls freely, he will not feel his own weight.” Equivalence of gravity and acceleration; “the happiest thought in my life.” Sketch of gravitational redshift and light bending; full equations required later Grossmann collaboration.
- **1919 confirmation (range reaches announcement)**: Royal Society Nov 1919 (Eddington/Dyson); “The results... leave little doubt that a deflection of light takes place... of the amount demanded by Einstein’s generalized theory.” J. J. Thomson: “one of the greatest achievements of human thought.” Einstein (Berlin): celebrated by buying violin; “Newton, forgive me.”

## Range 02 Extraction (lines 3401–6000): GR Triumph, Fame, Nobel, Personal Turmoil, Quantum Resistance, Unified Fields, Exile Prelude (ca. 1919–1933)

**Grounded claims extracted exclusively from this range of Isaacson (with verbatim quotes and context). All links use established or properly kebab-cased names only. Contributed to detailed biography and new pages below.**

- **1919 eclipse confirmation and instant fame**: Eddington’s expedition confirms bending of light by sun’s gravity, announced Nov 1919. Times of London: “REVOLUTION IN SCIENCE / NEWTONIAN IDEAS OVERTHROWN”. “Scientists belonging to two warring nations had collaborated again!” (Infeld). Einstein’s theory “require[s] a new philosophy of the universe, a philosophy that will sweep away nearly all that has hitherto been accepted.” Public frenzy: “Now every coachman and waiter argues about whether or not relativity theory is correct.” Einstein to Zangger: “The newspaper drivel about me is pathetic” but “This kind of exaggeration meets a certain need among the public.” [[events/1919-eclipse-confirmation-eddington]]

- **Publicity paradox and character**: Einstein bemoans being “hounded by the press”; dreams “I’m burning in Hell and the postman is the Devil eternally roaring at me”. Yet “There was a streak in him that enjoyed the photographers and the crowds” (C. P. Snow). “I am truly a ‘lone traveler’ and have never belonged to my country, my home, my friends, or even my immediate family, with my whole heart.” (Einstein). Infeld: “I do not know anyone as lonely and detached as Einstein. His heart never bleeds...” “My passionate sense of social justice... has always contrasted oddly with my pronounced lack of need for direct contact...” (Einstein). “I had no gift for empathy” (Levenson on Einstein).

- **Personal life (divorce aftermath, family, mother’s death)**: Divorce from Mileva finalized 1919; prize money sent to her per agreement. Hans Albert (15) announces engineering career: “I think it’s a disgusting idea,” Einstein says. Reconciliation follows. Mother Pauline moves in late 1919; dies Feb 1920 of stomach cancer. “One feels right into one’s bones what ties of blood mean.” “Einstein wept like other men.”

- **Zionism and anti-Semitism response**: Post-1919, embraces Zionism as ethnic kinship after anti-Semitism rises. “I am, as a human being, an opponent of nationalism. But as a Jew, I am from today a supporter of the Zionist effort.” Recruited by Kurt Blumenfeld. Attacks assimilationists: “The psychological root of anti-Semitism lies in the fact that the Jews are a group of people unto themselves.” 1921 US tour with Chaim Weizmann to raise funds for Hebrew University. Massive crowds, parades, press; “Subtle is the Lord, but malicious he is not” (at Princeton, re Michelson-Morley).

- **Anti-relativity campaign and Lenard**: 1920 Berlin Philharmonic rally led by Paul Weyland and Ernst Gehrcke (“Jewish nature” of theory). Einstein attends, laughs, then publishes front-page Berliner Tageblatt attack: Lenard “has not yet produced anything outstanding in theoretical physics”. Bad Nauheim debate Sept 1920 with Lenard; Planck chairs. Rathenau assassination June 1922 devastates Einstein; threats; “I regretted the fact that he became a government minister.”

- **Nobel Prize 1921 (awarded 1922)**: Delayed by committee experimental bias and Lenard opposition. Awarded “especially for his discovery of the law of the photoelectric effect” “without taking into account the value that will be accorded your relativity and gravitation theories”. Irony: based on Lenard’s observations. Prize money (~$32k) largely to Mari; used to buy Zurich houses. Einstein speaks on relativity, not photoelectric, at ceremony.

- **Ether and Mach’s principle revision**: 1920 Leiden lecture: reintroduces “ether” (new version) as spacetime has physical qualities. “Space without ether is unthinkable”. Abandons strict Mach: “In contradiction to what Mach would have predicted, even in an otherwise empty universe, you will feel pressed against the inner wall of the spinning bucket” (per Greene summary of lecture).

- **Quantum contributions and resistance (“God does not play dice”)**: 1916–17 radiation theory: stimulated emission (basis for lasers); “It can be demonstrated convincingly that the elementary processes of emission and absorption are directed processes.” “It is a weakness of the theory that it leaves the time and direction... to ‘chance’.” “I find the idea quite intolerable that an electron... should choose of its own free will not only its moment to jump off but also its direction.” “I would rather be a cobbler... than a physicist.” 1920 first meeting with Bohr; debates begin. “We took the streetcar and talked so animatedly that we went much too far.” First Solvay 1927 and 1930: Einstein’s thought experiments (slit/electron; photon box + clock); Bohr refutes using GR gravity effect on clocks. “Einstein, stop telling God what to do!” (Bohr). “God does not play dice” (Einstein). “One can’t make a theory out of a lot of ‘maybes.’ Deep down it is wrong, even if it is empirically and logically right.”

- **Unified field quest**: Multiple attempts (Weyl, Kaluza 5th dim, Eddington affine, “distant parallelism”). 1929 five-page paper frenzy: “It has been my greatest ambition to resolve the duality of natural laws into unity.” Headlines “Einstein Reduces All Physics to One Law”; crowds at window displays. Pauli: “within a year... you will have abandoned that whole distant parallelism”. Einstein to Pauli 1932: “So you were right after all, you rascal.” Continues quest through 1930s.

- **Turning 50, Caputh, companions**: 1929 birthday hideaway; city gift house saga (right-wing opposition); builds own in Caputh. Sailing “Tümmler”. Relationships: Betty Neumann (serious 1923), Toni Mendel, Ethel Michanowski, Margarete Lebach. “Of all the women, I am actually attached only to Frau L...” Elsa’s tolerance: “God has given him so much nobility, and I find him wonderful, although life with him is exhausting”. Helen Dukas hired 1928 as secretary/protector (“Cerberus”).

- **Sons and family strains**: Hans Albert marries Frieda Knecht 1927 (9 yrs older); Einstein opposes (“deterioration of the race”), later accepts. Eduard mental decline: university, Freud interest, suicide attempt 1930; “Tete really has a lot of myself in him... things won’t be easy for him.”

- **Pacifism and politics evolution**: Militant pacifist 1920s–early 1930s: “I am not only a pacifist. I am a militant pacifist.” Denounces Geneva disarmament as “travesty”. 1932 2% speech. Rathenau assassination and Nazi rise prompt revision: 1933 to Belgian king: “if I were a Belgian, I would not refuse military service”. “Recent events have taught me a lesson.” Advocates supranational authority with enforcement power.

- **Einstein’s God / cosmic religion**: “Veneration for this force beyond anything that we can comprehend is my religion.” “I am a determinist. I do not believe in free will.” “I believe in Spinoza’s God, who reveals himself in the lawful harmony of all that exists, but not in a God who concerns himself with the fate and the doings of mankind.” “Science without religion is lame, religion without science is blind.” “He does not play dice.” “The most beautiful emotion we can experience is the mysterious... In this sense, and in this sense only, I am a devoutly religious man.” “I am compelled to act as if free will existed... I know that philosophically a murderer is not responsible for his crime, but I prefer not to take tea with him.”

- **1932–33 decision to leave / refugee**: Flexner recruits for IAS Princeton $10k + Mayer post. “Today I resolved to give up my Berlin position and shall be a bird of passage for the rest of my life.” Last departure Dec 1932; “Take a very good look at it. You will never see it again.” Hitler Jan 30 1933; renounces German citizenship at Brussels consulate; resigns Prussian Academy. “Dependence on the Prussian government... is something that, under the present circumstances, I feel to be intolerable.” Le Coq sur Mer (Belgium); threats; revises pacifism. Arrives US Oct 1933; settles Princeton (IAS).

- **1939 Einstein–Szilard letter to FDR and Manhattan Project awareness**: Szilard approached Einstein at his Peconic, Long Island cottage. “Some popular accounts suggest that Einstein merely signed a letter that Szilárd wrote and brought with him. Along these lines, Teller told the writer Ronald W. Clark in 1969 that Einstein had signed, with ‘very little comment,’ a letter that Szilárd and Teller had brought that day. See Clark, 673. This is contradicted, however, by Szilárd’s own detailed description of that day and the notes of the conversation made by Teller that day. The notes and new draft letter in German as dictated by Einstein are in the Teller archives and reprinted in Nathan and Norden, 293. It is true that the letter dictated by Einstein was based on a draft Szilárd brought that day, but that was a translation of the one Einstein had dictated two weeks earlier. Some accounts, including occasional comments made later by Einstein himself, try to minimize his role and say he simply signed a letter that someone else wrote. In fact, even though Szilárd prompted and propelled the discussions, Einstein was fully involved in writing the letter that he alone signed.” Einstein to Franklin Roosevelt, Aug. 2, 1939 (longer version in FDR archives, Hyde Park, with copy in AEA 33-143; shorter in Szilárd archives, UCSD). FDR to Einstein, Oct. 19, 1939, AEA 33-192. Einstein follow-ups: to Alexander Sachs, Mar. 7, 1940, AEA 39-475; to Lyman Briggs, Apr. 25, 1940, AEA 39-484. FBI interview with Einstein regarding Szilard, Nov. 1, 1940 (FOIA, ironic given Einstein’s own denied clearance). Later: Einstein to Franklin Roosevelt, Mar. 25, 1945, AEA 33-109. - **Postwar one-world advocacy and nuclear restraint**: United Press interview, Sept. 14, 1945 (reprinted NYT Sept. 15, 1945). Einstein to J. Robert Oppenheimer, Sept. 29, 1945, AEA 57-294. On world government powers: “There would be no immediate need for member nations to subordinate their own tariff and immigration legislation to the authority of world government. ... In fact, I believe the sole function of world government should be to have a monopoly over military power.” (Einstein to John Balderston and other Oak Ridge scientists, Dec. 3, 1945, AEA 56-493). “Einstein realized there was no lasting ‘secret’ of the bomb to protect. As he said later, ‘America has temporary superiority in armament, but it is certain that we have no lasting secret. What nature tells one group of men, she will tell in time to any other group.’” (Einstein, “The Real Problem Is in the Hearts of Men,” New York Times Magazine, June 23, 1946). ECAS fund-raising and letters (e.g., Einstein, ECAS letter, Jan. 22, 1947, AEA 40-606). “Why Socialism?,” Monthly Review, May 1949 (reprinted Einstein 1954, 151). Continued public stance despite Soviet criticism and U.S. suspicions (e.g., “Einstein Hits Soviet Scientists for Opposing World Government,” NYT, Jan. 30, 1948).

- **Red Scare resistance**: Einstein to William Frauenglass, May 16, 1953, AEA 41-112 (published NYT June 12, 1953): “As long as a person has not violated the ‘social contract’ nobody has the right to inquire about his or her convictions. If this principal is not followed free intellectual development is not possible.” (Also in letter to student editor Dick Kluger, Sept. 17, 1953). “The current investigations are an incomparably greater danger to our society than those few communists in the country could ever be.” (Einstein to Felix Arnold, Mar. 19, 1954, AEA 59-118; see also to Norman Thomas and others). Supported Albert Shadowitz (letters Dec. 1953, AEA 41-659/660; Shadowitz cleared 1955). Criticized broader McCarthy-era probes; FBI maintained extensive files (Jerome analysis; Newark field office reports 1953).

- **Final years, death 1955, and Russell-Einstein Manifesto**: Continued work on unified field theory and violin (Mozart). Einstein to Maurice Solovine, Feb. 27, 1955, AEA 21-306. Bertrand Russell to Einstein, Feb. 11, 1955, AEA 33-199; Einstein to Russell, Feb. 16, 1955, AEA 33-200. Einstein to Niels Bohr, Mar. 2, 1955, AEA 33-204. Signed Bertrand Russell, “Manifesto by Scientists for Abolition of War,” sent Apr. 5, 1955, AEA 33-209 (issued publicly July 9, 1955). Other late letters: to Farmingdale Elementary School, Mar. 26, 1955; to Vero and Bice Besso, Mar. 21, 1955, AEA 7-245; final calculations AEA 3-12. Helen Dukas, “Einstein’s Last Days,” AEA 39-71. “Einstein, at 75, Is Still a Rebel,” Gertrude Samuels, New York Times Magazine, Mar. 14, 1954. Died 1955 in Princeton.

- **Epilogue on brain, mind, legacy, and character summary**: Brain removed at autopsy by pathologist Thomas Harvey; kept privately for decades; studies included Marian Diamond, “On the Brain of a Scientist,” Experimental Neurology 88 (1985); Sandra Witelson et al., “The Exceptional Brain of Albert Einstein,” Lancet, June 19, 1999 (noted parietal differences); public accounts in Abraham, Possessing Genius and Paterniti, Driving Mr. Albert. Ophthalmologist Henry Abrams took the eyeballs. Will (witnessed by Kurt Gödel): Helen Dukas and Otto Nathan literary executors; guarded papers zealously. Character: Einstein to Carl Seelig, Mar. 11, 1952, AEA 39-013 (see also Bucky, 29): “I am not more gifted than anybody else. I am just more curious than the average person, and I will not give up on a problem until I have found the proper solution.” “Einstein the Revolutionist” headlines upon death (NYT Apr. 19, 1955; Time May 2, 1955). From volume’s God-chapter notes (cross-referenced in epilogue context): “I believe in Spinoza’s God, who reveals himself in the lawful harmony of all that exists, but not in a God who concerns himself with the fate and the doings of mankind.” (Einstein to Herbert S. Goldstein, Apr. 25, 1929, AEA 33-272). “The most beautiful emotion we can experience is the mysterious... In this sense, and in this sense only, I am a devoutly religious man.” (Einstein, “What I Believe” / “The World As I See It”). “Science without religion is lame, religion without science is blind.” Humility/nonconformity/rebel profile emphasized throughout late notes and epilogue.

### Settlement and Princeton Life (references to 1933 arrival and ongoing)
- After settling in Princeton in 1933, Einstein "never once in the remaining twenty-two years of his life left the United States, except for the brief cruise to Bermuda that was necessary to launch his immigration process." He became "a solid American citizen" despite contrarian views. [[places/institute-for-advanced-study]] `part_of:` his final phase. "After settling in Princeton in 1933..."
- Routine: woke civilized hour, read papers, walked "slowly up Mercer Street to the Institute" ~10am. Stories: car hit tree after driver recognized "the beautiful old man walking along the street, the black woolen knit cap firmly planted on his long white hair." (Pais). `enabled:` continued work on unified field theory post-retirement (official at 66/end of war).
- Oppenheimer relationship: cordial but not close. Oppenheimer (1935 visit): Institute a “madhouse” with “solipsistic luminaries shining in separate and hapless desolation”; “Einstein is completely cuckoo.” Later: Einstein “a landmark but not a beacon”; “There was always in him a powerful purity at once childlike and profoundly stubborn.” (Oppenheimer). Walks with Gödel: discussed relativity, incompleteness, time. Gödel's rotating universe allowing time travel; Einstein called it “an important contribution” but noted he had thought of the issue; “It will be interesting to weigh whether these are not to be excluded on physical grounds.”
- Unified field in final decade: pursued non-symmetric Riemannian metric; “I am sending them to nobody else, because you are the only person known to me who is not wearing blinders...” (to Schrödinger). “We have squandered a lot of time on this, and the result looks like a gift from the devil’s grandmother.” (to Schrödinger). Fantova journal (1954): “Thinks he found a new angle...”; “Found an error in his work today, which is a setback.”; “He calculated like crazy today but accomplished nothing.” Final words before death: equations on scraps; “If only I had more mathematics.” (to Hans Albert). `produced:` no tangible results; “No pictures came to our aid.” (Hoffmann).

### Relationships in Exile
- Helen Dukas: "zealous guardian"; after Elsa’s death "even more of a loyal guardian"; shielded from letters; accompanied to Florida; set up bed next to Einstein's during final illness to apply ice. Suspicions by Hans Albert of affair dismissed by others as implausible.
- Family: Maja (sister) stroke-confined; Einstein read to her (Ptolemy, Don Quixote); "I miss her more than can be imagined" on her death June 1951; sat "pale and tense, staring into space." Margot: "When Margot speaks, you see flowers growing." Eduard: institutionalized, "There is something blocking me that I am unable to analyze fully" (Einstein on visiting). Mileva death May 1948 after stroke; money under mattress; Eduard "lapsed into a daze and never spoke of his mother again."
- Margarita Konenkova: affair during war (introduced by Margot); 1941 Long Island weekend (boiled chicken lunch, sailed, equations on porch, near-arrest on beach); "I recently washed my hair myself, but not with great success." (letter); "I watch these exaggerated patriotic exhibits with concern." (re May Day). Returned to Moscow 1945. No secrets passed; letters public 1998. FBI clueless.
- Colleagues: close walking partner Kurt Gödel; cordial with Oppenheimer; assistants (Straus, Hoffmann, Kaufman, Fantova journaled conversations).

### World Government, Arms Control, Russia Views (approach of/after war)
- Hard-nosed, not naïve: “If the idea of world government is not realistic, then there is only one realistic view of our future: wholesale destruction of man by man.” (1948). “I do not know how the Third World War will be fought, but I can tell you what they will use in the Fourth—rocks.”
- On Russia: disliked authoritarianism but saw greater danger in US "hysterical fear" of Russian aggression. “All of us should ask ourselves which of the two countries is objectively more justified in fearing the aggressive intentions of the other.” Mild excuses for coercion balanced with criticism. Rebutted Russian scientists 1947: “Any government is evil if it carries within it the tendency to deteriorate into tyranny.” Advocated proceeding without Russia: “I believe that if this were done intelligently (rather than in clumsy Truman style!) Russia would cooperate once she realized that she was no longer able to prevent world government anyhow.”
- Shift from pacifism: refused to support unilateral scientist refusals or rejoin War Resisters League; “Disarmament cannot be effective unless all countries participate.” “The war resistance movement actually serves to weaken the nations with a more liberal type of government...” “I can easily imagine that two socialist states might fight a war against each other.”
- H-bomb: declined to join appeal to delay; “As long as competitive armament prevails, it will not be possible to halt the process in one country.” Taped TV with Eleanor Roosevelt: “Each step appears as the inevitable consequence of the one that went before... And at the end, looming ever clearer, lies general annihilation.”

### Red Scare and Civil Liberties Defense
- Rosenberg: private letter to Judge Kaufman for commutation (not innocence); released publicly; angry letters (“You place the Jew first...”). Also appealed to Truman/Eisenhower. Joint declaration with Urey on Stalin's Jewish doctors trial.
- Frauenglass letter (1953): “The reactionary politicians have managed to instill suspicions of all intellectual efforts... Frankly, I can only see the revolutionary way of non-cooperation in the sense of Gandhi’s. Every intellectual who is called before one of the committees ought to refuse to testify.” “Standing up for the First Amendment was particularly a duty of intellectuals...” Published; editorials condemned (“genius in science is no guarantee of sagacity in political affairs”; “This country protected him from Hitler”); more support letters this time. Russell defended: “I am compelled to suppose that you condemn George Washington...”
- Shadowitz: visited unannounced; Einstein: “If you take this path then feel free to use my name...” Shadowitz cited Einstein to McCarthy/Cohn.
- Oppenheimer: “Einstein thinks that the attack on me is so outrageous that I should just resign.” “The trouble with Oppenheimer is that he loves a woman who doesn’t love him—the United States government.” Signed faculty petition; wrote Lehman: Oppenheimer “by far the most capable Director... Dismissing him... would arouse the justified indignation of all men of learning.”
- General: “If I were a young man again and had to decide how to make a living, I would not try to become a scientist or scholar or teacher. I would rather choose to be a plumber or a peddler...” “America is incomparably less endangered by its own Communists than by the hysterical hunt for the few Communists that are here.” FBI file: 1,427 pages, 14 boxes, "nothing incriminating"; bumbling agents (wrong on wives, sons, Fuchs). “We have come a long way toward the establishment of a Fascist regime.” But “American democracy righted itself.”

### Final Years, Death, Mind
- Health/decline: 1948 aneurysm diagnosis (abdominal aorta); Florida recuperation. Played Beethoven Missa Solemnis repeatedly. “Brief is this existence, as a fleeting visit in a strange house.” “The strange thing about growing old is that the intimate identification with the here and now is slowly lost. One feels transposed into infinity, more or less alone.”
- Israel offer (1952): Jerusalem paper campaign; Abba Eban telegram; phone call to Eban: “I am not the person for that and I cannot possibly do it.” Formal letter: “All my life I have dealt with objective matters, hence I lack both the natural aptitude and the experience to deal properly with people and to exercise official function.” “I am the more distressed... because my relationship with the Jewish people became my strongest human tie...” Ben-Gurion: “Tell me what to do if he says yes!... if he accepts, we are in for trouble.” Einstein not wearing socks at later reception.
- Death April 18 1955 (age 76): Aneurysm burst; refused surgery “It is tasteless to prolong life artificially. I have done my share, it is time to go. I will do it elegantly.” “You’re really hysterical—I have to pass on sometime, and it doesn’t really matter when.” (to Dukas). Last afternoon: calculations, talked science with Hans Albert (“If only I had more mathematics”), politics with Nathan (Germany rearm). Undelivered Israel speech draft: “I speak to you today not as an American citizen and not as a Jew, but as a human being.” 12 pages equations at bedside.
- Cremated Trenton; ashes Delaware River (12 attendees incl. Hans Albert, Dukas, Nathan, Buckys). Brain removed by Thomas Harvey without permission; kept, chopped, sent to researchers in jars; studies on glial cells, parietal lobe groove shorter/wider (15% ); small samples, age mismatch issues. “My dad’s got his brain” (5th grader).
- Mind explanation (epilogue): “I have no special talents, I am only passionately curious.” Compass as child; “What would it be like to race alongside a light beam?” Visual thinking > verbal; “Behind a formula he immediately saw the physical content...” Aesthetic: simplicity. “In building a theory, his approach had something in common with that of an artist... beauty for him was, after all, essentially simplicity.” (Rosen). Nonconformity: “The theme that I recognize in Galileo’s work is the passionate fight against any kind of dogma based on authority.” Humility + cosmic religion: “A spirit is manifest in the laws of the universe—a spirit vastly superior to that of man, and one in the face of which we with our modest powers must feel humble.” “Curiosity has its own reason for existing.” “When I am judging a theory, I ask myself whether, if I were God, I would have arranged the world in such a way.”
