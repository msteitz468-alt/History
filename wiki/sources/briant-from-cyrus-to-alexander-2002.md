# From Cyrus to Alexander: A History of the Persian Empire

Pierre Briant's magnum opus is the comprehensive history of the [[achaemenid-empire|
Achaemenid Persian Empire]] from the rise of [[cyrus-the-great|Cyrus the Great]] (559
BCE) to the death of [[darius-iii|Darius III]] and the Macedonian conquest (330 BCE).
Its central methodological wager — now the field's orthodoxy — is that the empire must
be reconstructed from the inside out: from Persepolis tablets, royal inscriptions,
Babylonian and Egyptian administrative documents, Aramaic letters, and the biblical and
numismatic record, with the Greek literary tradition (Herodotus, Ctesias, Xenophon,
Plutarch) read critically as the partial, hostile witness it is. Briant's organizing
idea is that the empire was a durable, well-engineered system of tribute extraction and
elite cooptation, not the decadent "colossus with feet of clay" of the Hellenocentric
narrative of decline.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Briant's book reshapes how the wiki should treat the Achaemenids in three ways. First,
it shifts the empire's centre of gravity away from the Greco-Persian Wars: the wars are
a frontier episode in a Mediterranean-and-Asian system whose real engine was the
[[achaemenid-tribute-economy|tribute economy]] and the network of [[achaemenid-kings-men|
royal "Faithful"]] bound to the King by reciprocal gift and obligation. Second, it
dismantles the "decline" thesis — the empire of [[artaxerxes-ii|Artaxerxes II]] and
[[artaxerxes-iii|Artaxerxes III]] is shown reconquering Egypt and dominating Greek
diplomacy down to the eve of [[alexander-the-great|Alexander]]'s invasion, so that
[[darius-iii|Darius III]]'s defeat is contingent (battlefield outcomes, elite defection)
rather than the inevitable collapse of a rotten structure. Third, it makes
[[achaemenid-royal-ideology|royal ideology]] — the King as builder, judge, warrior, and
guarantor of cosmic order under Ahura-Mazda — a first-class historical force, legible in
the [[behistun-inscription|Behistun]] text, the Persepolis reliefs, and the staged
imperial festivals.

Against already-ingested sources (CWH IV, the Daryaee chapter folded into
[[achaemenid-empire]]), Briant is more granular and more sceptical of the Greek
sources but broadly concordant on the empire's structural novelty: a multiethnic,
tolerant, production-supporting "world-system." Where the wiki's existing pages lean on
the classical narrative (the Wars, the satraps' revolts), Briant supplies the
administrative and ideological underframe and repeatedly cautions that the Greek
authors' "Persian decadence" is a literary topos, not evidence.
