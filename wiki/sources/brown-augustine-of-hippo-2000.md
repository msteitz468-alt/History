# Augustine of Hippo: A Biography — Peter Brown (1967; New Edition 2000)

Peter Brown's *Augustine of Hippo* is the classic modern life of Augustine (354–430),
bishop of Hippo Regius in Roman North Africa, and — through the *Confessions*, the *City
of God*, and the anti-Pelagian writings — one of the principal architects of the Latin
Christian mind. Brown reads Augustine's vast output biographically, tracking how a
provincial rhetor's inner development (Manichaeism, Neoplatonism, conversion, the
pastorate) became the intellectual substance of a civilization in transition from the
classical to the medieval world.

## Structure

- **Part I (354–386):** Africa, Monica, education, Manichaeism, the years in Carthage,
  Rome, and Milan; ambition and *Success*.
- **Part II (386–395):** Ambrose, the Platonists, conversion, Cassiciacum, Ostia, the
  return to Thagaste, ordination at Hippo, and the *Confessions*.
- **Part III (395–410):** Augustine as bishop; the Donatist controversy, preaching,
  *De doctrina christiana*, the theology of coercion.
- **Part IV (410–420):** the sack of Rome, the *City of God*, the outbreak of the
  Pelagian controversy, *Causa Gratiae*.
- **Part V (420–430):** Julian of Eclanum, predestination, old age, the end of Roman
  Africa, the Vandal siege, and death.
- **Epilogue (2000):** *New Evidence* (Divjak letters, Dolbeau sermons) and *New
  Directions* (thirty years of scholarship).
