# The Second World War (Antony Beevor)

Antony Beevor's *The Second World War* (2012) is the standard single-volume narrative of the deadliest
conflict in history — perhaps **60 million dead** — told as a genuinely **global** war linking the
European, Mediterranean, Eastern-Front, and Asia-Pacific theatres into one catastrophe. Fourteenth source
of **Gap 1 (Modern)**, and the wiki's anchor for the **[[second-world-war-1939|Second World War]]** as an
event.

## The Work

Beevor's distinctive argument is that the war was **truly global and interconnected**: he opens not in
Poland but with the 1939 **Nomonhan/Khalkhin Gol** clash between Japan and the USSR, using it to show how
the Soviet-Japanese settlement freed Stalin's hands for the Nazi-Soviet Pact and freed Japan to turn
south toward Pearl Harbor. From there he narrates the **fall of France**, the **Battle of Britain**, the
turning of the war's centre of gravity to the **Eastern Front** (Barbarossa, Stalingrad, Kursk — where
the war was overwhelmingly decided), the **Pacific** war, the Mediterranean and D-Day, and the final
convergence on Germany and Japan.

A hallmark is his unflinching treatment of the **war against civilians**: the **Holocaust** and the
*Einsatzgruppen*, the Soviet and German atrocities, the mass rape of 1945, strategic bombing, and the
famines (Bengal, China) that the war produced. Beevor insists the conflict's moral core lies on the
Eastern Front and in the deliberate targeting of civilians.

## Significance

The book supplies the wiki's full **narrative and human history** of the war, complementing the
**structural** account in [[hobsbawm-age-of-extremes|Hobsbawm's "Age of Catastrophe"]], the **origins**
debate of [[taylor-origins-second-world-war|A. J. P. Taylor]], and the [[genocide]] page (the Holocaust).
It feeds the [[1945-postwar-order|1945 transition]].
