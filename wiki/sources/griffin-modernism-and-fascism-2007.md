# Griffin, *Modernism and Fascism: The Sense of a Beginning under Mussolini and Hitler* (2007)

Ninth item of the fascism-studies batch. Griffin's second landmark after
[[griffin-nature-of-fascism-1991|The Nature of Fascism (1991)]]: where the 1991 book
defined fascism as [[palingenetic-ultranationalism|palingenetic ultranationalism]], this
one relocates that definition inside a maximalist theory of **[[modernism]]** — fascism
as a form of **programmatic modernism**, a political
[[revitalization-movement|revitalization movement]] seeking not to reject modernity but
to found an *alternative modernity* purged of decadence. Mussolini's Italy and Hitler's
Germany are analysed as **[[modernist-state|modernist states]]**.

## Claims and Findings

- **The maximalist ideal type of modernism** (verbatim definition, epiphanic/programmatic modes, primordialist grounding in Berger's sacred canopy, TMT, Cronus/aevum, the liminoid) → [[modernism]].
- **Political modernism** (Osborne's "temporality of the new"; Bolshevism as its first state; reactionary modernism re-read; the ch. 6 definition of fascism as "a revolutionary species of political modernism") → [[political-modernism]], [[generic-fascism]], [[communism-and-fascism]].
- **Wallace's revitalization-movement model** (mazeway resynthesis, propheta, liminoid crisis; Anabaptists and Ghost Dance; applied to Bolshevism, Fascism, Nazism, and the Iron Guard) → [[revitalization-movement]].
- **The modernist state** (Fascist Italy ch. 8; Third Reich chs. 9–11; Bauman's gardening state; perpetual liminality and the unrealizable anthropological revolution) → [[modernist-state]].
- **The aporia of "fascist modernism" and its dissolution** ("every 'although' is a misunderstood 'because of'"; Terragni/Libera, Mies/Gropius, Goebbels–Rosenberg, Maertz's ~10,000 paintings) → [[modernism]], [[german-modernism]].
- **The biopolitical modernism of the Third Reich and the "ecology" of genocide** (IBM/Dehomag, Generalplan Ost, Topf & Söhne, Klein's "festering appendix," Traverso, "not incidental, but integral") → [[the-holocaust]], [[nazi-germany]].
- **Case biographies**: [[evola-julius]] (Dada → Traditionalism → postwar guru), [[marinetti-filippo-tommaso]] (Futurism → San Sepolcro; Virilio's reductionism rejected), Heidegger and Robert Ley (conversion mechanics), [[dannunzio-gabriele]] (Fiume as total work of art), [[mussolini-benito]] (Vocian mazeway resynthesis), [[adolf-hitler]] (*Mein Kampf* as manifesto of political modernism).
- **Boundary and extension cases**: [[para-fascism]] (Estado Novo, Francoist Spain excluded from the modernist-state set), [[iron-guard]] (modernist re-reading), BUF and Brazilian Integralists; Postscript on religious extremism and the European New Right as political modernism.

## Volume Synthesis Note

The book completes the arc begun in [[griffin-nature-of-fascism-1991|*The Nature of Fascism*]]: where 1991 defined fascism as [[palingenetic-ultranationalism|palingenetic ultranationalism]], 2007 explains *why* palingenesis has such purchase under modernity, by embedding the definition in a maximalist theory of [[modernism]] grounded in a "primordialist" anthropology of the human need for a sacred canopy. Its structural payoff for the wiki is a set of four interlocking concepts — modernism, [[political-modernism]], the [[revitalization-movement]], and the [[modernist-state]] — that Griffin proposes as definitional components of [[generic-fascism]], displacing the "fascist minimum" hunt he himself had encouraged.

Against the wiki's existing definitional quadrilateral (Paxton behavioral / Payne typological / Sternhell genealogical / Griffin mythic), this volume deepens the Griffin corner rather than adding a fifth: the palingenetic myth is now derived rather than posited. Its most consequential empirical chapters for the wiki are ch. 8 (Fascist Italy as modernist state — the strongest single statement of the anti-"reactionary" reading of the regime) and ch. 11 (biopolitical modernism — the fullest modernist-studies account of the road to Auschwitz, convergent with Bauman and Traverso and explicitly anti-revisionist).

Cross-volume tensions to watch: Griffin's placement of Bolshevism inside political modernism sharpens the [[communism-and-fascism]] genus question (cf. Payne's hedged ten-point parallel and the curator-adjudicated [[queries/where-does-fascism-fall-on-the-political-spectrum|spectrum query]]); his dismissal of Bosworth-style empiricism and of the Goldhagen thesis are stated positions, not neutral summaries; and his verdict that the Iron Guard was modernist rather than religious-atavistic runs against parts of the Payne (1995) portrait already ingested — both readings are preserved on [[actors/iron-guard]]. The book is deliberately a Weberian "speculative syncretism"; wiki pages cite it as the flagship of the fascism-as-alternative-modernity school, not as narrative history.
