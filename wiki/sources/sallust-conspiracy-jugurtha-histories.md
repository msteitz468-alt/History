# Sallust, *Catiline's Conspiracy*, *The Jugurthine War*, *Histories*

The three historical works of **[[sallust|Sallust]]**, Rome's first surviving historian and the
inventor of the Latin **historical monograph**: a sustained anatomy of the **decline of the Republic**.
The sixth source of this Phase-4 batch. For the author and the decline thesis see **[[sallust|Sallust]]**.

## *Catiline's Conspiracy* (*Bellum Catilinae*)

A monograph on the conspiracy of **Lucius Sergius Catilina** in **63 BCE** — the bankrupt, disaffected
patrician who, after repeated electoral failure, plotted armed revolution and was crushed during
**[[cicero|Cicero]]'s** consulship (see the **[[catilinarian-conspiracy-63bce|Catilinarian
conspiracy]]**). Sallust uses the episode as a symptom of systemic rot: a brilliant, sinister portrait
of Catiline ("a sick mind, hostile to gods and men"), the corruption of Roman youth by luxury and
debt, and the great paired speeches of **Caesar** (against execution) and **Cato** (for it) in the
Senate, followed by the famous *synkrisis* of the two as the last great men of a degenerate age.

## *The Jugurthine War* (*Bellum Iugurthinum*)

The war of **118–104 BCE** against **Jugurtha**, king of Numidia (see the
**[[jugurthine-war-112-105bce|Jugurthine War]]**), chosen "because it was great and brutal… and because
that was the first time there was any opposition to the aristocracy's abuse of power." For Sallust the
war exposes the **venality of the optimate nobility** (bribed by Jugurtha — "a city for sale, and doomed
to quick destruction if it finds a buyer") and the rise of the *novus homo* **[[marius|Gaius Marius]]**,
whose consulship and army reforms prefigure the militarized politics that would destroy the Republic.

## The *Histories*

An unfinished **annalistic** history of the years **78–67 BCE** (from the death of Sulla), surviving
only in roughly 500 fragments, four speeches and two letters. It preserves Sallust's fullest statement
of the ***metus hostilis*** thesis — that the loss of the "fear of Carthage" after 146 unleashed the
factionalism and greed that "mutilated" the commonwealth.

## Significance

Together the three works fixed the **monograph form**, the moralizing analytical voice, and the
*optimates/populares* framework of Roman historiography; supplied the standard ancient narrative of
**Catiline and Jugurtha**; and transmitted to posterity the influential idea of **republican decline
through luxury, faction, and the loss of an external threat**. Their stylistic and intellectual heir is
**[[tacitus|Tacitus]]**.
