# Philosophy in the Islamic World (Adamson, 2016)

Peter Adamson's comprehensive survey (the third volume of *A History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*),
covering philosophy in the Islamic world from the translation movement to the twentieth century — and,
distinctively, across **all three Abrahamic faiths**, with dedicated coverage of medieval **Jewish
philosophy** in its Islamic context and of the Christian Baghdad school. Ingested **after** the three
Venture of Islam volumes as an **enrichment pass**: it links the philosophy figures already created
(al-Kindi, al-Farabi, Avicenna, al-Ghazali, Averroes, Ibn Arabi, Suhrawardi, Tusi, kalam, falsafah) and
fills the gaps Hodgson leaves — al-Razi, the Andalusians, the whole Jewish tradition, Illuminationism,
Mulla Sadra and the Safavid/Indian/Ottoman/modern traditions.

Deployed-subagent strategy, 6 chapter-block agents. Body content: lines **281–2,959**; later lines are
endnotes, further reading, and index.
