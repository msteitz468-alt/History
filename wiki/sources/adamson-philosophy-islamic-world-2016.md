# Philosophy in the Islamic World (Adamson, 2016)

Peter Adamson's comprehensive survey (the third volume of *A History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*),
covering philosophy in the Islamic world from the translation movement to the twentieth century — and,
distinctively, across **all three Abrahamic faiths**, with dedicated coverage of medieval **Jewish
philosophy** in its Islamic context and of the Christian Baghdad school. Ingested **after** the three
Venture of Islam volumes as an **enrichment pass**: it links the philosophy figures already created
(al-Kindi, al-Farabi, Avicenna, al-Ghazali, Averroes, Ibn Arabi, Suhrawardi, Tusi, kalam, falsafah) and
fills the gaps Hodgson leaves — al-Razi, the Andalusians, the whole Jewish tradition, Illuminationism,
Mulla Sadra and the Safavid/Indian/Ottoman/modern traditions.

Deployed-subagent strategy, 6 chapter-block agents. Body content: lines **281–2,959**; later lines are
endnotes, further reading, and index.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Adamson's survey was ingested as a **philosophy-focused enrichment pass** after the three Venture of Islam
volumes, and it does exactly what those couldn't: it links the figures Hodgson established
([[al-kindi]], [[al-farabi]], [[ibn-sina]], [[al-ghazali]], [[ibn-rushd]], [[suhrawardi-yahya]],
[[nasir-al-din-tusi]]) and fills the gaps a civilizational history leaves. Three are major: (1) the whole
**[[jewish-philosophy-in-islam|medieval Jewish philosophical tradition]]** — [[saadia-gaon|Saadia]],
[[ibn-gabirol|Ibn Gabirol]], [[maimonides|Maimonides]], [[gersonides|Gersonides]], [[hasdai-crescas|Crescas]],
[[kabbalah|Kabbalah]] — which nothing else in the collection covered and which Adamson uniquely situates in
its Islamic context; (2) the **post-classical "golden age"** ([[post-avicennan-philosophy]],
[[illuminationism]], the [[school-of-shiraz]], [[mulla-sadra|Mulla Sadra]]'s Safavid synthesis), correcting
the old "decline after Averroes" narrative; and (3) the **Andalusian** ([[ibn-bajja]], [[ibn-tufayl]],
[[hayy-ibn-yaqzan]]) and **modern** ([[muhammad-iqbal|Iqbal]], [[fatema-mernissi|Mernissi]]) traditions.
Together with Hodgson's three volumes, this completes the wiki's Islamic intellectual-history build
(Gap 4b): ~184 pages across the four sources.
