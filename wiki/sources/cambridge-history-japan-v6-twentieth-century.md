# The Cambridge History of Japan, Vol. 6: The Twentieth Century (Duus ed., 1988)

The deep, interdisciplinary authority on **twentieth-century Japan (c. 1900–1973)** — the
last of the Cambridge History of Japan backbone, completing the Japan spine from prehistory
to the postwar miracle. Its task here is to **build out and deepen** the wiki's thin
20th-century Japan coverage: the existing spine ([[meiji-period]], [[japanese-imperialism]],
[[pacific-war-1941-1945]], [[russo-japanese-war-1904]], [[industrialization]]) plus the modern
period pages ([[world-wars-era]], [[cold-war]], [[contemporary]]).

Editor **Peter Duus** organizes the century around three master trajectories: **"from autonomy
to dependence"** (Japan's external relations — the rise and catastrophic fall of empire, then
dependence within the American order); **"from success to success"** (the economy — continuous
modern growth, "interrupted but not broken" by the mid-century upheavals); and **"from
instability to stability"** (politics — from the narrow, unstable authority that followed the
Meiji oligarchs, through militarism, to the stable postwar order). He dates the "twentieth
century" from **1895** (the Sino-Japanese War) to the **1972–73 "shocks"** (the yen
revaluation and the oil crisis) that ended the era of rapid growth, and frames the whole
through the tension of **change vs. continuity** across the great 1945 divide.
