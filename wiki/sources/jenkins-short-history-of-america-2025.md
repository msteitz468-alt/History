## Overview

Simon Jenkins' *A Short History of America: From Tea Party to Trump* (Penguin Viking, 2025) is a single-volume popular narrative history of the United States (and North American continent) from the earliest human presence (~23,000 BP footprints) through European colonization, independence, expansion, civil war, industrialization, world wars, Cold War, to the Trump era and 2024 election/early second term. At ~102k words / 344 pages it is concise yet comprehensive, emphasizing political and constitutional themes over social or cultural detail.

The book's central thesis treats the US Constitution as the "leitmotif" and "single key" to American history: an 18th-century "experiment" in federal union and separation of powers that enabled continental-scale success while resisting dictatorship, even as it failed tests (Civil War) and carries enduring defects (Electoral College, Senate malapportionment, amendment difficulty). Jenkins (British perspective) praises the union's resilience and localism while critiquing myth-making, interventionist overreach (Vietnam, Iraq, Afghanistan), racial injustices, economic concentration, and recent populist strains under Trump.

It is explicitly not academic; quotes and references are indicated inline with a Further Reading list of mostly secondary works. The tone is balanced but skeptical of American exceptionalism and "indispensable nation" rhetoric.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Jenkins provides a concise, constitution-centered narrative sweep of US history with particular value for the Contemporary period (Chs 26–28 + Conclusion). The book frames the entire story as the unfolding (and recurrent testing) of the 1787 "experiment" in federal union, separation of powers, and localism — resilient enough to survive Civil War, world wars, and recent populist challenges, yet carrying structural defects (Electoral College, Senate malapportionment, amendment rigidity) that distort democracy and enable executive overreach. 

Key contributions from the four ranges:
- R1–R2: Strong on colonial origins, Revolution as fiscal/constitutional clash ("no taxation without representation"), early republic compromises on slavery/expansion, and the Civil War/Reconstruction as the constitution's greatest test (with enduring racial aftermath).
- R3: Gilded Age excess, Progressive "square deal"/conservation, Wilsonian intervention, New Deal as federal expansion, WWII mobilization, and emergence as global policeman ("Pax Americana").
- R4 (densest for wiki gaps): Detailed treatment of Cold War endgame, 9/11 response and "forever wars" (Afghanistan/Iraq costs, WMD intelligence failures, Abu Ghraib, "mission accomplished"), Obama (ACA success + limits on intervention), Trump I & II (2016/2024 wins, Jan 6 as constitutional stress test, 2025 executive orders, DOGE/Musk, Project 2025, "war on woke", Ukraine policy reversal, tech-billionaire influence). Conclusion returns to the union's mechanics as the "final achievement" while warning of myth, overreach, and the "beast" of majoritarian/populist threat.

British perspective adds detachment on exceptionalism and "indispensable nation" rhetoric. Complements 2025 tax/democracy and founding-tragedy ingests. All claims extracted with range-exclusive verbatim quotes. Modern sections fill collection gap on post-1750 US politics. 

Further Reading in source lists Black, Burk, Davidson, Ferguson, etc. (secondary). No primary depth.
