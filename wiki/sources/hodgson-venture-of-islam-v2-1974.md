# The Venture of Islam, Vol. 2 (Hodgson, 1974)

The Middle Periods of Islamicate civilization (c. 945–1503) — when the caliphal centre had dissolved
and Islam became a genuinely **international civilization** carried by Sufi orders, the 'ulama, and
Persian high culture rather than by a single state. **Book Three** (Earlier Middle Period) covers the
Seljuk political order, the a'yan-amir society, the maturity of philosophy and theology (Avicenna,
al-Ghazali), the rise of the [[sufism|Sufi tariqa orders]], the Sunni revival and the madrasa, and the
bloom of Persian literature. **Book Four** (the Mongol age) covers the Mongol irruption and its
aftermath (the Ilkhans, Timur), the intellectual conservation (Ibn Khaldun, Ibn Taymiyya), Islamic
visual arts, and the expansion of Islam into India, Southeast Asia, the Sudan, and Anatolia.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Vol. 2 is the interpretive heart of Hodgson's work and a major deepening of the wiki's Islamic
coverage. Its thesis: after the caliphate dissolved, Islam became a genuinely **[[international-islamic-civilization|international
civilization]] without political integration** — held together not by a state but by the
[[sufism|Sufi]] [[tariqa-orders|orders]], the [[ulama|'ulama]], the [[madrasa]], the [[waqf]], and a
shared [[sharia|Shari'ah-minded]] culture, all resting on the new [[military-patronage-state|military-patronage
state]] and [[ayan-amir-society|a'yan-amir society]]. The intellectual flowering it documents is now on
the wiki: [[ibn-sina|Avicenna]] and [[al-ghazali|al-Ghazali]]'s reconciliation of philosophy, theology,
and Sufism; [[ibn-arabi|Ibn al-Arabi]]'s mysticism; the bloom of [[persianate-literary-culture|Persian
literature]] ([[firdawsi|Firdawsi]], [[rumi|Rumi]], [[sadi-of-shiraz|Sa'di]]); the Mongol catastrophe
and its absorption ([[conversion-of-mongols-to-islam]], [[timur|Timur]]); and the late synthesizers
[[ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]] ([[asabiyyah]]) and [[ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]].

Two cross-cutting Hodgson theses recur: the **[[islamicate-contractualism|contractualism]]** of
Islamicate society (open, contractual, egalitarian) versus Occidental corporativism (the
[[islamdom-and-occident-high-medieval|Islamdom/Occident comparison]]); and the rise of the
**[[persianate-zone|Persianate zone]]** as a second cultural pole alongside the Arabic. Continues

Empires) follows. Reconciliation deduped two A/C naming collisions (madrasa, Nizam al-Mulk) and
