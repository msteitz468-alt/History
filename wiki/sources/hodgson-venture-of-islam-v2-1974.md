# The Venture of Islam, Vol. 2 (Hodgson, 1974)

The Middle Periods of Islamicate civilization (c. 945–1503) — when the caliphal centre had dissolved
and Islam became a genuinely **international civilization** carried by Sufi orders, the 'ulama, and
Persian high culture rather than by a single state. **Book Three** (Earlier Middle Period) covers the
Seljuk political order, the a'yan-amir society, the maturity of philosophy and theology (Avicenna,
al-Ghazali), the rise of the [[sufism|Sufi tariqa orders]], the Sunni revival and the madrasa, and the
bloom of Persian literature. **Book Four** (the Mongol age) covers the Mongol irruption and its
aftermath (the Ilkhans, Timur), the intellectual conservation (Ibn Khaldun, Ibn Taymiyya), Islamic
visual arts, and the expansion of Islam into India, Southeast Asia, the Sudan, and Anatolia.
