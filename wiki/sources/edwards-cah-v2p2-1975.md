# The Cambridge Ancient History, Vol. II, Part 2 (1975)

**History of the Middle East and the Aegean Region, c. 1380–1000 BC.**
Edited by I. E. S. Edwards, the late C. J. Gadd, N. G. L. Hammond and
E. Sollberger. Cambridge University Press, 3rd edition, 1975.

This is the direct continuation of [[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|Vol. II, Part 1]]. Where
II.1 carried the eastern Mediterranean to the threshold of the
[[late-bronze-age|Late Bronze Age]] (c. 1380 BCE), II.2 covers the **climax,
collapse, and aftermath** of that world: the Amarna international system at its
height, the Egyptian–Hittite struggle for Syria and its resolution in the first
recorded international peace treaty, the rise of Assyria, the **~1200 BCE
[[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]]** and the **[[sea-peoples|Sea Peoples]]**, the fall of
the Hittite and Mycenaean worlds, and the early [[early-iron-age|Iron Age]] order
that emerged from the wreckage — the Phoenician cities, the Philistines, the
**Hebrew kingdom** of Saul, David and Solomon, the Aramaean and Neo-Hittite states,
Egypt's decline into the Third Intermediate Period, and the Greek Dark Age that runs
down to Homer. It resolves the great gap flagged at the end of the
[[late-bronze-age|Late Bronze Age]] period page.

The remaining CAH backbone volumes — III.1–III.2 (10th–8th centuries BC) — remain
unsourced; III.3 and IV have been sourced. See [[../../Outstanding Sources|Outstanding
Sources]].

## Editors' Framing (from the Preface)

The Preface sets several intertwined themes: (1) the two famous kings at the volume's
opening — **Akhenaten** (religious/artistic revolution) and **Tutankhamun** (the
intact tomb), neither accepted as legitimate by the later king-lists; (2) in Western
Asia, the **end of the long Kassite rule** in Babylonia and the beginning of the
**Assyria–Babylonia rivalry**; the Hittite empire reaching its **peak, decline and
fall**, as did **Elam**; the first appearance of the **Phrygians**; new **alphabetic
writing** in Phoenicia and Ugarit; and the emergence of **Palestine from anonymity**
with the **Hebrew kingdom** culminating in **Solomon**; (3) the **Sea Peoples**,
beaten by Egypt's "succession of warrior-kings" — Merneptah and decisively
**Ramesses III** — but ushering in the Libyan infiltration of the Delta; (4) the
**Egyptian–Hittite indecisive battles** under Sethos I and Ramesses II, ending in a
**peace treaty** honoured until Hatti fell; and (5) the central Aegean theme — the
**spread of Mycenaean civilization** to its 14th-c. zenith (from Sicily to Miletus
to Cyprus), its **decline** through trade-collapse and internecine war (including the
destruction of Troy), and the **migrations** that ended the Aegean Bronze Age and
laid the **foundations of Greek religion, myth and the Homeric tradition**.
