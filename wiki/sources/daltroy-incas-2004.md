# The Incas: Inside an American Empire (Terence N. D'Altroy, 2004)

A lecture course by the leading Inca scholar, ingested (standard single-pass) to give the wiki its first
in-depth civilizational portrait of **Tawantinsuyu**, the largest empire of the pre-Columbian Americas.
The 14 lectures run from the Andean environment and the origins of Inca society, through the formation
and machinery of empire (militarism, Cuzco, the road system, the *mit'a* labour tax, the *khipu*),
the religion and royal ideology (split inheritance, the cult of the royal mummies, the sun cult), daily
life, and the Spanish conquest and its aftermath.

## Source Notes

D'Altroy gives the wiki its first **civilizational-depth** treatment of Tawantinsuyu, turning the
CWH-derived [[inca-empire]] page (state-and-religion, the Sun cult, the conquest) into a full portrait of
the **machinery of empire**. Five pages capture the distinctive Inca institutions: the
[[split-inheritance-and-royal-mummies|split-inheritance/royal-mummy cult]] (the engine that built
**perpetual conquest into succession** — each ruler inheriting throne but not treasury, so needing fresh
lands for his court and afterlife), the [[khipu|khipu]] knot-record (decimal, state-scale information
without alphabetic writing), the [[mita-and-andean-reciprocity|mit'a-and-reciprocity political economy]]
(empire without markets or money — labour-tribute, the ayllu, vertical complementarity, redistribution
from the storehouses, after Murra), the sacred capital [[cuzco|Cuzco]] (the puma-plan cosmogram, the
Qorikancha, the ceque/waq'a system), and the [[qhapaq-nan|Qhapaq Ñan]] (the 40,000-km road-and-tampu
logistical network). The [[inca-empire]] page itself was expanded with the conquest sequence
(Pachacuti → Thupa Inka → Wayna Qhapaq → the smallpox death & Waskhar–Atawallpa war), the
logistics-not-technology militarism, the decimal/kuraka administration, and the *mitmaq* resettlement
program; and [[andean-state-formation]] was enriched to frame the Inca as the **synthesis** of the deep
Andean lineage ([[norte-chico]] → [[tiwanaku]]/Wari → Inca).

The unifying theme — reinforcing Mann's [[pre-columbian-americas|hemispheric picture]] — is that the
**largest, most integrated state of the pre-Columbian Americas ran on wholly non-Eurasian principles**:
reciprocity and labour-tribute rather than markets and coin, knotted cords rather than script, mummified
kings as active political actors. As an audiobook lecture course it is **synthetic rather than
footnoted**, and it leans (naturally) on the Spanish *crónicas* and on D'Altroy's own and Murra's
frameworks; it will be balanced by the Andean **primary source** staged for this gap — **Guamán Poma's
*Nueva corónica*** (an Indigenous Andean view of Inca and colonial rule).
