# Africa: A Biography of the Continent (John Reader)

John Reader's *Africa: A Biography of the Continent* (1997) is the **synthetic spine** for the wiki's
Sub-Saharan Africa gap (**Gap 2**) — a single-volume history of the entire continent across deep time,
organized around an **environmental argument**: that Africa's ancient geology, thin and fragile soils,
capricious climate, and uniquely heavy **disease burden** (malaria, trypanosomiasis) set the constraints
within which its peoples built their societies, and that this ecological reality — not any cultural
deficiency — explains the distinctive paths of African history. As the birthplace of humanity, Africa is
also where the deepest human history is written.

This is the foundational, continent-wide source; the **[[reader-africa-biography#Section Plan|Section
Plan]]** below governs its Large-Volume ingest. The eight UNESCO **General History of Africa** volumes,
the **West Africa** reference, and the primary sources (**Sundiata**, **Ibn Battuta**) will deepen the
state-level pages this spine creates.
