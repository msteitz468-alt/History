# 1929: Inside the Greatest Crash in Wall Street History — and How It Shattered a Nation (Andrew Ross Sorkin, 2025)

Narrative reconstruction of the 1929 stock market crash and its immediate aftermath through 1933, centered on the human actors — bankers, financiers, Federal Reserve officials, industrialists, and politicians — whose decisions inflated the bubble and shaped the response. Draws on extensive new and unpublished primary sources.

## Overview and Thesis

Sorkin presents 1929 not merely as an impersonal market event but as the product of specific decisions by identifiable individuals operating under the incentives, culture, and information constraints of late-1920s Wall Street and Washington. The narrative emphasizes personal responsibility of those "who helped set it in motion." It highlights the interplay of hubris, overconfidence in perpetual prosperity, aggressive credit expansion (especially margin lending and bank involvement in securities), and policy missteps. The "gradually, then suddenly" loss of confidence is a recurring motif.

New archival access (Fed NY minutes) and the unpublished memoir allow finer-grained reconstruction of closed-door meetings, phone calls, and personal calculations than prior accounts.

## Key Actors Featured

- **Charles E. Mitchell** (National City Bank / National City Company) — central lightning-rod figure; aggressive expansion, public boosterism, and personal exposure.
- **J. P. "Jack" Morgan Jr.** and partners (Thomas W. Lamont, George Whitney, etc.) of J.P. Morgan & Co. — the "House of Morgan" as still-dominant but increasingly challenged institution; role in support efforts.
- **Albert H. Wiggin** (Chase National Bank)
- **George F. Baker** (First National Bank of New York)
- Other bankers: James Riordan (County Trust), Seward Prosser (Bankers Trust), Lewis Pierson (Irving Trust)
- Industrial/finance figures: John J. Raskob, Owen D. Young (GE/RCA), Walter Chrysler, Henry Ford, Charles M. Schwab, Alfred Sloan, Pierre and T. Coleman du Pont, William Fox, David Sarnoff
- International: Hjalmar Schacht (Reichsbank)
- Political: Herbert Hoover (implied context), Calvin Coolidge era backdrop
- NYSE leadership (Richard Whitney appears prominently in Epilogue)

## Collection Coverage Note

The post-1750 / modern US economic history coverage in the collection has historically been thin outside archaeology and select political narratives. This source supplies the first high-resolution, primary-document-driven account of the 1929 crash mechanics and the principal actors on the US financial side. It complements existing Great Depression coverage (derived from broader Cambridge/NCMH volumes) and European-focused 1930s sources. Expect significant new or deepened pages on US banking personalities, Fed NY 1929 decision-making, margin practices, and the cultural/institutional shift from 1920s boom to regulatory state.

## Historiography and Relation to Other Sources

Positions itself alongside (and in some details challenges assumptions in) canonical works such as John Kenneth Galbraith's *The Great Crash 1929*, John Brooks' *Once in Golconda*, and Gordon Thomas/Max Morgan-Witts' *The Day the Bubble Burst*. Sorkin's contribution is the depth of access to private papers and the deliberate focus on the "those who helped set it in motion" rather than solely on victims or macroeconomic aggregates. As a 2025 work it post-dates much of the existing wiki Depression material; its claims on specific meetings and motivations should be treated as fresh evidence requiring cross-check against older scholarship where they diverge.
