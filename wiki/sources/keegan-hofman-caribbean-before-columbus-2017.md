## Overview

Keegan and Hofman synthesise the full arc of pre-Columbian Caribbean archaeology, from the earliest Archaic Age inhabitants (~5000 BCE) through the Spanish encounter of 1492. The book is organised island-group by island-group and culture-stage by culture-stage, weaving together ceramic sequences, settlement patterns, subsistence systems, and social organisation. A central argument is that Caribbean indigenous societies were not static—they were dynamic, networked, and constantly in motion, best understood through a "kaleidoscope" metaphor: fixed structures producing unpredictable, ever-changing combinations.

The book decisively critiques the Arawak-versus-Carib binary inherited from Columbus's diario, arguing it is a colonial projection with no firm archaeological or linguistic foundation for the pre-contact period.
