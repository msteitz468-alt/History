# The Fatimids: 1 — The Rise of a Muslim Empire

Shainool Jiwa's *The Fatimids: 1 — The Rise of a Muslim Empire* (World of Islam series, I.B. Tauris /
Institute of Ismaili Studies, 2018) is a concise scholarly narrative of the **origins and first six decades
of the [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid Caliphate]]** — the earliest sustained Shi'i caliphate of the medieval
Muslim world. The volume covers the four Ifriqiyan Imam-caliphs from the proclamation of **al-Mahdi in 909**
through the **conquest of Egypt (969)** and the transfer of the dynasty to the new city of **Cairo (972–973)**;
a projected second volume, *The Fatimid Rule from Egypt*, treats the ten later Imam-caliphs (969–1171).

Its distinctive contribution to the wiki is the **North African / Ismaili-da'wa phase** of Fatimid history —
the missionary movement among the [[berbers|Kutama Berbers]], the overthrow of the [[aghlabid-dynasty|Aghlabids]],
the near-fatal [[khariji-revolt-of-abu-yazid-943|revolt of Abu Yazid]], and the consolidation under al-Mu'izz —
which the existing coverage (drawn from the UNESCO *General History of Africa* and Cobb's *Race for Paradise*)
treats only in outline. It is a self-consciously corrective source: Jiwa foregrounds the recently recovered
**Ismaili textual tradition** against the hostile Abbasid-Sunni chronicles that long framed the Fatimids.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Jiwa's volume closes the wiki's largest remaining gap on the **Fatimid rise** — the North African, Ismaili-da'wa
phase that prior sources (the UNESCO *General History of Africa* and Cobb's *Race for Paradise*) treated only in
outline. Its overarching argument is **corrective**: that the standard story of the Fatimids, long transmitted
through hostile Abbasid-Sunni chronicles after the destruction of the Cairo libraries, can now be rebuilt from
**recovered insider Ismaili texts** — Qadi al-Nu'man's *Iftitah al-da'wa* and *Da'a'im al-Islam*, the *Sirat
al-Ustadh Jawdhar*, and Idris Imad al-Din's *Uyun al-Akhbar*.

Three threads structure the volume. First, the **movement-to-state transformation**: a clandestine messianic
da'wa becomes a caliphate in 909, and the very first crisis (the 911 execution of the founding da'i Abu Abd
Allah) establishes the primacy of the Imam-caliph over the da'wa that made him. Second, **pragmatic pluralism**:
faced with a Sunni-majority, ethnically mixed Ifriqiya and the near-fatal Kharijite revolt of Abu Yazid, the
dynasty governs by accommodation — retaining Aghlabid officials, remitting taxes to all communities, appointing
a Sunni Maliki chief judge — a template it carries into Egypt in the tolerant *aman* of 969. Third,
**codification and reach**: under al-Mu'izz, Qadi al-Nu'man's *Da'a'im al-Islam* fixes Ismaili law around
*walaya*, the eastern da'wa is reconciled, and the deliberate, richly financed conquest of Egypt founds Cairo
and al-Azhar.

The volume dovetails cleanly with the wiki's existing Fatimid coverage, which begins essentially where Jiwa
ends (969): it supplies the origins that [[cobb-race-for-paradise-2014|Cobb]] and the peak/decline narrative on
[[fatimid-caliphate]] presuppose, and it enriches [[places/sicily|Sicily]]'s Kalbid century. No contradictions
with existing pages arose; the only internal tensions (the 943-vs-944 dating of Abu Yazid's revolt, and the
972-vs-973 framing of al-Mu'izz's Cairo residence) are noted on the relevant pages and reflect the sources'
own departure-versus-arrival framing rather than genuine scholarly dispute.
