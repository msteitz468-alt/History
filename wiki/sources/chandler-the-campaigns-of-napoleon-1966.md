# David G. Chandler, *The Campaigns of Napoleon* (1966)

The standard modern reference for the operational history of Napoleon's campaigns from his first emergence through the Hundred Days. Chandler provides both the most detailed English-language campaign narratives and a systematic analysis of Napoleon's methods in Part Three ("Napoleon's Art of War").

This Tier-4 source for the Napoleonic Wars hub supplies the granular operational detail (planning, marches, concentrations, orders of battle, the hinge points of campaigns) that complements the broader surveys (Doughty & Gruber) and tactical studies already ingested. It is the "operational bible" for the period.

## Historiography / impact note
Chandler writes as a Sandhurst lecturer with deep familiarity with staff-college operational analysis. The work is narrative-first but unusually strong on the "how" of campaigns (the square of battalions, the timing of concentrations, the role of Berthier's staff machine, the practical limits of centralization). Part Three remains one of the clearest expositions of the Napoleonic system in English. The book influenced generations of operational historians and remains the first port of call for any serious study of Napoleon's campaigns. Later scholarship has revised some casualty figures, emphasized logistics and coalition adaptation more heavily, and questioned aspects of the "genius" narrative, but Chandler's operational reconstructions and OOB appendices have held up exceptionally well. For the wiki, this is the authoritative source for the conduct of the campaigns; contradictions with other sources (e.g., on exact numbers or responsibility at Waterloo) will be flagged in the relevant event pages or controversies.

The hinge identified by Chandler — the failure to achieve conclusive peace after Jena, the turn to the Continental System (Dec 1806), and the subsequent Spanish and Russian disasters — is a structural through-line for the hub's decline narrative.

## Primary Claims from the Text (Grounded Extractions)

Organized by the 10 disjoint ranges used in the Deployed Subagent Strategy. Links restricted to pre-approved names established on the main thread. ### Range 1 Extraction (Gen. Intro + Part 1 + early Part 2; lines 958-4800)
- **Napoleon acknowledged the part played by sheer good luck**: "He freely admitted to Las Cases that the French Revolution paved the way for the career ouverte aux talents" (GENERAL INTRODUCTION). `produced:` meteoric rise; `part_of:` [[napoleon]].
- **Centralization**: "In war, men are nothing; one man is everything" and "Better one bad general than two good ones" (GENERAL INTRODUCTION). Enabled coordinated corps movement under single master intelligence while armies manageable; later a snare. `part_of:` [[napoleonic-warfare]].
- **Hinge of fate**: "this date, therefore, almost certainly forms the 'hinge of fate' in the Napoleonic Wars" (GENERAL INTRODUCTION, post-Jena). Berlin Decrees/Continental System rebounded, inducing Spain and Russia disasters. `produced:` major strategic errors.
- **Toulon mastery**: "It is the artillery that takes places; the infantry can only aid it" (Ch. 2 TOULON). Personal supervision of batteries at l’Eguillette enabled fleet evacuation. `produced:` first reputation.
- **Lodi as prototype**: "The Maneuver of Lodi, May 1796, the prototype of la manoeuvre sur les derrières (or strategical envelopment)" (Ch. 7 THE BRIDGE OF LODI). Dallemagne + cavalry seized Piacenza ferry while diversions fixed Beaulieu. `part_of:` [[manoeuvre-sur-les-derrieres]].
- **Personal magnetism**: "So it is that I, who fear neither God nor Devil, am ready to tremble like a child when I approach him" (GENERAL INTRODUCTION, Vandamme). Hypnotic power over troops. `part_of:` [[napoleon]].

**Range 1 summary**: Foundational personal traits (intellect, memory, work capacity, magnetism, centralization) + early formation (Toulon, 1794 plans from Guibert/Bourcet/du Teil, Lodi as first classic turning manoeuvre). Directly grounds [[napoleon]], [[napoleonic-warfare]], [[manoeuvre-sur-les-derrieres]].

### Range 2 Extraction (late Part 2 + early Part 3; lines 4801-7200)
- **Lodi crystallized destiny**: "It was only on the evening of Lodi... that I believed myself a superior man" (Part Two). `produced:` sense of destiny.
- **Unity of command**: "War, like government, is a matter of tact"; "Unity of command is the most important thing in war" (Part Two, post-Lodi). Secured undivided authority.
- **Castiglione central position**: "the Napoleonic method of campaign and battle was rapidly evolving" (Part Two). Massena/Augereau pinned; Sérurier turned left rear. `produced:` defeat in detail.
- **Arcola/Rivoli**: Multiple references to *manoeuvre sur les derrières* across marshes/dykes and multi-column defense using interior lines and rapid night marches (e.g., Massena 54 miles in 120 hours fighting). `part_of:` [[manoeuvre-sur-les-derrieres]].
- **Doctrinal inheritance**: Guibert prophecy of national energy + "a people which... would not be compelled by calculations of finance"; focus on "crush[ing] ... the enemy’s main body" (Part Three, Ch. 13). `part_of:` [[napoleonic-warfare]].
- **Ingredients**: "Make war offensively"; "I am accustomed to thinking out what I shall do three or four months in advance"; "rapidity is an essential and primordial factor" (Part Three, Ch. 14). Assembly then concentration; security + deception.

**Range 2 summary**: Climax of 1796-97 (Mantua crises, Arcola, Rivoli) showing system in action + transition to explicit analysis of doctrinal roots and ingredients (speed, concentration, central position, offensive, planning). Core for [[napoleonic-warfare]] and [[corps-system]] precursors.

### Range 3 Extraction (Part 3 core analytical; lines 7201-9500)
- **"Marches are war."** Speed via corps + "living off the countryside" + will. `part_of:` [[corps-system]] [[napoleonic-warfare]].
- **Time and space**: "Strategy is the art of making use of time and space... time never"; "I may lose a battle, but I shall never lose a minute" (strategic concepts). `part_of:` [[manoeuvre-sur-les-derrieres]].
- **Corps autonomy**: "A corps of 25,000-30,000 men can be left on its own... A division of 9,000-17,000 men can be left for an hour..." (1809 letter quoted). Enabled dispersed movement + rapid concentration. `produced:` [[corps-system]].
- **Manoeuvre sur les derrières**: Employed ~30 times 1796-1815; pin frontally, march by quickest safe route (cavalry screen) to rear/flank, occupy barrier, force unfavorable battle or surrender. Ulm prototype from 1796 Po crossing. `part_of:` [[manoeuvre-sur-les-derrieres]].
- **Central position / interior lines**: When inferior overall, seize "hinge" for blitz attack, interpose, destroy in detail (Castiglione/Rivoli exemplars).
- **Grand tactics sequence**: Cavalry screen → nearest corps pins as bait → supporting corps escalate → enveloping force reveals on flank/rear at precise moment (cannon/aide signal) → frontal pin coincides → masse de rupture (massed guns + columns + cavalry) → exploitation/pursuit. "It is by turning the enemy... that battles are won"; "the breach once made, the equilibrium is broken".
- **Castiglione prototype** (Ch. 17): Feigned withdrawal to draw Würmser; Sérurier/Fiorella to rear; Marmont horse guns at point-blank; grenadiers break hinge. Timing slips limited full victory.

**Range 3 summary**: The authoritative Chandler exposition of the fully developed system: ingredients, three strategic concepts (*manoeuvre sur les derrières*, central position, penetration), and the fused battle sequence. Direct template for hub battle analyses.

### Range 4 Extraction (Egypt + Marengo; lines 9501-13000)
- Pyramids squares vs. Mamelukes; Acre failure (sea power + Smith/Phélippeaux + 4-day delay at El Arish); Jaffa massacre; Mount Tabor squares.
- Brumaire and shift to Reserve army; Moreau forced Italy pivot.
- Alps crossing (Great St. Bernard, Fort Bard guns slipped by night); Milan/Stradella manoeuvre.
- Marengo: "This battle is completely lost, but it is only two o’clock—there is time to win another." Desaix + Marmont battery + Kellermann cavalry turned near-defeat. Lesson on reserves and avoiding over-detachment.

**Range 4 summary**: Transition to First Consul; maturation of operational methods (Alps, central position via Switzerland); explicit lessons on concentration/reserves.

### Range 5 Extraction (Grande Armée + Ulm/Austerlitz; lines 13001-16500)
- Detailed 1805 Grande Armée organization (7 corps + cavalry reserve + artillery reserve + Imperial Guard; Year XIII artillery mix of 6/12-pdrs + howitzers; interarm sequence: bombardment → cavalry/infantry attacks → horse guns at point-blank → cavalry exploitation).
- Berthier as chief of staff (literal executor); Bacler d’Albe topographical office with colored pins; 18-hour workdays.
- Ulm: 210k men, 200+ miles in 13-17 days; Lannes/Murat screen in Black Forest; Danube crossings at Donauwörth; envelopment; Mack capitulation ~30k prisoners.
- Vienna bridge coup (Murat/Lannes bluff); Hollabrunn armistice setback allowed Kutusov escape.

**Range 5 summary**: Perfected corps system, HQ, and classic *manoeuvre sur les derrières* at Ulm. Backbone for 1805 hub material.

### Range 6 Extraction (Jena + Eylau + hinge; lines 16501-20500)
- Prussian "museum piece" (linear tactics, huge trains, aged leaders, divided staff) vs. flexible French.
- Bataillon carré concentration; advance through Franconian Forest.
- Jena (Lannes expands bridgehead; Ney premature; Soult/Augereau flanks) + Auerstadt (Davout ~27k defeats main Prussian army while Napoleon at Jena; Brunswick killed).
- Bernadotte's failure to support Davout.
- "Napoleon had blown with his breath, and Prussian military might was no more" (~125k accounted for) but no political knockout.
- Berlin Decrees (21 Nov 1806): "the moment when Napoleon passed the true apogee... and started down the slope to ruin."

**Range 6 summary**: Peak of system at Jena-Auerstedt + first check (Eylau) and the strategic hinge (Continental System) that drove later disasters. Critical for hub decline narrative.

### Range 7 Extraction (Friedland + Peninsular; lines 20501-24500)
- Friedland: Lannes pins; Ney evening assault from Sortlach Wood + Victor + Sénarmont point-blank case-shot; 18-20k Russian losses.
- Tilsit: raft meeting; dynastic overthrow in Iberia agreed.
- Junot to Lisbon (Dec 1 1807 too late; court flees); engineered Spanish crisis (fortresses seized, Bayonne, Dos de Mayo).
- Bailén (Dupont trapped/surrendered ~23k); Vimiero (Wellesley reverse-slope + light infantry beats piecemeal French).
- "the sole long-term result... tying down eventually 200,000 of his best men... constant annual drain of 40,000 valuable lives."

**Range 7 summary**: Last conventional victory (Friedland) + shift to the "Spanish ulcer" that exposed limits of system against national war.

### Range 8 Extraction (Wagram + 1812 origins; lines 24501-29000)
- Aspern-Essling: hasty unprepared crossing; bridge breaks; Lannes mortally wounded; tactical repulse shakes invincibility (~20-22k French casualties).
- Wagram: six-week preparation (Lobau fortified, east-side crossing deception); arrowhead interposition; evening 5 July fails on Russbach; 6 July crises (Bernadotte abandons Aderklaa; Klenau nears Essling) resolved by 112-gun battery + Macdonald "rolling" attack; limited victory, no pursuit.
- "illusion of success": more judgment errors in 1809 than prior career combined; overconfidence, misjudged Austrian strength, army dilution (conscripts/allies).
- Russian break: Erfurt (Alexander as equal); Marie Louise marriage affront; ukase Dec 1810 breaches Continental System; "economic struggle with England"; Caulaincourt warning ("our winter will fight on our side") dismissed.

**Range 8 summary**: 1809 limits exposed; seeds of 1812 decision (rival + system enforcement).

### Range 9 Extraction (1812 invasion + retreat + Berezina; lines 29001-34000)
- Grande Armée 1812: <half French (~302k of 614k); green conscripts; supply system (26 transport bns, 25k vehicles) broke immediately on poor roads + scorched earth.
- Niemen crossing unopposed; Vilna/Smolensk manoeuvres failed to trap; Borodino frontal sledgehammer (Davout outflank rejected); ~30k French + 44k Russian casualties; Guard refused; "imperfect victory."
- Moscow occupation + fire; no peace; retreat decision; Maloyaroslavets won but Napoleon chose old devastated route.
- Collapse: first snow/frost 3 Nov; regiments dissolved; Berezina (Eblé trestle bridges at Studienka after thaw; Tshitshagov misdirected south; Victor rear guard; ~20-30k combatant + 30k noncombatant casualties); remnants escape.

**Range 9 summary**: Logistics and depth as decisive factors; overextension of corps system at army-group scale; Berezina as subordinate heroism under failed central command.

### Range 10 Extraction (Leipzig + 1814 + Waterloo + Postscript; lines 34001-42500)
- Leipzig: ~177k French (interior lines attempt) vs. converging Allies; 16 Oct draw (diversions cost reserves); 18-19 Oct retreat; bridge blown prematurely; ~68k+ French losses + 30k prisoners; "second Trafalgar on land."
- 1814: La Rothière near-disaster; brilliant Six Days recovery but France exhausted; abdication 6 April.
- 1815: Delays 15 June (Grouchy/Ney); Ligny plan (two wings + reserve); Quatre Bras errors (d’Erlon diversion); Waterloo 18 June: Hougoumont escalation, d’Erlon outdated massive columns routed by Union Brigade, Ney unsupported cavalry charges, La Haie Sainte taken too late, Grouchy literal at Wavre; Prussian arrival swings day.
- Chandler verdict: "the chief responsibility... can only be laid at the door of the Emperor himself... obstinate, arrogant and overconfident... should never have allowed these mistakes." Deterioration (appointments, control, lethargy).
- Postscript: French ~60k total losses 16-18 June; Allies ~55k; 117k rallied around Paris + 170k conscripts; political collapse via Fouché/Chambers; abdication 22 June; St. Helena.

**Range 10 summary**: Full operational narrative and responsibility analysis for Leipzig and Waterloo; explicit assessment of decline. Core source for both hub battle pages.

### Consolidated Volume Synthesis
Chandler's work establishes the operational narrative spine for the entire Napoleonic era: the birth of the system in 1796 Italy (central position, *manoeuvre sur les derrières*, speed, artillery primacy, personal leadership); its perfection in the corps d'armée organization, bataillon carré, inter-arm coordination, and HQ machine of 1805-06; the zenith at Ulm, Austerlitz, and Jena-Auerstedt; and the long decline triggered at the "hinge of fate" (Dec 1806 Continental System after incomplete political results at Jena), exposed in the Peninsula, and culminating in the over-extension and logistical/moral collapse of 1812 and the coalition mass/adaptation of 1813-15. Part Three supplies the clearest single exposition of the system's concepts and grand tactics sequence. The book is unmatched for OOB detail, march timings, and the practical "how" of concentrations and pursuits. For the wiki it is the authoritative grounding for all Napoleonic hub content, the [[corps-system]], [[manoeuvre-sur-les-derrieres]], and [[napoleonic-warfare]] pages, and the event reconstructions in [[events/napoleonic-wars]]. Later scholarship may adjust numbers or emphasize coalition learning/logistics more, but Chandler's reconstructions of what happened on campaign and why the system succeeded or failed remain the reference standard. Cross-volume tensions with political sources (e.g. CMH IX) are minimal on operational sequence.
