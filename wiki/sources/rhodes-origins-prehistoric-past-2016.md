# Origins: The Search for Our Prehistoric Past

Frank H. T. Rhodes, *Origins: The Search for Our Prehistoric Past* (Ithaca:
Comstock Publishing Associates / Cornell University Press, 2016). A single-volume
popular synthesis tracing the history of life from its origin through human
emergence, interwoven with the history of the sciences that reconstructed that
story. Rhodes (1926–2020) was an invertebrate paleontologist, ninth president of
Cornell University, and author of the earlier Penguin volume *Evolution of Life*
(1976), on which several figures here draw.

## Scope and Character

Seventeen chapters move from the methods of reconstructing the past (dating,
classification) through the major episodes of life's history — the origin of
life, the invertebrate and vertebrate radiations, the conquest of land and air,
the rise of mammals, human origins — and close with chapters on extinction and
on the discovery and mechanism of evolution. The bulk of the book treats
pre-human deep time and lies outside this wiki's human-historical framework; its
distinctive contribution **to the wiki** is concentrated in three areas, all
historiographical or directly bearing on Deep/Early Prehistory:

1. **The discovery of deep time and the measurement of geological age** (ch. 2):
   superposition (Steno), biostratigraphy and the first geological map (William
   Smith), the named geological systems, the long contest over the age of the
   Earth (Ussher's biblical chronology; Kelvin's cooling estimate; Becquerel's
   1896 discovery of radioactivity; Holmes and radiometric dating), resolving to
   ~4.55 billion years.

2. **Human origins** (ch. 14): the partnership of Louis and Mary Leakey, Olduvai
   Gorge, the Laetoli footprints, and the hominin sequence from *Australopithecus*
   through *Homo sapiens*, with the four traits (bipedalism, toolmaking, language,
   culture) used to define humanity.

3. **Extinction and the discovery of evolution** (chs. 16–17): Cuvier's
   establishment of extinction as real, the "Big Five" mass extinctions, the
   K–Pg impact-vs-volcanism debate, and the Darwin–Wallace formulation of
   natural selection (1858–59) with its documented evidence (Galápagos finches,
   peppered moth, fossil lineages).

## Reliability and Use

As a popular synthesis on extremely well-studied ground, the book is used here in
**light-touch** mode: it underwrites framework and historiography pages rather than
generating fine-grained event pages. Where it touches the wiki's existing
prehistoric coverage (the Leakeys, australopithecines, the hominin sequence) it
confirms rather than revises the [[periods/deep-prehistory|Deep Prehistory]] and
[[periods/early-prehistory|Early Prehistory]] accounts. Its figures for the age of
the Earth, the dates of the five mass extinctions, and the hominin chronology
reflect 2016 consensus and should be read against more recent specialist work.
The book is candid about uncertainty — the unresolved origin of life, the open
K–Pg cause debate, the contested hominin taxonomy — which makes it a good source
for the *state of debate* rather than for settled novel claims.
