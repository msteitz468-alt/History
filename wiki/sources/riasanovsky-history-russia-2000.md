## Volume Synthesis Note

Nicholas V. Riasanovsky's *A History of Russia* provides a comprehensive, highly balanced secondary synthesis of Russian history, tracing the evolution of East Slavic society from its prehistoric and nomadic roots to the contemporary post-Soviet era. 

The work centers on several key historical dynamics:
* **The Interplay of Geography and Statehood:** Riasanovsky emphasizes how Russia's flat, open geography (the forest and steppe zones) facilitated both vulnerable borders (resulting in the Mongol Conquest) and massive territorial expansion (Siberian conquest, division of Poland).
* **The Evolution of Autocracy and Serfdom:** The text highlights a defining paradox of Russian history: the simultaneous growth of state power and peasant bondage. The consolidation of autocracy under Ivan III, Ivan IV, and Peter the Great was structurally linked to the legal binding of the peasantry (formalized in the *Ulozhenie* of 1649 and expanded under Catherine the Great).
* **Westernisation and Cultural Schism:** The state-directed cultural and technological pivot toward Western Europe under Peter the Great and his successors created a profound social-cultural cleavage between the Westernised elite and the traditionalist masses, a tension later intellectualized in the Slavophile-Westernizer debates of the 19th century.
* **The Modernization and Revolution Cycle:** Riasanovsky details how the refusal of the tsarist regime to match economic modernization (rapid industrialization under Witte and Stolypin) with political reform created structural instability, culminating in the Revolutions of 1905 and 1917.
* **The Soviet Experiment and Post-Soviet Transition:** The narrative traces the rise of the Marxist-Leninist state, its mobilization under War Communism, the tactical retreat of the NEP, the violent transformation of Stalin's rapid industrialization and collectivization, the post-Stalin thaws and stagnation, and the final systemic collapse under Gorbachev. It concludes with Yeltsin's volatile transition, illustrating how Russia's deeply ingrained autocratic and bureaucratic political habits persisted even during periods of radical reform.
