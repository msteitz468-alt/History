# Oxford Handbook of Early Modern European History, Vol. II: Cultures and Power (2015)

The "power and ideas" half of the Handbook — intellectual life, empire, the state, and war —
edited by Hamish Scott. It supplies several major early-modern themes the wiki lacked as dedicated
pages.

## What it contributes

- **[[scientific-revolution]]** — the transformation of natural knowledge, c. 1543–1700 (new page).
- **[[fiscal-military-state]]** — the tax-, debt-, and war-driven growth of the early-modern state
  (Tracy on taxation/state debt; the warfare chapters) (new page).
- **[[court-society]]** — the royal court as the central institution of power and culture (Duindam;
  Elias) (new page).
- Deepens **[[absolutism]]**, **[[balance-of-power]]**, and **[[enlightenment]]**.

## Reading note

Well-trodden region, so light-to-moderate and flagged; the Handbook's value here is the synthesis
of recent scholarship on science, the state, and the court, which fills real gaps in the wiki.
