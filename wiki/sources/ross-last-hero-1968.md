# The Last Hero: A Biography of Charles A. Lindbergh

## Overview
Walter S. Ross's *The Last Hero* (1968) is a comprehensive, objective study of Charles A. Lindbergh. It traces his trajectory from his Minnesota boyhood and early barnstorming to the historic 1927 transatlantic flight, the kidnapping of his firstborn son, his scientific collaboration with Dr. Alexis Carrel, his support for rocket pioneer Robert Goddard, and his pre-WWII isolationist campaigning with the America First Committee.

## Volume Synthesis Note
Ross framing Lindbergh as the "last hero" highlights the transition from individual, solo exploits to collective, institutionalized technological endeavors (exemplified by the space program). The biography maintains a neutral tone, balancing Lindbergh’s heroic status with his stubborn personal isolation and his highly controversial pre-WWII political actions, showing how the same stubborn independence that enabled the Paris flight led to his political isolation.
