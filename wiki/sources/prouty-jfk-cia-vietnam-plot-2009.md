# Prouty, *JFK: The CIA, Vietnam, and the Plot to Assassinate John F. Kennedy* (2009)

L. Fletcher Prouty's *JFK* is a first-person revisionist account arguing that President Kennedy's assassination was a policy coup driven by opposition to his moves to curtail CIA power and withdraw from Vietnam. With a prominent introduction by Oliver Stone (director of the 1991 film *JFK*), the book synthesizes Prouty's experience inside the national security bureaucracy with a broader critique of the "invisible" structures of Cold War power.

## Argument and Evidentiary Character

Prouty (writing from retirement after a career that included liaison work with CIA covert operations) contends that a permanent "High Cabal" of overlapping intelligence, military, banking, and industrial interests had captured significant elements of U.S. policy-making. Kennedy, in this account, threatened that structure in two linked ways: (1) moves to rein in and restructure the CIA after the Bay of Pigs and during the Cuban Missile Crisis aftermath, and (2) concrete steps toward disengagement from Vietnam (NSAM 263 of 11 October 1963 ordering the withdrawal of 1,000 U.S. advisers by end of year, with broader phase-down planning).

Key recurring claims (all attributed to Prouty):
- The "Saigon Military Mission" (under Edward Lansdale and including Lucien Conein) operated as a CIA-directed parallel structure shaping South Vietnamese politics and paramilitary programs outside normal diplomatic or MAAG channels.
- The Strategic Hamlet program and related "pacification" efforts amounted to destructive social engineering ("Genocide by Transfer") that alienated the rural population and served as preparation of the battlefield rather than genuine development.
- Post-assassination policy reversal was immediate and structural: NSAM 263 (JFK) vs. the rapid escalation documents under LBJ (e.g., NSAM 288), with McGeorge Bundy playing a pivotal documented role in the transition paperwork.
- The assassination itself is presented as the decisive "coup d'état" moment that removed the obstacle to expanded war and preserved the covert-action apparatus.
- Broader context: the "invisible war," the military-industrial complex (Eisenhower warning), and media/institutional mechanisms that maintain official narratives.

The book draws on Prouty's personal operational knowledge, declassified documents available at the time, and cross-references to the Pentagon Papers (which Prouty criticizes for omissions). Oliver Stone's introduction endorses the thesis and situates it against the Warren Commission and mainstream media.

**Sourcing and reliability notes (see frontmatter).** This is advocacy memoir, not peer-reviewed scholarship. Claims of a coordinated assassination plot and the precise "High Cabal" motive are interpretive theses quarantined per protocol.
