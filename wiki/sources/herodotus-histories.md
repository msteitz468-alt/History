# Herodotus, *The Histories*

The earliest surviving work of **history** in the Western tradition — Herodotus's own word for his
project, *historiē* ("enquiry, investigation"), gave the genre its name. Composed in Ionic Greek in
the third quarter of the fifth century BCE, it sets out, in its famous first sentence, to record "the
results of the enquiry" so that great deeds of Greeks and barbarians "should not become *aklea*,
without fame," and to explain "the cause of their hostilities" — the **Greco-Persian Wars**. This is
the first item of **Phase 4 (primary sources)** and the first primary-source translation ingested
after the Cambridge reference backbone.

## The Work

The nine "books" (a later Hellenistic editorial division, not Herodotus's own) are organized at their
simplest as the successive reigns of four **Achaemenid Persian kings** — the "red thread" of Persian
imperial expansion that runs through the whole:

| King | Reign | Books |
|---|---|---|
| **Cyrus the Great** | 557–530 BCE | 1 (Croesus and Lydia; the rise of Persia; Babylon) |
| **Cambyses** | 530–522 BCE | 2 (the great Egyptian *logos*) and first third of 3 |
| **Darius I** | 521–486 BCE | rest of 3, and 4 (Scythia), 5–6 (the Ionian Revolt, Marathon) |
| **Xerxes** | 486–465 BCE | 7–9 (the great invasion: Thermopylae, Artemisium, Salamis, Plataea, Mycale) |

Dewald (the editor) stresses that this dynastic spine is often "almost a narrative convenience":
across it Herodotus "constructs a huge road-map of the known human world," digressing constantly into
ethnography, geography and marvels, and binding everything through a "dense web of causal connections
created… by personal reciprocities that span generations and cultures."

## Method and Style

- **Enquiry (*historiē*).** Herodotus claims to give, in each case, "the best version or versions" of
  the past that he could gather "from the most qualified informants" — "it is said," "they say" recur
  hundreds of times. He names a few informants, cites many by city, and reports eyewitness
  investigation (*opsis*) and his own reasoning, doubting, comparing variants, sometimes flatly
  disbelieving (the Neurian "werewolves", 4.105) and sometimes wrongly disbelieving a truth (the
  Phoenician circumnavigation of Africa with the sun on the right, 4.42).
- **Paratactic, "old-style" prose** (Aristotle's "strung-along style"): short, self-contained units
  "like beads on a string," with **ring composition** (an a-b-a sandwiching of digressions) and
  **associative thinking** rather than the tightly argued rhetoric of his successor Thucydides.
  Herodotus, formed *before* the Athenian sophistic revolution, "disguises his authorial control" and
  "lets us draw our own conclusions."
- **Worldview.** A conventional but undogmatic religion (the gods as a "superpersonal strand of
  causation" working through oracle, portent and dream); the linkage of **freedom with poverty and
  smallness, autocracy with wealth and size** (Solon to Croesus; Demaratus to Xerxes; "soft lands
  breed soft men," 9.122); and a deep sense of **balance, limits and reciprocity** — the overreacher
  (Croesus, Cyrus, Cambyses, Xerxes) triggering "regulatory mechanisms that eventually deflate them."
  Critics note the work's "melancholy": its recurring stories of loss.

## Reliability

Herodotus has been contested since antiquity — Thucydides's implicit disdain for *to muthōdes* (the
mythic), Ctesias's contradictions, Plutarch's *On the Malice of Herodotus*, Cicero's "father of
history… full of legends." Modern positions range from the moderate (Thucydidean) doubt about
recovering hard data from oral tradition (three generations being the usual limit of accurate oral
memory) to **Detlev Fehling's** radical charge that Herodotus *invented* his source-citations and is
a "liar," against defenders (Lateiner, Pritchett) who stress his critical apparatus and the cases
where archaeology corroborates him (Scythian burials in Book 4; the merchant Sostratus of Aegina,
4.152). The full debate is on the standing controversy page **[[herodotus-reliability]]**.
