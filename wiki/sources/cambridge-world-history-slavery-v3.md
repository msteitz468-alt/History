# The Cambridge World History of Slavery, Vol. 3: AD 1420–AD 1804 (2011)

The early-modern volume, edited by **David Eltis** and **Stanley Engerman** — the rise of the
Atlantic slave system set inside a global account of coerced labour from West Africa and the
Caribbean to Southeast Asia, China, indigenous America, and the serf-worked estates of East
Central Europe.

## What it contributes

- **[[creolization]]** — the concept of New World cultural formation among the enslaved (new
  page).
- Enriches **[[atlantic-slave-trade]]** and **[[plantation-economy]]** with the volume's global
  comparative framing (white indentured servitude, indigenous American slavery, Southeast Asia).
- Enriches **[[second-serfdom]]** (East Central European manorialism as a parallel early-modern
  coerced-labour regime) and **[[slavery]]**.
