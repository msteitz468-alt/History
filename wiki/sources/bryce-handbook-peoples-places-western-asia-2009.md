# The Routledge Handbook of the Peoples and Places of Ancient Western Asia (Bryce, 2009)

Trevor Bryce, *The Routledge Handbook of the Peoples and Places of Ancient Western
Asia: The Near East from the Early Bronze Age to the Fall of the Persian Empire*
(London/New York: Routledge, 2009). In collaboration with Heather D. Baker, Daniel T.
Potts, Jonathan N. Tubb, Jennifer M. Webb, and Paul Zimansky.

## What this source is

This is the wiki's **gazetteer-grade reference** for the peoples and places of ancient
western Asia — the geographical sweep "from the Aegean coast of Turkey in the west to
the Indus river in the east," covering Anatolia, Cyprus, Syria–Palestine, Mesopotamia,
Iran, and parts of Central Asia. It is an alphabetical encyclopedia of ~1,500 entries on
kingdoms, countries, cities, archaeological sites, and population groups, each anchored
to specific translated passages of the ancient texts and (for sites) to the excavation
record. It is descriptive and reference-oriented, not a narrative argument; its value to
the wiki is breadth of coverage and per-place precision rather than a thesis.

Because the ancient Near East is already one of the wiki's strongest regions (built from
CAH, [[sources/liverani-ancient-near-east-2014|Liverani]], [[sources/potts-archaeology-of-elam-2016|Potts]],
and the standing Mesopotamia/Anatolia/Levant scaffold), this was a **focused reference
ingest** (per the hybrid-depth rule): the Handbook was used to (a) fill genuine page-level
gaps where Bryce is the natural anchor and (b) enrich existing pages with his source-anchored
specifics, rather than to mine all 1,500 entries.

## The Historical Overview

Bryce prefaces the gazetteer with a ~30-page **Historical Overview** (pp. xli–lvi) — a
tight single-author synthesis of the whole region from the Sumerians to Darius III. It
moves through the Early Bronze Age (Sumerian city-states → [[akkadian-empire|Akkad]] →
[[third-dynasty-of-ur|Ur III]] → [[elam|Elam]]), the Middle Bronze Age (the [[amorites|Amorite]]
kingdoms: [[shamshi-adad-i|Shamshi-Adad's]] "kingdom of Upper Mesopotamia," [[iamkhad|Yamhad]],
Qatna, [[eshnunna|Eshnunna]], [[hammurabi|Hammurabi's]] Babylon), the Late Bronze Age (the
[[hittites|Hittite]] empire, [[mitanni|Mitanni]], the [[kassites|Kassite]] "Middle Babylonian"
kingdom, the rise of Middle [[assyria-middle|Assyria]], the system of Great Kingdoms ended by
the [[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|collapse]] c. 1200), the Iron Age ([[neo-hittite-states|Neo-Hittite]]
kingdoms, [[phoenicians|Phoenicia]], the [[arameans|Aramaeans]], [[hamath|Hamath]], the
[[neo-assyrian-empire|Neo-Assyrian empire]] and its rival [[urartu|Urartu]], [[mannaea|Mannaea]],
[[phrygians|Phrygia]], [[lydia|Lydia]], and Cyprus), and the Neo-Babylonian and Persian
periods ([[neo-babylonian-empire|Nebuchadnezzar II]], the [[medes|Medes]], and the
[[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid Persian empire]] from [[cyrus-the-great|Cyrus II]] to
[[darius-i|Darius I]] and the fall to [[alexander-the-great|Alexander]] in 330). This
Overview is the most useful single piece of running prose in the volume and underpins much
of the cross-period linking below.

## Reliability and use

Bryce's Handbook is a **descriptive reference baseline**: where it and an interpretive
source disagree, the interpretive source (Liverani, Potts, CAH) carries the argument and
Bryce supplies the factual/topographic/text-citation scaffolding. It is especially strong
on Anatolia (Hittites, Luwians, Phrygia, Lydia, the western Anatolian Greek/Carian/Lycian
coast) and on the excavation histories of named sites. It honestly registers chronological
uncertainty and the major source-critical debates (the "Median empire," the Israelite united
monarchy, the extent of Iron Age Luwian settlement in Syria), making it safe to cite without
over-committing to contested reconstructions.
