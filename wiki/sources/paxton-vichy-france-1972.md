# Paxton — Vichy France: Old Guard and New Order, 1940–1944 (Knopf, 1972)

The book that remade the historiography of the German occupation of France.
Against the postwar French consensus that Pétain's regime had played a double
game — shielding France while de Gaulle wielded the sword — Paxton used the
captured German diplomatic and military archives to show that **collaboration
was a French proposal, repeatedly offered to a largely indifferent Germany**,
and that the National Revolution was an indigenous French reactionary project,
not something dictated from Berlin. Vichy is analyzed not as a parenthesis but
as the revenge of the losers of France's internal quarrels of 1934–37.

## Argument summary

- **Prologue** — "This is not another book about why France fell. It is about
  what Frenchmen decided to do next." The armistice was a choice among viable
  alternatives (North Africa above all); the 10 July 1940 vote (569–80)
  reflected near-unanimous opinion, not a Laval coup; and the "shield" and
  "double game" legends are trial-defense constructions the German archives
  contradict.
- **Part I** — Collaboration was a French initiative, offered through three
  channels at once and escalated through Montoire, Darlan's Grand Design, the
  Paris Protocols, and the 14 July 1941 note verbale, to a Germany interested
  only in exploitation. "There is no doubt, from the German archives, who was
  the suitor and who was the wooed." Pétain was "Marshal Bitte," not "Marshal
  Nein."
- **Part II** — The National Revolution "was not Hitler's project… It tells
  us about France, not Germany": indigenous reaction against the Third
  Republic, including anti-Jewish laws enacted without German demand. On the
  fascism question: nearer the conservative than the fascist pole of the
  radical-right spectrum, yet "in that broader sense, Vichy was fascist."
- **Parts III–IV** — Vichy was "a persistence of the notables" (experts and
  grands corps above all, plus an ex-left contingent), fused by
  antibolshevism; from mid-1941 coercion displaced persuasion, and the
  armistice logic drew Vichy into doing "the Germans' dirty work" —
  culminating in the Milice, the STO, and the deportations.
- **Part V** — The comparative balance sheet destroys the "lesser evil"
  claim: on food, inflation, forced labor (3.3% of population vs. Belgium's
  3.4%), and the Jews, "judged by its fruits, Vichy negotiation was barren…
  A gauleiter would have made many Frenchmen suffer; in the end he might have
  gotten less." Yet in the long run "continuities probably prevail over
  breaks": Vichy's technocracy fed the postwar planned state.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Paxton's book performs, in one volume, three operations the wiki records
separately. First, a **source-criticism revolution**: by treating the
captured German archives as the control on French postwar testimony, it
showed that an entire national memory (shield, double game, Laval-as-scapegoat)
had been built out of trial defenses — Appendix A's "war question of January
1942" is a compact methodological demonstration of how isolated captured
documents mislead, applicable to any document-driven controversy in this wiki.
Second, a **causal inversion**: collaboration ran from Vichy toward Berlin;
the National Revolution ran from French history, not German dictation. Third,
a **comparative verdict**: measured against Belgium and Holland, the shield
protected nothing that Vichy could take credit for.

For this collection's standing fascism debate the book matters doubly: it is
the young Paxton's fascism theory in embryo (ideal type, conservative/fascist
spectrum, "no undiluted fascist regime has wielded power") *and* it supplies
the best-documented case study of ex-left figures inside a radical-right
regime — evidence each side of [[fascism-and-the-political-left]] reads its
own way. *The Anatomy of Fascism* is now ingested
([[paxton-anatomy-of-fascism-2004]]): the mature schema confirms the 1972
formulation — Vichy remains classified authoritarian ("certainly not fascist
at the outset, for it had neither a single party nor parallel institutions"),
now embedded in the five-stage model and the formal
fascism/authoritarianism boundary ([[generic-fascism]]).

Tension flagged with no existing pages; the WWII backbone

The book's acknowledged limits — German-eye view, French archives closed,
Paris collaborationists deliberately peripheral — mark where later
scholarship (Marrus & Paxton on the Jews, Burrin, Jackson) would extend it;
none of those are yet in the collection.
