# Joan of Arc: A History

*Joan of Arc: A History* by Helen Castor (HarperCollins, 2015) is a highly acclaimed biography of Joan of Arc. Unlike traditional accounts that tell Joan's story from childhood onward (essentially "telling the life backwards" from the perspective of her later trials and canonization), Castor begins the narrative in 1415, years before Joan's public appearance. This places Joan squarely within the brutal political and military realities of the Hundred Years' War and the Armagnac–Burgundian civil war, explaining *why* her message of salvation resonated so deeply in a broken France.

## Main Arguments & Contributions

- **Contextual Prioritization:** The book argues that Joan's impact can only be understood by reconstructing the political crisis of France in the 1410s and 1420s. Joan did not enter a vacuum; she entered a country already devastated by civil war, whose disinherited heir (Charles VII) was desperate for spiritual and moral legitimacy.
- **Sacral Legitimacy:** Joan's primary contribution was not tactical or military, but ideological. By crowning Charles VII at Reims, she gave him a sacral legitimacy that the English king Henry VI could not easily replicate, shifting the war's moral balance.
- **Historiographical Retrofitting:** Castor highlights how the records of the 1456 nullification trial, which are often used to write Joan's early life, are deeply colored by hindsight and the political necessity of legitimizing Charles VII's reign.
