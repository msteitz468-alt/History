# The West Point Atlas for the Great War (Greiss ed.)

This is the cartographic companion volume in the West Point Military History Series for the First World War / Great War (1914–1918). It supplies the detailed maps referenced in professional military education for the opening campaigns of 1914 and the subsequent attritional fighting on the Western and other fronts.

The primary value of this source is **visual**. It consists primarily of campaign and tactical situation maps showing unit positions, advances, terrain features, and order-of-battle information at critical points. Text is limited to captions and keys.

## Structure and Coverage (58 pages)

The atlas is organized chronologically and by theater, with emphasis on:
- 1914: Opening moves — Western Front (Frontiers, Marne, etc.), Eastern Front (Tannenberg, Galicia), Serbia, colonial (Tanga).
- Subsequent years: The development of trench warfare, major offensives (Somme 1916 as a key example), and attempts to restore mobility.
- Maps include both broad campaign views and more detailed battle situations, with consistent symbology for Allied/Central Powers forces.

Signature West Point style: clear, professional maps suitable for staff-college analysis of operational art and the challenges of industrial war.

## Historiography / Collection Note

These maps have long served as teaching tools at West Point for understanding the operational problems of 1914–1918. They provide clear visualizations of the scale of the 1914 battles, the stalemate that followed, and the challenges of breakthrough and exploitation. Modern detailed studies sometimes refine specific positions, but the overall campaign pictures remain foundational.

The Bibliotheca Alexandrina now makes selected maps directly available alongside the operational analyses.
