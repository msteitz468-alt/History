# Apollonius of Rhodes, *Jason and the Golden Fleece* (*Argonautica*)

The great epic of Hellenistic Alexandria and **the only Greek epic to survive between Homer and the
Roman era**: the voyage of **Jason** and the **Argonauts** to win the **Golden Fleece**. The tenth
source of this Phase-4 batch (the Epic and Myth group). For the author see **[[apollonius-of-rhodes|
Apollonius of Rhodes]]**.

## The Poem

Four books:
- **Book 1** — the gathering of the heroes (Heracles, Orpheus, the sons of the North Wind…), the
  launch of the *Argo* from Thessaly, and the early adventures (Lemnos, the loss of Heracles).
- **Book 2** — the outward voyage through the Bosporus and Black Sea: the boxing of Amycus, the blind
  seer Phineus and the **Harpies**, and the **Clashing Rocks** (Symplegades).
- **Book 3** — the poem's celebrated heart: at Colchis, **Hera and Aphrodite** send Eros to make the
  princess **[[medea|Medea]]**, the king's sorceress daughter, fall in love with Jason; her inner
  torment between passion, shame and family is the first great **psychological study of erotic love**
  in Western literature. With her magic Jason yokes the fire-breathing bulls and defeats the earth-born
  warriors.
- **Book 4** — the theft of the Fleece and the long, guilt-haunted flight home (Medea's complicity in
  the murder of her brother Apsyrtus, the wanderings past Circe, the Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, and
  Libya), ending at Iolcus.

## A New Kind of Epic

Hunter's introduction places the poem at the center of **Alexandrian poetics**. Apollonius, the
**[[apollonius-of-rhodes|Royal Librarian]]**, writes a full Homeric-scale epic that is nonetheless
deeply post-Homeric: **learned and allusive**, threaded with aetiologies (origins of cults, names and
customs — the scholarly impulse of the Library turned to verse), ironic toward its own heroic
machinery, and centered not on martial *aristeia* but on **psychology and emotion**. Jason is no
Achilles: he is resourceful, anxious, dependent on divine and female help — a hero for a courtly,
self-conscious age. (The famous supposed quarrel with **Callimachus** over the viability of long epic
dramatizes the real aesthetic stakes.)

## Significance

The *Argonautica* is the **hinge between Homer and Rome**. Apollonius's **Medea** — the young woman
destroyed by love — is the direct model for **Virgil's Dido**, and through Virgil the poem shaped the
entire later European epic tradition; it also transmitted the Argonaut and Medea myth to Ovid, Seneca
and beyond. As a document it is the fullest surviving voice of **Hellenistic [[hellenistic-world|
Alexandrian]] literary culture**.
