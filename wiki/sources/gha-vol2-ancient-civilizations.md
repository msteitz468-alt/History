# UNESCO General History of Africa, Vol. II: Ancient Civilizations of Africa

The second volume of the **UNESCO *General History of Africa*** (ed. **G. Mokhtar**, 1981) covers the
continent's **ancient civilizations** — Pharaonic Egypt, Nubia and Kush, Aksum, Carthaginian and Roman
North Africa, the Sahara, and the early Iron Age societies of sub-Saharan Africa — from roughly the third
millennium BCE to the seventh century CE. Written largely by **African scholars** as a conscious
**post-colonial reclamation** of African history, it is the authoritative reference for the
**[[sub-saharan-africa|Gap 2]]** ancient period, deepening the state-level detail that
[[reader-africa-biography|Reader]] treats lightly.

## The volume's argument

Editor Mokhtar frames ancient Africa around the **Nile valley as the great corridor** of the continent —
after the post-2400 BCE desiccation of the Sahara, the privileged route by which goods and ideas moved
between Mediterranean and sub-Saharan Africa. The volume's deeper purpose is **historiographical**: to
treat Egypt, Nubia/Kush and Aksum **as African civilizations**, against the colonial habit of detaching
them from the continent — most provocatively in **Cheikh Anta Diop's** opening chapter arguing the ancient
Egyptians were a **Black African** people (with a UNESCO symposium annex recording the scholarly dispute).
