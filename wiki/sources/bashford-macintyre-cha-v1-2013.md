# The Cambridge History of Australia, Volume 1: Indigenous and Colonial Australia

Edited by **Alison Bashford** (Sydney; later Vere Harmsworth Professor, Cambridge) and
**Stuart Macintyre** (Melbourne), published by Cambridge University Press in 2013 as the
first of two volumes. Volume 1 runs from the deep Indigenous past (continental occupation
~50,000 years) through first European encounters, British colonisation from 1788, convict
society, the nineteenth-century growth of the colonies, and the **Federation of 1901**.
A companion to [[australia|the Australia region hub]], it is a key source for closing the
collection's **Australia/Oceania gap**.

The volume is organised in two parts:
- **Part I (Ch 1–10)** — a chronological narrative from archaeology to Federation.
- **Part II (Ch 11–23)** — thematic deep dives (environment, demography, economy, race
  relations, education, law, religion, science, gender, culture, empire, region, ocean).

A "geographic sensibility" frames the whole: **land** as the central theme shaping
political, legal, and social history, and a reading of Australia within
**Pacific, Asian, maritime, and imperial** worlds rather than in isolation.

## Volume Synthesis Note

The volume's organising argument is a **"geographic sensibility"**: that **land** is the
master theme of Australian history, binding together the legal fiction of [[terra-nullius]],
the [[australian-pastoral-expansion|pastoral occupation]] of the interior, and the
[[australian-frontier-conflict|frontier violence]] and dispossession that occupation
required. Part I narrates this as event (1788 → Federation); Part II shows it pervading
every domain — economy, demography, law, religion, science, gender, and culture. The
through-line is that the [[colonial-australian-economy|colonial economy]] and the liberal
settler polity were built materially on seized Aboriginal land, with
[[settler-colonialism|settler colonialism]] as the structural logic and
[[aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal]] survival and agency a persistent counter-current.

A second, deliberately revisionist move is the volume's **anti-isolationism**: the final
chapters relocate colonial Australia inside "[[greater-britain|Greater Britain]]", the
[[australian-pacific-relations|Asia-Pacific region]], and a [[australia-maritime-world|maritime
world]], refusing the older nationalist frame of a continent apart. The
[[australian-federation-1901|Federation]] that closes the volume is read not as the
repudiation of empire but as its "confident vanguard" — its [[australian-labour-movement|labour]]
politics and White Australia program both bound up with the imperial-racial order.

Against the wiki's existing holdings this volume is the **first substantial backbone for
the Australia/Oceania gap**, complementing [[denoon-cambridge-pacific-islanders-1997|the
Cambridge History of the Pacific Islanders]]: where Denoon centres the islands, Bashford &
Macintyre centre the settler continent, and the two meet on the Pacific labour trade, the
maritime world, and missionary/colonial expansion. Its deep-history chapter also reinforces
[[colonization-australasia-50kbp|the Pleistocene colonization]] material already drawn from
CWH IV, while adding a colonial superstructure the collection previously lacked. The
sharpest live tension it introduces is the **[[australian-history-wars|history wars]]** over
the scale of frontier violence — promoted here to a controversy page.
