# Hitler 1889–1936: Hubris (Ian Kershaw, 1998)

**Source page for Ian Kershaw, *Hitler 1889–1936: Hubris* (1998).** First half of the standard modern scholarly biography.

Kershaw uses the life of Hitler as a lens to examine how an 'absurd, obsessive outsider' could gain and extend absolute power in a modern industrial society. The work deliberately fuses biography with social and structural history. Its most influential contribution is the concept 'working towards the Führer', which explains the cumulative radicalization of the regime without requiring constant direct orders from Hitler.

## Approach and Method

Kershaw, coming from structuralist historiography and studies of popular opinion/dissent, is skeptical of 'great man' explanations yet recognizes Hitler’s indispensable personal role. He employs Weber’s 'charismatic authority' to explain how Hitler’s power was constructed by the expectations and projections of German society and the Nazi movement. Extensive use of newly edited primary sources (speeches, Goebbels diary). Rejects Rauschning. Focus on the 'how' of power: the interaction of Hitler’s worldview, the movement’s dynamics, and the conditions of Weimar Germany.

**Key claims (structured, with grounding quotes + context):**

- **Night of the Long Knives mechanics and immediate consequences (30 June–2 July 1934):** Hitler personally ordered shooting of six SA men at Stadelheim (crosses on list; 'You have been condemned to death by the Führer! Heil Hitler!'). Röhm initially spared due to past services and loss-of-face concerns. In Berlin, Göring activated squads with password 'Kolibri'; victims included Bose, Jung, Strasser, Schleicher + wife, Bredow, Kahr (hacked near Dachau), Klausener (on Heydrich orders), mistaken victims (Schmid, Stempfle). Total documented ~85 (only ~50 SA); estimates 150–200. Röhm shot 1 July at Stadelheim by Eicke and Lippert after refusing suicide (pistol and Völkischer Beobachter left untouched). Hitler's terse announcement. Göring ordered file burn 2 July. 'The blood-lust had developed its own momentum.'

  Quote (Hitler Reichstag 13 July): 'Mutinies are broken according to eternal, iron laws. ... I was responsible for the fate of the German nation and thereby the supreme judge (oberster Gerichtsherr) of the German people… I gave the order to shoot those most guilty of this treason, and I further gave the order to burn out down to the raw flesh the ulcers of our internal well-poisoning...'

- **Cabinet legalization and army/SS gains:** 3 July cabinet (Blomberg: 'resolute and courageous action'; Gürtner: actions 'statesmanlike duty' and legal) passed Law for the Emergency Defence of the State ('The measures taken on 30 June and 1 and 2 July ... are, as emergency defence of the state, legal'). Unanimous. Army celebrated SA demise ('We love him because he has shown himself a true soldier' — Reichenau). SS freed from SA subordination (20 July 1934, responsible to Hitler alone). 'The army’s triumph was, however, a hollow one.'

- **Public reaction:** Overwhelmingly positive inside Germany. 'If only the Führer knew' excused negatives; seen as 'liberation from a strongly felt oppression' and restorer of order. Reports: 'unreserved recognition for the energy, cleverness, and courage of the Führer'; 'The Führer… is not only admired; he is deified.' Sopade: adaptation to violence paved way for 'strong sympathies for summary justice.' Churches silent. Schmitt: 'The Führer Protects the Law.'

- **Hindenburg death and total power (2 Aug 1934):** Hindenburg died; pre-emptive Law on the Head of State (1 Aug, while alive) combined offices (Hitler 'Führer and Reich Chancellor' for all time). Plebiscite 19 Aug: official 89.9% support (pressures acknowledged but 'reflected ... backing ... of the great majority'). 'Today Hitler is the Whole of Germany.'

- **Army oath (2 Aug 1934, initiative Blomberg/Reichenau):** Unconditional loyalty to person of Führer (not constitution/President). 'The distinction between loyalty to the state and loyalty to Hitler had been eradicated.' Fritsch/Beck mixed reactions; Guderian: 'A momentous oath. Pray God...'; majority accepted. 'The army chained itself to the Führer.'

- **Ch. 13 core — 'Working Towards the Führer' (Willikens 21 Feb 1934):** 'It is the duty of every single person to attempt, in the spirit of the Führer, to work towards him. ... the one who works correctly towards the Führer along his lines and towards his aim will ... have the finest reward of one day suddenly attaining the legal confirmation of his work.'

  Mechanism: 'Hitler’s personalized form of rule invited radical initiatives from below and offered such initiatives backing, so long as they were in line with his broadly defined goals. This promoted ferocious competition ... In the Darwinist jungle of the Third Reich, the way to power and advancement was through anticipating the “Führer will”...'

  Quote (Hessen, March 1936): 'The Führer had for outward appearances to ban individual actions against the Jews in consideration of foreign policy, but in reality was wholly in agreement that each individual should continue on his own initiative the fight against Jewry in the most rigorous and radical form.'

  Hitler's self-image: 'I go with the certainty of a sleepwalker along the path laid out for me by Providence.' (14 March 1936)

- **Governmental disorder and Hitler's style:** Cabinet meetings: 12 in 1935, 6 in 1937, none after 5 Feb 1938. Legislation by circulation; Lammers influence; Hitler intervened arbitrarily or ignored. 'A highly modern, advanced state without any central coordinating body and with a head of government largely disengaged from the machinery of government.' Corruption rife (Ley 'Reich Drunkard' examples; funds misused; feudal-style rewards). Access to Hitler key power resource. Hitler hated objections; 'wanted to be the hammer, not the anvil.'

- **SS-police empire (to mid-1936):** Himmler Gestapo Inspector April 1934; full Prussian control post-Röhm; concentration camps expanded (Hitler backed against Gürtner/Frick protests, e.g. 1935 lawyer ban). 17 June 1936: unified Reich police under Himmler (paper subordination to Frick). 'The most powerful agency of repression thus merged with the most dynamic ideological force.' Heydrich; new 'enemies' (Jews primary, Communists, freemasons, gypsies, homosexuals, 'antisocials', Jehovah's Witnesses, sects). Eichmann rise via initiative on 'Jewish Desk'.

- **Party dualism unresolved:** Heß deputy (weak); Ley organizational. No Politburo or senate; Gauleiter personal bonds with Hitler. Party veto rights on legislation (1934) and civil service appointments (1935). Hitler welcomed overlaps; blocked Frick's Reich reform.

- **Foreign policy steps:** Poland non-aggression (26 Jan 1934, Hitler's imprint; 'No parliamentary minister ... could have gone so far'). Deterioration with USSR. Army-party reconciliation speech 3 Jan 1935 (theatrical, suicide threat). Saar plebiscite 13 Jan 1935: ~91% return; 'any lingering doubts ... dispelled.' Hitler: 'no further territorial demands to ... France'; privately exploited.

- **Conscription (16 March 1935):** Hitler decision in Munich (Hoßbach summoned; no direct consult of Fritsch/Beck on size). Pretext French 2-year service + British White Paper. Announced 36 divisions (550k men). Blomberg/Fritsch initially appalled at risk/timing but acquiesced (Blomberg led cabinet 'Heil'). First major foreign step without prior military consultation. Domestic euphoria.

- **Economic strains 1935–36:** Food crisis; raw materials for rearmament down to 1–2 months' supply (Schacht demanded slowdown). Hitler overrode for Darré imports (60m RM seed-oil). Unemployment fears ~2.5m. Four Year Plan (1936) as new direct-to-Hitler agency.

- **Rhineland remilitarization (7 March 1936):** Domestic motives primary per Neurath/Hassell/Hitler (offset food crisis, Church struggle, sagging morale; 'new national slogan to fire the masses'). Foreign opportunity: Abyssinia crisis, Mussolini signals (Stresa 'finally dead'; no opposition to Rhineland action), Anglo-French rift (Hoare-Laval), French military/political reluctance. Hitler gambled on bluff; troops entered. No military retaliation. 'Hitler’s greatest triumph to date.' Reinforced myth; 'sleepwalker' quote. Plebiscite 29 March.

- Social composition on eve of putsch: ~55,000 members by Nov 1923 (35k joined Feb–Nov); 'Around a third were workers, a tenth or more came from the upper-middle and professional classes, but more than half belonged to the crafts, commercial, white-collar, and farming lower-middle class.'
- Decision for putsch: Triumvirate (Kahr, Lossow, Seißer) had pulled back; Hitler chose 8 Nov Bürgerbräukeller meeting to force their hand. 'I took the decision to strike four days earlier.' Pistol shot, announcement of national revolution, four-bullet threat in adjoining room. Ludendorff arrived; Kahr announced regency under duress. Once released they renounced.
- Failure: 9 Nov march; police cordon at Odeonsplatz; 'shots rang out. Who fired the first shot was never fully clarified... fourteen putschists and four policemen lay dead.' Scheubner-Richter killed at Hitler’s side; Hitler injured; fled to Uffing, arrested 11 Nov.
- Trial (26 Feb–1 Apr 1924): Turned into 'political carnival.' Hitler accepted full responsibility and glorified the act while threatening to expose triumvirate complicity in treason and Reichswehr mobilisation. Lenient verdict: 5 years (less time served), fine 200 Gold Marks; deportation rejected because 'Hitler is a German-Austrian. He considers himself to be a German.'
- Landsberg: Initial despair then transformation. Dictated *Mein Kampf* (first vol. July 1925). Adulation cemented self-belief. 'Landsberg was no "Jordan conversion"... it was a matter of adding new emphases to the few basic idées fixes... the concept of a war for "living-space"... rounded off his "world-view".' 'The year that ought to have seen the spectre of Hitler banished for good brought instead... the genesis of his later absolute preeminence.'
- Leaderless time: Fragmentation proved indispensability. Rosenberg as caretaker; squabbles with Ludendorff/Graefe/DVFP merger, Streicher/Esser vs. Strasser, northern Directorate. Hitler refused to commit publicly while imprisoned; equivocation strengthened his position as symbol of unity.
- Release 20 Dec 1924 and refoundation: Despite police/prosecutor opposition and attempts at deportation (Austria refused), parole granted. Refounded NSDAP 27 Feb 1925 in Bürgerbräukeller. Imposed sole leadership and Führerprinzip; no internal democracy. 'H[itler] does not recognize a Reich Leadership, and will never be part of such a soldiers’ council construction.'
- Strasser crisis: Gregor Strasser (Völkischer Block, 'socialist' northern wing) and northern activists resisted Munich clique and sought programmatic merger. Hitler isolated them; Goebbels converted by marathon speech. Crisis resolved in Hitler’s absolute personal mastery by 1926.
- Historiographic note from range: 'The "Hitler-Putsch" was... by no means merely Hitler’s putsch.' Bavarian elite collusion and paramilitary culture supplied the enabling conditions. The putsch + Landsberg experience redirected the movement to the 'legal' path while forging the charismatic Führer party structure.

### Methodological Reflections (Preface + Reflecting on Hitler)
- Kershaw came to Hitler biography 'from the wrong direction' after structuralist work on popular opinion and the 'Bavaria Project' under Martin Broszat; initial reluctance overcome by need to integrate personal and impersonal factors. "What has continued in the writing of the book to interest me more than the strange character of the man ... is the question of how Hitler was possible."
- Central analytical tool: Max Weber’s 'charismatic leadership' — power rooted in perceptions and needs of followers/society, not leader’s intrinsic traits. Quote opening Reflecting: 'Charismatic rule has long been neglected and ridiculed, but apparently it has deep roots and becomes a powerful stimulus once the proper psychological and social conditions are set. The Leader’s charismatic power is not a mere phantasm – none can doubt that millions believe in it.' (Franz Neumann, 1942).
- 'Working towards the Führer' concept originated in a 1934 speech by a Nazi functionary ('Working towards the Führer along the lines he would wish') found in Jeremy Noakes & Geoffrey Pridham documentary collection; adopted as leitmotiv for Ch. 13 and overall approach to how dictatorship operated without constant direct orders.
- Primary sources emphasized: multi-volume edition of Hitler’s pre-1933 speeches/writings; full Goebbels diaries (glass plates, Moscow); critical rejection of Hermann Rauschning’s *Hitler Speaks* as inauthentic ('on no single occasion cited'); caution on memoirs and 'table-talk'.
- Hitler’s century: "no other individual has stamped a more profound imprint"; dictatorship as "paradigm for the twentieth century" reflecting total state claim, repression, media manipulation, ultra-nationalism, racial ideology, and "collapse of modern civilization". "Without him, the course of history would have been different." But "a personalized explanation would be a gross short-circuiting of the truth" — Marx quote on men making history "under given and imposed conditions". Biography must bind personal with impersonal; avoid over-empathy or over-personalizing.

### Early Life, Family, Vienna (Ch. 1–2)
- Birth: 20 April 1889, 6:30 pm, Gasthof zum Pommer, Braunau am Inn (Vorstadt Nr. 219), Easter Saturday, overcast and chilly. Fourth child of Alois’s third marriage; first to survive infancy. In *Mein Kampf* Hitler stressed "providential" border birth for uniting Austria/Germany.
- Father Alois Hitler (b. 1837 Strones as Alois Schicklgruber, illegitimate; name change 1876 to Hitler after legitimation by uncle Johann Georg Hiedler; customs official, social climber to Higher Collector). Authoritarian, pompous, status-proud, strict, humourless, bad temper; devoted to duty and bee-keeping more than family. Married three times; Klara Pölzl (second cousin) after liaison with Franziska Matzelberger.
- Mother Klara Pölzl Hitler: eldest surviving of three from Nepomuk Hüttler’s daughter; worked as maid in Alois household; married 1885 after Church dispensation (second cousins). Affectionate, compensated for distant father. Died of cancer (breast) 21 Dec 1907; Hitler devastated.
- Childhood: frequent moves (Braunau → Passau 1892 → Linz area). Comfortable middle-class material existence (cook, maid, aunt Johanna). School: Realschule Linz; poor performance, left without qualifications; conflict with father over civil service career vs. artistic ambitions. Father died 1903 (stroke). Mother’s death 1907 left 18-year-old Adolf with small inheritance.
- Vienna years (1907–1913): Moved autumn 1907 after second Academy rejection (first rejection 1907; drawings "unsatisfactory"; "I was so convinced that I would be successful that when I received my rejection, it struck me as a bolt from the blue"). Lived in men’s hostels (Brigittenau), painted postcards/watercolours for living, sold via Jewish dealer; periods of homelessness. "Drop-out, the rejected and unrecognized artistic genius in the dingy surroundings of a men’s hostel."
- Antisemitism formation (Vienna critical): Crystallized here from earlier pan-German exposure (Schönerer). "Wherever I went, I now saw Jews, and the more I saw, the more sharply they set themselves apart in my eyes from the rest of humanity." Shifted from social/cultural to biological/racial; Jews as "racial tuberculosis"; linked to Marxism, capitalism, press, prostitution in his mind. Influenced by Karl Lueger (Christian Social mayor, pragmatic antisemitism), Lanz von Liebenfels (Ostara), völkisch pamphlets. Social Darwinist struggle worldview gelled. "The Vienna years were over. They had indelibly marked Hitler’s outlook."
- Munich move 1913: "intensified longing to go to Germany"; left Vienna May 1913 (age 24) ostensibly for artistic reasons but also to evade Austrian military service (liable since 1909, had avoided). Settled in Munich; continued painting; registered as "artist-painter".

### WWI and Immediate Postwar Radicalization (Ch. 3–4 start)
- Augusterlebnis 1914: In Munich; famous Odeonsplatz photo (2 Aug) shows Hitler in exuberant middle-class male crowd celebrating war declaration. "Overpowered by stormy enthusiasm, I fell down on my knees and thanked Heaven..." Enlisted voluntarily in Bavarian List Regiment (16th Bavarian Reserve Infantry); not Austrian army. Served as Meldegänger (dispatch runner/messenger) — relatively safe but exposed; promoted to corporal (Gefreiter).
- Decorations: Iron Cross, Second Class (Dec 1914, recommended by Jewish Lt. Hugo Gutmann); rare Iron Cross, First Class (4 Aug 1918) — "a rare distinction for a corporal".
- 1916 wounded (thigh, Battle of the Somme); 1918 gassed (mustard gas, 14 Oct near Ypres sector); hospital in Pasewalk (Pomerania). Learned of revolution and armistice while recovering; "stab in the back" myth internalized: "I knew that all was lost... What was the pain in my eyes compared to this misery?"
- Decision to enter politics: In Pasewalk, "I had become a politician" against the "November criminals". Returned Munich 21 Nov 1918 to chaotic barracks under soldiers’ councils. Kurt Eisner (Jewish, USPD) Minister President; Eisner assassinated Feb 1919; Räterepublik (councils republic) April 1919 under Communists; bloody Freikorps/Reichswehr "liberation".
- Hitler’s account in *Mein Kampf* laconic and likely obfuscating: claims repelled by councils, sent to Traunstein POW camp, "did nothing" during Räterepublik, fabricated arrest story. Reality: remained in Munich; first counter-revolutionary involvement post-liberation (investigating commission in regiment — "his 'first more or less purely political activity'").
- Discovery of talent (army education role): Retained by Reichswehr (Karl Mayr’s section) as "education officer"/informant to indoctrinate troops against Bolshevism and "Jewish" revolution. Sent to Lechfeld camp (Aug 1919) to address returning POWs. "Herr Hitler especially is, I might say, a born popular speaker who, through his fanaticism and his populist style in a meeting, absolutely compels his audience to take note and share his views." One soldier: "Goodness, he’s got a gob. We could use him." Hitler: "I was offered an opportunity of speaking before a larger audience; and the thing that I had always presumed from pure feeling without knowing it was now corroborated; I could 'speak'."
- Entry into DAP (Sept 1919): Attended meeting of tiny German Workers’ Party (DAP, ~40 members) in Sterneckerbräu as army observer. Spoke against Bavarian separatism; "Goodness, he’s got a gob" — Anton Drexler (DAP founder) impressed. Invited to join; became member #555 (or card shown). "The thing that I had always presumed from pure feeling without knowing it was now corroborated."

### Hitler's Daily Style and Routine (Vienna Men's Home, 1909–1913)
- Hitler resident at the Men's Home (Männerheim) in Meldemannstraße (Brigittenau district) from 9 February 1910. Hanisch (frequented it; registered nearby at Herzstraße; later resident Nov 1912–Mar 1913 under pseudonym Friedrich Walter) provides core eyewitness detail corroborated in part by Karl Honisch (1913 account; "little doubt that it was the same in 1910").
- Earned bare living painting watercolours/postcards: "He had painted, he said, only to earn a bare living and so that he 'could study'. He had not wanted to become an artist..." (disparaging own paintings to Hoffmann 1944 via Schroeder; "in Vienna around 1910 never received more than about (the equivalent of) 12 Reich Marks for a picture"). Proud of architectural drawings instead.
- Routine: read voraciously; political arguments and verbal aggression (e.g., towards Social Democracy). Hanisch asked about future aims in 1909: Hitler "confessed that he did not know what they were".
- Cross-verified anecdotes (Hanisch/Greiner overlap, deemed more reliable): poor state of Hitler's clothing; his support of the Schönerer movement; disturbances caused by his verbal aggression towards Social Democracy. Greiner likely embellished after hearing Hanisch stories in 1930s Vienna.
- Film note: Hanisch claimed Hitler impressed by silent film *The Tunnel* (demagogue stirring masses) ~this period, but film completed 1915; Hitler could not have seen it then.

### Formation of Antisemitism and Worldview (Vienna/Linz context; roots for character and later Jewish policy)
- MK account of "conversion": "Coming from a family more attuned to more cosmopolitan (weltbürgerlich) views, I became an antisemite in scarcely a year through the school of hard reality" (letter to anonymous 'Herr Doktor', 29 Nov 1921). "Wherever I went, I now saw Jews, and the more I saw, the more sharply they set themselves apart in my eyes from the rest of humanity." Shift to biological/racial; Jews as "racial tuberculosis"; linked to Marxism/capitalism/press/prostitution.
- MK specifics: singled out "four Jewish leaders of the working class: Viktor Adler, Friedrich Austerlitz, Wilhelm Ellenbogen, and Anton David" (frequently linked in Viennese antisemitic attacks).
- Linz debate: Hitler (MK, 55) claimed he was not antisemitic in Linz. Counter: stronger emphasis by some (Heer) on antisemitism in Hitler’s school in Linz and "support in the school and town for the antisemitic programme of Schönerer". Bukey: antisemitism in Linz "widespread and pernicious" but "far less significant than anti-Czech feeling". Vienna the crystallizing site.
- Influences noted: Schönerer’s racial antisemitism (Hamann, 344–7); Lueger (pragmatic, Christian Social); Lanz von Liebenfels (Ostara); völkisch pamphlets. Later claimed to Goebbels (Tb Reuth, iii. 1334, 17 Oct 1939) Vienna as place he first became an antisemite. Claimed 'intensively studied' Theodor Fritsch’s *Handbuch der Judenfrage*. "Menacing anti-Jewish atmosphere in Vienna" (Jones, Pulzer).
- Counter-views in sources: Greiner claimed brought antisemitism from Linz; Binion/Joachimsthaler see no significant hatred of the Jews in Hitler before as late as June 1919. Some accounts tie hard Vienna years to anti-Jewish turn (sister Paula surmise; no direct evidence).
- Broader: early exposure via pan-German circles; Social Darwinist struggle framework gelled here. "The Vienna years were over. They had indelibly marked Hitler’s outlook."

### Popular Support and Early Oratorical Appeal
- Linz: "support in the school and town for the antisemitic programme of Schönerer".
- Vienna: shaped by menacing popular antisemitic atmosphere.
- 1919 Reichswehr/DAP phase: Army reports: "Herr Hitler especially is, I might say, a born popular speaker who, through his fanaticism and his populist style in a meeting, absolutely compels his audience to take note and share his views." Listener: "Goodness, he’s got a gob. We could use him." Hitler on discovery: "I was offered an opportunity of speaking before a larger audience; and the thing that I had always presumed from pure feeling without knowing it was now corroborated; I could 'speak'."
- DAP entry meeting (12 Sept 1919, Sterneckerbräu): attendance list ~39 names (Hitler figure 20–25); spoke against Bavarian separatism. Drexler impressed: "Now we have an Austrian. He’s got such a gob" (or "Goschen"). Lotter/Drexler accounts: urged to join "because we could use such people" / "such people we necessarily need".
- Popular mood files (BHStA): references to 'Judenhetze' (Jew-baiting).
- Early support base hints: in Vienna/Munich völkisch and worker-nationalist circles (e.g., Franz Stein, Schönerer admirer targeting workers).

### Power Structure, Personal Style, and 1931 Context (tangential to later periods)
- 1919: Hitler as Reichswehr V-Mann/education officer (Karl Mayr section); unusually respectful address ("sehr verehrter Herr Hitler") from captain to corporal. Reports on DAP meetings preserved in files (BHStA, BAK).

**Key verbatim-grounded claims (Range 04 only):**

**30 January 1933 Appointment and immediate symbolism**
- 'Power had indeed not been “seized”. It had been handed to Hitler, who had been appointed Chancellor by the Reich President in the same manner as had his immediate predecessors.' Torchlight procession: Hindenburg watched respectfully; Hitler received 'wild acclaim'. Stylized immediately as 'day of the national uprising'; Hitler preferred 'takeover of power' to underline formal legality.
- 'After 30 January 1933, Germany would never be the same again.' 'It signified the start of that astonishingly swift jettisoning of constraints on inhumane behaviour whose path ended in Auschwitz, Treblinka, Sobibor, Majdanek...'

**Enabling Act (23 March 1933)**
- 'With 441 votes to the ninety-four votes of the Social Democrats, the Reichstag, as a democratic body, voted itself out of existence.' 'The “Act for the Removal of Distress from People and Reich” – the Enabling Act – went into effect the next day.' Hitler: 'we appeal in this hour to the German Reichstag to grant us that which we could have taken anyway.'
- Promises (later broken): Reichstag and Reichsrat existence not threatened; Reich President rights untouched; Länder not abolished; Churches rights not reduced. Zentrum support after verbal assurances and under armed SA/SS/Stahlhelm guard around the Kroll Opera House. SPD leader Otto Wels spoke courageously for 'humanity, justice, freedom, and socialism'; Hitler replied savagely: 'Germany will become free, but not through you.'
- Atmosphere: 'The atmosphere for their opponents, particularly the SPD deputies, was menacing. A giant swastika dominated the chamber. Armed men from the SA, SS, and Stahlhelm guarded all exits and surrounded the building.'

**Gleichschaltung (spring/summer 1933)**
- 'During the spring and summer of 1933, Germany fell into line behind its new rulers. Hardly any spheres of organized activity, political or social, were left untouched by the process of Gleichschaltung.' 'What is striking is not how much, but how little, Hitler needed to do to bring this about.'
- Reich Governors law 7 April 1933: 'the sovereignty of the individual states was decisively undermined.' Hitler anxious to counter 'party revolution' from below (especially Bavaria/SA-SS HQ). 'Elbow-power, as usual, was what counted.'
- 'By the autumn, the Nazi dictatorship – and Hitler’s own power at its head – had been enormously strengthened.'

**SA problem and Röhm vs Reichswehr (early 1934)**
- SA outlived purpose: 'Everything had been predicated on the attainment of that single goal [winning power].' Unemployment higher among SA; 'Talk of a “second revolution”... was... bound to find strong resonance among rank-and-file stormtroopers.'
- Röhm memorandum Feb 1934 demanding national defence for SA; Hitler 28 Feb: 'the Wehrmacht must be the sole bearer of weapons of the nation.' Röhm overheard (reported by Lutze): 'What the ridiculous corporal declared doesn’t apply to us. Hitler has no loyalty and has at least to be sent on leave. If not with, then we’ll manage the thing without Hitler.'
- Papen Marburg speech 17 June 1934 criticised 'permanent condition of chaos'; Hitler at Gera: 'the fist of the nation that is clenched and will smash down anyone who dares...'

**Purge mechanics (30 June 1934) — verbatim**
- Decision forced by 21 June audience: Blomberg on steps: if government incapable, 'the President would declare martial law and hand over control to the army.' Hindenburg: 'to bring the revolutionary trouble-makers finally to reason.'
- 28 June at wedding: immediate strike decided. 30 June early hours Munich: Hitler ripped badges from Schneidhuber and Schmid, shouting 'You are under arrest and will be shot.'
- Bad Wiessee ~6.30 a.m.: 'Hitler, followed by members of his entourage and a number of policemen, stormed up to Röhm’s room and, pistol in hand, denounced him as a traitor... Edmund Heines... was found in a nearby room in bed with a young man.'
- Brown House midday: 'Hitler was beside himself, in a frenzy of rage. One of those present later recalled spittle dribbling from Hitler’s mouth... He spoke of “the worst treachery in world history”... He would have them all shot.' Heß pleaded to shoot Röhm.
- 'the blackest day of my life' (Hitler on Munich SA demonstration).

**Justification and army reaction (quotes in range)**
- Hitler Reichstag 13 July 1934: 'I gave the order to shoot those most guilty of this treason, and I further gave the order to burn out down to the raw flesh the ulcers of our internal well-poisoning…'
- Reichenau guidelines 28 Aug 1934: 'The Reich Chancellor kept his word when he nipped in the bud Röhm’s attempt to incorporate the S A in the Reichswehr. We love him because he has shown himself a true soldier.'

**Consolidation to total power**
- 'The rapidity of the transformation that swept over Germany between Hitler’s takeover of power on 30 January 1933 and its crucial consolidation and extension at the beginning of August 1934, after Reich President Hindenburg’s death and following close on the major crisis of the “Röhm affair”, was astounding...' Within a month civil liberties extinguished; within two months Reichstag surrendered powers (Enabling Act); within four months trade unions dissolved; less than six months all opposition parties gone; January 1934 Länder sovereignty formally abolished; 30 June 1934 Night of the Long Knives. 'This process of “coordination” (Gleichschaltung) was for the most part undertaken voluntarily and with alacrity.'
