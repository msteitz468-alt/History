# The Cambridge Ancient History, Vol. I, Part 1: Prolegomena and Prehistory

First volume of Phase 2 (the Cambridge Ancient History antiquity backbone).
Third edition, 1970, edited by I. E. S. Edwards (Keeper of Egyptian Antiquities,
British Museum), the late C. J. Gadd, and N. G. L. Hammond. Entirely rewritten
from the 1st edition and roughly twice its length, reflecting the postwar
explosion of Near Eastern prehistoric archaeology (Çatalhöyük, Jericho, Jarmo,
Ras Shamra, Khirokitia) and the arrival of radiocarbon dating ("Libby" method,
explicitly flagged by the editors as still imprecise). Originally published as
separate fascicles, which is why chapters read as free-standing specialist essays
rather than an integrated narrative.

## Editorial Framing (from the Preface)

- The volume self-consciously rests on **new excavation** and **radiocarbon
  dating** as the two engines of revised knowledge; the editors note C-14 "is
  still lacking in precision."
- **Chronological consistency** across many civilizations was imposed top-down:
  the editors commissioned Hayes (Egypt), Rowton (Western Asia) and Stubbings
  (Aegean) to fix a uniform dating scheme (Ch. VI), with contributors free to
  dissent in footnotes — they "gratifyingly" rarely did. This is the CAH's
  characteristic method: a refereed house chronology.
- Coded bibliographic referencing (§ section, book, page) is introduced here as
  an innovation.

This Preface, together with the apparatus chapters (I–II, IV, VI), supplies the
material for the series-methodology concept page mandated by CLAUDE.md.

## Volume Synthesis Note

**What the volume argues.** CAH I.1 is less a narrative than an *apparatus*: it
assembles, evidence-class by evidence-class, the foundations on which the history of
the ancient Near East and Mediterranean can be built — geological deep time, the
reconstructed postglacial environment, the Palaeolithic/Mesolithic record, the
evidence of language, the physical anthropology of early populations, and the
machinery of chronology — before tracing the regional Neolithic and Chalcolithic
from the Levant and Anatolia through Mesopotamia, Egypt, Cyprus and the Aegean. Its
implicit thesis is **continuity**: the city grows out of the village and the shrine
(the eighteen temples of [[eridu|Eridu]]), writing out of accounting (the
Protoliterate tablets of [[uruk|Uruk]]), the state out of long cultural sequences
(Badarian→Naqada in [[predynastic-egypt|Egypt]]). Nothing in this world is sudden.

**What it adds to the wiki.** Phase 1 (CWH) gave a *global, thematic, recent* frame;
CAH I.1 begins the work of thickening the **Near Eastern and Mediterranean** strand
beneath it with regional depth. Its most durable contributions here are not facts
(many are superseded) but **structures the wiki had been missing**: a documented
[[postglacial-environment-near-east|environmental stage]] for Near Eastern
agriculture; the standing controversies on [[indo-european-origins|Indo-European
origins]] and [[mesopotamian-chronology|Mesopotamian chronology]]; a
[[ancient-near-eastern-chronology|chronology-methods]] page; the first dedicated
[[jericho|Jericho]], [[catalhoyuk|Çatal Hüyük]] and [[eridu|Eridu]] place pages; and
[[predynastic-egypt|Predynastic Egypt]] and [[aegean-neolithic|Aegean Neolithic]]
processes that seed the next CAH volumes.

**Cross-volume tensions.** The volume's chief friction with already-ingested sources
is **vintage, not substance**. Its hominin taxonomy ("Pithecanthropines"),
uncalibrated absolute dates, and Mellaart's "shrine"/cult readings of Çatal Hüyük
are superseded by the science behind [[christian-cwh-v1-2015|CWH Vol. I]] and later
fieldwork; these were captured as *historiography* (the 1960s state of the field)
rather than allowed to overwrite current pages, and no `[CONTRADICTION]` flags were
raised because no *live* scholarly dispute is at stake. The one genuinely productive
tension is methodological: Butzer's 1970 anti-determinism and Albright's rejection of
the Indo-European "horse-nomad" model show that several debates the wiki had treated
as recent (environmental determinism; the steppe hypothesis) are in fact decades
old — a useful corrective now recorded on the relevant controversy pages.

**Where this leaves Phase 2.** The methodology page
([[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]]) and the chronology apparatus are now in
place to receive the rest of the CAH. The next volume — **CAH Vol. I, Part 2: Early
History of the Middle East** — will carry the story from these Neolithic/Chalcolithic
foundations into the Early Bronze Age (Early Dynastic Mesopotamia, the Egyptian Old
Kingdom), updating [[chalcolithic|Chalcolithic]] and [[early-bronze-age|Early Bronze
Age]] and the new place pages created here.
