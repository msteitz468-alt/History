# Cold War on Five Continents: A Global History of Empire and Espionage

Alfred W. McCoy (Bloomsbury/Dispatch Books, 2024). A global narrative history of
the Cold War organized around covert action, espionage, and the contest of empires,
told through the "men on the spot" — the operatives, station chiefs, and local
clients whose decisions, McCoy argues, shaped the fate of nations more than the
speeches of statesmen.

## Structure

Preface + 16 chapters in four parts, plus an appendix ("Men on the Spot,
1946–91"):

- **Ch. 1** — The Cold War's Long Shadow (framework; Mackinder geopolitics)
- **Part One — The Struggle over Europe and its Colonial Empires**
  - Ch. 2 USA vs. USSR · Ch. 3 Europe—Cockpit of Conflict (Greek Civil War) ·
    Ch. 4 War in Korea · Ch. 5 Fighting Communism in Southeast Asia ·
    Ch. 6 End of Empire in the Middle East (Iran 1953) · Ch. 7 Covert Action in
    the Caribbean (Guatemala 1954, Bay of Pigs) · Ch. 8 The Cold War Comes to
    Africa (Congo/Lumumba)
- **Part Two — One, Two, Many Vietnams**
  - Ch. 9 The Vietnam War · Ch. 10 Jakarta Is Coming (Indonesia 1965 mass
    killings) · Ch. 11 CIA Coup in Chile (1973)
- **Part Three — Surrogate Wars in the Third World**
  - Ch. 12 The Struggle for Southern Africa (Angola) · Ch. 13 Civil Wars in
    Central America · Ch. 14 Afghanistan's Imperial Graveyard
- **Part Four — Close of the Cold War**
  - Ch. 15 Collapse of the Soviet Empire · Ch. 16 Toward a New Cold War

## Curator's Note

McCoy is authoritative on the operational mechanics of covert action (his lifelong
specialism) and on the documented human cost of Cold War proxy conflicts. His
distinctive value to the wiki is the covert-action layer — CIA/KGB tradecraft,
coups, and clandestine networks — that narrative diplomatic histories (Leffler &
Westad, Gaddis) treat more thinly. Where his interpretive framing (empire, covert
causation, the geopolitical New Cold War thesis) exceeds the documented record, it
is quarantined to controversy positions per the source protocol.
