# Chancellorsville (Stephen W. Sears, 1996)

**Source page for Stephen W. Sears's acclaimed operational and narrative history of the 1863 Chancellorsville Campaign and Battle (30 April–6 May 1863).** The book is grounded in rich primary testimony (OR, letters, diaries, Hooker Papers) and provides the definitive modern treatment of Joseph Hooker's turning movement, Robert E. Lee's most remarkable victory, Stonewall Jackson's famous flank march and mortal wounding, the intense fighting around the Chancellor house, Sedgwick's operations at Salem Church, and the campaign's aftermath. Sears portrays Chancellorsville as the most complex campaign of the war, the most intense concentrated fighting, Lee's greatest triumph—and the most disheartening Union defeat—with the irreplaceable loss of Jackson turning victory hollow for the Confederacy. It balances high-level operational design and command decisions with granular eyewitness accounts.

### Range 1 Extraction (setup, command, plans)
**Sears-Chancellorsville-R1-01: LincolnLetterToHookerJan1863**  
**Grounding quote:** "I think it best for you to know that there are some things in regard to which, I am not quite satisfied with you... During General Burnside’s time of command, you have taken counsel of your ambition... I have heard... of your recently saying that both the Army and the Government needed a Dictator... Only those generals who gain successes, can set up dictators. What I now ask of you is military success..."  
**Locator:** "Joe Hooker Takes Command"  
**Analysis:** Lincoln explicitly framed expectations and constraints on Hooker after the Burnside revolt. The letter shaped Hooker’s reforms and campaign planning.
