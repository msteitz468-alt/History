# UNESCO General History of Africa, Vol. VIII: Africa since 1935

The eighth and **final** volume of the **UNESCO *General History of Africa*** (eds. **Ali A. Mazrui & C.
Wondji**, 1993) covers **Africa from the Second World War to the early 1990s** — the catalytic war, the
**struggle for independence**, the post-colonial predicaments of **underdevelopment and nation-building**,
the flowering of modern **African culture**, **Pan-Africanism** and the **liberation of Southern Africa**.
It completes the wiki's **Gap 2** ingest and connects the African story to the wiki's already-strong modern
leg ([[decolonization]], [[cold-war]], [[contemporary]]).

## The volume's argument

Mazrui's volume frames the period as the **recovery of African sovereignty and its limits**. The Second
World War accelerated nationalism; decolonization restored political independence ("seek ye first the
political kingdom," after Nkrumah); but **economic dependence (neo-colonialism)** persisted, and
**nation-building** in artificial colonial states proved fraught (coups, one-party rule, civil wars). The
volume is unusually rich on **socio-cultural** renaissance (literature, religion, the arts) and on
**Pan-Africanism** as the unfinished project — of continental integration and of the final liberation of
**Southern Africa** from white-minority rule.
