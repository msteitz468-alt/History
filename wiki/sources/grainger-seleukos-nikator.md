# Seleukos Nikator: Constructing a Hellenistic Kingdom (John D. Grainger)

The standard biography of **[[seleucus-i-nicator|Seleucus I "Nikator"]]** — the Macedonian officer who,
over forty years, assembled the largest of the kingdoms of the Diadochi, the **[[seleucid-empire|Seleucid
empire]]**. The seventh source of the Phase-9 batch.

## The Argument

Grainger traces, stage by stage, how Seleucus **constructed a kingdom** out of the wreck of Alexander's
empire — "adding province to province, kingdom to kingdom":

- from a minor Companion to **satrap of Babylon**, expelled and then restored (his return in 312/311
  marking the Seleucid era);
- his eastward campaigns securing the **Iranian** provinces, and the famous settlement with
  **Chandragupta Maurya** (ceding the far north-west of India for 500 war-elephants);
- the destruction of **Antigonus the One-Eyed** at the **battle of Ipsus (301)**, which won him **Syria**
  and made him the dominant Successor;
- his great programme of **city-foundation** — **Seleucia-on-the-Tigris**, **Antioch** and the Syrian
  tetrapolis — that anchored Greek-Macedonian power in the Near East;
- and his final victory over **Lysimachus** at **Corupedium (281)** and his death, murdered as he crossed
  into Europe to claim Macedon itself.

Grainger stresses the **administrative and strategic intelligence** required to hold so vast and diverse
a realm together, and presents Seleucus as the true **architect of the Hellenistic Near East**, not a
mere lucky survivor.

## Significance

The book gives the wiki its detailed account of **how a Hellenistic kingdom was actually built** — the
mechanics of conquest, settlement and consolidation behind the abstraction "successor kingdom" — and the
fullest study of the founder whose dynasty ruled Syria and the East for two centuries.
