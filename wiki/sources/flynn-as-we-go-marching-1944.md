# Flynn, *As We Go Marching* (1944)

John T. Flynn's most analytical book: a three-part study arguing that fascism is
not an alien import but a policy syndrome — deficit spending, autarky, corporatist
economic planning, militarism, and imperialism, run under one-man government —
that grew out of the fiscal desperation of parliamentary states, and that the
United States under the [[new-deal|New Deal]] was assembling the same syndrome
("the good fascism"). Part One traces the Italian road from Giolittian deficit
politics to [[mussolini-benito|Mussolini]]; Part Two the German road from
Bismarckian state socialism through [[weimar-republic|Weimar]] to
[[adolf-hitler|Hitler]]; Part Three applies the eight-point definition to
[[roosevelt-franklin-d|Roosevelt]]'s America.

Companion piece to [[hayek-road-to-serfdom-1944|Hayek's Road to Serfdom]]
(same year, parallel convergence thesis from a different method) and precursor
to Flynn's own [[flynn-roosevelt-myth-1948|The Roosevelt Myth]] (1948). Central
document for [[fascism-and-the-political-left]] and
[[roosevelt-and-new-deal-revisionism]].

## Evidentiary character

Flynn's professional strength is fiscal history, and the book's most durable
material is quantitative: Italian debt figures (15.77bn lire 1914 → 92.6bn
1922; Salvemini's exposure of Mussolini's off-book installment-contract
subterfuge, 148.6bn by 1934), German figures (the 4.47bn-mark 1871 French
indemnity spent and re-borrowed by 1875; Weimar deficits of 20.8bn marks
1926–31 and Parker Gilbert's exposure of the "double budget"; contested Nazi
debt estimates from Stolper's 40bn to a 1943 Berne report's 203bn), and US
deficit tables 1933–38. Interpretation is uniformly directed at the
convergence thesis. **Known factual error:** Flynn dates Hitler's
chancellorship to January 25, 1933 (standard: January 30) — noted, not
silently corrected, on [[nazi-seizure-of-power-1933]]. His separation of
fascism from anti-Semitism ("quite different evils though they may be found
together") and his claim that Hitler's policy produced the war rather than
war-desire producing Hitler are interpretive positions to be attributed, not
adopted.

## Volume Assessment

The book is the fullest contemporary statement of the economic-structural
theory of fascism, and its Italy/Germany fiscal chapters remain useful
attributed documentation. Its weaknesses are the mirror image: a definition
broad enough to indict most wartime states, near-total silence on racial
doctrine and paramilitary violence as constitutive features (explicitly
bracketed by Flynn), and a 1944 prediction of creeping American fascism whose
dictatorial terminus did not arrive — though defenders note the permanent
military-Keynesian state he forecast did. Read alongside
[[hayek-road-to-serfdom-1944]] (same year, convergent thesis, different
mechanism) and against [[paxton-anatomy-of-fascism-2004]] (the modern
consensus Flynn's definition contrasts with).
