# The Cambridge History of Southeast Asia, Vol. 2 (Tarling, ed., 1992)

The modern half of the Cambridge History of Southeast Asia — the nineteenth and twentieth centuries,
from the establishment of the European colonial regimes through nationalism, the Japanese occupation,
decolonization, the independent nation-states, and contemporary regionalism (ASEAN). **Thematically
organized**: each of the 10 chapters sweeps across the whole region (Burma, Siam/Thailand, the Malay
world, the Dutch East Indies/Indonesia, French Indochina/Vietnam-Laos-Cambodia, the Philippines) for a
single theme. This fills the wiki's long-standing modern-Southeast-Asia gap (previously only
pre-modern polities + the Vietnam War).

Ingested via the **deployed-subagent strategy**. Body content: lines ~**350–33,000**; later lines are
consolidated bibliography.

## Volume Synthesis Note

Volume 2 supplies the wiki's first sustained **modern Southeast Asia** — previously the region had only
pre-modern polities ([[indianization-of-southeast-asia|Indianization]], Angkor, [[southeast-asian-age-of-commerce|the age of commerce]])
plus the [[vietnam-war|Vietnam War]]. The 54 pages now trace the whole arc: the establishment of the
European [[colonialism-in-southeast-asia|colonial regimes]], the colonial state and its
[[plural-society|plural societies]] and export economies, the rise of [[southeast-asian-nationalism|nationalism]]
and religious anti-colonialism, the [[japanese-occupation-southeast-asia|Japanese occupation]] and
[[decolonization-southeast-asia|decolonization]], the independent nation-states, and contemporary
[[asean-and-regionalism|regionalism]].

Because the volume is **thematic** (each chapter sweeps the whole region), the wiki backbone is the six
nation pages, with the chapters' analytic categories hung off them. Two structural themes recur: the
contrast between **Siam's uncolonized path** ([[siam-chakri-dynasty]], [[thailand]]) and the colonized
remainder; and the **plural-society legacy** ([[plural-society]]) of colonial labour migration that
shaped post-independence communal politics from Malaysia to Burma. The volume pairs with the
already-ingested [[tarling-cambridge-history-southeast-asia-v1-1992|Vol. 1]] (to c. 1800) to give SE Asia
end-to-end coverage.
