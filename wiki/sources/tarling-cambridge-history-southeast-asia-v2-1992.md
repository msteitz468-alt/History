# The Cambridge History of Southeast Asia, Vol. 2 (Tarling, ed., 1992)

The modern half of the Cambridge History of Southeast Asia — the nineteenth and twentieth centuries,
from the establishment of the European colonial regimes through nationalism, the Japanese occupation,
decolonization, the independent nation-states, and contemporary regionalism (ASEAN). **Thematically
organized**: each of the 10 chapters sweeps across the whole region (Burma, Siam/Thailand, the Malay
world, the Dutch East Indies/Indonesia, French Indochina/Vietnam-Laos-Cambodia, the Philippines) for a
single theme. This fills the wiki's long-standing modern-Southeast-Asia gap (previously only
pre-modern polities + the Vietnam War).

Ingested via the **deployed-subagent strategy**. Body content: lines ~**350–33,000**; later lines are
consolidated bibliography.
