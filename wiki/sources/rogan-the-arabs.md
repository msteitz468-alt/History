# Eugene Rogan, *The Arabs: A History* (2009)

The standard one-volume narrative of the **modern Arab world**, c. 1500–2009 — the spine for a
region the wiki has barely covered after the medieval Islamic centuries. Its task here is to
deepen [[middle-east-modern]] and build out the modern Arab/Middle East story.

## Rogan's thesis

Rogan argues that **"modern Arab history begins with the Ottoman conquest of the Arab world in
the sixteenth century,"** when the Arabs first came to be ruled by an external power — and that
ever since, the Arabs have **negotiated the modern age "largely by the rules set by the
dominant powers of the day."** He divides modern Arab history into four eras by the dominant
power: the **Ottoman**, the **European colonial**, the **Cold War**, and the present age of
**US domination and globalization**. The Arabs were "most empowered when there was more than
one dominant power" to play off against the others; each transition opened opportunity but also
deeper foreign intrusion. He frames the present as an "**Arab malaise**" (Samir Kassir) of
powerlessness, set against two earlier eras of greatness: the first five Islamic centuries and
the nineteenth-century cultural renaissance, the ***nahda***. Rogan stresses Arab **agency** —
the Arabs are "responsible for their successes and failures alike," not passive victims — and
the recurring gap between Western promises of "liberation" and the reality of occupation
(Napoleon 1798, Maude in Baghdad 1917, Bush 2003).

## Volume Synthesis Note

*The Arabs* **closed Phase C** and built out the wiki's thinnest modern region — the Arab world
after the medieval Islamic centuries. It created five discrete pages
([[arab-nationalism]], [[colonial-partition-of-the-arab-world]], [[arab-israeli-conflict]],
[[gamal-abdel-nasser]], [[political-islam]]) and deepened the [[middle-east-modern]] anchor and
three others.

Rogan's organizing thesis runs through every page: modern Arab history is the story of a people
**living "by the rules set by the dominant powers of the day"** across four eras — Ottoman,
European-colonial, Cold War, and the present US/globalization age — and of the recurring bid to
**escape those rules and master their own destiny**. The wiki's new pages trace that arc: the
[[colonial-partition-of-the-arab-world|colonial partition]] (the WWI "three promises" and the
mandates) substituted European for Ottoman rule and fixed the borders the Arabs still inhabit;
secular [[arab-nationalism|Arab nationalism]] (born of the *nahda*) and its champion
[[gamal-abdel-nasser|Nasser]] briefly seemed to rewrite the [[cold-war|Cold War]] rules in the
Arabs' favour (Suez 1956); the repeated defeats of the
[[arab-israeli-conflict|Arab–Israeli conflict]] — above all 1967 — broke that project; and from
the 1970s **[[political-islam|political Islam]]** rose as the successor language of opposition
and identity. The recurring note is the "**Arab malaise**" (Kassir) — powerlessness measured
against past greatness — tempered by Rogan's insistence on Arab **agency**.

Where it touches the rest of the wiki it joins the Ottoman backbone (Imber/Finkel/Mikhail/Kinross)
to its Arab-provincial and post-Ottoman sequel, and links the modern Middle East to
[[decolonization]], the [[cold-war|Cold War]], and [[political-islam|the global resurgence of
religion]]. With Rogan, **Phase C (Ottoman / post-1500 Islamic world) is complete**: the wiki
now has both the Ottoman imperial spine and the modern Arab world. The campaign next turns to
**Phase D (China)**.
