# Eugene Rogan, *The Arabs: A History* (2009)

The standard one-volume narrative of the **modern Arab world**, c. 1500–2009 — the spine for a
region the wiki has barely covered after the medieval Islamic centuries. Its task here is to
deepen [[middle-east-modern]] and build out the modern Arab/Middle East story.

## Rogan's thesis

Rogan argues that **"modern Arab history begins with the Ottoman conquest of the Arab world in
the sixteenth century,"** when the Arabs first came to be ruled by an external power — and that
ever since, the Arabs have **negotiated the modern age "largely by the rules set by the
dominant powers of the day."** He divides modern Arab history into four eras by the dominant
power: the **Ottoman**, the **European colonial**, the **Cold War**, and the present age of
**US domination and globalization**. The Arabs were "most empowered when there was more than
one dominant power" to play off against the others; each transition opened opportunity but also
deeper foreign intrusion. He frames the present as an "**Arab malaise**" (Samir Kassir) of
powerlessness, set against two earlier eras of greatness: the first five Islamic centuries and
the nineteenth-century cultural renaissance, the ***nahda***. Rogan stresses Arab **agency** —
the Arabs are "responsible for their successes and failures alike," not passive victims — and
the recurring gap between Western promises of "liberation" and the reality of occupation
(Napoleon 1798, Maude in Baghdad 1917, Bush 2003).
