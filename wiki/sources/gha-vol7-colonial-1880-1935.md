# UNESCO General History of Africa, Vol. VII: Africa under Colonial Domination, 1880–1935

The seventh volume of the **UNESCO *General History of Africa*** (ed. **A. Adu Boahen**, 1985) covers the
**colonial conquest and the high colonial period** — the **Scramble** and its wars, the systems of colonial
rule, the **colonial economy** and its social upheavals, and the birth of modern **African nationalism**. It
is the **colonial-era reference for [[sub-saharan-africa|Gap 2]]**, the counterpart from the African side to
the wiki's [[imperialism|imperialism]] and [[scramble-for-africa|Scramble]] pages, bridging GHA Vol. VI (the
independent nineteenth century) and Vol. VIII (decolonization).

## The volume's argument

Boahen's volume is built around the framework of **"African initiatives and reactions."** It rejects the
old colonial narrative of passive "pacification," insisting that Africans **actively resisted, negotiated,
collaborated with, and adapted to** conquest and colonial rule — from armed primary resistance (Samori, the
Maji Maji, the Herero, Ethiopia's victory at Adwa) to the religious, economic, and ultimately **nationalist**
responses of the colonial decades. It treats colonialism as a **brief, violent, and exploitative episode**
whose "balance sheet" was overwhelmingly negative for Africa — but one in which Africans were agents, not
merely victims.
