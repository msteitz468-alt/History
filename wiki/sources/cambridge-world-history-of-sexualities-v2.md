# The Cambridge World History of Sexualities, Vol. 2: Systems of Thought and Belief (2024)

The second volume surveys sexuality through the **belief and thought systems** that gave it
meaning across world history — the volume that supplies the comparative content behind the
[[history-of-sexuality]] field framework of Vol. 1.

## What it contributes

- **[[sexuality-in-world-belief-systems]]** — the comparison page: how the major
  civilizational and religious traditions construed and regulated sex.
- Enriches **[[history-of-sexuality]]** (concrete cross-cultural cases for the
  constructionist argument).

## Argument

Read comparatively, the volume confirms the constructionist thesis with evidence: there is
**no single "natural" sexuality**, but a wide range of frameworks — the Greco-Roman ethics
of **active/passive and status**; the South Asian integration of **kama** (pleasure) as a
legitimate aim alongside dharma and asceticism; the Chinese **cosmological-medical**
(yin–yang) approach; and the **Abrahamic** procreation-centred regulation in its Jewish,
Christian, and Islamic variants — alongside the diverse systems of Africa, Oceania, and the
Americas, and the modern reframings by science, Marxism, feminism, and post-colonial
critique.
