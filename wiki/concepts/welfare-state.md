# Welfare State

The twentieth-century state form in which government assumes broad
responsibility for citizens' economic security — social insurance (pensions,
unemployment, health), redistributive taxation, public education and housing,
and countercyclical economic management. Emic from the 1940s (a
self-description of post-war British and Scandinavian politics); etic as the
standard comparative category for post-1945 Western political economy
(Esping-Andersen's "three worlds" typology being the most influential etic
framework).

This page's critique section currently draws on Part III of
[[hayek-constitution-of-liberty-1960|Hayek's *Constitution of Liberty*]] (1960),
the classic contemporary liberal indictment — recorded as attributed positions.

## Genealogy

- **Bismarckian social insurance (1880s)** as conservative state-building:
  Hayek traces the conception to the German academic *Sozialpolitiker*
  ("socialists of the chair") from c. 1870, first implemented by
  [[otto-von-bismarck|Bismarck]] — compulsory insurance *through a unitary
  state-run organization*, a model exported internationally. [[prussia|
  Prussia's]] 1891 progressive income tax (0.67%–4%; Gneist protested it
  betrayed equality before the law) was the first modern progressive income
  tax; Britain and the US followed in 1910/1913 with top rates of 8¼% and 7%
  that reached 97½% and 91% within thirty years.
- **New Liberalism and Britain**: Fabians, Pigou, Hobhouse, Lloyd George; the
  [[beveridge-report-1942]] and the post-war National Health Service.
- **The United States**: [[roosevelt-franklin-d|Franklin Roosevelt]] "followed
  in the footsteps of Bismarck and Lloyd George" — the [[new-deal]], the Social
  Security Act (1935), and the 1937 Supreme Court "general welfare" rulings
  (Helvering v. Davis, Steward Machine Co. v. Davis, Carmichael v. Southern
  Coal).
- **Post-1945 consolidation** across Western Europe and the Anglosphere
  (cf. [[australian-welfare-state]]); Hayek's 1960 diagnosis was that
  "doctrinaire socialism" had been discredited ("Marxism was killed in the
  Western world by the example of Russia") and the welfare state had succeeded
  it as the live program — redistribution being "much easier to push" than
  discredited nationalization. He quotes Labour's R. H. S. Crossman conceding
  that nationalized corporations had produced "a new feudalism" threatening
  social democracy.

## Hayek's Critique (attributed position)

**Aims vs. means.** Hayek explicitly accepts a broad set of welfare *aims* as
compatible with freedom — provision for the indigent and disabled, public
health, a rising guaranteed minimum of sustenance, state financing (not
provision) of education, even state initiative in social insurance: "There is
no reason why the volume of these pure service activities should not increase
with the general growth of wealth." The test is coercion and monopoly: welfare
activities threaten freedom when, "though they are presented as mere service
activities, they really constitute an exercise of the coercive powers of
government." He distinguishes achievable **limited security** (an equal minimum
for all) from **absolute security** (a guaranteed relative standard), the
latter requiring the coercive apparatus of "social justice" — which, pursued
consistently, "requires an allocation of all resources by a central authority"
(the bridge to the [[socialist-calculation-debate]] and
[[road-to-serfdom-thesis]]). Monopolistic single-channel provision also
forecloses discovery: "the controlled single-channel development... may well
become the chief obstacle to future improvement" (see [[knowledge-problem]]);
the endpoint is the "household state." The chief operational danger is
administrative discretion — quoting Roscoe Pound on expert administrators —
linking the critique to the decline of the [[rule-of-law]].

**Sector analyses (chs. 18–24):**
- *Unions*: legal privileges (Britain's Trade Disputes Act 1906 — Dicey:
  "freedom from civil liability for the commission of even the most heinous
  wrong"; the US Clayton Act 1914 and Norris-LaGuardia Act 1932) enable
  coercion primarily *of fellow workers*; unions raise members' wages only by
  restricting labor supply at other workers' expense; under Keynesian
  full-employment doctrine their wage push forces validation by inflation.
  Hayek proposes rule-of-law reforms (no mass picketing, no closed shop), not
  prohibition — and records that he abandoned his *Road to Serfdom*-era
  equation of enterprise and labor monopoly as equally dangerous.
- *Social security*: pay-as-you-go pensions transfer current output rather than
  drawing on accumulated capital, creating an irreversible intergenerational
  burden; "insurance" was retained in the 1935 US act by "a stroke of
  promotional genius"; the Beveridge health vision ("whatever medical treatment
  he requires... without an economic barrier") founders on the absence of any
  objective standard of medical need — and Beveridge's plan "was promptly
  turned into [a redistribution instrument] by the politicians." Instead of
  Beveridge's five giants, the apparatus risks new ones: "inflation, paralyzing
  taxation, coercive labor unions, an ever increasing dominance of government
  in education, and a social service bureaucracy" with arbitrary power.
- *Progressive taxation*: from Guicciardini's warning against Florence's
  *decima scalata* (c. 1538) through classical-liberal rejection (Turgot,
  McCulloch, Mill: "a mild form of robbery") to explicit adoption in the
  Communist Manifesto (1848); the marginal-utility justification collapsed with
  interpersonal comparison, leaving "a frankly political postulate"; steep top
  rates yield only a small revenue fraction (~8½% of US federal revenue),
  their function symbolic — sustaining the illusion that the burden falls on
  the wealthy.
- *Money*: inflation is "the welfare state's characteristic vice" — union wage
  push, full-employment policy, and pension burdens all press toward it, and
  its effects generate demand for further intervention ("a vicious circle");
  "inflation is never an unavoidable natural disaster." He endorses Simons's
  rules-over-authorities, deems gold-standard restoration impractical, favors a
  commodity reserve standard, and blames Keynes's "fundamentally antiliberal
  aphorism, 'in the long run we are all dead.'" A 1913–1958 comparison of the
  real value of fixed-interest savings: Switzerland 70%, US/Canada ~58%, UK
  ~50%, Germany 37%, France/Italy 11–12%.
- *Housing*: rent control (a [[first-world-war-1914|WWI]] emergency measure
  retained for decades) did more harm than any measure except inflation;
  broad public housing replicates its allocative coercion; Britain's Town and
  Country Planning Act 1947 (100% development charge) conferred "despotic
  power" and was repealed within seven years.
- *Agriculture*: price supports and acreage controls — usually introduced by
  conservatives — delay necessary structural shift and "if generally applied,
  would lead to a totalitarian control of all economic activity." (He notes,
  via Schlesinger, that New Deal allotment plans drew on interwar German
  agricultural controls.)
- *Education*: endorses Friedman's voucher financing against state provision
  (Mill's epigraph: state education as "a despotism over the mind"); notes the
  irony of Humboldt building Prussian state education; criticizes Tawney's
  egalitarianism and warns meritocratic state selection creates "an official
  ranking of people into a hierarchy" — quoting C. P. Snow that the welfare
  state made the British social pattern "not less rigid but more so."

**Empirical coda**: Hayek contrasts stagnating egalitarian Britain and
Scandinavia with dynamic post-war West Germany, Belgium, and Italy, and argues
Britain's deliberate leveling "abdicat[ed] its leading position."

## The 1979 refinement (*Law, Legislation and Liberty* III, ch. 14)

Hayek's late statement is more concessive on ends and more radical on means than 1960. Concessions: rejection of the "minimal state" label; tax-financed collective goods, disaster and epidemic response, and the minimum-income floor affirmed as "a wholly legitimate protection against a risk common to all, … a necessary part of the Great Society" — explicitly severed from any claim of "just" distribution ([[social-justice]]). Radicalizations: service functions devolved to local authorities forced "to compete for residents"; no service a government *monopoly* (education via vouchers; transport, communications, social insurance, and money itself opened to competition); and public finance indicted as "developed in an endeavour to outwit the taxpayer" — citing Britain's 50-percent-plus government share of national income against poll evidence that ~80% of Britons wanted income tax cut. Trade-union privilege is the sharpest target: labour monopoly, unlike much enterprise monopoly, "is due to the coercive suppression of competition"; "incomes policy" cannot work because no authority can know the correct relative prices; the resulting rigidity is "the 'English disease'." Galbraith's countervailing power and the Myrdal/Galbraith "complete organization" thesis are rejected on Olson's logic — comprehensive organization is impossible, so organized groups persistently exploit "unorganized and unorganizable groups" (consumers, taxpayers, the aged). Attributed position, as above.

## Limitations and Critiques of the Concept (and of Hayek's critique)

- The category lumps very different regimes (universalist Scandinavian,
  corporatist continental, residual Anglo-American — Esping-Andersen).
- Periodization dispute: continuous growth of the modern state vs. a distinct
  post-1945 settlement.
- Against Hayek: the predicted slide from welfare state to serfdom did not
  materialize in Scandinavia or Britain; his 1950s stagnation contrast has been
  read as conjunctural (post-war reconstruction effects) rather than
  structural; defenders (Titmuss, later Esping-Andersen) argue universal
  services build social solidarity Hayek's framework cannot register. His
  aims/means concessions have also drawn fire from his own side (Hamowy) as
  admitting most of what the critique excludes.
