# Viereckschanzen

## Definition and Scope

Viereckschanzen (singular Viereckschanze) are a distinctive form of rural sanctuary that appeared in temperate Europe (central France eastwards to southern Germany and Bohemia) in the first century BCE. They consist of square ditched enclosures, often with shafts or pits containing votive deposits. They coincide chronologically with the development of early states (oppida, civitas structures) and represent new ideological/ritual expressions supporting the emerging political order.

## Characteristics and Context

"From central France eastwards to southern Germany and Bohemia, a new form of rural sanctuary, comprising a square ditched enclosure (Viereckschanze) with shafts containing votive deposits, appeared in the first century BC at the same time as the early states were developing" (Ch. 10, ~p. 319, cache ~2890-2900).

They are distinct from earlier defended sites or hillforts (primarily economic/defensive) and from the monumental urban or royal centres. They underscore the role of ideology and ritual in legitimating new centralized polities alongside practical institutions like coinage, writing for records, and magistrates.

In southern England, contemporary political centres (e.g. Colchester) contained the first recognizable temples, paralleling the phenomenon.

## Significance

The appearance of these specialized ritual sites alongside oppida and state institutions marks a shift in how social and political power was projected and maintained: from (or in addition to) elite grave display and competitive exchange to monumental building and votive practice tied to the new order. "This ideological underpinning of society also found expression in the prevailing burial rites" and sanctuaries (Ch. 10, ~p. 319, cache ~2870s).

They are part of the broader "use of ideology to project and promote the new political order" that accompanied state formation.

**Grounded claim from Champion et al. 1984 range lines 18501-22500 (Ch. 10):** Exact quote and context above. They are synchronous with "the early states were developing" and part of monumental building programs that in eastern Europe took different forms (e.g. Dacian sanctuaries at Sarmizegethusa, Thracian Seuthopolis).
