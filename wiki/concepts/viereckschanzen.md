# Viereckschanzen

## Definition and Scope

Viereckschanzen (singular Viereckschanze) are a distinctive form of rural sanctuary that appeared in temperate Europe (central France eastwards to southern Germany and Bohemia) in the first century BCE. They consist of square ditched enclosures, often with shafts or pits containing votive deposits. They coincide chronologically with the development of early states (oppida, civitas structures) and represent new ideological/ritual expressions supporting the emerging political order.

## Characteristics and Context

"From central France eastwards to southern Germany and Bohemia, a new form of rural sanctuary, comprising a square ditched enclosure (Viereckschanze) with shafts containing votive deposits, appeared in the first century BC at the same time as the early states were developing" (Ch. 10, ~p. 319, cache ~2890-2900).

They are distinct from earlier defended sites or hillforts (primarily economic/defensive) and from the monumental urban or royal centres. They underscore the role of ideology and ritual in legitimating new centralized polities alongside practical institutions like coinage, writing for records, and magistrates.

In southern England, contemporary political centres (e.g. Colchester) contained the first recognizable temples, paralleling the phenomenon.

## Significance

The appearance of these specialized ritual sites alongside oppida and state institutions marks a shift in how social and political power was projected and maintained: from (or in addition to) elite grave display and competitive exchange to monumental building and votive practice tied to the new order. "This ideological underpinning of society also found expression in the prevailing burial rites" and sanctuaries (Ch. 10, ~p. 319, cache ~2870s).

They are part of the broader "use of ideology to project and promote the new political order" that accompanied state formation.

**Grounded claim from Champion et al. 1984 range lines 18501-22500 (Ch. 10):** Exact quote and context above. They are synchronous with "the early states were developing" and part of monumental building programs that in eastern Europe took different forms (e.g. Dacian sanctuaries at Sarmizegethusa, Thracian Seuthopolis).

## Additional Detail from Bogucki & Crabtree 2004 (Range 03, dedicated Viereckschanzen entry)

**Cultural affiliation, date, distribution:** "The Viereckschanzen are associated with pre-Roman Celtic populations living at the end of the Iron Age who produced a material culture known as the Late La Tène culture. Precise dendrochronological (tree-ring dating) measurements of oak timbers preserved in wells at four Viereckschanzen in southern Germany (Riedlingen, Fellbach-Schmiden, Plattling-Pankofen, and Pocking-Hartkirchen) range across a 130-year period, from 181 to 51 B.C. These dates correspond to the La Tène C2 and D1 horizons..." "Southern Germany, including the states of Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg, is the main focus of the distribution of Viereckschanzen, where approximately five hundred enclosures have been identified. Significantly smaller numbers of sites are present in the Czech Republic and Moravia (to the east) and in northern Switzerland (to the south)."

**Description:** "The classic Viereckschanze is identifiable by its standardized form and construction... A typical enclosure was created by excavation of a steep-sided, V-shaped ditch in a square, rectangular, or slightly trapezoidal form. The excavated soil was placed on the inside edge of the ditch, forming a simple earthen berm or rampart. Ditches were maintained through periodic re-excavation. There is some evidence that a wooden palisade or other superstructure was placed along the top of the rampart to increase the height of the walls. Although the ditch was continuous, a single opening was left in the rampart. This opening was usually in the eastern or southern side of the enclosure, but never to the north. Access to the interior required construction of a wooden causeway over the ditch, which led to a small timbered gatehouse..." "Dimensions of the enclosures range from less than 50 meters to more than 100 meters on a side, but most sites are between 80 and 100 meters across and enclose about 1 hectare." Some have internal divisions, external annexes, or pre-dating rectilinear palisades. Interiors: post-built structures, wells (often wood-lined), pits, hearths; diversity (some short-term, others intensive occupation). "Well shafts... and distinctive buildings with wraparound porches or ambulatories are known from a number of excavated sites..."

**Physical setting and function:** Found on stream terraces, loess plains, upland slopes/ridges; often near springs or with internal wells. "The ditch and wall suggest that defense was an important function of a Viereckschanze; however, the topographic placement of many enclosures shows that they were not effective fortifications... approximately 40 percent of known enclosures are located on low-lying or sloped terrain, where their interiors would have been vulnerable to attack by ranged weapons... Viereckschanzen generally do not take advantage of the most strategically valuable terrain, so it is likely that defense was not a primary motive for their construction." Close geographic relationship to undefended rural settlements (nearby or mutually visible); some within larger settlement complexes. Often reference older monuments (e.g., tumulus cemeteries; dramatic example at Hohmichele).

**Material culture and interpretation:** Common pottery, metalwork (tools/weaponry/jewelry rare at these vs. larger sites; some iron hoards), glass, coins, animal bone (normal species proportions, one site high horse). "No Viereckschanzen have yielded deposits of animal parts that compare to patterns of ritual consumption and sacrifice at Belgic sanctuaries like Gournay-sur-Aronde." Human remains infrequent. Famous unique finds (e.g., stone head at Msˇecké Zˇehrovice, wooden carvings at Fellbach-Schmiden). History: 19th/early 20th c. debate (Roman storehouses/forts, Celtic stockyards, farms, cultic places, or fortifications); modern consensus as rural sanctuaries with votive deposits, contemporaneous with oppida and early states. "From central France eastwards to southern Germany and Bohemia, a new form of rural sanctuary, comprising a square ditched enclosure (Viereckschanze) with shafts containing votive deposits, appeared in the first century BC at the same time as the early states were developing."
