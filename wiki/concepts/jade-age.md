# The Jade Age

A **periodization label** that [[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook (2017)]] apply to
the **Middle and Late Neolithic of the [[east-asian-heartland-region|East Asian Heartland]]
together (c. 3500–2000 BCE)** — "a uniquely East Asian variant of the Middle and Late Neolithic
era." The defining feature is that elite cultures devoted major human and material resources to
the precise crafting of **jade (nephrite)** for **symbolic and ritual**, not utilitarian,
purposes — making worked jade the long ceremonial prelude to the bronze ritual order of the
[[shang-dynasty|Shang]].

## Why "Jade," Not Metal

Nephrite **cannot be cut with metal tools**; it must be shaped by **abrasion** — grit of quartz
or garnet worked with silk or horse-tail bowstring "saws" and bamboo tools in a grit slurry. The
labor embedded in a finished jade was therefore immense, and its possession a pure marker of
status and ritual authority. Almost all ancient Chinese jade is **nephrite**; jadeite (from
Myanmar) was rarely used before c. 1800 CE. Native-copper trinkets appear at some
[[yangshao-culture|Yangshao]] sites, but true metallurgy lay far in the future — so the era's
prestige economy was jade, not metal.

## The Cultures and Their Forms

- **[[hongshan-culture|Hongshan]]** (c. 3500–2500 BCE, the Near Northeast) — the earliest Jade
  Age culture; C-shaped jade **"pig-dragon"** pendants; the Niuheliang "Goddess Temple."
- **[[liangzhu-culture|Liangzhu]]** (c. 3200–2300 BCE, Lower Yangzi) — the classic ritual jades:
  the ***cong*** (a prismatic tube, circle-within-square) and the ***bi*** (flat pierced disk),
  carved with a stereotyped **"metamorphic" deity-mask** figure. In elite burials *bi* were set
  near the head and *cong* draped over the body.
- **[[longshan-culture|Longshan]]** (c. 2600–2000 BCE, Yellow River) — absorbed the Liangzhu
  deity-mask iconography; long jade blade "scepters" traded as far as [[sanxingdui|Sanxingdui]].

## Cosmological Afterlife

Later Chinese cosmology read the *cong* as a symbol of **Earth** and the *bi* as a symbol of
**Heaven** (consistent with the round-Heaven / square-Earth scheme), and the Lingjiatan jade
plaque's "compass rose" of directional arrows points to early Heartland interest in cosmic
orientation. The Liangzhu mask-décor is, "with several intermediate steps," ancestral to the
**taotie-style mask décor of [[shang-dynasty|Shang]] bronze ritual vessels** ~2,000 years later
— the thread that ties the Jade Age to the Bronze Age. Jade body-coverings of the elite
culminate, in the early empire, in the famous **jade burial suits** sewn with gold wire.

## Status as a Concept (contested)

"Jade Age" is a useful **heuristic sub-period**, not a universally adopted term; it overlaps
what other schemes call the Late Neolithic and [[chalcolithic|Chalcolithic]] and is descriptive
of elite material culture rather than a distinct techno-economic stage. [[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|
Liu (2004)]] treats the same societies through **[[settlement-archaeology|settlement and mortuary
patterning]]** (cyclical [[chiefdom|chiefdoms]]) rather than through a jade-defined era — a
complementary, not contradictory, reading.
