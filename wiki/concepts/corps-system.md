# Corps System

The **corps system** (corps d'armée) was the organizational foundation of Napoleonic operational art: the division of the army into several semi-independent combined-arms corps, each capable of marching and fighting alone for a day or more until the others could concentrate.

## Definition and Scope

Each corps typically comprised two or more infantry divisions, attached cavalry, and artillery — "a miniature army." Under the *marche en bataillon carré* (square march) or dispersed advance, corps moved on separate routes within supporting distance. This multiplied the army's speed and foraging capacity ("living off the country") while allowing rapid concentration at the decisive point.

## Key Features (Napoleonic)

- Self-sufficiency for short independent action
- Common doctrine and training allowing interoperability
- Directed by a single commander (Napoleon) via the *directed telescope* and chief of staff (Berthier)
- Enabled the *manoeuvre sur les derrières* and the search for annihilation battles

The system reached maturity with the Grande Armée of 1805–1809 and was one of the principal reasons for French superiority until the coalitions adapted and the scale of operations outran the command structure.
