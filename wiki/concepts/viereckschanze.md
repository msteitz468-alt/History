# Viereckschanze

**Viereckschanze** (plural Viereckschanzen) designates a class of rectilinear bank-and-ditch enclosures characteristic of the Iron Age in central and western temperate Europe. They are frequently interpreted as ritual or cult sites on the basis of votive deposits, although some scholars argue for a domestic or multi-functional role.

## Definition and Scope

The glossary entry in Bogucki & Crabtree 2004 supplies the standard definition:

> "Viereckschanze (pl. Viereckschanzen): A rectilinear enclosure defined by a bank and ditch. The frequent presence within the enclosure of pits and wells containing votive deposits fosters interpretation of these monuments as ritual enclosures, although some argue that they were habitations as well." (range_08:60–67)

These monuments are typically square or rectangular, bounded by a bank and external ditch, and occur in landscapes associated with Hallstatt and especially La Tène period settlement. Their size varies; many enclose areas of several hectares. Internal features often include deep pits or shafts interpreted as ritual wells or offering places.

## Interpretive Debate

The glossary explicitly notes the contested nature of function:
- Primary interpretation: ritual or cult enclosures, supported by "frequent presence within the enclosure of pits and wells containing votive deposits."
- Alternative view: some may have served as habitations or farmsteads.

This tension reflects broader debates in Iron Age archaeology between ritual specialization and everyday settlement function. Many Viereckschanzen are isolated or located on the edges of settlement systems, supporting a specialized role. Their distribution overlaps with oppida and hillfort landscapes but they are distinct in plan and often lack the massive defensive scale of major fortified sites.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Linked to [[concepts/hallstatt-culture]] and [[concepts/la-tene-culture]] as part of the material and ideological repertoire of Iron Age temperate societies.
- Distinct from but sometimes co-occurring with [[concepts/oppida]] (larger urban-style defended settlements).
- Comparable to other enclosed ritual spaces (e.g. certain British Iron Age sanctuaries or continental cult sites).
- Construction technique often involves wattle-and-daub or timber elements within the bank (see glossary "wattle and daub").

## Geographic and Chronological Scope

Primarily attested in southern Germany, Switzerland, eastern France, and adjacent areas during the later Hallstatt and La Tène periods (roughly 6th–1st centuries BCE). The form is less common or absent in northern Europe and the Mediterranean core.

## Historiography

Viereckschanzen were identified through aerial photography and systematic field survey in the twentieth century. Early interpretations leaned heavily toward ritual because of the structured votive deposits; more recent work has explored possible multifunctional or seasonal use. The glossary's balanced phrasing ("fosters interpretation... although some argue") reflects the state of knowledge circa 2004.

## Collection Coverage Note

The encyclopedia treats Viereckschanzen as a key diagnostic feature of Iron Age "barbarian" ritual and settlement in temperate Europe. Strong on central European examples; thinner on Insular parallels. The glossary definition provides the authoritative reference point for the collection.
