# Helots

The **Helots** (Greek *heilotai*) were the subjugated, state-owned agricultural population
of [[sparta|Sparta]], most conquered in the archaic-period wars of expansion into Laconia
and, especially, [[events/third-messenian-war-464bce|Messenia]]. Unlike chattel slaves
owned by individual masters elsewhere in Greece, helots were collectively owned by the
Spartan state, bound to work the land (*kleroi*) allotted to individual Spartan citizens,
and organized as an ethnically distinct, hereditarily unfree population rather than a
generic category of "the enslaved" — a status ancient and modern scholars alike have
found difficult to classify precisely against comparative categories of slavery and serfdom.

## Control and Revolt

Because helots vastly outnumbered the Spartan citizen body, their control shaped Spartan
society profoundly: the militarized Spartan citizen (*homoios*) lifestyle, the *krypteia*
(a sanctioned secret-police-like practice of terrorizing and killing helots), and Sparta's
historic reluctance to commit its army far from home are all frequently explained in
ancient and modern scholarship as responses to permanent fear of helot revolt — a fear
realized dramatically in the [[events/third-messenian-war-464bce|Third Messenian War]]
(c. 464–455 BCE). Modern historians debate how far to extend the comparative category of
"helot-like" populations (Sparta's helots, Cretan serf classes, the Ottoman *devshirme*,
and other forms of state-controlled unfree labor) without flattening real institutional
differences.
