# Interior lines

Interior lines (or operating on interior lines) is Jomini's signature operational advantage: the ability, from a central position, to strike separated enemy forces in succession before they can concentrate or support one another.

## Jomini's Formulation

From a secure base and central position within the theater, the commander uses shorter interior lines to achieve local superiority against one enemy force, defeat it, and then shift against the next. This is the classic application of massing force at the decisive point when the enemy is divided.

Jomini contrasts this with exterior lines (operating from multiple directions against a central enemy), which he generally views as riskier unless the attacker has overwhelming resources or superior mobility.

The concept is closely tied to:
- Choice of theater and base
- Selection of decisive points
- Lines of operations and maneuver

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Core element of [[jominian-strategy]].
- Frequently paired with turning movements and concentration.
- Later applied (and critiqued) in analyses of Frederick the Great, Napoleon, Moltke, and 19th-century campaigns.
- Contrasts with raiding/persisting distinctions in other theorists (e.g. [[sources/jones-the-art-of-war-in-the-western-world-1987]]).

## Primary Grounding from Jomini's Text

"Interior lines of operations are those adopted by one or two armies to oppose several hostile bodies, and having such a direction that the general can concentrate the masses and maneuver with his whole force in a shorter period of time than it would require for the enemy to oppose to them a greater force." "Exterior lines lead to the opposite result." From a central position, strike separated enemy forces in succession. Maxims: with equal forces on same frontier, single interior preferable to double/exterior. Historical validation throughout (Archduke Charles at Würzburg, Napoleon campaigns). Strategic lines of maneuver can be interior and decisive even if temporarily exposing the primary line (if calculated with rapidity).

Full maxims, observations, and examples (Art. XXI, observations on French Revolutionary wars, maxims 1–17): [[sources/jomini-the-art-of-war-1862]].

## Historiography

Became a standard term in Western staff colleges. Some later writers (including in the American Civil War context) noted that railroads and telegraphs could neutralize the classic interior-lines advantage. Primary grounding in [[sources/jomini-the-art-of-war-1862]]; secondary treatment in [[sources/freedman-strategy-a-history-2013]], [[sources/paret-makers-of-modern-strategy-1986]], and campaign analyses.
