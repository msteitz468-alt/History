# Manichaeism

A major universalizing religion of late antiquity that taught a radical cosmic dualism: an eternal struggle between forces of Light (good, spirit) and Darkness (evil, matter). Founded by Mani, it spread rapidly along the Silk Roads under Sasanian and later Uighur patronage, reaching as far as China, and competed with Christianity and Zoroastrianism in the Roman and Iranian worlds before being suppressed as heresy in most regions.

## Characteristics and Historical Role
- Dualist cosmology, strict ethical code for the Elect, support from Hearers.
- Strong missionary drive and use of art/texts in multiple languages.
- Viewed by many contemporaries (Augustine among them) as a dangerous rival or distortion of "true" religion.
- Survived longest in Central Asia; important vector for the transmission of ideas and artistic motifs.

Debated: degree of originality vs. syncretism; social base (urban elites?); role in the religious "marketplace" of late antiquity and early medieval Eurasia; reasons for ultimate decline (persecution + competition from more adaptable traditions).
