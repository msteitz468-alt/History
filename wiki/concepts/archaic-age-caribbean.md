## Definition

The **Archaic Age** (also called Lithic Age or pre-Agroalfarero) designates the pre-ceramic, pre-agricultural horizon of Caribbean prehistory, conventionally dated to c. 6500–400 BCE in the Greater Antilles (with some Archaic Age communities persisting alongside Ceramic Age populations until the European encounter). The term is etic — a modern archaeological periodisation — and encompasses considerable internal diversity.

Two major complexes are distinguished:

**Flaked-stone (Lithic Age) complex:** Characterised by flaked chert tools, absence of pottery, and reliance on hunting and gathering. Represented at sites like Barrera-Mordán (Dominican Republic, c. 4000 BCE). Origins debated: South American mainland (Colombia/Venezuela), Central America, and possibly North America all proposed.

**Ground-stone complex:** Characterised by ground-stone tools and food-processing equipment; some sites show incipient horticulture. Represented at Banwari Trace (Trinidad, c. 5000 BCE). The ground-stone tradition appears closely related to Archaic Age South American cultures and provided the substrate into which Saladoid pottery-using migrants moved.

## Contested Aspects

- **Origins:** Multiple migration routes proposed; South American and Floridian source populations debated.
- **Duration:** Archaic Age populations persisted in some areas (notably western Cuba, as Ciboney/Guanahatebey) into the historic period, creating complex overlaps with Ceramic Age cultures.
- **Archaic Age pottery:** Some Late Archaic sites in the Greater Antilles show early pottery that complicates the ceramic/pre-ceramic divide.
- **Relationship to Ceramic Age arrivals:** Whether Archaic Age populations were displaced, absorbed, or persisted is debated site-by-site.

## Relationship to World-Historical Periods

Archaic Age Caribbean chronology runs from roughly the Mesolithic through the Neolithic in Old World terms, though the Caribbean never experienced a "Neolithic Revolution" in the same sense — agriculture arrived with Ceramic Age migrants rather than developing in situ.

## Historiography

Early Caribbean archaeology treated Archaic Age populations as simple "pre-Arawak" primitives. More recent work (Keegan and Hofman 2017; Rodríguez Ramos 2010) emphasises their adaptive sophistication, inter-island mobility, and possible contributions to later Ceramic Age cultures. Dating methods: primarily radiocarbon; stratigraphy for site sequences.
