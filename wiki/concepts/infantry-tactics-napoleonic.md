# Infantry Tactics in the Napoleonic Era

Practical methods of employing line infantry and light troops in battle during the Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars. Key issues include formation choice (column for movement and assault vs line for firepower), the effectiveness and limitations of musketry, the role of the bayonet, the use of skirmish screens, and the critical importance of unit cohesion and morale under fire.

Muir's analysis stresses that actual combat bore only partial resemblance to parade-ground drill. Fire was often delivered at very short ranges or in volleys of limited effect; many "charges" succeeded through psychological pressure rather than sustained hand-to-hand fighting; light infantry skirmishing was ubiquitous and valuable.
