# Gothic Peoples

Germanic-speaking tribal confederations (Goths) that played a central role in the Migration Period and the fall of the Western Roman Empire. Divided (in later tradition and historiography) into Visigoths (western, settled in Gaul/Spain) and Ostrogoths (eastern, kingdom in Italy). Known from Roman sources as raiders, allies (foederati), and eventual state-builders.

## Definition and Scope

- Not a single "people" but shifting alliances of warriors, families, and subject groups.
- Pressure from Huns and other steppe peoples contributed to movements across the Danube (376 CE) and Rhine.
- Key events: Sack of Rome (410 by Alaric's Visigoths), Battle of Adrianople (378), establishment of Visigothic kingdom in Spain, Ostrogothic kingdom under Theodoric in Italy.
- Cultural: Arian Christianity, adoption/adaptation of Roman institutions, eventual conversion to Catholicism.

## Historiography and Debates

Roman sources (Ammianus, Jordanes) are biased and often conflate groups. Modern archaeology and genetics complicate simple "Germanic invasion" narratives. Debates on scale of migration vs. elite takeover, role in "fall of Rome" (continuity vs. rupture), and whether "Goths" were primarily ethnic or political labels. Collection coverage in late-antiquity and hunnic-migrations material notes their interaction with Huns and Romans.

## From Geary, *Before France and Germany* (1988)

Geary turns the Goths into the textbook case of [[ethnogenesis]] — peoples "with widely differing ethnic,
cultural, and geographical backgrounds" who "become Goths." The first-century Goths (Gutonen, the name
"probably simply means 'the people'") lived between the Oder and Vistula; their tradition-bearers infiltrated
south to the Dnieper near Kiev, organising "a powerful confederation" of Pontic, Sarmatic, Slavic, and
Germanic peoples. They fought Rome from 238 (under Cniva, killing Emperor Decius in 251); their defeat by
Claudius II and Aurelian (271) split the confederation into the **Tervingi** (lower Danube) and the **Amal**
kingdom to the east — "just as victory could be the decisive event in the creation of a people, so, too,
could defeat."

The **Tervingian** confederation was "hardly a tribe… but rather the political constellation of smaller
groups, or *kunja*," united by cult and led by individual *reiks*; Ariarich made the first *foedus* with
Constantine (332), and Tervingian elites deeply imitated Roman forms. The Hunnic shock of **376** turned
Fritigern's followers into the [[visigoths|Visigoths]], while Greuthungi absorbed into the
[[huns|Hunnic confederation]] re-emerged by 453 as the [[ostrogoths|Ostrogoths]]. Across the whole sequence,
Geary stresses, "anyone who fought with them was a member of their tribe, regardless of previous ethnic,
linguistic, political, or cultic origins." Source: [[geary-before-france-germany-1988]].
