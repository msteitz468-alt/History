# Gothic Peoples

Germanic-speaking tribal confederations (Goths) that played a central role in the Migration Period and the fall of the Western Roman Empire. Divided (in later tradition and historiography) into Visigoths (western, settled in Gaul/Spain) and Ostrogoths (eastern, kingdom in Italy). Known from Roman sources as raiders, allies (foederati), and eventual state-builders.

## Definition and Scope

- Not a single "people" but shifting alliances of warriors, families, and subject groups.
- Pressure from Huns and other steppe peoples contributed to movements across the Danube (376 CE) and Rhine.
- Key events: Sack of Rome (410 by Alaric's Visigoths), Battle of Adrianople (378), establishment of Visigothic kingdom in Spain, Ostrogothic kingdom under Theodoric in Italy.
- Cultural: Arian Christianity, adoption/adaptation of Roman institutions, eventual conversion to Catholicism.

## Historiography and Debates

Roman sources (Ammianus, Jordanes) are biased and often conflate groups. Modern archaeology and genetics complicate simple "Germanic invasion" narratives. Debates on scale of migration vs. elite takeover, role in "fall of Rome" (continuity vs. rupture), and whether "Goths" were primarily ethnic or political labels. Collection coverage in late-antiquity and hunnic-migrations material notes their interaction with Huns and Romans.
