# Proto-Elamite (Susa III horizon and script)

**"Proto-Elamite"** is the conventional name for a late-4th / early-3rd-millennium BCE
(c. 3100–2900 BCE) phenomenon centred on **[[susa|Susa]]** (the **Susa III** period,
Acropole levels 16–14b): an undeciphered writing system and an associated material
culture that spread across the Iranian plateau. [[potts-archaeology-of-elam-2016|Potts]]
argues the label is a **misnomer** and prefers "Susa III texts," because **no link to
later historical [[elam|Elam]] or to the Elamite language has been demonstrated**,
"either linguistically or graphically."

## The script and its spread

About **1,557 Proto-Elamite texts** come from Susa, with smaller numbers from
**[[anshan|Tal-i Malyan]]** (32), Tepe Yahya in Kerman (26), Tepe Sialk, Tepe Sofalin,
Shahr-i Sokhta in Seistan, and elsewhere — the **most geographically extensive early
writing system in the ancient world**, spanning ~1,000 km. Its **numerical systems** are
mostly identical to or derived from the proto-cuneiform systems of Uruk (transmitted via
the preceding [[susa|Susa II / Late Uruk]] contact), but its **ideographic signs are far
more abstracted** than proto-cuneiform and diverge so sharply "as to suggest a separate
system." It remains **undeciphered** (only ~1,557 texts; small sign overlaps with later
**Linear Elamite** are dismissed as generic pictographic convergence). Potts holds the
system **developed at Susa, not at Malyan** — Malyan's tablets are palaeographically
later and record far smaller transactions, whereas Susa accounts run to 17,100 units of
grain and 23,600 animals.

## Emic vs. etic; the continuity question

The concept is **etic** — a modern construct. The core interpretive dispute is whether
the Proto-Elamite horizon is **ancestral to historical Elam**:

- **Continuity view** (Scheil's original intuition; some readings by Steve, Vallat):
  Proto-Elamite → Linear Elamite → Elamite, an indigenous highland literate tradition.
- **Rupture view** (Damerow & Englund; Potts): the Susa II→III transition is a **complete
  disjunction** (new ceramics, abandonment of the *haute terrasse*, a new script); a gap
  of **~900 years** separates the last Proto-Elamite texts (~2900 BCE) from the earliest
  Old Elamite ([[puzur-inshushinak|Puzur-Inshushinak's]] Linear Elamite, ~2100 BCE),
  making genetic descent "impossible to sustain." Potts therefore brackets the whole
  phenomenon as **"pre-Elamite and strictly speaking outside the scope"** of Elamite
  history proper.

A related, now-rejected thesis held that Susa in period III was **annexed by highlanders
from Malyan**; Potts counters that the Susa III script derives from local Susa II/Uruk
procedures and that Susa's economic scale dwarfs Malyan's.

## Limitations and critique

Because the script is undeciphered, the **language** behind Proto-Elamite is unknown — to
call it "Elamite" prejudges the question. The early French excavations at Susa were of
poor stratigraphic quality, and the radiocarbon curve plateaus c. 3400–2900 BCE, so
absolute dating of the horizon is weak. The term persists by convention despite Potts's
objection, illustrating how an early, intuitive label can fix a contested assumption into
the literature.
