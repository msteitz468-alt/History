# Center of Gravity (Schwerpunkt)

The **Center of Gravity** (*Schwerpunkt*) is one of Carl von Clausewitz's most important and enduring contributions to operational art and campaign planning. Drawing an analogy from Newtonian physics, Clausewitz conceptualizes the center of gravity as the hub of all power and movement upon which everything else depends.

In Book Eight, Chapter Four, Clausewitz provides the canonical definition:
> "What the theorist has to say here is this: one must keep the dominant characteristics of both belligerents in mind. Out of these characteristics a certain center of gravity develops, the hub of all power and movement, on which everything depends. That is the point against which all our energies should be directed."

---

## The Various Forms of the Schwerpunkt

Clausewitz emphasizes that the center of gravity is not always a military force; its location depends on the political and social structure of the belligerents. He identifies several distinct forms that the *Schwerpunkt* can assume:

* **The Army:** For states led by great military monarchs or generals (such as Alexander the Great, Gustavus Adolphus, Charles XII, or Frederick the Great), the center of gravity is their army. If the army is destroyed, the state collapses.
* **The Capital:** "In countries subject to domestic strife, the center of gravity is generally the capital." The capture of the political hub breaks the cohesion of the regime.
* **The Army of a Protector:** "In small countries that rely on large ones, it is usually the army of their protector." Defeating the protector's army is the key to defeating the client state.
* **Community of Interest:** "Among alliances, it lies in the community of interest." Striking at the political bonds that hold a coalition together is the most effective way to disintegrate its power.
* **Public Opinion and Leadership:** "In popular uprisings it is the personalities of the leaders and public opinion."

---

## Operational Implications for Campaign Planning

Clausewitz derives two fundamental principles of action from the center of gravity concept:

### 1. Decisive Concentration
Strategy must avoid dispersals and minor conquests, concentrating all efforts against the core of the enemy's power:
> "Not by taking things the easy way—using superior strength to filch some province, preferring the security of this minor conquest to great success—but by constantly seeking out the center of his power, by daring all to win all, will one really defeat the enemy."

The general must "strike with all his strength and not just against a fraction of the enemy’s." If the enemy is thrown off balance, they must not be given time to recover; "blow after blow must be aimed in the same direction."

### 2. Reduction to a Single Center
If a state faces multiple enemies, the strategist must attempt to reduce the conflict to a single center of gravity by identifying the dominant partner:
> "if you can vanquish all your enemies by defeating one of them, that defeat must be the main objective in the war. In this one enemy we strike at the center of gravity of the entire conflict."

---

## Connection to the Culminating Point of Victory

The concept of the *Schwerpunkt* is closely linked to the [[culminating-point-of-victory]]. In offensive campaigns, the attacker must destroy the enemy's center of gravity *before* the momentum of the offensive is exhausted. If the attacker passes this culminating point without shattering the *Schwerpunkt*, the initiative reasserts itself in favor of the defender, often leading to a catastrophic reversal.
