# Areopagus (Council of the Areopagus)

The ancient Athenian council that met on the Areopagus hill. In early times a powerful aristocratic body of former archons that supervised magistrates and tried serious crimes. After Solon and especially after Ephialtes' reforms (462 BCE), it lost most political power and became primarily a homicide court, though it retained prestige and some conservative influence (e.g., in the aftermath of the Persian Wars and under the Thirty).

## Evolution and Role
- Pre-Solonian: supreme council.
- Solon: homicide jurisdiction + some oversight.
- Ephialtes: stripped of most non-judicial powers; transfer to the demos/boule.
- Later: symbol of conservative tradition (Isocrates, etc.).
