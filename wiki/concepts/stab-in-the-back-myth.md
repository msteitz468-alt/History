# Stab-in-the-Back Myth (Dolchstoßlegende)

The claim — historically false but politically devastating — that the German
army was undefeated in the field in 1918 and was "stabbed in the back" by
civilian revolutionaries and socialists at home: the "November criminals."

## Content and Origins

An emic myth of the German right. Evans (2003) traces its cultural template to
Wagner's *Twilight of the Gods* (the betrayed and murdered Siegfried) mapped
onto the defeat of 1918. [[hindenburg-paul-von|Hindenburg]] and
[[ludendorff|Ludendorff]] promoted it almost immediately after the war, citing
an English general's supposed corroboration: "An English general said
correctly: the German army was stabbed in the back." Kaiser Wilhelm II's
memoirs repeated it: the army "was forced to collapse by the stab-in-the-back
from the dagger of the revolutionist, at the very moment when peace was within
reach!" Even [[ebert-friedrich|Friedrich Ebert]], greeting returning troops on
10 December 1918 with "No enemy has overcome you!", inadvertently reinforced
it from the democratic side.

## Why It Gripped

Evans's mechanism (attributed): the high command concealed the reality of
looming defeat until the very end — Hindenburg and Ludendorff told the Kaiser
only at the end of September 1918 that defeat was inevitable, while censorship
kept newspapers proclaiming victory. The Armistice and
[[november-revolution-1918|Revolution]] therefore hit the public as a sudden,
inexplicable betrayal rather than the foreseeable culmination of military
exhaustion. Ludendorff had also pushed democratization precisely so that
civilian politicians, not the army, would bear the blame for the peace.

## Consequences

The myth contributed_to the delegitimization of the [[weimar-republic|Weimar
Republic]] from birth and supplied the ideological fuel for
[[freikorps|Free Corps]] and Organization Consul violence against "November
criminals" — the assassinations of [[walther-rathenau|Rathenau]], Erzberger,
and Haase — and for Nazi rhetoric throughout the rise to power ([[adolf-hitler|Hitler]]'s
speeches routinely invoked the "November criminals" into 1933).

## Limitations and Critique

Etic consensus: the myth is discredited — the army was militarily beaten by
autumn 1918 (see [[first-world-war-1914]]). Its historiographical importance
is as a political myth whose power derived from information suppression, not
plausibility.
