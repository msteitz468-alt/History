# Tell el-Yahudiya Ware

**Tell el-Yahudiya ware** is a distinctive class of small Middle Bronze Age
juglets — typically dark, burnished, **cylindrical** vessels decorated with patterns of
**combed or punctured incision** filled with white paste — that serves as a key
chronological and trade-network marker for the Egyptian
[[processes/second-intermediate-period|Second Intermediate Period]] and the world of the
[[hyksos|Hyksos]]. It is named for its discovery at Tell el-Yahudiya in the Wadi Tumilat.

## Why it matters

The ware ties together a far-flung distribution that lets archaeologists correlate sites and
phases across Egypt, Nubia, and the Levant. [[places/avaris|Avaris (Tell el-Dabʿa)]] "was
the centre for the manufacture and distribution of Tell el-Yahudiya juglets, the contents of
which have not yet been identified but were clearly much prized." The juglets turn up at
Tell el-Yahudiya, in the Hyksos strata at Tell el-Dabʿa, in the latest (wholly Egyptian)
burials at Lisht, at [[places/buhen|Buhen]] in Nubia (Cemetery K, including types not yet
present at Avaris), and in **pan-grave** (Medjay) contexts at Deir Rifa.

Because specific juglet types belong to specific stratigraphic phases, the ware functions as
a **cross-dating tool**: a cemetery like Maiyana, mixing Tell el-Yahudiya juglets with
imported Cypriote base-ring jugs, can be pinned to the transition between the SIP and the
early 18th Dynasty. It is thus part of the same ceramic-chronology toolkit — alongside the
contested Tell el-Dabʿa stratigraphy — on which the absolute dating of the east-Mediterranean
Middle Bronze Age depends.
