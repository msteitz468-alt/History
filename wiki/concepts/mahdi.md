# Mahdi (the Rightly Guided One)

The **Mahdi** ("the rightly guided one") is the Islamic figure expected to **restore justice** — in the common
formulation drawn from hadith, to "fill the world with justice as it has been filled with injustice." A broad
Muslim expectation, it was given its most politically consequential forms in **Shi'i** and messianic movements.

## Definition and scope

Belief in a coming Mahdi was widespread across early Islam and near-synonymous with the ***qa'im*** ("the one
who rises"). Groups differed sharply on his **identity, timing, and role** at his manifestation (*zuhur*):
- In **[[shia-islam|Shi'i]]** thought the Mahdi is typically an [[imamate|Imam]] of the Prophet's house; in
  **Twelver** Shi'ism he is the hidden Twelfth Imam in occultation (*ghayba*), awaited at the end of time.
- Within the early **[[ismaili-movement|Ismaili]]** [[dawa|da'wa]], some held that **Muhammad b. Isma'il**
  himself was the Mahdi, concealed and to return; others (the Salamiyya leadership) held that the Mahdi would be
  a **living descendant Imam**.

## The Fatimid claim (Jiwa)

The [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] rise turned the expectation into a **reigning caliphate**. In 909 the Ismaili
Imam Abd Allah made his public manifestation and took the regnal title **[[al-mahdi-billah|al-Mahdi bi'llah]]**
("the One Rightly Guided by God"), claiming to be the awaited figure (see
[[foundation-of-the-fatimid-caliphate-909]]). This realized, dynastic claim was precisely what the
**Qarmatians** rejected — they awaited the return of Muhammad b. Isma'il and split from the Fatimids over it.
The Mahdist expectation recurred across North African history, later animating the **Almohad** movement of Ibn
Tumart. See [[jiwa-fatimids-rise-2018]].

## Limitations and critiques

The Mahdi idea is doctrinally **unstable and contested**: because it could be claimed by any charismatic leader,
it repeatedly fueled revolt and dynastic legitimation (Fatimids, Almohads, and many later "Mahdist" movements),
while orthodox scholars of both Sunni and Shi'i traditions disputed its scriptural basis and application.
