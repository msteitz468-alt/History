# Bewegungskrieg ("War of Movement")

A German operational-doctrinal tradition prioritizing rapid maneuver,
envelopment, and decisive battle over static, attritional warfare — the
conceptual counterpart to *Stellungskrieg* ("war of position") — most
closely associated in modern historiography with the analytical work of
[[citino-robert-m|Robert M. Citino]].

## Definition and Scope

Bewegungskrieg names a persistent Prussian-German preference, traceable from
Frederick the Great and the elder Moltke through the Schlieffen tradition to
the Wehrmacht's early Second World War campaigns, for seeking rapid,
decisive victories through maneuver against numerically or materially
superior opponents that Germany's more limited resource base could not
outlast in a prolonged war of attrition.

## Origin (Emic/Etic)

The term itself is a genuine German military-professional category (emic),
used by German officers themselves to describe their preferred operational
approach; its use as an overarching analytical frame for explaining German
strategic culture across nearly two centuries is a more explicitly etic
historiographical construction, developed systematically in Citino's work.

## Applications and Examples

The doctrine is credited with shaping German operational planning at
multiple points, from the Franco-Prussian War through the 1914 Schlieffen
Plan to the early Blitzkrieg campaigns of 1939–1941; its practical
limitations became apparent once German forces faced adversaries (the Soviet
Union, the Western Allies after 1943) whose resources allowed them to absorb
initial maneuver defeats and grind down German forces in precisely the
attritional conditions Bewegungskrieg was designed to avoid.

## Limitations and Critiques

Critics note that treating Bewegungskrieg as a fixed, continuous
"German way of war" risks understating genuine doctrinal change over time
and overstating coherence across distinct historical periods with very
different technology, politics, and strategic circumstances; the doctrine
also offered no solution to Germany's fundamental resource disadvantage in
a prolonged multi-front war, a limitation starkly exposed after 1942.
