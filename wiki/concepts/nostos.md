# Nostos (Homecoming)

Ancient Greek literary and mythological concept (*nostos*, plural *nostoi*) denoting the theme and narrative arc of a hero's perilous return home after war — most famously embodied in Homer's *Odyssey*, whose entire plot is structured around Odysseus's decade-long *nostos* from Troy to Ithaca. The broader "Nostoi" tradition (partly preserved in a lost epic of that name and referenced across Greek literature) covered the varied and often disastrous homecomings of other Trojan War veterans — Agamemnon murdered on arrival, Menelaus blown off course to Egypt, Ajax the Lesser drowned for hubris — establishing homecoming, rather than the war itself, as a distinct and fraught narrative category in Greek epic.

*Nostos* is etymologically related to the modern word "nostalgia" (*nostos* + *algos*, "the pain of homecoming/longing for home"), and functions in Homeric epic as a structuring desire — Odysseus's persistent longing to return to Ithaca and Penelope despite divine and mortal obstacles and temptations (Calypso, Circe, the Sirens) — that defines his heroic identity in contrast to Achilles's choice of a short, glorious life without return.

## Significance

Nostos is one of the foundational thematic categories of Greek epic, establishing "the difficult return home" as a narrative structure of comparable weight to the war narrative itself, and giving rise to the concept of nostalgia in later Western literary and psychological vocabulary.
