# Periplus of the Erythraean Sea

## Overview
First-century CE practical commercial guide to the western Indian Ocean for Greco-Roman merchants sailing from the Red Sea. It provides one of the earliest detailed descriptions of ports, goods, peoples, and navigation along the African, Arabian, and Indian coasts.

## Key Content
- Describes the port of Muza (modern Mocha area) as a major hub for trade with the “far side” (East African) coast.
- Details Barygaza (Bharuch, India) and other Indian ports with lists of exports/imports (pepper, ivory, textiles, Roman goods).
- Notes Muziris (South India) and Arikamedu (archaeological correlate).
- Records Roman/Indian exchange networks, including overland routes and monsoon sailing knowledge (Hippalus).

## Historiography
Valuable primary source for pre-Islamic trade systems but limited on the lived experience of sailing or local cultures (focus on commerce). Cross-referenced with archaeology (Berenike, Arikamedu) and other texts (Pliny, Strabo). In Alpers, it anchors the ancient Indian Ocean chapter.
