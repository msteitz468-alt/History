# Gunpowder Weapons

The category of firearms and artillery — muskets, arquebuses, matchlocks,
and siege and field cannon — that spread from their Chinese origins across
Eurasia and, by the sixteenth century, into West Africa, transforming
warfare, fortification design, and the relative military balance between
societies with and without access to the technology.

## Definition and Scope

Gunpowder weapons displaced earlier missile and melee technologies gradually
rather than instantly, and their military effect depended heavily on
supply chains for powder and shot, metallurgical capacity for
manufacture, and trained personnel — factors that determined which
polities could field them effectively and which depended on imported
weapons and foreign or mercenary expertise.

## Origin (Emic/Etic)

The technology's spread is a well-documented material and technological
process rather than a contested interpretive category; the analytically
distinct concepts of [[gunpowder-empires|"gunpowder empires"]] and the
[[military-revolution|"military revolution" thesis]] address, respectively,
specific early modern Islamic empires built partly on gunpowder military
advantage and the broader (and more contested) argument that gunpowder
adoption drove a revolutionary transformation in European state
formation.

## Applications and Examples

The decisive military effect of imported gunpowder weapons and mercenary
expertise is illustrated starkly by the Moroccan conquest of the Songhai
Empire in 1591, where a relatively small Moroccan expeditionary force,
equipped with arquebuses and supported by renegade European mercenaries,
defeated a far larger Songhai army that lacked comparable firearms,
collapsing one of West Africa's major states.

## Historical Significance

The uneven global diffusion of gunpowder technology and the expertise
needed to manufacture and employ it effectively became, from the sixteenth
century onward, a significant factor in the shifting military balance
between societies with domestic gunpowder industries, those dependent on
imports, and those without reliable access to the technology at all.
