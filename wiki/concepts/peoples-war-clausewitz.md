# Peoples' War (Volkskrieg)

In Book Six, Chapter Twenty-Six of *On War* ("Arming the Nation" / *Landsturm*), Carl von Clausewitz outlines the strategic role of popular uprisings, partisan warfare, and civilian insurrections. Writing in the wake of the Spanish *guerrilla* against Napoleon and the Prussian mobilization of 1813, Clausewitz was one of the first major theorists to conceptualize irregular warfare as a vital element of national defense.

---

## Historical Genesis and Context

Clausewitz frames the popular uprising as a product of the political changes of his era:
> "a popular uprising should, in general, be considered as an outgrowth of the way in which the conventional barriers have been swept away in our lifetime by the elemental violence of war. It is, in fact, a broadening and intensification of the fermentation process known as war."

The introduction of requisitioning, universal conscription, and national militias dismantled the old, limited cabinet wars of the eighteenth century, leading directly to the arming of the general populace. Clausewitz argues that any nation that uses this resource will "gain some superiority over those who disdain its use."

---

## Operational Principles of Volkskrieg

Clausewitz establishes several crucial principles for how popular uprisings must be conducted to be strategically effective:

### 1. Nebulous and Elusive Resistance (The Evaporation Metaphor)
A popular uprising must avoid concentrating into a conventional target:
> "By its very nature, such scattered resistance will not lend itself to major actions, closely compressed in time and space. Its effect is like that of the process of evaporation: it depends on how much surface is exposed. The greater the surface and the area of contact between it and the enemy forces, the thinner the latter have to be spread, the greater the effect of a general uprising. Like smoldering embers, it consumes the basic foundations of the enemy forces."

If the insurgency attempts to fight as a "concrete body," the enemy will easily crush it: "the people will lose heart and, believing that the issue has been decided and further efforts would be useless, drop their weapons."

### 2. Nibbling at the Shell
 civilian forces "cannot and should not be employed against the main enemy force—or indeed against any sizable enemy force. They are not supposed to pulverize the core but to nibble at the shell and around the edges." They should focus on the enemy’s flanks, rear, and lines of communication.

### 3. Never Turn into Tactical Defense
Insurglents must never get locked into defending static positions:
> "it is the principle of seldom, or never, allowing this important strategic means of defense to turn into tactical defense... A defensive action ought to be a slow, persistent, calculated business, entailing a definite risk... they had better scatter and continue their resistance by means of surprise attacks, rather than huddle together in a narrow redoubt, locked into a regular defensive position from which there is no escape."

---

## Preconditions for Success

Clausewitz lists five conditions necessary for a popular uprising to succeed:
1. The war must be fought in the interior of the country.
2. It must not be decided by a single catastrophe.
3. The theater of operations must cover a large area.
4. The national character must suit the measures.
5. The country must be rough, broken, wooded, or mountainous.

Furthermore, he stresses that popular uprisings cannot stand alone: **"To be realistic, one must therefore think of a general insurrection within the framework of a war conducted by the regular army, and coordinated in one all-encompassing plan."** The regulars provide the "encourage[ment]" and organizational shell, while the insurgents act as a strategic force multiplier.

---

## Legacy and Impact

Clausewitz's chapter on arming the nation became a foundational text for later theorists of revolutionary war, including Vladimir Lenin and Mao Zedong. The concept of the people's war as a slow, decentralized process that wears down a regular occupier remains the starting point for modern doctrines of insurgency and counterinsurgency.
