# Duat

The Duat (also transliterated Tuat) was the ancient Egyptian underworld or realm of the dead, a perilous landscape through which the sun god Ra was believed to travel each night in his solar barque, battling the chaos-serpent Apep (Apophis) before being reborn at dawn — a journey elaborately depicted in New Kingdom royal funerary texts such as the *Amduat* ("That Which Is in the Underworld") and the *Book of Gates*. The deceased likewise had to traverse the Duat, ultimately arriving at the judgment hall of [[actors/osiris|Osiris]], where the heart was weighed against the feather of [[actors/maat|Ma'at]] (truth/cosmic order) while the deceased recited the "negative confessions" of the *Book of the Dead* denying specific transgressions; a heart found heavier than the feather was devoured by the monster Ammit, denying the deceased continued existence.

## Emic and Etic

Duat is an entirely emic category — the term and its associated cosmology come directly from ancient Egyptian religious texts and iconography, not a modern analytical construct.

## Significance

The Duat and its judgment sequence were central to Egyptian royal and elite funerary ideology across nearly the entire pharaonic period, underpinning tomb construction, mortuary ritual, and the broader Egyptian conception of cosmic order (Ma'at) as something the deceased, like the living pharaoh, was obliged to uphold even in death.
