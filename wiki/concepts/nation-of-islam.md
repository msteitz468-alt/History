# Nation of Islam

The **Nation of Islam** (NOI) is a religious and political movement founded in 1930 in Detroit by Wallace Fard Muhammad (also known as W. D. Fard). After Fard's disappearance in 1934, leadership passed to Elijah Muhammad (Elijah Poole), who built the organization into a significant force among African Americans, especially in northern cities, through the 1950s and 1960s.

## Definition and Scope

The NOI combines elements of Islam, Black nationalism, and economic self-help. Core teachings (as developed under Elijah Muhammad) include:
- A distinctive theology (e.g., Yacub's history, the "white devil" narrative) that departs significantly from mainstream Sunni or Shia Islam.
- Emphasis on moral discipline, clean living, and economic self-sufficiency (businesses, farms, schools).
- Black separatism and rejection of integration into white America as the path to liberation.
- Charismatic, centralized leadership centered on the "Messenger," Elijah Muhammad.

It is distinct from both orthodox Islam and from the mainstream civil rights movement (NAACP, SCLC, etc.). At its peak under Elijah Muhammad it claimed tens of thousands of members and had a high public profile through ministers such as Malcolm X and (later) Louis Farrakhan.

## Key Historical Role

The NOI provided a powerful alternative vision of Black dignity, manhood, and self-reliance during the era of legal segregation and its aftermath. It attracted many who felt the nonviolent integrationist approach of the mainstream civil rights movement was insufficiently radical or too accommodating.

Malcolm X's rise, ministry, and 1964 break with the NOI are central to the organization's history in the mid-20th century. His departure and subsequent evolution highlighted internal tensions over theology, leadership, and political strategy.

## Historiography and Sources

Scholarship has treated the NOI variously as:
- A millenarian or new religious movement.
- A vehicle of Black economic nationalism and proto-Black Power.
- A target of intense FBI surveillance and disruption (COINTELPRO).

Manning Marable's biography of Malcolm X (2011) provides the most detailed recent examination of the internal dynamics of the NOI in the 1950s–60s, Elijah Muhammad's personal conduct, and the circumstances surrounding Malcolm's suspension and departure.
