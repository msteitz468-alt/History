# Death Ray / Teleforce

In the 1930s [[actors/tesla-nikola|Tesla]] publicly described a beam weapon he called **"teleforce"** — popularly the **"death ray"** — said to project concentrated streams of charged particles capable of destroying aircraft and armies at great distances, which he offered as an ultimate weapon of national defense that would make war obsolete. He claimed (without disclosing full working details) that it could be built from a new type of open-ended vacuum tube and electrostatic generator.

In Seifer's *Wizard*, the death ray is treated as the most notorious of Tesla's late, unverifiable claims: it generated sensational press, drew interest from foreign governments, and — after his death in 1943 — motivated the U.S. **Office of Alien Property** and FBI seizure and examination of his papers (see [[events/marconi-radio-priority-dispute-1943|the seizure of Tesla's papers]] and the FBI files). No working particle-beam weapon was ever found among them. Seifer distinguishes the genuine physics of charged-particle beams from Tesla's specific, undemonstrated claims, and traces how the episode fed Cold-War secrecy and the enduring Tesla conspiracy mythology.
