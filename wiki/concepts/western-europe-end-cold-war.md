# Western Europe and the End of the Cold War, 1979–1989

Western Europe contributed significantly to the way the Cold War ended. With its large, well-educated population, industrial output, technology, and strategic access to the North Atlantic, the region always remained the greatest potential prize.

Even after Afghanistan, West European desire to continue détente acted as a brake on US policy during the “new” Cold War and encouraged improvement afterwards. West Europeans rescued their economies after the 1970s crises and built the most successful customs union of the era via the European Community (direct elections 1979, ERM, accessions of Greece/Spain/Portugal stabilizing new democracies, Single European Act 1987, Social Charter).

Despite tax cuts and privatisation, state spending remained historically high; social security, free education, and public health systems stayed intact. The combination of free-market policies with welfare and democratic politics stood in stark contrast to the Eastern bloc’s central planning, poor-quality goods, and limited provision for the poor. Male life expectancy in East Germany rose only 1.1 years 1970–1991 versus 5.2 in West Germany.

Gorbachev’s election (1985) and the INF Treaty (1987) plus his 1988 UN speech accepting the Western human rights agenda and “freedom of choice” (de facto repeal of the Brezhnev Doctrine) allowed West Europeans to press forward. After the June 1989 Kohl-Gorbachev summit the EC established the PHARE programme for Poland and Hungary. Communist governments collapsed and the Warsaw Pact disintegrated “without a major conflagration, at least partly because of shared views that had evolved after 1985 between the two blocs on the need to reduce the risk of nuclear war, to develop economic co-operation across the Iron Curtain, and to respect human rights.”

The end of the Cold War on Western terms can be seen as the result not only of American strength or Mikhail Gorbachev’s policies, but also of the creation of a thriving liberal democratic bastion on the very doorstep of the Warsaw Pact.
