# Al-Ghazali's Spiritual Crisis

The personal intellectual and psychological crisis experienced by [[actors/al-ghazali|Abu Hamid al-Ghazali]] around 1095, while at the height of his career as the most prestigious professor at the Nizamiyya madrasa in Baghdad, described in his later autobiographical work *Al-Munqidh min al-Dalal* ("Deliverance from Error"). Al-Ghazali recounts falling into a period of severe doubt about the certainty of all his acquired knowledge — theology, philosophy, and jurisprudence alike — accompanied by physical symptoms (he describes an inability to speak or teach) that he attributed to a deeper spiritual malaise: a conviction that his outward mastery of religious sciences had not brought him genuine certainty or closeness to God.

The crisis led him to abandon his prestigious teaching post and public life abruptly, departing Baghdad for roughly a decade of travel, seclusion, and ascetic practice, during which he immersed himself in Sufi spiritual discipline as a path to direct experiential certainty rather than purely discursive or rational knowledge. He eventually returned to teaching, and the works he produced afterward — most importantly *Ihya' Ulum al-Din* ("The Revival of the Religious Sciences") — integrated Sufi spirituality with orthodox Sunni theology and law in a synthesis that proved enormously influential on subsequent Islamic thought. Historians treat the *Munqidh*'s account with some caution as a retrospective, stylized spiritual narrative shaped by genre conventions of religious autobiography, but its core details of Ghazali's departure from Baghdad and turn to Sufism are corroborated by other contemporary evidence.
