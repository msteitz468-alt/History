# Nizamiyyah Madrasa

Network of state-sponsored Sunni madrasas (colleges) founded by the Seljuk vizier Nizam al-Mulk in the late 11th century, most famously the Nizamiyyah in Baghdad (1067). They institutionalized Ash'ari theology, Shafi'i law, and Sunni orthodoxy against Shi'i (especially Ismaili) challenges and philosophical rationalism.

## Founding Conditions and Structural Features

- Patron: Nizam al-Mulk (d. 1092), powerful vizier under Alp Arslan and Malikshah.
- Purpose: Train loyal Sunni ulema and administrators to counter Fatimid Ismaili da'wa and internal heterodoxy.
- Curriculum: Focused on fiqh (law), kalam (theology), hadith, Arabic grammar; later incorporated Sufism.
- Funding: Waqf endowments providing salaries for teachers and students (unlike informal earlier study circles).
- Model: Influenced later madrasa systems across the Islamic world.

## Interaction with Key Figures

Closely tied to al-Ghazali (taught there, later had spiritual crisis and left). Al-Juwayni also associated. Part of the broader Sunni revival and institutionalization of madhhabs and Sufi orders.

## Historiography

Seen as a key instrument of Seljuk "Sunni revival" and state-building. Lapidus and other surveys note how it made education more systematic and politically directed. Debates on whether it stifled or channeled philosophical inquiry (Ghazali's critique of falsafa). Collection sources (al-Ghazali biographies, Islamic societies surveys) provide context on its role in 11th-12th c. religious politics.
