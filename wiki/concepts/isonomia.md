# Isonomia

**Isonomia** — "equality of political rights," or equality under the law — is the principle
[[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 5, Martin Ostwald) identifies as the heart of
**[[cleisthenes|Cleisthenes']] [[cleisthenic-reforms-508bce|reform]]**. The word "enters
the Greek language for the first time, as far as we know, about the time of Cleisthenes'
reforms," and may well have begun life as the **propaganda slogan** by which he "made the
common people his partners in reforming the Athenian state."

## Meaning (emic)

Isonomia is **not** the same as *demokratia*. For Ostwald the Cleisthenic constitution was
"neither a democracy nor an aristocracy": its principle was that **no legislation was valid
unless approved by Council and Assembly**, in which every citizen counted as the political
equal of the nobles — even though the **implementation** of policy, and eligibility for high
office, remained restricted to the wealthy census classes. Equality thus lay in the **power
to validate or reject**, not in the power to govern. Related Archaic ideals include
**isegoria** (equal freedom of public speech), which Herodotus credits for Athens' surge of
military energy after 507.

## Reach beyond Cleisthenes

The term was quickly absorbed into Athenian political memory. The **"Harmodius song"**
celebrated the [[assassination-of-hipparchus-514bce|Tyrannicides]] for having "made Athens
*isonomos*" — a claim Ostwald and Thucydides judge undeserved (it was Sparta that freed
Athens), but one that shows isonomia as the prized civic value of the years around 500.
Ostwald reads several Cleisthenic measures as applications of the same principle to
different spheres: **[[ostracism]]** (the demos chooses between rival policies), the
**popular appeal in crimes against the state** (a counterweight to the Areopagus), and the
ten elected **generals** (a counterweight to the polemarch).

The slogan also crossed the Aegean. [[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 8, Murray)
shows **Aristagoras proclaiming *isonomia* at Miletus** to launch the
[[ionian-revolt-499bce|Ionian Revolt]] (499) — laying down his own tyranny and triggering a
general expulsion of the Persian-backed tyrants, so that "freedom from Persia and freedom
from tyranny became identified." (Maeandrius of Samos had offered the Samians *isonomia*
similarly c. 520.) In the economically advanced Ionian cities, where survival "depended on
the skills and courage of the fleet," *isonomia* implied **broad popular participation** —
its appeal to the rowers is what gave the revolt mass support. Murray notes that
[[herodotus-reliability|Herodotus]] shows "curiously little sympathy" for Ionian *isonomia*,
because by his own day Athenian-imposed "democracy" had become a **weapon of empire** rather
than a word for freedom.

## Critique and limitations

The concept is contested. It is hard to date precisely, hard to separate from later
democratic ideology read back onto 507, and genuinely ambiguous: how "equal" was a system
that barred the *thetes* from office and (until after Marathon) from the hoplite ranks?
Ostwald's own reading — isonomia as a balance between popular validation and aristocratic
execution — is one influential interpretation among several of what Cleisthenes actually
established.
