# Pacification (Vietnam War)

**Pacification** in the Vietnam War context refers to the civil-military effort to secure the rural population from Viet Cong/NLF influence, establish GVN control and administration in the hamlets, and separate the insurgents from their popular base through a combination of security, political, economic, and social programs. It was conceived as the "parallel war" or "other war" alongside main-force combat operations.

## Definition and Scope

Pacification encompassed "all political, military, economic, and security (police) programs" aimed at "winning the people" in the hamlets and villages. It included Revolutionary Development (RD), hamlet evaluation, territorial forces (RF/PF or "Ruff-Puffs"), civic action, and targeted neutralization of the Viet Cong Infrastructure (VCI) via the Phoenix Program. Distinct from pure military destruction of enemy units; focused on population security and governance.

`part_of:` broader [[counterinsurgency]] doctrine and Westmoreland's three-pillar strategy (attrition + pacification + ARVN development). `contrasts_with:` conventional search-and-destroy and attrition metrics.

## Causal Drivers

- Recognition that the war was "political as well as a military war" with ultimate goal "to regain the loyalty and cooperation of the people."
- Insurgency strength derived from rural population support ("fish in the sea"); main-force operations alone insufficient without separating guerrillas from base.
- Failures of earlier programs (Strategic Hamlets) due to forced resettlement, lack of sustained security, and ARVN focus on sweeps.

From Willbanks (range 3 cache): CORDS (Civil Operations and Revolutionary Development Support, initiated 1967) integrated US civilian and military advisory efforts under a single chain. Province and district senior advisers (military or civilian) supported province/district chiefs in "civil and military aspects of the South Vietnamese pacification and development programs." By end 1967, 4,000 US personnel in CORDS; by Vietnamization era, half of ~13,500 US Army advisors in CORDS organizations. (cache lines ~42-102)

Phoenix Program (part of pacification): focused on VCI identification and neutralization through intelligence fusion centers. Controversial due to metrics (neutralizations often included killed/captured) and allegations of abuses; seen as necessary for disrupting shadow government but difficult to measure success cleanly.

## Major Phases

- Pre-1967: Fragmented efforts (USOM, MACV civil affairs, Strategic Hamlets); military ops often undermined gains.
- 1967–1968 (CORDS establishment): Komer as Deputy COMUSMACV for CORDS; 9-step pacification plan; HES (Hamlet Evaluation System) metrics.
- Post-Tet 1968: Major regression in holdings (per Komer/Daddis cross: lost 38% / 2.7M people from secure); RF/PF expansion priority.
- Vietnamization (1969+): Pacification advisory burden shifted more fully to ARVN/RF/PF as US combat units withdrew; "one war" integration attempted under Abrams.

## Geographic Spread and Interaction

Applied nationwide but varied by corps tactical zone (I–IV). Stronger in some areas with good territorial forces; undermined where main-force battles or firepower created refugees and destroyed infrastructure. `interacts_with:` [[vietnamization]] (ARVN/RF/PF had to assume the load); [[army-concept]] (frequently subordinated to big-unit ops and resource priorities).

From range: "the elements of the parallel war in fact tended to work against one another. The destruction caused by military engagements undermined the construction aims of pacification planners."

## End Conditions / Assessment

Pacification achieved measurable gains in population security by 1970–71 per HES (attributed by some to Tet VCI losses + CORDS + RF/PF more than US main forces), but fragile. Collapsed under 1972 NVA conventional offensive in many areas; ultimate failure tied to South Vietnamese political legitimacy and military sustainability after US withdrawal. Historiography debates its relative contribution vs. conventional operations and the decisive role of North Vietnamese main-force will.

**Willbanks grounding (range 3, Vietnamization/pacification advisory chapters):** Detailed advisory structure under CORDS for pacification and development; province senior advisers and district teams; integration with ARVN training push during drawdown. Training and advisory quality issues persisted into Vietnamization phase. (cache ~66-280+)
