# Heterarchy

## Definition

**Heterarchy** (Carole Crumley) is a system whose elements are **ranked in
multiple ways, or are unranked** — as opposed to a single, fixed vertical
**hierarchy**. In the study of early cities it names the recognition that complex,
large, dense settlements did **not** all concentrate power in a despotic ruler:
power could be **distributed** among horizontally related corporate groups,
councils, assemblies, oligarchies, factions, and middle-level elites. It is the
organizing concept of Part IV of [[yoffee-cwh-v3-2015|CWH Vol. III]],
"The Distribution of Power," and the key analytical advance the volume offers
over Childe's top-down [[urban-revolution]].

## Why it matters

The older "archaic state" model (and the "oriental city") assumed early cities
were totalitarian monarchies with palaces owning all land. Archaeology has
overturned this for many cases. Heterarchy supplies the vocabulary for the
alternatives the volume documents:

- **[[jenne-jeno|Jenne-jeno]]** (R. McIntosh) — the paradigm case: a large, dense,
  internally differentiated city built on **reciprocal relations among specialized
  corporate groups** (fishers, herders, smiths, weavers), with no citadel, no
  vertical stratification, no despotic surplus-extraction. "Cities without citadels."
- **[[teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]]** (Clayton) — anonymous, possibly collective/council
  rulership; no depicted kings, no royal tomb; an originating synoikism.
- **Greek poleis** (Morris & Knodell) — shallow hierarchies; oligarchies,
  assemblies, and capital-intensive ([[city-state|city-states]]) rather than
  palace-command economies.
- **[[uruk|Uruk]]** — even here, councils of elders and young men appear alongside
  the ruler in later tradition.

## Relation to hierarchy

Heterarchy is **not** the absence of inequality or power — it is a *different
structure* of power. Most real cities mixed heterarchical and hierarchical
elements; the analytical task is to specify the balance, not to choose a label.
This is the subject of the [[hierarchy-vs-heterarchy-early-cities]] controversy.

## Critiques

- Can be stretched to fit any non-monarchy, losing precision.
- The absence of monuments/citadels is negative evidence; some argue undetected
  hierarchies may exist (the debate is live for Jenne-jeno and the Indus).
- Pointedly draws on sub-Saharan African and American cases to de-center the
  Mesopotamian template — a corrective some find overstated.
