# Gleichschaltung

**Emic** Nazi term (1933) for the "coordination" of all institutions —
federal states, parties, unions, professional bodies, associations, press,
churches' lay organizations — into conformity with the regime: a mix of legal
decree, violence, intimidation, and eager self-coordination
(*Selbstgleichschaltung*) from below. **Etic** usage by historians extends it
to the whole 1933–34 destruction of pluralism and, more loosely, to the
continuing penetration of society (culture chambers, Hitler Youth, Labour
Front) through the peacetime years.

## The 1933 Process in Detail (Evans, 2003)

Evans's etymology: "a metaphor drawn from the world of electricity, meaning
that all the switches were being put onto the same circuit... so that they
could all be activated by throwing a single master switch at the centre." His
Vol. I account ([[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003]]) runs the process from
the civil service (7 April law; 500 municipal officials and 70 Lord Mayors out
by end May; the 1.6 million "March Fallen" joining the party) through the
judiciary (professional bodies merged into Hans Frank's Front of German Law),
business (self-coordination into the Reich Corporation of German Industry;
the Adolf Hitler Donation steering 30m RM/year to the party), agriculture
(Darré's forced mergers), and the women's movement (the Federation of German
Women's Associations dissolved itself on 15 May after the Nazi Women's Front
demanded submission and expulsion of its Jewish members).

The micro-level exemplar is Northeim (from Allen's study, deployed by Evans):
45 council employees dismissed; SA spitting on departing SPD councillors;
singing clubs, shooting societies, and sports clubs merged, dissolved, or
Nazified; guilds drinking away their funds "in huge drinking sessions and
lavish banquets" before confiscation; the nearby Moringen camp's local SS
guards ensuring "what went on there must have been well known to the
townspeople." Evans's summary: society was "reduced to an anonymous and
undifferentiated mass and then reconstituted in a new form in which
everything was done in the name of Nazism" — with the purpose not merely to
foreclose opposition but to render Germany "amenable to indoctrination and
re-education." Haffner's contemporary diagnosis of why the 56% who had voted
against the Nazis caved: "The simplest... nearly always the most basic reason
was fear."

## Limitations and Critiques

The term's totalizing implication overstates success: Evans and the
*Alltagsgeschichte* literature show coordination was never complete — churches,
family, and grumbling milieux (workers, Catholics) retained partial autonomy;
"coordination" coexisted with polycratic chaos among competing party and state
agencies.
