# Universal Religions (Axial / World Religions)

The emergence, between roughly the 8th century BCE and the 7th century CE, of religious and philosophical traditions that offered membership and salvation independent of ethnicity, city, or kinship. Key examples: Buddhism, Jainism, Confucianism/Daoism (in transformed forms), Zoroastrianism, Second Temple Judaism → Christianity, and (slightly later) Islam. These "universal" or "world" religions spread along trade routes (Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, Mediterranean) and often became state ideologies or mass movements.

## Key Characteristics and Debates
- Transcended local cults and city-state or tribal identities.
- Textual canons, ethical universalism, individual salvation or enlightenment.
- Institutional carriers (monks, clergy, scholars) and missionary or conversion dynamics.
- Interaction with empires (e.g., Ashoka and Buddhism, Constantine and Christianity, Sasanians and Zoroastrianism).

Contested: "Axial Age" as a coherent synchronous breakthrough (Jaspers) vs. longer, multiple "axialities"; degree of "universalism" vs. continued ethnic/ political embeddedness; role of material conditions (trade, empires, literacy) vs. autonomous intellectual/religious creativity.
