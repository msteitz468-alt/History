# Manoeuvre sur les Derrières

The **manoeuvre sur les derrières** (manoeuvre against the enemy's rear or communications) was Napoleon's signature operational method: using superior mobility and dispersed corps to place a concentrated force on the enemy's line of retreat or flank, cutting his communications, forcing battle on unfavorable terms, and converting tactical victory into decisive strategic destruction through pursuit.

## Core Mechanism

- Dispersed advance on a broad front to conceal the main effort and live off the land.
- Rapid concentration (*marche de flanc* or *manoeuvre sur les derrières*) to strike the enemy's flank or rear rather than frontal assault on prepared positions.
- Battle of annihilation followed by relentless pursuit to prevent reorganization.
- Examples in Chandler: Castiglione (1796), Ulm (1805), Jena–Auerstedt (1806), and the design (though not fully realized) for later campaigns.

## Distinctions

Not merely a flanking march, but a deliberate operational turning movement intended to produce a decisive result by threatening the enemy's base and lines of supply/retreat, often inducing the enemy to fight at a disadvantage or surrender without full battle (Ulm).
