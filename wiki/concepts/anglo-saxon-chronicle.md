# Anglo-Saxon Chronicle

The **Anglo-Saxon Chronicle** is a set of related vernacular annals, originally compiled in
[[kingdom-of-wessex|Wessex]] around 890 and thereafter continued and copied in divergent
recensions (conventionally manuscripts A through E). It is, with Bede's *Historia
Ecclesiastica*, the indispensable narrative source for early English history — and one
[[sources/kirby-earliest-english-kings-2000|Kirby]] handles with marked caution.

## Source-Critical Status

Kirby stresses that the Chronicle's pre-ninth-century entries are **not contemporary
records** but a late-ninth-century construction. Its early West Saxon annals — the genealogical
descent of the [[kingdom-of-wessex|Cerdicing dynasty]], the regnal years of
[[ceawlin-of-wessex|Ceawlin]] and his predecessors, the conquest dates — are a tendentious
back-projection serving the legitimacy of Alfred's house, and their chronology is often
artificial (regnal lengths padded to round figures, events duplicated). The Chronicle's
application of the title [[bretwalda|*bretwalda*]] to [[ecgberht-of-wessex|Ecgberht]] (s.a.
829) is a piece of the same West Saxon self-fashioning.

## Method of Use

Throughout *The Earliest English Kings* Kirby cross-checks Chronicle dates against the
independent witness of Bede, the Irish annals (Ulster, Tigernach), the Welsh *Annales
Cambriae*, charters (Sawyer numbers) and indictional dating in papal letters — repeatedly
correcting the Chronicle by a year or more. The lesson generalised across the book is that
no single annalistic date can be taken at face value without external control.
