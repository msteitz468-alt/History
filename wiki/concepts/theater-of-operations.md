# Theater of operations

For Jomini, the theater of operations is the geographic area within which the campaign is conducted. Selection of the theater is the first major strategic decision and shapes almost everything that follows.

## Jomini's Treatment

- The commander must "carefully study the theater of war, and select the most suitable base of operations."
- The theater contains decisive points whose control can decide the campaign.
- Operations within it are framed by zones, fronts, lines of operations, and strategic positions.
- Different combinations of the theater admit different strategic plans.
- The concept includes both the physical geography and the political/military context (frontiers, allies, fortified places).

Jomini devotes significant attention to operations in mountainous countries and to grand invasions across large distances.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Starting point for [[jominian-strategy]], choice of base, decisive points, and lines.
- Closely related to [[lines-of-operations]] and zones of operations.
- Informs later ideas of operational art and campaign design.

## Primary Grounding from Jomini's Text

"The theater of a war comprises all the territory upon which the parties may assail each other... The theater of operations of an army embraces all the territory it may desire to invade and all that it may be necessary to defend." Composed of fixed base, principal objective point, fronts of operations, zones/lines of operations, obstacles, geographical strategic points, accidental intermediate bases, points of refuge. "The value [of features] will depend much upon the spirit and skill of the general." Different combinations admit different strategic plans. Mountains and distant theaters require special adaptations.

Full composition and maxims (Art. XVII): [[sources/jomini-the-art-of-war-1862]].

## Historiography

Became a standard term in 19th- and early 20th-century military literature. Primary articulation in [[sources/jomini-the-art-of-war-1862]]. Treated in secondary works on the development of strategic thought (Freedman, Paret, etc.).
