# American Way of War

The distinctive pattern of American military strategy identified by Russell F. Weigley: a preference, rooted in abundant resources, democratic political culture, and lack of continuous involvement in limited European-style balance-of-power conflicts, for seeking total military victory through the destruction (annihilation) of the enemy's armed forces rather than through attrition, maneuver for limited political ends, or graduated pressure.

Weigley traces its roots in the shift from Washington's early strategy of attrition (limited resources, 1775–1781) to Grant's strategy of annihilation in the Civil War, its application in the world wars, and its crisis in the nuclear/perplexed era after 1945.

## Core Characteristics
- Emphasis on bringing overwhelming force to bear for decisive battle and complete defeat of enemy armies.
- Impatience with prolonged wars of limited objectives.
- Contrast with "strategy of attrition" or wars of position.
