# Contextual Archaeology

**Contextual archaeology** is the approach, associated most closely with Ian Hodder,
holding that material culture (artefacts, structures, deposits) only carries meaning
within its full historical and cultural context — its specific relationships to other
finds, its find-spot, and the particular symbolic and social codes of the society that
produced it — rather than through universal, cross-cultural laws of the kind sought by
processual/"New" archaeology (see [[concepts/processual-archaeology]]). It treats
excavation itself as an interpretive, hermeneutic act: reading material remains somewhat
as a text, whose meaning must be built up contextually rather than read off directly.

## Relation to Post-Processual Archaeology

Contextual archaeology is often used near-interchangeably with
[[concepts/post-processual-archaeology|post-processual archaeology]], and Hodder himself
sometimes framed it as the constructive, methodological core of the broader
post-processual critique — an attempt to move beyond critique toward a workable
interpretive method. Critics have raised the same objections leveled at post-processualism
generally: a risk of interpretive relativism and reduced testability compared to
processual approaches.
