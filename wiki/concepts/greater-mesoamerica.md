# Greater Mesoamerica

## Definition

**Greater Mesoamerica** is the expanded culture-area concept that integrates **Western and
Northwestern Mexico** (and related interaction zones) into the definition of
[[mesoamerica-culture-area|Mesoamerican]] civilization, rather than treating them as a
"backwater" measured only against Central Mexican norms.

## Argument (Gorenstein)

Earlier scholarship, inheriting an Aztec- and Central-Mexican-centric view via Spanish and
later national narratives, either understudied the west/northwest or declared it non-
Mesoamerican when it failed to match Central Mexican developmental sequences. From the
1970s, denser fieldwork revealed complex societies and long developmental histories.
Gorenstein argues that high culture should be defined from the **range of cultural
manifestations in an interactive area**, not by modern political borders or internalist
Central Mexican models.

## From CHNPA II.1 (Gorenstein Ch.7; Adams Ch.1; Sheets Ch.9)

### West and Northwest Mexico as civilization zone (Gorenstein)

Until recently West and Northwest Mexico were treated as a Mesoamerican backwater under a
Central Mexican (Aztec-centric) model; when high culture was found, the region was still
declared “not Mesoamerican” because it did not match the Central Mexican civilizational form.
With continuing aggregation of data, the concept of Mesoamerica is being amended: the
integrating concept that folds West/NW Mexico into the full manifestation of civilization in
Middle America is **Greater Mesoamerica** (Gorenstein 2000).

**Geographic frame:** Western Mexico roughly coastal west to ~101° W (Sinaloa, Durango,
Nayarit, Zacatecas, Jalisco, Guanajuato, Michoacán, Guerrero in whole or part); Northwestern
Mexico includes parts of Jalisco, Durango, Zacatecas, Chihuahua, Sonora. Six archaeological
regions: (1) Western Mesa Central; (2) Central Mesa Central; (3) Balsas–Tepalcatepec Basin;
(4) Coastal West Mexico; (5) Sonora and Northern Sinaloa; (6) Sierra Madre Occidental and
Western Mesa del Norte (Gorenstein 2000). Process coverage:
[[west-and-northwest-mexico-prehistory]].

**Premise of high culture here:** large dense settlements with public functions; stratified
specialized populations; material culture reflecting those conditions—comparable in social
structure and cultural content, not only by contiguity. Distinctive contributions: long-distance
trade role, metallurgy (first appears in Greater Mesoamerica in West Mexico by ~A.D. 800,
technically well developed; Hosier et al. on South American technical affinities), and a
particular form of the state—the [[purepecha|Tarascan/Purépecha]] centralized administrative
empire vs Aztec “coordinated” mode (Gorenstein 2000). Nodes such as Amapa (Nayarit coast) and
mining/processing centers of the [[chalchihuites|Chalchihuites]] tradition (Alta Vista as
turquoise staging ground) exemplify Greater Mesoamerican interaction.

**Long-distance routes (Classic–Late Postclassic):** inland La Quemada → Chalchihuites →
Schroeder → Zape → [[casas-grandes-paquime|Casas Grandes]] → U.S. Southwest; coastal Central
Mexico via Lerma–Chapala → Ixtlán → Amapa → Chametla–Culiacán–Guasave, then north or east
across the Sierra Madre. Late Postclassic Tarascans took over routes, cutting the eastern/
Central Mexican branch and building their own Southwest connection (Gorenstein 2000).

### Frontiers as bidirectional filters (Adams)

Adams notes that both “frontiersmen” (Gorenstein on the west/northwest; Sheets on the
southeast) regard their areas as **interaction zones** that acted not only on their own behalf
in cultural evolution but also as **transmission filters** through which cultural traits,
material goods, and services passed in **both directions** (Adams 2000)—not one-way diffusion
from a Central Mexican core.

### Frontier vs boundary (Sheets Ch.9)

A **boundary/border** is a line; a **frontier** is a dynamic **zone** of contact between at
least two societies, often with enclaves of both expanding and autochthonous groups, and can
facilitate social differentiation as elites exploit resources or labor. Plural “frontiers”
matters: political, economic, ethnic, and religious frontiers differ and shift over time
(Sheets 2000).

**Southeastern line:** generally drawn as a N–S line along the Ulua and Lempa Rivers, or
somewhat east along Ulua–Comayagua–Choluteca. Costa Rica effectively lay **beyond** the
Mesoamerican frontier—minimal contact, still less impact (Lange via Sheets 2000). See
[[southeast-mesoamerican-frontiers]].

### Oaxaca and multi-directional webs (Marcus & Flannery)

Southern highland participation (Monte Albán–Teotihuacan special relationship; Plumbate in
tombs; magnetite mirrors to San Lorenzo) binds Oaxaca into the same multi-directional
interaction sphere as West/NW trade and metallurgy corridors (Marcus & Flannery 2000; synthesis
claim R06-47).

## Implications

- Brings Tarascan/[[purepecha|Purépecha]] state formation, West Mexican shaft-tomb and
  Teuchitlán traditions, [[chalchihuites|Chalchihuites]], and [[casas-grandes-paquime|Casas
  Grandes/Paquimé]] into the same analytical frame as the Basin of Mexico and Maya.
- Softens the northern "edge" of Mesoamerica into a dynamic frontier continuum with the
  U.S. Southwest; softens the southeastern edge into a multi-type frontier zone vs the
  Intermediate Area.
- Contested where interaction intensity drops below civilizational thresholds — boundary
  drawing remains a judgment call.
- Enlarges the political typology of Mesoamerican high culture beyond the Central Mexican
  template (centralized Tarascan administration; multipolar Mixtec *cacicazgos*; early Zapotec
  primary state at Monte Albán).

## Colonial–modern extension (Deeds, CHNPA II.2 Ch. 13)

Susan M. Deeds’s northwest Mexico chapter places the colonial–modern indigenous history of
Baja California, Sinaloa, Sonora, Chihuahua, and Durango in the same **Greater Southwest /
Greater Mesoamerica** debate zone as the prehistoric Paquimé literature: long-distance
trade (copper, turquoise, shells, salt, pottery, obsidian) and an unresolved controversy
over whether 15th-c. collapse of larger settlements preceded Spanish arrival or whether
Old World disease traveled existing routes **before** Europeans reached northern Mexico.
Contact-era demography and colonial outcomes for [[northwest-mexico-indigenous-colonial-modern]]
therefore inherit different baselines depending on that debate. See also
[[casas-grandes-paquime]] and [[west-and-northwest-mexico-prehistory]].
