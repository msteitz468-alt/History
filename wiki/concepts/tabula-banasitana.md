## Definition

The Tabula Banasitana is a bronze inscription recovered from the Roman city of Banasa in Mauretania Tingitana, recording a dossier of imperial rescripts (from the second century CE, spanning the reigns around Marcus Aurelius and Commodus) that granted Roman citizenship to members of the local Zegrensian tribal elite while explicitly preserving their existing tribal law and obligations.

## Emic vs. Etic

A primary emic source: the tablet is itself an official Roman administrative record, not a later historian's construct, though the modern name (from the site of Banasa) is a scholarly convenience.

## Significance

It is one of the most important surviving pieces of direct documentary evidence for how Rome extended citizenship to provincial elites in North Africa — illustrating the practice of enfranchising loyal local leaders (often in exchange for military or administrative service) without immediately erasing indigenous tribal structures, a key mechanism of Roman provincial integration and a central piece of evidence for [[places/roman-africa|Roman Africa's]] administrative history.
