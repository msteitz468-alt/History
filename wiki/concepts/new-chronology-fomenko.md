# New Chronology (Fomenko)

## Definition and Scope

The "New Chronology" (Novaia khronologiia) is a radical revisionist theory of historical dating developed by mathematician Anatoly T. Fomenko and collaborators. It holds that the conventional chronology of ancient and medieval history (the "Scaliger-Petavius" system still foundational in modern scholarship) contains massive duplications, shifts, and fabrications. According to the theory, most events traditionally dated before roughly the 11th–16th centuries AD actually occurred later, often in the medieval or early modern period. "Ancient" history is viewed as a phantom or projected version of later events, particularly those involving a hypothesized "Great Empire" with strong Russian/Horde components.

The theory is presented as the result of applying mathematical and astronomical methods (empirico-statistical analysis of texts, dynastic parallels, eclipse and zodiac retrocalculations) rather than traditional philological or archaeological approaches.

## Emic vs Etic / Nature of the Concept

This is an explicitly etic, modern, quantitative framework imposed on the historical record. It is not an emic concept used by historical actors. The authors contrast it with the "consensual Scaligerian chronology" they seek to replace.

## Key Components / Methods (as described in the source)

- Statistical analysis of chronicle length, name frequencies, and "volume functions."
- Detection of dynastic parallels (nearly identical sequences of rulers and events displaced in time).
- Astronomical dating (eclipses recorded in sources; Egyptian zodiacs and horoscopes).
- Critique of radiocarbon, dendrochronology, and archaeological dating as circular or unreliable when calibrated to traditional chronology.
- Re-localization of events (e.g., certain "ancient" sites and battles reassigned).

## Major Claims (attributed positions)

- Jesus born circa 1053 AD, crucified circa 1086 AD.
- Much of the Old Testament describes medieval events.
- The Book of Revelation post-dates 1486 AD.
- Systematic chronological shifts of hundreds to over a thousand years.
- Russia/Horde identifications with various "ancient" and steppe polities; Ottomans as "Atamans."
- The Renaissance and classical revival as artifacts of chronological error.

These are recorded as the content of the source; they are not asserted as historical fact.

## Limitations and Critiques

The New Chronology is rejected by the overwhelming consensus of historians, archaeologists, Egyptologists, classicists, and scientists working with dating methods. Critics cite:
- Contradictions with calibrated radiocarbon, dendrochronology, varves, ice cores, and other independent physical dating.
- Epigraphic, papyrological, and contemporary documentary evidence.
- Stratigraphy and material culture sequences that cannot be compressed as proposed.
- Methodological issues in the statistical parallels and astronomical interpretations.

It has nonetheless circulated widely in popular and alternative literature.
