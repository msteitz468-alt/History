# ʿIlm al-ʿUmrān (the Science of Civilization)

The "**science of civilization**" or of human social organization that [[ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]]
proposed and pursued in [[the-muqaddimah|the Muqaddimah]] — a discipline he claimed to have invented,
distinct from history, rhetoric, politics, and law. Its purpose was to identify the **inherent nature of
human society** and its regularities, so that the historian could distinguish reports that are possible
from those that are absurd: a claim is to be judged not only by the reliability of its transmitters but
by whether it accords with the laws of *ʿumrān*.

Ibn Khaldun's *ʿumrān* analyzes society through its material and social basis — subsistence and
environment, the bond of [[asabiyyah|group feeling]], the cycle of [[the-dynastic-cycle-ibn-khaldun|dynastic
rise and fall]], the economics of labor and the city. Because it seeks general causal laws of collective
human life from empirical observation, it is widely regarded as an anticipation of **sociology** and the
social sciences, some five centuries before Comte.
