# American Exceptionalism

The claim that the United States differs qualitatively — not merely in
degree — from other Western societies in its origins, ideology, and
development. Both an **emic** national self-understanding (city on a hill,
"sample democracy of the world") and an **etic** comparative-analytic
question. This page centers the analytic version, whose canonical
statement is [[hartz-louis|Hartz]]'s [[lockean-consensus]]: uniqueness is
only knowable comparatively — "How can we know the uniqueness of anything
except by contrasting it with what is not unique? The rationale for a
separate American study, once you begin to think about it, explodes the
study itself."

## Hartz's exceptionalist claims

- **The inverted class problem.** Instead of Europe's frustrated middle
  class straining against aristocracy, America produces "a 'frustrated
  aristocracy' — of men... trying to break out of the egalitarian confines
  of middle class life but suffering guilt and failure in the process":
  the antebellum South above all ([[fitzhugh-george]],
  [[calhoun-john-c]]), with New England a minor case. Southern
  "feudalists" and American socialists (De Leon) are structurally twinned
  failures — neither had genuine class materials to work with.
- **The Sombart question answered.** No feudalism → no class consciousness
  → no socialism. The American worker was an incipient entrepreneur;
  with Toryism (the successful man's ethical refuge) and socialism (the
  failed man's rationalization) both foreclosed, "'success' and 'failure'
  became the only valid ways of thought" — behind William James's
  "bitch-goddess" disgust and Sorel's *énergie exceptionnelle*. In Europe,
  Liberal Reform could drift into socialism (Lloyd George → MacDonald);
  in America there was no bridge: "how could a man move to Marx from a
  Progressivism which even in its midnight dreams ruled out the concepts
  of socialism?" See [[why-no-socialism-in-america]].
- **Constitutional durability by accident.** The Constitution survived
  (unlike France's Restoration Charter) not because the Founders read
  conflict rightly but because their Hobbesian premises were wrong for a
  unified society — "their conclusions were 'right' only because their
  premises were wrong." The one genuine rupture was the
  [[american-civil-war-1861-1865|Civil War]].
- **The messianism/isolationism polarity.** "Americanism, when it does not
  retreat, goes abroad" — see [[isolationism]] and [[wilson-woodrow]].
  Kennan: Americans judge other nations "by the extent to which they
  contrive to be like ourselves."

## Limitations and critiques

Hartz's is an anti-celebratory exceptionalism — the exception is a
*deficit* (of ideological self-knowledge) as much as a blessing. Critics
of exceptionalism generally: comparative historians note parallel
"born-modern" settler societies (Hartz himself extended the fragment
thesis to Canada, Australia, Latin America, South Africa in *The Founding
of New Societies*, 1964, where the fragments differ by which European
moment they froze); the multiple-traditions school (Smith) denies the
liberal uniformity on which the claim rests; and historians of slavery and
empire argue American development was continuous with, not exempt from,
Atlantic-world patterns. The emic, providentialist version remains a live
political trope rather than a scholarly position.
