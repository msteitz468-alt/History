# Eurocommunism

The movement among Western European Communist parties (especially Italian PCI, Spanish PCE, French PCF) in the 1970s to distance themselves from Soviet control, embrace democratic institutions, human rights, and national paths to socialism, while maintaining Marxist goals.

Key figures: Enrico Berlinguer (PCI), Santiago Carrillo (PCE).

It peaked around 1975-77 with "historic compromise" ideas in Italy, but declined by early 1980s due to internal contradictions, Soviet pressure, and electoral setbacks.

## Relation to Cold War End

As analyzed in Vol. III, Eurocommunism represented a "rise and fall" that both challenged Soviet hegemony in the communist world and ultimately weakened the appeal of traditional communism in the West, contributing to the ideological erosion that facilitated 1989.

## From Paxton & Hessler, *Europe in the Twentieth Century*

[[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011|Paxton & Hessler]] identify **Enrico Berlinguer**
(PCI secretary general, 1972–1984) as the movement's chief spokesperson, and his
"historic compromise" strategy — approaching the Christian Democrats' left wing rather
than the Socialists — as its central tactic. The PCI took **34% of the vote in June
1976**, the highest ever for a Western Communist Party in a free election, and played a
role in a 1977–1979 "government of national solidarity" without holding cabinet seats.
Berlinguer broke definitively with Moscow after the December 1981 Polish martial-law
declaration, calling Soviet communism "a spent force." Two contributing causes worth
preserving as `contributed_to` links: the 1973 overthrow of Allende in Chile
`contributed_to` Berlinguer's rejection of maximalist revolutionary tactics, and the
1968 Prague Spring suppression `contributed_to` the broader turn (see
[[prague-spring-1968]]).

Comparative note: Spanish Communists under Santiago Carrillo followed a closely similar
path. Portuguese Communists under Álvaro Cunhal explicitly *rejected* Eurocommunism —
see [[carnation-revolution-1974]] for their contrasting trajectory. The French PCF under
Georges Marchais occupied a middle position, entering a 1977 "Common Program" with the
Socialists.
