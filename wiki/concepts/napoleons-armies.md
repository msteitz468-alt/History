# Napoleon's Armies

The organizational and command system developed by Napoleon for the French army after 1800, building on Revolutionary foundations but adding the permanent corps d'armée structure, a more professionalized staff, and the integration of all arms for rapid, dispersed movement followed by concentration at the decisive point.

Rothenberg emphasizes that while the Revolutionary armies introduced the mass and the tactical flexibility (ordre mixte, skirmishers, combined-arms pressure), Napoleon's armies institutionalized these into a tool capable of the great maneuvers of 1805–1809 while retaining the ability to live off the country and maintain high operational tempo.

## Core Characteristics (per Rothenberg)
- **Corps system**: semi-independent combined-arms formations able to march on separate routes and concentrate quickly.
- **Imperial Guard and reserve artillery**: instruments of decisive intervention.
- **Command style**: personal oversight by Napoleon with a small headquarters; reliance on talented subordinates (Davout, Ney, etc.) within a directive framework.
- **Scale and composition**: multi-national by the later Empire; heavy use of allied contingents alongside the French core.
