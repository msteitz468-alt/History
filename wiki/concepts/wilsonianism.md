# Wilsonianism

Cluster of ideas associated with [[wilson-woodrow|Woodrow Wilson]]'s foreign policy and its afterlife: a world order of (formally) equal states, collective security via a [[league-of-nations|League of Nations]], open diplomacy, and the wartime Fourteen Points as a liberal peace program. Kissinger's line — that American foreign policy has marched to the "drumbeat of Wilsonian idealism" — is Cox's opening exhibit for Wilson's enduring consequence.

## Emic vs etic

**Emic:** Wilson cast U.S. belligerency as a war to make the world "safe for democracy" and the League as institutionalizing that peace. **Etic:** Realist critics (Kennan, Lippmann) attack moralism and overstretch; postcolonial and civil-rights critics stress the gap between self-determination abroad and racial/gender hierarchy at home and in the colonial settlement. Cox radicalizes the second critique: the same man who denied women and Black Americans full voice practiced "democracy" as a brand.
