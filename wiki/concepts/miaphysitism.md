# Miaphysitism

**Miaphysitism** (from Greek *mia physis*, “one nature”) is the preferred modern scholarly term for the christology of the non-[[council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedonian]] churches of the East — especially the **Syriac Orthodox** (“Jacobite”), **[[coptic-church|Coptic]]**, and **Armenian Apostolic** traditions — that affirm one *united* nature of the Incarnate Word, following Cyril of Alexandria, against what they saw as a Nestorianizing two-natures formula at Chalcedon (451).

## Emic vs. etic

- **Emic:** These churches understand themselves as defenders of Cyril’s unity of Christ, not as denying Christ’s full humanity.
- **Etic:** Latin and Byzantine polemic labeled them **[[monophysitism|Monophysite]]** (“one nature” in a heresiological sense implying absorption of humanity into divinity). Specialist literature and the living Oriental Orthodox traditions reject that caricature; **Miaphysite** marks the refined one-united-nature teaching.

## Historical role (Tannous frame)

[[tannous-making-medieval-middle-east-2018|Tannous]] treats Miaphysite–Chalcedonian (and Church of the East) rivalry as the matrix of **[[post-chalcedonian-confessional-formation|post-Chalcedonian confessional formation]]**: schools (e.g. [[qenneshre]]), sacramental discipline, polemic, and occasional violence competed for a population of [[simple-believers|simple believers]] who often did not grasp the speculative stakes. That fractured Christian landscape is what Arab conquerors encountered in the seventh century.
