# Evolutionary Origins of War

**Evolutionary origins of war** posits that the human capacity and propensity for coalitional lethal violence has deep phylogenetic roots in the primate lineage and was shaped by natural and sexual selection over hundreds of thousands to millions of years. War is not a recent cultural "invention" but an ancient adaptation (or byproduct of adaptations) that persisted and was culturally elaborated across the transition to agriculture, states, and modernity.

## Core Claims

- Chimpanzee (and to lesser extent bonobo) patterns of coalitionary killing and territorial raiding provide the closest living model; humans share the same last common ancestor ~6-7 mya.
- Ultimate causes: competition over scarce resources (food, territory), mates (women as reproductive resource), and status within and between groups.
- "Web of desire": intertwined proximate motivations (hunger, lust, fear, revenge, glory, ideology) that reliably produced lethal intergroup conflict under ancestral conditions.
- Kin selection, reciprocal altruism, and coalitional psychology enabled larger, more effective fighting groups.

## Evidence Types

- Ethnographic forager data (see [[hunter-gatherer-warfare]]).
- Archaeological: trauma on skeletons, defensive site locations, mass graves, weapon specialization.
- Cross-species: lethal raiding in chimpanzees (Goodall, Wrangham et al.).
- Genetic and psychological universals: male coalitional psychology, in-group/out-group bias, status-seeking.

## Distinction from Later Forms

Evolutionary framework explains the *persistence* and *recurrence* of war; it does not dictate specific tactics, scale, or ideology in any given society. Agriculture and the state dramatically increased the scale, frequency, and organization of war while altering its payoffs (Leviathan, tribute, empire).

## Relation to Historiography and IR Theory

- Challenges "war is a cultural invention" (Margaret Mead and followers) and pure cultural-relativist accounts.
- Supplements (and critiques) structural realism in IR by supplying the "reasons" actors fight beyond abstract security dilemmas (see Gat's engagement with Morgenthau, Waltz, Mearsheimer).
- Complements but is distinct from [[military-effectiveness]], Luttwak's paradoxical logic, and modern doctrine.
