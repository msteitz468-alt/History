# Fiannaíocht (the Fenian Cycle)

One of the four principal cycles of early Irish literature, centered on the legendary warrior-hunter Fionn mac Cumhaill and his band of roving warriors, the Fianna, distinguished from the earlier heroic Ulster Cycle (which centers on [[actors/cu-chulainn|Cú Chulainn]] and the court of King Conchobar) both in setting and in tone — Fiannaíocht material is generally more episodic, pastoral, and folkloric, drawing on hunting and wilderness themes rather than the Ulster Cycle's court-and-cattle-raid heroic register.

Fiannaíocht material survives across a very long span, from early medieval manuscript fragments through a large and continuously expanding medieval and early modern corpus (notably the *Acallam na Senórach*, "The Colloquy of the Ancients," c. 1200), and remained a living, popular oral tradition among Irish and Scottish Gaelic speakers into the early modern period and beyond — considerably longer-lived as popular culture than the more antiquarian Ulster Cycle. Modern scholars treat the cycle's longevity and continued oral vitality as evidence of its deep roots in popular rather than purely aristocratic or court culture, in contrast to the Ulster Cycle's closer association with early medieval Irish elite ideology.
