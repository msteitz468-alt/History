# Yellow Sea Interaction Sphere

The **Yellow Sea Interaction Sphere** is [[barnes-archaeology-east-asia-2015|Gina Barnes's]]
organising frame for the protohistory of East Asia: the maritime basin enclosed by the China
Mainland, the [[korea-three-kingdoms|Korean Peninsula]] and the Japanese Islands functioned, from
at least the late 2nd millennium BC, as a **single connected field of trade, tribute, technology
transfer and political emulation** rather than three national stories running in parallel. The
"Yellow Sea Basin" — running from the Shanghai Delta in the southwest to southern Kyushu in the
southeast — is the central geographic reference point of her whole synthesis. The water is made
yellow by [[yellow-river-valley|Yellow River]] loess discharged into Bohai Bay.

The concept is an *etic* analytical tool (Barnes's, not the historical actors'), and it is a
deliberate corrective to two older habits: treating Chinese, Korean and Japanese archaeology as
sealed national sequences, and reading early Korea and Japan through the much later national
chronicles. It pairs with her companion coinage [[pen-insular|"Pen/Insular"]] — the Korean
**Pen**insula and the **Insular** Japanese archipelago as one developmental zone "downstream" of
the Mainland.

## What the Sphere Describes

- **A ~2,000-year developmental lag, transmitted across water.** State societies appear on the
  Mainland by the mid-2nd millennium BC ([[erlitou-culture|Erlitou]], [[shang-dynasty|Shang]]);
  state formation in the Pen/Insulae comes only *after* the collapse of the Mainland's successor
  states in the 3rd century AD. The sphere is the mechanism by which Mainland models reached the
  periphery — making Korean and Japanese polities **secondary states** (see
  [[pen-insular-state-formation]]).
- **Coin and metal circulation.** Late Zhou knife-shaped coins distributed from the northern
  Peninsula south toward Okinawa mark Late Zhou economic reach into the basin; the
  [[zhou-dynasty|Zhou]] state of **Yan**, pushing through the Bohai Corridor and occupying
  Liaodong c. 282 BC, drew the Hui and Mo peoples (forerunners of [[puyo|Puyo]] and
  [[koguryo|Koguryo]]) into Mainland networks.
- **The commandery hinge.** The [[han-dynasty|Han]] conquest of the northern Peninsula in 108 BC
  and the founding of the [[lelang-commandery|Lelang commandery]] turned the sphere into a
  tribute system: Lelang became a "staging point for surrounding peoples wishing to submit to the
  Han Court," enticing [[samhan|Samhan]] chieftains to extract local goods (stimulating a southern
  Korean iron industry) and drawing [[yayoi-culture|Yayoi]] chieftains in western Japan into the
  Han *heqin* order. North Kyushu chiefly hierarchies were structured around access to Lelang
  goods — and **fell apart when Han goods dried up** during Wang Mang's usurpation (9 AD).
- **The iron axis.** Once Late Han bronzes became inaccessible, the sphere's primary content
  became **iron** from the southern Peninsular coast (Pyonhan/Chinhan, around Kimhae and Ulsan),
  shipped to Lelang and to the Japanese Islands; iron later became the strategic military good
  that underwrote [[kaya|Kaya]]–[[yamato-japan|Yamato]] alliance politics.

## Analytical Value and Limits

The sphere's payoff is that it makes otherwise puzzling facts legible as one system: why a
[[mumun-culture|Mumun]] burial type or a stoneware kiln appears on both shores of the Korea
Strait within decades; why Yayoi grave hierarchies track Chinese mirror supply; why a southern
Korean ingot doubles as Japanese burial currency. It treats **modern state boundaries as
meaningful to prehistory "only in terms of the investigating organizations,"** which is also why
Barnes uses it to defuse the nationalist controversies over Mimana, keyhole tombs and
the Koguryo dispute (see [[koguryo]], [[kaya]]).

Its limit is the limit of any interaction-sphere model: it foregrounds connection and can
understate internal, autochthonous development — a charge Barnes pre-empts by insisting the
Pen/Insular societies *selected* and *adapted* (Jomon "resistance" to wet rice; the bilateral
"Yayoinisation" at Etsuji) rather than passively receiving. Contrast with the older
[[state-formation|center-out diffusion]] model she explicitly rejects in favour of a **network**
of contacts.
