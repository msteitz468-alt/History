# Business Trust (Corporate Trust)

The "trust" was the legal-organizational innovation by which competing corporations placed their stock in the hands of a common board of trustees, enabling centralized control across state lines at a time when one corporation could not easily own another's shares. The [[actors/standard-oil|Standard Oil Trust]] of 1882, devised by Standard's counsel S.C.T. Dodd, was the model later imitated across American industry, lending its name to the entire phenomenon of monopoly combination ("the trusts") and to the [[events/standard-oil-dissolution-1911|antitrust]] movement that arose against it (Sherman Antitrust Act, 1890).

**Emic vs. etic:** Rockefeller and his publicists pointedly avoided the words *trust*, *monopoly*, and *cartel*, preferring "cooperation"; critics and later economists used "trust" as the defining etic category of Gilded Age combination.
