# Thing (Assembly)

The *thing* (Old Norse *þing*) was the public assembly of free men that
functioned as the chief institution of law, judgement, and political decision in
medieval Scandinavia. Things operated at local, regional (the great provincial
*lögþing*, e.g. the Gulating and Frostating in Norway, the *landsting* in Denmark,
the *landsting* of the Swedish provinces), and — uniquely — at the level of a whole
polity in [[iceland|Iceland]]'s **Althing** (*Alþingi*, founded c. 930), the
assembly of the [[icelandic-free-state|Free State]]. The thing both made and
declared law (recited by a Lawspeaker before written codes), settled disputes, and
acknowledged or elected kings.

As royal power and written law grew through [[unification-of-the-scandinavian-kingdoms|state
formation]], the things were progressively subordinated to the crown, though they
remained important to Scandinavian legal and political culture. The institution is
a touchstone in debates over early Germanic "democracy" and consensual kingship.
