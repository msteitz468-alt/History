# Spooky Action at a Distance (Quantum Entanglement / Non-locality)

**Einstein's label for the apparent instantaneous correlation between distant entangled particles in quantum mechanics, which he viewed as evidence of the theory's incompleteness (violating separability and locality).**

## Definition and Scope

If two particles interact and then separate, measuring an attribute of one (e.g., spin or polarization) appears to determine the corresponding attribute of the other instantly, regardless of distance. Einstein: "Physics should represent a reality in time and space, free from spooky action at a distance." Assumed: realism (objective properties exist independent of observation) + locality (no influence faster than light).

## Relation to EPR

EPR paper (1935): measuring position or momentum of one particle allows certain knowledge of the distant twin "without in any way disturbing" it. Concluded quantum description cannot be complete. Bohr response: particles remain one entangled system; complementarity preserved.

## Modern Status and Historiography

Experiments (Aspect 1980s onward) confirm correlations. No usable faster-than-light information transfer (special relativity intact). "The reasonable thing just doesn’t work" (Bell). Isaacson details Einstein's 1935 formulation and its roots in 1919–1930s debates.
