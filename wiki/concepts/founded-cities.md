# Founded and Imagined Cities (Cities as Creations)

## Definition

Some cities were not slow accretions but **deliberate creations** — willed into
being at a moment, by a founder or a community, as instruments and images of
power, religion, or memory. And once made, cities live a second life **in the
imagination**: as remembered, mythologized, and re-created places whose meaning
can outlast or exceed their physical fabric. This is the theme of Part V of
[[yoffee-cwh-v3-2015|CWH Vol. III]], "Cities as Creations" / "Imagined Cities."

## Two faces

- **Foundation** — the act of creating a city: choosing (or being "destined" to)
  a site, planning, mobilizing labor, and inaugurating it, often with ritual
  (horoscopes, cosmograms, dedicatory feasts and sacrifices).
- **Imagination** — the city as a "diagram" of cosmic order (Kostof) and as an
  object of collective memory. Yoffee: cities "loom important... because they
  instantiate the images that form historical imaginations" (citing Sontag's
  "collective memory is not a remembering but a stipulating" and Huizinga).

## The three cases

- **[[baghdad|Baghdad]]** (Micheau) — al-Mansur's Round City (762 CE), a cosmogram
  with the Caliph at the center of the world; **known only through later texts**,
  its founding wrapped in prophecy and horoscope — an imagined imperial city.
- **[[jerusalem|Jerusalem]]** (Killebrew) — created and **re-created repeatedly**
  "in stone and in imagination," its power resting on layered sanctity for three
  religions.
- **[[cahokia|Cahokia]]** (Pauketat et al.) — a single planned "big bang" (~1050
  CE) building an **axis mundi** for a converging, immigrant population; designed,
  extended into its hinterland, then deliberately unmade and forgotten.

## Relation to performance and landscape

Founded/imagined cities overlap heavily with [[ritual-and-urban-performance|cities
as performance arenas]] (cosmograms, processions) and with [[ruralization]]
(designs extended into the countryside). The distinctive emphasis here is on the
**act of creation** and on the city's **afterlife in memory** — including how
that memory shapes modern uses of ancient cities (Babylon for Iraq, the Cycladic
figurine at the Athens Olympics, "culture wars" over Jerusalem and Ayodhya).
