# Debt Peonage

A coercive labor system in which workers are bound to an employer or patrón by debts they
can never repay — typically because wages are paid in advance or in goods (often at inflated
prices through a company store or *habilitación*), and the debt is inherited or
manipulated to prevent the worker leaving. Though formally distinct from chattel slavery,
in practice it produced comparable unfreedom, and it became the dominant labor regime on
many South American frontiers where outright enslavement was illegal.

## Mechanics and Variants

- **Habilitación / enganche** — advance of goods or cash that creates the binding debt;
  central to the [[amazon-rubber-boom|Amazonian rubber economy]].
- **Hacienda peonage** — resident laborers (*yanaconas*, *colonos*, *huasipungueros*) tied
  to the [[hacienda-system|estate]] through debt, access to subsistence plots, and
  obligation.
- **Frontier and extractive peonage** — applied to indigenous labor in rubber, timber,
  and quina (cinchona) extraction across [[lowland-peoples-independent-nation-states-19th-century|
  lowland South America]].

## Limitations and Critique

Historians debate how sharply debt peonage should be distinguished from slavery and from
"free" wage labor: in some cases debt was a genuine constraint, in others a fiction
masking outright coercion, and in still others a reciprocal tie peons themselves invoked to
claim subsistence guarantees from patrones. The category is most useful as a description of
a continuum of unfree labor rather than a single institution.
