# Stave Church

The characteristic medieval timber church of [[norway|Norway]] (ON *stavkirke*),
built of vertical wooden staves. Per
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]] (Nilsén),
Norway's preference for wooden construction throughout the Middle Ages was at once
**economic** (stone ashlar was expensive), **technological** (ship-building and
woodworking skill applied to timber framing), and **material-geographic** (wood was
abundant).

## Form and Survival

Of approximately 1,200 medieval churches built within present-day Norway, only about
**270 were of stone** (concentrated in the richest agricultural districts of
Østlandet and Trøndelag); the rest were timber. **Twenty-eight stave churches
survive.** **Urnes** in Sogn (mid-12th century) is among the oldest, incorporating
carved Viking-Age decoration ([[runestones|Urnes style]]) reused from an earlier
church on the same site. Two main construction types are distinguished: earth-fast
poles set directly in the ground, and the later, more durable sill-frame on a stone
foundation.

## Significance

Because the timber interior did not invite the continuous painted narratives that
covered the walls of stone churches, **no Romanesque wall paintings survive in
[[iceland|Iceland]]** (which shared the timber tradition), and decoration in stave
churches took carved rather than painted form. The stave church is the most
distinctive Scandinavian contribution to medieval ecclesiastical architecture. See
[[norway]], [[saga-literature]].
