# Congregación (Colonial Resettlement)

## Definition

**Congregación** (also *congregación de indios*) was the Spanish colonial policy of
**forcibly or administratively resettling** dispersed indigenous populations into nucleated
towns (*pueblos*) for evangelization, tribute collection, labor drafts, and political control.
It is the Mesoamerican counterpart in function (not always in form) to Andean
[[reduccion|reducción]]. In wiki taxonomy, operational policy content prefers this slug;
[[reduccion]] covers the broader Iberian resettlement cluster (including Toledo’s Andean
*reducciones*).

## Emic / etic

An **etic** administrative category imposed by colonial authorities; indigenous communities
experienced it as dislocation, sometimes as the foundation of lasting *pueblo* identities
that later defended land and autonomy.

## In MacLeod’s CHNPA II.2 overview (central Mexico debate)

Murdo J. MacLeod’s overview of Mesoamerica since the Spanish invasion
([[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v2-2|CHNPA II.2]] Ch. 12) does **not** define
congregación’s operational mechanics. He names it among colonial impositions — alongside new
tributes, Spanish officials, and Indian slave migrations — that the **Nahuatl continuity school**
(Lockhart and others; Sarah Cline’s central Mexico chapter as representative) tends to regard as
**important but secondary** factors compared with persistence of preconquest ways of life
([[altepetl]], *calpulli*/*tlaxilacalli*, local nobilities under Spanish guises). Critics reply
that loss of ~90% of population in an agrarian society (emptying countryside; disrupted
leadership promotion) must have had considerable impact, and that local administrative records
may systematically understate plague, death, and revolution.

For lowland Maya contexts, regional chapters treat periodic roundups as *reductions or
congregations* responding to residential mobility that obstructed control and tribute — a
“leaking dam” never fully plugged ([[lowland-maya-colonial-modern]]). Congregación thus sits
at the hinge of continuity-vs-disruption debates in [[colonial-mesoamerica-native-peoples]].

## Limitations

MacLeod’s overview supplies historiographical placement, not a full institutional treatise.
Mechanics of forced nucleation, legal instruments, and regional chronologies must be filled from
regional monographs and from [[reduccion]] / mission literature
([[spiritual-conquest-and-colonial-church]]).
