# Wampum

## Definition

Shell beads (typically quahog and whelk) strung or woven into belts and strings used in the Northeast for diplomatic agreement, historical record, gift exchange, condolence ritual, and—after European contact—increasingly as a trade medium circulating in large quantities. Haudenosaunee and coastal Algonquian peoples were central to its production and political use. In CHNPA I.1, Peter Nabokov treats wampum as a material mnemonic of [[indigenous-historical-consciousness]]; Neal Salisbury treats its seventeenth-century commercialization as a pivot of the [[eastern-north-american-fur-trade]] and [[eastern-woodlands-colonial-encounter-1600-1783]].

## Indigenous Meanings — Words, Condolence, History

To northeastern Indians, wampum beads were symbolic “words” communicating messages of peace and condolence as well as recording their histories. For the Iroquois ([[haudenosaunee-confederacy]]), wampum was the means whereby the mythical Deganawida assuaged the grief of Hiawatha, paving the way for the formation of the Five Nations Confederacy.

Haudenosaunee condolence canes (walnut pictographs and older birch peg arrangements) aid roll call of founding Confederacy chiefs at installations (Fenton). Wampum belts and strings of white and purple shell beads formalize national and international pledges (Parker). Sir William Johnson in 1753 compared them to letters or rather bonds: “they regard no message or invitation, be it of what consequence it will, unless attended or confirmed by belts or wampum, which they look upon as we our letters, or rather bonds.” Lewis H. Morgan saw belts fortifying confederacy-formation traditions. Foster’s consultants stress prospective organizing of present/future alliances more than purely retrospective recall—so wampum is as much a technology of ongoing diplomacy and [[treaty-sovereignty-native-north-america]] as an archive of the past.

## Seventeenth-Century Commercialization

During the 1620s coastal Algonquians adapted to growing English, Dutch, French, and Iroquois power; a key medium was indigenously crafted marine shell beads. The Dutch found commercial value on Narragansett Bay and Long Island Sound shores; by the mid-1620s specialized winter wampum production developed. Narragansett and Pequot sachems exchanged beads for European goods; the Dutch used beads to attract New France’s allies and sold them to Plymouth traders for Abenakis and Algonquians north of Cape Cod. Once scarce and shaman-handled, wampum now circulated in large quantities favoring Indians adept with Europeans.

### Pequot War and redistribution of production

Smallpox 1633–4 and English immigration undermined fur/wampum traffic. The [[pequot-war-1637]] (Mystic massacre and aftermath) crushed Pequot autonomy; Narragansetts became a primary wampum source. Few Pequot survivors who sought Mohawk support with wampum were killed by Mohawks already gifted more by Narragansetts. Result: English secure on Connecticut; Narragansetts primary wampum producers under English pressure.

### Mohawk distribution and Beaver Wars

Mohawk elevation (after Mahican triumph; minor Pequot role) gave unrivaled access to manufactures and wampum, stimulating trapping until Iroquois territories depleted. With Dutch guns, Mohawks attacked Algonquins and Hurons carrying pelts to Montreal; Mohawks distributed southern New England wampum throughout the Five Nations. After the Dutch West India Company relinquished the Fort Orange monopoly (1639), weapons flow escalated and Iroquois warfare intensified ([[beaver-wars-17th-century]]; [[mohawk]]; [[huron-wendat]]).

### Regional tributary and alliance uses

- **Munsees / New Netherland:** Late-1630s Mahican/Mohawk demand for Munsee wampum tribute; 1643–45 warfare with Dutch and Mahicans; subsequent Peach and Esopus wars.
- **Narragansett–Wampanoag–Mohawk:** For more than two decades after the Pequot War, Narragansetts, Niantics, Pocumtucks and others supplied wampum to Mohawks for the Beaver Wars and received furs for English goods. Pattern of Indian clients providing furs, wampum, maize, land, and labor to English patrons. Archaeology: Narragansett and Wampanoag burials in large cemeteries (high contact-induced mortality) accompanied by European goods and wampum solidifying alliances ([[wampanoag]]; [[narragansett]]).
- **Abenaki frontier:** Plymouth traders from 1625 traded wampum and maize for pelts among Kennebec Abenakis; 1630s Kennebecs’ introduction of New France wampum was initially rejected (fear Canadian furs would go to the English); response improved after some joined Sillery war parties vs Iroquois.
- **King Philip’s War:** Algonquian–Iroquois polarization ended southern New England fur trade and wampum trade to Mohawks, depriving Natives of powerful outsider ties as English demographic/economic growth pressed expansion—context for [[king-philips-war-1675]].

## Material Culture and Historical Consciousness

Nabokov places wampum among multi-scale material historicities: surface-oscillation mnemonics (alongside Plains winter counts, Pima calendar sticks, Ojibwa birchbark charts) that encode diplomatic pledges and historical claims in portable form. Unique named belts, bundles, and clan houses also star in modern repatriation controversies. Wampum thus links ritual performance, interstate diplomacy, commercial transformation under colonialism, and indigenous theories of the past.
