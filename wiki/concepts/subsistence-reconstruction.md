# Subsistence Reconstruction

**Subsistence reconstruction** is the systematic recovery and interpretation of evidence for how prehistoric populations obtained, processed, and scheduled food and other resources. It treats economy as inseparable from social organization and environment.

## Development and Techniques

"Reconstruction of prehistoric subsistence economies... has increased greatly in recent years. New techniques have been devised for the recovery of plant and animal remains from archaeological sites... There has also been a great increase in research on the prehistoric environment, especially the earth's vegetation and man's effect on it, studied through the analysis of pollen deposits." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.1 / range lines ~440-460)

Particular attention is paid to "the careful scheduling necessary on the part of hunter-gathering communities for successful use of the local resources, which may include a wide variety of foods, but which may also be highly seasonal in their availability and of fluctuating occurrence from year to year." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.1 / range lines ~490-500)

The complex variations in human–animal relationships (hunting to domestication) are also central.

## Integration with Society

"The subsistence economy cannot be understood in isolation from the society which practised it, for the two are inseparable." Among foragers, resource exploitation and mobility set limits on population density, group size, and settlement. For farmers, domestication provided new resources and labour scheduling shaped the annual timetable. Social demands could also drive production (e.g., surplus for status or urban supply). (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.1 / range lines ~510-535)

Storage, exchange to even out variation, and elite regulation of food supply are key topics.
