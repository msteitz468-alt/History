# Colonial Forms of Knowledge

The idea, crystallized by **Bernard Cohn** in *[[cohn-colonialism-forms-of-knowledge|Colonialism and Its
Forms of Knowledge]]* (1996), that **colonial rule was constituted through the production of knowledge** —
that to govern a subject society the colonizer had first to make it **knowable**: surveyed, mapped,
enumerated, codified and classified. Knowledge was not a by-product of conquest but one of its
instruments. The empirical complement to [[orientalism|Said's *Orientalism*]].

## The Core Claim

Cohn's "the conquest of India was a conquest of knowledge" names a process by which colonial states
deployed **"investigative modalities"** — the survey, the census, legal codification, linguistics,
history-writing, the museum, the ritual of the durbar — to convert an alien society into an object that
could be administered. In doing so they **reified** fluid social realities into fixed administrative
categories: bounded **castes**, mutually exclusive religious **communities** ("Hindu"/"Muslim"), and a
timeless "**customary tradition**." Identities that had been plural, situational and negotiable became, on
the census form and in the law court, hard and countable.

## Significance

- It specifies the **mechanism** behind Said's discourse: not just representation in literature, but the
  bureaucratic machinery of rule (census, cadastre, law).
- It reframes "tradition," caste and communalism as in part **products of the colonial state**, with large
  downstream consequences for nationalism, partition and post-colonial politics.
- It is foundational to **"colonial governmentality"** (Foucault's *governmentality* applied to empire),
  on which the wiki's [[imperialism|imperialism]] page draws.

## Critique (etic)

The strong constructivist reading is **contested**: many South Asianists argue that caste, religious
identity and statecraft had deep **pre-colonial** roots that colonial knowledge reshaped rather than
invented, and warn against crediting the colonial state with total power to remake society. As with
[[orientalism|Orientalism]], the framework risks erasing **indigenous agency**.
