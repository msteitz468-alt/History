The intimate but hierarchical relationship between tactics and strategy. Tactics is simultaneously the base of strategy and its servant. Strategy provides the "why" and context; tactics the "how" in battle. Friedman argues against artificial separation (e.g., the operational level as sometimes practiced) and insists that tactical theory must always consider strategic subordination while strategy cannot ignore tactical realities.

Key from Friedman: strategy and tactics are "intimately related"; a tactical system cannot be developed in a vacuum of strategy.
