# Kiddush ha-Shem (Jewish Martyrdom)

**Kiddush ha-Shem** ("sanctification of the Name") is the Jewish ideal of dying rather
than transgressing — in classical rabbinic law, rather than committing idolatry, murder,
or forbidden sexual acts even under compulsion. In the medieval period it acquired a
distinctive and intense form in **Ashkenaz** (northern Christian Europe), where it came
to encompass **active martyrdom**, including parents killing their children and
themselves rather than submit to forced baptism.

## The Ashkenazic Form

The paradigmatic episode is the **Rhineland massacres of the
[[first-crusade-1095|First Crusade]] (1096)**, whose Hebrew chronicles record Jews
choosing death — "to sanctify Thy Great Name" — over conversion. Per Jacob Katz, several
factors made this ideal so pronounced in Ashkenaz: the view of Christianity as
theologically idolatrous (obliging death rather than submission), revulsion at Christian
symbols such as the cross, and possibly emulation of the surrounding Christian
martyrological culture. It became the organizing motif of a rich Ashkenazic
**"chain of persecution"** literature (the three chronicles of 1096; Ephraim of Bonn's
*Sefer Zekhira*) and of the *qinot* (dirges) that fed what Salo Baron called the
"[[lachrymose-conception-of-jewish-history|lachrymose conception]]" of Jewish history.

## The Islamic-World Contrast

[[cohen-under-crescent-cross-1994|Mark Cohen]] draws a sharp comparison: under Islam,
faced with the [[almohads|Almohad]] forced conversions, the response was markedly
different. [[maimonides|Maimonides']] and his father's epistles on the persecution
**rationalized mass apostasy** as survivable (covert continued Jewish practice), on the
grounds that Islam — unlike Christianity — is not idolatrous, rather than glorifying
martyrdom. Correspondingly, the Islamic world produced very little martyrological
memorial literature; where Jews under Islam did commemorate, the genre was typically
*miraculous deliverance* (the "Egyptian Scroll" of 1012), not *kiddush ha-Shem*. The
Sijilmasa letter of 1148 (≈150 killed "sanctifying the Name") shows the ideal was known
there too, but it did not seed a literature of suffering.
