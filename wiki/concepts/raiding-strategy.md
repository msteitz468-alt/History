# Raiding Strategy

In Archer Jones' framework, a strategy aimed at the destruction, exhaustion, or denial of the enemy's resources, forces, or will without the objective of permanent territorial occupation or control. It avoids decisive battle when at a disadvantage and seeks to impose costs through harassment, denial, or selective destruction.

Characteristics:
- Lower logistic burden (live off the land or avoid fixed positions).
- Often used by the weaker party or in terrain unfavorable to persisting armies (guerrilla, steppe nomads, commerce raiding at sea).
- Examples: Parthian horse archers vs Romans, Fabian strategy against Hannibal, English chevauchées in Hundred Years' War, Spanish guerrillas vs Napoleon, submarine commerce war, modern insurgencies.
- Can be offensive (exhaust the enemy) or defensive (deny the enemy the fruits of occupation).

Related to persisting strategy as the alternative fundamental type. Often combined or alternated.
