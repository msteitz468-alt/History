# Ayllu

## Definition

The **ayllu** is the foundational **kin-based, land-holding corporate group** of Andean
society: a community whose members claim descent from a common ancestor (often a mummified
*mallki* or a founding *huaca*), hold rights to land collectively, and owe one another
reciprocal labor. Ayllus were typically nested and **dually organized** into upper and lower
moieties (*hanan/hurin*), an arrangement scaled all the way up to the organization of Cuzco
and of [[inca-empire|Tawantinsuyu]] itself.

The ayllu is **emic** — an indigenous category still in use — and it is the social unit through
which the Andean economy of **reciprocity** and the **[[vertical-archipelago|vertical
archipelago]]** operated: land in multiple ecological tiers was allocated to ayllu households,
and ayllu labor obligations fed the **mit'a** rotational service owed to lords and to the state.

## Significance in Volume III

In [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|Volume III]], the ayllu is the connective
tissue between archaeology and ethnohistory: the [[aymara-kingdoms|Aymara señoríos]], the Inka
imperial system, and the colonial **reducciones** all rest on (or attack) the ayllu. Spanish
colonial restructuring tried to convert ayllu labor and tribute to its own ends (encomienda,
the Toledan mita), making the ayllu both a victim of and a vehicle for survival under
colonial rule.
