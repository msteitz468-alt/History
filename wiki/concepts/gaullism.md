# Gaullism

Gaullism refers to the foreign policy doctrine associated with Charles de Gaulle and his successors, prioritizing French national sovereignty, nuclear independence (force de frappe), opposition to superpower dominance, and a "Europe of states" rather than supranational integration dominated by the US or UK.

In the Cold War context (Ch. 8 in Leffler/Westad Vol. II), it manifested in France's withdrawal from NATO integrated command (1966), recognition of China, criticism of US in Vietnam, and pursuit of détente with the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe on French terms.

## Key Elements from Vol. II

- Opposition to US hegemony and "Atlanticism".
- Promotion of French grandeur and leadership in Europe.
- Skepticism of European integration if it diluted national sovereignty.
- Independent diplomacy toward the Third World and recognition of realities like divided Germany and China.
