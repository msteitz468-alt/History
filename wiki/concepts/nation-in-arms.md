# The Nation in Arms (levée en masse)

The mobilization of an **entire national population and economy for war**, born of the
**[[french-revolution-1789|French Revolution]]**: the *levée en masse* of 1793 conscripted citizens
en masse and harnessed national enthusiasm, manpower, and industry to the war effort. For Doughty &
Gruber this is the **decisive break with [[limited-war|limited war]]** — it gave revolutionary and
then Napoleonic France **mass armies that could absorb losses, fight for ideological as well as
dynastic ends, and pursue the enemy's destruction** rather than a province.

The nation in arms supplied the *manpower* that the **[[napoleonic-warfare|Napoleonic system]]** then
organized into decisive instruments; over the nineteenth century, married to industrialization and
[[otto-von-bismarck|Prussian]] conscription, it pointed toward the **[[total-war|total war]]** of the
twentieth.
