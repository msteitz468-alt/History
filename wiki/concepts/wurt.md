# Wurt

**Wurt** (plural wurten; also known as terp) refers to an artificial dwelling mound constructed in low-lying or tidal coastal areas of northern Europe to raise settlements above flood levels.

## Definition and Scope

The glossary entry cross-references the term succinctly:

> "wurt (pl. wurten): See terp." (range_08:88–89)

In the encyclopedia's treatment of Early Middle Ages / Migration Period settlement (Part 7), wurts/terps represent a characteristic adaptation of Germanic and related coastal populations in the North Sea and Baltic margins. These mounds were built incrementally from refuse, clay, and other materials, creating stable platforms for houses, byres, and fields in marshy or tidally flooded landscapes.

## Construction and Function

Wurts allowed year-round occupation and animal husbandry in environments otherwise unsuitable for permanent settlement. Typical features include:
- Layered construction accumulating over centuries.
- Associated with specialized economies exploiting marine resources, salt, and pasture.
- Often paired with systems of ditches, weirs (glossary: "weir: A barrier set in water to channel fish..."), and water management.

The glossary also defines related terms useful for understanding the context:
- "transhumance: The movement of livestock seasonally between upland and lowland pasture."
- "weir: A barrier set in water to channel fish or other quarry into a trap or a fence in tidal areas..."

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Central to the settlement archaeology of the [[periods/early-middle-ages]] in northern and western Europe.
- Associated with Anglo-Saxon, Frisian, and other Germanic groups; links to [[actors/celts]] in broader Insular and coastal contexts where Celtic-speaking populations interacted with these systems.
- Distinct from inland Viereckschanzen or hillforts; represents a response to environmental constraints rather than primarily defensive or ritual needs.
- Complements discussion of emporia (wics) and early towns in the same period.

## Geographic and Chronological Scope

Concentrated in the coastal lowlands of the Netherlands, northern Germany, Denmark, and parts of eastern England and the Baltic from the late Roman / Migration Period through the Early Middle Ages (roughly 4th–10th centuries CE and earlier local precursors). The form persisted or evolved into medieval terp landscapes.

## Historiography

Wurts/terps were among the first systematically investigated settlement monuments in northern Europe. Excavations have revealed long sequences of rebuilding and rich environmental data (animal bones, plant remains) preserved in the anaerobic mound deposits. The glossary's cross-reference reflects the interchangeable use of "wurt" and "terp" in the literature.
