# Archon

The chief magistracy of Athens (and, in variant local forms, of many other Greek city-states), originally the highest executive office of the polis before the development of the developed Classical democracy. Athens had a board of nine annual archons, headed by the archon eponymous (who gave his name to the year for dating purposes), alongside the archon basileus (retaining religious and some judicial functions once held by the old kings) and the polemarch (originally military commander, later with judicial responsibilities over resident foreigners).

## Historical Development

Originally the pinnacle of aristocratic political power in Archaic Athens, filled from and by the leading aristocratic families, the archonship's practical political weight diminished as the fifth-century democracy shifted effective power toward the Assembly, the Council of 500 (boule), and elected generals (strategoi); by the Classical period the archonship was filled by lot among eligible citizens and had become largely ceremonial and judicial rather than a locus of real executive authority. Because Athenian chronology was reckoned by the sequence of eponymous archons, ancient historians (including Thucydides) used archon lists — alongside comparable systems such as dating by Argive priestesses — to fix and cross-check dates for events.
