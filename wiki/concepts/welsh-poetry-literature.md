# Welsh Poetry Literature

## Definition and Scope

Welsh poetry literature spans from early heroic and praise poetry (cynfeirdd, gogynfeirdd) through the cywyddwyr of the later Middle Ages to modern and revival contexts. It features strict metres, praise of patrons, elegies, and debates on the craft of poetry itself. Distinct yet interactive with Welsh prose literature.

## Key Characteristics and Examples (Grounded Verbatim)

Early references note “numerous references to [ymrysonau] in the poems of the Gogynfeirdd.” “There are numerous references to gold torcs (eurdorchawr) in the early Welsh heroic elegies known as the Gododdin. In the Llywarch Hen saga englynion, the hero Gwên fab Llywarch is once referred to as eurdorchawc ‘wearing a gold torc’.”

“Trawsganu Cynan Garwyn mab Brochfael (Eulogy [?] for Cynan Garwyn son of Brochfael) is a Welsh praise poem, in 50 two-stress rhyming lines, which survives in the 14th-century manuscript Llyfr Taliesin... On the basis of his position in the genealogies and the death of his son and successor Selyf (Solomon) at the battle of Chester (Caer) c. 615, Cynan’s period of activity was c. 575×c. 610.”

“Ymrysonau (bardic contests or debates) are a common feature of early Welsh poetry... by the time of the Cywyddwyr the ymryson was mainly used as a framework for a debate concerning the craft and purpose of poetry itself. One of the earliest of these debates for which we have evidence was that between Gruffudd Gryg (fl. 1357–70), a defender of the native bardic tradition, and Dafydd ap Gwilym, the innovator. Another famous debate... occurred between Siôn Cent and Rhys Goch Eryri (c. 1365–c. 1440). The best known of the medieval ymrysonau is a very lengthy poetic debate consisting of 54 cywyddau between Wiliam Cynwal (†1587/8) and Edmwnd Prys (1543/4–1623), which spanned the years 1581–7.”

“Ymryson y Beirdd, a light-hearted bardic competition between teams of poets from all parts of Wales (Cymru), is a popular feature in the literary pavilion of the National Eisteddfod (Eisteddfod Genedlaethol Cymru) and a similar contest called Talwrn y Beirdd is also a successful radio series.”

Poetry often preserves post-Roman heroic epithets and pre-Roman status symbols in literary form (e.g. torc references as heroic epithet).

## Interaction

Interacts with prose (e.g. in manuscript compilations), French romance contacts (via Arthurian adaptations), and modern revival (Eisteddfod). Central to bardic order and patronage systems in medieval Wales.
