# Decisive points

In Jomini's system, decisive points are locations "capable of exercising a marked influence either upon the result of the campaign or upon a single enterprise." Identifying and acting upon them is central to successful strategy.

## Jomini's Treatment

- Strategic decisive points are selected after choosing the theater and base of operations.
- They determine the "most favorable direction for operations."
- Commanders demonstrate greatness "by massing force against weaker enemy forces at some decisive point."
- Tactical decisive points appear once armies are in contact and the battle develops.
- The fundamental principle of strategy is to bring the mass of one's forces against these points while threatening the enemy's communications.

The concept assumes roughly equal units; superiority at the decisive point (in numbers, position, or timing) decides the outcome.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Closely tied to [[jominian-strategy]], [[interior-lines]], bases of operations, and lines of operations.
- Contrasts with Clausewitz's [[center-of-gravity]]: Jomini's version is more geometric and locational; Clausewitz's is more dynamic and often moral or systemic.
- Influenced later ideas of concentration, Schwerpunkt, and key terrain.

## Primary Grounding from Jomini's Text

"Decisive points" are locations "capable of exercising a marked influence either upon the result of the campaign or upon a single enterprise." Identify them after choosing theater/base; they determine the most favorable direction. "Commanders would show their greatness by massing force against weaker enemy forces at some decisive point." Strategic (geographic permanent or communications junctions, capitals, defiles) vs. accidental maneuver-based (enemy flank for cutting base/support; center if extended). Objective points of maneuver prioritize destroying enemy forces over mere territory. "In every position a general may occupy, he has only to decide whether to operate by the right, by the left, or by the front."

Full details and locators (Art. XIX, etc.): [[sources/jomini-the-art-of-war-1862]].

## Historiography

Widely taught in the 19th century. Later critics note that "decisive points" could become self-neutralizing if both sides followed the same principles, and that real campaigns often turned on factors Jomini under-weighted (morale, chance, politics).
