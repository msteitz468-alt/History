# Archaeological Science

An umbrella term for the family of laboratory-based scientific methods applied to archaeological material to extract information unavailable from typology or excavation context alone: radiocarbon and other absolute dating techniques, ancient DNA analysis ([[archaeogenetics]]), stable isotope analysis (for diet and mobility), dendrochronology, and residue and use-wear analysis, among others.

## Scope and Impact

These methods have transformed archaeology's evidentiary base since the mid-twentieth century, allowing absolute chronology independent of stylistic seriation, direct genetic reconstruction of ancient population movements and admixture, and reconstruction of individual life histories (diet, migration, health) from skeletal remains — evidence classes entirely unavailable to earlier generations of archaeologists working from artifact typology and stratigraphy alone.

## Relationship to Traditional Archaeology

Archaeological science does not replace conventional excavation, typology, and contextual analysis but supplements and increasingly disciplines it, at times overturning long-held culture-historical interpretations (for example, ancient DNA studies have repeatedly revised assumptions about whether cultural change reflected population migration or purely local adoption of new practices).
