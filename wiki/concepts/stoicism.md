# Stoicism

**Stoicism** was the dominant Hellenistic and imperial philosophical school counselling a life of **virtue in agreement with nature**, founded by [[zeno-of-citium|Zeno of Citium]] in the painted colonnade (*Stoa Poikile*) of [[athens|Athens]] c.310–300 BCE. Alongside [[epicureanism|Epicureanism]] it shaped elite ethics from the third century BCE through the high Roman Empire (Seneca, [[marcus-aurelius|Marcus Aurelius]], Epictetus).

## Definition and Scope

Conventionally divided into **Old** (Zeno, Cleanthes, Chrysippus), **Middle** (Panaetius c.185–109; Posidonius 135–51), and **Late**/Roman phases (Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius). **Jonathan Barnes**’s survey in the *Oxford History of the Classical World* (1986) stresses that the school was not doctrinally uniform: Ariston of Chios reduced philosophy to ethics alone; Chrysippus differed from Zeno and Cleanthes on many points — “if Chrysippus had not existed, the Stoa would not have existed either.” Old Stoic map: reject hedonism, life of virtue; materialism without atomism; empiricism with a major role for reason.

## Core Doctrines (Old Stoa — Barnes)

| Branch | Stoic position (Barnes / OHCW) |
|---|---|
| Ethics | Goal = live in agreement with nature = virtue; path from self-constitution (*oikeiosis*) to social altruism; *kathekon* (appropriate acts reason selects); virtues as knowledge/theorems; perfect virtuous acts = *katorthomata*; “virtue alone is good” means indifferents (health, wealth) are preferred/demoted but good only when well used; joy (*chara*) not pleasure; paradoxes often verbal |
| Physics | Two principles — passive qualityless matter and active reason/god (itself material); continuous gapless infinitely divisible blend (no void, no atoms); active principle as fire/breath creates elements and cosmos; providence; souls as corporeal fragments of active principle; cyclical conflagration and identical recurrence |
| Logic | Propositional: *axioma*; connectives if/and/or; five Chrysippan **indemonstrables** as base of a sophisticated system; proof reveals unclear conclusion via agreed premises; signs as antecedent in sound conditional; criterion of truth = only “apprehensive impressions” (could not derive from any other object) |

Passions (desire, delight, fear, grief) are expellable because, for Chrysippus, they are **beliefs** and thus rationally controllable. Shared ethical goal with Epicureans is *eudaimonia*/tranquillity (*ataraxia*), but Stoics **expel passions** rather than redefine pleasure. Stoics urged **political involvement** (the wise man may assume kingship or accompany a king), contrasting Epicurean withdrawal from politics (while valuing friendship). Barnes notes that to an external observer a sober Epicurean and a virtuous Stoic would look little different.

### Free will and fate (Barnes)

Epicurus’ **atomic swerve** (uncaused) vs Chrysippus’ fate as a chain of causes with distinction of **perfect vs auxiliary** causes (cylinder-roll analogy) — “inspired the subtlest ancient analysis of causation.” When Stoics say everything happens by fate in virtue of antecedent causes, they mean “not perfect and principal but auxiliary and proximate causes.”

### Logic and epistemology (Barnes)

“Many people believe that if the gods studied logic it would be the logic of Chrysippus.” Epicureans rejected dialectic as redundant, preferring “canonics” (confirmation/disconfirmation; later Philodemus *On Signs*). Academics attacked “apprehensive impressions” with twins/eggs/wax apples; without belief life is impossible (precipice argument — also used by Lucretius for sense-trust).

## Middle and Late Stoicism

- **Posidonius of Apamea** (135–51 BCE; friend of [[cicero|Cicero]] and [[pompey|Pompey]]): Middle Stoic polymath who continued [[polybius|Polybius]] as historian; Celtic ethnography; Atlantic tides; logic/maths/botany/zoology/seismology — “he Aristotelizes.” Barnes: a giant apart with **no followers**; polymathy outmoded. First-century BCE “turbid waters”: Middle Stoa eclectic; New Academy ends as Antiochus of Ascalon (fl. c.85) becomes Stoic in all but name; Aenesidemus rekindles Pyrrhonism; Andronicus of Rhodes (fl. c.50) edits Aristotle.
- **Roman Late Stoa:** Seneca the Younger (tutor/minister under Nero); **Epictetus** (former slave; *Discourses* via Arrian; “Endure and Renounce”; control vs non-control); **Marcus Aurelius** (*Meditations* as private campaign notes — “Platonizing Stoic” lean).

## Interaction

- Contrasts with [[epicureanism]] on pleasure vs virtue as *summum bonum*, political engagement, and physics (atoms vs continuum).
- Interacts with [[presocratics|Presocratic]] and Academic traditions; later with Middle Platonism and early Christian ethics (“Seneca saepe noster,” Tertullian).
- Institutional home remained Athens’ Porch while science professionalized at [[alexandria|Alexandria]] under the [[ptolemaic-egypt|Ptolemies]].
- Lasting legacy (Barnes): Porch/Garden systems + **narrower introspective conception of philosophy** vs Aristotelian unity of science and philosophy.

## Historiography

The OHCW (Barnes; Meredith) is a mid-1980s synthesis: clear three-phase scheme, strong on ethical comparison with Epicureanism and on Chrysippan logic/physics, lighter on papyrological and technical logical scholarship developed since. Doctrinal diversity within the “school” label is already flagged. Astrology’s link to Stoic fatalism is tenuous for the Old Stoa and Hellenistic astronomers (Barnes).
