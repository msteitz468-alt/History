# Byzantine Grand Strategy

The distinctive strategic system of the eastern Roman (Byzantine) empire, characterized by the integration of diplomacy, intelligence, religious and dynastic tools, prestige, and military methods emphasizing maneuver, economy of force, attrition, and avoidance of decisive battle when possible. Luttwak presents it as a richer, more sophisticated evolution of Roman strategy adapted to chronic resource inferiority and multiple fronts.

## Core Features (per Luttwak)

- Heavy reliance on "persuasion" (envoys, religious statecraft, imperial prestige, dynastic marriages, geography of alliances) alongside force.
- "Invention" in the 5th–6th centuries in response to the Hunnic crisis and western collapse. Luttwak holds the foundations were already in place before Justinian.
- “Operational code” = a set of observed and imputable behavioral norms (term adopted from Leites’ Politburo study; Luttwak notes resonance with the “Byzantine Generals Problem” in distributed computing). Drawn from military manuals (Strategikon of Maurikios, Leo VI's Taktika, Nikephoros Phokas, etc.): preference for indirect approaches, intelligence, fortifications, naval power (Greek fire, dromons), relational maneuver, small-unit action, and calibrated use of force.
- Long-term survival for 800+ years despite repeated existential threats from steppe, Persia/Islam, and later the West.

## From Mitchell, Later Roman Empire (2015)

Source: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015]].

- **Maritime empire after Justinian.** End-Justinian reconquest transformed strategic geography: power keys no longer Illyrican roads and fortified cities but major ports (Carthage, Cartagena, Marseilles, Genoa, Portus, Naples, Lilybaeum, Syracuse, Otranto, Brundisium, Ancona, Ravenna, Venice, Salona) for armies, officials, tax, and food. Same maritime logic later explains residual Italian holdings vs Lombards and eventual ability to hold the capital while losing land depth in Balkans/Levant—a strategic structure complementary to Luttwak’s diplomacy-and-maneuver model.
