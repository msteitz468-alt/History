# Early Medieval Clothing and Textiles

## Definition and Scope

Textile and clothing production was an essential domestic industry in preindustrial early medieval Europe (c. A.D. 800–1000, continental Europe and Viking Age Scandinavia). Entire garments are rarely preserved, but fragments, tools, written records, and visual representations allow reconstruction of production and dress. Production was primarily household-based by women, with some professional centers and significant imports (silk, cotton from Middle/Near East and eastern Mediterranean).

## Production

**Fibers and preparation:** Flax for linen cloth; wool for woolen cloth. Silk and cotton imported, not locally produced. Wool fibers combed with wooden combs with long iron teeth (aligned fibers, separated short from long). Flax fibers removed from tough stem then combed.

**Spinning:** Distaff held loose combed fibers; spindle weighted with whorl used to twist yarn. Spinner held distaff in one hand, spinning and dropping spindle to pull and twist fibers downward.

**Weaving:** Warp-weighted looms used throughout most of Europe and Scandinavia. Two uprights (~2 m tall) leaning against wall or rafter; horizontal beam with holes for attaching warp (lengthwise) strands. Loom weights (stone or baked clay) held warp taut. Fabric woven top to bottom; weaver walked back and forth inserting weft and beating upward. Woven cloth wrapped around beam as work progressed.

**Dyeing and finishing:** Evidence of dye plants at sites like York. Secondary techniques included dyeing.

## Clothing (Viking Age Scandinavia)

**Female costume:** Several layers. First: linen shift (smooth or pleated, long sleeves, long skirt). Over this: tunic of imported silk or other fabric held by pair of tortoiseshell brooches. Shirt or caftan over tunic fastened with trefoil brooch, equal-armed brooch, or large round fibula. In cold weather: cape or coat closed with fibula. Leather booties and cap or other headgear.

**Male costume:** Leggings or wide knee-length breeches. Woolen jacket with overlapping front or sleeved coat with bronze buttons (similar to riding caftan). Leather belt, boots, hat or cap.

Both adorned with trimmings and ornamentation from luxury materials (silk, precious metals, furs): woven bands, braid work, embroidery.

## Key Sites and Preservation

- **Oseberg (Norway):** Grave of wealthy woman (A.D. 834) with textile production tools including weaving tablets with unfinished braid.
- **York (England):** Early medieval urban center (Anglian Northumbria then Danelaw). Evidence of raw wool/flax, dye plants, spinning/weaving equipment, textile fragments. Textiles survived under waterlogged conditions.
- **Birka (Sweden):** A.D. 750–970. Many linen/woolen fabrics + imported Byzantine silk. Textiles preserved by metallic salts from metal objects.

Textiles are fragile. Agents of decay: water, oxygen, pH, bacteria, salts, temperature, overburden, organisms. Preservation in absence of oxygen (waterlogged) or close contact with metals (salts act as biocides or impregnate fibers). In wet climates of Europe/Scandinavia: primarily waterlogged sites or metal-contact contexts.
