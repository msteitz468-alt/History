# Zoroastrianism

## Definition

**Zoroastrianism** is the ancient Iranian religion founded on the teachings of the
prophet **Zoroaster (Zarathustra)**, centered on the supreme creator **Ahura Mazda
(Ohrmazd)**, a cosmic dualism of truth/order (*asha*/*arta*) against falsehood/chaos
(*druj*), and an ethical demand that humans align with the good in a struggle that
will end in cosmic renewal. It is one of the five "classic" traditions of the
[[axial-age|Axial Age]] (though the case CWH IV and Bellah treat most cautiously,
for lack of sources), and the ideological backbone of successive Iranian
[[empire|empires]].

## Origins and Dating

The earliest layer of the **Avesta** — the **Gathas** attributed to Zoroaster — is
linguistically dated to roughly **1000 BCE**, contemporaneous with the appearance of
Iranian-speaking peoples (Medes, Parthians, Persians) on the Iranian Plateau
([[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|Daryaee, CWH IV Ch 10]]). The prophet's exact date and place
are genuinely uncertain (estimates range over centuries), which is why this concept
is marked **contested** and why Zoroastrianism is the most disputed of the Axial
cases (see [[axial-age-controversy]]).

## Zoroastrianism and Empire

The religion's political role grew across the period:
- **[[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid]]** kings invoked **Ahura Mazda** and ruled in
  the name of *arta* (cosmic order) and *data* (law) — though they tolerated the cults
  of subject peoples; the degree of their "Zoroastrianism" is debated.
- The **[[parthian-empire|Parthians]]** adopted Zoroastrian legitimating ideology
  (the "Kingly Glory") but promoted religion lightly.
- The **[[sasanian-empire|Sasanians]]** made Zoroastrianism a true **imperial state
  religion** and "orthodoxy": kings shown receiving the diadem from Ohrmazd and
  Anahita, the Magi codifying the Avesta, fire-temples founded across Iranshahr, and
  rival faiths (Manichaeism, at times Christianity, Judaism, Buddhism) persecuted as
  the priesthood consolidated doctrine.

This trajectory exemplifies the [[axial-age]] thesis that post-Axial empires drew
new legitimacy from association with a universalizing religious vision (see
[[empire]], [[universal-religions]]).

## Legacy

After the [[arab-conquests|Arab conquest]], Zoroastrianism declined under Islam but
left deep marks — on Iranian Islamic culture, on Jewish, Christian, and Gnostic
ideas of dualism, angelology, and eschatology, and on [[manichaeism|Manichaeism]].
Surviving communities (the Parsis of India, the Zoroastrians of Iran) preserve it
today.
