# Brownian Motion

**1905**. Einstein’s paper provided the first quantitative molecular-kinetic explanation of the random jitter of microscopic particles suspended in liquids, thereby furnishing decisive evidence for the physical reality of atoms and molecules.

## Definition and Scope

In 1828 Scottish botanist Robert Brown observed that tiny pollen grains (and later inorganic particles) suspended in water exhibit continuous, irregular zig-zag motion visible under a microscope. Earlier explanations invoked currents, light, or other macroscopic causes. With the rise of kinetic theory, some attributed it to molecular collisions, but skeptics noted a water molecule seemed too small to budge a visible particle (10,000× larger).

Einstein showed that although any single collision is negligible, the statistical imbalance of millions of random collisions per second produces observable net displacements. The suspended particle executes a “random walk.” The mean square displacement grows proportionally to time (specifically, distance ∝ √t). Einstein derived precise, testable formulas relating displacement to particle size, temperature, and viscosity.

Paper (May 1905): “In this paper it will be shown that... bodies of a microscopically visible size suspended in liquids must, as a result of thermal molecular motions, perform motions of such magnitudes that they can be easily observed with a microscope.”

He deliberately framed it as a theoretical deduction from molecular-kinetic theory rather than an *ex post facto* explanation of observed Brownian data (though he knew of the observations). This paralleled his approach in the light-quanta and relativity papers.

## Immediate Impact

Within months Henry Seidentopf confirmed the predictions with a powerful microscope. The work “did more than any other work to convince physicists of the reality of atoms and molecules” (Max Born). It also supplied an independent route to Avogadro’s number. “It bristles with new ideas... the final conclusion... never fails to cause a moment of astonishment” (Abraham Pais).

## Historiography (Isaacson)

Einstein presented the result as flowing from his ongoing statistical analysis of fluctuations (linking heat, radiation, and molecular behavior). The demurral that he might not be describing *the* Brownian motion was technically accurate: the paper is a prediction from first principles. The patent-office context and prior papers on molecular dimensions made the 1905 cluster coherent. Later experimentalists (Perrin et al.) used it to pin down molecular sizes definitively.

**Grounding quotes (range 1)**: “Such movement of suspended bodies has actually been observed by physiologists who call it Brownian molecular motion.” (Habicht letter). “If the motion discussed here can be observed, then classical thermodynamics can no longer be viewed as strictly valid.” “Let us hope that a researcher will soon succeed...”
