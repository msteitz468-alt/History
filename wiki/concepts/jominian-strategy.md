# Jominian strategy

Jominian strategy is the prescriptive, geometric system of operational art articulated by Antoine-Henri Jomini. It treats strategy as the application of fixed, teachable principles to bring the mass of an army successively against the decisive points of a theater of war while protecting one's own base and lines of communication.

## Core Elements (from Jomini's text)

- **Selection of theater, base, and zone of operations**: The commander, in concert with the head of state, chooses the theater and base, which determine the zone.
- **Objective points and lines of operations**: Choose the first (and subsequent) objective point(s) and the line leading to it "giving it the most advantageous direction."
- **Decisive points**: Identify the points "capable of exercising a marked influence either upon the result of the campaign or upon a single enterprise."
- **Interior lines**: Operate from a central position to strike separated enemy forces in succession (the classic Jominian advantage).
- **Distinction of levels**: "Strategy decides where to act; logistics brings the troops to this point; grand tactics decides the manner of execution and the employment of the troops."
- **Fundamental principle**: Bring the greatest part of the forces upon the important point of the theater or zone.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Contrasts with Clausewitzian strategy (see [[clausewitz-carl-von]], [[clausewitz-on-war-1832]], [[paradoxical-logic-of-strategy]]): Jomini offers clarity and rules; Clausewitz emphasizes friction, uncertainty, and war as continuation of policy.
- Related to later ideas of concentration, economy of force, and maneuver.
- Frequently paired with "interior lines" and "decisive point" as the signature Jominian tools.
- Critiqued in the 20th century as the "false science" (Freedman) that could lead to stalemate if both sides learned the same maxims.

## Primary Grounding from Jomini's Text (1862)

Strategy is "the art of bringing the greatest part of the forces of an army upon the important point of the theater of war or of the zone of operations." It "decides where to act"; logistics brings the troops; grand tactics decides execution. The single great principle: mass the army successively on decisive points of the theater (and communications) while securing one's own. Choose theater/base/zone/objective/line of operations accordingly. Interior lines enable concentrating faster than the enemy can oppose. Change of strategic front is among the most important grand maneuvers. "The whole of strategy is contained in" examples such as Ulm, Jena, Ratisbon.

Full claims, verbatim quotes, and locators (Ch. III, Arts. XVI–XXIX, Epitome, etc.): [[sources/jomini-the-art-of-war-1862]].

## Historiography

Jomini was the dominant interpreter of Napoleonic warfare for most of the 19th century and shaped American military education. Later scholarship (Paret's *Makers of Modern Strategy*, Freedman's *Strategy: A History*) positions him as more prescriptive and optimistic than Clausewitz, easier to teach but ultimately shallower. His influence persisted in staff-college curricula even as Clausewitz rose in prestige.
