# Tahāfut al-Tahāfut (The Incoherence of the Incoherence)

[[ibn-rushd|Ibn Rushd]]'s point-by-point rebuttal (c. 1180) of [[tahafut-al-falasifa|al-Ghazālī's
*Incoherence of the Philosophers*]] — the work's title literally inverts al-Ghazālī's. Averroes quotes
al-Ghazālī passage by passage and answers each of the twenty Discussions, defending the
[[islamic-philosophy|falāsifa]] and, above all, the authentic Aristotle.

Its argument cuts two ways. Against [[al-ghazali|al-Ghazālī]] it defends the
[[averroes-on-eternity|eternity of the world]], real [[averroes-on-causality|natural causation]] (against
[[occasionalism-islamic|occasionalism]]), and God's knowledge of the world, and it charges that
al-Ghazālī's refutations are merely dialectical (*jadalī*) rather than demonstrative. But Averroes also
turns on [[ibn-sina|Avicenna]], arguing that many of the doctrines al-Ghazālī attacked were Avicenna's
Neoplatonizing distortions ([[emanationism-avicennan|emanation]], the
[[necessary-being-avicenna|necessary/contingent being]] distinction) and not the genuine teaching of
Aristotle. The work became the classic statement of the philosophers' case in the
[[philosophy-versus-theology-in-islam|debate between falsafa and kalam]], and — translated into Latin and
Hebrew — a major channel of [[arabic-philosophy-latin-reception|Averroism into Europe]].
