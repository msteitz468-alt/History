# Scaligerian Chronology

"Scaligerian chronology" is the term fringe theorist [[actors/anatoly-fomenko|Anatoly Fomenko]] and other "New Chronology" proponents use for the standard, consensus chronological framework of world history — the conventional dating of ancient and medieval events accepted by mainstream historians, classicists, and archaeologists. The name derives from Joseph Scaliger, the Renaissance humanist whose systematic cross-synchronization of Greek, Roman, Egyptian, Babylonian, and biblical calendars and king-lists established the methodological basis for modern comparative chronology, later refined and independently cross-checked by astronomical dating (eclipse records), stratigraphy, dendrochronology, and radiocarbon dating.

Mainstream chronology does not rest on Scaliger's 16th–17th-century work alone; it has been extensively revised, corrected, and independently corroborated over four centuries by multiple, mutually reinforcing dating methods drawn from archaeology, astronomy, epigraphy, and the natural sciences, producing a chronology treated as settled and mutually consistent across the vast majority of professional historians and scientists.

The "Scaligerian" label functions in New Chronology literature primarily as a rhetorical device to cast the accepted timeline as a single fallible authority's outdated construct, a framing rejected by the overwhelming scholarly consensus, which instead treats the convergence of independent methods (rather than any one historical figure's authority) as chronology's actual evidentiary foundation.
