# Arianism

**Arianism** names the christological position associated with **Arius of Alexandria**: that there was a time when the Son was not, that Christ was created *ex nihilo*, and thus that the Son is a creature rather than fully God. Nicene and post-Nicene councils defined the opposing *homoousios* formula; the teaching nonetheless shaped Germanic Christianity for generations after its formal defeat in the Roman churches.

## Emic vs. etic

- **Emic:** Arius and many later “Homoean” or non-Nicene parties framed their teaching as biblical monotheism protecting the Father’s uniqueness; opponents branded them heretics who denied the Son’s full divinity.
- **Etic:** Modern scholarship distinguishes strict “Arianism” from a wider spectrum of non-Nicene positions (Homoean, etc.) among emperors and Germanic kingdoms; “Arian” remains a conventional shorthand for Germanic non-Nicene Christianity in the fifth–sixth centuries.

## Definition and Nicene response

Per Logan’s summary: Arius taught that Christ was created from nothing—creature, not God. The councils of **Nicaea (325)**, **Constantinople (381)**, and **Chalcedon (451)** affirmed *homoousios* (same substance as the Father). Orthodoxy prevailed in the imperial church, but the defeated teaching did not disappear.

## Germanic afterlife

> "The Arians were defeated, but their teaching was to live on among many German tribes who were converted to Christianity in its Arian form."

Gothic and other Germanic conversions in non-Nicene form (see [[ulfilas|Ulfilas]]) produced a fifth-century western pattern of **Germanic Arian** and **Roman Catholic** societies side by side—hostility in some kingdoms, relative tolerance under rulers such as Ostrogothic Theodoric. Catholic conversions of Franks ([[clovis|Clovis]], c.500) and Visigoths ([[third-council-of-toledo-589|Third Council of Toledo]], 589) closed much of that confessional gap in the West.

## Limitations and critiques

“Arian” can over-homogenize diverse non-Nicene theologies and political churches. Logan’s church-history treatment is a compressed textbook synthesis, not a specialist history of the controversy’s phases.

## From Gibbon, Decline and Fall (1776–1788)

[[actors/gibbon-edward|Gibbon]] treats Arianism as the most diffuse and empire-shaping of early heresies — doctrine, diphthong-war, and Barbarian conversion. **Gibbon’s views** (ironical on metaphysical contests).

- **Three imperfect systems:** (1) Arian — Logos created from nothing, not infinite duration; (2) three coequal infinite minds (risk of three gods); (3) Sabellian names/attributes reducing the Logos — “none of these systems, in a pure and absolute sense, were exempt from heresy and error.”
- **Eighteen creeds:** Arian chiefs’ inconstancy “erected eighteen different models of religion”; Hilary on Asia — as many creeds as opinions; Homoousion rejected, received, explained away by successive synods.
- **Homoousian vs Homoiousian:** “the furious contests which the difference of a single diphthong excited between the Homoousians and the Homoiousians.”
- **West less metaphysical:** Hilary still a stranger to the Nicene creed decades later; at Rimini (>400 bishops) Latin simplicity tricked into a heretical-leaning creed — Jerome: “the world was surprised to find itself Arian” — then reversed.
- **Valentinian vs Valens:** western toleration of Pagans, Jews, Christian sects vs eastern Arian Valens baptized by Eudoxus.
- **Julian’s recall of banished bishops:** all parties restored so he “had nothing to dread from the union of the Christians.”
- **Ruin under Theodosius / Ambrose:** chapter frame “Death Of Gratian. -- Ruin Of Arianism. -- St. Ambrose.”
- **Ulfilas and the Goths:** invented a 24-letter alphabet, translated Scripture (suppressing Kings lest they irritate Barbarian ferocity); Fritigern’s people Arianized.
- **1 John 5:7 as African fraud:** under Vandal Arian pressure, Orthodox alleged the three heavenly witnesses at Hunneric’s Carthage conference — later multiplied in European Bibles as pious fraud (**Gibbon’s thesis**).

## From Mitchell, Later Roman Empire (2015)

Source: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015]].

- **Label is pejorative and misleading.** “Arianism” is largely pejorative (esp. Athanasius); the issue was core doctrine of God’s nature and Christ’s embodiment. Arius (letters + *Thalia*): Son not unbegotten, begotten before time by Father’s will, has a beginning, from nothing—subordinationist lineage from Origen; support from Eusebius of Nicomedia and Eusebius of Caesarea. Spectrum: Sabellianism (Son no independence) vs Anomoeans (Son dissimilar); Nicaea’s *homoousios* vs Arius fell between extremes; post-Constantine eastern *homoiousios* close enough that Hilary of Poitiers (exiled 358) argued no important difference.
- **Nicaea and after.** Constantine sought unity; Ossius failed at Alexandria 324; Nicaea June 325 nearly 300 eastern/Danubian bishops + Roman deputies forced *homoousios*—all but two Libyan bishops signed; Eusebius of Caesarea’s letter shows reconciling alternative senses of key terms. Nicaea failed to end debate; Constantius drove homoean compromise via Ariminum/Seleucia 359 and Constantinople 360—Jerome: “whole world groaned to find itself Arian,” but formula not far from Nicene.
- **Politics over theology.** Athanasius’s restoration became East–West political football under Constans and Constantius; Serdica failed to meet jointly; Ossius’s 356 letter to Constantius: emperor must not govern the Church. Post-381: Arianism ceases to drive internal Roman christology but structures Romano-Germanic difference for the fifth century.
