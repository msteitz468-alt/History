# Racial Hygiene and Eugenics in Germany before 1933

The pre-Nazi history of German eugenic thought and practice — the discursive
and institutional precursor of the [[nazi-racial-hygiene-program-1933-1939|Nazi
racial hygiene program]], analytically distinct from it because Weimar's own
institutions actively resisted implementation. Extracted from Evans
([[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003]]).

## Imperial Origins (1890s–1914)

Evans traces "racial hygiene" to a selectionist turn in German Social
Darwinism in the 1890s. Key figures form what he calls a "motley crowd,"
importantly *not* uniformly proto-Nazi: Ludwig Woltmann ("the Germanic race
has been selected to dominate the earth," 1900 — yet not antisemitic and an
admirer of the French Revolution); Wilhelm Schallmayer (Krupp prize essay
1900; explicitly rejected Aryan-superiority claims); Alfred Ploetz (founded
the Racial Hygiene Society, 1905, with Ernst Rüdin; proposed doctors' panels
deciding at birth whether infants were "fit to survive"); Ernst Haeckel
(bestselling popularizer; advocated killing the mentally ill, but his Monist
League followers became WWI pacifists — "not a doctrine that would endear
them to the Nazis"); Alexander Tille (advocated killing the "unfit" outright).

Shared premises (Evans's synthesis): heredity determines character; the state
should manage population "efficiency"; a scientific sorting into "valuable"
(*wertvoll*) vs. "worth-less" (*minderwertig*) — a term already in social-work
and medical use before 1914, directly prefiguring Nazi extermination
categories; and a secularized, instrumental morality rejecting the Christian
sanctity of life. Before 1914 these ideas had little effect on policy,
remaining in professional circles and fringe propaganda (Lanz von Liebenfels's
*Ostara*), but Evans calls their emergence "a significant element in the
origins of Nazi ideology."

## Weimar Radicalization (1918–1933)

The war and the overstrained welfare state hardened the discourse. Germany
mobilized ~13 million men, lost over 2 million (1 death per 35 inhabitants —
nearly double the British rate), and carried 500,000+ war widows, a million
fatherless children, and 2.7 million disabled veterans on an ambitious but
chronically underfunded welfare apparatus. Out of that apparatus grew a
racial-biological turn: welfare card-indexes of the "asocial"; over 500,000
police ten-finger fingerprint cards in Berlin alone by 1930; Theodor
Viernstein's Criminal-Biological Information Centre (Bavaria 1923, replicated
in Thuringia, Württemberg, Prussia) indexing "hereditary criminality"; court
assessments citing pseudo-biological "degeneracy" markers — Florian Huber
(Bavaria 1922) was condemned to death partly on an assessment citing facial
asymmetry and a stutter as evidence of hereditary degeneracy, despite his war
record and Iron Cross.

The pivotal text: Karl Binding (lawyer) and Alfred Hoche (forensic
psychiatrist), 1920, coining "life unworthy of life" (*lebensunwertes Leben*):
"ballast existences... The incurably ill and the mentally retarded were
costing millions of marks and taking up thousands of much-needed hospital
beds... So doctors should be allowed to put them to death."

## The Weimar Block — and Its Removal

Evans is explicit that Weimar prevented enactment: "The Republic's fundamental
insistence on the rights of the individual prevented even the doctrine of
compulsory sterilization from gaining any kind of official approval," backed
by most doctors' hostility and Catholic opposition (a 1930 papal encyclical).
The Depression eroded the block — asylums filled as families could no longer
pay for care while budgets were cut; the 1932 Prussian Health Council
discussed a voluntary sterilization law drafted by Fritz Lenz; "security
confinement" of hereditarily "incorrigible" criminals entered late-Weimar
draft criminal codes. The relationship to the Nazi program is
contributed_to/enabled, **not** direct causation: the Third Reich removed the
rights-based obstacles and applied the accumulated apparatus "with a draconian
severity that few under Weimar had even dreamed of."

## Emic/Etic Note and Limitations

"Racial hygiene" (*Rassenhygiene*) is the emic term of the movement itself;
Evans uses it descriptively. The etic literature stresses that eugenics was an
international movement (US sterilization laws, Scandinavian programs) — the
German *Sonderweg* lay not in the ideas but in their later state
implementation; see [[american-influence-on-nazi-race-law]] for the legal
borrowing debate.
