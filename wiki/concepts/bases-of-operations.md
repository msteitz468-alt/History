# Bases of operations

In Jomini's framework, the base of operations is the secure territorial foundation from which an army draws its strength, supplies, and reinforcements, and to which it can retreat if necessary. Choice of base is one of the first and most important decisions in strategy.

## Jomini's Key Points

- The base is selected in consultation with the political leadership and in light of the state's frontiers and those of its allies.
- It determines the zone of operations and the general line of operations.
- As the army advances, it must maintain connection to the base via lines of supply and depots.
- If the line of operations becomes too long or threatened, the army may need intermediate points of support, temporary bases, strategic reserves, or *têtes de pont*.
- In retreat, the army falls back toward its base to be reinforced or to occupy fortified posts.

Jomini distinguishes the initial base from eventual or temporary bases created during the campaign.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Foundational to [[jominian-strategy]], [[theater-of-operations]], [[lines-of-operations]], and [[decisive-points]].
- Directly linked to logistics (see Chapter VI of the primary text).
- The security of the base and communications is repeatedly stressed as a condition for bold offensive operations.

## Primary Grounding from Jomini's Text

"A base of operations is the portion of country from which the army obtains its reinforcements and resources, from which it starts when it takes the offensive, to which it retreats when necessary." Selection determines zone and general line of operations. Must have fortified points and good communications. Preference for perpendicular (re-entrant angle/double) bases over parallel (allows cutting enemy communications while retaining dual retreat lines). Examples: Napoleon Saale/Jena 1806, Bohemia 1813; Russians 1812. Sea bases generally dangerous for large continental armies. Temporary/eventual bases and pivots of operations required in enemy territory. Depots echeloned on converging lines; war supports war via requisitions when possible.

Full treatment (Art. XVIII, maxims, examples): [[sources/jomini-the-art-of-war-1862]].

## Historiography

Jomini's emphasis on bases and lines became standard doctrine. Critics later observed that the concept assumed relatively slow-moving, supply-dependent armies and could be less relevant in highly mobile or raiding styles of warfare.
