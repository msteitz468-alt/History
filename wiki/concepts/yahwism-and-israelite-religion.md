# Yahwism and Israelite Religion

**Yahwism** is the religion of ancient [[kingdom-of-israel|Israel]] and
[[kingdom-of-judah|Judah]] as historians reconstruct it from the **material and epigraphic
record** — a religion that began as one variant of [[canaan|Canaanite]] cult within the
[[divine-patronage-ancient-near-east|divine-patronage]] system and only gradually moved
toward the exclusive monotheism of later Judaism.
[[sources/noll-canaan-israel-antiquity-2013|Noll (2013)]] presents it as the test case for
his thesis that biblical "monotheism" is really henotheism hardening into monolatry.

## The Canaanite pantheon Israel inherited

Canaanite gods varied by place and generation — even at [[ugaritic-religion|Ugarit]] the
god-lists and offering-lists do not fully agree — but a few figures were stable across the
Bronze and Iron Ages, arranged in a hierarchy that mirrored human society:

- **El** — the high god, *'el-qoneh-'eretz*, "El, creator of Earth" (attested on an Iron Age
  ostracon from Jerusalem and in the name *Elkanah*). An elderly, wise, benevolent god who
  dwelt "where the sources of the freshwater rivers flow" (cf. Gen. 2.10–14); he created
  variously by word, by shaping clay, or by intercourse with his consort. The Bible repeatedly
  **equates El with the god of Israel** (Gen. 33.20; Exod. 34.6; etc.).
- **Asherah** — El's wife, mother of "70 gods," nurse of the royal heir; also called Qudshu
  ("holy place"). She enjoyed independent cult (storage jars "for Asherah" at Philistine
  Ekron) and was represented by a wooden pole or stylised tree. At Kuntillet Ajrud, a Negev
  trade-route oasis run by [[kingdom-of-israel|Samaria-Israel]], inscriptions pair Yahweh
  "and his Asherah."
- **Baal** — the storm/sky god who, on one influential hypothesis, **usurped El's position**
  as chief god and commonest patron during the Bronze→Iron transition (at Ekron Baal had even
  appropriated Asherah). A Kuntillet Ajrud text seems to set "the name of El" parallel to
  "Baal." El nonetheless survived in places (the *El-Shaddai* of the Deir Alla inscription
  and of Gen. 35.11).

## Yahweh's rise

On this reconstruction **Yahweh** entered the highland pantheon and, over the Iron Age,
absorbed the attributes of El (creator, head of the divine council) and competed with Baal
(storm imagery). The religion of the two monarchies was **henotheistic**: Yahweh as supreme
patron god over a populated divine realm of angels, the "host of heaven," and household gods.
Royal reform movements — associated in the Bible with [[hezekiah|Hezekiah]] and especially
[[josiah|Josiah]]'s **temple centralization** — pushed toward **monolatry**, the exclusive
worship (not yet the sole existence) of Yahweh. Full **monotheism** is a still-later
development; Noll reads even the apparently monotheistic lines (Isa. 45.5–7; the late glosses
of Deut. 4.35) as henotheistic flatteries within an anthology that otherwise teems with
supernatural agents.

## Revelation: divination and prophecy

The two commonest forms of divine communication were **divination** (the most common — reading
the god's will from signs) and **prophecy** (the second). Noll treats biblical prophets within
this ANE matrix rather than as unique moral innovators, and notes that prophetic miracle-stories
and visions of the future are literary conventions shared across the region.

## Contested status

The reconstruction is contested. Confessional scholarship long resisted reading the Bible as
non-monotheistic; maximalists treat Yahwism's "pagan" features as later corruptions of an
original pure monotheism, the reverse of the developmental model here. The epigraphic anchors
(Kuntillet Ajrud's "Yahweh and his Asherah," the Ekron and Deir Alla texts) are themselves
debated in reading and significance. The wiki records the developmental model as the mainstream
critical position while flagging the confessional alternative.
