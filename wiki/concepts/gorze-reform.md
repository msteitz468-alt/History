# The Gorze Reform

The **Gorze reform** was a tenth-century monastic reform movement centered on the abbey
of Gorze, near Metz, one of several independent centers of Benedictine renewal in the
Ottonian Empire alongside Brogne, Einsiedeln, and others — distinct from, and historically
sometimes overshadowed by, the more famous [[cistercian-order|Cluniac reform]] launched
from Cluny in Burgundy. Unlike Cluny, which was founded free of lay or episcopal control
(*libertas*, direct subjection only to Rome), Gorze-affiliated houses typically remained
proprietary or episcopal foundations, reformed and sponsored by local bishops as part of
the Ottonian *Reichskirche* system that bound the imperial church closely to royal
authority.

## The Gorze–Cluny Distinction

The historian **Kassius Hallinger** drew an influential structural distinction between
the "Gorze" and "Cluny" reform types — proprietary/episcopal versus self-governing —
which shaped mid-20th-century historiography of monastic reform. Later scholars (notably
Joachim Wollasch) have refined and partly softened this dichotomy, emphasizing that
tenth-century reform was in practice a diverse, multi-centered movement with considerable
local variation, of which Cluny, though the most famous, was only one strand rather than
the summary or origin of the whole.
