# The Muqaddimah

The *Muqaddimah* ("Introduction" or "Prolegomenon") is the introductory volume of [[ibn-khaldun|Ibn
Khaldun]]'s universal history, the *Kitāb al-ʿIbar*, written in 1377. It is one of the most original
works of the pre-modern world: a self-conscious attempt to found a **new science** — the science of
human social organization, [[ilm-al-umran|'ilm al-'umran]] — by which the reports of historians could be
critically tested against the inherent nature of civilization.

In six chapters it lays out a general theory of society: the environmental conditioning of human life;
the contrast of [[bedouin-and-sedentary-society|desert and sedentary]] peoples; the dynamic of
[[asabiyyah|group feeling]] (ʿaṣabiyya) that propels tribal groups to power and the
[[the-dynastic-cycle-ibn-khaldun|three-generation life-cycle]] of the dynasties they found; the rise and
decay of cities; the economics of labor, profit, and taxation; and a critical survey of all the
[[ibn-khaldun-on-the-sciences|sciences]]. Rediscovered by Ottoman and then European scholars, it is now
regarded as a founding text of the philosophy of history, sociology, and economics.
