# Madhhab (the Schools of Islamic Law)

A *madhhab* is a **school of legal doctrine and method** in [[sharia|Islamic law]] — a guild-like
interpretive community organized around a founding master's approach to deriving the law. Out of the
diverse regional jurisprudence of the early centuries, the Sunni tradition consolidated into four
enduring schools — the [[hanafi-school|Ḥanafī]], [[maliki-school|Mālikī]], [[shafii-school|Shāfiʿī]], and
[[hanbali-school|Ḥanbalī]] — alongside the Shīʿī (Jaʿfarī) school and the now-extinct [[zahirism|Ẓāhirī]]
and others.

The madhhab was the institutional vehicle of [[fiqh|fiqh]] and [[usul-al-fiqh|legal theory]]: it
trained jurists, transmitted authoritative doctrine through [[taqlid-and-ijtihad|taqlīd]], and gave the
law a continuity independent of dynasties. Affiliation to a school structured legal education, judicial
appointment, and the issuing of *fatwās* across the pre-modern Muslim world.
