# Reality Distortion Field

The “reality distortion field” (RDF) is the term used at Apple to describe Steve Jobs’s charismatic, sometimes terrifying ability to bend perceived reality toward his vision. Coined by Bud Tribble in 1981 during early Macintosh development, it referred to Jobs’s combination of intense staring, absolute conviction, selective memory, and insistence that made impossible deadlines or product claims seem achievable. Team members reported both inspiration (superhuman effort) and frustration (denial of constraints). Isaacson treats it as central to Jobs’s leadership method: a tool that could create breakthrough products but also alienate colleagues and distort self-assessment. The concept has entered broader business and technology discourse as a label for visionary (or delusional) founder behavior.
