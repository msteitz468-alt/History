# Dual Power (Dvoyevlastiye)

**Dual Power** (*dvoyevlastiye*) was the division of political authority and physical power in Russia between the February Revolution and the October Revolution of 1917. The structure was characterized by the co-existence of two rival governing bodies: the **Russian Provisional Government** (which held formal, legal state authority but lacked coercive power) and the **Petrograd Soviet** (which enjoyed the popular mandate and physical control over the military garrison and workers but lacked formal authority).

## Definition

Dual Power is an etic and emic political concept that describes the paralysis of state authority in the Russian Republic in 1917. The arrangement consisted of:
- **The Provisional Government:** Formed by the members of the State Duma to represent the liberal middle class and gentry. It held the formal offices of state, directed foreign policy, and was recognized by foreign powers as the legal government of Russia. However, having abolished the tsarist police and lacking the trust of the garrison troops, it possessed no physical power to enforce its decisions.
- **The Petrograd Soviet (and the local Soviets):** Composed of delegates elected directly by factory workers and soldiers. It commanded the immediate loyalty of the popular masses, controlled the transport and communication infrastructure, and, through **Order No. 1**, held a veto over the military. It acted as an unofficial monitoring body and a rival authority.

## Context and Origin

The structure arose spontaneously during the **February Revolution** on March 12, 1917, when both the Provisional Government and the Petrograd Soviet established themselves in different wings of the Tauride Palace in Petrograd. 

Moderate socialists (Mensheviks and SRs) who dominated the Soviet in the early months intentionally supported the "bourgeois" Provisional Government, believing that Russia was not ready for a socialist revolution. However, the Soviet repeatedly acted authoritatively on its own, sometimes in direct contradiction to the ministers. For example, the government committed to continuing the war effort, while the Soviet issued Order No. 1 (demoralizing the army) and campaigned for a defensive peace.

The paralysis of Dual Power was exploited by **Vladimir Lenin** upon his return to Russia in April 1917. In his "April Theses," Lenin rejected any cooperation with the Provisional Government and called for the immediate end of the dual-power compromise, advocating instead for the concentration of all authority in the workers' councils under the slogan **"All Power to the Soviets!"**

## Limitations and Critiques

Dual Power led to the complete breakdown of administrative authority in Russia:
- **Military Paralysis:** The army could not be effectively commanded, as officers had to negotiate tactical operations with elected soldiers' committees loyal to the Soviet.
- **Inability to Solve Crises:** The Provisional Government was unable to check inflation, restore transportation, or implement land reform because it lacked the power to enforce regulations without the Soviet's approval, while the Soviet refused to take formal responsibility for governing.
- **Radicalization:** The friction between the two authorities disillusioned the population, driving the workers and soldiers to abandon the moderate socialists in the Soviet and support the Bolsheviks, who promised to end the dual-power compromise through armed insurrection.
