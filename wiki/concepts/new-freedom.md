# New Freedom

[[wilson-woodrow|Woodrow Wilson]]'s 1912–16 domestic program label: tariff reduction (Underwood), banking reform (Federal Reserve Act, 1913), antitrust (Clayton Act; FTC), and related progressive economic regulation. Framed as restoring competitive individualism against "privilege," in contrast to Rooseveltian acceptance of big units under public control.

## Emic vs etic

**Emic:** Wilson and Democratic progressives presented the New Freedom as liberation of the small man from monopoly and special favor. **Etic:** Historians debate how different it was from New Nationalism in practice (Hartz/White "Tweedledum and Tweedledee"); Cox adds that the program's democracy rhetoric cohabited with exclusion of women and Black Americans from the demos being "freed."

## Limits

Does not by itself include suffrage or racial equality; Cox's book is a reminder not to let economic-progressive achievements stand in for a full civil-rights ledger. Legislative legacy (income tax implementation after 16th Amendment, Fed, FTC, parks expansion) remains structurally important regardless of the suffrage critique.
