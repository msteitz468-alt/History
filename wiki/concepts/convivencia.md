# Convivencia

The historical coexistence and cultural interaction of Muslims, Christians, and Jews in the Iberian Peninsula under Muslim rule (Umayyad caliphate and taifas) and subsequent Christian kingdoms (Castile, Aragon, etc.), from the 8th to the 15th centuries, marked by periods of relative tolerance, economic collaboration, intellectual exchange, and eventual breakdown into restriction and expulsion.

## Definition and Scope

Convivencia describes shared urban spaces, economic life (markets, crafts), administrative service (Jews and Muslims in Christian courts), linguistic blending (Arabic, Romance, Hebrew), artistic/architectural motifs (Mudejar style), and translation projects (Toledo school). It included dhimmi autonomy under Muslims and later protected status under Christians, with Mozarabs (Arabized Christians), muwalladun (converts), Mudejars (Muslims under Christian rule), and Jewish communities.

## Historiographical Debate

Celebrated as model of pluralism and conduit for Greek/Arabic learning to Europe (philosophy, science via Toledo translations). Critiqued as romanticizing hierarchy: non-Muslims/ non-Christians were subordinate; episodes of violence, restrictions (special dress, taxes), and eventual forced conversions/expulsions (Jews 1492, Moriscos 1609-14) reveal limits. Lapidus notes ambivalence: rulers valued productivity but feared subversion; economic interests sometimes prevailed over religious purity demands.

## Key Features from Sources

- Under Umayyads/taifas: dhimmi self-government, Arabic as high culture, intermarriage, shared vernaculars.
- Post-Reconquista: treaties preserved communal law/judges (qadi) initially; Mudejars retained farms, crafts, mosques; Valencia had large Mudejar majority initially.
- Cultural legacy: Mudejar architecture in Christian buildings; Aljamiado literature; shared poetic forms (muwashshah).
- Breakdown: 14th-15th c. pressures (Inquisition, sumptuary laws, loss of autonomy) culminated in expulsions.

**Grounded in Lapidus Ch. 27 (Range 2):** "In Spain, the living together of Muslims, Christians, and Jews is called convivencia... The historical story, however, is more complex. Historians who celebrate the convivencia overlook the parallel history of the breakdown of the security and prosperity... the expulsion of the Jews and Muslims..."

## The "Golden Age" and Its Myth (Cohen)

The Andalusian phase of convivencia is the historical kernel of what
[[cohen-under-crescent-cross-1994|Mark Cohen]] calls the
"[[jews-under-islam-myth-and-countermyth|myth of the interfaith utopia]]." A
nineteenth-century Lutheran Hebraist's phrase for a *literary* golden age of Hebrew
poetry (roughly 940–1040) was expanded by Heinrich Graetz and other founders of
scientific Jewish historiography into a claim about the whole political and social
order of Muslim Spain — a bright foil to the
"[[lachrymose-conception-of-jewish-history|lachrymose]]" picture of Jewish life under
Christendom. Cohen's caution parallels the debate recorded above: real Jewish
achievement and relative security under Andalusi Islam were genuine, but neither the
utopian gloss nor its neo-lachrymose inversion (Islam as innate persecutor) survives
structural scrutiny.
