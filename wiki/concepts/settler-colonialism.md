# Settler Colonialism

**Settler colonialism** is the analytical framework distinguishing colonies founded for
permanent settlement and the replacement of Indigenous societies from colonies of
extraction or administration. Its signature logic — what Patrick Wolfe called the
"logic of elimination" — is the drive to acquire **land** rather than merely to exploit
Indigenous labour, making dispossession structural rather than incidental.

In [[bashford-macintyre-cha-v1-2013|CHA Vol. 1]] the framework is implicit in the
volume's "geographic sensibility": land as the central theme of Australian history.
Australia is a paradigmatic settler colony — convict and free migration, pastoral
[[australian-pastoral-expansion|expansion]] onto Aboriginal land, [[terra-nullius]] as the
legal premise, and [[australian-frontier-conflict|frontier violence]] as the mechanism.

## Limitations and Critiques

Critics note the framework can flatten regional and temporal variation, understate
Indigenous agency and survival, and impose a single teleology of "elimination" on
processes that were uneven and contested. As an **etic** category it was unavailable to
contemporaries, who used languages of improvement, civilisation, and emigration.
