# AC Induction Motor

The **AC induction motor** is an electric motor that runs on alternating current and exploits the **[[concepts/rotating-magnetic-field|rotating magnetic field]]**: alternating currents in the stator windings induce currents in the rotor, which is dragged around without any direct electrical connection (no brushes or commutator). This makes it simple, rugged, and reliable — the workhorse motor of industry.

[[actors/tesla-nikola|Nikola Tesla]] patented the induction motor in 1888 and developed it commercially with [[actors/westinghouse-george|Westinghouse]] at Pittsburgh, where the standardization of system frequency (the path toward 60 Hz) was worked out. Together with [[concepts/alternating-current|polyphase AC]] transmission, the induction motor made centralized power generation economically transformative — the motive basis of the [[processes/second-industrial-revolution|Second Industrial Revolution]] and modern [[processes/electrification|electrification]].
