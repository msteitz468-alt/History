# Systems Approach (Flannery)

The **systems approach** views culture as a system comprising interrelated subsystems (population, subsistence, exchange, technology, social organization) whose interactions are the proper object of explanation. Small changes in one subsystem can produce large consequences through linkages; explanations must therefore be multivariate and relational rather than single-factor.

## Formulation in the Source

The "New Archaeology" argued for "a 'systemic' framework for understanding prehistory which views culture as a system which comprises interrelated subsystems such as population, subsistence, exchange and technology, and regarded the interactions between subsystems as critical for explaining the past." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.1 / range lines ~735-750)

"As Kent Flannery has described it, we are not only looking for the Indian behind the artifact but also for the system behind the Indian." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.2 / range lines ~1245-1255)

## Contrast with Prior Approaches

Earlier culture-historical work sought "where a feature... had come from" and explained change via diffusion, migration, or conquest between reified cultures. The systems view replaces this with analysis of internal dynamics and adaptive responses to changing conditions (environment, demography, social demands).

"The interrelationships between the different elements of culture, such as subsistence, exchange, settlement, population, technology and social organization, are so varied that a small change in one can produce changes in some or all of the others, and can indeed be multiplied to produce much greater consequences through these links." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.1 / range lines ~710-720)

## Application

The approach underpins the entire thematic structure of Champion et al.: each chapter examines how subsistence, settlement, exchange, technology, and social organization interact in a given period. It also informs the rejection of simple diffusionist or intelligence-based narratives in the Palaeolithic.
