# The Pre-Columbian Americas (an Overview)

This page is the wiki's **synthetic overview of the Western Hemisphere before European contact** — the
"Weak — archaeology only" gap that Charles C. Mann's [[mann-1491-2005|*1491*]] (2005) opens with a
narrative frame. It connects the wiki's scattered archaeology-derived pages ([[olmec]], [[teotihuacan]],
[[maya-civilization]], [[aztec-empire]], [[inca-empire]], [[cahokia]], [[moche]], [[tiwanaku]],
[[norte-chico]]) into a single picture, organised around the late-20th-century revolution in
scholarship — the overturning of [[holmbergs-mistake|"Holmberg's Mistake"]].

## The independent Neolithic Revolutions

The peoples of the Americas left Eurasia **before the Neolithic Revolution** and so, in Alfred Crosby's
phrase, "had to do everything on their own." Remarkably, they did: the hemisphere saw **at least two (and
probably three) independent inventions of agriculture** — in **Mesoamerica** (c. 10,000 years ago) and
the **Andes/coastal Ecuador** (squash cultivation perhaps even older), with **Amazonian** manioc
cultivation a further centre. Their signature crop, **[[maize-domestication|maize]]**, was a feat of
deliberate genetic engineering with no edible wild ancestor (bred from the grass **teosinte**) — so
improbable that it took millennia and may have slowed the spread of farming. Maize-beans-squash-avocado
gave Mesoamerica a diet "arguably more nutritious than its Middle Eastern or Asian equivalent."

## A hemisphere of civilizations

By 1000 CE the Americas held a "panoply of diverse civilizations." Mann's "guided tour" sketches them:
in the Andes, the twin highland states of **[[tiwanaku|Tiwanaku]]** (around Lake Titicaca, perhaps
115,000 in the city by some counts) and militarised **Wari**, both reeling c. 1000 from droughts (the
"mega-Niño" events), succeeded by **Chimor** and ultimately the **[[inca-empire|Inca]]**
(*Tawantinsuyu*); in Mesoamerica, the deep lineage from the **[[olmec|Olmec]]** (from c. 1800 BCE, "the
first technologically complex culture in the hemisphere") through **[[teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]]** and the
**[[maya-civilization|Maya]]** (whose city-states such as Calakmul/**Kaan** fought century-long wars) to
the **[[aztec-empire|Aztec Triple Alliance]]**; in North America the **[[cahokia|Mississippian]]**
mound-builders; and in **[[terra-preta|Amazonia]]** and the Bolivian **Beni**, vast engineered
landscapes of causeways, raised fields, and fish-weirs (see [[pristine-myth]]). Mesoamericans invented
**zero** independently (a Maya carving of 357 CE, possibly before its Sanskrit use) and a 365-day
calendar more accurate than Europe's — yet, famously, used the **wheel** only on children's toys, having
no draft animals (only the Andean llama).

## The three revisions

Mann organises the new scholarship as three reversals of the old view (detailed at
[[holmbergs-mistake]]): the Americas were **more populous** (the [[pre-columbian-american-population|High
Counters]]), settled **far earlier** than the [[clovis-first-vs-pre-clovis|Clovis-first]] model allowed,
and far more **environmentally engineered** (the [[pristine-myth|"pristine wilderness" was a myth]]). All
three converge on a single, dark corollary: the "empty wilderness" Europeans reported was largely the
**aftermath of catastrophe** — the [[virgin-soil-epidemics|epidemic die-off]] of a managed, populous
hemisphere (the [[columbian-exchange-1492|Columbian Exchange]]).

## Collection coverage note

CLAUDE.md flags the Americas as **"Weak — archaeology only; narrative absent."** *1491* is the wiki's
first dedicated narrative source, and it deliberately synthesises the **revisionist** ("High Counter")
case — mainstream among specialists today but contested at the edges (totals, dates, the scale of
earthworks). It will be balanced and deepened by the specialist Americas sources staged for this gap
(Berdan on the Aztecs, D'Altroy on the Inca, the primary sources — *Popol Vuh*, *Broken Spears*, Bernal
Díaz, Guamán Poma — and Burkholder & Johnson on colonial Latin America).
