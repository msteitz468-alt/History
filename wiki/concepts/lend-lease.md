# Lend-Lease

U.S. program (Lend-Lease Act 1941) providing military equipment, industrial goods, food, vehicles, and services to Allies (primarily Britain/Commonwealth and USSR) on credit or "lease" terms, without immediate payment. Key mechanism for Allied material coordination and sustaining coalition partners while U.S. mobilized.

## Definition and Scope

"Lease-Lend" allowed the President to "sell, transfer title to, exchange, lease, lend, or otherwise dispose of" defense articles to any country whose defense the President deemed vital to U.S. security. Total U.S. aid ~$50B+; Britain ~$30B (64% military equipment, 12% food); USSR $10.7B (food large share 1944-45, plus trucks, rail, radios, telephones).

Reciprocal aid from recipients (e.g., British Empire bases, services ~$7.5B to U.S.).

## Causal Drivers

U.S. industrial superabundance + desire to avoid direct early combat while supporting Britain (pre-Pearl Harbor) and then USSR. Roosevelt administration used agencies like War Production Board to allocate. Enabled Britain to maintain consumption (spending drop only 13%) and USSR to focus on military output after 1941 losses.

## Major Phases

- 1941: Initial to Britain (Destroyers for Bases precedent); extended to USSR summer 1941 after Barbarossa.
- 1942-43: Ramp-up; food and industrial goods critical for Soviet revival.
- 1944-45: Peak volumes; 55-60% of aid to USSR in food/industrial in later years.

## Interaction

Concurrent with and enabled Soviet home front coercion and production revival; British endurance. Interacted with U.S. mass production (Kaiser shipyards, Ford) and Lend-Lease as "arsenal of democracy." Axis had no equivalent coordination.

Produced Allied material preponderance; contributed to tipping point at 1942 midpoint.

## End Conditions

Program continued to victory; postwar settlements (some debts canceled or converted). Highlighted coalition advantages vs. Axis unilateralism/fragmentation.

## Historiography

Soviet postwar line: aid "did not matter much." Post-1991 evidence: Stalin acknowledged significance ("could not have continued the war"). Debates on exact contribution to specific victories (Moscow/Stalingrad vs. later sustainment). Compared to other aid (British reciprocal). Sources emphasize logistics challenges (Arctic convoys, Persian route, Pacific).

Grounded in West Point Vol. 1 Range 5 (Overy) + Murray-Millett claims.
