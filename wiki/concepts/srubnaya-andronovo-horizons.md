## Definition

The Srubnaya ("Timber-Grave") and Andronovo horizons designate two closely related Middle-to-Late Bronze Age (roughly 1900–900 BCE) archaeological complexes spanning the western (Srubnaya, Pontic-Caspian steppe/Volga-Ural) and eastern (Andronovo, Kazakhstan/southern Siberia/Central Asia) portions of the Eurasian steppe. Both descend from the earlier [[concepts/sintashta-arkaim-culture|Sintashta-Arkaim culture]] and, more distantly, the [[concepts/yamnaya-horizon|Yamnaya horizon]], and share fortified settlements, chariot technology, metallurgical production, and mixed pastoral-agricultural economies.

## Emic vs. Etic

Purely etic archaeological categories, named for type-sites (the Volga village of Srubnaya, the Siberian Andronovo site) rather than any indigenous self-identification; no contemporary texts survive from these populations.

## Significance and Contestation

The Andronovo horizon in particular is widely associated by archaeologists and geneticists with the spread of Indo-Iranian-speaking populations into Central Asia and, ultimately, into Iran and the Indian subcontinent, making it central to reconstructions of Indo-Iranian and broader Indo-European dispersal. As with the Yamnaya and Sintashta horizons, the degree to which shared material culture reflects a single coherent ethnolinguistic population versus a loosely connected exchange network of related but distinct groups remains debated among specialists.
