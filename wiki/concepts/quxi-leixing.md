# Quxi Leixing (Regional Systems and Local Cultural Series)

***Quxi leixing*** — "regional systems and local cultural series" — is the framework, coined
by **Su Bingqi** (Su and Yin 1981), that reorganised the study of Chinese prehistory around
**multiple, independently developing regional traditions** instead of a single radiating
core. It is the methodological expression of the shift from the **center-periphery /
Central-Plains-nuclear model** to a **multi-regional** account of Chinese civilisation, and a
recurring reference point in [[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's *The Chinese Neolithic*]].

## Definition and Content

The model is built primarily on **ceramic assemblages** and stresses the independent
development of, and interaction between, regional cultural traditions. Su (1991) identified
**six relatively stable regional divisions (*quxi*)** after 10,000 BP:

1. **Northern** — Yan Mountains / Great Wall area
2. **Eastern** — Shandong ([[dawenkou-culture|Dawenkou]] → Shandong [[longshan-culture|Longshan]])
3. **Central Plains** ([[central-plains-china]]) — central Shaanxi, southern Shanxi, western Henan
4. **Southeastern** — Lake Tai ([[liangzhu-culture|Liangzhu]])
5. **Southwestern** — Lake Dongting / Sichuan Basin
6. **Southern** — Lake Poyang to the Pearl River delta

Each *quxi* subdivides into local phases (*leixing*), each with its own origins and
developmental line, interacting with the others. The concept dovetails with the sociologist
**Fei Xiaotong's** *duoyuan yiti* ("single entity with multiple components") view of the
Chinese nation — a Han core formed by amalgamation under mutual influence.

## Emic / Etic and Critique

*Quxi leixing* is an **emic Chinese-archaeological** framework (an indigenous theory-building
device), to be distinguished from etic anthropological models of complexity. **Yan Wenming**
proposed a rival "**flower and petals**" variant: the Central Plains as the central flower
with regional cultures as petals — preserving a *leading* role for the Central Plains rather
than Su's equal weighting. Critics (An 1993a) charge the concept with **vagueness** in both
theory and application, even as it became dominant in the discipline. Its rise was not mere
political propaganda but reflected real new discoveries in peripheral regions and a changing
concept of **multi-ethnic nationalism** after 1950 (see [[nationalism-and-archaeology]]).

## Significance

For Liu, *quxi leixing* underwrites the empirical finding that Neolithic China had **many
regional centers and no single artistic or political horizon** — the foundation of the
debate filed at [[origins-chinese-civilization-monocentric-vs-multiregional]].
