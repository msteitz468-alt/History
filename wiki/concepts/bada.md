# Bada' (Divine "Change of Mind" in Shi'i Theology)

In Shi'i theology, *badā'* denotes God's alteration of a previous determination on grounds of expediency or new consideration (not ignorance). It serves to explain apparent changes in divine plans, the failure of expected deliverances, and alterations in the line of Imams (e.g., the replacement of Isma'il by Musa al-Kazim after Ja'far al-Sadiq).

## Key arguments recorded in EI1
- Qur'anic proofs: 13:4; 14:1 (end); 55:29; 7:153 (repentance averts punishment); 10:3 (people of Jonah spared); 37:101-110 (Abraham's sacrifice); 7:133 (Moses' 40 nights).
- Tradition attributed to Ja'far al-Sadiq: "God has never been soled by a new consideration (to alter his determination) as in the case of my son Isma'il (mā badā'a li-llāhi kamā badā'a fī Ismā'īl ibnī)".
- Later moderation: some altered "ibnī" (my son) to "abī" (my father) to refer to Abraham's son.
- Used to account for the non-fulfilment of hopes attached to particular Imams or uprisings.

## Context and critiques (per source)
To some moderate Shi'i theologians the doctrine in its crasser form appeared discreditable. It is discussed at length in works such as al-Kulini, Usul min al-Jami' al-Kafi and later usul texts. EI1 (Goldziher) notes the tension with divine foreknowledge.
