# Encellulement (and Incastellamento)

## Definition

**Encellulement** ("encelling," from *cellule*, cell) is **Robert Fossier's** term for the process, peaking in the long tenth and eleventh centuries, by which the dispersed rural population of post-Carolingian Europe was gathered ("celled") into fixed, bounded units of settlement and lordship — the **village**, the **parish**, the **castellany**, and the **seigneurie** — within which a person's social existence was henceforth defined. Where early medieval people had lived in shifting, scattered hamlets loosely tied to fluid kindreds, the tenth-century population came to be enclosed in nucleated villages (often around a church or a castle), framed by ecclesiastical cells (the parish with its tithe district and cemetery) and seigneurial cells (the area of a lord's banal command). Fossier set out the concept in *Enfance de l'Europe* (1982) and develops it in his "Rural economy and country life" chapter in [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]].

## Incastellamento

The closely related Italian process is **incastellamento** — the term coined by **Pierre Toubert** (*Les structures du Latium médiéval*, 1973) for the tenth-century concentration of central-Italian rural populations onto **fortified hilltop sites** (*castra*). In NCMH III the Italian villages "began to move on to hilltops in central Italy" around 920 is given as one of the markers of the age. Encellulement is the broader, trans-regional concept of which incastellamento is the Italian variant.

## Mechanisms

- **Demographic growth and climatic improvement** (Fossier notes the beech climbing the Alpine foothills from c. 950) expanded settlement and pressed people together.
- The **freezing of settlement** around durable foci — the proprietary church (which became the parish), the seigneurial *curtis*, and the new **residential castle**.
- The **laudatio parentum** and the shift to agnatic lineage consciousness enclosed families; the parish enclosed souls (one had to be buried with one's ancestors); the castellany enclosed jurisdiction.
- The slow encroachment of **banal lordship** over the formerly free allodial peasantry, drawing them into the lord's "cell" of command.

## Contested Status

Encellulement is a component of the larger [[feudal-revolution-debate|"feudal revolution" model]] and shares its vulnerabilities. Critics (Reuter cites **Leyser** and **Campbell**) question whether incastellamento and encellulement describe a genuine, datable transformation or merely the moment at which long-existing arrangements became visible in newly-loquacious charters; and whether a model built on Latium and Catalonia travels to Germany, England, or northern Europe at all. Fossier himself stresses that the process was "very slow" and uneven, "scarcely beginning before the millennium" north of the Loire and the Alps.
