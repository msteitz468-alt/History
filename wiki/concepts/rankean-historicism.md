# Rankean Historicism

The founding program of professional academic history: Leopold von Ranke's fusion of
philological source criticism, archival primacy, and the doctrine that each epoch must
be understood on its own terms. Primary source for this page: Richard J. Evans, *In
Defence of History* (1997), ch. 1.

## Ranke's Program (Evans's threefold analysis)

1. **History as an independent discipline**, separated from philosophy and literature.
   The 1824 manifesto: "To history... has been assigned the office of judging the past,
   of instructing the present for the benefit of future ages. To such high offices this
   work does not aspire: it wants only to show what actually happened."
2. ***Wie es eigentlich gewesen* — and its mistranslation.** Evans argues the famous
   phrase is better rendered "how it *essentially* was": Ranke sought the inner being
   and interconnectedness of the past, not bare fact-collection. The Anglo-American
   tradition read it as "what actually happened," licensing a naive empiricism Ranke
   did not hold — a foundational misreading with a century of consequences. (Contested
   translation point; treat as Evans's position, widely but not universally shared.)
3. **Historicism proper (*Historismus*)**: the past may not be judged by present
   standards — "God in His eternity made no distinction between periods of history; all
   were the same in His eyes." Strip away the Enlightenment's "posthumous
   condescension" (Voltaire) and understand each age on its own terms. The doctrine's
   conservative underside: what existed was divinely ordained; Ranke called European
   states "spiritual substances... thoughts of God." His universalism nonetheless
   distanced him from partisan Prussian nationalists like Treitschke.

To these add the **method**: philological source criticism imported from classical
studies (authenticity testing, internal consistency, cross-checking) and the primacy of
archival, contemporary documents — the "purest, most immediate" sources — over memoirs
and later histories (the primary/secondary distinction later formalized by
Momigliano). Institutionally, the Berlin **seminar** became the discipline's training
laboratory ("evolved from a nursery of dogma into a laboratory of scientific truth" —
Herbert Baxter Adams); foreign students carried the model worldwide.

## Trajectory and Collapse of "Scientific History"

The Rankean self-image fed a strongly inductive ideal — facts "out there" like stars,
the historian filling gaps; Langlois and Seignobos predicted a time when "all the
documents are known" and criticism would be "finished." This confidence broke on:
the endless growth of evidence (archaeology; the mass press — Strachey: "The history
of the Victorian age will never be written... we know too much about it"); the
nationalist capture of "scientific" history (Macaulay, Michelet, Treitschke; the
Prussian school's inevitabilist histories of unification); and above all **World War
I**, when professional historians of every belligerent produced partisan document
collections and German scholars' war enthusiasm discredited the Wissenschaft ideal —
Clarence Alvord: "I had learned to babble the words of von Ranke... The meaning we
historians had read into events was false, cruelly false." Interwar relativism
followed (Croce: "All history is contemporary history"; Collingwood: history as
"re-enactment in the historian's mind"; Fisher's denial of "plot, rhythm,
predetermined pattern"), then a post-1945 empiricist reassertion (Namier — whose
claims Evans rates "ludicrously inflated" and whose method decayed into "mindless
prosopography"; Popper's falsificationism), then the social-science and Annales wave
(Le Roy Ladurie: "History that is not quantifiable cannot claim to be scientific"),
itself deflated by Kuhn's paradigm theory and cliometric failures (see
[[history-as-science]]).

## Critiques of Ranke Himself

Ranke's practice fell short of his precept: his "objectivity" leaned heavily on
Venetian ambassadorial dispatches, themselves stylized productions. John Pemble: "To
the next generation Ranke was not Rankean enough"; Flaubert's jibe that "writing
history was like drinking an ocean and pissing a cupful. Ranke it seemed was doing the
opposite." Later German academic culture kept his monument-building model alive —
Evans: "the ghost of Ranke stalks the corridors of academe" (Nipperdey's and Wehler's
competing multi-volume Germanies).

## Emic/Etic Note

*Historismus* is emic to 19th-century German scholarship; "Rankean" as used in
Anglo-American method debates is largely etic and — per Evans's translation argument —
partly a construct of the misread Ranke. The method (verification, archival primacy)
survives across all theoretical schools and is what Evans defends against
[[postmodernism-and-history]]; the metaphysics (providential states, judgment-free
history) does not.
