# Morale and Cohesion in Napoleonic Battle

The intangible factors — training, leadership, unit identity, recent success or failure, confidence in commanders, fear, fatigue, and the bonds between soldiers — that determined whether a unit held its ground, advanced, or broke under fire and shock in Napoleonic combat.

Muir devotes substantial attention (Ch 10–11 and throughout) to these elements as often more decisive than numbers, weaponry, or formal tactics. Eyewitness accounts repeatedly show that units that had "seen the elephant" or were well led by trusted regimental officers behaved very differently from raw or poorly officered troops.
