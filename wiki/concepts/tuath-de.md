## Definition

The Tuatha Dé Danann ("peoples of the goddess Danu") are the divine race of ancient Ireland's Mythological Cycle, a body of medieval Irish literary narrative recounting successive legendary invasions of Ireland. According to the *Lebor Gabála Érenn*, the Tuatha Dé Danann arrived possessing magical arts and treasures, defeated the previous inhabitants (the Fir Bolg) at the First Battle of Mag Tuired, and later overthrew the monstrous Fomorians at the Second Battle of Mag Tuired before eventually being displaced themselves by the invading Milesians (the ancestors of the Gaels), after which tradition holds they withdrew into the *sídhe* (fairy mounds), becoming the Aos Sí of later Irish folklore.

## Emic vs. Etic

Emic to medieval Irish literary and mythological tradition: the Tuatha Dé Danann are figures within the native corpus itself (the Mythological Cycle), compiled by medieval Christian scribes systematizing older oral material into a pseudo-historical framework rather than an outside scholarly category.

## Significance

The Tuatha Dé Danann figures — including Nuada, Lugh, the Dagda, and the goddess Danu herself — remained central to later Irish literary and folkloric tradition as the origin of Ireland's fairy-mound (sídhe) beings, and the *Lebor Gabála's* euhemerized invasion narrative was long treated (before modern critical scholarship) as a quasi-historical account of Ireland's early population.
