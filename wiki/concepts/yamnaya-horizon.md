## Definition

The Yamnaya ("Pit Grave") horizon designates a broadly uniform set of Chalcolithic–Early Bronze Age material practices — kurgan (burial mound) construction over single inhumations, wagon technology, pastoral mobility, and characteristic pottery — spread across the Pontic-Caspian steppe from roughly 3300–2600 BCE. It is not a single polity but an archaeological "horizon": a recurring cultural package attested across a vast, thinly populated territory from the lower Danube to the Ural river.

## Emic vs. Etic

Wholly an etic, modern analytical category: no contemporary written testimony survives, and the label derives from twentieth-century archaeological typology rather than any self-description by the populations concerned.

## Significance and Contestation

Ancient-DNA studies since the 2010s have linked large-scale Yamnaya-associated population movement into both Western Europe (via the Corded Ware culture) and South Asia to the spread of Indo-European languages, making the horizon central to current Indo-European-origins debates. The genetic and archaeological case for steppe-driven migration is now widely (though not universally) accepted among specialists; the precise linguistic and social mechanisms of spread, and the relationship to successor steppe cultures such as the [[concepts/srubnaya-andronovo-horizons|Srubnaya and Andronovo horizons]], remain actively debated.
