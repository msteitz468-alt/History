## Definition and Scope

This concept covers the evolutionary origin of *fully syntactic language* — human
language with phrase-structure grammar capable of generating a potentially
infinite variety of sentences and narratives — and Ehret's argument (Ch. 14) that
its acquisition, around 70,000 BCE, was the critical threshold producing
behaviorally modern humans.

Ehret distinguishes syntactic language from mere verbal communication. Earlier
hominins and archaic humans surely made vocal utterances — even a large range of
distinct utterances — but there is "a chasm of difference" between communicating
things seen or actions taken and the modern ability to "put words and thus ideas
together in the endless variety that the capacity for syntax allows."

## The Anatomical Evidence (the Pharynx)

A notable anatomical difference distinguishes fully modern humans of the past
~50,000 years from both Eurasian archaic hominins (Neanderthals) and the archaic
humans of Africa:
- In all early hominins, including archaic humans of ~100,000 years ago, the
  pharynx extended downward at an oblique angle and was *shorter* than the oral
  cavity.
- In all fully modern humans (by age 6–8), the pharynx descends at a *right angle*
  and the two segments of the vocal tract are roughly equal in length.
- All humans since ~50,000 years ago possess this configuration.

This modern configuration carries a high biological cost — it makes humans
uniquely susceptible to choking to death — but it is "the essential enabler of our
human ability to pronounce the full range of speech sounds, especially the primary
vowels." Its communicative benefits outweighed the costs.

## Syntax and Cognition

The ability to make all vocal sounds does not by itself transform communication;
*syntactical capacity* is the essential accompaniment. The new vocal tract must
have largely coevolved with cerebral hardwiring for full syntactic language.
Syntactic language enables:
- Abstraction; classifying things and experiences
- Organizing knowledge and inferring patterns (and the *absence* of pattern)
- Conceiving things not immediately present or never experienced
- Planning, thinking ahead to consequences
- Coordinating cooperative activity
- Formulating ideas about the meaning of existence

Most importantly, it "engendered a new scale of social cooperative abilities" —
the conceptualizing and formalizing of kinship relations as a basis for larger
territorial groupings and cooperative networks (see [[paleolithic-foraging-lifeways]]).
This contrasts sharply with the tiny residential groups (nuclear-family-sized) of
Neanderthals.

## The Toba Bottleneck: Cause or Consequence?

Geneticists find that the ancestors of all living humans passed through a
population bottleneck — under 10,000 individuals, possibly as few as 1,000 breeding
pairs — around 70,000 BCE. Two interpretations:

1. **Bottleneck as cause** (Ambrose): The eruption of the **Toba** volcano in
   Indonesia (~72,000 BCE), the most massive in millions of years, caused a
   volcanic winter, crashing populations and creating the selective pressure for
   new capacities.
2. **Bottleneck as consequence** (Ehret's preferred reading): Mutations encoding
   full syntactic language and modern pronunciation, being mutually reinforcing
   and advantageous, would propagate fastest in a "cohering and compact" small
   population. The acquisition of syntactic language would itself set this
   population sharply apart and reduce interbreeding with archaic groups. On this
   view, the bottleneck is a *signature* of the rapid spread of the new capacities
   within a small founding population — not necessarily a demographic catastrophe.
   (Climatic stress like Toba may still have contributed.)

The archaeological transition to the Later Stone Age began ~68,000 BCE, close in
time to the Toba eruption.

## The Linguistic Evidence

Ehret marshals comparative linguistics in support of a single African origin:
- **Consonant complexity gradient**: The earliest modern humans likely had an
  elaborate consonant system (perhaps 100+ consonants). The predominant (not
  universal) trend since has been *decreasing* consonant complexity with distance
  from the African homeland. The most complex consonant systems today occur in
  eastern and southern Africa (e.g., Khoesan click languages); one of the simplest
  (8 consonants) is Hawaiian, "as far out in the Pacific as one can get from
  Africa." (Atkinson's "serial founder effect" model.)
- **Demonstratives**: Reconstructed ancient demonstrative root words recur across
  languages from southern Africa to South America, implying descent from a single
  original era of human language with a complex demonstrative system.
- **Conclusion**: All present-day languages descend from a single original
  language, or a cluster of mutually evolving, mutually interacting dialects — in
  a "relatively compact, geographically contiguous cluster of interacting, related
  communities," consistent with the ~10,000-person founding population.

## Relationship to the Emergence Debate

Ehret's chapter is a major statement of the **late-emergence** position in the
[[homo-sapiens-emergence-date]] controversy: anatomically modern humans existed
from ~200,000 BP, but the full behavioral package — driven by syntactic language —
took shape only ~70,000–48,000 BCE. The pharynx evidence (~50,000 years), the
abrupt appearance of the Later Stone Age/Upper Paleolithic package outside Africa
(~48,000 BCE), and the linguistic gradient all support this.

## Emic vs. Etic

The reconstruction of language origins is wholly etic — built from comparative
anatomy, genetics, and historical linguistics. No direct evidence of Paleolithic
speech exists.

## Limitations and Critiques

- The timing of the vocal-tract/syntax changeover is uncertain; diagnostic skeletal
  material from the crucial 100,000–50,000 BP window in Africa is lacking.
- Linguistic reconstruction beyond a few thousand years is highly contested; deep
  "macrofamily" claims (Nostratic, "proto-human") are rejected by most linguists.
- Whether a single mutation, a suite of mutations, or gradual change produced
  syntax is unknown.
