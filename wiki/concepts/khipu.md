# The Khipu (Inca Knot-Record)

The **khipu** (Quechua *khipu*, "knot"; also *quipu*) was the recording technology of the
[[inca-empire|Inca empire]] — a device of **knotted, coloured cords** that allowed a state without
alphabetic writing to run a census, tax system, and administration across a million square kilometres.
Documented for the wiki through Terence D'Altroy's [[daltroy-incas-2004|*The Incas*]]. It is one of the
great puzzles of the pre-Columbian world: a fully functional information system built not of marks on a
surface but of **fibre, colour, position, and knot**.

## How it worked

A khipu consisted of a **primary cord** from which hung many **pendant cords**, themselves sometimes
carrying subsidiary cords — the **position, colour, ply, and knotting** of each carrying meaning.
**Leland Locke** showed in **1923** that the numerical khipu encode a **base-10 (decimal) place-value
system**: units knotted at the bottom of a cord, tens above, then hundreds and thousands, with **zero
marked by the absence of a knot** in a position. This dovetails with the Inca's **decimal administration**
(households grouped in units of 10 to 10,000). Specialist record-keepers, the **khipu kamayuq**,
maintained and "read" them, holding in memory the contextual information needed to interpret a given
cord-record.

## Numbers — and narrative?

Most surviving khipu (perhaps two-thirds) appear to be **numerical**: census counts, tribute and labour
(*mit'a*) obligations, military organisation, calendrical and astronomical data, storehouse inventories.
But Spanish sources report that khipu also recorded **histories, genealogies, and even poetry**, and
about a third seem **non-numerical** in structure — raising the still-open question of whether the khipu
encoded **language** itself (a form of writing) or served as a structured **mnemonic** aid. The "code"
of the narrative khipu has **not been broken**; modern decipherment efforts (Gary Urton's Harvard Khipu
Database; Frank Salomon's study of living khipu traditions) continue.

## Significance

The khipu bears directly on the wiki's recurring debate over **what counts as "writing"** and over
[[holmbergs-mistake|the supposed simplicity of Native American societies]]: here was a logical,
expandable, state-scale information technology in a medium Europeans did not recognise as record-keeping
(the Spanish destroyed many khipu as idolatrous objects, even as colonial courts sometimes accepted
khipu testimony). It underpinned the [[inca-empire|Inca administrative machine]] — the census, the
[[mita-and-andean-reciprocity|labour tax]], the storehouse economy — and stands beside Mesoamerican
glyphs (the [[maya-civilization|Maya]] script, the only full writing system of the Americas) as one of
the hemisphere's two great solutions to the problem of recording information.
