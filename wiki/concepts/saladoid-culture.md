## Definition

**Saladoid** designates a ceramic tradition and associated cultural complex originating in the Middle Orinoco / northwestern Amazonia region of South America, which spread into the Lesser Antilles and Greater Antilles during the Early Ceramic Age (c. 400 BCE – AD 600). The term is an etic, archaeological classification.

### Diagnostic Features
- White-on-red (WOR) and zone-incised crosshatched (ZIC) pottery — highly distinctive
- Fired clay figurines and pendants (zemí-related cosmology)
- Village settlements with middens arranged around a central open space (plaza)
- Manioc (cassava) horticulture supplemented by fishing and gathering
- Long-distance exchange networks connecting islands to mainland

### Migration Sequence

Proto-Arawakan-speaking peoples expanded from northwestern Amazonia northward through the Orinoco drainage, then island-hopped northward through the Lesser Antilles chain beginning around 400 BCE, reaching Puerto Rico and the Virgin Islands by c. 200 BCE – AD 200. Key early sites: Trants (Montserrat, c. 400 BCE–AD 500), Hacienda Grande (Puerto Rico, c. AD 150–500), Morel (Guadeloupe, c. 400 BCE–AD 1400), Golden Rock (St. Eustatius, c. AD 200–850).

### Post-Saladoid Divergence

After c. AD 600, the Saladoid tradition diversified regionally:
- **Ostionoid** series in the Greater Antilles
- **Meillacoid** in Hispaniola and adjacent islands
- **Chicoid** (including Taíno-related) in eastern Hispaniola, Puerto Rico, and parts of the Lesser Antilles
- Various sub-Saladoid traditions persisting in the Lesser Antilles

## Contested Aspects

- **Migration vs. diffusion:** Whether Saladoid spread primarily by migration of people or diffusion of ceramic technology is debated; current consensus favours migration but acknowledges adoption by Archaic populations.
- **Arawak identity:** The equation Saladoid = Arawak-speaking = peaceful is challenged; linguistic, archaeological, and genetic evidence do not map cleanly onto each other.
- **Origins:** Northwestern Amazonia most widely accepted; alternative models (Central Amazonia, Guianas) have been proposed.

## Historiography

Rouse's (1992) series-based taxonomy organises Caribbean prehistory around the Saladoid horizon, treating it as the founding moment of "Caribbean culture." More recent network-oriented approaches (Hofman, Keegan) emphasise heterogeneity within the Saladoid tradition and reject the model of a single homogeneous migration wave.
