# Second-class citizenship (racial)

Legal and practical regimes that assign formal or nominal citizenship or nationality to members of disfavored racial or ethnic groups while systematically denying them full political rights (voting, office-holding, equal protection in practice) and often imposing additional disabilities in civil, economic, or social spheres.

## US Forms (1930s context)

- Southern Black disfranchisement via poll taxes, literacy tests, grandfather clauses, white primaries, and violence/intimidation after the end of Reconstruction.
- Treatment of "non-citizen nationals" in US territories (Philippines, Puerto Rico, etc.) and restrictions on naturalization and immigration on racial/national-origin grounds (Chinese Exclusion, 1924 Immigration Act national-origins quotas).
- De facto second-class status through segregation, employment discrimination, and unequal justice even where formal citizenship existed.

Nazis explicitly studied these techniques as models for creating a racial hierarchy of rights inside the Reich (full *Staatsbürger* vs. mere *Staatsangehörige* / subjects).

## Nazi Engagement

Featured prominently in the Prussian Memorandum (1933) and discussions at the June 5, 1934 meeting. Nazi observers noted how the US maintained the formal appearance of constitutional citizenship while engineering practical exclusion on racial grounds. Some Nazi texts praised the "devious pathways" and "covert" methods; others pushed for more open, statutory racial categorization.

## Distinctions

US versions operated within (and against) a constitutional order that still contained egalitarian language and eventual reform potential. Nazi implementation removed or overrode such constraints and was paired with escalating physical persecution and, later, extermination.
