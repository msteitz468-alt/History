# God's Wife of Amun

The **God's Wife of Amun** (or Divine Adoratrice) was a high priestly office held by a royal
woman at Thebes that, over the [[processes/third-intermediate-period|Third Intermediate]] and
[[actors/late-period-egypt|Late Periods]], became one of the most powerful institutions in
Egypt — a quasi-royal religious office through which distant dynasties controlled the south.

## Origin and transformation

The title goes back to the early [[new-kingdom-egypt|New Kingdom]] (Ahmose-Nefertari, wife of
[[ahmose-i|Ahmose I]], was its first great holder, endowed with an independent economic estate).
In the Third Intermediate Period it was reshaped: the office was "usually filled by the daughter
of a king or high priest" and the holder was now "expected to be **celibate**, an innovation
perhaps associated with the establishment of the theocratic state." Succession passed not by
birth but by **adoption** of the next chosen daughter.

## A political instrument

This design made the God's Wife the perfect tool of remote rule: "while the 'god's wife'
enabled the distant royal house to be represented at Thebes, her celibacy meant that no
subdynasty could arise," so successive dynasties — Libyan, Kushite, Saite — placed their
daughters in the role and bound the chain by adoption. [[actors/piye|Piye's]] sister
**Amenirdis I** was installed with a Kushite retinue; [[saite-egypt|Psamtek I]] secured the
reunification of Egypt by having his daughter **Nitiqret** adopted as heiress, capturing "the
major southern repository of ecclesiastical power."

By the 25th Dynasty the God's Wife appears "in roles previously reserved for the king" —
offering to the gods (including **presenting Maat**), being embraced by deities, conducting
foundation ceremonies, even celebrating a **sed-festival**. She headed a great "domain" of
celibate priestess-singers, scribes, and a powerful **chief steward** — and those stewards
became key agents "in the reintegration of the south into a fully unified Egypt under the 26th
Dynasty."
