# Strategy of Annihilation

The approach to war, classically articulated in Russell F. Weigley's analysis, that seeks the complete destruction of the enemy's military forces and capacity to resist as the primary means to victory, rather than wearing down the enemy through attrition or achieving limited political objectives through maneuver and negotiation.

Exemplified by Ulysses S. Grant's 1864–65 campaigns (continuous pressure, acceptance of high casualties to destroy Lee's army) and carried into the unconditional surrender demands and total war of 1941–45. Contrasted by Weigley with earlier American strategies of attrition (Washington) and with limited-war traditions.
