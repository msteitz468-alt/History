# Alternating Current (Polyphase AC System)

**Alternating current (AC)** is an electric current that periodically reverses direction, in contrast to the steady **direct current (DC)** championed by [[actors/edison-thomas|Thomas Edison]]. AC's decisive practical advantage is that its voltage can be raised and lowered efficiently with transformers, allowing power to be transmitted at high voltage over long distances and stepped down for local use — overcoming the short transmission range that limited DC.

[[actors/tesla-nikola|Nikola Tesla]]'s contribution was the **polyphase** system: using multiple alternating currents out of phase with one another to create a **[[concepts/rotating-magnetic-field|rotating magnetic field]]**, which made possible a practical, brushless **[[concepts/induction-motor|AC induction motor]]**. Tesla patented the complete system (generators, transformers, transmission, and motors) in 1887–88; [[actors/westinghouse-george|George Westinghouse]] licensed it, and AC defeated DC in the **[[processes/war-of-the-currents|War of the Currents]]**, becoming the worldwide standard for electrical power.

## Significance

The polyphase AC system is the technical foundation of modern electrical grids and the [[processes/electrification|electrification]] of industry and daily life — a core technology of the [[processes/second-industrial-revolution|Second Industrial Revolution]].
