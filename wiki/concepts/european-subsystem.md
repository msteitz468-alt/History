# European Subsystem (Abu-Lughod)

In Janet Abu-Lughod's influential model of a 13th–14th century Afro-Eurasian world system (*Before European Hegemony*, 1989), the "European subsystem" denotes one of several roughly co-equal, interlocking regional trade circuits — alongside Middle Eastern, Indian Ocean, and Asian subsystems — that together constituted a genuinely global (Old World) economy well before Europe's later rise to hegemony. The European subsystem centered on the Champagne fairs and northern Italian maritime republics (chiefly Genoa and Venice), whose merchants connected northwestern European production and consumption to Mediterranean and, via Mongol-secured overland routes and Black Sea/Levantine entrepôts, to Middle Eastern and further Asian trade.

## Emic and Etic

The term is an entirely etic, modern analytical construct devised by Abu-Lughod for comparative world-systems analysis; medieval merchants operated within these networks without conceiving of them as a discrete "subsystem."

## Significance

Abu-Lughod used the concept to argue against Eurocentric narratives that treat European commercial dominance as either inevitable or original, instead presenting medieval Europe as one node among several comparably developed circuits, with the 14th-century contraction of the wider system (accelerated by the Black Death, itself spread via [[actors/mongol-empire|Mongol]]-linked networks) creating the later opening for a specifically European ascent.
