# Dictatus Papae (1075)

## Definition

The ***Dictatus Papae*** ("the pope's dictates") is a list of **twenty-seven terse
propositions** on the authority of the Roman Church, entered in the register of
[[gregory-vii|Pope Gregory VII]] in March 1075. Each is an absolute statement of papal
prerogative. Among the most consequential: that the Roman Church was founded by God
alone; that **the pope alone may depose and reinstate bishops**; that he alone may use
the imperial insignia; that **all princes shall kiss his feet alone**; that **he may
depose emperors**; that he may be judged by no one; that the Roman Church has never
erred and never will; and that a rightly ordained pope is, by the merits of St Peter,
**undoubtedly made holy**.

## Significance and Interpretation

The *Dictatus* is the sharpest single statement of the [[gregorian-reform|Gregorian]]
conception of the [[papal-monarchy|papal monarchy]] and the supremacy of *sacerdotium*
over *regnum*. Its claim that the pope might depose emperors furnished the doctrinal
charge for the **[[investiture-contest|Investiture Contest]]**: within a year Gregory
had deposed and excommunicated [[henry-iv-holy-roman-emperor|Henry IV]].

[[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] (Robinson) cautions, however, against treating the
document as a finished papal constitution. Its exact purpose and audience are
debated — it may have been a memorandum, a set of chapter-headings for a planned
canonical collection, or a private aide-mémoire rather than a public manifesto — and
most of its claims can be paralleled in earlier canon law. Its importance lies less in
legal novelty than in the **uncompromising spirit** with which it gathered papal
claims into a programme of action.
