# Cywyddwyr

The **Cywyddwyr** were the class of Welsh poets who composed in the *cywydd* meter — a
freer, more personal strict-metre verse form (built on rhyming couplets ornamented with
[[concepts/cynghanedd|cynghanedd]]) that emerged in Wales from around the mid-14th century
and largely displaced the older, more formal *awdl* and *englyn* traditions of the court
poets (*Cynfeirdd* and *Gogynfeirdd*). The style is most closely associated with
**Dafydd ap Gwilym** (fl. mid-14th century), whose innovative, often playful and
personal treatment of love and nature established the cywydd as the dominant Welsh
poetic form for the following two centuries.

## Significance

The rise of the cywyddwyr coincided with a broadening of Welsh poetic patronage beyond
the courts of native princes (extinguished by the Edwardian conquest of 1282–83) to the
gentry class, reflecting the changed social order of post-conquest Wales. Their work
remains a central primary source for medieval and early modern Welsh literary and social
history.
