# Mixteca-Puebla Style

## Definition and scope

The **Mixteca-Puebla style** is a Postclassic Mesoamerican **painting and polychrome ceramic style** marked by vivid colors and highly standardized geometric images of animals, plants, sky, ritual objects, and deities. It appears on Borgia-group ritual codices (probably produced at or near [[cholula|Cholula]]), Mixtec historical codices (e.g. Nuttall), murals (Tehuacán Viejo, Tizatlán, Ocotelulco), and polychrome ceramics (Cholula, Mixteca Alta, Chalco) (Smith, EW2001 “Mixteca-Puebla Style”).

**Emic vs etic:** Historical actors did not use the term “Mixteca-Puebla”; it is an etic art-historical/archaeological label uniting related media across regions. It should not be collapsed with Mixtec *ethnicity* or political identity alone — though Mixtec codices and Alta ceramics are major carriers of the style (see also Monaghan on multivalent “Mixtec” in EW2001).

## Chronology and ancestry

| Phase | Character (EW2001) |
|---|---|
| **Postclassic Religious style** (A.D. ~750–1200) | Earlier coastal Epiclassic–Early Postclassic ancestral complex |
| **Mixteca-Puebla style** (from 12th/13th c.) | Crystallizes in NW Oaxaca–southern Puebla after Toltec fall; evolves *from* the Postclassic Religious style after A.D. ~1200 |
| **International style** (Late Postclassic, century before Conquest) | Mixteca-Puebla mural/manuscript variant spread Mesoamerica-wide: Mitla, Cempoala, Tulum, Santa Rita, Iximché, etc., via commerce and elite interaction **partly independent of the Aztecs** |

Smith (EW2001): “The Mixteca-Puebla style thus evolved out of the Postclassic Religious style.” Plunket & Uruñuela / Smith (Puebla-Tlaxcala and Postclassic entries): Middle Postclassic Mixteca-Puebla region (S Puebla, NE Oaxaca), especially Cholula, created the style; Late Postclassic **International style** is its mural variant “from Central Mexico to Yucatán to highland Guatemala.”

## Media and exemplars

- **Codices:** Borgia group (ritual); Mixtec historical screenfolds (Nuttall and related)
- **Murals:** Tehuacán Viejo; Tizatlán; Ocotelulco; Tulum / Tancah / Xelha / Santa Rita Corozal late 15th-c. coastal Maya wall painting in “international” Maya–Mixteca-Puebla style (EW2001 Maya Lowlands: North)
- **Polychrome ceramics:** Cholula, Mixteca Alta, Chalco; Valley of Oaxaca elite residences by ~1400 (Marcus & Flannery tradition already on [[mixtec]] / [[monte-alban]])
- **West Mexico:** Aztatlán and late coastal polychromes “resemble Mixteca-Puebla” (Amapa; Weigand) — related, not identical corpora

## Geographic reach and mechanisms

Spread across southern Mesoamerica through **commerce and elite interaction**, not only imperial Aztec imposition. Late Postclassic International-style murals/manuscripts reach the Gulf (Cempoala), Maya east coast (Tulum), and highland Guatemala (Iximché). Some scholars once treated **Cotzumalhuapa** style on the Pacific Guatemalan piedmont as an *early* manifestation of Mixteca-Puebla (alternative to Parsons’s Teotihuacán-influence model) — a contested attribution (Bove, EW2001 Cotzumalhuapa).

## Limitations and critiques

- **Label slippage:** “Mixteca-Puebla,” “International style,” and “Postclassic Religious style” are nested or sequential scholarly constructs; different authors draw boundaries differently.
- **Not a polity:** The style does not prove a single Mixtec–Puebla empire or Cholula “capital of art.”
- **Chronological debates:** Cotzumalhuapa-as-early-Mixteca-Puebla is not consensus; Aztatlán coastal polychromes may share iconographic vocabulary without full stylistic identity.
- **Collection bias:** Surviving codices and looted polychromes over-represent elite ritual; village use is harder to see.
