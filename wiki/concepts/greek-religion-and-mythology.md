# Greek Religion and Mythology (Formation, to Homer)

The earliest, formative phase of Greek religion — how the historical pantheon of the
**Olympian gods** and the body of Greek myth came together out of two roots: the
religion the **[[coming-of-the-greeks-1900bce|Indo-European Greek-speakers]]** brought
with them, and the religion of the **pre-Greek Aegean** (Minoan Crete and the
[[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean]] mainland) they settled among.
[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II.2]] (Ch. XL, W. K. C. Guthrie) treats this
"formation," using the Minoan-Mycenaean monuments, the [[aegean-scripts|Linear B]]
tablets, and the poems of [[homer|Homer]] and Hesiod.

## An amalgam: Olympian and chthonian

Guthrie's organizing idea is that Greek religion is "an **amalgam** of the forms of
worship indigenous to the Aegean basin with the cults and beliefs which [the Greeks]
brought with them" — a duality still visible in the historic contrast between two
strands:

- **Olympian (Uranian)** — the sky-gods of Homer: a society of immortal, powerful,
  fully **anthropomorphic** personalities with whom relations are external,
  maintained by sacrifice and prayer "in a spirit of bargaining." Suited to a
  warrior aristocracy. The Greek inheritance from the Indo-European invaders.
- **Chthonian** — earth- and fertility-cults, **mystical, ecstatic**, promising
  union with the divine and a blessed afterlife (Eleusis, the mysteries, later
  Orpheus/Pythagoras). Rooted in the settled, agricultural pre-Greek population.

The historical Greeks "would happily worship both," even uniting them under one name
with a change of epithet — but the disparity remains detectable, and is "most
convincingly interpreted as a tension between original Greek and Minoan beliefs."

## Minoan-Mycenaean religion

Known almost wholly from monuments — "a picture-book," now with a little Linear B
text. Its features: **cave- and peak-sanctuaries** (Mount Juktas, Dicte); **no
communal temples** but **domestic shrines** within palaces and houses (the deity
"shared the house of the king" — a fact that explains the historic city-goddess as a
royal patron, e.g. Athena in Erechtheus' palace); and a recurring sacred apparatus —
the **horns of consecration**, the **double axe**, **sacred pillars and trees**,
**snake-cult**, libation-vessels, and the "**Mistress of Animals**." The dominant
deity appears to be a **great goddess** (the male god subordinate), with a young
consort — the matrix of the later Cretan **Zeus *kouros*** and of Artemis as
"Mistress of Animals."

## The Linear B evidence

The decipherment supplied, for the first time, **divine names** in the Bronze Age.
Guthrie notes tablets attesting **Dictaean Zeus** and **Eileithyia at Amnisus** (both
matching later Greek cult-geography), **Potnia** ("the Lady," e.g. "of the
Labyrinth"), **Poseidon**, **Enyalios** (= Ares), **Paiawon** (Paean), **Hermes(?)**,
and offerings of gold vessels to gods. This proves that **several Olympians were
already worshipped in Mycenaean times** — though the tablets give only "a momentary
glimpse of the externals," never the beliefs behind them.

## The debt of Greek religion to its predecessors

Guthrie marshals the case (after Nilsson) that historic Greek religion owed "an
immense amount" to its Minoan-Mycenaean past:
- the **heroic sagas** are tied to **Mycenaean** centres (Agamemnon at Mycenae,
  Heracles at Tiryns, Oedipus at Thebes, the Argonauts from Orchomenus);
- **cult continuity** of sacred sites — later temples built over Mycenaean palaces
  (Athens, Mycenae, Tiryns, Eleusis) and at Delphi;
- the **Minotaur/labyrinth** complex, **Daedalus**, the **Eleusinian mysteries**, and
  the conception of **Elysium / Islands of the Blest** are all of Cretan/pre-Greek
  origin;
- **Zeus** took on incongruous Cretan traits — birth, nurture as a *kouros*, and even
  a **tomb** — by assimilation to a pre-Greek vegetation-god.

## The diverse origins of the Olympians

Guthrie reads the pantheon as a "strange collection of divine beings of widely diverse
origins" welded by the epic tradition into one family:
- **Zeus** — the one securely Indo-European inheritance: *Father*, sky- and
  weather-god, his name from a root "to shine."
- **Poseidon** — probably Indo-European ("Husband/Lord of Earth"), a fertility-spirit
  with equine form, his sea-character acquired only after settlement around the Aegean.
- **Hera, Demeter, Artemis, Athena** — local or pre-Greek **earth-/nature-goddesses**
  (the "Mistress of Animals," the Earth-Mother), absorbed and reassigned.
- **Apollo** — non-Greek, most likely met in **Anatolia** (with northern/shamanic and
  Lycian elements), yet became "the embodiment of the Hellenic spirit," god of
  purification, prophecy (Delphi), and law.
- **Aphrodite** — largely **Oriental** (the Cyprus cult, Syrian Adonis), given a Greek
  pedigree by Homer.
- **Ares, Hephaestus, Dionysus** — foreign (Thracian, Lycian/Lemnian, Phrygian-Lydian)
  importations.

## Cosmogony and the Near East

Hesiod's **Theogony** systematizes far older myth and shows striking **Near Eastern
parallels**: the **separation of heaven and earth**, the **castration of the
sky-father** (Uranus by Cronus), and the **succession of divine generations** echo the
Babylonian Marduk and especially the **Hurrian-Hittite Kumarbi** cycle (a father who
eats his children and is fobbed off with a stone). The likely channel of transmission
is **Late Bronze Age contact** — Mycenaean traders at [[ugarit|Ugarit]], later Greeks
and Phoenicians at Al-Mina — connecting Greek myth to the wider
[[ugaritic-religion|Near Eastern]] world.

## Homeric religion

Homer's gods are "the most fully anthropomorphic that have ever been known" — yet
this is itself a kind of **rationalism**, reducing the uncanny and magical "to a
minimum," for which later Greek religion was "greatly in Homer's debt." Zeus presides
over a quarrelsome divine family; the gods are superior in **power and immortality**,
not in morality, though Zeus upholds the oath, the suppliant, and the stranger.
**Fate (*moira*)** is the "allotted portion," not clearly a power above the gods. The
dead are strengthless shades in **Hades** — a bleak Olympian view that later yielded,
with the return of older beliefs, to **hero-cult** and the promise of the mysteries.

## Significance and contestation

This formation is the deep background to all later Greek (and much Western) religion,
myth, and literature. It is also **contested**: the neat Olympian/chthonian =
Greek/pre-Greek division "does not develop like that," and Guthrie repeatedly cautions
against a clear-cut racial or chronological split. Whether the **succession myth** is
borrowed from the East or a native memory of the conflict of old and new religions
"cannot be decided yet"; the readings of Minoan symbols (horns, double axe) remain
disputed; and the whole reconstruction depends on reading ambiguous monuments and a
few brief tablets "like an interpreter of Christianity with nothing but artistic
representations." Per CLAUDE.md this page records the framework and the open questions
rather than adjudicating them.
