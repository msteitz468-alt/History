## Definition

The earliest two great traditions of hominid stone toolmaking:

- **Oldowan (Mode 1), from ~2.5 million BP.** Simple fist-sized cobbles ("cores")
  from which a flake or two was struck. As [[tattersall-world-from-beginnings-2008|
  Tattersall (2008)]] stresses, the prized product was usually not the chipped cobble
  but the **sharp flakes** — highly efficient cutting tools (experimental
  archaeologists have butchered whole elephants with them) whose exact shape did not
  matter. Makers of the very first tools (~2.5 mya, Kenya/Ethiopia) are uncertain —
  possibly *Australopithecus garhi*; the named maker *Homo habilis* dates ~1.9–1.8 mya.

- **Acheulean (Mode 2), from ~1.5 million BP.** The teardrop-shaped, bifacially
  worked **handaxe** (with cleavers and picks), made by [[homo-ergaster|*Homo
  ergaster*]] to a **mental "shape template"** held before knapping began — the first
  tools standardized to a preconceived form ("the Swiss Army knives of the
  Paleolithic"). Sometimes produced in excess of practical need.

## Cognitive Significance

- **Oldowan implies planning and visualization.** Toolmakers carried suitable
  cobbles for miles in anticipation of need (cores of non-local rock found at
  butchery sites) — foresight beyond any living ape, none of which has learned to
  strike a usable flake. The first deliberate flake-maker held an idea we can grasp,
  but in a mind "vastly different from our own."
- **Acheulean implies a further leap** — a stable mental template — yet **need not
  signal new species or new lifeways.** A core Tattersall theme: behavioral
  innovation arises within existing species in individuals, and new species need not
  bring new tools (the small-brained Dmanisi hominids and early *ergaster* used only
  Mode 1; the Acheulean appears long *after* both *ergaster* and the first
  [[out-of-africa-1-1800kbp|dispersal from Africa]]).

## Distribution and Limits

Oldowan tools accompany the first hominid expansion across Eurasia (Dmanisi, Riwat).
The Acheulean reached Europe relatively late and **barely crossed the Movius Line**
into eastern Asia. Both were succeeded by the **prepared-core (Levallois)**
technique (~300 kya), associated with [[homo-heidelbergensis]] and perfected by the
[[neanderthals]] — a more complex concept yielding finished, hafted flake tools.

## Caveats

Stone tools are an indirect and partial proxy for behavior: durable lithics
massively overrepresent material culture made from perishable wood, fiber, and hide
(cf. the Schöningen spears). Technological change was non-linear — forms appeared,
faded, and reappeared. Both points caution against reading cognition or lifeway
straight off the stone record.
