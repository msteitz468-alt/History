# Tír na nÓg

The Irish Otherworld paradise of eternal youth, often reached by sea voyage or síd mound; setting for Oisín's adventures, Niamh, and many immrama / echtrai. Parallel to Welsh Annwfn / Annwn. Central symbol in Celtic mythology and later Romantic reception.

## Definition and Scope
Tír na nÓg ('Land of Youth' or 'Ever-Young') is the Irish Otherworld realm of eternal youth and beauty.

- Dedicated sub-entries in Vol V Contents: "Tír na nÓg [1] Irish background 1671 [2] connection with Wales 1671".
- Verbatim journal reference: “Al Liamm (The link), a journal written entirely in Breton, is the result of the merging in 1948 of three pre-existing Breton journals: Al Liamm, Tír na nÓg (Irish, Land of Youth), and Kened (Beauty)”.

## Historiography
Koch positions Tír na nÓg as central to Irish Otherworld traditions with explicit Welsh connections. Modern reception via journals and revival. Strong Insular textual coverage in collection.
