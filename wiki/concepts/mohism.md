# Mohism (*Mojia*)

The teaching of **Mozi** (Mo Di, c. 479–381 BCE) and the tightly organized movement he founded —
the great **utilitarian, anti-aristocratic, anti-war** rival to early [[confucianism|Confucianism]]
in the [[warring-states-period-475-bce|Warring States]], and one of the leading currents of the
"hundred schools." From [[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook (2017)]].

## Mozi and the Mohist Order

Unlike the [[confucius|Confucians]], who drew on the minor-aristocratic *shi* class, Mozi is
thought to have come from the **artisan class** — and his doctrine bears the marks of a craftsman's
practicality and suspicion of wasteful display. His followers formed a **disciplined, quasi-military
order** with initiation rituals and obligatory obedience to its masters (*juzi*), capable of acting
collectively — most famously as **specialists in defensive fortification**.

## The Ten Doctrines

Mohist teaching was organized as **ten core doctrines** (each originally a set of three essays):

1. Elevating the Worthy · 2. Conforming to Superiors · 3. **Impartial Caring** · 4. Rejecting
Aggression · 5. Restraint in Consumption · 6. Restraint in Funerals · 7. Heaven's Will · 8.
Elucidating Ghosts · 9. Rejecting Music · 10. Rejecting Fate.

The signature doctrine is **"impartial caring" (*jian ai*, "universal love")**: because partiality
toward one's own family, village, or state inevitably breeds conflict, only **caring for all people
equally** removes the root of disorder. From this followed the famous Mohist condemnation of
**aggressive warfare** — with the corollary that **defensive** warfare is justified, hence the
Mohist career as fortification engineers. Their austerity (against elaborate funerals and music)
was a direct attack on Confucian ritualism, while "Heaven's Will" and "Elucidating Ghosts" gave
their utilitarian ethics a religious sanction the Confucians lacked.

## "Ru" — the Term and the Rivalry

Major & Cook note that the word ***Ru*** (later the standard name for Confucians/"classicists")
is first attested as a **pejorative coined by the Mohists** in the *Mozi*'s "Against the Ru"
chapters; Confucius's followers apparently **appropriated and rehabilitated** the slur (much as
"Yankee" was reclaimed), so that by Han times *Ru* denoted those who revered Confucius and the
canonical Five Texts. Mohism is thus partly responsible, by opposition, for the self-definition of
the tradition that eclipsed it.

## Eclipse and Recovery

Mohism faded after the Warring States and effectively vanished as a living school under the
[[han-dynasty|Han]]. Its survival is itself a Major & Cook archaeology-and-transmission point: the
text of the *Mozi* had to be **reconstructed from fragments preserved in the Daoist Canon
(*Daozang*)**, whose final recension dates only to the Ming (c. 1444 CE). Modern interest in
Mohist **logic, ethics, and proto-scientific** passages has revived the school's standing.

## Critique (etic)

As a "school," Mohism is better attested than the retrospective label [[legalism|"Legalism"]];
but Major & Cook's broader caution applies — Warring States thought is better seen as **overlapping
trends than bounded schools**, and Mohist ideas interpenetrate with statecraft and Daoist currents.
