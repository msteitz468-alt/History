# Quantum Electrodynamics (QED)

The relativistic quantum field theory of the electromagnetic force and its interaction with charged particles (primarily electrons and positrons). It is the most precisely tested theory in physics; predictions such as the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron agree with experiment to many decimal places.

Post-World War II, QED faced a crisis: calculations beyond the lowest order produced infinite (nonsensical) results for quantities such as the electron mass and charge. Three independent approaches solved the problem of renormalization in 1947-1949: Julian Schwinger’s operator formalism, Richard Feynman’s diagrammatic and path-integral methods, and Sin-Itiro Tomonaga’s work (with later contributions from Dyson’s synthesis). The 1965 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded jointly to Schwinger, Feynman, and Tomonaga.

Feynman’s visual tools made the calculations practical and intuitive for working physicists and became the community standard.
