# Lebensraum

**Lebensraum** ("living space") is the ideological doctrine that Germany's survival as a great power required vast territorial expansion — in Hitler's formulation, agricultural colonization land seized in the East at the expense of the Slavic peoples and the Soviet Union. It is an **emic** concept: a term the historical actors themselves used, programmatically and in official documents (the [[hossbach-memorandum-1937|Hossbach Memorandum]] frames the entire war-planning discussion around it). Historians treat it **etically** as the ideological through-line connecting [[adolf-hitler|Hitler's]] 1920s writings to [[operation-barbarossa-1941|Barbarossa]], the [[hunger-plan-1941|Hunger Plan]], and the New Order.

## The Mein Kampf formulation

Shirer's chapter on Hitler's ideology assembles the canonical passages (Shirer 1960, ch. 4). Hitler dismissed colonial ambitions — "Territorial policy cannot be fulfilled in the Cameroons but today almost exclusively in Europe" — and dismissed restoration of the 1914 frontiers as "a political absurdity." The direction was explicit: land "could be obtained by and large only at the expense of Russia, and this meant that the new Reich must again set itself on the march along the road of the Teutonic Knights of old, to obtain by the German sword sod for the German plow." And unambiguously: "If we speak of soil in Europe today, we can primarily have in mind only Russia and her vassal border states." Hitler projected 250 million Germans on the continent within a century. Shirer's argument — that Mein Kampf was a precise public blueprint whose later execution should have surprised no one — has held up better than his broader national-character thesis (see [[german-sonderweg]]).

## From doctrine to policy

- 1936: the [[four-year-plan-1936|Four-Year Plan]] readied the economy for the war of conquest.
- 5 November 1937: the [[hossbach-memorandum-1937|Hossbach conference]] set timetables for seizing "greater living space."
- 1939–41: conquest of Poland and invasion of the USSR opened the intended colonization zone; Hitler's 16 July 1941 conference (Bormann memorandum) laid out annexation of the Baltic, Crimea, the Volga colonies and the Kola Peninsula, and the razing of Leningrad — "cutting up the cake… first, to dominate it; second, to administer it; third, to exploit it" (Shirer 1960, ch. 27).
- The doctrine's demographic corollary was starvation and enslavement of the existing population: the [[hunger-plan-1941]], the [[forced-labor-nazi-germany|slave-labor program]], and ultimately [[the-holocaust]].

## Limitations and critiques

The concept was economically incoherent — Germany obtained less from occupied Russia than pre-war trade had provided (Alexander Dallin's finding, noted by Shirer) — and demographically fictitious: there was no German rural surplus population wanting eastern farms. Historians debate how far Lebensraum was a fixed program (intentionalist reading, which Shirer's blueprint argument anticipates) versus a legitimating rhetoric radicalized by circumstance (functionalist reading). See [[colonialism-nazism-continuity]] for the debate over its relation to prior colonial ideologies.
