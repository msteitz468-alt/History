# Einstein's World Government Advocacy

Einstein's proposal for a supranational authority with sole control over nuclear weapons and enforcement power to prevent war, transcending national sovereignty. Not sentimental pacifism but "hardnosed assessment of human nature": “If the idea of world government is not realistic, then there is only one realistic view of our future: wholesale destruction of man by man.” (1948). “I do not know how the Third World War will be fought, but I can tell you what they will use in the Fourth—rocks.”

## Definition and Scope

A democratic world federation or "supranational regime" with directly elected parliament, monopoly on atomic (and later other) arms, and authority to settle disputes. Explicitly rejected pure pacifism or unilateral disarmament after Nazi and Soviet examples. West should proceed even without immediate Soviet participation ("if this were done intelligently (rather than in clumsy Truman style!) Russia would cooperate once she realized...").

Distinguished from world socialism: “I can easily imagine that two socialist states might fight a war against each other.”

## Causal Drivers

- Lifelong aversion to nationalism and militarism (childhood memories of Prussian soldiers).
- Revision of absolute pacifism after 1933: "if I were a Belgian, I would not refuse military service."
- Horror at atomic weapons and arms race logic ("Each step appears as the inevitable consequence of the one that went before... general annihilation").
- Belief that national sovereignty was as obsolete as absolute time/space had been in physics.

`enabled:` refugee experience and US platform; `produced:` Einstein-Russell Manifesto (April 1955, signed days before death), Pugwash Conferences.

## Major Phases and Interaction

- Pre-1945: internationalism and Zionism without state chauvinism.
- 1945–1950s: intense advocacy via articles, letters, broadcasts (e.g. with Eleanor Roosevelt); criticism of both US hysteria and Soviet vetoes/repression.
- Resistance from Soviets (1947 article "Dr. Einstein’s Mistaken Notions": world government as "flamboyant signboard for the supremacy of capitalist monopolies"); from US realists as naïve.
- Linked to civil liberties defense: free thought required to sustain any supranational order.

`contrasts_with:` pure pacifism and unchecked nationalism. `analogous_to:` his scientific search for unifying principles (invariants over local frames).

## Limitations and Critiques

Einstein acknowledged complexity but presented simply ("Physicists are not used to trimming or compromising their equations"). Critics (Truman officials, editorials, Soviet scientists) saw it as childlike or serving one side. No implementation; influenced discourse (Baruch Plan echoes) but Cold War institutions prevailed.

## Historiography

Isaacson emphasizes it was "based not on gooey sentiments but on this hardnosed assessment of human nature." Part of consistent individualism + internationalism. Debated as unrealistic vs. prescient warning. Collection coverage via Isaacson strong on personal evolution and quotes; gaps in full diplomatic history of related proposals.
