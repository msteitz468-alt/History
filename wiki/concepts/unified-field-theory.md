# Unified Field Theory

**Einstein's decades-long effort (beginning seriously after 1915 GR) to find a single set of field equations describing gravity and electromagnetism (and ideally eliminating quantum indeterminacy) as manifestations of one underlying reality.**

## Definition and Scope

Unlike quantum mechanics' probabilistic turn, a unified field theory would restore strict determinism and causality via classical continuous fields in spacetime. Einstein: "The mind striving after unification cannot be satisfied that two fields should exist which, by their nature, are quite independent." Goal: "a mathematically unified field theory in which the gravitational field and the electromagnetic field are interpreted only as different components or manifestations of the same uniform field."

## Major Phases

- Weyl (1918), Kaluza (1919, 5th dimension), Eddington (affine), Einstein's own variants (nonsymmetric metric, "distant parallelism").
- 1929 paper frenzy: "It has been my greatest ambition to resolve the duality of natural laws into unity." Public headlines but rapid abandonment.
- Continued attempts into 1930s and beyond; "Einstein Completes Unified Field Theory" (NYT 1931) repeated pattern.

## Historiography

Isaacson (2007) shows the mathematical formalism increasingly detached from new experimental physics (new forces/particles). "Even if his attempt does not succeed, it will have forced us to think about the great questions" (Cartan). Einstein remained committed: "I still believe in the possibility of a model of reality."
