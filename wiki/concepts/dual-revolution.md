# The Dual Revolution

The interpretive concept — coined by **Eric Hobsbawm** in *[[hobsbawm-age-of-revolution|The Age of
Revolution]]* — that the modern world was born from **two near-simultaneous, intertwined revolutions**
around the turn of the nineteenth century: the **French Revolution** (political) and the **British
Industrial Revolution** (economic). Together, Hobsbawm argued, they constituted a single "twin upheaval"
that propagated from a Franco-British base across the globe.

## The Idea

- **The political revolution (French, 1789).** Supplied the **political vocabulary and template of
  modernity** — nation, citizen, rights, constitution, the sovereignty of the people, "left" and "right"
  — and, through the Revolutionary and Napoleonic wars, broadcast it across Europe and beyond.
- **The economic revolution (British, Industrial).** Achieved the breakthrough to **self-sustaining
  industrial capitalism** — the factory system, mechanized production, sustained growth, and the new
  social classes (industrial bourgeoisie and proletariat).
- **Their fusion.** Hobsbawm's claim is that these were not two separate stories but the **twin crater of
  one volcano**: together they produced the triumph of **bourgeois-liberal capitalism** and generated the
  great modern ideologies — **liberalism, nationalism, and socialism**.

## Significance and Critique

The "dual revolution" became one of the most influential framing-devices in modern historiography,
organizing the study of the **[[long-19th-century|long nineteenth century]]** around the joint making of
industrial and political modernity. It is **contested**: critics fault its **Eurocentrism** (the
"Franco-British base"), its **Marxist class framework**, the tidy pairing of two very different processes,
and its relative neglect of the wider world (the [[atlantic-revolutions|Atlantic revolutions]] beyond
France, Asian and African agency, the slave economies that underwrote industrialization). It remains a
productive starting point precisely because it links political and economic transformation in one frame.
