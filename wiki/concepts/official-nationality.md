# Official Nationality

**Official Nationality** was the state ideology of the Russian Empire during the reign of Nicholas I (1825–1855). Formulated by the Minister of Education, Count Sergei Uvarov, in 1833, it was defined by the famous triad: **Orthodoxy**, **Autocracy**, and **Nationality** (*Pravoslaviye, Samoderzhaviye, Narodnost*). The ideology was designed to justify the absolute authority of the tsar, defend serfdom, and counter the influence of Western liberal and revolutionary ideas.

## Definition

Official Nationality was an emic state ideology that sought to define the unique character and destiny of the Russian Empire. Its three core pillars were:
1. **Orthodoxy:** The assertion that the Russian Orthodox Church was the source of Russian morality, truth, and spiritual unity, and that the state had a duty to defend it.
2. **Autocracy:** The belief that absolute autocracy was the natural, historical, and indispensable form of government for Russia, without which the state would collapse.
3. **Nationality (*Narodnost*):** The claim that the Russian people possessed a unique, submissive, and loyal character, marked by a deep, organic connection to the Orthodox Church and the tsar. It emphasized traditional customs and language as a defense against Westernization.

## Context and Origin

The concept arose in the aftermath of the Decembrist Revolt of 1825 and the European revolutions of 1830. Nicholas I and his ministers believed that the empire had been destabilized by the introduction of Western ideas (such as liberty, equality, and constitutionalism). Count Sergei Uvarov, upon assuming the ministry of education in 1833, presented the triad to the emperor as the guiding principle for Russian schooling and intellectual life. 

Official Nationality was promoted through government publications, school curricula, and censored literature. It was supported by conservative intellectuals, such as the historians Mikhail Pogodin and Stepan Shevyrev, who argued that Russia was spiritually and politically superior to a decaying, revolutionary Western Europe.

## Limitations and Critiques

Official Nationality represented a rigid, defensive effort to preserve the status quo (including serfdom and bureaucratic corruption) by suppressing intellectual development. It had several limitations:
- **Intellectual Oppression:** To maintain the triad, the government enforced severe censorship, restricted university enrollment, and banned the discussion of political reform. This alienated the educated class, driving them into the radical underground.
- **Russification:** The emphasis on "Nationality" led directly to policies of forced Russification of non-Russian national minorities (such as the Poles and Ukrainians), generating deep ethnic resentment.
- **The Slavophile Critique:** Although the Slavophiles shared the government's admiration for Orthodoxy and nationality, they bitterly criticized Official Nationality. They argued that the state's "Autocracy" was not organic but had become a rationalist, German-style bureaucracy (introduced by Peter the Great) that oppressed the free spirit of the Russian people.
- **The Westernizer Critique:** The Westernizers, led by Belinsky and Herzen, rejected the entire triad, viewing it as a tool of ignorance and despotism designed to keep Russia backward.
