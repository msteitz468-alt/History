# Tribal Hidage

The **Tribal Hidage** is a list of some thirty-four peoples south of the Humber with an
assessment of each in *hides* (units of tribute/render), surviving only in later copies. It
opens with [[mercia|Mercia]] (assessed at 30,000 hides) and runs through large peoples such
as the South and East Saxons, the East Angles and Kent down to tiny groups of a few
hundred hides.

## Significance

For [[sources/kirby-earliest-english-kings-2000|Kirby]] the Hidage is the single best
window onto the real political geography behind the schematic
[[heptarchy|"Heptarchy"]]: it reveals a dense mosaic of peoples — the Hwicce, Magonsæte,
Wreocensæte, Middle Angles, Lindsey, the Gyrwe and many others — far more numerous than
the seven canonical kingdoms, and shows over-kingship operating as a system of graded
**tribute assessment** rather than territorial administration.

## Date and Purpose — Contested

The document's origin is disputed. The Mercia-first arrangement and the way other peoples
are assessed *relative* to Mercia have suggested a Mercian tribute-roll, perhaps of the
later seventh century (the reign of Wulfhere or Æthelred); others have argued for a
Northumbrian overlord's assessment of the southern peoples, or a still later compilation.
Kirby uses it cautiously as evidence for the structure of [[bretwalda|overlordship]] while
acknowledging that its precise date and the authority that drew it up cannot be fixed.
