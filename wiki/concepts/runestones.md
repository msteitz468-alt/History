# Runestones

Raised stones bearing inscriptions in the runic alphabet (*futhark*), the
characteristic epigraphic medium of pre-Christian and conversion-era Scandinavia.
The Elder Futhark (24 runes) gave way to the 16-rune Younger Futhark in the Viking
Age. The great age of monumental runestones — overwhelmingly memorial inscriptions
"X raised this stone after Y" — falls in the 10th–11th centuries, concentrated in
central Sweden (Uppland). They are a primary source for personal names, kinship,
inheritance, travel (including expeditions east and west), early Christianity, and
the [[christianization-of-scandinavia|conversion]]; the [[jelling|Jelling]] stones
are the most famous royal monuments. They complement and predate the written
[[saga-literature|saga tradition]].
