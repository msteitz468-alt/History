# Rotating Magnetic Field

The **rotating magnetic field** is the physical principle underlying the AC [[concepts/induction-motor|induction motor]]: two or more alternating currents, out of phase and fed to spatially offset coils, produce a magnetic field whose orientation rotates in space. A conductor (the motor's rotor) placed in this field has currents induced in it and is dragged around, producing torque without any electrical contact (brushes or commutator) to the rotor.

In Seifer's *Wizard*, [[actors/tesla-nikola|Tesla]] describes conceiving the idea in a flash of insight during a walk in a Budapest park in 1882, reciting Goethe's *Faust*, and sketching the principle in the dirt with a stick — the founding image of the Tesla legend. The principle made the **[[concepts/alternating-current|polyphase AC system]]** practical and is the basis of the brushless motors that drove twentieth-century industry.
