# Revolutionary Armies and their New Art of War

The tactical, organizational, and motivational transformation of French forces between 1792 and the Consulate, marked by the levee en masse, the rise of light infantry and skirmishers, the flexible use of column and line (ordre mixte), and a more aggressive, politically motivated style of warfare that replaced the limited, maneuver-heavy warfare of the *ancien régime*.

Rothenberg stresses that these changes were pragmatic responses to numbers, supply shortages, and the political energy of the Revolution rather than purely theoretical. The "new art" combined mass with mobility and fire, and proved capable of defeating the professional linear armies at Valmy and after.

## Key Innovations (per Rothenberg)
- Shift from small professional forces to large citizen armies.
- Greater reliance on skirmishers and light troops for screening and harassment.
- Combined-arms pressure (artillery preparation + infantry column or line + cavalry exploitation).
- National and ideological motivation replacing dynastic loyalty.
