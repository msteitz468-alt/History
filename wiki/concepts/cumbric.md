# Cumbric

Cumbric was an extinct Brittonic (P-Celtic) language spoken across the "Hen Ogledd" (Old North) of Britain, the zone of Brittonic-speaking successor kingdoms including [[places/ystrad-clud|Ystrad Clud]] (Strathclyde/Alt Clut), Rheged, and Gododdin, in what is now southern Scotland and northern England. Because no substantial texts in the language survive, Cumbric is reconstructed almost entirely from place-name evidence (many "Cumbria," "Cumberland," and related toponyms derive from the same root as "Cymru," the Welsh name for Wales) and a small number of technical or legal terms that persisted in later English and Scots usage, most famously traditional sheep-counting scores ("yan, tan, tethera...") recorded in parts of northern England into the modern era.

## Emic and Etic

The label "Cumbric" is a modern (etic) scholarly coinage; speakers themselves would presumably have understood their language simply as a variant of the broader Brittonic speech shared with Wales and Cornwall.

## Decline

Cumbric is generally believed to have died out by around the 12th century, displaced by the expansion of Gaelic from the north and English/Scots from the south and east as the political independence of Ystrad Clud and related Brittonic polities ended.
