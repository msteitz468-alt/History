# Eranshahr (the Sasanian Idea of Iran)

## Definition and Scope

**Ēranšahr** ("Empire/Realm of the Aryans") is the name the
[[sasanian-empire|Sasanian]] dynasty gave to its state and, more deeply, to a
political-theological *idea of Iran* — a sacred territory, ruled by a divinely elected
**[[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrian]]** king of kings, defined against the non-Iranian world
(*Anērān*). [[sources/wiesehofer-ancient-persia-2001|Wiesehöfer]] argues it is **emic**
(a self-designation of historical actors) and, crucially, a **Sasanian invention**: the
title "king of kings" was old and shared with the [[parthian-empire|Arsacids]] and the
[[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenids]], "but it was not combined with the word *Eran*." Shapur
I's full titulature — *šāhān šāh Ērān ud Anērān, kē čihr az yazdān* ("king of kings of
Iran and non-Iran, whose origin is from the gods") — fuses three legitimating claims at
once: heirs of the ancient Iranian empire of the "forefathers," successors of the
mythical Kayanian kings, and champions of the Mazdean creed. Shapur extended the formula
to *…ud Anērān* to absorb newly conquered, non-Iranian regions and their princes.

## What it is and is not

- It is **not** simply a geographic or administrative label. Middle Persian, "the
  language of the kings and priests, was not imposed as a state language in the
  multilingual Sasanian empire, indeed it was not even a lingua franca." Eranshahr was an
  ideological-religious construct administered through the Zoroastrian clergy, not a
  linguistic or ethnic uniformity imposed on subjects.
- It is **not** a continuous "national" memory reaching back to Cyrus. The Sasanians
  knew their [[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid]] predecessors only dimly (see
  [[rediscovery-of-ancient-iran]]); the deep past they claimed was the **mythical
  Kayanian** lineage of the Avesta, not the historical Teispids/Achaemenids.

## Causal Drivers and Function

The concept did legitimating work. The empire's official "national history," the
**Xwadāy-nāmag** ("Book of Lords") — committed to writing from the 5th century, with a
first authoritative version under [[khosrow-i|Khosrow I]] — fixed Iran and its royal
lineage in narrative form, running from the first man Gayōmard through some fifty kings
and queens to Khosrow II. Within it the Sasanians lived on as "Iranian kings par
excellence" while the [[parthian-empire|Parthians]] were demoted to "petty kings"
(*mulūk aṭ-ṭawāʾif*) — an ideological erasure (see
[[parthian-empire|the Parthian page]]).

## Interaction with kingship and religion

Eranshahr is inseparable from the Sasanian theory of kingship: the king bears the
divine **[[concepts/achaemenid-royal-ideology|xwarrah]]** (Glory/Fortune) that marks the
rightful ruler, and styles himself *bay* ("god/lord") — though Wiesehöfer notes the careful
linguistic distinction between *bay* (the king, a "god-man" / material divine being) and
*yazd* (the true, spiritual gods such as Ohrmazd). The supposed primordial union of
"throne and altar" Wiesehöfer treats as a **later back-projection**; centralization of
the realm of Eranshahr is "a phenomenon of a later period," clearest in Khosrow I's
reforms.

## End Conditions

The political Eranshahr fell with the Arab-Muslim conquest (mid-7th c.), but the *idea*
of Iran survived: transmitted through the Xwadāy-nāmag into Perso-Arabic historiography
and ultimately Ferdowsi's *Shāhnāma*, it became one of the most durable political concepts
of the Iranian world.
