## Definition

*Volksgemeinschaft* ("people's community" or "folk community") was the central domestic promise and organizing ideology of the National Socialist regime. It designated a racially defined German national community that was supposed to replace class conflict, confessional divisions, regional loyalties, and the party-political fragmentation of Weimar democracy with an organic unity grounded in shared racial identity. The Nazis "made no secret of its view that the people in question was a racially defined German nation" (Smith 2011, ch. 22).

The concept's origins predate National Socialism. As the publisher Eugen Diederichs wrote in 1929: "ecclesiastical Protestantism in its present form has become barren for the life of the people (Volksleben) and, hence, also barren for the coming people's community (Volksgemeinschaft), which cannot be created without a religious ethos." This sentence shows the concept functioning in the cultural-religious crisis of Weimar before the Nazis claimed exclusive ownership of it.

Source: [[smith-oxford-modern-german-history-2011]], Chapters 20, 22, 24.

## Emic vs. Etic

As an **emic** concept, *Volksgemeinschaft* was a rallying cry that millions of Germans sincerely embraced and that structured their expectations of what the Nazi regime would deliver: social mobility, racial solidarity, economic improvement, the dissolution of class distinctions within the racial in-group.

As an **etic** analytical category, historians use *Volksgemeinschaft* to interrogate the social bases of Nazi popular support, the distribution of benefits from racial persecution, and the mechanisms by which ordinary Germans were enrolled in or acquiesced to the regime's violence.

## How the Concept Functioned

### Domestic social promise

The Nazis "characterized the implementation of this vision as a return to an earlier, traditional social unity that allegedly predated the German Revolution of 1918 and the Weimar Republic." In practice, Volksgemeinschaft meant:

- Suppression of class-based politics (Social Democracy, trade unions, Communist Party crushed immediately)
- Eugenic programs: forced sterilization of "hereditary defectives," euthanasia of psychiatric patients (T-4 program, ~70,000 killed)
- "Strength Through Joy" (KdF) programs offering tourism, leisure activities, and consumer benefits to workers
- Social mobility for Nazi functionaries, SA men, and career-minded professionals who could now advance past older elites
- Material redistribution: expropriation of Jewish property ("aryanization") distributed benefits widely among the German population

Götz Aly's *Hitler's Volksstaat* (2005) argues that the Nazis were "classic feel-good politicians" who "on a daily basis bought themselves public backing or anyway indifference" through social redistribution, much of it funded by plunder of Jews and occupied territories. While Aly's formulation is contested for overstatement, the broad consensus supports the claim that living standards in Germany were maintained (at the expense of occupied Europe) through the wartime years.

### Racial boundary-drawing

Volksgemeinschaft entailed precise exclusions. The Nuremberg Laws (1935) defined racial citizenship in terms of descent, stripping Jews of citizenship. The concept was elaborated to cover "community aliens" (Gemeinschaftsfremde) — homosexuals, the homeless, addicts, repeat offenders, "asocials" — who were deemed hereditary violators of communal norms and subjected to concentration camp detention, sterilization, and eventually murder.

Peter Fritzsche has argued that Nazi policies "forced average Germans to engage with concepts of race and nation in daily life situations" — constructing family trees, navigating boycotts, encountering street violence — "whether or not race held any significance for them personally."

### Imperial dimension

Hagen (Chapter 24) argues that Volksgemeinschaft at the domestic center **depended structurally** on east European empire: "the 'American-style' prosperity and modernity the Nazis sought for their favored subjects could only, they believed, be attained through the ruthless, colonial exploitation of conquered Eastern Europe." This connection makes the domestic social promise of Volksgemeinschaft causally imbricated in the Holocaust: "Thus, Volksgemeinschaft entailed eastern empire which, as projected by its anti-Semitic conquerors, demanded the 'removal' of its Jewish population, eventually by means of mass murder."

### As mobilizing myth

The regime used *Volksgemeinschaft* propagandistically to simulate the public unity it simultaneously claimed to describe. Goebbels' 1943 campaign using Soviet atrocities at Katyń to cement German complicity in the Holocaust — "let no one fool himself into believing he can come forward later on and say: I was always a good democrat among those terrible Nazis" (Göring, October 1942) — illustrates the coercive dimension of Volksgemeinschaft ideology: public avowal of solidarity in crime as a bond of complicity.

## Contested Aspects

**Consensualism vs. coercionism**: How much did ordinary Germans genuinely embrace Volksgemeinschaft? The consensualist reading (Gellately, Fritzsche) emphasizes popular enthusiasm and material self-interest. The coercionist reading emphasizes the approximately 65 daily arrests for political offenses throughout the Reich during wartime, 30,000 death sentences against German soldiers, and 16,000 death penalties imposed on civilians — creating an environment of terror in which feigned assent was survival strategy. Peter Longerich's *'Davon haben wir nichts gewusst!'* (2006) shows that even within the NS party membership, awareness of genocidal policies was received with unease rather than enthusiasm.

**Class dimension**: The egalitarian promise of Volksgemeinschaft contained a contradiction: it offered racial Germans of all classes dignity and national privilege, but simultaneously served professional middle-class hopes of restoring social privilege eroded by Weimar democracy. These two promises were structurally in tension.

**Realization**: Was Volksgemeinschaft ever actually realized, even partially? The historiographical consensus is that it remained an aspirational ideology that the regime constantly propagandized but never fully achieved. Class distinctions, confessional attachments, regional loyalties, and private skepticism persisted throughout the Third Reich.

## Resolution Status

Open. Active areas of research include the social history of "everyday Volksgemeinschaft" in provincial settings, the role of consumption and leisure (KdF tourism), gender dimensions (women as bearers of racial community), and the relationship between domestic Volksgemeinschaft ideology and imperial practice in occupied territories.
