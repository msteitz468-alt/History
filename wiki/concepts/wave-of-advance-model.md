# Wave-of-Advance Model

The **wave-of-advance model** is a theoretical framework that posits a steady, gradual rate of spread of cultural traits (especially agriculture) across a landscape over time, most commonly applied to the Neolithic transition in Europe.

## Definition and Scope

The glossary of the encyclopedia defines it concisely:

> "wave-of-advance model: A theory that postulates a steady rate of spread, usually of cultural traits, across a landscape over time. Principally associated with the demic diffusion model for agriculture in Europe." (range_08:77–81)

This model contrasts with "leapfrog" or frontier models of colonization and with purely indigenous adoption scenarios. It implies a relatively constant rate of expansion driven by population growth and local carrying-capacity thresholds, producing a roughly radial or front-like advance from points of origin (Anatolia / southeastern Europe) into central, western, and northern Europe.

## Application to the Neolithic

In the context of the encyclopedia's Part 3 "Transition to Agriculture," the wave-of-advance model supplies one major explanatory framework for the spread of farming across Europe after ~7000–6500 BCE in the southeast and later in the northwest. It is linked to:
- The [[concepts/linearbandkeramik]] and Cardial / Impressed Ware horizons as early expressions of the advancing front.
- Demographic models in which farming populations expand at the expense of forager groups or through admixture.
- Environmental and soil factors modulating the pace of advance.

The model is explicitly tied to "demic diffusion" — the movement of people carrying the new subsistence package — as opposed to acculturation or idea diffusion alone.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Closely related to the [[concepts/secondary-products-revolution]] and the full establishment of mixed farming economies.
- Interacts with the [[concepts/beaker-phenomenon]] and later cultural horizons as subsequent waves or re-organizations.
- Contrasts with more punctuated or regionally variable processes documented in the Mesolithic–Neolithic transition (e.g. at Iron Gates, Franchthi Cave, or Atlantic facade).
- Informs discussion of the [[concepts/three-age-system]] refinements, especially the subsistence-based definition of the Neolithic.

## Contested Status

The glossary entry does not expand on debates, but the model has been contested on multiple grounds:
- Radiocarbon date distributions often show "bursts" and "pauses" rather than uniform advance.
- Genetic and isotopic evidence (post-2004) has complicated simple demic models.
- Regional frontier dynamics (e.g. rapid adoption by indigenous groups in some areas) suggest hybrid processes.

The 2004 encyclopedia presents it as the principal association for the spread of agriculture without claiming exclusivity.

## Historiography

The wave-of-advance formulation gained prominence through quantitative modeling in the 1970s–1980s (Ammerman & Cavalli-Sforza). It provided a testable, spatially explicit alternative to earlier migration or diffusion narratives. By the early 2000s it remained a benchmark against which more nuanced, multi-strand models were measured.

## Collection Coverage Note

The encyclopedia's coverage of the Neolithic transition (Balkans, LBK, Mediterranean, northwest Europe) supplies abundant data against which the wave-of-advance model can be evaluated. Strong site coverage (Achilleion, Bylany, Hambledon Hill, etc.) allows discussion of both support for steady advance and local deviations.
