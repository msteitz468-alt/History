## Definition

**Archaeogenetics** is "the study of the human past using the techniques of
molecular genetics" (Renfrew) — the collaboration of geneticists with
archaeologists, anthropologists, historical linguists, and climatologists to
reconstruct prehistoric population history. It is one of the four
method-streams that have transformed the study of [[agricultural-origins]]
(alongside archaeobotany/zooarchaeology, stable-isotope analysis, and
organic-residue analysis), and the subject of Ch. 2 of CWH Vol. II (Pala,
Soares, Chaubey & Richards).

## How it works

**Three marker systems.**
- **mtDNA** (mitochondrial DNA) — inherited down the maternal line, non-recombining,
  passed as a single block; mutations accumulate to define **haplogroups**. The
  richest system for genealogical dating because of high variation in its 16.5 kb
  unit. >150,000 control-region sequences and >15,000 whole "mitogenomes" by 2014.
- **MSY** (male-specific Y chromosome) — the paternal-line equivalent; more
  controversial because Y mutation rates are still poorly calibrated.
- **Autosomes** — the recombining bulk of the genome; now read as
  genome-wide SNPs and whole genomes, analysed with STRUCTURE/ADMIXTURE
  clustering algorithms, but harder to read genealogically.

**Phylogeography** combines three variables — the genealogical tree, the
geographic distribution of lineages, and the **molecular-clock** time-depth of
clusters — on the principle that every new variant arose at a particular time
and place. **Founder analysis** formalizes this to date colonization events by
subtracting source-region from sink-region diversity. (E.g. mtDNA haplogroup L3
arose ~70,000 years ago in East Africa, and its descendant founders date the
[[out-of-africa-2-60kbp|Out of Africa 2]] dispersal to ~50,000–60,000 years ago.)

**Ancient DNA (aDNA)** — DNA recovered directly from archaeological remains —
is the newest and most powerful strand: it can test models built on modern
variation and reveal *population extinctions* invisible in modern data. So far it
is abundant only for Europe.

## The central debate it has driven: the European Neolithic

Archaeogenetics has been "virtually defined" by one question: **how much of
modern European ancestry derives from Neolithic farmers migrating from the Near
East** (demic diffusion) versus indigenous Mesolithic foragers who adopted
farming (cultural diffusion)? See [[demic-vs-cultural-diffusion]].
- **1960s–90s:** Cavalli-Sforza's classical-marker PCA maps showed a southeast→
  northwest gradient (along the gradient of Neolithic 14C dates), read as
  large-scale Near Eastern **demic** expansion.
- **mid-1990s–2000s (modern mtDNA):** Richards et al. estimated only ~10–22% of
  European maternal lineages arrived with the Neolithic — most were already
  present in Mesolithic/Palaeolithic foragers. Emphasis shifted to **continuity**.
- **2005 onward (aDNA):** Haak et al.'s LBK burials carried haplogroup N1a,
  nearly absent today — proof of **discontinuity** and population turnover, *and*
  proof against modern contamination. The talk shifted back to migration.

The reconciling model (and the volume's preferred reading): **leapfrog pioneer
colonization from Anatolia + assimilation of indigenous foragers**, with major
expansions *within* Europe during the Neolithic, and later turnover that
overwrote much early-farmer ancestry. Pigs and cattle were imported from the
Near East, then partly replaced by local lineages.

## Limitations and disputes (why `contested: yes`)

- **Method war.** Phylogeographers (founder analysis) vs. population geneticists
  (admixture models) disagree sharply; critics call phylogeography "story-telling,"
  defenders note its predictions (e.g. modern-human origins) keep being confirmed.
- **Molecular-clock uncertainty.** Recalibration (Soares et al., correcting for
  purifying selection) shifted putative "late Glacial" lineages into the
  Mesolithic; Y-chromosome rates remain too poorly known to settle the European
  question.
- **Modern data obscure the Neolithic** under millennia of later migration —
  hence the field's pivot to aDNA. A 20:80 modern ratio is *not* demographically
  meaningful (1% of incomers could expand to 20%).
- **Coverage bias:** robust aDNA exists essentially only for Europe; Africa,
  the Americas, and SE Asia are read mostly from modern data.

## Application beyond Europe

- **Sub-Saharan Africa:** the [[bantu-expansion]] *(Cycle 5)* reshaped the
  genetic landscape — Bantu-speakers carry a maternal founder effect ~2,000 years
  ago and assimilated lineages from forest foragers (Batwa/"Pygmy") and Khoe-San.
- **SE Asia/Pacific:** the "express-train from Taiwan" model of the
  [[austronesian-dispersal]] *(Cycle 5)* does **not** hold up genetically — most
  Pacific lineages (incl. the mtDNA "Polynesian motif") have deep island ancestry
  shaped by post-glacial sea-level rise, with eastern Indonesia/Near Oceania as a
  key source. The Austronesian spread may owe more to social networks than mass
  migration.
