# Orientalism (Said)

The concept, defined by **Edward Said** in *[[said-orientalism|Orientalism]]* (1978), of a **systematic
Western discourse about "the Orient"** that constructed the East as the West's exotic, irrational,
despotic and unchanging "Other" — and in so doing helped to **justify and enable European domination**
over Asia and the Middle East. One of the most influential and contested ideas in the modern humanities.

## The Argument (emic to the critique)

- **Knowledge as power.** Drawing on **Foucault**, Said treats "Orientalism" not as innocent scholarship
  but as a **discourse** — a self-reinforcing system of texts, images and assumptions (in philology,
  literature, art, travel-writing, administration) that produced "the Orient" as an object of Western
  knowledge and control.
- **The West and its Other.** European identity was defined *against* an imagined East: the Orient as
  sensual, stagnant, feminine, despotic — a mirror affirming Western rationality, progress and
  superiority.
- **Complicity with empire.** This body of knowledge was **bound up with imperial power**: it framed the
  peoples it described as needing Western tutelage, and it outlasted formal empire in persistent cultural
  stereotypes.

## Significance

*Orientalism* founded **postcolonial studies** and reshaped how historians read European accounts of
Asia, Africa and the Middle East — making the **representations themselves** an object of historical
critique. It is essential to the wiki's historiography of [[imperialism|imperialism]] and of European
knowledge of the non-European world.

## Critique (etic)

The thesis is heavily **contested**: critics (Bernard Lewis, Robert Irwin, and many area specialists)
charge Said with selective evidence, a caricatured and monolithic "Orientalism" that ignores genuine
scholarship and internal Western dissent, neglect of German and Russian traditions, the erasure of
**Oriental agency**, and a tension between Foucauldian anti-realism and Said's own humanism. Even
critics, however, concede that it permanently changed the terms of debate.
