# Finnegans Wake

**Finnegans Wake** (1939) is the final novel of [[joyce-james|James Joyce]], written over
some seventeen years and famous as one of the most formally radical works in the English
literary canon: composed in a dense, multilingual, pun-saturated invented idiom that
dissolves conventional narrative, character, and even standard vocabulary, structured
(per Joyce's stated intention, drawing on the cyclical historical theory of Giambattista
Vico) as an endless cyclical "dream" of history repeating itself, its final sentence
looping back to its opening words.

## Reception and Significance

Following the more accessible (if still demanding) *Ulysses* (1922), *Finnegans Wake*
pushed Joyce's experimentation with language and consciousness to its furthest extreme,
dividing critics and readers from publication onward between admiration for its ambition
and formal invention and frustration at its near-impenetrability. It has become a
touchstone of high literary modernism and a major subject of specialized academic
scholarship (a dedicated "Wake studies" field), while remaining one of the least-read
canonical novels in the English language relative to its fame.
