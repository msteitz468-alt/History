# Feynman Diagrams

Pictorial representation of the mathematical expressions describing the behavior and interaction of subatomic particles in quantum field theory, particularly quantum electrodynamics (QED). Lines represent particle world-lines (electrons forward in time, positrons backward), wavy lines photons; vertices represent interactions. Each diagram corresponds to a term in a perturbative expansion; the sum of all diagrams (with proper rules for signs, factors, and integration) yields observable quantities.

Feynman introduced them as a private calculational tool and visual “how-to” for handling the infinities that had plagued QED. Freeman Dyson showed they were equivalent to (and often far more practical than) the operator methods of Schwinger and Tomonaga, and supplied the rigorous justification that allowed the community to adopt them. They became (and remain) the standard language of particle physics calculations.
