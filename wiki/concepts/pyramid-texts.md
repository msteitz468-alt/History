# Pyramid Texts

The **Pyramid Texts** are the corpus of funerary spells inscribed on the walls of the
burial chambers of late Old Kingdom royal pyramids — "the earliest large religious
composition known from ancient Egypt."

## Origin and transmission

They first appear on the walls of the burial chamber of the pyramid of **Unas** (last
king of the 5th Dynasty, r. ~2375–2345 BCE) and were then used by all the remaining
major Old Kingdom rulers (Teti, Pepy I, Merenra, [[actors/pepy-ii|Pepy II]]) and for
some queens. "Some of their elements were created well before the reign of Unas and map
out the development of Egyptian religious thought from Predynastic times."

## Content and purpose

The texts interweave several strands of belief: "the Osirian religious doctrine is by
far the most important … but there are also ideas associated with the sun-god, as well
as the remains of star-oriented concepts." In the spells the dead king is identified
with **Osiris** (e.g. "Osiris Unas") and assured of ascent to the sky and the company of
the sun-god. Their function was practical-magical: "to provide the deceased king with
texts that were regarded as essential for his survival and well-being in afterlife.
Their mere presence was probably deemed sufficient to make them effective." The
complexity of the compilation makes individual spells hard to interpret.

## Significance

The Pyramid Texts are the royal ancestor of the later democratized funerary literature —
the Middle Kingdom **Coffin Texts** and the New Kingdom **Book of the Dead** (see
[[sources/romer-egyptian-book-of-the-dead-2008]]) — through which afterlife provisions
spread beyond the king. They are a primary witness to the rise of the
**[[concepts/divine-kingship|cult of divine kingship]]** and of the Osiris and solar
theologies in the [[actors/old-kingdom-egypt|Old Kingdom]].
