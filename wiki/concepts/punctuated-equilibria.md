## Definition

Punctuated equilibria is the paleontological thesis that the fossil record of most
species shows **long-term morphological stasis interrupted by short bursts of
rapid change**, rather than the slow, continuous transformation ("phyletic
gradualism") predicted by a naïve reading of Darwinian natural selection. Species
typically appear in the record suddenly, persist relatively unchanged for periods
that may stretch into millions of years, and then disappear — to be replaced by
other species that may or may not be their close relatives.

The corollary that matters most for paleoanthropology is **species-as-individuals**:
species are discrete, individuated historical entities — actors in the evolutionary
play — not arbitrary segments of steadily evolving lineages. Most morphological
change is concentrated around speciation events (estimated by Eldredge and Gould at
~5,000–50,000 years — "in geological terms, the blink of an eye"), occurring in
small, isolated populations.

## Etic, not emic

This is a purely **etic** (analyst's) framework: a 20th-century scientific model
applied to deep time. It distinguishes sharply between (a) *speciation* — the
establishment of reproductive isolation, which is a *result* arising for many
possible reasons (genetic, chromosomal, behavioral, anatomical) — and (b) the
acquisition of anatomical novelties used to *recognize* species in fossils. The two
do not coincide, which is why species are hard to read off morphology alone.

## Application to Human Evolution

In [[tattersall-world-from-beginnings-2008|Tattersall's]] hands, punctuated
equilibria underwrites the whole shape of [[human-evolution]]:

- **Trends are species sorting, not within-lineage fine-tuning.** The apparent
  steady enlargement of the hominid brain over ~2 million years is better explained
  by the relative success of larger-brained *species* in competition than by
  gradual selection on individuals within each population.
- **Abrupt morphological origins.** The archaic-to-modern shift in body plan
  (to [[homo-ergaster]], ~1.6 mya) has no known intermediates and appears suddenly —
  consistent with a regulatory-gene change (evo-devo; Jeffrey Schwartz's *Sudden
  Origins*, 1999), not eons of fine-tuning.
- **A bushy phylogeny.** Multiple coexisting, individuated hominid species
  (australopiths, [[homo-erectus]], [[homo-heidelbergensis]], [[neanderthals]],
  *Homo sapiens*) competing and going extinct — against the old "main line of
  descent."
- **Behavior need not track speciation.** New behaviors (toolmaking) arise within
  existing species in individuals; new species need not bring new behaviors, and
  vice versa.

## Limitations and Critiques

Punctuated equilibria was and remains **contested**. Critics argue that (1) apparent
stasis may partly reflect the coarse temporal resolution and gaps of the fossil
record rather than genuine evolutionary standstill; (2) "rapid" in geological terms
is still many millennia of ordinary microevolution, so the model may not require
mechanisms beyond the Modern Synthesis (the dispute is partly about emphasis and
scale); and (3) gradual sequences *are* documented in some well-sampled lineages.
Defenders reply that the gaps are themselves data. For human evolution the framework
is bound up with the **splitter–lumper** dispute over how many hominid species the
morphology supports — Tattersall is a leading splitter, and lumpers reading the same
fossils as fewer, more variable species also read the tempo differently. Relatedly,
[[symbolic-cognition]] is explained by Tattersall not by punctuated *anatomical*
change at all but by exaptation and emergence plus a cultural release.
