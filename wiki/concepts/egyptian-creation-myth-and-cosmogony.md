# Egyptian Creation Myth and Cosmogony

The body of Egyptian myth about the origin and ordering of the world — above all the
**Memphite Theology** of the Shabaka Stone and the cosmogonic schemes embedded in king
lists — read by Barry Kemp ([[sources/kemp-ancient-egypt-anatomy-2018|*Anatomy of a
Civilization*]], Ch. 2) not as inherited folk belief but as the deliberately codified
**intellectual architecture of the early state**: the myth that made [[concepts/divine-kingship|kingship]]
and the [[events/unification-of-egypt-3100bce|unified kingdom]] appear cosmically
necessary.

## Gods Before Men: Cosmic Time

The **Turin king list** extends royal time backwards from the first human king (Menes)
through unnamed "spirits" (devotees) to a list of gods — including Seth and probably
ending with [[actors/maat|Maat]] — each written in a cartouche "as if a king" and assigned
a precise reign length (Thoth's reign is given as 7,726 years). Divine rule is thus set in
deep cosmic time, with order ([[actors/maat|Maat]]) standing at the origin.

## The Horus–Seth Settlement (Memphite Theology)

The Shabaka Stone narrates **Geb** adjudicating the quarrel of Horus and Seth: first
dividing Egypt between them (Seth in Upper, Horus in Lower Egypt), then revoking Seth's
portion and awarding sole rule to **Horus, "the firstborn"** son of Osiris. This is the
archetypal tension every king re-resolves — order contained, chaos pacified. Kemp argues
the composition is **not an Old Kingdom text** copied from a "worm-eaten document" but a
relatively **late** work (perhaps of Shabaka's own time, c. 721–707 BCE) written in
deliberately archaic language — itself an instance of the "invention of tradition."

## From Paired Beasts to Horus and Seth

Kemp traces the myth's iconographic prehistory. Predynastic slate palettes show
**harmonious pairs of fierce beasts** (lions, wild dogs, serpopards) symbolizing the
reconciliation of opposites — a generalized cosmology. The Dynasty 1 codification
**upgraded** this into the specific political dyad of **Horus vs. Seth**; the earliest
"unity of two kingdoms" images in fact use *two facing Horus figures*, a transitional form
before the differentiated pair. Seth becomes the embodiment of all disorder — storms,
hostile deserts, foreign gods, even red-haired people — yet ultimately acquiesces in the
judgment against him. The motif of **"containment of unrule"** (animals captured and
controlled) survived into historic temple art, e.g. New Kingdom scenes of the king
trapping wild birds in a clap-net, captioned as the containment of disorder.

## Epistemology: Knowing the Name

Kemp links the cosmogony to a distinctive Egyptian epistemology preserved in the
**onomastica** (lists of names of things, e.g. Amenemope's *Onomasticon*, c. 1100 BCE):
knowledge was largely the accumulation of **names** and their associations, and to know a
thing's name was to have power over it — names as "discrete units of knowledge."

## Limitations and Critiques

Kemp's "invented at the Dynasty 0/1 transition" reading is an interpretive synthesis of
archaeology and myth; the dating of the Shabaka Stone and the historicity (or not) of a
two-kingdoms phase remain debated. See [[controversies/unification-of-egypt-historical-vs-mythical]]
if promoted.
