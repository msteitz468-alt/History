# Civil War Infantry Tactics

The specific application of linear tactics by Union and Confederate infantry during the American Civil War. Characterized by continued reliance on two-rank lines for combat, multiple supporting lines, column formations for movement and reserves, increased (but still limited) use of skirmishers, and complex regimental/brigade maneuvers executed under fire.

## Key Characteristics (Hess)
- Strong preference for **line** over column when engaging the enemy.
- Columns used extensively to hold units in readiness and to shift supports within multiple-line deployments.
- Combat almost invariably at short ranges (majority of casualties inflicted inside 100–150 yards).
- High levels of small-unit proficiency and "articulation" among veteran regiments (multiple maneuvers in one fight).
- Frontal attacks and prolonged firefights were the norm; decisive shock assaults relatively rare and often costly.
- Problems of coordination were more pronounced at division/corps/army level than at regimental level.

## Relation to Broader Linear Tactics
Direct continuation of the flexible linear system of the Revolutionary/Napoleonic era rather than a revolutionary break caused by the rifle musket. The rifle's theoretical range advantage was rarely exploited due to trajectory, visibility, training, and the persistent preference for decisive short-range fire.
