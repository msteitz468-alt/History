# Scara (Carolingian Cavalry Unit)

The *scara* (pl. *scae* or *scarae*; also *scara francisca*, *scara sua*) was the elite rapid-response formation of armoured heavy cavalry in the Carolingian army. It served as the professional mobile striking force and royal bodyguard element, distinct from the larger general levy of free men.

## Definition and Scope

A *scara* consisted of excellent, well-equipped horsemen capable of quick marches, raids, garrisons, and independent or parallel operations. It replaced the Merovingian *antrustiones* as the core of the king's personal military following. By the second half of the ninth century the term had become technical for a formation of armoured cavalry.

Not the entire army, but a select, expedita manu (quick unit) that could operate while the main host mustered or campaigned elsewhere.

## Historical Development

Under Pepin III and Charlemagne the need for permanent troops on frontiers and for rapid response drove the expansion of the *scara* system:

- 766: Pepin III placed the scara of the Franks as garrison at Bourges against the duke of Aquitaine.
- 768: Counts, warriors of the scara, and leudes sent in four directions pursuing the duke of Aquitaine (explicitly horsemen).
- 773 (Lombard campaign): One army held at Mount Cenis fortifications; Charles sent his scara by another route to attack.
- 774: After Pavia, from Ingelheim Charles sent four *scareae* against the Saxons.
- 776: *Scarae* garrisoned conquered Saxon castles (Eresburg, Lippe).
- 778: After Spanish campaign, from Auxerre the *scara Francisca* sent against plundering Saxons (rapid pursuit).
- 782 (Lippspringe assembly): Three leaders of the scara (Adalgis, Geilo, Worad) attacked revolted Saxons on their own initiative but in disarray ("not as if they were attacking an enemy in battle order, but as if they were about to pursue a beaten enemy and take the spoils. Every warrior rode as fast as his horse could gallop"). Result: serious defeat; leaders and ~20 noble men killed.
- 784: Charles the son and the scara defeated Westphalians in *equestri proelio* (combat of horsemen).
- 791: Scara of Pepin (son of king) from Italy attacked Avars ahead of main Bavarian expedition.
- 806, 812: Multiple *scareae* used for parallel invasions (e.g., over Elbe into Sorbs while main army operated elsewhere; three *scareae* vs. Wilzians).
- Frontier and raid use: 809-810 sent to marches; 793/803 when no general campaign, scara sent where needed.

Recruitment drew from young royal vassals kept at palace as bodyguard and from landed vassals. As conquests waned, emphasis remained on quality armoured horsemen over mass levies.

## Equipment and Character

The *scara* comprised the heavily armoured cavalry (brunia, helmet, leg guards, quality swords, lances, bows) that gave the Franks superiority. They were "a group of excellent warriors."

## Relation to Later Organisations

The *scara* represents the institutional ancestor of the later knightly *conroi* and *bataille* system. It demonstrates that organised, disciplined units of armoured horsemen, capable of coordinated rapid manoeuvre, existed centuries before the high medieval period. Failures (782) and successes alike illustrate problems of order and command that recur in 12th-13th century sources.

## Historiography and Sources

Grounded in the *Annales regni Francorum*, Einhard, and related Carolingian texts. Verbruggen uses these passages to refute the notion that medieval (or even early medieval) cavalry lacked organisation or discipline. The 782 episode is particularly telling: the disaster arose precisely because the scara attacked *without* proper battle order.
