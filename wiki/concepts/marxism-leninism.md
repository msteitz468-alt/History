# Marxism-Leninism
## Definition
**Marxism-Leninism** was the official state ideology of the Soviet Union (USSR) and the ruling communist parties of the Soviet Bloc. It represents a synthesis of Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels' materialist philosophy with Vladimir Lenin's theoretical and practical adaptations. The doctrine postulates **dialectical materialism** as the ultimate key to understanding reality, views human history as a sequence of class struggles driven by economic forces (the base determining the political and cultural superstructure), and holds that the working class, guided by a disciplined vanguard party, must overthrow capitalism to build a classless, socialist society.

## Context and Origin
Marxism-Leninism emerged as a formalized dogma following Lenin's death in 1924, particularly under Joseph Stalin who codified the teachings to legitimize his policies of "socialism in one country," rapid industrialization, and forced collectivization. The ideology incorporated several key additions by Lenin:
- **The Vanguard Party:** The belief that the working class on its own only develops a "trade union consciousness" and must be led by a tightly knit party of professional revolutionaries.
- **Theory of Imperialism (1916):** The assertion that capitalism in its dying stage becomes monopoly finance capitalism (imperialism), leading to colonial exploitation, international crises, and global wars. This theory explained how a socialist revolution could erupt in a backward agrarian state like Russia (the "weakest link" in the capitalist chain) rather than in advanced industrial nations.
- **Peasant-Proletarian Alliance:** The tactical inclusion of poor peasants as allies of the industrial proletariat in the revolutionary struggle.

## Limitations and Critiques
Critics and historians point to several contradictions and critiques of Marxism-Leninism:
- **Authoritarianism:** The ideology's claim to be a rigorously scientific, infallible truth led to extreme political intolerance and the suppression of all dissenting opinions, treating critics as class enemies (Line 5615).
- **Ethics and Ruthlessness:** The subordination of ethics to the economic base meant that the regime could justify mass executions, deportations, and terror in the name of building a future socialist Utopia.
- **Deterministic vs. Activist Paradox:** While claiming that the collapse of capitalism is historically inevitable, the ideology demands constant, highly coercive state intervention to force society along the prescribed historical path.
- **Failed Predictions:** Real-world developments disproved key Marxist predictions: Western working-class living standards improved rather than deteriorated, and polarization into capitalists and proletarians did not occur due to the growth of the middle class (Line 5629).
