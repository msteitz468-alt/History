# Common Celtic (Proto-Celtic)

**Common Celtic** (Proto-Celtic) is the reconstructed common ancestor language of the
attested Celtic languages — the Continental Celtic languages (Gaulish, Celtiberian,
Lepontic) and the Insular Celtic branches, Goidelic (Irish, Scottish Gaelic, Manx; see
[[concepts/goidelic]]) and Brythonic (Welsh, Cornish, Breton). Like all proto-languages, it
is a scholarly reconstruction derived by the comparative method from shared innovations
and correspondences among its descendants, not a language directly attested in any text;
its speakers left no direct written record of the proto-stage itself.

## Scholarly Debate

Linguists broadly agree Common Celtic descends from Proto-Indo-European (see
[[controversies/indo-european-origins|the Indo-European origins controversy]]) but disagree
over its homeland, date of breakup, and internal structure — notably whether Insular
Celtic forms a genuine genetic subgroup (the "Insular Celtic hypothesis") or whether
Continental and Insular similarities instead reflect independent parallel developments, an
open and actively debated question with implications for models of Celtic expansion across
Iron Age Europe.
