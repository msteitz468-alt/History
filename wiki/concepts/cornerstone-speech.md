# Cornerstone Speech (Alexander H. Stephens, 21 March 1861)

The address delivered by Confederate Vice President Alexander H. Stephens in Savannah, Georgia, on 21 March 1861, in which he declared that the new Southern government rested on the "great truth" that the negro is not equal to the white man and that slavery is his "natural and normal condition." The speech named the "Cornerstone" of the Confederacy as racial hierarchy and scientific racism, in explicit contrast to the Declaration of Independence.

## Immediate and Global Circulation

Telegraphed and reprinted throughout the United States and Europe within days, the speech became the single most quoted piece of Confederate self-incrimination. Abolitionists and anti-Confederate publicists (Frederick Douglass, Charles Sumner, British and French liberals) treated it as authoritative proof that the rebellion was not a conventional struggle for independence or self-determination but a project to entrench and expand human bondage on a new national scale.

## Historiographical Status

Lost Cause apologists and some later Southern historians attempted to minimize or contextualize the speech as extemporaneous or unrepresentative. Modern scholarship (and Bonner) restores it to its central place: the clearest contemporaneous statement of the Confederate project's ideological foundation at the moment of birth. In the 21st century it remains the primary text cited in arguments for the removal of Confederate symbols and monuments.

## Link to Pariah Status

The speech supplied the factual premise for the claim that the Confederacy was a "new power of a new species" that could not be admitted to the community of civilized nations without corrupting them. It turned Confederate diplomacy into an uphill battle against its own Vice President's words.
