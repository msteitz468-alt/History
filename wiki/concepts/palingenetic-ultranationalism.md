# Palingenetic Ultranationalism

Roger Griffin's ideal-type definition of [[generic-fascism|fascism]], stated
twice verbatim in [[griffin-nature-of-fascism-1991|*The Nature of Fascism*]]:

> "Fascism is a genus of political ideology whose mythic core in its various
> permutations is a palingenetic form of populist ultranationalism."

The most widely cited one-sentence definition in fascism studies — the core
of the so-called "new consensus."

## Components

- **Palingenesis** (*palin*, again + *genesis*, birth): "the sense of a new
  start or of regeneration after a phase of crisis or decline." Griffin
  stresses the myth is a general human archetype, not fascist property — his
  examples run from Christian resurrection symbolism and medieval
  millenarianism through the French Revolutionaries' "regeneration of the
  world," the Russian Revolution, perestroika, the New Deal, and green
  catastrophism to "turn over a new leaf." Crucially it is **non-
  restorationist**: "The arrow of time thus points not backwards but
  forwards, even when the archer looks over his shoulder for guidance on
  where to aim." The "new man" is its characteristic sub-myth.
- **Populist** (after Eley): forces that "in practice or in principle...
  depend on 'people power' as the basis of their legitimacy," even if
  elite-led — excluding both dynastic nationalism and mere "pseudopopulist"
  dictatorships that fake consensus (Pinochet, Ceaușescu, Saddam).
- **Ultra-nationalism**: nationalism that "'goes beyond', and hence rejects,
  anything compatible with liberal institutions or... Enlightenment
  humanism" — in Weberian terms, repudiating traditional and legal-rational
  authority for charismatic authority.

Combined, the terms "delimit each other" into a precise concept: "a genus of
political energy... whose mobilizing vision is that of the national
community rising phoenix-like after a period of encroaching decadence which
all but destroyed it." The full three-component synthesis adds the **myth of
decadence**: fascism fuses the rebirth archetype, populist ultra-nationalism,
and the fin-de-siècle decadence myth; "from the moment populist nationalism
coincided with a climate of palingenetic expectancy fascism was 'bound' to
appear."

## What the type includes and excludes

The definition deliberately drops Nolte's "negations" and Payne's style/
organization clusters from the *definition* (every ideology has an
anti-dimension and a style — "Liberalism is anti-despotism"). It includes
Nazism emphatically — "an outstanding specimen of generic fascism once
analysed in terms of this core myth," *pace* [[sternhell-thesis|Sternhell]] —
with biological racism as one contingent variant of the mythic core, not a
generic separator: "It is not intrinsic to fascism that particular groups...
should be picked out for persecution, let alone for systematic genocide."
It excludes conservative, authoritarian, restorationist, and military
regimes lacking the palingenetic mass-mobilizing myth (see
[[para-fascism]]), and "reformist" ultra-right parties. Fascism is
"radically anti-conservative"; taxonomically Griffin settles for "a
sub-category of the ultra-right but with special qualities of its own" —
"palingenetic ultra-right" — while conceding the "beyond left and right"
framing. Fascism is also "an alternative modernism rather than a rejection
of it," and a political **myth**, not a "political religion" (Voegelin
rejected by name).

## Theoretical apparatus

- **Sorel as godfather**: Griffin names [[sorel-georges|Sorel]] "the
  symbolic father-figure of the theory adopted here" for the concept of the
  mobilizing myth.
- **Five inferences from the Italian case**: fascism requires an objective
  institutional crisis; neither leader nor uniforms are definitional;
  factiousness is intrinsic (left and right wings from birth); action has
  primacy over programme; its politics is charismatic and irrational.
- **Structural weakness**: because the core myth is permanent revolution,
  fascism cannot survive normalization or leader-succession — Eugen Weber's
  "continuous treadmill of the future" passage is load-bearing; fascist
  regimes are "doomed" to entropy or catastrophe (cf. Paxton's
  radicalization-or-entropy stage five).
- **Psycho-historical basis** (Ch. 7): built on Koestler's
  **integration vs. identification** distinction — "it is the perversion of
  self-transcendent emotions, not self-assertive ones, which has been
  largely responsible for the chronicle of atrocities." Fascism's myth
  "calls a priori for an act of identification"; hence "Fascism is not
  'resistance to transcendence' (pace Nolte) but the result of succumbing to
  a perverted and peculiarly modern form of it." The trigger is a **nomic
  (sense-making) crisis** — Platt, Tudor, Durkheim's anomie, Weber's
  disenchantment — and Griffin flags the syndrome as "predominantly
  masculine" (cf. Theweleit's Freikorps study). Sewell and Hunt's work on
  French-Revolutionary regeneration shows the palingenetic myth also powers
  non-fascist revolutions — it is the *combination* that is fascist.

## Corroboration and critiques

Griffin claims independent corroboration: Mosse's "new fascist man" as the
stereotype of all fascisms; Payne's own "new nationalist authoritarian
state... new... culture"; Mohler's identification of *Neugeburt* as "the
exact German equivalent of 'palingenesis'" in the Conservative Revolution;
Zunino's decadence-to-new-civilization content analysis of Fascist print
culture; and the neo-fascist insider Bardèche's own 1961 definition.

Critiques on record: [[payne-typology-of-authoritarian-nationalism|Payne]]
finds the definition over-inclusive ("palingenetic" and "populist" fit
non-fascist cases like Bolivia's MNR); [[paxton-anatomy-of-fascism-2004|
Paxton]] judges that the "zeal to reduce fascism to one pithy sentence seems
to me more likely to inhibit than to stimulate analysis"; Sternhell's school
objects that mythic coherence understates fascism's doctrinal content
(Griffin's counter: Sternhell "excessively homogenize[s] and
intellectualize[s]" it). Griffin in turn contests Payne's claim that fascism
was "primarily limited to Europe" in the world-wars era, applying the type
to Brazil's Integralism, Japan's Tōhōkai, and South Africa's AWB.

## Griffin (2007): Palingenesis Grounded in Modernism

*Modernism and Fascism* re-derives the 1991 definition from an anthropological base: the "palingenetic myth" is "an archetype of the human mythopoeic faculty," activated whenever modernity's nomic crisis becomes acute (see [[modernism]] and [[revitalization-movement]]). The book's leitmotif is **Aufbruch** — "the sense of a beginning, the mood of standing on the threshold of a new world" (Kracauer's word for early Weimar) — as "a key element in the genesis, psychology, ideology, policies, and praxis of fascism." Fascist rebirth is "a 'palingenesis' brought about not through suprahuman agency, but engineered through the power of the modern state" ([[modernist-state]]). Case texture added: Barrès declaring 3 August 1914 "a sacred day"; Papini's *Lacerba* greeting the war as "a Malthusian operation"; squadrismo as palingenetic purification ("even civil servants or shopkeepers can and must be 'new Italians'"); Caporetto triggering the transposition of the fatherland "onto an absolute plane"; Kroll's "category of renewal" as the common matrix uniting Hitler, Goebbels, Darré, Rosenberg, and Himmler; Moeller van den Bruck's "reconnection forwards" as the temporal signature (Fest's "aggressive utopia"; Osborne's rigorously futural reaction). (Source: [[griffin-modernism-and-fascism-2007]].)
