# Persisting Strategy

A fundamental category in Archer Jones' analytical framework: a strategy whose objective is to occupy and control territory (and the resources and population in it) in order to achieve political goals. It typically involves seeking decisive battle or siege to break the enemy's main forces and then occupying the country.

Contrasted with **raiding strategy**.

Characteristics (per Jones):
- Requires secure lines of communication and supply (or ability to live off the land in a controlled way).
- Often leads to set-piece battles or sieges.
- Success measured by control of ground and ability to sustain presence.
- In ancient terms: Alexander's conquests, Roman provincial control, Napoleonic occupation goals.
- In modern: conventional campaigns aiming at regime change or territorial gain.

Related: concentration against weakness, turning movements, combined arms to enable the occupation.
