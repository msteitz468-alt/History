# Invention of the Barbarian

[[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] Ch.11 discusses how Archaic Greek engagement with non-Greeks coexists with a later, sharper **barbarian** stereotype crystallized around the Persian conflicts. The phrase “invention of the barbarian” (cf. Edith Hall and related scholarship) names that Classical construction rather than a timeless Greek attitude.

## Archaic baseline

Archaic Greeks traded, intermarried, fought, and guest-friended with non-Greeks across the Mediterranean and Near East (Phoenicians, Lydians, Egyptians, Thracians, Scythians, etc.). Material culture and elite lifestyle often adopted eastern luxuries. Hall’s point: Archaic interaction does **not** presuppose a binary Greek/barbarian identity of Classical type. Linguistic “barbaros” usage and ethnographic curiosity develop unevenly; classical linguistic Hellenism as political ideology is later.

## Crystallization after the Persian Wars

The external dimension of Greek identity — defining Hellene against *barbaros* — intensifies after Marathon, Salamis, and Plataea. Tragedy, oratory, and historiography (Herodotus as complex witness) stage Persian alterity. Hall links post-Marathon ostracisms of Pisistratid/Alcmaeonid associates and Medism accusations (e.g. Kallias ostraka) not only to treason fear after [[actors/hippias-son-of-pisistratus|Hippias]] at Marathon but to demotic critique of elite eastern luxury and guest-friendship: proscribing barbarian customs “tamed” elite behavior after 508 and after Salamis naval *dêmos* confidence.

## Relation to panhellenism

`preceded_by` / `concurrent_with:` internal [[concepts/panhellenism]] (sanctuaries, games, genealogy) develops largely in the sixth century as an elite project; the barbarian binary is more strongly a **post-480** political-cultural formation, though roots and prototypes exist earlier. Do not collapse the two: shared festivals do not automatically invent the barbarian Other.

## Emic / etic

- **Emic:** Classical Greeks used *barbaros* and related stereotypes in law, drama, and politics.
- **Etic:** “Invention of the barbarian” is a modern historiographical label stressing contingency and political context, not eternal essence.

## Limitations and critiques

Risk of overstating sudden invention vs gradual stereotyping; Herodotus is not a simple inventor of the binary; regional Greeks under Persian rule complicate pure opposition; elite orientalizing fashion coexists with barbarian discourse. Hall’s account is process-oriented and periodizing, not a total cultural psychology of Greeks.
