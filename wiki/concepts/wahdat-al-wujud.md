# Wahdat al-Wujud (Unity of Being)

The doctrine that all reality is one and identical in essence with God; the manifested universe is the self-disclosure (tajalli) of the divine being.

## Definition and Scope

Everything that exists is God. The absolute divine essence transcends all manifestations, yet the created world is identical with the divine in essence. Creation proceeds through hierarchical planes (attributes/names of God, spiritual existences, archetypes, sensible world) renewed at every "breath" of the Lord. Key symbols: insan al-kamil (perfect man), haqiqa muhammadiya (Muhammadan reality) as condensations of the cosmos.

## Origin and Development

Articulated by Muhyi al-Din Ibn al-ʿArabi (b. Murcia 1165, d. Damascus 1240). Claimed authority equivalent to prophetic revelation (dictated by Gabriel in Bezels). Drew on Hermetic, neo-Platonic, Isma'ili, and prior Sufi sources (Ibn Sina emanationism, Suhrawardi light metaphysics). 

## Application and Critiques

In gnostic/theosophical Sufism it justified contemplative union over strict ritual observance and reinforced saint veneration, shrine pilgrimage, and popular belief in miracles and the invisible hierarchy of saints (qutb/pole). Critics (including some reformist and scripturalist traditions) saw it as leading to antinomianism, pantheism, or dilution of law and tawhid. It coexisted with (and was sometimes integrated into) orthoprax Sufism.

## Historiography

Lapidus (Ch. 23) presents it as culmination of centuries of Sufi gnostic contemplation with profound (if unintended) effects on popular practice: "For ordinary believers, it came to mean the veneration of saints and the tombs of saints." Debates center on whether it represents legitimate esoteric exegesis or deviation from scriptural norms. Strong collection coverage via Lapidus + Schimmel, Hodgson, Adamson.

**Grounded quote (Lapidus Range 2):** "In Ibn al-ʿArabi’s thought, all reality is one. This is the doctrine of wahdat al-wujud, the unity of being. Everything that exists is God... By implication, theosophical metaphysics diminished the importance of observance of law and ritual... For ordinary believers, it came to mean the veneration of saints and the tombs of saints."
