# Lines of Operations

**Lines of operations** is a foundational concept in Jominian strategic theory: the
geographic routes connecting an army's [[concepts/bases-of-operations|base of operations]]
to its objective, along which forces, supplies, and communications move. Jomini classified
lines as interior (shorter, more concentrated lines allowing a central force to shift
rapidly between separated enemy forces — the geometric basis of Napoleon's preference for
operating on interior lines against divided coalitions) or exterior (longer, more dispersed
lines typically used by an enveloping or numerically superior force).

## Use and Legacy

Along with related Jominian concepts such as [[concepts/theater-of-operations|theater of
operations]] and decisive points, lines of operations became a standard analytical
vocabulary in 19th- and 20th-century staff-college strategic education, particularly
influential in American military thought. Critics (from Clausewitz onward) have argued
that Jomini's geometric framework, while a useful descriptive tool, risks reducing
strategy to spatial calculation at the expense of the political, moral, and
unpredictable ("friction") dimensions of war.
