# Señoríos Naturales (Andean Chiefdoms / Native Lordships)

## Definition

***Señoríos naturales*** ("natural lordships") was the **Spanish colonial juridical category**
for indigenous polities ruled by hereditary lords (*caciques*, in the Andes *curacas*) whose
authority predated and, in Spanish legal theory, could be recognized as legitimate under the
Crown. Juan and Judith Villamarín's chapter in
[[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|Volume III]] ("Chiefdoms: The Prevalence and
Persistence of *Señoríos Naturales*") uses the term to argue that **middle-range chiefdoms**,
not just empires, were the **prevalent** form of complex political organization across South
America at contact — and that they **persisted** under colonial rule because the Spanish found
them useful for extracting tribute and labor.

These polities — the [[muisca|Muisca]] of the Colombian highlands, the chiefdoms of the
Ecuadorian sierra and coast, Caribbean and Amazonian *cacicazgos* — sit on the
[[chiefdom|chiefdom]] rung between egalitarian societies and states: ranked, with hereditary
elites and tribute, but lacking the bureaucratic apparatus of the [[inca-empire|Inka]].

## Contested Points

The chapter intervenes in the **anthropological "chiefdom" debate**: whether "chiefdom" is a
real evolutionary stage or an artifact of neo-evolutionary typology. Villamarín stress
**persistence and prevalence** against narratives that foreground only states and empires. The
colonial recognition of *señoríos* also raises the question of how far Spanish categories
distorted the indigenous realities they claimed to describe — a theme of Salomon's source-
criticism chapter.
