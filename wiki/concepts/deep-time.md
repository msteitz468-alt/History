# Deep Time and the Age of the Earth

**Deep time** is the recognition that the Earth and life have a history vastly
longer than recorded human history — measured in millions and billions of years
rather than the thousands implied by biblical chronology. The discovery of deep
time, and the development of methods to measure it, was a precondition for every
science of the prehuman past (geology, paleontology, paleoanthropology) and for
the theory of [[concepts/evolution-by-natural-selection|evolution by natural
selection]], which required immense spans of time for slow change to accumulate.

## Relative Time: Reading the Rock Record

The first achievement was **relative** dating — ordering events without numbers.

- **Superposition.** Nicolas Steno (1638–1686), a Danish anatomist and bishop,
  enunciated that in an undisturbed sedimentary sequence older rocks lie below
  younger. The Grand Canyon is the classic layer-cake illustration.
- **Biostratigraphy and the geological map.** William Smith (1769–1839), an
  English surveyor, discovered that strata could be identified and correlated
  over great distances by the distinctive fossils each contained, and in 1815
  published the first geological map of a whole country (England and Wales) —
  "the map that changed the world." Plagiarized and bankrupted, he was later
  honored as the Father of English Geology.
- **The named systems.** Walking the outcrops, geologists named successive
  periods after places and peoples — Cambrian, Ordovician, Silurian (Welsh
  tribes), Devonian, Carboniferous, Permian, Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous —
  a relative scale in general use by the mid-19th century.

This produced a sequence (older/younger) but no absolute ages. To say a fossil
was "Silurian" placed it but did not date it in years.

## Absolute Time: The Contest Over the Age of the Earth

- **Biblical chronology.** Archbishop James Ussher (1581–1656), building on
  calculations by Kepler and Newton, fixed creation at 4004 BCE — serious
  scholarship for its time, but resting on the false assumption that the age of
  the Earth equalled the age of humanity.
- **Process-based estimates.** Nineteenth-century attempts divided total
  sediment thickness or ocean salinity (John Joly, 1899: ~80–150 million years)
  by annual accumulation rates — wildly variable. George Darwin (1898) estimated
  56 million years from tidal slowing of the Moon.
- **Kelvin's cooling estimate.** William Thomson (Lord Kelvin) calculated, from
  the time required for an originally molten Earth to cool, an age he narrowed to
  ~20–40 million years — too short for the slow processes geologists and
  biologists (Lyell, Darwin) believed they observed.
- **The radioactive revolution.** Henri Becquerel's 1896 discovery of
  radioactivity overturned Kelvin: radioactive decay supplied an unaccounted
  internal heat source *and* a new clock. The constant, measurable decay of
  unstable isotopes (uranium-238 → lead, plus potassium-40, rubidium-87, and
  carbon-14 for younger materials) let the ratio of parent to daughter give an
  age. Arthur Holmes built the first radiometric geological time scale (*The Age
  of the Earth*, 14 editions 1913–1949).

## The Modern Figure

Earth's oldest known rocks (Acasta Gneiss, NW Canada) are ~4.03 billion years
old, and detrital zircons from Western Australia reach ~4.3 billion years — both
minimum ages, since older crust was recycled. Because meteorites and the Moon
formed with the Earth but escaped that recycling, their ages (~4.54 billion
years) proxy for the planet's, giving the accepted **~4.55 billion years**. The
oldest indirect chemical traces of life appear by ~3.85 billion years ago.

## Significance and Limits

Deep time is **etic** — a framework historians and scientists impose, alien to
the cyclical or short-linear time of premodern cosmologies (Hindu/Buddhist
cycles; the Abrahamic created order). Its acceptance was as much a cultural as a
scientific shift. For prehistoric periods, absolute dates always carry
confidence intervals — often ±100,000 years or more for early hominins (see
[[periods/deep-prehistory|Deep Prehistory]]) — and the youngest reaches of the
scale are refined by the [[concepts/radiocarbon-revolution|radiocarbon
revolution]]. Deep time is the temporal foundation of this wiki's framework,
which begins (Period 1) with all of Earth history before 3.3 million BP.
