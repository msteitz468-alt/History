# Bironovshchina

## Definition
**Bironovshchina** (Bironism) refers to the period of political terror, police persecution, and dominant influence of foreign (specifically Baltic German) favorites during the reign of Empress Anne of Courland (1730–1740) in the Russian Empire (L3196-3198). The term is derived from the name of Ernst-Johann Biren (or Biron), the Duke of Courland and Anne's lover, who became the most hated figure of the era (L3196-3198).

## Context and Origin
Upon ascending the throne in 1730, Empress Anne tore up the restrictive conditions imposed on her by the Supreme Secret Council and restored autocracy (L3136-3138). Distrustful of the Russian nobility, she brought a band of favorites with her from Courland, placing them in key court and government offices (L3196). Biren became the de facto manager of court affairs, accumulating wealth and titles, while other foreigners like Ostermann managed foreign affairs and Munnich ran the military (L3196).

*Bironovshchina* became synonymous with the work of the security police and the reign of terror, which resulted in the execution of several thousand people and the exile of 20,000 to 30,000 others to Siberia (L3198).

## Limitations and Critiques
While nineteenth-century Russian national historiography heavily castigated the 1730s as a dark age of foreign German domination, modern revisionist historians offer several qualifications:
* **Continuity of Russian Development:** The policies of the 1730s in foreign affairs and social legislation were an integral part of the broader Russian evolution in the 18th century, rather than an alien Prussian/German deviation (L3196).
* **Composition of Victims:** A significant portion of those executed or exiled under Biren's terror were common criminals and Old Believers rather than political dissidents (L3198). 
* **Comparison of Terror:** The level of brutality, while severe, was not out of line with the standards of the 18th century, though it was remembered with greater bitterness than the subsequent gentle rule of Empress Elizabeth (L3198).
