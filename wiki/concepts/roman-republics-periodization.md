# Roman Republics Periodization (Flower)

A historiographical **periodization** of Roman republican politics proposed by Harriet I. Flower in *Roman Republics* (2010): instead of one continuous “Roman Republic” (~509–49/27 BCE) ending in a multi-decade Late Republican “crisis,” Flower maps **six successive republics** and several transitional or non-republican phases, defined by **internal political culture** rather than by external wars or dynastic careers.

This page records **Flower’s scheme as a position** — an analytical tool offered as one alternative among others — not as the wiki’s default chronology. Conventional Early / Middle / Late labels and the long-crisis narrative remain widely used (see [[fall-of-roman-republic]], [[roman-republic]], [[periodization-world-history]]).

## Core Claim

Periodization is constitutive of historical writing, not optional packaging. A single ~450-year “Republic” with a quasi-biological beginning–middle–end invites teleology and flattens discontinuous systems of rules, offices, and elite practice into one organism. Numbering successive republics (as modern French history does) forces attention to **which** political culture is being described, when it started, and how it ended.

Romans themselves did not label successive regimes this way: *res publica* is not a technical constitution-name; Latin lacks a definite article that would force “a republic” vs “the Republic”; the ordinary dating system was consular years, not republican eras. Flower’s map is therefore **etic** (historian-constructed from hindsight), consciously so.

## The Thirteen Periods (Six Republics)

| # | Dates (BCE) | Label | Character (Flower) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ca. 509–494 | Pre-republican transition | Immediate aftermath of monarchy |
| 2 | 494–451/0 | Proto-republic | Before the first written law code |
| 3 | 450–367/6 | **Republic 1** | Experiment, including consular tribunes |
| 4 | 366–300 | **Republic 2** | Patrician–plebeian shared republic emerging |
| 5 | 300–180 | **Republic 3** | Republic of the *nobiles* 1 |
| 6 | 180–139 | **Republic 4** | Republic of the *nobiles* 2 (*lex Villia Annalis* break) |
| 7 | 139–88 | **Republic 5** | Republic of the *nobiles* 3 (secret ballot from 139) |
| 8 | 88–81 | Transition | Sulla’s coup, *dominatio* of Cinna, Sullan dictatorship |
| 9 | 81–60 | **Republic 6** | Sulla’s New Republic (modified substantially in 70) |
| 10 | 59–53 | “Triumvirate” / Big Three | Pompey, Caesar, Crassus (not formal *tresviri*) |
| 11 | 52–49 | Transition | Breakdown after Crassus’ death; Pompey sole consul 52 |
| 12 | 49–44 | Caesar’s dictatorship | Plus short transition after the murder |
| 13 | 43–33 | Second Triumvirate | Octavian, Lepidus, Antony → Actium 31 → Principate 27 |

Breaks at **180** (career rules under the *lex Villia Annalis*, amid other post-Bacchanalia institutional evolution) and **139** (secret ballot beginning with the *lex Gabinia*) subdivide the classic *nobiles* era; the **~88** collapse ends the last traditional republic of the *nobiles*; **81** founds a new system; **~60** ends functional republican government under the modified Sullan frame.

## What This Periodization Is For

- Distinguishing **early experimental republics** from the mature **culture of the *nobiles*** (compromise, short office, electoral competition that stabilizes rather than subverts).
- Treating the age of the **Gracchi and subsequent violence** as the opening of a destructive new era, not as the middle of an eighty-year “crisis.” Exactly fifty years separate Tiberius Gracchus’s death (133) from Sulla’s return to Italy after the Peace of Dardanus — Flower’s preferred frame for violence and collapse.
- Reading second-century **peaceful reform as normal** republican practice (*lex Villia annalis* 180; permanent *quaestio* 149; secret ballot phased from 139; political year shifted to 1 January from 153) rather than as pre-decline stasis.
- Reading **Sulla** as founder of a different republic, not restorer of the old one (see [[sullan-constitution]]).
- Placing **78–49** *after* the shipwreck of traditional republican politics rather than inside a single continuous Late Republic. If the last traditional republic ends in 88, Gruen’s “last generation” is displaced to ~120–88 (after Gaius Gracchus to the Social War), not the 70s–50s.
- Dating the **final loss of republican function to ~60**, so that Caesar’s invasion of 49 targets a community that has already lost republican shape; the 50s “no longer belong to a period of republican history.”

## What It Is Not

- Not a claim that Romans experienced six named regimes.
- Not a full social, economic, or military periodization (Flower prioritizes internal politics).
- Not a dismissal of unitary-Republic narratives; Flower offers an **alternative time map** to open questions, not a new orthodoxy.
- Not identical to French-style republican numbering in substance — only analogous as a historiographical habit of counting successive systems.

## Relation to Other Frameworks

| Framework | Relation to Flower |
|---|---|
| Traditional Early / Middle / Late Republic | Coarser; “Middle” roughly covers Republics 3–5; “Late” mixes end of Republic 5, transition 8, Republic 6, and non-republican aftermath |
| Long crisis 133–49 | Explicitly rejected as misuse of “crisis” and as teleological |
| Polybius’ mixed constitution / rise 220–167 | External-war periodization; Flower notes 180 breaks Polybius’ fifty-year rise if one prioritizes internal politics |
| Syme *Roman Revolution* | Shares stress on discontinuity and new men; Flower relocates the decisive political death earlier (nobiles republics end ~88; function gone ~60) |
| CAH / structural-empire strain accounts | Compatible as *causes within periods*; Flower objects to packaging them as one long Republic’s fate |
| Wiki [[periodization-world-history]] | Instance of periodization as contested historical tool applied to one civilization |

## Contested Status

Open. Flower’s scheme is influential as a teaching and research prompt but has not replaced standard textbook periodization. Points of likely pushback: (1) whether 180 and 139 are the best internal breaks; (2) whether Sulla’s system deserves the label “republic”; (3) whether dating the end of function to ~60 underplays continuity of offices, law, and ideology into the 40s; (4) whether external imperial transformation should co-define periods with internal politics. Record disagreements on [[fall-of-roman-republic]] and on the pages for [[sulla]], [[roman-republic]], and [[sullan-constitution]].
