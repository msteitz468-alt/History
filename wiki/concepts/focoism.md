# Focoism (Foquismo)

Revolutionary warfare doctrine associated with Che Guevara (and elaborated by Régis Debray in *Revolution in the Revolution?*) that a small, mobile armed vanguard ("foco") operating in the countryside can create the subjective conditions for revolution and ignite a broader popular uprising, without waiting for the full "objective" maturation of class contradictions required by classical Marxist or Maoist protracted people's war theory.

## Definition and Scope

In foco theory the guerrilla band is not merely a military instrument but the political vanguard that demonstrates the feasibility of armed resistance, radicalizes the peasantry through example and contact, and forces the state into repressive over-reaction that alienates the population. The Cuban Revolution (Sierra Maestra phase) was taken as proof-of-concept: a tiny force grew and prevailed against a larger conventional army.

Focoism inverts the Maoist sequence (political mobilization first, then violence) in favor of violence itself as the catalyst that mobilizes political support.

## Key Texts and Practice

Che's *Guerrilla Warfare* (1960) and articles; his own application in the Congo (1965) and Bolivia (1966–67). Debray's 1967 book systematized the lessons for Latin America. Anderson documents Che's belief that a small determined group could succeed where mass parties had failed, and the practical difficulties that arose when local Communist parties (especially pro-Soviet) refused cooperation.

## Critiques and Outcomes

In practice, most foco attempts outside Cuba failed (Che's own Congo and Bolivia expeditions being the most prominent). Critics argued that the theory underestimated the need for prior political work, misread the uniqueness of the Cuban situation (Batista's weakness, island geography, U.S. distance), and invited disastrous isolation. It was influential among 1960s–70s Latin American and European New Left groups but largely discredited by the 1970s–80s as counterinsurgency improved and many rural focos were destroyed.
