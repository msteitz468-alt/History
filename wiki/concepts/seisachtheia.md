# Seisachtheia

The **seisachtheia** (“shaking off of burdens”) is the conventional name for Solon’s early-sixth-century measures addressing debt, personal security, and agrarian dependence at Athens. [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] Ch.8 treats it within the Solonian crisis and Archaic relations of production.

## Measures (later narrative)

Solon’s first acts (per later narrative, esp. *Ath. Pol.* / Plutarch): forbid loans on the security of the individual; cancel private and public debts; liberate those enslaved at home and repatriate those sold abroad. He pleased neither the wealthy (unable to recoup debts) nor the poor (who hoped for land redistribution). Hall notes Late Classical/Hellenistic topicality of debt-cancellation and redistribution requires caution with *Ath. Pol.*/Plutarch, but Solon’s own poetry remains important contemporary testimony despite genre limits and selection bias of later quoters.

## Hektêmoroi and horoi — competing interpretations

One sixth is a surprisingly low sharecropping rent. Interpretations Hall reviews:

1. *Hektêmoroi* as hired smallholders **receiving** rather than paying one sixth, later a social obligation whose defaulters could be enslaved.
2. More common: impoverished smallholders mortgaging land, sixth as interest, *horoi* as mortgage markers that Solon tore up. **Problem:** no literary/epigraphic evidence for *horoi* as mortgage markers before the 360s.
3. Alternatives: *horoi* as metaphor for ending stasis; or markers of elite appropriation of common land (enclosures), restored by Solon to the community.

## Causal context

Wider Archaic gap between wealthy and dispossessed: elite need for labor on larger holdings; *dmôes*, *thêtes*, sharecroppers, and dependent communities ([[concepts/helotage]] as extreme parallel elsewhere); loans on personal security. Athens’ crisis is the best-documented flashpoint of a broader phenomenon.

## Emic / etic

- **Emic:** *seisachtheia*, *horoi*, *hektêmoroi*, Solon’s self-presentation in elegy.
- **Etic:** modern reconstructions of mortgage vs enclosure vs sharecropping; Hall stresses underdetermination.

## Limitations

Heavy dependence on fourth-century and later prose; Solon’s poetry selected by later quoters; *horoi* terminology anachronism risk; no land redistribution despite poor hopes.
