# Diffusionism (in Archaeology)

**Diffusionism** in prehistoric archaeology is the explanatory model that attributes major innovations and cultural changes primarily to the spread of ideas, technologies, or people from a limited number of centres (most often the Near East) rather than to independent invention or local development.

## Core Assumptions in the Childean Framework

Prior to absolute dating, European prehistory relied on cross-dating with historically anchored sequences in Egypt and Mesopotamia. This chronological necessity became conflated with historical explanation: "The pursuit of such links between Europe and the Near East was essential since there was no method of dating prehistoric finds from Europe except by correlation... The dependence of archaeology on these connections for the establishment of a prehistoric European chronology tended to be confused with the dependence of prehistoric Europe on the East for all its advances." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.1 / range lines ~370-380)

All or most major innovations — farming, stone architecture, bronze and iron metallurgy — "were attributed to diffusion from a single centre of invention in the Near East." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.1 / range lines ~365-370)

Cultures were treated as the material signatures of ethnic groups; trait lists and distributions were read as histories of influence, migration, or conquest.

## Impact of the Radiocarbon Revolution

Radiocarbon dating, and especially its calibration, "revolutionized the pattern of Europe's past." Many traditional links "are no longer tenable," events previously thought contemporary "were in fact separated by many centuries," and "it has greatly lengthened the time-span of European prehistory; the neolithic period in particular can now be seen to have lasted millennia rather than just centuries." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.1 / range lines ~390-405)

The result was the collapse of the diffusionist scaffolding and the necessity "for rethinking the traditional prehistory of Europe." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch.1 / range lines ~395-400)

## Legacy and Current Status

Diffusionism as a master explanation is rejected. Regional sequences, absolute chronologies, and evidence for independent invention (e.g., megalithic architecture, early metallurgy in the Balkans) have replaced it. However, documented cases of long-distance exchange, migration, and technological transfer remain important; the distinction is now between specific, evidence-based instances and a priori assumptions of Eastern primacy.

The term "diffusion" is retained only for precisely demonstrated movements of traits or people; it is no longer the default explanation for change.
