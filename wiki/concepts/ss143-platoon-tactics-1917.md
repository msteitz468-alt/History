# SS 143 – Platoon Tactics (BEF, February 1917)

The key British infantry training pamphlet of February 1917 that codified the platoon as the basic tactical unit capable of fighting a miniature all-arms battle.

## Content and Significance (Griffith)
- Each section within the platoon had a distinct role (riflemen, Lewis gunners, rifle bombers/grenadiers).
- Emphasis on decentralised execution under a protective creeping barrage.
- Explicit instructions for the platoon to combine fire and movement, deal with strongpoints, and consolidate.
- Griffith describes SS 143 as “in essence a stormtrooper’s handbook” but applied to the whole BEF rather than to a small élite.
- Techniques had been developed and refined on the Somme; the manual made specialist skills available to the mass of the infantry by winter 1916–17.

## Place in Warfare Hub
Primary reference for British small-unit tactics in 1917–18 analyses. Demonstrates the practical realisation of combined-arms at the lowest level, the shift from “cult of the bomb” toward integrated rifle + automatic + mortar + artillery support, and the institutional effort to spread lessons beyond individual formations.

Directly relevant to 9 principles of war (especially Unity of Command at platoon level, Maneuver, Surprise, Economy of Force) and to comparisons with German 1918 infiltration tactics.
