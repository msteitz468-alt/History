# The Tale of Genji (Genji Monogatari)

The masterpiece of [[murasaki-shikibu|Murasaki Shikibu]] (c. 1001–1020) — a long prose
romance of the [[heian-court-culture|Heian court]] widely regarded as the **world's first
psychological novel** and the central work of Japanese literature. Read here in Arthur
Waley's [[waley-tale-of-genji|translation]].

## The work

In **54 chapters**, the *Genji* follows **Prince Genji** — "the Shining One," the brilliant,
beautiful son of an emperor by a low-ranking, doomed concubine (Kiritsubo) — through a life of
love affairs, political fortune, exile and return, and old age, and then continues after his
death with the next generation (the "Uji chapters"). Its true subject is not event but
**feeling**: the inner lives, relationships, and griefs of a closed aristocratic world. Waley
renders it in six parts (*The Tale of Genji*, *The Sacred Tree*, *A Wreath of Cloud*, *Blue
Trousers*, *The Lady of the Boat*, *The Bridge of Dreams*).

## Themes

- **Psychological realism.** Murasaki "deals with society as it was and people as they are";
  her characters have an interior depth and capacity for development that, Waley argued,
  **anticipates the modern European novel** by some eight centuries.
- ***Mono no aware*** — "the pathos of things." The pervading mood is the
  [[heian-court-culture|Heian]] sensibility to the **transience and beauty** of life, love,
  and the natural world, deepened by a Buddhist sense of impermanence (see [[japanese-buddhism]]).
- **Love and women.** Genji's relationships — as lover, husband, father, protector — span the
  whole range of Heian society; the women (above all **Murasaki**, his ward and second wife,
  for whom the author is nicknamed) are realized with rare sympathy and individuality.
- **A documentary of the court.** The novel is also the richest portrait of Heian aristocratic
  life — its etiquette, poetry-exchange, religion, politics, and aesthetics.

## Form and language

Written in the new phonetic **kana** script (which freed Japanese-language prose from
Chinese), it is a triumph of the vernacular **women's literature** of the age. Murasaki even
voices, through Genji, a defense of the **art of fiction** as a serious vehicle of human
truth — a remarkable early statement of novelistic theory.

## Significance and afterlife

The *Genji* became the **inexhaustible touchstone of Japanese culture**: the model of taste
and *mono no aware*; a perpetual source for later poetry, **Noh** drama, and **yamato-e**
painting (the celebrated 12th-century **Genji scroll**); and the subject of a millennium of
commentary. It is Japan's foremost contribution to world literature and the crowning monument
of [[heian-court-culture|Heian court culture]].
