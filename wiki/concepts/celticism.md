# Celticism

**Summary**: The modern construction and reception of 'Celtic' identity, scholarship, and cultural movements from the Romantic period onward, including 19th–20th c. philology, revivalism, and the post-1990s scholarly reassessment (Celtoscepticism and its aftermath).

## Definition and Scope

Celticism refers to the scholarly, literary, and popular investment in 'the Celts' as a category, distinct from the historical peoples who spoke Celtic languages. It encompasses the Romantic discovery and idealization of Celtic languages and literatures, the linkage of archaeological cultures (Hallstatt, La Tène) with linguistic identity in older models, and the 20th–21st c. debates over the validity and scope of the term 'Celtic' for non-linguistic phenomena.

## Key Debates

- Relationship between linguistic Celticity and material culture.
- The 'invisibility' of Celtic languages in modern nation-states and the politics of name forms.
- Role of Celtic studies in modern national identities (Irish Republic, devolution, Brittany, etc.).

## Historiography

See Koch (ed.), *Celtic Culture: A Historical Encyclopedia* (2006), Vol I Introduction and 'Celticism' entry: explicit reframing after Celtoscepticism — linguistic facts remain secure; non-linguistic extensions require circumspection. The Encyclopedia itself is a product of the Celticity Project's response to the fragmentation of the field.
