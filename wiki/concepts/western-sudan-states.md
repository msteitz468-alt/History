# Western Sudan States

A historiographical grouping for the succession of major Sahelian empires
that rose and fell across the "Western Sudan" belt of West Africa — the
[[ghana-empire|Ghana Empire]], [[mali-empire|Mali Empire]], and
[[songhai-empire|Songhai Empire]] — built on control of trans-Saharan gold
and salt trade routes and, from roughly the eleventh century onward,
increasingly integrated into the wider Islamic world.

## Definition and Scope

The term distinguishes this western Sahelian sequence of large, trade-based
imperial states from the "Central Sudan" states further east (such as
Kanem-Bornu and the Hausa city-states), which followed a related but
distinct trajectory. Each Western Sudan state in turn succeeded and drew on
the infrastructure of its predecessor, controlling shifting but overlapping
territory across the upper Niger and Senegal river regions.

## Origin (Etic)

"Western Sudan" is a modern etic geographic-historiographical category
(derived from the Arabic *bilad al-sudan*, "land of the Blacks," as used by
medieval Arab geographers) rather than a term the states themselves used to
describe a shared political identity; each polity was a distinct state with
its own internal political structure.

## Applications and Examples

The [[fulani|Fulani]] and other Sahelian peoples were progressively drawn
into these states' religious and commercial networks; the states'
integration into trans-Saharan Islamic trade and scholarship (exemplified
by [[mansa-musa|Mansa Musa's]] famous 1324 pilgrimage) marked their
increasing connection to the wider Islamic and Mediterranean world across
the Late Middle Ages.

## Limitations and Critiques

The collection's coverage of these states individually (Ghana, Mali,
Songhai) is stronger than its coverage of the "Western Sudan" as a
connected regional system; comparatively little source material addresses
the transitions between successive states or their shared regional
institutions as a continuous subject, reflecting the broader thinness of
Sub-Saharan African coverage in this collection.
