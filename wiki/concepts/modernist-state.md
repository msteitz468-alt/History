# Modernist State

Etic analytical concept from [[griffin-modernism-and-fascism-2007|Griffin, *Modernism and Fascism* (2007)]]: a regime that attempts to realize a "temporalized utopia" — an alternative modernity purged of decadence — through the cultural, social, institutional, and technocratic power revealed by the modern state in the First World War. Griffin's set: **Fascist Italy, the Third Reich, and Bolshevik Russia** (explicitly *not* liberal Britain or Franco's Spain — cf. [[para-fascism]]). The concept is intertwined with Zygmunt Bauman's **"gardening state"** waging "a war against ambivalence, identified with chaos and lack of control": Hitler and Stalin as "legitimate offspring of the modern spirit," their camps the "ultimate logic of a hyper-modernity."

## Fascist Italy as Modernist State

Griffin's ch. 8 verdict: "the Fascist regime is to be seen as a modernist state, on a par with the modernist state being created in Bolshevik Russia at the time, and with the one to be constructed later in the Third Reich." Its apparatus:

- **The sacralization of politics** (Emilio Gentile, cited as the scholarly authority): totalitarianism as "the integral politicisation of existence... institutionalised in the form of a political religion" aiming "to shape the individual and the masses through an anthropological revolution... and create the new man."
- **The challenge to time**: the Fascist Era calendar (1922 = Year I, on the French Revolutionary model); Mussolini's boast that Romans could "challenge Time," read via Benjamin's "tiger's leap."
- **Romanità as futural myth**, not nostalgia (Bottai: "not a restoration but a renovation, a revolution in the idea of Rome"); the 1937–38 Augustan bimillennium identifying Mussolini with Augustus.
- ***Bonifica* as palingenesis** (Ruth Ben-Ghiat): agricultural, human, and cultural "reclamation" — Pontine Marshes drainage, new towns, the demographic campaign (Ascension Day Speech, 26 May 1927), Corrado Gini's "Scientific Basis of Fascism" — society approached "as an organism to be manipulated by means of a vast surgical operation," feeding the home-grown racism behind the 1938 anti-Semitic laws.
- **Hegemonic pluralism in culture** (Marla Stone): no prescribed style; Terragni's rationalist Casa del Fascio beside Piacentini's stile Littorio; Etlin's "voracious amoeba" ("it swallowed everything around it in order to proclaim that it was all a Fascist achievement"); the 1932 Mostra della Rivoluzione Fascista (≈4 million visitors) as engineered total experience.
- **Corporativism** as social-modernist third way, whose real purpose (Panunzio, 1933) was "the myth of the nation for whom millions and millions of men sacrifice themselves."

Griffin's verdict on the reactionary/modernizing debate: "the Fascist regime was neither reactionary nor anti-modern, but modernist, its fundamental failure due to the impossibility of translating fictions into myths, palingenetic discourse into historical reality." Bottai's 1942 confession of the failure of "every official religion of the State" states the ultimate aporia: the modernist state must engineer a collective myth precisely under the modern conditions that dissolve such myths.

## The Third Reich as Modernist State

Ch. 11's portrait: "a totalitarian 'gardening state' [created] by a modern [[revitalization-movement]] to implement a wide-ranging modernist programme... in order to purge Germany of decadence and create a healthier society." Nazi modernity was "not a two-headed Janus, but a three-headed Chimaera": mythicized past + intense modernization + **the systematic purge** of everything deemed dysgenic. Components documented: Zuse's V4 computer and IBM/Dehomag punch-card logistics; the "rooted modernity" of consumer/leisure culture (television at the 1936 Olympics, the Volkswagen, Nazi chic); biopolitics in Griffin's extended sense (a revolution "conceived biologically", the Volkskörper discourse); Generalplan Ost and the Pripet Marshes schemes ("there was virtually no limit to their visions of the modern east"); the slave-labour economy (7.6 million by 1945; Krupp, IG Farben, Daimler Benz, BMW, Opel, AEG); DAF/KdF Taylorist welfare for "Aryans"; euthanasia advocates seeing themselves as "the vanguard of the nation's modernization." The genocide is integral, not incidental: "The resulting crimes against humanity were thus not incidental, but integral to Nazism's version of modernity" — genocide as "a disinfection, a purification — in short an 'ecological' measure" (Traverso). Full documentation at [[the-holocaust]] and [[nazi-germany]].

## Structural Failure

Both regimes, "by envisaging a future based on perpetual charismatic rule, continuous dynamism, ceaseless territorial expansion, and permanent revolution... gave themselves no hypothetical way out of the second, liminal phase" — "no exit strategy, no way of turning off the perpetuum mobile... moribund in their vitalism." The ultimate failure is the "anthropological revolution": the New Man was "intrinsically unrealizable" (Evans: the totalitarian impulse "was forced to compromise with the intractability of human nature"). Consensus years: c. 1929–36 in Italy, 1933–41 in Germany.

## Critiques

The concept presupposes Griffin's maximalist [[modernism]] and inherits its contested primordialist grounding. Rival framings it displaces or absorbs: Benjamin's "aestheticization of politics" (Griffin: a half-truth that misses the sacralization), Herf's "reactionary modernism" (re-read via Osborne), Zitelmann's "Hitler as modernizer" (criticized by Frei and Kershaw for marginalizing the racial purge; Fritzsche's "modernist, not modernizer" is Griffin's preferred correction), and Golomstock's "law of totalitarianism." Emilio Gentile warns against letting the analysis "aestheticize" Fascism itself and lose its "fundamentally political nature."
