# Synoikism (Synoecism)

**Synoikism** (synoecism) denotes processes of political and/or physical unification of communities into a single *polis*. [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] distinguishes senses carefully and subjects the Attic Theseus tradition to source criticism (Chs.4, 9 / Exc. III material).

## Senses of the term (Hall)

1. **Political unification** — Theseus’s mythical Attic unification under one *bouleutērion* and *prytaneion* (Thuc. 2.15.2); rare in written sources as a documented historical process.
2. **Creation of a new *polis* by merging/absorbing neighbors** — e.g. Rhodes 408/7 from Camirus, Ialysus, Lindus.
3. **Physical coalescence** — for Sparta, Thucydides’s model of nearby villages expanding until continuous habitation; compared by some archaeologists to Aristotle’s coalescence of villages.

“The anglicized term ‘synoecism’ is applied to a variety of processes.”

## The Attic case

By the fifth century Athens controlled ~2,500 km² with rural residents holding the same civic rights and duties as city residents — anomalous versus Sparta’s larger ~7,500 km² via perioikoi and helots. Attica’s four microzones: *pedion*, *paralia*, *mesogaia*, Thriasian Plain (Eleusis). Athenians explained this via Theseus’s aition: disbanding local jurisdictions and making Athens political/judicial center, commemorated by the **Synoikia** festival still celebrated in Thucydides’ day.

### Rejected early unifications

- **Late Bronze Age Theseus-unification** highly unlikely (tholos tombs outside Athens do not prove autonomy; any early unity would not have survived Dark Age depopulation of Attic countryside).
- **Dark Age “internal colonization”** from Athens carrying urban loyalty, completed by the eighth century because Catalogue of Ships names only Athens for Attica — weak: political machinery still lay in the future; ceramic homogeneity ≠ political unity; Catalogue Attic and Salamis lines show clear interference.

### Late synoikism thesis (Cleisthenes)

Recent argument: full political synoecism only with Cleisthenes, as a primary reform motivation. Before the last decade of the sixth century Athens exerted only weak influence beyond the *pedion*. Indications: Athens not dominant in Archaic interstate affairs; long struggle for Salamis; ease of Cylon, Pisistratus, and Cleomenes/Isagoras seizing the acropolis suggests no standing army until late sixth century; “mixing” demes unifies outlying areas with the city. Supporting details: Tettikhos gravestone ca. 560 contrasts *astos* and *xenos*; funerary kouroi/korai clustered in southern peninsula; Alcmaeonid property base at Anaphlystos. Counter: late synoecism ought to leave clearer literary traces — yet further indications (naukrariai) still point late.

## Emic / etic

- **Emic:** *synoikismos*, Synoikia festival, Theseus charter myth.
- **Etic:** typology of political vs physical merger; dating of Attic unity as research problem.

## Limitations

Myth and festival do not date institutional completion; archaeology of Attica is uneven; Cleisthenic “full synoikism” remains interpretive.
