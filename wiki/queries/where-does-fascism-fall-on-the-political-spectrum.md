# Where Does Fascism Fall on the Political Spectrum?

*Filed from a curator–assistant discussion on 2026-07-01, following the ingest of
[[dsouza-big-lie-2017]]. Positions attributed throughout: "curator" = the
collection's owner; "analysis" = the assistant's assessment. The neutral record
of the scholarly dispute is at [[fascism-and-the-political-left]]; this page is
the adjudicated discussion the controversy page deliberately avoids.*

## The framing problem: two axes, one vocabulary

The question is underdetermined until the axis is specified, and much of the
public dispute consists of the two sides using different axes with the same
words.

1. **State vs. individual** (the Hayek axis, weighted by the curator). Here
   fascism, Nazism, and communism cluster at one pole; classical liberalism
   sits alone at the other. Gentile's "all is in the state" is the common
   denominator of the totalitarianisms and the precise negation of Lockean
   individualism. On this axis the "fascism is left-wing collectivism" reading
   follows, and the label "fascist" applied to American conservatism — which
   descends from a *liberal* revolution and has no throne-and-altar tradition
   to react toward (Hartz) — is a category error.

2. **Egalitarianism vs. hierarchy** (the axis of academic fascism studies).
   Here fascism and socialism split: socialism's professed claim is universal
   equality; mature fascist doctrine explicitly repudiated egalitarianism —
   hierarchy of nations and races, the leader principle, struggle as the
   proper condition. On this axis fascism is right-wing in the oldest sense:
   the party of hierarchy against the party of equality, revolutionary rather
   than conservative.

**Analysis:** fascism is best described as a *revolutionary, anti-egalitarian
collectivism* — right-wing on the hierarchy axis, totalitarian on the state
axis, genuinely descended from a mutation within the revolutionary left. It
shares its methods and statism with communism, its values with the old
European right, and nothing with Anglo-American classical liberalism.

**Curator's position:** the state–individual axis is the meaningful one;
socialism's egalitarianism is pretense, so the species distinction collapses
and fascism/socialism are "the same exact thing."

## The pretense argument and its limits

The curator's outcomes case is strong and has a serious pedigree: Djilas (*The
New Class*), Orwell, and the uniform empirical record — every socialist state
produced nomenklatura hierarchy, never equality. Classified by what they
*build*, the totalitarianisms converge into one genus (Arendt).

The analysis concedes the genus but retains the species, because the professed
ideal — sincere or not — did observable work: (a) **victim selection** —
communist terror was class-defined and in principle escapable by conversion;
Nazi terror was blood-defined and was not; (b) **recruitment reach** —
universalist doctrine built an International; fascism was in principle
non-exportable; (c) **defection pattern** — the god-that-failed literature
(Koestler, Silone, Orwell) exists because communism attracted genuine
egalitarians who discovered the pretense; fascism has no equivalent because it
never made the promise.

**Convergence reached in discussion:** international socialists vs. national
socialists — one collectivism with a universal claim, one with a tribal claim,
both crushing the individual — with the caveat that the national version also
rewired the internals (race replacing class as the unit; *Volksgemeinschaft*
redefining "equality" as solidarity strictly inside the racial community),
and that Stalin's "socialism in one country" shows the international/national
line was unstable in practice.

## The causal chain: 1914 as falsification event

The historically supported core of the "fascism from the left" genealogy
(Sternhell's documented thesis, distinct from the polemical uses of it):

- **August 1914**: the Second International's prediction fails — the German
  SPD votes war credits; every proletariat marches behind its own flag. Class
  solidarity dissolves on contact with national feeling.
- **The fork**: Lenin answers that the workers were betrayed and need a
  vanguard (Bolshevism). The Italian revolutionary syndicalists — Sorel's
  heirs, Corradini ("proletarian nation"), Michels — answer that the workers
  revealed the true mobilizing collective: build the revolution on the
  *nation*. Mussolini's 1914–15 interventionism is this discovery in action.
- **Gentile's actual role**: he did **not** add the national element (a common
  compression, incl. in [[dsouza-big-lie-2017]]). A liberal-idealist
  philosopher, he joined fascism only in 1923 and supplied the *post-hoc
  philosophy of the totalitarian state* (actual idealism; credited with
  *totalitario* in its positive sense). The syndicalists found the move,
  Mussolini played it, Gentile wrote the rulebook.
- **The fork's price**: the moment the socialists made the national turn, the
  anti-egalitarian content entered with the nationalism — which is why
  genealogy (out of the left) and mature identity (anti-egalitarian right, on
  axis 2) can both be true.

## Points of agreement

Whatever the axis: totalitarianism of every variety is the enemy of the
Lockean tradition, not its cousin; behavior at the moment of power (coalition
with conservative elites, destruction of the parties of the left, camps first
filled with socialists and communists) is stronger evidence than platform
planks; and "fascist" as an all-purpose epithet for the American right is
intellectually empty.
