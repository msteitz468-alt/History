# US Political Polarization (1970s–present)

Long-duration process by which the United States shifted from relatively high mid-century cross-aisle consensus and mixed ideological parties to highly sorted, affectively polarized parties with little overlap and high mutual animosity. 

## Definition and Scope
Not merely policy disagreement but hardening of partisan identities, geographic sorting, media choice reinforcement, and erosion of shared factual/epistemic ground. Distinct from but intertwined with racial, economic, and cultural "fault lines."

## Causal Drivers
- Mid-20th century centripetal forces (strong federal government, broad middle-class prosperity, unions) eroded after 1960s upheavals.
- 1970s institutional rebuild privileged division and competition over consensus (Kruse/Zelizer core thesis).
- Media transformation from three-network "consensus" to fragmented partisan outlets (cable, talk radio, internet platforms) that confirmed biases.
- End of Cold War removed last major external source of national consensus.
- Economic restructuring (deindustrialization, inequality, gig/service shift) and racial/cultural backlash politics amplified sorting.

## Major Phases
- 1970s crises (Watergate legitimacy collapse, stagflation/energy shocks, post-civil-rights identity/equality battles) as seedbed.
- 1980s Reagan era: conservative institutionalization, fairness doctrine end, cable rise, culture-war mobilization.
- 1990s–2000s: Gingrich tactics, Clinton impeachment amplification, 9/11/War on Terror, Fox rise.
- 2010s–2020s: Tea Party/ACA fights, Trump as product + accelerator (rally rhetoric, "alternative facts," Jan 6), social media silos (X/Musk shift), Dobbs, 2024 realignment.
