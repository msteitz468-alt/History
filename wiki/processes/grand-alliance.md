# The Grand Alliance

The wartime coalition of the United States, the United Kingdom, and the Soviet Union (with China as a nominal fourth major power) against Nazi Germany, Fascist Italy, and Imperial Japan, a term popularized by [[actors/churchill-winston|Winston Churchill]] for the improbable alignment of liberal-democratic and communist powers forced together by Germany's June 1941 invasion of the USSR and Japan's December 1941 attack on the United States.

## Definition and Scope

The Grand Alliance was not a formal treaty organization but a working coalition coordinated through summit diplomacy — chiefly the conferences at Tehran (1943), [[events/yalta-conference-1945|Yalta]] (February 1945), and Potsdam (July–August 1945) — among [[actors/roosevelt-franklin-d|Franklin D. Roosevelt]], Churchill, and [[actors/stalin-joseph|Joseph Stalin]]. It combined American industrial and financial power (including Lend-Lease aid to Britain and the USSR), British global reach and early resistance, and Soviet manpower bearing the overwhelming weight of ground combat against Germany on the Eastern Front.

## Causal Drivers

The alliance was driven purely by military necessity and the absence of any alternative: none of the three powers could defeat Germany and Japan alone, and ideological hostility between the Western democracies and the Soviet Union (evident before 1941 and resumed almost immediately after 1945) was deliberately subordinated to the common war effort.

## Major Phases

Early phase (1941–1943) centered on establishing supply lines (Lend-Lease) and coordinating strategy amid Soviet pressure for a "second front" in Western Europe; a middle phase (1943–1944) saw major summit coordination (Tehran) and the launch of the Normandy invasion; a final phase (1944–1945) saw the alliance's cohesion strain as military victory approached and postwar territorial and political questions — particularly the fate of Eastern Europe — moved to the center of disputes, most visibly at Yalta.

## Geographic Spread

Global in scope, spanning the European, North African, Pacific, and China-Burma-India theaters, with the alliance's political center of gravity in the Anglo-American-Soviet summit relationship.

## Interaction

The Grand Alliance's cooperative machinery — and the mutual distrust generated by disputes over Poland, occupation zones, and postwar settlement visible already at Yalta — fed directly into the onset of the [[periods/cold-war|Cold War]] once the shared enemy was eliminated; many Cold War institutions and disputes (the division of Germany, the United Nations Security Council's structure) trace directly to arrangements made within the Grand Alliance framework.

## End Conditions

The alliance ended with the defeat of Germany (May 1945) and Japan (August–September 1945); with the common enemy gone, the underlying ideological and geopolitical tensions between the Western powers and the Soviet Union resurfaced rapidly, and by 1947–1948 the wartime partners had become Cold War adversaries.
