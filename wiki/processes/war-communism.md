# War Communism
## Definition and Scope
**War Communism** was the extreme, centralized economic and political system adopted by the Bolshevik government in Soviet Russia from the summer of 1918 to the spring of 1921. It represented a desperate, militant attempt to mobilize all domestic resources to win the Russian Civil War and survive foreign intervention. Characterized by the near-total abolition of private trade, the nationalization of all industries, compulsory labor, and the forcible requisitioning of grain from the peasantry, the policy was also viewed by many Bolsheviks as a direct leap toward a fully socialized economy.

## Causal Drivers
- **Military Survival:** The primary driver was the need to feed the Red Army and the urban population during a major, many-faceted Civil War where the Whites, peasant bands, and foreign forces threatened Bolshevik control.
- **Ideological Radicalism:** Many Bolsheviks, including Lenin, believed that the state could immediately replace the market with direct state distribution, rationing, and nationalized control.
- **Economic Disintegration:** The collapse of the ruble and the breakdown of transport and trade meant that traditional commercial methods were no longer functional.

## Major Phases
- **Emergence (Summer 1918):** Prompted by the loss of agricultural and industrial regions to the Whites and Germans. The law of June 28, 1918, nationalized major industries (mining, metallurgy, textiles, utilities, etc.).
- **Militant Consolidation (1919–1920):** Private trade was suppressed and replaced by government rationing. On February 19, 1918, land was nationalized, and a **food levy** (prodrazvyorstka) was decreed, ordering peasants to surrender all surplus produce to the state.
- **Crisis and Collapse (Winter 1920–1921):** The end of the Civil War exposed the complete breakdown of the economy. Cultivated land shrank, industrial output fell to 20% of pre-war levels, and catastrophic famine ensued (Line 5827).

## Geographic Spread
The process was concentrated in the Bolshevik-controlled core of European Russia (including Moscow and Petrograd) and was gradually extended to Ukraine, Siberia, and Transcaucasia as the Red Army reclaimed these borderlands from White armies and foreign forces.

## Interaction
- **With the Peasantry:** Forcible grain requisitions met with intense peasant resistance, leading to armed rebellions in the countryside (known as "green resistance") and a refusal to till the land.
- **With Politics:** War Communism was accompanied by the "Red Terror," executed by the Cheka, to hunt down speculators, strikers, and political opponents.

## End Conditions
War Communism ended in March 1921. The combination of rural uprisings, factory strikes, a massive famine, and the **Kronstadt naval rebellion** (March 1921) forced Lenin to admit the failure of the policy. At the Tenth Party Congress, he replaced War Communism with the **[[new-economic-policy|New Economic Policy (NEP)]]**, which reintroduced limited private enterprise and replaced grain requisitions with a tax in kind.
