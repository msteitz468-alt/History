# The Australian Settlement

The regulatory and protective policy regime constructed by the early
[[commonwealth-of-australia|Commonwealth]] to underwrite a prosperous, egalitarian
white settler democracy — and the subject of one of the central debates in
modern Australian historiography.

## Definition and Scope

As named by the journalist Paul Kelly (*The End of Certainty*, 1992), the
Settlement comprised five interlocking pillars:

1. **Tariff protection** of domestic manufacturing.
2. **Wage arbitration** — the Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration
   and the "fair and reasonable" basic wage (Harvester judgment, 1907).
3. **[[white-australia-policy|White Australia]]** — racially restricted immigration.
4. **State paternalism** — old-age pensions, maternity benefit, an interventionist state.
5. **Imperial benevolence** — dependence on Britain for markets, capital and defence.

It is **not** merely a list of policies but a coherent settlement of class, race
and national-security questions that, Kelly argued, lasted largely unchallenged
into the 1970s and condemned Australia to "insular mediocrity."

## Causal Drivers

Born of the 1890s [[australian-1890s-depression|Depression]], drought and violent
labour disputes; the founding compromise between protectionist liberals and the
rising [[australian-labour-movement]].

## Major Phases

- **Construction (1901–1914):** tariff, arbitration, pensions, White Australia.
- **Consolidation:** survived two world wars and the 1930s Depression intact.
- **Strain (1960s–70s):** loss of British markets, end of the long boom,
  stagflation.
- **Dismantling (1983–2000):** Hawke–Keating floating of the dollar, tariff cuts,
  financial deregulation and the turn to [[neoliberal-reform-australia|neoliberal reform]].

## Geographic Spread

National policy framework; its imperial pillar tied Australia to Britain and the
[[british-commonwealth]].

## Interaction

Reinforced by the [[white-australia-policy]]; eroded by globalisation and the
collapse of protection; its end opened the way to neoliberal restructuring and
[[multiculturalism-australia]].

## End Conditions

Contested. Several Vol. 2 contributors reject the "lasting settlement" thesis,
reading the arrangements instead as repeatedly renegotiated nation building.
See the controversy at [[australian-settlement-debate]].
