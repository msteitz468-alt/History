# Forced and Slave Labor in Nazi Germany (1939–1945)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: the system by which the Reich conscripted foreign civilians, POWs, and concentration-camp inmates to replace the men bled away by the eastern war (~60,000 German dead per month, June 1941–May 1944) — a choice driven equally by Hitler's refusal to mobilize German women fully (see [[volksgemeinschaft]]).

## Major Phases

1. **1939–41:** Polish POWs and civilians (700,000, mostly agriculture; only ~15% ever voluntary) and 1.2 million Western POWs; ~300,000 Western European civilian "volunteers."
2. **From October 1941:** Soviet POWs ordered into war work — but of 3.35 million captured, only 5% were usable by March 1942; the rest were dead or dying (see [[operation-barbarossa-1941]]).
3. **From 21 March 1942:** [[sauckel-fritz|Sauckel's]] plenipotentiary office ran mass coercive recruitment — armed commissions, hostage-taking, village burnings, and "Operation Hay," the planned seizure of up to 50,000 children aged 10–14. Nearly **8 million foreign workers** were in the Reich by autumn 1944 — over a quarter of the workforce (46% of agricultural labor, a third of mining).
4. **1942–44:** concentration-camp labor industrialized after the camps passed to Pohl's WVHA (16 March 1942); ~715,000 inmates by January 1945; in October 1944 allocation passed from the SS to Speer's ministry.

## The Racial Hierarchy in Practice

Western workers were nominally paid German rates; Poles lived under the discriminatory decrees of 8 March 1940 (a compulsory badge Evans calls the "forerunner of the Jewish star," segregation, curfew, death for sexual relations with German women — a 17-year-old Pole was publicly hanged at Gotha, 24 August 1940, before compelled witnesses); Soviet workers came last. [[goring-hermann|Göring's]] guidelines of 7 November 1941: "Shovelling dirt and quarrying stones are not their jobs – that is what the Russian is there for . . . Russians to arrange own food (cats, horses, etc.) . . . Range of punishment: from cutting food rations to execution by firing squad; generally nothing in between." Two Russian women workers in early 1942: "We're no better off here than pigs." Sauckel's marginal ameliorations were openly instrumental ("flogged, half-starved and dead Russians do not mine coal for us"). From late 1942 the racial-hygiene apparatus reached the workers' infants: "racially good" babies forcibly removed for Germanization; the rest sent to "foreign-children's nursing homes" with catastrophic mortality (96% of Polish/Russian infants died at one Helmstedt home in 1944; an SS general reported to Himmler, 11 August 1943, that the children were being "allowed to starve slowly to death").

## Camp Labor and Extermination Through Work

At **IG Farben's Buna plant at [[auschwitz|Auschwitz]]-Monowitz** (agreed February 1941; the SS paid 3–4 RM per inmate shift, earning ~20 million RM), 35,000 inmates passed through in 1943–44 and ~23,000 (possibly 30,000) died of exhaustion, disease, or "selection" back to the gas chambers, which managers used explicitly as a threat; director Otto Ambros wrote privately, "Our new friendship with the SS . . . is proving very beneficial." The plant never produced buna. Camp mortality overall: 57,000 of an average 95,000 prisoners died in the second half of 1942 alone; Glücks's January 1943 order to cut mortality was purely capacity-driven. A parallel logic distinguished victims: Poles at Auschwitz "must die a natural death" (falsified records required); Jews required none.

## Business Complicity

Dresdner Bank financed Sachsenhausen construction and Crematorium II at Auschwitz; Topf & Sons engineered the gas-chamber crematoria; Degussa refined the gold from murdered Jews' dental fillings (~2 million RM earned; a worker's testimony: "The teeth were still there and sometimes still bloody and with pieces of gum on them"). Evans's assessment: business "most probably gained financially" through 1943, but much late-war camp-labor investment was economically irrational — driven by positioning for postwar plant and property once industrialists judged the war lost.

## Scale and End Conditions

~8.4 million foreign workers drafted over the war, ~490,000 of them dead by mid-1945; of 4.6 million POWs used as labor, 1.16 million did not survive. The system dissolved with the Reich in 1945; survivors waited nearly half a century for compensation (the German industry foundation settlements of the 1990s).
