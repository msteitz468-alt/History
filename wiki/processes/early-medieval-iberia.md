# Early Medieval Iberia

**The transformation of the Iberian Peninsula from late Roman province through Visigothic and Islamic periods to the origins of medieval Spain and Portugal (c. 400–1000 CE).**

## Definition and Scope

Although early medieval Spain and Portugal may seem to stretch the definition of the “barbarian world” considerably—from the point of view of contemporaries they were perhaps one of the most “civilized” parts of the Western world at the time—they provide an interesting view of the transformation of the classical tradition as it merged with other cultures and gradually developed into new traditions that we recognize in the modern world. (range 07 cache, Early Medieval Iberia entry by David Yoon)

The process encompasses the collapse of Roman administration, Germanic successor kingdoms (Suevian enclave NW; Visigothic unification), the 711–720 Arab/Berber conquest establishing al-Andalus, the rise of the Umayyad emirate then caliphate at Córdoba, and the parallel emergence of small Christian realms in the north. Archaeology has transformed understanding since the late 20th century, transcending the Latin/Arabic source divide. (range 07)

## Causal Drivers

**Germanic invasions and Visigothic unification.** In A.D. 400 Spain and Portugal were part of the Roman Empire... A few years later... the Suevians, Vandals, and Alans... entered... The Visigoths (a Germanic tribe from eastern Europe) formed a kingdom in southern France that eventually expanded into Spain. ... By the early seventh century they united all of the Iberian Peninsula... under their rule... capital in Toledo. (range 07)

**Arab conquest and Islamic rule.** Between 711 and 720, an invasion by a small Arab and Berber army from North Africa overthrew the Visigothic kingdom... Arab rule seems to have been established quickly and with little disruption... independent Arab emirate in al-Andalus... By the tenth century this evolved into an independent caliphate, centered on the city of Córdoba. (range 07)

Unlike the Visigoths, the Arabs were unable or unwilling to maintain central control in the mountains of northern Spain. ... small Christian realms were formed... By A.D. 1000... the Kingdom of León, the Kingdom of Pamplona, and the County of Barcelona... (range 07)

**Religious and ethnic distinctions as drivers of cultural change.** Islam manifested its differentness... in many aspects of daily life, from what one could eat or drink, to the daily routine of prayer, to the appropriate placement of the dead... This religious distinction is not only more visible archaeologically, but it also would have given the boundary between Muslims and Christians more force in processes of cultural change. (range 07)

The Visigoths and Suevians constituted only a small minority... They seem to have adopted the culture of the Roman provinces very rapidly... By the seventh century, the ethnic distinction... seems to have merged with and been superseded by concepts of social class and wealth. (range 07)

## Major Phases

**Roman to Visigothic transition (5th–7th c.).** Roman government... powerful... taxation... standing army... As the Roman Empire disintegrated, its successors... attempted to retain as much of the Roman administrative system... They produced coinage derived from Roman types, but in limited quantities... The decline of state control... people tended to live in more clustered settlements, often in defensible locations... (range 07)

By the seventh century... people lived mostly in small sites... commonly located in mountainous areas or inaccessible hilltops. (range 07)

**Arab conquest and al-Andalus (8th–10th c.).** The immediate effect of the Arab conquest on the archaeological record was probably small... The process of Islamization... by A.D. 1000 a majority of the population had converted to Islam... Pig bones... gradually declined... An exception... Rábita de Guardamar... lacks pig bones. (range 07)

Towns... late Roman level immediately followed by deposits of the tenth or eleventh century... paucity of associated habitation material... (range 07)

**Christian north.** In the Kingdom of León, castles were built and villages were established as in France, but they seem to have happened separately... In Islamic al-Andalus... castles... served as defensive refuges and as local outposts of the central administration... (range 07)

## Geographic Spread

The Visigothic kingdom eventually covered the entire peninsula (plus small portion of southern France). Al-Andalus controlled most after 711–720, with northern Christian polities (Asturias/León, Pamplona/Navarre, Catalonia/Barcelona) as counterweights by ~1000. (range 07)

## Interaction with Other Processes

Closely tied to broader early medieval state formation, religious conversion, and economic reorientation after Roman collapse. Long-distance Mediterranean trade (African Red Slip etc.) declined sharply after ~550 and ceased by late 7th c.; resumed 10th c. Specialized local production also declined (fine pottery replaced by coarse, often hand-built). (range 07)

Feudalization processes differed: Catalonia followed French model; elsewhere in Spain/Portugal more disputed. (range 07)

## End Conditions / Transformation

By A.D. 1000 the foundations of later medieval Spain and Portugal were visible in the north-south political/religious divide. Demographic and economic growth from the late 9th/10th centuries set the stage for later Reconquista dynamics. The period ended with the caliphate of Córdoba at its height and the northern kingdoms poised for expansion. (range 07)
