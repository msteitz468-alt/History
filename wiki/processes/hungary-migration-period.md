# The Magyar Migration into the Carpathian Basin

The migration and conquest by which the Magyars (Hungarians), a Finno-Ugric-speaking steppe people, moved from the Pontic steppe into the Carpathian Basin in the late ninth century, displacing or subordinating the region's existing Slavic and remnant [[actors/avars|Avar]] populations and founding the polity that became medieval Hungary.

## Definition and Scope

Distinct from the earlier Avar presence in the same region (the Avar Khaganate, which had dominated the Carpathian Basin from the sixth century until Charlemagne's campaigns and internal collapse effectively ended it around 800), the Magyar migration filled the resulting political vacuum roughly a century later. It is conventionally dated from Magyar groups' appearance west of the Carpathians in the 860s–890s through their decisive settlement (the "Honfoglalás," or Land-Taking) traditionally placed around 895–907.

## Causal Drivers

The migration was driven by pressure from the Pechenegs, a rival steppe people pushing the Magyars westward from their prior lands north of the Black Sea, combined with the opportunity presented by the Carpathian Basin's weak political control following the Avar collapse and the fragmentation of Frankish and Moravian power on its western and northern edges.

## Major Phases

An initial phase of raiding and mercenary service for regional powers (including Frankish and Byzantine patrons) preceded full-scale westward movement and settlement; once established in the Carpathian Basin, the Magyars conducted decades of far-ranging raids across Western and Southern Europe (into Germany, Italy, and as far as Iberia) until a decisive defeat by Otto I at the Battle of Lechfeld (955) ended the raiding phase and pushed Hungarian society toward territorial consolidation and, under [[actors/hungary-arpad|the Árpád dynasty]], eventual Christianization and statehood.

## Geographic Spread

Centered on the Carpathian Basin (roughly modern Hungary and surrounding borderlands), with raiding activity extending across much of Western and Central Europe during the ninth and tenth centuries.

## Interaction

The migration occupied territory left politically open by the earlier collapse of Avar power and interacted directly with the fragmenting Frankish successor states and expanding German kingdoms, whose eventual military response (Lechfeld) forced the shift from a raiding steppe polity to a settled, later Christianized kingdom.

## End Conditions

The migration and raiding phase ended with the defeat at Lechfeld (955) and the subsequent consolidation of Magyar rule into a stable territorial state, culminating in the Christian Kingdom of Hungary founded under [[actors/hungary-arpad|Stephen I]] around 1000/1001.
