# Second Intermediate Period (Egypt)

The era of Egyptian political fragmentation (~1650–1550 BCE) between the
[[middle-kingdom-egypt|Middle]] and [[new-kingdom-egypt|New]] Kingdoms, dominated by
the **[[hyksos|Hyksos]]** — Asiatic rulers of the Delta — and ended by the Theban
war of liberation. Like the [[first-intermediate-period|First Intermediate Period]],
it is a phase of disunity that the Egyptians remembered as catastrophe but which
proved transformative. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]]
(Chs. II, VIII, Hayes & James).

## Definition and Scope

Conventionally the **Thirteenth–Seventeenth Dynasties**. After the unified
Thirteenth Dynasty lost control, Egypt split among: the **Hyksos** "Great Kings"
(15th Dynasty) at **[[avaris|Avaris]]**, ruling the Delta and claiming all Egypt;
lesser Hyksos chiefs (the nominal 16th Dynasty); a native **Theban 17th Dynasty**
holding Upper Egypt from Elephantine to Abydos; an independent **princedom of Kush**
in Nubia; and the rump 13th-Dynasty/Xoite kinglets. Egypt was, in the words of the
*Quarrel of Apophis and Seqenenre*, "a divided land," the whole country nominally
tributary to the Hyksos.

## Causal Drivers

- The slow **decline of the [[middle-kingdom-egypt|Middle Kingdom]]** (rapid royal
  turnover under the 13th Dynasty; the secession of Xois).
- **Asiatic ([[amorites|West-Semitic]]) settlement** in the eastern Delta, swelling
  through the 13th Dynasty until the immigrants could elevate their own chiefs.
- The **occupation of [[avaris|Avaris]]** (~1720) and then **Memphis** (~1674),
  which "broke the Middle Kingdom to pieces."

## Major Phases

1. **Decline and infiltration** (13th–14th Dynasties): central authority weakens;
   Asiatics settle the Delta.
2. **The Hyksos ascendancy** (15th Dynasty, ~1674–1567): **Salitis**, then Khyan and
   the long-reigning **Apophis I**, rule from Avaris under the god **Seth**; Thebes
   pays tribute (the Rhind Papyrus is dated by Apophis's regnal year).
3. **The Theban recovery** (17th Dynasty): the line of Nubkheperre Inyotef,
   Sobkemsaf, Senakhtenre, **Seqenenre Tao II** (who died in battle, his skull
   axe-shattered), and **Kamose** (whose Karnak stelae record the first offensive).
4. **The war of liberation**: **[[ahmose-i|Ahmose I]]** takes Avaris and expels the
   Hyksos (~1550), founding the [[new-kingdom-egypt|New Kingdom]].

## Interaction

The period runs parallel to — and is bound up with — the wider
[[middle-bronze-age|Middle Bronze Age]] Near East: the Hyksos were part of the same
[[amorites|West-Semitic]] world as [[hammurabi|Hammurabi's]] Babylon, and the
Pan-Grave/Medjay Nubian warriors who fought for Thebes belong to the
same age. CAH (1973) firmly **rejects** the idea that the Hyksos came as a single
organized invasion or that they were linked to the [[hurrians|Hurrian]] or
Indo-Aryan movements; theirs was a **gradual infiltration**.

## End Conditions

Ahmose's capture of Avaris and his pursuit of the Hyksos to **Sharuhen** in Palestine
ended the period and reunified Egypt. Its great legacy was psychological and
military: it shattered Egypt's old insular complacency and, by forcing the Thebans
to fight and to adopt Asiatic military technology (the **horse-drawn chariot**, the
**composite bow**, bronze weapons), it "laid the foundations and largely determined
the character of the New Kingdom" — the [[new-kingdom-egypt|imperial Egypt]] that
followed.

## Significance

The SIP is the hinge on which Egypt turned **outward**. Where the
[[first-intermediate-period|First Intermediate Period]] had been an internal
collapse, the second was an encounter with Asia that converted a self-sufficient
river-kingdom into an **expansionist military empire** — and one of the Late Bronze
"great powers." Historiographically it is also a caution against trusting New
Kingdom propaganda, which painted the Hyksos as ruthless barbarians; the
contemporary evidence shows a largely Egyptianized, not especially oppressive,
foreign administration.
