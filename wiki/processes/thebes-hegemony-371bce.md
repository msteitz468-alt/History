# The Theban Hegemony, 371–362 BCE

## Definition and Scope

The **Theban hegemony** is the brief decade, from the victory at
**[[battle-of-leuctra-371bce|Leuctra]]** (371) to the death of **[[epaminondas]]** at
**[[battle-of-mantinea-362bce|Mantinea]]** (362), in which **Thebes** — leader of the
revived **Boeotian Confederacy** — was the foremost power in Greece.
[[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 7, J. Roy) stresses how Thebes "moved quickly from
a position of relative weakness to become a leading power," acting in central Greece,
Thessaly, Macedon, the Peloponnese and — uniquely for a land power — the Aegean. It is a
hegemony almost wholly the work of two men, and one that **did not outlive them**.

## Causal Drivers

- The shattering of Spartan prestige at **Leuctra** and the **deep grievances** that
  Spartan domination had bred across the Peloponnese (Mantinea's wish to refound itself,
  Elis' claim to Triphylia, the Arcadians' desire for a federal state).
- The **death of Jason of Pherae** (370) and of **Amyntas of Macedon**, which removed
  northern checks on Theban ambition.
- The genius of **[[epaminondas]]** and the diplomatic skill of **Pelopidas**.

## Major Phases

1. **Securing central Greece (371–370).** Orchomenus was forced into the Boeotian
   federation and a ring of allies (Phocis, Locris, Aetolia, Acarnania, Euboea — the
   last defecting from the [[second-athenian-confederacy|Athenian Confederacy]]) made
   Thebes secure as never before.
2. **The Peloponnesian earthquake (370–369).** The Arcadians, in revolt against Sparta,
   formed the **Arcadian League**; with Argos and Elis they drew Thebes south.
   [[epaminondas|Epaminondas]] led a great **invasion of Laconia** — the first hostile
   army to reach the Eurotas in memory — and then performed his decisive act: the
   **[[liberation-of-messenia-369bce|liberation of Messenia]]**, founding the city of
   **Messene** and stripping Sparta of its helot base forever. He also fostered
   **Megalopolis** as the Arcadian federal capital.
3. **Repeated campaigns (369–366).** Further invasions reduced Sicyon, Pellene and others;
   by 366/5 a Common Peace detached Corinth and the remnants of the
   [[peloponnesian-league|Peloponnesian League]] from Sparta — "Thebes had now deprived
   Sparta of both Messenia and the Peloponnesian League."
4. **North and sea (366–364).** Pelopidas dominated **Thessaly** (dying in victory at
   **Cynoscephalae**, 364) and intervened in **Macedon** (holding the young **Philip** as
   a hostage at Thebes). Epaminondas built a **100-trireme fleet** (probably Persian-
   funded) that sailed the Aegean to Rhodes, Chios and Byzantium — a remarkable but
   barren venture that "did not venture forth again."
5. **Mantinea and the end (362).** A split in the Arcadian League brought Thebes back to
   war; at **[[battle-of-mantinea-362bce|Mantinea]]** Epaminondas broke the enemy line but
   **was killed**, and the hegemony died with him.

## Interaction

The Theban hegemony reshaped every other power: it **permanently crippled Sparta**,
sharpened the **Athens–Thebes rivalry** (Athens allied with Sparta from 369), and left the
Peloponnese a patchwork of feuding federations. Roy stresses that Thebes
"compartmentalized" its many fronts rather than combining them, straining its small
citizen base.

## End Conditions

Mantinea ended the hegemony not by defeat but by **decapitation**: Thebes won the field
yet lost Epaminondas, and "lost some of the capacity she had had... to initiate change in
Greek inter-state politics." Xenophon ended his *Hellenica* here, on the famous note that
after the battle "there was even more confusion and disorder in Greece than before." The
deeper legacy was **mutual exhaustion** — Sparta broken, Thebes spent, Athens checked —
the vacuum into which **Philip of Macedon** (a former hostage at Thebes) would move within
a generation.
