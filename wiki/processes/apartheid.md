# Apartheid and the Liberation of Southern Africa

**Apartheid** ("apartness") was the system of **institutionalized racial segregation and white-minority
rule** in South Africa (1948–94), and the last great battleground of African liberation. Documented for the
wiki through the **UNESCO [[gha-vol8-since-1935|General History of Africa, Vol. VIII]]** (chs. 10, 25), which
treats the freeing of Southern Africa as the culmination of the continent's decolonization and of
[[pan-africanism|Pan-Africanism]].

## The apartheid system

Apartheid extended and formalized the racial order built by the [[southern-african-mineral-revolution|
mineral revolution]] and the segregation laws of earlier decades. After the **National Party's victory in
1948**, the Afrikaner government legislated a comprehensive system: the **Population Registration**,
**Group Areas**, and **pass laws** classified and confined people by race; the **"homelands" (Bantustans)**
sought to strip Black South Africans of citizenship; education, labour, residence, and marriage were all
racially controlled — all to entrench white political power and cheap Black labour.

## Resistance and liberation

GHA VIII frames the **liberation struggle** as the final phase of African decolonization:

- mass nonviolent protest gave way, after the **Sharpeville massacre (1960)** and the banning of the
  **African National Congress (ANC)** and **PAC**, to **armed struggle** (Umkhonto we Sizwe) and the long
  imprisonment of **Nelson Mandela** (1964–90);
- the **Soweto uprising (1976)** and sustained internal revolt, mass strikes, and the United Democratic
  Front kept the country ungovernable;
- the struggle was **regional**: the independence of the Portuguese colonies (**Angola, Mozambique**, 1975),
  **Zimbabwe** (1980), and **Namibia** (1990) progressively isolated apartheid South Africa, which waged
  destabilizing wars across the **"Frontline States."**

## The Cold War and the world

Southern Africa became a **[[cold-war|Cold War]]** arena: the apartheid regime cast itself as an
anti-communist bulwark and intervened (with US tacit support at times) against Soviet- and Cuban-backed
movements in Angola, while the liberation movements drew Soviet and Cuban support (see
[[westad-cold-war|Westad]]). **International sanctions, the sports and cultural boycott, and disinvestment**
campaigns made apartheid a global moral cause.

## The transition

The end came with the Cold War's: in **1990** President de Klerk unbanned the ANC and released **Mandela**;
negotiations led to the **first non-racial democratic elections in April 1994**, which made **Nelson
Mandela** president — the symbolic completion of African liberation, and the event Reader called Africa's
"flickering hope."

## Significance

Apartheid's defeat was the **last act of African decolonization** and a landmark of the twentieth century —
the fall of the continent's final white-minority regime. GHA VIII presents it as the vindication of the
liberation strand of [[pan-africanism|Pan-Africanism]], even as post-1994 South Africa inherited the deep
inequalities the system had built.
