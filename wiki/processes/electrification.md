# Electrification

**Electrification** is the long-duration process by which electrical power generation, transmission, and use spread through industry, transport, and daily life from the late nineteenth century onward — one of the defining technological transformations of the modern world and a core strand of the [[processes/second-industrial-revolution|Second Industrial Revolution]].

## Definition and Scope

Electrification required not just a power source but a complete system: efficient long-distance transmission (solved by [[concepts/alternating-current|polyphase AC]] and the transformer), reliable motive power (the [[concepts/induction-motor|AC induction motor]]), lighting (Edison's incandescent bulb and later fluorescent/neon lamps), and the financing and organization of central generating stations and grids.

## Causal Drivers

- The [[processes/war-of-the-currents|War of the Currents]] settling AC as the transmission standard.
- Landmark demonstrations: the [[events/worlds-columbian-exposition-1893|1893 Columbian Exposition]] and the [[events/niagara-falls-power-project-1895|Niagara Falls power project]].
- Heavy capital from financiers such as [[actors/morgan-john-pierpont|J. P. Morgan]] and industrial firms (Westinghouse, General Electric).

[[actors/tesla-nikola|Nikola Tesla]]'s AC system is the foundational enabling technology for the transmission and motive-power dimensions of electrification.
