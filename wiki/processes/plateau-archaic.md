# Plateau Archaic

## Definition and Scope

Holocene hunter-gatherer (and later semisettled riverine) trajectory of the **Northwest Plateau** (Columbia and Fraser drainages) — cooler and wetter than the Great Basin, structured by **salmon runs** and plant foods (esp. **camas**). Part of [[paleoindian-and-archaic-north-america]]; contrasts with [[desert-archaic]].

## Causal Drivers

- Columbia and Fraser anadromous fish productivity
- Camas and other root/plant foods
- Earth-lodge village technology enabling seasonal sedentism
- Cascade Landslide and other geomorphic events reshaping salmon access

## Major Phases

- Many Plateau bands in **semipermanent villages of semisubterranean earth lodges by the first millennium B.C.**
- **Nightfire Island**: earth lodges in use by at least **2100 B.C.**
- **Cascade Landslide ~A.D. 1265** appears to have restored salmon runs to upper Columbia tributaries; thereafter **Sahaptin** speakers of the upper Columbia readapted to migratory fish (they had been more Basin-like foragers if the Cascade blockage had cut salmon access)

## Geographic Spread

Columbia–Fraser Plateau; interaction with Great Basin to the south and [[northwest-coast-archaic|Northwest Coast]] to the west (side-notched points from Plateau contributed to early NW Coast Archaic).

## Interaction

`contrasts_with: [[desert-archaic]]` · `part_of: [[paleoindian-and-archaic-north-america]]` · enabled later historic Plateau salmon economies

## End Conditions

Riverine Archaic lifeways persist into contact with varying degrees of intensification; horse and trade later transform Plateau societies (beyond Snow ch. 3).
