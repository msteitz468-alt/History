# The Vedic Age and the Second Urbanization

## Definition and Scope

This is the long arc of state formation in early South Asia, from the composition of the
**Rig Veda** (c. 1500 BCE) to the rise of the **[[mauryan-empire|Mauryan Empire]]** (c. 321
BCE). In [[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (Kaul, Ch 18) it is the foundation of "civilization
in mainland South Asia as we know it today" — the transition from semi-nomadic pastoral
tribes to agrarian states, cities, money, writing, and the **caste** order. (It is distinct
from, and conventionally traced separately after the decline of, the earlier Bronze Age
**Harappan/Indus** civilization, c. 1800 BCE.)

## Early and Later Vedic

- **Early Vedic** (c. 1500–1000 BCE): **Indo-Aryan**-speaking (archaic Sanskrit,
  Indo-European), pastoral and semi-nomadic, cattle-centered. Tribes (*jana*) under a chief
  (*rajan*) who received voluntary offerings (*bali*); a kin-based, pre-state, pre-class
  society with reciprocal gift-exchange and no clear money. Reconstructed almost entirely
  from the **sacred texts** themselves.
- **Later Vedic** (c. 1000–500 BCE): eastward expansion into the fertile **Ganga basin**;
  the decisive shift from pastoralism to **agriculture** (wheat, rice, lentils) with the
  ox-plough and (debated) iron. Tribes became **janapadas** ("footprints of the *jana*") —
  fixed territories under *kshatriya* ruling clans; *bali* became compulsory **tribute**;
  lavish royal sacrifices (*ashvamedha*) enacted a "prestige economy" served by *brahmana*
  priests rewarded with land.

## State Formation and the Second Urbanization

By **500–300 BCE** these processes culminated in the **sixteen great states**
(*mahajanapadas*) — monarchies (*rajya*) and oligarchies (*gana-sangha*) in perpetual war —
from which **Magadha** rose supreme (its capital eventually [[pataliputra|Pataliputra]]).
This is the **Second Urbanization**: new cities (Varanasi, Rajagriha, Kaushambi, Mathura),
massive ramparts, the **Northern Black Polished Ware**, the first metal **[[coinage|coins]]**
(*karshapanas*), the **Brahmi** script (late 4th c. BCE), guilds, and long-distance trade
along the **Uttarapatha** and **Dakshinapatha** routes.

## Interaction

The urban ferment of the sixth–fifth centuries BCE produced the **[[axial-age|Axial Age]]**
faiths of South Asia — **[[buddhism|Buddhism]]** and **[[jainism|Jainism]]** — which rejected
Vedic ritual authority and won merchant and royal patronage. The fourfold **[[caste-varna|varna]]**
order crystallized in the Later Vedic period as occupational stratification fused with ritual
hierarchy.

## End Conditions

The arc culminates in the **[[mauryan-empire|Mauryan Empire]]** (321 BCE), South Asia's
first and largest ancient empire — the political consummation of Vedic-to-state
development.

## Historiography

The Early Vedic is reconstructed **almost entirely from sacred oral texts** (the Rig Veda's
earliest manuscript is no older than the 12th c. CE), with thin and uncertain archaeology
(Painted Grey Ware). Two debates are central: the **Indo-Aryan migration** question
(immigration from outside vs. indigenous origin — "heated and racially tinted," majority view
favoring migration; see [[indo-european-origins]]), and the **role of iron** as prime mover
of Gangetic state formation (challenged in favor of socio-political drivers). The "varna"
system's divine self-account (the *Purusha Sukta*) is read by historians as a conscious
structure for controlling access to resources.
