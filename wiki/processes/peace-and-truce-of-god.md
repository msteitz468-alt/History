# Peace and Truce of God

## Definition and Scope

The **Peace of God** (*pax Dei*) and the **Truce of God** (*treuga Dei*) were related late-tenth- and eleventh-century ecclesiastical movements that sought to **limit knightly violence** by oath, excommunication, and the sacralisation of protected persons and times. [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]] presents them as **partial but real restraints** on war before Clermont (1095), not as a failed pacifism that simply “failed” into crusade.

## Causal Drivers

- Proliferation of **private war** and castle-based predation after Carolingian collapse
- Episcopal responsibility for **clergy, churches, and the poor**
- Use of **oaths** and **excommunication** as enforcement tools
- Expansion of **sacred time** (Sunday, feast seasons) into longer truce windows

## Major Phases

1. **Peace of God (from late 10th c.).** Aquitaine councils urged sparing clergy and the poor; at **Poitiers (1000)** settling disputes by arms was forbidden under threat of excommunication. Oaths sealed the programme.
2. **Truce of God.** Called for total abstinence from war during specified times. The protected window expanded from Sunday/holy-day daylight to **Wednesday evening–Monday daybreak** by the mid-eleventh century.
3. **Incomplete reception.** **William of Normandy** accepted and extended the Truce (**1042**) yet fought **Hastings on Saturday 14 October 1066** — Logan’s emblem that restraints were real but incomplete.
4. **Transition toward sanctified war.** Logan places Peace/Truce in the run-up to Urban II’s **Clermont** preaching: the church’s long effort to discipline *milites* fed into the ideal of redirecting violence against “infidels.”

## Geographic Spread

Strongest in **France** (Aquitaine and adjoining regions as early centres); variants and echoes across western Europe as episcopal and royal peaces developed.

## Interaction

- `preceded_by:` post-Carolingian fragmentation and knightly violence
- `enabled:` moral language later recycled in [[first-crusade-1095|crusade]] preaching (soldiers of Christ vs war on brothers)
- `concurrent_with:` [[gregorian-reform]] — another programme of ordering Christian society
- Contrasts with pure **Augustinian just-war** limits: Peace/Truce sacralise *when* and *whom* one may fight, not only *why*

## End Conditions

The movements did not vanish as statutes so much as they were **absorbed**: royal and communal peaces, reformed canon law, and the crusade ideal offered alternative frameworks for channelling and legitimating force. Logan’s point is that they show the Latin church already **regulating war** before it **sanctified** distant holy war.
