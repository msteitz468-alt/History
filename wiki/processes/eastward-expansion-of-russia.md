# Eastward Expansion of Russia

## Definition and Scope
The **Eastward Expansion of Russia** refers to the rapid territorial acquisition, exploration, and demographic integration of the lands beyond the Ural Mountains, stretching across the Eurasian steppe and northern forests to the Pacific Ocean (L2494-2496, L2586-2592). Spanning the late 16th to mid-19th centuries, this process brought the vast resources of Siberia under the control of the Russian Tsardom, established a major colonial administration, and brought Russia into direct contact with the empires of East Asia (L2586-2588, L2594-2596).

## Causal Drivers
* **The Fur Trade:** Valued fur-bearing animals (sable, ermine, beaver) were the primary economic driver of expansion (L2596). Furs were a cornerstone of Muscovite state finance and foreign trade, prompting the government to collect a fur tax (*iasak*) from the native populations (L2596).
  From Lieven (Cambridge Vol. II ch.1, ll. 4305): “Initially it was furs which drew the Russians into Siberia, the early period of Russian empire beyond the Urals thereby having something in common with the French fur-based empire in Canada.” Central Asia cotton parallels British Egypt but also “Anglo-Russian struggle for geopolitical advantage.” From Bassin ch.3 (ll. 5160–5171): Siberia perceived as colonial “Other” (“our Peru,” “Russian Brazil,” “our little India”); Urals boundary enabled metropole/colonial divide. Danilevskii (ll. 5488–5491): “Russia never possessed colonies” and “entirely mistaken to regard Siberia as an example of one”; unified natural region. From Weeks ch.2 (ll. 4885–5010): Siberian/Alaska furs sparse Russian presence; Central Asia push 1860s–90s (Tashkent, Samarkand etc.) for cotton + anti-British buffer; limited Russification, iasak/tribute continuity. From ch.7 Martin: 1812 evacuation to east reinforced frontier perceptions.
* **Cossack Daring and Exploration:** Warlike adventurers and Cossacks, seeking freedom, wealth, and adventure, led the advance into uncharted wilderness, establishing fortified posts (*ostrog*) along key rivers (L2224-2226, L2592-2594).
* **Industrial Entrepreneurs:** Wealthy merchant families, such as the Stroganovs, established salt and fur extraction industries in the Urals and funded private military expeditions to secure and expand their holdings (L2138-2140).
* **From Cambridge Vol. I range:** "It was not long before the reach of the Stroganovs’ entrepreneurial activity encroached on the borders of the khanate." Kuchum (Chingisid restored 1563) imposed tribute on Khanty/Mansi; "disputes over tribute-paying Khanty and Mansi led to clashes". Ermak ambushed/killed 1585; Moscow initially "chastised the Stroganovs for hiring a band of the unruly Volga cossacks" and "accused the Stroganovs of needlessly provoking Kuchum Khan". Reversed after Ivan IV death 1584; "Moscow declared an annexation of Siberia and promptly dispatched the troops". "In some sense, Siberia was conquered in spite of the Muscovite government, which preferred a slow and cautious pace of expansion." Tiumen 1586, Tobol’sk 1587. "By the end of the sixteenth century, with the exception of the Crimea, the Muscovite rulers could claim control over the entire territory of the former Golden Horde." Terms: shert’ (oath), amanat (hostages), iasak. Natives saw peace treaty; Moscow saw vassalage. "Moscow’s policy of demanding an immediate submission to the tsar was typical for both the southern and eastern frontiers." (Khodarkovsky l8613-8820)

## Major Phases
1. **The Conquest of the Khanate of Sibir (1582–1584):** The Stroganovs dispatched an expedition of 1,650 Cossacks and volunteers led by Ermak (L2138-2140). Utilizing firearms and superior organization, they defeated the native forces of Khan Kuchum and seized his headquarters, opening Western Siberia (L2140-2142).
2. **Rapid Trans-Siberian Advance (1610–1640):** Russian explorers moved rapidly eastward along northern river routes, covering 3,000 miles from the Ob River to the Pacific Ocean (L2586-2592). Tiumen (1586) and Tobolsk (1587) were built as administrative centers (L2142). In 1639, Ivan Moskvitianin's detachment reached the Pacific coast (L2594).
3. **Arctic Exploration and Sino-Russian Contact (Late 17th Century):** In 1648, Cossack Semen Dezhnev sailed from the Kolyma River around the northeastern tip of Asia through the Bering Strait (L2594). Concurrently, Russian advances in the Amur River basin led to clashes with the Qing Dynasty, resulting in the **Treaty of Nerchinsk** (1689), which established the Sino-Russian border (L2594-2596).
4. **Scientific and Pacific Consolidation (18th Century):** Peter the Great and his successors organized academic expeditions (e.g., Vitus Bering's voyages and the First Academic Expedition of 1733–1742) to map the coastlines and catalog Siberia's geography, leading to the discovery of Alaska in 1732 (L2820, L3762).

## Geographic Spread
Expansion occurred along the northern forest belt and river systems (Ob, Yenisei, Lena, Kolyma) rather than the southern steppe, which was blocked by powerful nomadic confederations (L2138, L2592-2594). The advance eventually extended into Kamchatka (1696) and across the Bering Strait into North America (L2594, L3762).

## Interaction
* **Native Relations:** Muscovite policy was generally paternalistic; native populations were required to pay the *iasak* but were not to be forcibly baptized (L2600-2602). Native converts were legally treated as Russians, exempting them from the *iasak*, which sometimes discouraged the government from promoting conversion (L2602).
* **Social Development:** Because Siberia lacked a local nobility, it escaped the institution of serfdom (L2602). The frontier society developed a sturdy, independent, and democratic character, heavily influenced by the assimilation and intermarriage of Russian settlers with native populations (L2602-2604).
* **Administrative Control:** The Siberian Prikaz (*Sibirskii Prikaz*) in Moscow managed the territory, sharing authority with the Church (which established a Siberian archbishopric in 1621) (L2598). Local governors (*voevody*) exercised broad, often arbitrary power due to the immense distances from the capital (L2598-2600).

## End Conditions
The active phase of eastward territorial acquisition in Asia concluded with the Treaty of Aigun (1858) and the Treaty of Peking (1860), which secured the Amur and Ussuri regions from China, establishing the modern far-eastern borders of Russia.
