# Democratization of the Afterlife

The "**democratization of the afterlife**" (John Wilson's phrase) is the extension of
once-exclusively-royal funerary privileges to non-royal Egyptians — the gradual opening, from
the [[processes/first-intermediate-period|First Intermediate Period]] onward, of the means of
eternal survival to officials and eventually ordinary people.

## Definition and scope

In the [[actors/old-kingdom-egypt|Old Kingdom]] the apparatus of the afterlife — the
[[concepts/pyramid-texts|Pyramid Texts]], Osirian rites, monumental tomb, mortuary cult — was
the king's prerogative. As royal authority weakened, these privileges spread outward: the
**Coffin Texts** (Pyramid-Text extracts plus new compositions, inscribed on the coffins of
provincial elites) emerge in the First Intermediate Period; the idea that **every person
possesses a *ba*** is dramatized in the *Dialogue between a Man Tired of Life and his ba*,
"the world's earliest debate on the issue of suicide"; and **shabti** figures (earliest from
the time of [[actors/mentuhotpe-ii|Mentuhotep II]]) provide every tomb-owner with substitute
labourers for the next world.

## Causal drivers

The process is rooted in the **provincialization** of the First Intermediate Period, when
"Old Kingdom court traditions … spread to provincial populations, simultaneously broadening
access and reducing quality." It accelerated under the 12th Dynasty: the Osiris-focused
nationwide temple programme of Senusret I produced "a more significant levelling between the
king's belief in the afterlife and the beliefs of his subjects." Mentuhotep II's
Deir el-Bahri complex — the first royal monument to stress Osirian belief — embodies the same
levelling at the royal level.

## Major phases

1. **First Intermediate Period** — Coffin Texts originate in the provincial milieu; offering
   figures, cartonnage masks, and slab stelae spread.
2. **12th Dynasty** — Osiris cult and afterlife provision broaden; Coffin Texts standard on
   elite coffins (their use ceasing mid-dynasty with the mummiform coffin).
3. **New Kingdom** — the **Book of the Dead** (see [[sources/romer-egyptian-book-of-the-dead-2008]])
   makes the descended royal-afterlife literature available, in principle, to anyone who could
   afford a papyrus.

## Interaction

The process both expresses and erodes the exclusivity of [[concepts/divine-kingship]]: the
king remained uniquely divine, but the **path to Osiris** was no longer his alone. It is
inseparable from the rise of **Osiris** as the god of the universal dead.

## End conditions

By the New Kingdom the "democratization" is essentially complete as an ideology of access;
what then varied was wealth, not entitlement. The trajectory continued into later funerary
literature.
