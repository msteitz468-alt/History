# Woman Suffrage in the United States (c. 1848–1920)

Long-duration campaign to secure women's voting rights from Seneca Falls through ratification of the [[nineteenth-amendment-1920|Nineteenth Amendment]]. Cox's *Light Withdrawn* narrates the final Wilson-era phase as a confrontation between this process and a southern Democratic president whose racial and gender ideology made him "superbly unsuited for the moment."

## Definition and Scope

Includes state enfranchisements, the federal amendment track, organizational history (NWSA/AWSA → NAWSA; Congressional Union → NWP), and the intersection with Reconstruction amendments, Jim Crow, and Black women's activism. Not identical to broader women's-rights or feminist processes (education, property, reproductive autonomy).

## Causal Drivers

- Enlightenment and abolitionist rights language ([[stanton-elizabeth-cady]], [[anthony-susan-b]], early "kindred causes")
- Political learning from state victories and defeats
- Progressive-era federalization of reform
- Wartime democracy rhetoric creating hypocrisy costs for opponents

## Major Phases

1. **Founding and Reconstruction (1848–1870s):** Seneca Falls; post-war split over 14th/15th Amendments and whether to prioritize Black male suffrage.
2. **Doldrums and state strategy (1878–1910):** Anthony Amendment repeatedly introduced; western states enfranchise women; organizational consolidation as NAWSA.
3. **Federal militant turn (1913–1917):** [[woman-suffrage-procession-1913]]; Paul/Burns pressure politics; Wilson resistance.
4. **War and victory (1917–1920):** [[silent-sentinels-1917]]; Winning Plan; congressional passage; ratification.

## Geographic Spread

West-first state map; southern resistance tied to racial hierarchy; Washington, D.C. as federal decision center under Wilson.

## Interaction

- Intersects [[reconstruction]] and Jim Crow (who counts as a voter)
- Intersects Progressive economic reform ([[new-freedom]]) without being reducible to it
- Race fracture inside the movement ([[wells-ida-b]]) amplified by Wilson-era white supremacy

## End Conditions

Constitutional amendment 1920 ends the formal national campaign; does not end racial or other practical disfranchisement. Successor organizations (League of Women Voters; NWP's Equal Rights Amendment track) transform the process.
