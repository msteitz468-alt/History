# Rise of Nazism (1919–1933)

The fourteen-year process by which a small nationalist splinter group in
postwar Munich grew into the party that seized control of the German state,
spanning [[drexler-anton|Anton Drexler's]] founding of the German Workers'
Party (DAP) in 1919 through Adolf Hitler's appointment as Chancellor on 30
January 1933.

## Definition and Scope

This page traces the connective political narrative across a sequence of
more granular events already covered individually in this collection —
Drexler's founding of the DAP, Hitler's early takeover of the party and its
1920 refoundation as the NSDAP, the failed 1923 Beer Hall Putsch, the
party's 1925 refoundation after Hitler's release from prison, its
Depression-era electoral breakthrough, and the negotiated
[[nazi-seizure-of-power-1933|Machtergreifung]] of January 1933 — rather than
duplicating their detailed treatment.

## Causal Drivers

Germany's defeat in the First World War, the perceived humiliation of
Versailles, the 1923 hyperinflation crisis, and above all the mass
unemployment of the Great Depression after 1929 created recurring waves of
political radicalization that the NSDAP exploited more effectively than
rival extremist movements, aided by Hitler's oratorical skill and the
party's paramilitary (Sturmabteilung) presence in the streets.

## Major Phases

An obscure founding phase (1919–1923) ended in the failed Beer Hall Putsch;
a rebuilding phase (1925–1928) produced only marginal electoral results;
the Depression-driven surge (1930–1932) transformed the NSDAP into
Germany's largest party; and a final phase of elite political maneuvering
(1932–January 1933) resulted in President Hindenburg's decision to appoint
Hitler Chancellor, believing conservative coalition partners could contain
him.

## Geographic Spread

The movement began in Bavaria and expanded nationally through the 1920s,
achieving its strongest electoral support in Protestant, rural, and
small-town northern and eastern Germany by the early 1930s.

## Interaction

The process is inseparable from the Weimar Republic's structural
weaknesses — proportional representation encouraging fragmentation,
presidential emergency-powers provisions, and elite distrust of mass
democracy — which together made the NSDAP's rise possible without a
majority electoral mandate.

## End Conditions

The process ends with Hitler's legal appointment as Chancellor on 30
January 1933, after which the [[nazi-seizure-of-power-1933|Machtergreifung]]
rapidly consolidated one-party dictatorship within months.
