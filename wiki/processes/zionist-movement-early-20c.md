## Definition and Scope

The specific early phase of [[processes/zionism|Zionism]] running from the 1897 First Zionist Congress through the consolidation of British control over Palestine in the early 1920s, encompassing the organizational buildup of the World Zionist Organization, the second and third waves of aliyah (Jewish immigration to Ottoman/Mandatory Palestine), and the diplomatic campaign that secured the 1917 Balfour Declaration.

## Causal Drivers

Herzl's organizational founding of the movement; continued Jewish emigration pressure from Eastern Europe (pogroms, economic hardship); and, decisively, the wartime diplomacy of Chaim Weizmann and allies with the British government, which culminated in the Balfour Declaration's statement of support for a Jewish national home in Palestine, subsequently incorporated into the League of Nations Mandate.

## Major Phases

Pre-war institutional consolidation (1897–1914); wartime diplomacy and the Balfour Declaration (1914–1917); post-war ratification through the San Remo Conference and League of Nations Mandate (1920–1922). International supporters, including prominent public intellectuals such as [[actors/einstein-albert|Albert Einstein]], undertook fundraising and advocacy tours (e.g., Einstein's 1921 US tour) on behalf of Zionist institutions such as the planned Hebrew University of Jerusalem.

## Geographic Spread

Organizational centers in Europe (Vienna, Berlin, London) directing immigration and settlement activity concentrated in Ottoman, then British Mandatory, Palestine.

## Interaction

Directly enabled by and dependent on the collapse of Ottoman control over the Levant during the First World War, and closely tied to the broader [[processes/zionism|Zionist movement's]] longer-term trajectory toward statehood.

## End Conditions

This phase concludes with the confirmation of the British Mandate in 1922, after which the movement's institutional and demographic buildup within Mandatory Palestine became the dominant frame, continuing within the broader arc of [[processes/zionism]].
