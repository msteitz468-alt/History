# Evolution of War

Long-term historical process tracing the transformation of human intergroup lethal violence from small-scale forager raiding through agrarian tribal and state warfare to industrial total war and the contemporary mixed landscape (decline among developed liberal democracies alongside persistent and novel threats).

## Definition and Scope

Not a single event but the co-evolution of human psychology, social organization, technology, economy, and political structures around the practice of war. War both drove and was driven by the major thresholds in human history (agriculture, state formation, military revolution, industrialization, nuclear revolution).

## Major Phases

1. **Evolutionary / Forager baseline** (Deep Prehistory–Mesolithic): Small-band raiding, ambush, revenge; high per-capita risk; motivations rooted in evolved psychology (resources, mates, status). See [[hunter-gatherer-warfare]] and [[concepts/evolutionary-origins-of-war]].
2. **Agrarian / Tribal intensification**: Sedentism, population growth, storable wealth, and pastoral mobility increase frequency and stakes of conflict. Fortifications, specialized warriors, endemic raiding and feuding.
3. **State emergence and "Leviathan"**: Armed force as central to monopolizing legitimate violence internally while projecting it externally. War-making and state-making mutually constitutive (Tilly parallel; Gat deeper biological + historical account). Eurasian steppe–sown dynamics as recurring macro-pattern.
4. **Early Modern military revolution & fiscal-military state**: Gunpowder, drill, trace italienne, permanent forces, taxation/bureaucracy feedback loops. Global projection.
5. **Industrial / Total War**: Mass armies, machine production, railroads/telegraph, air power, total mobilization of society and economy. World Wars as climax.
6. **Modern transformation (affluent liberal democracies + ultimate weapons)**: Dramatic decline in war among rich liberal states; persistence or transformation elsewhere (failed states, ethnic conflict, terrorism, proliferation). Nuclear deterrence and economic interdependence as structural constraints.

## Interaction with Other Processes

- Closely coupled with [[state-formation]], [[industrialization]], [[industrialization-of-warfare]], [[fiscal-military-state]].
- Reciprocal with demographic processes (population pressure, virgin-soil effects) and ideological ones (just war, holy war, nationalism, totalitarianism).
- Feedback with technology (chariot → iron → gunpowder → mechanized → nuclear).

## End Conditions / Current State

No terminal end. Among developed liberal democracies the "calculus" of war has changed so that major war is widely regarded as irrational and self-defeating (affluence raises opportunity costs; nuclear weapons make victory pyrrhic; democratic accountability raises domestic price). Globally, war has not disappeared and may reappear in new guises (cyber, autonomous systems, WMD terrorism).

## Historiography

Gat (2006) provides the most ambitious single-volume evolutionary-to-modern synthesis. Earlier "invention of war" views (Mead) largely superseded by archaeological and ethnographic data. Realist IR theory supplies proximate mechanisms but often lacks ultimate causes. Military historians (Delbrück, Keegan, Parker, etc.) document the operational face of each phase.
