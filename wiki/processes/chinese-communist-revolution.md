# The Chinese Communist Revolution

The long revolutionary process that, out of the wreckage of the
[[fall-of-the-qing-1911|fallen empire]] and the failed Republic, produced the **People's
Republic of China (1949)** and remade Chinese society — the culmination of China's "search for
modern China." From Jonathan Spence's [[spence-search-for-modern-china|*The Search for Modern
China*]].

## From the Republic's failure to May Fourth

The [[fall-of-the-qing-1911|1911 Revolution]] left a weak republic that fragmented after Yuan
Shikai's death (1916) into **warlordism**. Disillusion peaked in the **May Fourth Movement
(1919)** — a cultural and nationalist awakening (sparked by the Versailles award of German
Shandong to Japan) that attacked Confucian tradition and embraced "science and democracy," and,
for many intellectuals, **Marxism**. With Comintern help, the **Chinese Communist Party (CCP)
was founded in 1921**.

## Alliance, rupture, and the rural turn

The CCP first allied with [[arab-nationalism|Sun Yat-sen's]] **Guomindang (Nationalists)** in a
united front and the **Northern Expedition** to reunify China — until **Chiang Kai-shek's bloody
purge of the Communists (1927)** shattered the urban movement. The survivors, led increasingly by
**[[mao-zedong|Mao Zedong]]**, turned to **peasant-based rural revolution** (land redistribution,
guerrilla bases). Driven from the Jiangxi Soviet, they undertook the **Long March (1934–35)** to
**Yan'an**, where Mao became paramount.

## War, civil war, and victory

The **Japanese invasion (1937–45)** was decisive: while the Guomindang bore the brunt of the
conventional war and was exhausted by it, the Communists built peasant support through resistance
and reform in the countryside (the patriotic, agrarian appeal Spence emphasizes). In the
**civil war (1945–49)** the Communists, backed by land reform and a Manchurian base, defeated the
corruption- and inflation-ridden Guomindang, which fled to **Taiwan**. On **1 October 1949** Mao
proclaimed the **People's Republic of China**.

## Building — and convulsing — the new society

The revolution then transformed China: **land reform**, the [[cold-war|Korean War]], the **First
Five-Year Plan**, and collectivization. But Mao's radicalism produced catastrophe — the **Great
Leap Forward (1958–61)** and its tens-of-millions famine, and the **Cultural Revolution
(1966–76)**. After Mao's death (1976), **Deng Xiaoping** launched the **"Four Modernizations" and
"reform and opening"** — decollectivizing agriculture, creating Special Economic Zones, and
turning toward a market economy under one-party rule — the foundation of China's
[[contemporary|contemporary rise]] (the 1989 Tiananmen suppression marking the limits of
political reform).

## Interaction

The revolution is the East Asian arm of the global [[cold-war|Cold War]] and the most populous
case of twentieth-century [[communism|Communist]] revolution; it completed the "search for modern
China" begun with the [[opium-wars|Opium Wars]] — restoring a strong, unified, sovereign Chinese
state at immense human cost.
