# Reconstruction (United States, 1865-1877)

**Reconstruction** was the postwar process (1865–1877) of readmitting the former
Confederate states to the Union, redefining the legal and political status of formerly
enslaved African Americans, and rebuilding Southern society and institutions after the
[[events/american-civil-war-1861-1865|American Civil War]]. It proceeded through
successive, contested phases — Presidential Reconstruction under Andrew Johnson's lenient
terms, followed by Congressional ("Radical") Reconstruction from 1867, which imposed
military districts on the South, guaranteed Black male suffrage, and produced the
Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments — and saw, for the first time, African Americans
elected to Southern state legislatures and the US Congress.

## Retreat and Legacy

Reconstruction faced sustained, often violent resistance from white Southern paramilitary
and political opposition (the Ku Klux Klan and similar groups), and Northern political will
to sustain federal enforcement gradually eroded. The disputed 1876 presidential election
was resolved by the Compromise of 1877, which withdrew remaining federal troops from the
South in exchange for Rutherford B. Hayes's inauguration — commonly treated as the formal
end of Reconstruction — after which Southern state governments progressively dismantled
Black political and civil rights, culminating in the Jim Crow segregation system that
endured for nearly a century.

## Historiography

Reconstruction's historiography has shifted dramatically: the early 20th-century "Dunning
School" portrayed it as a corrupt, vindictive failure imposed on a prostrate South; W.E.B.
Du Bois's *Black Reconstruction in America* (1935) offered an early, long-marginalized
counter-narrative emphasizing Black agency and genuine democratic achievement; and
post-1960s scholarship (notably Eric Foner's synthesis) has largely reversed the earlier
consensus, treating Reconstruction as an ambitious, ultimately overturned experiment in
interracial democracy rather than a failed imposition.
