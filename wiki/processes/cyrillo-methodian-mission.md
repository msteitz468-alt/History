# The Cyrillo-Methodian Mission

## Definition and Scope

The **Cyrillo-Methodian mission** was the Byzantine evangelisation of the Slavs led by the
brothers **Constantine (Cyril)** and **Methodius** from 863, which created a written Slavic
liturgical language and set the cultural trajectory of Orthodox Slavdom. In
[[mckitterick-ncmh-v2-1995|NCMH II]] (Shepard, Ch. 9) it is the pivotal episode in the ninth-century
Christianisation of the Slavs.

## The Mission

Responding to an appeal from **Prince Rastislav** of [[great-moravia|Great Moravia]] — who sought
missionaries independent of the Frankish (Bavarian) church — the Byzantine emperor dispatched
Constantine and Methodius, both pious Greek-speaking Slavophones, who arrived in Moravia in 863.
Constantine devised a new script (almost certainly **Glagolitic**) and the brothers translated
substantial liturgical and scriptural texts into **Slavonic**, creating the first written Slavic
literary language. They also adapted Byzantine canon law (the *Synagoge of 50 Titles*) for Moravian
conditions.

## Latin Resistance and the Move to Bulgaria

The Slavonic liturgy met fierce resistance from the **Frankish clergy**, who held that worship should
be in Latin (or only the three "sacred" languages). The brothers travelled to Rome (867), where the
papacy initially **sanctioned the Slavonic script and liturgy**; Methodius was made an archbishop. But
after Methodius's death (885) and under the unsympathetic Svatopluk, his disciples were expelled from
Moravia. They found refuge in **[[first-bulgarian-empire|Bulgaria]]**, recently converted under
[[conversion-of-boris-i-864|Boris]], where Slavonic Christianity and a Slavic book-culture took
permanent root — and where the **Cyrillic** script (named for Cyril but developed by his successors)
was elaborated.

## Significance

The mission produced the **liturgical and literary vehicle of Orthodox Slavdom**, ensuring that the
southern and (later) eastern Slavs would worship and write in a Slavic rather than Latin or Greek
idiom — one of the deepest cultural fault-lines of medieval Europe, dividing the Latin-Catholic from
the Slavonic-Orthodox world.

## Historiography

The mission is known from the Slavonic *Lives* of Cyril and Methodius (hagiographic and partisan), papal
letters, and Frankish annals. The exact form of the original script, the date and authorship of Cyrillic
versus Glagolitic, and the degree of papal endorsement are all debated (NCMH II, Shepard).
