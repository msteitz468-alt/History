# Occupation of Japan (1945–1952)

**Definition and Scope:** The Allied (primarily US) military and political occupation of Japan following unconditional surrender, August 1945 – April 1952. Directed by Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers (SCAP), Gen. Douglas MacArthur. Distinct from occupation of Germany (divided zones); Japan was a unitary occupation under US control with nominal Allied advisory council. Encompassed demobilization of Japanese armed forces, purge of militarists, constitutional rewrite, economic restructuring, war crimes accountability, and eventual reintegration as Cold War partner. Not a colonial annexation but transformative "democratization from above."

**Causal Drivers (from range_4 / Vol. 2 context):**
- 1943 Casablanca / 1945 Potsdam commitment to unconditional surrender as non-negotiable (range_4: "the avowed goal of imposing unconditional surrender on their enemies" decided in 1942 turning points).
- Material reality exposed by island hopping (Guadalcanal prototype in range_4: Japanese "material disadvantage"; Iwo/Okinawa costs demonstrated invasion price).
- Atomic bombings + Soviet entry (9 Aug 1945) as shocks breaking Japanese "decisive battle" delusion (range_4: "made two atomic bombings necessary").
- US industrial/air/sea dominance allowed imposition of terms without full invasion.
- Prewar planning (State-War-Navy Coordinating Committee) for occupation.

**Major Phases:**
- **Surrender and initial occupation (Aug–Dec 1945):** Formal surrender 2 Sep on USS Missouri. US forces land; Emperor's 15 Aug broadcast; demobilization of ~6M+ troops (overseas repatriation chaotic). SCAP issues directives; Japanese government retained but subordinate.
- **Reform peak (1945–1948):** New constitution drafted (MacArthur's "model" with Article 9 renouncing war); land reform; zaibatsu breakup; women’s suffrage; education purge; Tokyo Tribunal (1946–48). Demilitarization thorough.
- **"Reverse course" and Cold War shift (1948–1952):** With Chinese Communist victory and Korean War, US prioritizes Japanese economic recovery and limited rearmament over radical reform. Dodge Plan austerity; San Francisco Peace Treaty (1951, effective 1952) ends occupation; US-Japan Security Treaty signed.
- **End conditions:** Treaty of San Francisco restores sovereignty (except Okinawa/Ryukyus under US admin until 1972); Japan re-emerges as US ally.

**Geographic Spread:** Primarily Home Islands (Honshu, Kyushu, Shikoku, Hokkaido); limited in former colonies (Korea/Taiwan independence; Manchuria to Soviets/China). US bases retained strategically.

**Interaction with other processes:**
- **Strategic bombing and island hopping (Vol. 2 Ch.5):** Preconditions — destruction of Japanese merchant fleet, cities, and military (range_4 Guadalcanal onward) made resistance impossible.
- **Demobilization (Ch.6 Waddell):** Massive, rapid; millions returned; veterans reintegrated under new order. GI Bill parallel in Japan via reforms.
- **Cold War onset:** Occupation transforms from punitive/democratic experiment to anti-communist bulwark.
- **War crimes / historiography:** Tokyo trials (parallel Nuremberg) addressed leadership responsibility.

**End Conditions / Transformation:** Occupation ended 1952 with Japan a parliamentary democracy under US security umbrella. "Peace Constitution" endured; Self-Defense Forces created 1954 as limited rearmament. Economic "miracle" followed. Range_4 / Ch.6 frames assessment of victory: unconditional surrender achieved, Axis threat eliminated, but new global (Cold War) challenges emerged immediately. Japanese "will" and discipline redirected from militarism to recovery.

**Historiography Note (per CLAUDE.md and Vol. 2):** US sources (SCAP reports, MacArthur memoirs) emphasize successful "democratization." Revisionist critiques (e.g., on zaibatsu continuity, Emperor retention, atomic necessity) debate continuity vs. rupture. Japanese scholarship covers civilian suffering and "victim consciousness." Vol. 2 (Waddell Ch.6) provides operational assessment of demobilization success and "assessing victory" — material superiority + atomic enforced terms; occupation as capstone to Pacific war. Collection coverage strong on US operational side; weaker on Japanese societal experience and long-term legacy. Note US-centric bias in West Point account.
