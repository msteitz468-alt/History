# Out-of-Africa Migrations

Umbrella term for the successive waves of hominin dispersal out of the African continent into Eurasia and, eventually, the rest of the inhabited world. The wiki treats these as (at least) two major, chronologically distinct episodes: an early dispersal by archaic hominins (*Homo erectus* and related forms), beginning roughly 1.8 million years ago, that populated much of Eurasia over subsequent hundreds of thousands of years; and a later dispersal of anatomically and behaviorally modern *Homo sapiens*, beginning roughly 60,000 years ago, that ultimately replaced or absorbed earlier hominin populations across Eurasia, Australasia, and eventually the Americas.

## Definition and Scope

This process is not a single migration event but a recurring pattern of range expansion out of the African homeland where hominin evolution took place, driven by population pressure, climatic opportunity (interglacial "green corridors" through the Sahara and Levant), and — in the case of the later, modern-human dispersal — enhanced behavioral and technological capacities. It does not include subsequent internal migrations within Eurasia, the Americas, or Oceania, which are treated as separate processes.

## Causal Drivers

Climatic oscillation opening and closing dispersal corridors (particularly through the Sinai/Levant and periodically across the Sahara); population growth pressing against carrying capacity in core African regions; for the later dispersal, the emergence of modern human cognitive and technological capacities enabling adaptation to new environments.

## Major Phases

The two principal, well-evidenced phases are documented on dedicated event pages: the earlier *Homo erectus*-era dispersal (~1.8 million BP) and the later anatomically-modern-human dispersal (~60,000 BP), the latter now understood (via genetic evidence) to be the primary ancestral source of all non-African human populations today, with limited but real interbreeding with archaic Eurasian hominins (Neanderthals, Denisovans) encountered along the way.

## Geographic Spread

From the African homeland into the Levant, across Eurasia (reaching East Asia and eventually Sahul/Australia), and much later into the Americas via Beringia — the eventual, near-total peopling of the habitable world.

## Interaction

Closely tied to the broader question of human origins and the emergence of behavioral modernity in Africa; genetically and archaeologically entangled with the fate of archaic Eurasian hominin populations encountered and eventually replaced or partially absorbed during the dispersal.

## End Conditions

The process is generally considered to conclude, in its primary phase, with the peopling of Sahul (Australia/New Guinea) and, much later, the Americas — after which subsequent human movement is treated as internal migration rather than an "out of Africa" dispersal.
