# Third Intermediate Period (Egypt)

The era of political fragmentation and foreign-origin rule between the
[[new-kingdom-egypt|New Kingdom]] and the [[saite-egypt|Saite]] reunification — the
**21st–25th Dynasties** (c.1069–664 BCE). As John Taylor argues in the
[[sources/shaw-oxford-history-ancient-egypt-2000|Oxford History]], the label "intermediate"
is misleading: "the period as a whole was stable and represents far more than a temporary
lapse from traditional pharaonic rule."

## Definition and scope

Three structural phases:

1. **21st Dynasty (theocracy)** — power split between the Tanite kings at
   [[places/tanis|Tanis]] in the Delta and the Theban generals/high priests of
   [[amun-priesthood|Amun]], who ruled the south in the god's name (Amun's will conveyed by
   **oracle**; see [[concepts/divine-kingship]]).
2. **22nd–24th Dynasties (Libyan rule)** — the [[actors/libyan-dynasties-egypt|Libyan
   dynasties]] founded by [[actors/sheshonq-i|Sheshonq I]], with progressive fragmentation into
   a "federation of semi-autonomous rulers."
3. **25th Dynasty (Kushite)** — the Nubian conquest under [[actors/piye|Piye]] and
   [[actors/taharqa|Taharqa]], ending in the catastrophic confrontation with Assyria.

## Causal drivers and character

A "substantial influx of non-Egyptians (Libyans and Nubians) permanently modified the profile
of the population." The Libyan north organized itself as a **chiefdom-federation** —
tolerating "two or more 'kings' simultaneously, each entitled 'king of Upper and Lower
Egypt'," retaining Libyan names and chiefly markers (the feather in the hair) and long oral
**genealogies** rare in earlier Egyptian texts. Power decentralized as kings appointed their
own sons to the Theban high priesthood and provincial offices.

## Economy and culture

The period "began at a time of economic stress" — Levantine and African revenues much reduced
— but agricultural surplus persisted, and **artistic standards stayed high**: superb silver
coffins ([[places/tanis|Tanis]]), faience, and the earliest large **hollow-cast bronze**
figures, which "were to serve as important influences on early Greek bronzeworking." The
north–south divide is legible even in script: demotic evolved in the north while Thebes kept
"abnormal hieratic." Two cultural currents that intensify here — [[concepts/gods-wife-of-amun|the
celibate God's Wife of Amun]] and **archaism** (revival of Old/Middle Kingdom models) — carry
through into the Saite age.

## End conditions

The Assyrian invasions broke Kushite Egypt: after the [[events/sack-of-thebes-663bce|sack
of Thebes in 663 BCE]], the Assyrian vassal **Psamtek I of Sais** exploited the vacuum to
reunify the country, opening the [[saite-egypt|Saite 26th Dynasty]] and the
[[actors/late-period-egypt|Late Period]].
