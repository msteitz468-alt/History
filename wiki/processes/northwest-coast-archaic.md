# Northwest Coast Archaic

## Definition and Scope

Holocene forager trajectory of the **Northwest Coast** (California–Oregon border through SE Alaska): technically hunter-gatherers despite spectacular post-contact art and architecture; ≥6 language families. Temperate rainforest (hemlock, cedar, Sitka spruce) maps the culture area; massive salmon, herring, smelt, eulachon, shellfish, sea mammals. Part of [[paleoindian-and-archaic-north-america]]; contrasts with [[california-archaic]] paths to density/complexity.

## Causal Drivers

- Anadromous fish and marine mammal abundance
- Cedar planks for houses and dugout war canoes
- Dual ancestral streams: Paleoarctic microblade + Plateau side-notched points
- Material wealth as stratification marker without political centralization of chiefdoms

## Major Phases

- Archaic in place by at least **7700 B.C.** from (1) Paleoarctic microblade hunters and (2) interior Plateau side-notched points.
- Merged into general Northwest Coast development after **3500 B.C.** with large shell middens and bone/ground stone.
- By **A.D. 1**: labrets, stone clubs, ground-slate points; cedar technology elaborated.
- By **A.D. 500**: “strong regional tendency toward social stratification and the use of material wealth to mark it,” yet “the importance of trade was not great enough to lead to the emergence of politically centralized chiefdoms” (uniform resources; geography over exchange).

## Geographic Spread

Coastal strip CA/OR border to SE Alaska; interior links to [[plateau-archaic]].

## Interaction

`part_of: [[paleoindian-and-archaic-north-america]]` · `contrasts_with: [[california-archaic]]` · related to Arctic maritime sequences farther north

## End Conditions

Stratified forager societies continue into contact (potlatch, ranked houses — historic ethnography); colonial transformation beyond Snow ch. 3.
