# Canaanite Civilization (the Bronze Age Levant)

The **urban culture of the Syro-Palestinian coast and hinterland** through the
[[middle-bronze-age|Middle]] and [[late-bronze-age|Late]] Bronze Ages — the world of
walled **Canaanite city-states** that the Egyptians called **Retenu** and the
Akkadians **Kinakhna** (Canaan, "purple-dye land"). Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (Chs. III & XI, Kenyon; Ch. X, Drower).

## Definition and Scope

"Canaanite" is, Kenyon stresses, a **cultural, not an ethnic, term**: the population
was predominantly West-Semitic ([[amorites|Amorite]]/Canaanite), with [[hurrians|
Hurrian]] elements in the north and Aegean traders on the coast. The civilization
runs as a **single cultural continuum** "from Ras Shamra in the north to Tell
el-'Ajjul in the south," and as an **unbroken developmental sequence** from the
Middle Bronze through the Late Bronze, with the pottery the clearest tracer.

## Causal Drivers

It emerged from the **fusion** of the old Early Bronze urban tradition with the
revitalizing [[amorites|Amorite]] ("E.B.–M.B. intermediate") nomadic intrusion. After
that non-urban interlude, towns reappear sharply ~1900 BCE — a **second urbanization**
that Kenyon traces to small **infiltrating groups**, not an invasion. The likely
hearth and conduit is **[[byblos|Byblos]]**, whose metalwork and metal-imitating
red-burnished pottery are the typological ancestors of the new Canaanite ceramic
repertoire; from the coast the culture spread inland and south.

## Major Phases

- **Middle Bronze (MB I–II, ~1900–1550):** the network of small **walled city-states**
  — Jericho (~7 acres), Megiddo (~13), **Hazor** (~182 at its peak) — close-packed,
  unplanned, with massive **plaster-glacis ramparts** (Jericho's great rampart). The
  age was materially modest: little wealth, little foreign trade. The
  **[[hyksos|Hyksos]]** period in Egypt falls within MB II, but the culture is *not*
  "Hyksos."
- **Late Bronze (~1550–1200):** the same towns, now drawn into the **Egyptian
  empire** and the [[interpolity-law|international system]] — a sharp rise in imported
  **Cypriot and Mycenaean** pottery and in gold and ivory wealth (Megiddo, Tell
  el-'Ajjul). The Syrian cities ([[ugarit|Ugarit]], [[alalakh|Alalakh]], Qatna,
  Qadesh, Tunip, Amurru) are caught between [[new-kingdom-egypt|Egypt]],
  [[mitanni|Mitanni]], and the [[hittites|Hittites]], their feuds and pleas preserved
  in the [[unknown|Amarna letters]].

## Geographic Spread and Interaction

Two cultural spheres are visible from the start — **coastal Syria** (Ugarit,
Byblos) and **inland Syria** (Qatna), the latter marked by band-painted pottery —
linked by trade and migration to **Palestine**. The whole zone was the **land-bridge**
of the Bronze Age, fought over by every great power and threaded by the caravan and
maritime routes that carried tin, copper, timber, and Aegean goods.

## End Conditions

The Bronze Age Canaanite world was shattered in the **~1200 BCE [[unknown|Sea
Peoples]] disruption** that destroyed [[ugarit|Ugarit]] and many inland towns (the
[[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]]). But the culture did not vanish: it continued into
the **Phoenician** city-states of the coast and the emergent **Israelite** highlands
of the Iron Age.

## Significance

Canaan's enduring gift to world history is the **alphabet** — the West-Semitic
consonantal script (seen at Ugarit and in the Proto-Sinaitic/Proto-Canaanite
inscriptions) that is the ancestor of the Phoenician, Greek, and ultimately all
Western alphabets — and the **literature** (the Ugaritic Baal Cycle and its kin) that
forms the background of the Hebrew Bible. The Canaanite city-state is also the basic
political unit of the [[interpolity-law|Amarna international system]].
