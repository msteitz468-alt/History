# The Great Purge
## Definition and Scope
The **Great Purge**, also known as the Great Terror or the **Ezhovshchina** (after NKVD chief Nikolai Ezhov), was a massive campaign of political repression and terror in the Soviet Union from late 1936 to late 1938. Directed by Joseph Stalin, the purge targeted the Communist Party leadership, government bureaucrats, the Red Army officer corps, and the general population. It resulted in the arrest of at least eight million people, widespread executions, and the expansion of the forced-labor camp system (the Gulag), establishing Stalin's absolute personal dictatorship. It was in this volatile situation that the engine of a Great Terror was cranked up and set in motion... None the less the Great Terror would not have taken place but for Stalin’s personality and ideas. He it was who directed the state’s punitive machinery against all those whom he identified as ‘anti-Soviet elements’ and ‘enemies of the people’ (Service, lines 1644, 1648).

## Causal Drivers
- **Stalin's Paranoia and Personality:** Stalin had a scarily odd personality. He was in his element amidst chaos and violence... His hyper-suspicious, imperious temperament came to the fore... He was unflustered about murder (lines 1730). He was fascinated by Genghis Khan... ‘The deaths of the vanquished are necessary for the tranquillity of the victors.’... ‘One of Ivan the Terrible’s mistakes was to overlook the five great feudal families... He ought to have been still more decisive!’ (lines 1732, 1734). Among his purposes was a desire to use his victims as scapegoats for the country’s pain; and in order to sustain his mode of industrialization he also needed to keep his mines, timber forests and construction sites constantly supplied with slave labour. It was probably also his intention to take pre-emptive measures against any ‘fifth column’ (line 1648).
- **Consolidation of Power and Institutional Rivalries:** Stalin sought to eliminate the "Old Bolsheviks" and replace them with a new, younger generation... He had broken the party as an independent, supreme political agency... Stalin had succeeded in elevating himself above party, people’s commissariats, army, trade unions and police (lines 1768). The central leadership was like a gang... The penalty for disagreement with Stalin was constant: ‘seven grams of lead’ in the head (line 1824).
- **Preparing for War and Pretext:** With the rise of Nazi Germany... Stalin wanted to eliminate any potential "fifth column"... The assassination of Leningrad Party boss Sergei Kirov... On 1 December 1934 an astonishing event triggered an upward ratcheting... Leonid Nikolaev... shot him. Stalin exploited the assassination as a pretext to rush through a set of decrees granting full authority to the NKVD to arrest, try and execute at will (lines 1648, 1668).

## Major Phases
- **Prelude and 17th Congress (1934):** The Seventeenth Congress... ‘Congress of Victors’... Stalin in his speech... ‘Consequently it is necessary not to sing lullabies to the party but to develop its vigilance...’ (lines 1658, 1660). On 1 December 1934... Kirov... shot. Stalin first took revenge upon Zinoviev and Kamenev... consigned to ten and five years... 663 past supporters of Zinoviev in Leningrad were seized... (lines 1668, 1670). Another clear-out... by May 1935, 281,872 persons had ceased being Bolsheviks (line 1672).
- **Show Trials (1936–1938):** On 29 June 1936, a secret letter... ‘the terrorist activities of the Trotskyist-Zinovievite counter-revolutionary bloc’. In August 1936, Kamenev and Zinoviev were dragged from their cells and re-tried... The defendants were privately threatened with the death sentence unless they ‘confessed’... The court condemned them to death and sentence was carried out early next morning. This was the first execution of anyone who had belonged to the Party Central Committee (lines 1686). In 1937 Pyatakov and Radek... Pyatakov was shot and Radek sent to a labour camp. In February, unhinged by Pyatakov’s execution, Ordzhonikidze shot himself – or possibly he was murdered on Stalin’s orders (line 1692). Bukharin, who was put on show-trial in March 1938... (line 1714).
- **Military Purge and Ezhovshchina (1937):** Marshal Tukhachevski... and several high-ranking commanders were arrested in May and beaten into confessing to plotting a coup d’état... they were shot in mid-June. On the same occasion he announced that Bukharin, Tomski and Rykov were guilty of espionage (line 1700). On 2 July, at Stalin’s instigation, the Politburo passed a resolution ‘On Anti-Soviet Elements’, and Yezhov scuttled back... scheme for the NKVD to arrest 259,450 persons over the following four months. In mid-August 1937 torture was sanctioned as a normal procedure of interrogation in Soviet prisons. The Great Terror was raging. It did not cease until the end of 1938 (line 1704). Victims were tried by trios (troiki)... The quota system was applied... 72,950 of victims – twenty-eight per cent – should be shot... (line 1708).
- **Scale and Methods:** According to official central records, 681,692 persons were executed in 1937–8. This may well be an underestimate, but the total number of deaths caused by repression in general was anyway much higher... Between one million and one and a half million persons... were killed by firing squad, physical maltreatment or massive over-work in the care of the NKVD in those two years alone (line 1710). Vans and lorries marked ‘Meat’ or ‘Vegetables’ could carry the victims... Cattle-trucks were commandeered for journeys to the labour camps of the Gulag... (line 1716). By 1939 the total number of prisoners... was 2.9 million (line 1720).
- **De-escalation (1938–):** On 19 November 1938, when Yezhov unexpectedly resigned... Yezhov was arrested in April 1939 and executed in the following February (line 1762). Beria, when replacing Yezhov, entered the small circle of Stalin’s close advisers (line 1768).

## Geographic Spread
The purge affected every union republic and administrative region of the USSR... In searching out ‘anti-Soviet elements’, troiki were enjoined to capture escaped kulaks, ex-Mensheviks, ex-Socialist-Revolutionaries, priests... As the Great Terror was intensified... it was applied to virtually anyone who had been active in or sympathetic to a communist oppositionist faction (line 1708). Stalin resumed and expanded the policy of national deportations. Especially vulnerable were national and ethnic groups... The Poles were removed from Soviet Ukraine by a secret decree of April 1936... the Kurds... the Koreans from eastern Siberia. Uninhabited tracts of Kazakhstan became a dumping ground (line 1724).

## Interaction
- **With the Nomenklatura and Institutions:** Only one in thirty delegates to the Seventeenth Party Congress in 1934 returned to the Eighteenth Congress in 1939. The loss from the Central Committee was also drastic: just sixteen out of seventy-one members survived. Another devastated institution was the Red Army. Tens of thousands of officers fell... including fifteen out of the sixteen army commanders (line 1722). The blood-purge of the armed forces disrupted the USSR’s defences... The arrest of the economic administrators... The destruction of cadres in party, trade unions and local government undermined administrative co-ordination (line 1728).
- **With Society and Gulag:** Even a harmless old Russian peasant woman muttering dissatisfaction with conditions in the kolkhoz or her young worker-son blurting out complaints about housing standards would be dispatched to the horrors of the Gulag... Casual jokes against Stalin... were treated as the most heinous form of treason (line 1726). In each camp there were gangs of convicted thieves who were allowed by the authorities to bully the ‘politicals’... Alexander Solzhenitsyn... wrote that experience of the camps could ennoble... More typical... it was every person for himself (line 1720).
- **With Stalin's Rule:** Stalin had engineered a second revolution... But his victory was not yet totally secure... Stalin had tried to root out every possible challenge... His victory was not yet totally secure. For Stalin, the realization of the First Five-Year Plan could only be the first victory in the long campaign for his personal dictatorship (lines 1510, 1506).

## End Conditions
The active phase of the Great Purge quieted down by late 1938... Stalin had succeeded in elevating himself above party... (line 1768). The Great Terror followed the pattern of state economic planning since 1928: central direction was accompanied by opportunities for much local initiative... Old scores were murderously paid off (line 1746). The convulsion... had the severest consequences... This extreme destabilization endangered Stalin himself... his reaction was hysterically out of proportion to the menace he faced (line 1728). Yet Stalin’s very success brought about a crisis of its own... he had started the carnage of 1937–8 because of real hostility... Yet his reaction was hysterically out of proportion (line 1728). By 1939... Stalin had succeeded in elevating himself above party, people’s commissariats, army, trade unions and police (line 1768). The purge succeeded in creating a highly centralized, terrorized state structure that obeyed Stalin implicitly.
