## Definition and Scope

This process page tracks the emergence and Last Glacial Maximum-era homelands of
Africa's four major language families, as reconstructed by Christopher Ehret
(CWH Vol. I, Chs. 14–15) through the combination of archaeology, genetics, and
**historical linguistics** (the comparative reconstruction of ancient "lexicons
of culture" — proving that an ancient root word for an item of culture existed at
a particular era shows the speakers of that ancestral language knew the item).

This is one of the rare domains where deep African history can be reconstructed in
detail despite the scarcity of textual sources — a direct rebuttal to the
assumption that pre-literate African history is unrecoverable. It is therefore
central to filling the collection's flagged critical gap on Sub-Saharan Africa.

## The Four Families and Their Homelands (c. 16,000–15,000 BCE)

| Family | LGM-era homeland | Associated Later Stone Age complex | Kinship | Religion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Khoesan** | Eastern/southern Africa | Eastern African Microlithic | Iroquois-type cousin terminology; bilateral | Shamanism (trance, rock art) |
| **Niger-Kordofanian** (incl. Niger-Congo) | Just west of the Nile / South Sudan | (spread west later) | Matrilineal clans/lineages | Ancestor veneration; territorial spirits; a transcendent Creator God |
| **Nilo-Saharan** | Between the Nile and W. edges of Ethiopian highlands | (middle/upper Nile) | Matrilineal clans; Iroquois cousin terms; cross-cousin marriage | Shamanism |
| **Afrasian** (Afroasiatic) | Horn of Africa (Ethiopian highlands) — **not** the Near East | (Red Sea hills / Horn) | Clans (matri- or patrilineal uncertain) | Henotheism (clan deities) |

The three families Nilo-Saharan, Afrasian, and Niger-Kordofanian clustered in
northeastern Africa near the headwaters of the Nile during the Last Glacial
Maximum — a region of relatively high rainfall and permanent water that served as
a population refugium during the arid LGM. East Africa, where all three deep
genetic lineages of humanity anciently overlapped, was the homeland of the
Khoesan-speaking Eastern African Microlithic.

## Kinship Systems (Ehret's reconstruction)

- **Khoesan**: retained the ancient "Iroquois" cousin terminology (parallel
  cousins = siblings; cross cousins distinguished) — see [[paleolithic-foraging-lifeways]].
  Notably called mother's brother by the same term as grandfather, and father's
  sister by the same term as grandmother.
- **Niger-Kordofanian and Nilo-Saharan**: developed deeper unilineal kinship —
  matrilineal lineages and clans (descent reckoned through the mother). These may
  have grown directly out of the earlier female-coalition social organization. A
  shared idiom: "blood comes from one's mother" (one is a literal blood descendant
  of one's mother but not one's father), surviving among the Uduk (Nilo-Saharan)
  and Akan of Ghana (Niger-Kordofanian).
- **Afrasian**: organized around clans, each owing allegiance to a particular
  deity.

## Religious Systems

- **Shamanism** (Khoesan, Nilo-Saharan): the inherited Paleolithic religion of
  trance and spirit mediation (see [[shamanism-paleolithic-religion]]); attested
  by the Khoesan rock art tradition (e.g., Game Pass, KwaZulu-Natal) carried on by
  Sandawe artists into the 20th century. The Batwa cultural complex of the Congo
  Basin had a distinct rock art tradition of geometric weather-signs, used for
  rain-making ritual.
- **Henotheism** (Afrasian): each clan or alliance of clans had its own deity to
  whom it owed allegiance, while accepting that other gods existed (the gods of
  other clans). This conception survived among Omotic-speakers of SW Ethiopia into
  the 20th century. **Ehret argues it lies behind pre-dynastic Egyptian religion
  (each nome with its god) and the origin of Yahweh** — originally just such a
  clan/tribal god of the ancient Israelites ("Thou shalt have no other gods before
  me" presumes other gods exist). The ancient Afrasian root for this kind of deity
  is *\*netl'-*; the ancient Egyptian word for "god," *ntr*, derives from it.
- **Ancestor veneration** (Niger-Kordofanian/Niger-Congo): the ancestral spirits
  of the clan/lineage were the primary focus; territorial spirits and a single
  transcendent Creator God developed early as well. Ehret stresses this is
  *specifically* a Niger-Congo tradition, not (as often assumed) typical of "all
  Africans."

## Holocene Dispersals (the process's outflow)

From ~12,700 BCE (Bølling-Allerød warming) and especially from ~9500 BCE
(Holocene onset), these families began to disperse from their LGM homelands:
- Nilo-Saharan speakers (Koman, Central Sudanian, Northern Sudanian) spread along
  the middle Nile and into newly reclaimed Saharan lands.
- Afrasian/North Erythraic speakers spread north from the Horn toward Egypt
  (~15,000 BCE), carrying wild-grain collecting; Semitic (the only Afrasian branch
  outside Africa) was carried into the Levant by Mushabian communities; Berber/
  Capsian speakers spread west across North Africa (~8500 BCE).
- Niger-Kordofanian (Western Niger-Congo) spread west toward West Africa
  (12,000–11,000 BCE), replacing the late-persisting Middle Stone Age there.

These dispersals set up the linguistic geography that the later Bantu expansion
and Holocene African history build upon. (The Bantu expansion itself, a Niger-
Congo sub-branch phenomenon, post-dates this volume's scope.)

## Method and Limitations

- Historical-linguistic reconstruction of "lexicons of culture" is Ehret's
  signature method (see his *History and the Testimony of Language*, 2011). It is
  powerful but **contested**: deep reconstructions and the dating of proto-
  languages by glottochronology/Bayesian phylogenetics are not universally
  accepted.
- Archaeological evidence for the LGM-era homelands (especially South Sudan and
  the Horn) is scant; much rests on linguistic inference.
- The mapping of language families onto archaeological complexes (e.g., Khoesan =
  Eastern African Microlithic) is a hypothesis, not direct evidence.
