# The Peopling of East Asia

The long demographic process by which hominins, and then anatomically modern humans, occupied the
China Mainland, the Korean Peninsula and the Japanese Islands. [[barnes-archaeology-east-asia-2015|Barnes]]
frames it as **two great waves out of Africa** overprinted by the glacial geography of what she calls
"East Asialand" — the northeastern landmass exposed at low sea-stand.

## Out of Africa 1 — *Homo erectus*

The earliest accepted human colonisation of East Asia is **~1.66 mya** at sites in the **Nihewan
Basin**, north China. *Homo erectus* (defined *sensu stricto* from Java and [[zhoukoudian|Zhoukoudian]])
spread across the Mainland; contested early claims include Xihoudu (redated by palaeomagnetism to 1.37
mya) and the Yuanmou teeth (1.7 mya, disputed). The consensus is that **only *H. erectus*** reached
eastern Asia in the Early Pleistocene. Dating rests on **palaeomagnetism** (Brunhes/Matuyama chrons)
and **loess stratigraphy** correlated to the marine-isotope curve — the Baoji loess, up to 180 m
thick, records 166 climatic events over 2.5 myr. The **Movius Line** marks the easternmost limit of
Acheulean handaxes; East Asian handaxe clusters (Bose Basin; the Imjin/Hantan basin's **Chongokni**,
≥350 kya, on the Korean Peninsula) are read as **convergent**, independently invented, and helped
discredit Movius's claim of a technologically backward East.

## Out of Africa 2 — *Homo sapiens*

Anatomically modern humans evolved in Africa from ~200 kya; the dispersal ancestral to all
non-Africans crossed the Red Sea ~72 kya. Modern humans likely reached East Asia **60–50 kya** via a
southern/"beachcomber" route — the greatest mtDNA diversity is in **southwestern China**, supporting a
south-to-north colonisation. Securely dated modern remains: **Tianyuan Cave** (near Zhoukoudian)
42–39 cal. kya; the Zhoukoudian **Upper Cave** 29–24 kya; Okinawan island finds 35–16 kya. Chinese
"archaic *H. sapiens*" fossils (Maba, Dali, Jinniushan) feed the **multiregional-continuity** reading
that the consensus model rejects, allowing only brief interbreeding (Neanderthal genes reached the
Altai and the modern Chinese gene pool).

## Reaching Korea and Japan

Korea has Palaeolithic stone tools at 100+ sites from the Middle Pleistocene on, but few well-dated
Upper Palaeolithic sites. **Japan's Palaeolithic was thrown into crisis in 2000** when a 25-year
fraud at 186 "sites" (planting tools under datable tephra to push occupation to 700 kya) was exposed;
the Japanese record is now accepted only **after ~40 kya**. New **prepared-core technologies** — blades
from Siberia/Mongolia after 43 kya (the Shuidonggou type site), the andesite **Setouchi technique** of
western Honshu, and **microblades** (Korea ~28–26 kya; Hokkaido ~23 kya, possibly linked to the
peopling of the Americas) — track these later movements. Glacial sea-level lowstands (−120 m at
23–19 kya) exposed the "Yellow Plain" and "Seto Plain" and bridged the Tsugaru Strait; the post-19-kya
rise drowned countless coastal sites.

## The Seam into the Holocene

Barnes argues the formal **Palaeolithic/Neolithic boundary is no longer valid** in East Asia: edge-
ground tools (224 Japanese sites, 38–32 cal. kya), nut and plant processing, coastal fishing, and —
decisively — **the world's earliest pottery** all appear in the Late Pleistocene. Ceramics are now
attributed first to **south China** (Xianrendong, 21.7–19.3 cal. kya, within the LGM), spreading to
the Russian Far East and Japan ~17–14 kya (Incipient [[jomon-culture|Jomon]]); lipid analysis shows
the earliest pots boiled aquatic foods, not plants. Barnes holds that **pottery diffused east-to-west**
across Eurasia — the opposite direction to blade technology — by "idea diffusion" rather than
migration of potters. From this seam flow the divergent Holocene trajectories: Mainland
[[early-agriculture-china|cereal farming]] and Pen/Insular [[early-agriculture-japan|foraging
husbandry]].
