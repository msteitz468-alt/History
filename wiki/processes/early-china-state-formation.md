# Early China State Formation

## Definition and Scope

This page tracks the **independent emergence of the first state in China**, in the middle and
lower [[yellow-river-valley|Yellow River valley]], from the first Neolithic social hierarchies
(~4000 BC) to the consolidated [[erlitou-culture|Erlitou]] state (~1800–1500 BC). It is the
central process of [[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's *The Chinese Neolithic*]], whose
thesis is that this was a **trajectory, not a ladder**: social change was driven by **multiple
interacting factors in no predetermined sequence**, with both development and decline, rather
than a unilineal ascent. China is one of the world's few **pristine (primary) states**;
compare [[mesoamerican-state-formation]] and [[andean-state-formation]].

## Causal Drivers

Liu's variables, none individually sufficient:

- **Environmental circumscription** (Carneiro): mountain/river-bounded basins concentrated
  population and intensified competition, producing the most integrated polities (highland
  [[taosi|Taosi]], western Henan).
- **Yellow River instability**: at least two prehistoric course changes and recurrent
  flooding triggered **population movements** and competition for arable land; a course change
  ~2600 BC removed the barrier between Henan and Shandong, enabling broad late-Longshan
  interaction; flooding ~2000 BC may have given **charismatic crisis-managers** (the Yu
  archetype) an opening.
- **Factional competition and feasting**: from the mid-[[yangshao-culture|Yangshao]] onward,
  households and kin groups competed through ritual feasting; at Erlitou this scaled up into
  **state ritual** using bronze vessels.
- **Craft specialisation and prestige-goods exchange**: control of scarce resources (cinnabar,
  jade, later bronze) and long-distance elite exchange built and bound hierarchies — but
  network/prestige-goods economies were **unstable**, explaining the cyclical collapses.
- **Regional interaction and warfare**: intensifying conflict (arrowheads, scalping, human
  sacrifice, rammed-earth walls) accompanied the late Neolithic throughout.

## Major Phases

1. **First hierarchy (mid-Yangshao, ~4000–3500 BC):** a three-tier settlement system, a large
   ritual building, and cinnabar-producing feasting households in western Henan — the earliest
   clear social hierarchy.
2. **Eastern mortuary stratification ([[dawenkou-culture|Dawenkou]], ~3500–2600 BC):** ranked,
   richly furnished graves and the first hereditary status on the lower river.
3. **[[longshan-culture|Longshan]] chiefdoms (~2600–2000 BC):** dense competing polities,
   walled towns, the most complex chiefdom at Taosi (~3,300 km², primate curve) — but **cyclical
   rise and collapse**, no durable ruling apparatus.
4. **State emergence ([[erlitou-culture|Erlitou]], ~1800 BC):** a four-tier, primate-center
   system in the [[yiluo-basin|Yiluo basin]] with a palatial core, attached bronze production,
   and territorial outposts — the first internally specialised administration.

The developmental table runs: Yangshao (2–3 tiers, ~230–310 km²) → multi-centered Longshan
(2–3 tiers, ~1,250–1,520 km²) → Taosi (3 tiers, ~3,300 km²) → Erlitou (4 tiers, ~5,689 km²,
54,000–82,000 people).

## Interaction and the Key Inversion

The decisive finding is **non-linear**: the first state emerged **not** from the most complex
chiefdom (Taosi, which collapsed entirely, becoming Erlitou's periphery) but from the **less
integrated, corporate, group-oriented competing systems** of central Henan and the Yiluo
basin. Cultural **decline** in some regions (Shandong, central Shaanxi's "diminished
chiefdoms," the Taosi highlands) may even have **facilitated** development at the Central
Plains focal areas — "a system of interacting processes," not a single unified one. The state
was thus **created, not evolved** (Flannery): one polity absorbing its neighbours and adding
**delegated decision making** (Spencer) to break the chiefdom's ~20 km logistical ceiling.

## End Conditions

The process culminates in the Erlitou [[territorial-state|territorial state]] and continues
into the [[shang-dynasty|Shang]]; the contemporary **Proto-Shang** (Xiaqiyuan) remained a
system of competing chiefdoms. Whether Erlitou equals the [[xia-dynasty|Xia]] is disputed
([[xia-dynasty-historicity]]).

The narrative-survey complement to Liu's settlement model is
[[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook (2017)]], who likewise prefer the neutral
**"Erlitou Period"** to "Xia" and stress the **plurality** of the
[[east-asian-heartland-region|Heartland]] (the alien [[sanxingdui|Sanxingdui]] bronze culture)
against any single Central-Plains origin — the same anti-monocentric thrust as
[[origins-chinese-civilization-monocentric-vs-multiregional|the origins debate]].

## Barnes: definitional plurality, the southern path, and climatic collapse

[[barnes-archaeology-east-asia-2015|Barnes (2015)]] reinforces the non-linear reading while flagging how
**unsettled the definition of "the state" is**: social stratification, administrative artifacts, a
four-tier hierarchy, and the traditional Chinese/Korean criterion (a ruler within walls, *cheng* 城 →
*guo* 國) "do not always match up," so she labels the Late Neolithic (3300–2000 BC) as **"polity
formation… chiefdoms or incipient states."** She widens the picture beyond the Yellow River with the
**southern powerhouse of [[liangzhu-culture|Liangzhu]]** — stratified into aristocrats and commoners
from its founding, with the 10-m **Mojiaoshan** terrace, the jade-rich Fanshan cemetery (1,100 jades),
and a 290-ha late enclosure — as a parallel, jade-based path that, like [[taosi|Taosi]], **collapsed
rather than maturing into a state**. Crucially she presses the **4.2 kya abrupt climate event** as the
synchronous trigger of Late Neolithic collapse across regions (Longshan, Liangzhu and Shijiahe, with
flood-sands over Liangzhu paddies at Maoshan as direct evidence) — "social explanations cannot account
for the synchronicity of collapse… at a large regional scale." **Source:**
[[barnes-archaeology-east-asia-2015]].
