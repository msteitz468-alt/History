# Japanese Imperialism (1874–1945)

The expansion of [[japan|Japan]] into a colonial and then would-be hegemonic empire — from
the [[meiji-period|Meiji]] acquisition of Taiwan and Korea to the wartime **Greater East
Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere** and its collapse in 1945. From Mason & Caiger's
[[mason-caiger-history-of-japan|*A History of Japan*]] (chs 15–17).

## Definition and drivers

Having achieved [[meiji-restoration-1868|modernization]], Japan pursued empire for
intertwined motives: **security** (controlling the Korean "dagger pointed at Japan" and the
Manchurian buffer), **resources and markets** for its industry and growing population,
**emulation** (an overseas empire as "a badge of national greatness"), and increasingly an
**ultranationalist ideology** (the [[shinto|kokutai]], the mission of a "new moral order in
East Asia"). Mason and Caiger note the Meiji statesmen often **reacted to events** rather
than following a master plan, vacillating and pushed by a public "several times more
bellicose" than the government.

## Major Phases

- **Defining the frontiers (1870s–90s).** The **Taiwan expedition (1874)**; the
  Sakhalin–Kurile exchange with Russia (1875); incorporation of **Okinawa (1879)**; the
  Korea treaty of Kanghwa (1876).
- **The Sino-Japanese War (1894–95).** Victory over Qing China won **Taiwan** and a large
  indemnity (Treaty of Shimonoseki); the **Triple Intervention** (Russia, Germany, France)
  then robbed Japan of its mainland gains — a lasting grievance.
- **The [[russo-japanese-war-1904|Russo-Japanese War (1904–5)]].** The first defeat of a
  European great power by an Asian state: Japan won Port Arthur, southern Sakhalin, and
  paramountcy in Korea (**annexed 1910**) and southern Manchuria. The **Anglo-Japanese
  Alliance (1902)** confirmed great-power status.
- **The First World War & the 1920s.** As a nominal Allied power Japan seized Germany's
  Pacific islands and Shandong, pressed the **Twenty-One Demands** on China (1915), and
  intervened in Siberia.
- **The descent to total war (1931–45).** The army's seizure of **Manchuria (1931,
  Manchukuo)**; the **China War from 1937** (the **Rape of Nanking**); the proclamation of
  the **New Order / Co-Prosperity Sphere**; the move into Indochina; and the
  [[pacific-war-1941-1945|Pacific War (1941–45)]].

## Character and end

Mason and Caiger stress the gap between **rhetoric and reality**: the "Co-Prosperity
Sphere" channeled Asia's oil, rubber, and metals to Japan and imposed Japanese control,
taking "little note of local aspirations" — so that rising Asian nationalism turned against
it. Yet, paradoxically, Japan's conquests and the **humiliation of the Western colonial
powers**, followed by Japan's own defeat, **hastened the independence** of Burma, Indonesia,
Indochina, the Philippines, and Malaya. The empire ended utterly with the
[[pacific-war-1941-1945|1945 defeat]].

## Interaction

Japanese imperialism is the East Asian arm of the global age of [[imperialism]]; it
collided with Chinese and Korean nationalism, with Russian/Soviet expansion, and ultimately
with the United States — the collision that produced the Pacific War.
