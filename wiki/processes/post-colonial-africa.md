# Post-Colonial Africa

**Post-colonial Africa** — the era of the independent African states since c. 1960 — is the subject of the
later sections of the **UNESCO [[gha-vol8-since-1935|General History of Africa, Vol. VIII]]** (Sections
III–IV). Its central theme is the gap between **political independence won and economic and political
sovereignty achieved**: the predicament of building viable nations and economies on colonial foundations.

## The limits of independence: neo-colonialism

GHA VIII stresses that **political independence did not bring economic independence**. The new states
inherited **export-dependent, externally-oriented economies** (see [[colonial-economy]]) — producing primary
commodities for markets, prices, and corporations they did not control. **Nkrumah** named this
**"neo-colonialism": the last stage of imperialism** — formal sovereignty hollowed out by continued foreign
economic control. The volume surveys the **competing strategies of economic decolonization**: state-led
development, **African socialism** (Nyerere's *ujamaa* in Tanzania, Senghor, Nkrumah), capitalist
"open-door" models, and regional integration — most of which struggled against deteriorating terms of trade
and, by the 1980s, the **debt crisis** and IMF/World Bank **structural adjustment**.

## Nation-building in colonial borders

The new states faced the task of forging **nations out of the arbitrary territories** the
[[scramble-for-africa|Scramble]] had drawn — multi-ethnic, multi-religious units with weak, inherited
extractive states and a thin administrative class. GHA VIII (Mazrui, Ki-Zerbo) examines the strains:

- the early **euphoria** and developmental ambition of independence (real growth, expanded schooling and
  health) giving way to disillusion;
- the slide toward **one-party states** and **"presidents for life,"** justified as nation-building and
  development but often masking patronage and repression;
- a wave of **military coups** (over seventy in the first decades), and outright **civil wars** where
  ethnic, regional, or secessionist tensions broke open (the **Congo crisis**, **Biafra/Nigeria**
  (1967–70), Sudan, the Horn);
- the entanglement of these conflicts in the **[[cold-war|Cold War]]**, which armed and funded rival
  factions across the continent (see [[westad-cold-war|Westad]]).

## Significance

The volume's verdict (echoed in [[reader-africa-biography|Reader's]] bleaker "dreams and nightmares" coda)
is that independence restored **dignity and political agency** but left Africa wrestling with **structural
dependence** and the **mismatch between colonial states and African societies** — the defining challenges of
the post-colonial era, into which the liberation of [[apartheid|Southern Africa]] and the projects of
[[pan-africanism|Pan-Africanism]] and regional integration were the great hopeful counter-currents.
