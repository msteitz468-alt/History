# The Sexual Revolution

The **sexual revolution** was the broad liberalization of sexual attitudes and behavior in
much of the Western world (and beyond) from roughly the 1960s to the 1980s — a loosening of
norms around premarital sex, contraception, divorce, pornography, and homosexuality that
reshaped private life in the second half of the twentieth century. From
[[cambridge-world-history-of-sexualities-v4|*The Cambridge World History of Sexualities*,
Vol. 4]] and part of the wider transformation of [[modern-sexuality|modern sexuality]].

## Drivers

- **Reliable contraception** — the **oral contraceptive pill** (from 1960) decoupled sex
  from reproduction for women on a mass scale.
- **Sexual science and survey** — the **Kinsey Reports** (1948, 1953) and later research had
  already made private behavior a subject of public discussion.
- **Social movements** — **second-wave feminism** (women's right to sexual self-determination)
  and **gay liberation** (the Stonewall riots, 1969) politicized sexuality and pressed for
  rights and decriminalization.
- **Youth counterculture and affluence** — the postwar baby-boom generation, rising prosperity,
  and a weakening of traditional religious authority.

## Developments

The period saw the **decriminalization of homosexuality** in many jurisdictions, the
liberalization of **divorce, contraception, and abortion** law, the relaxation of
**censorship** (the "permissive society"), and a new public frankness about sex. It was
uneven — varying greatly by country, class, and gender — and concentrated in the urban West,
though its currents reached much of the world.

## A critical reassessment

Vol. 4 treats the revolution skeptically, "challenging liberal assumptions about agency,
liberation, and progress." The same decades that proclaimed sexual freedom saw the intense
**commodification** of sex (the marketing of cosmetics, contraception, sex toys, and
pornography — especially to and about women), which "did little to challenge gender norms"
and often **intensified** the commodification of female bodies for male visual pleasure.
The "liberation" was thus entangled with the market ("parasexuality") and provoked a durable
**conservative and religious backlash** that made sexuality a central front of the late-20th-
and 21st-century **culture wars**.

## Significance

The sexual revolution permanently altered norms of marriage, family, gender, and identity in
much of the world and laid the groundwork for later movements for reproductive and LGBTQ+
rights. But, as the volume stresses, it was neither uniform nor simply emancipatory — a
contested transformation whose gains, commodifications, and backlash all persist.
