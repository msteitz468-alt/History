# The Sexual Revolution

The **sexual revolution** was the broad liberalization of sexual attitudes and behavior in
much of the Western world (and beyond) from roughly the 1960s to the 1980s — a loosening of
norms around premarital sex, contraception, divorce, pornography, and homosexuality that
reshaped private life in the second half of the twentieth century. From
[[cambridge-world-history-of-sexualities-v4|*The Cambridge World History of Sexualities*,
Vol. 4]] and part of the wider transformation of [[modern-sexuality|modern sexuality]].

## Definition and Scope

The term names a **cluster of changes in sexual norms, law, and behavior** concentrated in
the urban West c. 1960–80: the separation of sex from reproduction and marriage, the relaxation
of censorship and sexual law, and the emergence of public movements for women's and gay
rights. It is uneven and contested — varying by country, class, and gender — and is best read
as an acceleration within the longer history of [[modern-sexuality]] rather than a single
clean break.

## Causal Drivers

- **Reliable contraception** — the **oral contraceptive pill** (from 1960) decoupled sex from
  reproduction for women at scale.
- **Sexual science and survey** — the **Kinsey Reports** (1948, 1953) had made private
  behavior a public subject.
- **Social movements** — **second-wave feminism** and **gay liberation** (Stonewall, 1969).
- **Youth counterculture and affluence**, and a weakening of traditional **religious
  authority**.

## Major Phases

1. **Onset (late 1950s–early 1960s):** the Pill, Kinsey's aftermath, early loosening of
   censorship.
2. **Peak (later 1960s–early 1970s):** the counterculture, decriminalization of homosexuality
   in many jurisdictions, liberalized divorce/contraception/abortion law, the "permissive
   society."
3. **Consolidation and reaction (mid-1970s–1980s):** institutionalized rights gains met by an
   organized conservative/religious backlash.

## Geographic Spread

Concentrated in **North America and Western Europe** and in big cities, its currents reached
much of the world unevenly, mediated by local law, religion, and politics; it was far from
uniform even within the West.

## Interaction

The revolution was entangled with the **commodification** of sex that
[[modern-sexuality|Vol. 4]] stresses (the marketing of contraception, cosmetics, and
pornography — "parasexuality"), with **feminism** and **gay liberation** (which both drove and
contested it), and with the **[[cold-war]]** culture of the affluent West. Its gains and its
commodifications advanced together, complicating any simple story of "liberation."

## End Conditions

The permissive surge slowed from the later 1970s amid **conservative and religious backlash**
(and, from the 1980s, the AIDS crisis), making sexuality a central front of the late-20th- and
21st-century **culture wars**. Its core legal and normative changes nonetheless persisted,
feeding later reproductive- and LGBTQ+-rights movements.
