# The Polonization of Lithuania
## Definition and Scope
**The Polonization of Lithuania** was the long-term process of cultural, linguistic, and political integration of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania into the Polish cultural sphere between the late fourteenth and mid-sixteenth centuries. 

Initiated by the dynastic Union of Krewo in 1385 CE, this process led to the adoption of the Polish language, Roman Catholicism, and social customs by the Lithuanian and West Russian (Ruthenian) nobility, culminating in the formal merger of the two states at the Union of Lublin in 1569 CE.

## Causal Drivers
* **Dynastic Marriage (1385 CE):** The marriage of Lithuanian Grand Duke Jagiello to Polish Queen Jadwiga established a personal union between the two crowns.
* **Conversion to Roman Catholicism (1386 CE):** Jagiello's rejection of Orthodoxy in favor of Catholicism, and the subsequent conversion of his pagan Lithuanian subjects, made the Catholic Church the primary vehicle of Polish cultural influence.
* **The Attraction of Gentry Liberties:** Poland's political system, which granted extensive legal rights, privileges, and political representation to the nobility (*szlachta*), was highly attractive to Lithuanian and West Russian landlords who sought similar status and autonomy from their grand prince.
* **Educational and Clerical Influx:** The lack of domestic higher education forced the Lithuanian elite to study at the University of Cracow. Polish clergy dominated the Lithuanian church, and Polish specialists (architects, artists, and diplomats) migrated to the Grand Duchy (l. 1989).

## Major Phases
1. **The Dynastic Horizon (1385–1413 CE):** The Union of Krewo (1385) and the Union of Horodlo (1413) established formal personal and administrative links. At Horodlo, forty-seven Polish noble families offered their coats of arms to their Lithuanian counterparts, cementing personal and social alliances.
2. **Social and Legal Alignment (15th–early 16th century CE):** The Lithuanian upper classes adopted Polish legal practices, and the grand princes granted charters (*privilegia*) that replicated Polish gentry liberties, reducing the power of the central administration (l. 1991).
3. **The Sovereign Merger (1569 CE):** Under pressure from King Sigismund II and the threat of Muscovite expansion, the two states signed the Union of Lublin, establishing a single Commonwealth.

## Geographic Spread
The process began at the royal court in Vilnius and among the ethnic Lithuanian nobility. It gradually spread south and east into the vast West Russian (Ruthenian/Ukrainian/Belorussian) lands of the Grand Duchy, influencing the local Orthodox boyar aristocracy.

## Interaction
* **De-Slavicization of Lithuanian Administration:** In the fourteenth century, the Grand Duchy of Lithuania was culturally dominated by its Russian subjects, using Russian as its official administrative language. Polonization reversed this trend, replacing Russian with Polish at the court and in government.
* **Social and Confessional Fracture:** As the Lithuanian and Ruthenian landlords adopted Polish culture and Roman Catholicism to secure their gentry privileges, they became culturally and religiously alienated from their own Orthodox peasantry. This created a deep social and confessional fracture in the West Russian lands, setting the stage for future rebellions (l. 2001).

## End Conditions
The process culminated in the Union of Lublin in 1569 CE. By merging the administrations and diets, Poland established its position as the senior partner in the Commonwealth. The Lithuanian principality ceased to exist as an independent political entity, and its elite was fully absorbed into the Polish cultural sphere.
