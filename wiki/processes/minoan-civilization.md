# Minoan Civilization (Bronze Age Crete)

The first **advanced civilization of European soil** — the palace culture of
Bronze Age **Crete**, named by Sir Arthur Evans for the legendary King **Minos**.
It is defined by the great **palaces** ([[knossos|Knossos]], Phaestus, Mallia,
Hagia Triada), a brilliant naturalistic art, a seaborne commerce reaching Egypt and
the Levant, and the earliest **[[aegean-scripts|writing]]** in the Aegean. Treated
in [[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (Chs. IV(b) & XII, Matz).

## Definition and Scope

"Minoan" is an etic label (Evans's coinage) for the Bronze Age culture of Crete,
which the Greeks remembered as the realm of **Minos** and his sea-empire. Its
chronology follows Evans's tripartite scheme — Early, Middle, Late Minoan
(EM/MM/LM), each subdivided — anchored to Egypt by synchronisms (the **Khyan lid**,
a Twelfth-Dynasty statuette at Knossos, a Thutmose III vase). The civilization runs
from the **first palaces ~2000 BCE** to their **final destruction ~1380 BCE**;
unlike the mainland and the Levant there is **no migration-rupture** at its base —
Minoan civilization grows by unbroken local development out of the
[[early-bronze-age-aegean|Early Bronze Aegean]].

## Major Phases

- **Early Palace Period (MM I–II, ~2000–1700):** the first palaces rise, "marking
  the Minoans' decisive entry into the civilized world." Power is centralized under
  a prince supported by a landowning nobility (whose family tombs are the Messara
  *tholoi*); the **potter's wheel** and the eggshell-thin polychrome **Kamares
  ware** appear; pictorial art is born in **seal-engraving**; and administration
  begins with **[[aegean-scripts|Cretan hieroglyphic and Linear A]]** (the Phaestus
  sealing-archive). The palaces are repeatedly wrecked by **earthquakes** and each
  time rebuilt grander.
- **Late Palace Period (MM III–LM II, ~1700–1380):** the **zenith**. The rebuilt
  [[knossos|Knossos]] reaches its final form — multi-storey domestic quarters,
  light-wells, drainage and flush latrines, the throne-room — and Cretan art peaks
  in **fresco** (the bull-leaping "Toreador," processions, the miniature frescoes),
  gem-cutting, and the marine-style **Palace Style** pottery. The **eruption of
  Thera** (~1500) and its earthquakes fall in this span.

## Causal Drivers and the Thalassocracy

Minoan power rested not on land armies but on **ships and isolation**: the palaces
were **essentially unfortified** — proof, Matz argues, that the Cretan fleet kept
piracy from the coasts. The Greeks from Herodotus on remembered a Minoan
**"thalassocracy"** (sea-empire), and though its political reality is disputed
(see [[fall-of-knossos]]), the archaeology confirms genuine seapower: Minoan trade
and settlement reached **Thera, Melos (Phylakopi), Kythera, Miletus and Rhodes**,
and exchange ran to **Egypt** (the *Keftiu* of the tomb-paintings), **Byblos**,
**[[ugarit|Ugarit]] and Qatna**, and **[[cyprus|Cyprus]]** for copper. The palaces
were **economic hubs** — vast storerooms, sealed archives, and a centralized
redistribution of grain, oil, wool and crafts.

## Religion and Society

Minoan religion centred on a **mother-goddess** (revealed in epiphany as bird or
snake, attended by the **double-axe** and "horns of consecration"), with
**bull-sacrifice and bull-leaping**, open-air peak sanctuaries, and — strikingly —
**no cult images and no temples**: the divine appears, it is not enshrined. Women
are unusually prominent in the frescoes (the "Ladies in Blue," the "Parisienne"),
fuelling the much-debated idea of a matriarchal element. Society was a
**centralized monarchy** over a court nobility (the villa-dwellers of Tylissos,
Vathypetros) and a modest urban middle class of artisans, sailors and farmers
(the small towns of **Gournia**, Palaikastro, Pseira).

## End Conditions

From ~1450 BCE the **[[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean Greeks]]** took over
[[knossos|Knossos]] — proved by the **[[aegean-scripts|Linear B]]** tablets, which
are in **Greek** — ending Minoan independence; the palaces were finally destroyed
~1380 BCE and never rebuilt. The reasons (mainland conquest, native revolt, natural
cataclysm) are unresolved (see [[fall-of-knossos]]). Thereafter the centre of
Aegean history shifts permanently to the **Greek mainland**: *Creta capta ferum
victorem cepit* — captive Crete took its rude conqueror captive, for the Mycenaeans
inherited Minoan art, script and administration wholesale.

## Significance

Minoan Crete is **Europe's first civilization** and the cultural mother of the
Mycenaean (and so, indirectly, the classical Greek) world. Its great inventions —
the **pictorial image** as an art-form, the palace economy, and the **first Aegean
writing** — passed to the mainland; its art, religion and the Minos/Theseus myths
left a deep substrate in later Greek culture.
