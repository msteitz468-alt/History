# Christianization of Scandinavia

## Definition and Scope

The conversion of the Scandinavian peoples and the building of an institutional
church across [[denmark|Denmark]], [[norway|Norway]], [[sweden|Sweden]], and the
Norse Atlantic, from the early Carolingian missions to the establishment of native
archbishoprics. It is distinct from, but entangled with,
[[unification-of-the-scandinavian-kingdoms|royal unification]] — conversion was
often a top-down royal project as much as a popular religious change.

## Causal Drivers

Royal ambition (Christian kingship offered ideology, alliances, and an
administrative church); sustained contact with Christian Europe through the
[[viking-age|Viking expansion]]; organised missions from Hamburg-Bremen and
England; and the gradual erosion of the pagan cult centres (Old Uppsala, Lade).

## Major Phases

- **9th c. missions:** Ansgar to Hedeby and Birka (c. 826–850), with limited
  durable result.
- **10th c. royal conversions:** [[harald-bluetooth|Harald Bluetooth]] of Denmark
  (c. 960s, [[jelling|Jelling]] stone "made the Danes Christian").
- **c. 1000 turning point:** [[olaf-tryggvason|Olaf Tryggvason]] and
  [[olaf-haraldsson|St Olaf]] in Norway; Iceland's conversion by decision of the
  [[thing-assembly|Althing]] (c. 1000); slower change in Sweden.
- **11th–12th c. institutionalisation:** bishoprics, the archbishopric of **Lund**
  (1103/04) for all Scandinavia, then native provinces — **Nidaros** (Norway,
  1152/53) and **Uppsala** (Sweden, 1164).

## Geographic Spread

Denmark and Norway first (10th–early 11th c.); Iceland c. 1000; Sweden latest,
with pagan resistance at Old Uppsala into the late 11th century; the Sámi and
Finnish north only partially and later.

## Interaction

Reinforced [[unification-of-the-scandinavian-kingdoms|state formation]], imported
Latin literacy that underpins [[saga-literature|written vernacular literature]],
and integrated Scandinavia into Latin Christendom.

## End Conditions

The process is conventionally complete with the native archbishoprics and a
functioning parish/tithe system by the mid-12th century, though popular religious
change continued. See [[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]].
