# The Arab–Israeli Conflict

The long struggle between the **Zionist/Israeli** and **Arab/Palestinian** national movements
over the land of Palestine — the central political wound of the modern
[[middle-east-modern|Middle East]], and (for Rogan) the recurring measure of Arab
powerlessness. From Eugene Rogan's [[rogan-the-arabs|*The Arabs: A History*]].

## Origins: the Palestine mandate

The conflict's roots lie in the [[colonial-partition-of-the-arab-world|wartime partition]]: the
**Balfour Declaration (1917)** pledged "a national home for the Jewish people" in a Palestine
already promised to the Arabs, and the British **Mandate** oversaw rising Jewish immigration and
land purchase against mounting Arab–Jewish violence. In 1947, unable to reconcile the two
communities, Britain referred Palestine to the **United Nations**, which voted to partition it,
and announced its withdrawal for May 1948.

## The Nakba: the 1948 war

Civil war between Palestinian Arabs and the Jewish **Haganah** erupted in late 1947. From spring
1948 Jewish forces went on the offensive; the death of the Palestinian commander **Abd al-Qadir
al-Husayni** (April 1948) and massacres such as **Dayr Yasin** broke Arab morale and turned the
flight of refugees into a flood. By the end of the civil war, Jewish forces had driven
**200,000–300,000 Palestinians** from their homes — "We must prevent at all costs their return,"
Ben-Gurion told his cabinet. On **14 May 1948** the Jews of Palestine declared the state of
**Israel**; the next day the armies of Egypt, Transjordan, Iraq, Syria, and Lebanon invaded, and
the **first Arab–Israeli war** began. The Arab states, Rogan stresses, were "more at war with
each other than with the Jewish state," and were defeated; Israel emerged larger than the UN
plan, the Palestinians stateless and dispersed — the catastrophe Arabs call the ***Nakba***.

## The wars and their consequences

- **1956 (Suez):** Israel joined Britain and France in invading Egypt after
  [[gamal-abdel-nasser|Nasser]] nationalized the canal — a military success but a political
  defeat, reversed by US/Soviet pressure.
- **1967 (the Six-Day War):** Israel's crushing defeat of Egypt, Jordan, and Syria — seizing
  Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and the Golan Heights. Rogan treats it as the
  **decisive blow to [[arab-nationalism|pan-Arabism]]** and to [[gamal-abdel-nasser|Nasser's]]
  prestige.
- **1973 (October/Yom Kippur War):** Egypt and Syria's initial successes (Sadat's "crossing" of
  the canal) restored Arab pride and led, via the **oil embargo**, to the US-brokered diplomacy
  that produced the Egyptian–Israeli peace of 1979 — and Sadat's assassination by
  [[political-islam|Islamists]].

## Significance

The conflict produced the **state of Israel** and the **Palestinian refugee diaspora**, and
became the emotional and political center of Arab politics. Rogan makes it the great engine of
the ideological shifts he traces: the repeated defeats **discredited secular
[[arab-nationalism|Arab nationalism]]** and helped drive the turn to
**[[political-islam|political Islam]]**. The Palestinian question — occupation, refugees,
statelessness — remains unresolved into the present.
