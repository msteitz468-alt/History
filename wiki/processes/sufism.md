# Sufism

The mystical and inward dimension of Islam — *tasawwuf* — devoted to the purification of the self and
the direct experience of God. From the ascetic and devotional [[early-sufism|early Sufism]] of the
classical age, it developed through the High and Late Middle Periods into the dominant form of lived
Islamic piety. Hodgson treats the **Middle Periods as the age of Sufism's triumph**: the era of the
great theorists (al-Ghazali's reconciliation of Sufism with the [[sharia|Shari'ah]], Ibn al-Arabi's
metaphysical monism, Rumi's poetry) and, above all, of the **[[tariqa-orders|tariqa brotherhoods]]** —
organized devotional orders that gave Islamicate society a religious structure independent of the state.

As caliphal authority dissolved, the Sufi orders and the [[ulama|'ulama]] became the connective tissue
of an international civilization, and Sufism became the principal vehicle by which Islam spread to India,
Anatolia, Southeast Asia, and sub-Saharan Africa.
