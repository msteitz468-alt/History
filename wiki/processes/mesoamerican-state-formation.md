# Mesoamerican State Formation

## Definition and Scope

This page tracks the **independent emergence of complex, state-level societies in
Mesoamerica** — the cultural region of central and southern Mexico and northern Central
America — from the Olmec to the Classic collapse (c. 1500 BCE–900 CE), as set out in
[[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (Begun & Brashler, Ch 20). It is one of CWH IV's central
demonstrations that **civilization arose autonomously in the Americas**, on indigenous
foundations and through indigenous processes, with no Old World contact.

## Causal Drivers

CWH IV grounds Mesoamerican complexity in **maize** (with beans and squash, the
"three sisters") and, decisively, in **interregional trade**. Rejecting a simple
Olmec-origins model, the chapter argues that complexity emerged through **trade networks and
intercultural relations** linking several regions at once — prestige-goods exchange (iron-ore
mirrors, shell, feathers, obsidian, jade) created and sustained elites who could command labor
and proclaim authority. Shared **religion and a calendrical-cosmological order** (the
[[mesoamerican-calendar|Mesoamerican calendar]], ball game, bloodletting) bound the region
into a single civilizational sphere.

## Major Phases

- **Preclassic/Formative (c. 1500 BCE – 100 CE):** the **[[olmec|Olmec]]** ceremonial centers
  of the Gulf Coast (San Lorenzo, La Venta) and the parallel rise of the **[[zapotec|Zapotec]]**
  in the Valley of Oaxaca (San José Mogote, then Monte Albán) — the first writing, calendar,
  and martial iconography.
- **Classic (c. 100–650/900 CE):** the metropolis of **[[teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]]** dominates
  central Mexico through obsidian and trade; the **[[classic-maya|Classic Maya]]** city-states
  flourish under divine kings; Monte Albán peaks.
- **Classic collapse (c. 650–910 CE):** Teotihuacan falls (~650), triggering a cascade; the
  Maya centers fail one by one (last Long Count date 910 CE).
- **Postclassic (c. 900–1519 CE):** [[kedar-cwh-v5-2015|CWH V]] (Smith, Ch 23) details
  this phase, which earlier volumes left as an aftermath. After the Classic collapse,
  Mesoamerica grew "very dynamic politically with multiple historical trajectories":
  polities were generally **smaller, shorter-lived, and less despotic** than Classic
  ones, while commercial networks expanded and a shared **"international" art style and
  symbol set** spread across the region. The Toltecs at Tula and Chichén Itzá gave way
  to the large [[aztec-empire|Aztec (Triple Alliance)]] and Tarascan empires — which
  "developed in contradistinction" to the small-polity trend — down to the Spanish
  conquest.

## Geographic Spread

State formation radiated and pulsed across the Gulf Coast, the Oaxaca highlands, the Basin of
Mexico, and the Maya lowlands and highlands — and its influence reached the far **northwestern
frontier**, where [[chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]] and the Hohokam adopted Mesoamerican political
and material forms (see [[north-american-states-debate]]).

## Interaction

This process is the Americas' counterpart to Old World [[state-formation]] and [[empire]]. It
interlocks with [[north-american-mound-building]] (via the Southwest) and runs parallel to
[[andean-state-formation]] — three independent New World trajectories CWH IV insists must be
understood on their own terms, not measured against Afro-Eurasia.

## End Conditions

No single end: the Classic collapse closed one cycle, but state formation **recurred** — a
"successive progression of state rise and collapse" down to the Spanish conquest, which cut
these trajectories short through "superior technology and disease pathogens."
