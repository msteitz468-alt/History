# Subarctic Archaic

## Definition and Scope

Postglacial peopling and Archaic differentiation of the North American **Subarctic** — nearly all heavily glaciated before 10,000 B.C. except Beringia. Laurentide covered Hudson Bay until after 7000 B.C.; glacial lakes Agassiz, Barlow-Ojibway; Tyrrell Sea then isostatic rebound by 3000 B.C. Part of [[paleoindian-and-archaic-north-america]].

## Causal Drivers

- Ice retreat and boreal forest colonization
- Piano-tradition big-game bands pushing north
- Maritime mammal hunting on Atlantic Subarctic coasts
- Possible early Na-Dene / Northern Archaic association (dates may be too early for proto-Na-Dene)

## Major Phases

| Tradition | Dates (Snow) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Piano bands on southern fringes | 8500–6000 B.C. | From south and west |
| **Shield Archaic** | after 5500 B.C. – ~A.D. 500 | New Subarctic forests N of Great Lakes and around southern/western Hudson Bay |
| **Maritime Archaic** | 4000–1500 B.C. | Whales/seals, dugouts, complex funerary ritual early (Red Paint / L’Anse Amour nested on parent process) |
| Northern Interior Microblade / Paleo-Arctic | until 1000 B.C. | Beringian-linked |
| **Northern Archaic** | from 2500 B.C. in Yukon to A.D. 500 | Possibly early Na-Dene — “archaeological dates may put the development too early” |
| Taltheilei | after 500 B.C. | Contests expanding Eskimos |
| Historic linguistic map | post A.D. 500 | Athapaskan west; Algonquian (via Laurel) east |

## Geographic Spread

Boreal Canada from Yukon through Hudson Bay to Labrador/Newfoundland coasts; interfaces with [[arctic-small-tool-tradition]] and later Eskimo expansions.

## Interaction

`part_of: [[paleoindian-and-archaic-north-america]]` · concurrent with Arctic maritime sequences · feeds historic Athapaskan and Algonquian distributions

## End Conditions

Regional Archaic replacements and linguistic expansions continue into contact; fur trade later restructures Subarctic economies (beyond Snow ch. 3).
