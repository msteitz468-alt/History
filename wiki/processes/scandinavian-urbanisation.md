# Scandinavian Urbanisation

## Definition and Scope

The emergence and growth of towns in Scandinavia, from the Viking-Age trading
emporia through the episcopal and royal towns of the high Middle Ages to the
Hanseatic-dominated ports of the late medieval Baltic and North Sea.

## Major Phases

- **Emporia (c. 700–1000):** Ribe (Denmark, c. 700), [[hedeby|Hedeby]], and
  [[birka|Birka]] — seasonal/permanent markets and minting places of the
  [[viking-age|Viking Age]].
- **Episcopal and royal towns (11th–13th c.):** Lund, Trondheim/Nidaros, Oslo,
  [[bergen|Bergen]], Sigtuna, then Stockholm — administrative, ecclesiastical,
  and commercial centres of the consolidating kingdoms.
- **Hanseatic ports (13th–15th c.):** [[bergen|Bergen]] (stockfish),
  [[gotland|Visby]], Stockholm, and Danish towns drawn into the
  [[hanseatic-league|Hanseatic]] network, often with large resident German
  merchant communities.

## Interaction

Tied to [[unification-of-the-scandinavian-kingdoms|state formation]] (royal
mints and tolls), [[christianization-of-scandinavia|the church]] (episcopal
seats), and Baltic trade. Towns remained small and few by continental standards.
