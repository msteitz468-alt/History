# Brezhnev Stagnation
## Definition and Scope
The **Brezhnev Stagnation** (often referred to in Russian as the *Zastoy*) was a period of economic, social, and political immobilization in the Soviet Union spanning from the mid-1960s to the mid-1980s. Under General Secretary Leonid Brezhnev, the Soviet leadership prioritized political stability and military build-up, rejecting structural reforms. This led to a steady deceleration in economic growth, chronic failures in agriculture, widespread corruption, and the growth of a large underground economy, laying the groundwork for the eventual collapse of the Soviet state.

## Causal Drivers
- **Rigid Central Planning:** The command economy, built on coal and iron, failed to adapt to the new technological era of computers and electronics.
- **Nomenklatura Conservatism:** The ruling Party elite prioritized their security, privileges, and job security (ensured by Brezhnev's policy of "stability of cadres") over any reforms that might threaten their status.
- **Military Overburden:** The arms race and global imperial commitments (including aid to client states and the invasion of Afghanistan) consumed an estimated 15% to 20% of the Soviet GDP.
- **Resource Curse:** High oil and natural gas prices in the 1970s allowed the government to export fuel to buy foreign grain and consumer goods, temporarily concealing systemic economic failures without addressing their root causes (Line 7261).

## Major Phases
- **Consolidation and Emasculated Reform (1964–1970):** The government repealed Khrushchev's reorganizations. The Liberman-Kosygin economic reforms of 1965, which attempted to prioritize profits and sales over volume, were gradually emasculated by the bureaucratic ministries.
- **Détente and Oil Boom (1971–1978):** A period of apparent prosperity. Living standards rose, and the USSR achieved strategic parity with the US. The export of Siberian oil and gas kept the economy afloat.
- **Severe Deceleration and Crisis (1979–1985):** A sharp economic downturn. The year 1979 marked the first of an unprecedented four successive years of bad grain harvests. Growth rates of the GNP fell to an estimated 2.0% by 1981–1982, and chronic shortages of basic foods and consumer goods became ubiquitous (Line 6647, 6629).

## Geographic Spread
The stagnation affected the entire Soviet Union and the Warsaw Pact satellite states. It led to extreme regional disparities, with Moscow and Leningrad favored in food supply, while provincial towns and agricultural republics suffered severe shortages.

## Interaction
- **With the Underground Economy:** As the command economy failed to provide quality goods, a massive "second economy" (black markets, barter, and illegal private production) emerged, closely linked with official corruption.
- **With Society:** Stagnation contributed to low workplace morale, absenteeism, and a dramatic rise in alcoholism and mortality rates, particularly among working-age men.

## End Conditions
The stagnation ended with the death of Brezhnev's short-lived successors (Andropov and Chernenko) and the election of **[[gorbachev-mikhail|Mikhail Gorbachev]]** as General Secretary in March 1985. Gorbachev launched **[[perestroika|Perestroika]]** and **[[glasnost|Glasnost]]** specifically to break the country out of this economic and social cul-de-sac.
