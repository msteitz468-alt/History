# Ruralization (The Remaking of the Countryside as Urban Hinterland)

## Definition and Scope

**Ruralization** is the mirror image of [[urbanization]]: when cities form, they
do not merely grow on a stable rural base — they **restructure the countryside**
around them. Autonomous villages become "peripheries"; the hinterland is
reorganized to provision the center; and the very categories of "rural" and
"urban" are produced together. This is the central argument of Part III of
[[yoffee-cwh-v3-2015|CWH Vol. III]] — "Early Urban Landscapes" — and Yoffee's
formulation in the introduction: in the evolution of cities, "social life both in
and outside of cities changed utterly, redefined in the process of urbanization
and ruralization."

## Causal Drivers

- `caused_by:` [[urbanization]] — cities cannot exist without a provisioning
  hinterland; their formation forces rural reorganization.
- `contributed_to:` surplus mobilization and storage — the countryside is "forced
  to produce a considerable amount of surplus" once drawn into the urban network.
- `contributed_to:` central-place organization — settlements rank into tiers
  feeding the metropolis (Adams & Nissen on [[uruk|Uruk]]'s countryside).

## How it appears in the case studies

- **[[uruk|Mesopotamia]]** (Nissen; Emberling, Ch 12) — Late Uruk southern
  Mesopotamia: the alluvium fills with 100+ settlements in a three-tier
  central-place system feeding Uruk; the countryside is effectively depopulated
  into urban hinterland. Emberling traces the long urban *process* (3500–1600
  BCE) of cities and their reshaped landscapes.
- **[[teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]]** (Clayton) — "the urbanization of Teotihuacan
  was concurrently a process of ruralization"; hinterland villages (Axotlan)
  adopt urban compound forms and orientations, integrated into the state.
- **[[tiwanaku|Tiwanaku]]** (Janusek) — raised fields and qochas make productive
  "rural" landscapes *within* the city; the urban–rural dichotomy dissolves in a
  distributed, animate landscape.

## The urban landscape itself

Part III also concerns the *internal* restructuring of the cityscape:
**neighborhoods** (Tiwanaku compounds; Teotihuacan barrios; Mesopotamian
quarters), the relation of administrative/ceremonial districts to residential
areas, and the **routinization of daily life** (Adam T. Smith; the commute, the
plaza, the workshop) by which cities make urban subjects.

## Interaction and End Conditions

Ruralization is inseparable from [[urbanization]], state formation, and
[[agriculture-and-urbanism|agrarian intensification]]; it generates enduring
**rural–urban inequality**. Ongoing wherever cities exist.
