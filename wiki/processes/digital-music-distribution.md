## Definition and Scope

Digital music distribution is the process by which recorded music shifted from physical media (vinyl, cassette, CD) to digital formats and delivery, fundamentally restructuring the recording industry's business model. It is not a single event but a multi-decade transformation spanning the collapse of the CD market, the rise and fall of paid downloads, and the subsequent shift to subscription streaming.

## Causal Drivers

The process was driven by the maturing MP3 audio-compression standard, which made high-quality music files small enough to share over consumer internet connections, and by peer-to-peer file-sharing services — above all Napster (1999) — that enabled mass, largely unlicensed distribution of copyrighted recordings, devastating CD sales through the early 2000s.

## Major Phases

The unlicensed peer-to-peer phase (1999–2003) triggered industry litigation (notably against Napster itself) without providing a viable legal alternative. Apple's iTunes Store, launched in 2003 alongside the existing iPod, offered a legal, low-friction paid-download model (initially 99 cents per song) that restored a licensed digital market and made Apple briefly the dominant force in recorded-music retail. From the early-to-mid 2010s, subscription streaming services (Spotify, and later Apple Music, among others) supplanted downloads as the dominant digital format, shifting industry revenue from per-unit sales to recurring subscription and advertising income.

## Geographic Spread

The process began in the United States but spread globally within a few years, following consumer broadband internet penetration and smartphone adoption.

## Interaction

Closely tied to the broader personal-computing and mobile-device revolution associated with Apple under [[actors/jobs-steve|Steve Jobs]], whose iPod/iTunes ecosystem was central to the paid-download phase.

## End Conditions

The process has not ended but has transitioned rather than concluded: streaming now dominates recorded-music consumption globally, with ongoing debate over royalty structures and artist compensation under the subscription model.
