# Soviet Industrialization
## Definition and Scope
**Soviet Industrialization** refers to the rapid, state-directed transformation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR) from a backward agrarian country into a major industrial superpower. Initiated under Joseph Stalin's First Three Five-Year Plans (1928–1941), the process was characterized by a massive concentration of national investment into heavy industry, machine-building, energy production, and military manufacturing. This crash program was financed internally by squeezing the agricultural sector, suppressing real wages, and enforcing strict labor discipline. Constantly the Politburo quickened the projected pace of industrialization. Cheap labour was made available by peasants fleeing the villages (Service, line 1454). And yet both agriculture and industry were altogether too chaotic to be described without reservation as being integrated within ‘a planned economy’. For example, the Five-Year Plan of 1928–33 was drawn up six months after it was said to have been inaugurated (and the Plan was said to be completed a year before it was meant to end). Rough commands were of a more practical importance than carefully-elaborated planning (line 1458).

## Causal Drivers
- **Ideological Imperative:** Classical Marxism assumed that a socialist society could only exist on the basis of an advanced industrial economy.
- **National Security:** Stalin and his associates believed the USSR was surrounded by hostile capitalist states. Stalin’s guesses about Europe were very faulty... he correctly anticipated that Hitler would stir up a deal of trouble for the Allies... Yet when due allowance is made, his comment underestimated the profound danger of Nazism to the USSR (lines 1500, 1502).
- **Economic Impasse of the NEP and Drive for Defense:** The mixed economy of the New Economic Policy struggled... Stalin diverted investment increasingly towards the defence sub-sector. Nearly six per cent of such capital was dedicated to the Red Army’s requirements: this was higher than the combined total for agricultural machines, tractors, cars, buses and lorries (line 1498). It was easier for Stalin to bring this about than it would have been for Bukharin who wanted peasant aspirations to be taken into account.
- **Labor and Coercion:** In spring 1929 Stalin... appointed a Central Committee commission under N. Yanson to explore opportunities for convicts... The prisons were already crammed with peasants who had resisted being pushed into collective farms: Yanson recommended their transfer to the forced-labour camps subject to the OGPU (line 1454).

## Major Phases
- **The First Five-Year Plan (1928–1933, announced completed early):** The large factories and mines had been governmentally-owned since 1917–19, but the number of such enterprises rose steeply after 1928. Thirty-eight per cent of industrial capital stock by the end of 1934 was located in factories built in the previous half-dozen years (line 1474). The First Five-Year Plan was meant to end in September 1933; in fact its completion was announced in December 1932. Mines and factories were claimed to have doubled their production since 1928. This was exaggeration. Yet even sceptical estimates put the annual expansion in industrial output at ten per cent between 1928 and 1941; and the production of capital goods probably grew at twice the rate of consumer goods during the Five-Year Plan. The USSR had at last been pointed decisively towards the goal of a fully industrialized society (line 1474).
- **Stakhanovism and Second Five-Year Plan Adjustments (1935+):** In the Don Basin... the miner Aleksei Stakhanov hewed 102 tons of coal in a six-hour stint in August... A Stakhanovite movement was founded. Suddenly it was found that practically every industrial machine could be made to function much, much faster... Critics of Stakhanovism in any enterprise were not merely reprimanded but arrested as ‘wreckers’ (lines 1680, 1682). The new expectation was for a doubling of the output of industrial producers’ goods in the half-decade before the end of 1937. This was still a very rapid growth, but not at the breakneck speed of the First Five-Year Plan. The Politburo began to lay its emphasis upon completing the construction of the half-built factories and mines and getting them into full production (line 1547).
- **Defense Prioritization:** Nearly six per cent of such capital was dedicated to the Red Army’s requirements (line 1498). Deceit was deeply embedded in the mode of industrial and agricultural management. It has been asserted that shoddy, unusable goods were so high a proportion of output that official claims for increases in output were typically double the reality (line 1498).

## Geographic Spread
The process transformed the geography of the Soviet Union. While traditional centers like Ukraine, Moscow, and Leningrad were heavily expanded, massive new industrial zones were created in the interior: the Urals (Magnitogorsk), western Siberia (Kuznetsk)... The foundation of new cities such as Magnitogorsk was celebrated (although Pravda was not allowed to report that a segment of the labour-force used for the construction consisted of Gulag prisoners) (Service, line 1535).

## Interaction
- **With Collectivization:** Industrialization was funded by the coercive collectivization of agriculture... Revenues had to be diverted not only to agronomists, surveyors and farm chairmen but also to soldiers, policemen and informers (line 1470). The price was awful... peasants were killing their livestock (line 1466).
- **With Society and Labor:** Industrial workers were fortunate by comparison. Except during the famine of 1932–3, their consumption of calories was as great as it had been under the NEP... wages for blue-collar jobs fell in real terms by a half in the course of the Five-Year Plan (line 1486). This was also one of the reasons why the working class endured the Five-Year Plan’s rigours without the violent resistance offered by peasant communities. The OGPU was efficient at detecting and suppressing such dissent (line 1488). About one and a half million managers and administrators in 1930–33 had recently been elevated from manual occupations (line 1486).
- **With Party and Deceit:** The picture of over-fulfilled economic plans painted by the newspapers involved much distortion... The statistics themselves were fiddled not only by a central party machine wishing to fool the world but also by local functionaries wanting to trick the central party machine (line 1496).

## End Conditions
The initial phase of rapid industrialization was interrupted by the Nazi invasion in June 1941. During the war, the industrial infrastructure of the western USSR was either destroyed or evacuated eastward. Post-war reconstruction under the Fourth and Fifth Five-Year Plans resumed the same heavy-industry-focused model, which continued until Andropov and Gorbachev recognized the stagnation of this traditional "coal and iron" model in the computer age. By the time of the harvest of 1931, collective farms held practically all the land... Stalin and the Politburo had won the agrarian war (line 1464). Yet the system contained self-limiting features that Service highlights as integral to the "Soviet compound."
