## Definition and Scope

Human evolution is the long-duration biological and behavioral process by which
the zoological family **Hominidae** — all species more closely related to *Homo
sapiens* than to any living ape — originated in Africa around 7 million years ago
(BP) and diversified, ultimately producing the single surviving hominid species,
ourselves. Following [[tattersall-world-from-beginnings-2008|Tattersall (2008)]],
this page treats the process **as a bushy, experimental radiation rather than a
linear ascent** — a central methodological commitment that distinguishes modern
paleoanthropology from the gradualist "ladder of being" or "main line of descent"
that long dominated the field.

It is *not* a single optimizing trajectory: the present condition of one hominid
species alone on Earth is, in Tattersall's framing, an unusual state, not the
natural outcome of a directional trend.

## Causal Drivers

- **Environmental change.** Late-Miocene polar cooling and declining equatorial
  rainfall (~10.5 mya) fragmented African forests into woodland and grassland
  mosaics; the hominid family established itself as these open habitats spread.
  Pleistocene climatic instability (after ~1.8 mya, intensifying to ~100,000-year
  cycles by ~500 kya) repeatedly reshaped habitats and geography, conditions
  "ideally suited to local adaptation and evolutionary innovation."
- **Speciation and competition.** Under [[punctuated-equilibria]], change
  concentrates around rapid speciation events in small isolated populations;
  long-term trends (e.g. brain enlargement) reflect species sorting/competition,
  not gradual within-lineage selection.
- **Developmental/regulatory change.** Abrupt morphological shifts — archaic to
  modern body plan ([[homo-ergaster]]) without intermediates — are attributed to
  regulatory-gene changes with cascading effects (evo-devo; Schwartz's "sudden
  origins").
- **Cultural-cognitive feedback (late).** Only with *Homo sapiens* does cultural
  innovation become a primary driver, culminating in [[symbolic-cognition]].

## Major Phases

1. **Earliest hominids (~7–4.4 mya).** *Sahelanthropus* (Chad, ~6–7 mya),
   *Orrorin* (Kenya, ~6 mya), *Ardipithecus* (Ethiopia, ~5.8–4.4 mya) — a "motley"
   set of putative early bipeds establishing experimentation from the start. See
   [[bipedalism-origins]].
2. **Australopith radiation (~4.2–1 mya).** First confirmed upright biped
   *Australopithecus anamensis* (~4.2 mya); [[australopithecus-afarensis]] ("Lucy,"
   ~3.18 mya); gracile (*A. africanus*) and robust (*Paranthropus*) lineages; small
   brains, ape-grade cognition.
3. **Emergence of *Homo* (~2.5–1.8 mya).** First Oldowan tools (~2.5 mya, makers
   uncertain — possibly *A. garhi*); *Homo habilis* (~1.9–1.8 mya); the modern body
   plan of [[homo-ergaster]] (Turkana Boy, ~1.6 mya).
4. **First dispersals from Africa (~1.8 mya onward).** [[out-of-africa-1-1800kbp]]:
   Dmanisi (Georgia, ~1.8 mya, small-brained, crude tools), Java/[[homo-erectus]],
   Riwat. Acheulean handaxes (~1.5 mya) postdate both *ergaster* and the diaspora.
5. **Middle Pleistocene "getting brainier" (~600–200 kya).** [[homo-heidelbergensis]]
   spreads across the Old World; prepared-core/Levallois technology (~300 kya);
   regional radiations including the [[neanderthals]] in Europe/western Asia.
6. **Modern human origins (~200–40 kya).** Anatomically modern *Homo sapiens* in
   Africa (Omo ~195 kya, Herto ~160 kya); [[out-of-africa-2-60kbp]]; the late,
   abrupt appearance of [[symbolic-cognition]] and replacement of all other
   hominids; the [[cro-magnons]] in Europe (~40 kya).

## Geographic Spread

Wholly African until ~2 mya, then Asia and (by ~800 kya) Europe via successive
diasporas of successive species — each new African form potentially re-colonizing
and sometimes returning. *Homo sapiens* finally reached Australia (~60 kya) and the
Americas (~15–12 kya, possibly earlier), the only hominid to occupy every habitable
continent.

## Interaction

Deeply coupled to [[paleolithic-global-dispersal]], [[out-of-africa-1-1800kbp]],
[[out-of-africa-2-60kbp]], and the rise of [[paleolithic-art-symbolic-culture]].
Its end conditions flow directly into the [[neolithic-revolution]] and
[[agricultural-origins]] — but those are economic/cultural, not further *biological*
speciation: behaviorally and anatomically, the human actor was already complete.

## End Conditions

As a *speciation* process within the genus *Homo*, it effectively closed with the
extinction of all other hominids ([[neanderthals]] ~40–30 kya; *Homo erectus* on
Java ~40 kya; *Homo floresiensis* <20 kya) and the global spread of a single
species. Biological evolution of *Homo sapiens* continues (e.g. skin pigmentation,
lactase persistence), but the dominant force shaping the human world since ~10,000
BP has been cultural and economic, not anatomical.

## Historiography

The shift from the linear "single-species hypothesis" (1960s, an absorption of the
Modern Synthesis into paleoanthropology) to a bushy, multi-species phylogeny is the
defining historiographical movement in the field, and Tattersall is among its
leading partisans. Two live debates qualify the account here: **(1) splitters vs.
lumpers** — how many hominid species the morphological record really supports; and
**(2) Out-of-Africa vs. multiregional continuity** and the degree of archaic
admixture, on which ancient-DNA work after 2010 (Neanderthal and Denisovan nuclear
genomes) showed limited but real interbreeding, softening the strict replacement
model this source defends. See [[modern-human-origins-debate]]. Dating rests on
radiometric methods (K/Ar and Ar/Ar on volcanic tuffs, ESR, TL, radiocarbon for the
last ~50 kya) calibrated against the oxygen-isotope marine record; see
[[radiocarbon-revolution]] and [[three-age-system]].
