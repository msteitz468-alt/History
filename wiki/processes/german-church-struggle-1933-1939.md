# The German Church Struggle (Kirchenkampf)

The Nazi attempt to coordinate, split, marginalize, and ultimately supplant
Germany's churches — and the churches' partial, mostly self-limiting
resistance. Based on Evans, *The Third Reich in Power* (2005), ch. 3.

## Definition and Scope

Covers (1) the Protestant struggle between the pro-Nazi **German Christians**
and the **Confessing Church**; (2) the erosion of the July 1933 **Concordat**
with the Catholic Church; (3) Nazi neo-paganism and the regime's long-term
aim (private, not announced) of full church–state separation; (4) persecution
of small sects, above all the **Jehovah's Witnesses**.

## Major Phases

**The Catholic capitulation of 1933 (Evans 2003).** Evans's Vol. I
([[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003]]) documents the Catholic side of the
opening year: the bishops' May 1933 collective declaration of support once
Concordat assurances were in hand; Cardinal Bertram lifting the ban on
uniforms in church after Catholic SA men drifted to Protestant services; the
Fulda pastoral letter (1 June) welcoming the "national awakening";
Vicar-General Steinmann declaring Hitler God-given; Catholic student
organizations hailing "our Leader Adolf Hitler." The Concordat (agreed 1 July
1933) banned priests from politics and bought the Centre Party's
self-dissolution (see [[dissolution-of-german-parties-1933]]). Clemens August
Count von Galen — six-foot-seven Westphalian aristocrat-priest, believer in
the [[stab-in-the-back-myth|stab-in-the-back myth]] who had called the Weimar
constitution "godless" — was consecrated Bishop of Münster on 28 October 1933,
the first post-Concordat elevation, swearing his loyalty oath to Göring while
SA columns lined the episcopal procession: "The reconciliation of Nazism and
Catholicism seemed, at least for the time being, complete." (His later
anti-euthanasia sermons belong to [[aktion-t4-1939]].) The regime's emollient
framing throughout early 1933 was "positive Christianity" — above confession,
attuned to the Germanic spirit.

**German Christians ascendant (1932–33).** Organized May 1932 (~600,000
members by mid-decade), the German Christians sought a centralized "Reich
Church" purged of "the Jewish mission," "Christian cosmopolitanism," and
"racial mixing"; with heavy Goebbels-press backing they swept the church
elections of 23 July 1933 and installed Ludwig Müller as Reich Bishop. Prussian
commissioner August Jäger declared Hitler was "completing what Luther had
begun" and Jesus "a flaring-up of the Nordic species."

**Backlash (Nov 1933–1934).** At the Berlin Sports Palace rally (13 November
1933, 20,000 attendees) Reinhold Krause demanded removal of the "Jewish" Old
Testament and called the cross itself a Jewish symbol — a scandal that
shattered the movement's momentum. The **Pastors' Emergency League**
([[niemoller-martin|Niemöller]], Bonhoeffer) grew into the **Confessing
Church** at the Barmen Synod (May 1934, Karl Barth's declaration repudiating
the Aryan Paragraph). Evans's sociology: Confessing pastors were
disproportionately from academic/clerical families (only 5% NSDAP members vs.
40% of Berlin's German Christian pastors); its Bible-study base was majority
female. Crucially, its opposition stayed narrowly religious — its antisemitism
differed from the German Christians' only in holding that baptized Jews were
no longer Jews.

**Repression (1935–37).** Hanns Kerrl's Reich Church Ministry (July 1935)
banned Confessing pastors from preaching and teaching; over 700 Protestant
pastors were imprisoned by end-1937. The Gestapo conceded its measures "only
made the pastors more insubordinate still." Niemöller's rearrest on Hitler's
personal order (March 1938) capped the phase.

**The Catholic front.** The Centre Party dissolved itself in 1933 expecting
the Concordat to protect lay organizations; the regime violated that
expectation almost immediately (Himmler banned most Bavarian Catholic
organizational activity, 19 September 1933; SS murders of Erich Klausener and
Fritz Gerlich in the [[night-of-the-long-knives-1934|1934 purge]]). From 1935
Goebbels ran manufactured financial-corruption and then sex-scandal
show-trial campaigns against monks and priests (over 1,000 reportedly awaiting
trial by April 1937; his 28 May 1937 broadcast vowed "this sexual plague must
be exterminated root and branch") — aimed at destroying the Church's standing
as educator of youth. The papal encyclical *Mit brennender Sorge* (1937)
protested; Hitler contemplated but avoided repudiating the Concordat for
diplomatic reasons. Denominational schooling was destroyed by engineered
"parental votes" (Munich: 84% denominational enrollment in 1934 → 5% by end
1937; all converted to secular community schools by summer 1939).

**Neo-paganism and dechristianization at the elite.** Rosenberg's *Myth of the
Twentieth Century* (which Hitler disowned as unreadable); Himmler and Heydrich
formally left the Church in 1936; SS Christmas replaced by Midsummer; "Deist"
(*gottgläubig*) registration covered over 25% of the SS by 1938.

**Jehovah's Witnesses** — the only group to refuse the oath, salute, and
conscription outright: ~10,000 of fewer than 30,000 imprisoned over the
regime's course, ~2,000 to camps, ~950 dead there; officials justified the
persecution by exaggerating their numbers to "nearly two million" and
inventing a Communist connection. Höss later testified Himmler held up their
steadfastness as a model for the SS.

## Interaction

Interlocks with [[gleichschaltung]] (the failed coordination of the one
institution besides the army never fully coordinated), with
[[nazification-of-german-education-1933-1939]] (the schools were the real
prize), and with [[nazi-racial-hygiene-program-1933-1939]] (Catholic
opposition to sterilization).

## End Conditions

Stalemate by 1939: the German Christian project collapsed; the Confessing
Church survived, repressed but never a general opposition; the Catholic Church
retained its people but lost its schools and public organizations. Evans's
larger judgment: Nazism was **not** a coherent "political religion" — too
incoherent, no scripture, no redemptive endpoint, no universal appeal — but it
did intend, in the long run, to reduce the churches to voluntary bodies and
let them wither.
