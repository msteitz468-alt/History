# The Second Industrial Revolution

The **Second Industrial Revolution** (c. 1870–1914) was the wave of technological and
industrial transformation — built on **steel, electricity, chemicals, and petroleum**, and on
the systematic **application of science to industry** — that followed the first (coal-and-steam)
industrialization. It made Germany and the United States the leading industrial powers, created
mass-production economies, and supplied the material base of the **[[new-imperialism|New
Imperialism]]**. From [[new-cambridge-modern-history-v11|*The New Cambridge Modern History*,
Vol. XI]].

## Definition and Scope

The term denotes the **second phase of industrialization**, distinguished from the first by its
**new industries** (steel, electrical, chemical, oil), its **science-based innovation**
(industrial research laboratories), and its shift of leadership from Britain to **Germany and
the United States**. It covers roughly 1870 to the First World War.

## Causal Drivers

- **New materials and energy** — cheap **steel** (Bessemer, Siemens-Martin, Gilchrist-Thomas
  processes), **electricity**, and **petroleum** as a new power source.
- **Science applied to industry** — the **chemical** industry (dyes, fertilizers, explosives)
  and electrical engineering, born from organized R&D.
- **Mass markets** — growing, urbanizing populations and improved transport/communication
  (railways, steamships, the telegraph).

## Major Phases

1. **Steel and chemicals (1870s–80s):** mass steel and the German chemical/dye industry.
2. **Electricity and oil (1880s–1900s):** power generation, the electrical industry, and the
   petroleum economy.
3. **Mass production (c. 1900–14):** machine tools, the assembly line, and consumer industries.

## Geographic Spread

Centred on **Germany and the United States** (which overtook Britain in steel and chemicals by
c. 1900), it spread across the North Atlantic and, distinctively, to **Japan** (the first
non-Western industrializer) — beginning the diffusion of industry that would, much later, close
the [[great-divergence|Great Divergence]].

## Interaction

The Second Industrial Revolution underwrote the **[[new-imperialism|New Imperialism]]** (markets,
materials, and the military technology — steel warships, the machine gun — that made conquest
cheap), reshaped the **[[balance-of-power|balance of power]]** (the rise of Germany and the
USA), drove the growth of **socialism** and mass politics (an industrial working class), and
built the economic and military base for the **[[first-world-war-1914|First World War]]**.

## End Conditions

The phase merged into the twentieth-century industrial economy; its trajectory was interrupted
and then redirected by the **First World War** and the war economies it spawned (see
[[war-economy]]).
