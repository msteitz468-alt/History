# Barbarization of the Roman Army

**The progressive incorporation of barbarians — individuals, units, and finally whole
peoples — into the Roman military between the second and fifth centuries**, and the parallel
militarization of Roman society at large. In Patrick Geary's argument it is the master process
by which Rome itself "created" the barbarian world: the army was the great machine of
assimilation that turned outsiders into Romans and Romans into a soldiery increasingly
indistinguishable from the enemies it was raised to fight.

## Definition and Scope

By the early fourth century "military organization and structure, along with the soldier's
cultural and political values, had become the primary model along which Roman society was
ordered. But these soldiers were no longer the Italian peasants of an earlier age — increasingly
they were the very barbarians they were enlisted to oppose." The process is therefore two things
at once: the entry of barbarians into Roman service, and the reshaping of Roman society on a
military template.

## Major Phases

**1. Individual recruitment (from Marcus Aurelius).** Faced with plague, falling birthrates, and
frontier pressure, Marcus Aurelius incorporated Germanic warrior groups along the *limes*. Only
citizens could serve in the legions and praetorian cohorts; foreigners served in *auxilia* — but
by the third and fourth centuries the ranks of all units filled with barbarians. Roman policy
bribed frontier leaders with citizenship, gifts, gold, and grain in return for troops, who served
a term and went home: "the migrant labor force of the ancient world."

**2. The laeti and the comitatenses.** The first barbarians *settled* inside the empire — the
**laeti** — were refugees or prisoners of war assigned to prefects or landowners in depopulated
regions of Gaul and Italy, their communities obligated to supply recruits. Constantine (c. 300)
created mobile elite units, the **comitatenses**, stationed within or near major provincial cities;
their proximity to civilian communities accelerated assimilation. Since Hadrian, recruits served in
their native provinces, and by the fourth century military service was a hereditary obligation,
making legions locally rooted and self-perpetuating.

**3. The foederati and "imperial Germans."** Unlike laeti, **foederati** kept their own chiefs and
stayed in contact both with the Roman population they dominated and with tribes across the Rhine and
Danube. Germanic commanders rose to the top: the Franks **Arbogast, Bauto, and Richomer** served as
*magistri militum* and consuls under Gratian and Valentinian II — "virtual rulers who could make and
break emperors." These men were culturally Roman: Bauto's consular panegyric (385) was written by the
future St Augustine; Richomer was an acquaintance of Symmachus. See [[frankish-ethnogenesis]].

**4. Settlement of whole peoples (gentes).** The decisive, most misunderstood phase was the entry of
entire peoples. Precipitated by the Huns' arrival in 376 and the destruction of the Gothic
confederation, it ran through the catastrophe of **Adrianople** (378), where the Goths killed the
emperor Valens, to **Theodosius's treaty of 382**, which settled the [[visigoths|Visigoths]] in Thrace
under their own chiefs as foederati — the template for all subsequent incorporations.

## The mechanism of settlement (debated)

How barbarian armies were quartered on Roman soil is contested: the traditional view (*hospitalitas*
assigning one-third of estates) versus a revisionist view (assigning shares of tax income, leaving
proprietors undisturbed). The reality varied by region, but warriors in Gaul and Italy "did not
usually physically occupy the lands to which they were assigned. They tended to remain in cities or at
strategic points," collecting the rents or taxes of allotted properties.

## Interaction

The process is inseparable from the [[late-antiquity|late-Roman transformation]] of [[roman-gaul|Gaul]]
(militarization, taxation, the rise of the landowning aristocracy), from [[ethnogenesis]] (army service
became a primary marker of tribal membership), and from the [[comitatus]] tradition the recruits carried.
It produced the [[merovingian-franks|Frankish]], [[visigoths|Visigothic]], and [[ostrogoths|Ostrogothic]]
kingdoms — barbarian armies that had become Roman armies, ruling provincials.

## End Conditions

The process did not "end" so much as **complete itself**: when whole barbarian armies became the effective
government of the western provinces, the distinction between a barbarized empire and a Romanized barbarian
kingdom dissolved. [[clovis|Clovis]]'s kingdom is its endpoint — an imperial-German military commander who
absorbed Roman provincial administration to make himself a legitimate king.
