# Early Bronze Age Anatolia

The emergence, flowering, and violent disruption of an **urban, metal-rich
civilization** across the Anatolian plateau and its western coastlands during the
third millennium BCE. Unlike the literate river-valley civilizations of
[[early-dynastic-sumer|Sumer]] and [[old-kingdom-egypt|Egypt]], Bronze Age
Anatolia was **illiterate** — it left no written records of its own — yet it was
prosperous, politically organized under local kings, and a central node of the
Near Eastern economy. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]]
(Ch. XVIII, **James Mellaart**), whose higher, radiocarbon-informed chronology
(EB1 ~3500, EB2 ~2800–2300, EB3 ~2300–2000 BCE) revised the earlier Aegean-anchored
dates upward.

## Definition and Scope

This process covers the Anatolian peninsula from the close of the Late
Chalcolithic to the threshold of the Middle Bronze Age and the
[[kultepe|Assyrian trading colonies]]. Most of Anatolia developed **out of its
own local Late Chalcolithic** without external rupture (Cilicia, oriented to
Syria, is the exception). By the EB2 the peninsula held **fully urban
communities**, walled and ruled by kings, of which [[troy|Troy II]] is the
type-site.

## The Metal-Wealth Thesis

Mellaart's organizing argument: Anatolian prosperity rested on **metal**. The
plateau was exceptionally rich in **gold, silver, copper, lead, and — crucially —
the tin** needed for true bronze, in a Near East where the great alluvial
civilizations (Sumer, Syria, Egypt) were **metal-poor** and had to import. Anatolia
was therefore "**the metal market from which much of the metal wealth**" of Assyria
and Syria was drawn — a supplier civilization whose wealth came from its ores and
its position astride the routes carrying them. This export economy, more than
agriculture, underwrote the Anatolian cities and explains the concentration of
treasure in their tombs and citadels. It is the EBA forerunner of the
documented Old Assyrian metal trade through [[kultepe|Kanesh]].

## A Cultural and Linguistic Cleavage

Mellaart stresses a deep **west/south versus centre/east** division running
through Anatolian material culture — in pottery, architecture, and burial — which
he reads as an **ethnic and linguistic** fault line as well. This division frames
the later distribution of the Anatolian Indo-European languages (Luwian in the
south and west, Hittite/Nesite in the centre) and is one strand of evidence in the
[[indo-european-origins|Indo-European origins]] debate.

## The Royal-Tomb Horizon (EB2, c. 2500–2300 BCE)

The peak of Anatolian wealth shows in spectacular elite burials:

- **Alaca Höyük** — thirteen "royal tombs" yielding gold and electrum vessels and
  jewellery, **iron** daggers (among the earliest worked iron), bull and stag
  figurines, and the famous bronze **ritual "standards"** (openwork sun-disks and
  animal emblems mounted on poles), implying a court religion and a warrior
  aristocracy.
- **Dorak** — a reported (and **never independently verified**) royal-tomb find
  published by Mellaart, including a gilded throne said to bear the cartouche of
  the Egyptian pharaoh **Sahure** (Fifth Dynasty) — used to cross-date the horizon
  to [[troy|Troy IIc–g]] and the mid-third millennium. The objects vanished; the
  synchronism must be treated with great caution (see Historiography).

This horizon — Alaca, Troy II, the [[ur|Royal Tombs of Ur]] in Mesopotamia, and
Old Kingdom Egypt — marks a **mid-third-millennium zenith** of interconnected
Bronze Age wealth across the eastern Mediterranean and Near East.

## The End-EB2 Catastrophe (c. 2300 BCE)

The Anatolian Early Bronze 2 ended in **widespread violent destruction**: Mellaart
counted a collapse from some **450 known settlements to about 125**, with
burn-layers at [[troy|Troy II]], Poliochni, Beycesultan, and many other sites. He
attributed this to an **invasion from the northwest** — across the Dardanelles and
down from the Balkans/Pontic region — by **Indo-European speakers he identified
with the Luwians**, the first wave of the Anatolian branch of the family. The
supporting evidence he cited includes the hieroglyphic-Luwian-looking **seal from
Beycesultan** and the obelisk at [[byblos]] inscribed for "**Kukun son of the
Lukka-man**" (the Lukka = Luwians). This destruction horizon is roughly
synchronous with the broader **~2300–2200 BCE upheaval** that toppled the
[[fall-of-old-kingdom-2160bce|Egyptian Old Kingdom]] and the
[[early-bronze-age-levant|Levantine EB cities]] — though whether these share a
single cause (climate, the [[4.2-kiloyear event]], folk-movements) or are
independent is unresolved.

## Interaction

Early Bronze Anatolia connects the Aegean ([[troy]], the Cyclades, Early
Helladic Greece) with the Near East via the **metal trade**, supplying tin and
finished metalwork to Mesopotamia and Syria. Its end-EB2 disruptions are part of
the same horizon as the [[early-bronze-age-levant|Levantine]] and Egyptian crises.
Its successor is the world of [[middle-bronze-age|Middle Bronze]] Anatolia, the
[[kultepe|Old Assyrian kārum network]], and the rise of the Hittites.

## End Conditions

The EB2 catastrophe and a poorer EB3 recovery give way around 2000 BCE to the
Middle Bronze Age, into which Anatolia enters as the destination of the
[[kultepe|Assyrian merchant colonies]] — the moment Anatolia first becomes
**visible in written records** (the Old Assyrian tablets of Kanesh), even though
its own peoples still did not write.

## Historiography

Mellaart was a brilliant and controversial figure. The **Dorak affair** — in which
he published drawings of a sumptuous treasure he said he had been shown but which
could never be produced, examined, or located — has cast a long shadow, and the
**Sahure synchronism** that hangs on it is accordingly fragile; some scholars
suspect the whole Dorak assemblage was a fabrication. His **Luwian-invasion**
explanation of the end-EB2 destructions is a mid-20th-century **migrationist**
reading, attractive for its tidy link to the [[indo-european-origins|Indo-European
question]] but vulnerable to the general critique of invasion-explanations
(destruction layers need not mean ethnic incomers). The **chronology** is
pre-calibration. Treat the *settlement data, the metalwork, and the metal-wealth
thesis* as durable, and the *Dorak material and the single-cause invasion model* as
contested. See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
