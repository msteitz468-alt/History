# Corporate Consolidation in the Gilded Age

The transformation of the post–Civil War American economy from many small competing firms into a landscape dominated by large integrated corporations and combinations ("the trusts"). Driven by the [[processes/second-industrial-revolution|Second Industrial Revolution]], the cost structures of railroads and capital-intensive industry, and the desire to escape destructive price competition, the process found its archetype and pacesetter in [[actors/rockefeller-john-d|Rockefeller]]'s [[actors/standard-oil|Standard Oil]], which moved from horizontal consolidation of refining to full vertical integration and the [[concepts/business-trust|trust]] form (1882). It provoked the regulatory and [[events/standard-oil-dissolution-1911|antitrust]] reaction (Interstate Commerce Act 1887; Sherman Act 1890) that defined Progressive-era political economy.
