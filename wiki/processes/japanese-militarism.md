# Japanese Militarism (the Collapse of Party Government, 1930–1945)

The slide of [[taisho-period|Taishō-democratic]] [[japan|Japan]] from **party government into
a military-dominated state** in the 1930s — the unravelling of the [[meiji-constitution-1889|Meiji
constitutional]] order that culminated in total mobilization and the
[[pacific-war-1941-1945|Pacific War]]. From the
[[cambridge-history-japan-v6-twentieth-century|Cambridge History of Japan Vol. 6]] (Gordon
Berger, ch. 2; Haruhiro Fukui, ch. 3, on the postwar reversal).

## Definition and Scope

Japanese militarism was less a single seizure of power than the **transfer of authority from
the parliamentary parties to the "specialist elites"** — above all the army and navy general
staffs and the civil bureaucracy. It exploited a structural flaw the
[[taisho-period|party-cabinet era]] had papered over: the **independence of the supreme
command** and the **divided powers** of the [[meiji-constitution-1889|Meiji constitution]],
which left the military answerable to the emperor rather than the cabinet or Diet. Where the
parties had *interpreted* the constitution to permit party rule, the militarists
**re-interpreted** the same document to marginalize the parties.

## Causal Drivers

- **The Depression and agrarian distress.** Finance Minister Inoue's deflationary
  gold-standard policy deepened the rural misery of the early 1930s, discrediting party
  government and feeding rural-rooted radicalism.
- **The London Naval Treaty controversy (1930).** [[taisho-period|Shidehara's]] "cooperative
  diplomacy" and the Hamaguchi cabinet's acceptance of naval limits were attacked as
  sacrificing security for Anglo-American harmony, and led the elite to grant the **general
  staffs a larger voice** in defense policy — a decisive enhancement of military influence.
- **The army's drive for continental autonomy.** Perceived threats from Chinese nationalism
  and the Soviet buildup made Manchuria seem a "lifeline," motivating the army's independent
  action.
- **Right-wing terror.** "Double-patriot" assassinations of business and political leaders
  intimidated the parties.

## Major Phases

- **The [[manchurian-incident-1931|Manchurian Incident (1931)]]** — the Kwantung Army's
  unauthorized seizure of Manchuria, which the cabinet could not control: the demonstration
  that the military now drove policy.
- **The end of party cabinets (1932).** The assassination of Prime Minister **Inukai
  Tsuyoshi (15 May 1932)** ended the era of party government; thereafter "national unity"
  cabinets led by bureaucrats and admirals replaced party leaders.
- **The February 26 Incident (1936)** — a bloody army mutiny by radical "Imperial Way"
  officers, suppressed but leaving the army's "Control faction" dominant and the civilian
  government cowed.
- **National mobilization (1937–40).** The [[pacific-war-1941-1945|China War]] from 1937
  brought the **National Mobilization Law (1938)** and the rise of "reformist" bureaucrats
  bent on a planned, controlled economy.
- **The New Order (1940).** The dissolution of the political parties into the single
  **Imperial Rule Assistance Association (IRAA)** under Konoe — the formal end of competitive
  politics — and the move toward the Axis alliance and southern expansion that led to war.

## Interaction

Militarism was bound up with [[japanese-imperialism|imperial expansion]] (Manchuria → China →
Southeast Asia) and culminated in the [[pacific-war-1941-1945|Pacific War]]. It rested on the
ideology of *kokutai* and the [[japanese-revolt-against-the-west|intellectual revolt against
the West]].

## End Conditions

Militarism ended utterly with the **1945 defeat** and the [[occupation-of-japan-1945-1952|Allied
Occupation]], which abolished the armed forces, purged militarists, and rewrote the
constitution. Fukui's chapter traces the **reverse trajectory** thereafter — "from instability
to stability" — as postwar Japan built a stable, civilian, party-democratic order under the
conservative **Liberal Democratic Party (from 1955)**, the mirror image of the prewar collapse.
