# The Germanization of Occupied Poland (1939–1944)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: the forced ethnic reordering of German-annexed Poland — expulsion of Poles and Jews, "repatriation" of ethnic Germans, racial screening, cultural suppression — conceived by Hitler on the analogy of British settlement of Australia and American westward expansion.

## Legal Basis and Drivers

Hitler's decree of 7 October 1939 made [[himmler-heinrich|Himmler]] **Reich Commissioner for the Strengthening of the German Race**, charged to repatriate ethnic Germans, "eliminate the harmful influence" of dangerous population elements, and plant new German colonies. Part of the broader [[german-occupation-of-poland-1939-1945|occupation regime]]; extension of the prewar [[nazi-racial-hygiene-program-1933-1939|racial-hygiene program]] into conquered territory.

## Major Phases

- **Expulsions.** 88,000 Poles and Jews arrested in Posen (December 1939) and deported in unheated freight cars in midwinter — 40 children frozen to death on one trainload reaching Cracow. By early 1941, 365,000 deported from Posen alone; over 1 million (a third Jewish) across former Poland, stripped of all property. The physician-diarist Zygmunt Klukowski recorded deportee convoys arriving "completely broken"; the officer Wilm Hosenfeld (14 December 1939): "There's system in it, the intention is to make these people sick, poor, helpless, they are to perish."
- **The German Ethnic List (Deutsche Volksliste)**, extended to all occupied territories 4 March 1941, ranked inhabitants for privileges. Greiser screened carefully; Forster enrolled whole villages regardless of ancestry (600,000 new applications by end-1942, most involuntary) — the two Regional Leaders' rivalry a case study in Nazi institutional Darwinism. Klukowski returned his form crossed out, signed "Polish National."
- **Child kidnapping.** Himmler's memo of 15 May 1940 (Hitler-approved) ordered removal of "racially valuable" children from resistant families to deny "this subhuman people of the east . . . a leader class"; Polish orphanages were emptied from spring 1941, children renamed with forged birth certificates and placed with vetted German families after indoctrination — 80% never returned to their families.
- **Ethnic German resettlement.** ~516,000 "repatriated" from the Baltics, eastern Poland, Romania, and elsewhere; screened at 1,500+ transit camps by SS Race and Settlement assessors with four weeks' training and 21 physical criteria. By December 1942 settlers held 20% of businesses in the annexed territories — yet only 500,000 of 1.25 million registered settlers had actually been resettled; most languished in camps for years.
- **The Zamość clearances** (November 1942–March 1943): Himmler's showcase Germanization of the Lublin region against Frank's objections — 110,000 Poles (31% of the regional population) expropriated and expelled, 47 villages cleared. Klukowski recorded an 8-year-old Polish boy shot dead by a German settler for picking apples (27 August 1943).

## Settler Mentality

Evans documents the colonizers' self-understanding: the Posen anatomist Hermann Voss wishing "the whole of Polish society" reduced to ashes while the SS used his university crematorium to burn shot Poles at night; the Hitler Youth press officer Melita Maschmann looting Polish farms at gunpoint for settlers in a spirit of colonial "civilizing mission."

## End Conditions

Halted and reversed by the Soviet advance of 1944–45 — followed by the westward [[expulsion-of-germans-1945|expulsion of the Germans]] themselves. `enabled: [[the-holocaust]]` (its expulsion and screening apparatus fed ghettoization); `contrasts_with` the Soviet social revolution in eastern Poland.
