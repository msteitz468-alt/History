# North American Mound-Building and Emergent Complexity

## Definition and Scope

This page tracks the long development of **monumental construction and social complexity in
North America north of Mexico**, from Archaic earthworks to the Mississippian climax, as set
out in [[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (Begun & Brashler, Ch 20). It covers two distinct
trajectories — the **eastern Woodlands** and the **Southwest** — and underpins CWH IV's
insistence that Native North America was **densely interconnected and historically dynamic**,
not "simple, isolated, and history-less."

## Eastern North America

Mound construction began in the Archaic, perhaps **5000 BCE**, by **egalitarian foragers** —
corporate cooperation without entrenched hierarchy. The astonishing **Poverty Point**
(Louisiana, from c. 3600 BCE), with its concentric ridges and 22 m Mound A, moved
extraordinary volumes of earth and drew in exotic lithics from up to 1,000 km away — yet
shows "no obvious evidence of social inequality." Its builders are now read as **egalitarian
hunter-gatherers**, the site a center of "ritual pilgrimage, cosmology, and powerful
charismatic leaders" rather than a managed economic hub.

- **Middle Woodland Hopewell (200 BCE–400 CE):** a vast **exchange and ceremonial complex**
  drawing obsidian (Wyoming), Knife River chalcedony (Dakota), copper, mica, marine shell, and
  more into the Ohio core. Geometric earthworks (**Newark**, Hopewell, Seip) encoded
  **astronomy and standard measurement** and served as **pilgrimage centers** (supported by
  mtDNA evidence of non-local interments). Socio-politically still a network of **egalitarian
  band/tribal communities**, not a hierarchy. Hopewell "disappeared" gradually by ~400 CE.
- **Late Woodland (c. 400–950 CE):** once dismissed as the dull "gray cultures," now seen as
  the seedbed of complexity — **maize agriculture became dominant** and **platform mounds**
  spread (common after 800 CE), setting the stage for the hierarchical, urban
  **[[cahokia|Mississippian]]** order from c. 950 CE.

## The Southwest

A separate trajectory, shaped by early **maize** from Mesoamerica (before 2100 BCE) and by
continuing contact with it. After ~200 BCE mobile farmers settled into **pithouse villages**;
**kivas** (ritual structures) appear 200–500 CE. Two centers crystallized:

- **Hohokam** (southern Arizona): from ~500 CE, vast **irrigation canal** systems, plazas, and
  Mesoamerican-derived **ball courts** (e.g. Snaketown) — an unusual "middle-range irrigation
  society" that never reached full statehood.
- **Ancestral Pueblo** (Four Corners): aggregated villages and, exceptionally,
  **[[chaco-canyon|Chaco Canyon]]**, where elite-controlled "great houses" and a road system
  managed turquoise exchange reaching 2,000 km to Mesoamerica.

## Interaction and Historiography

CWH IV stresses that complexity in North America cannot be reduced to subsistence or
socio-political organization alone: **ritual, cosmology, and pilgrimage** repeatedly drove
monumental labor among societies that were not states. Only "within the last fifteen years"
have archaeologists taken seriously the evidence for **elites and hierarchy** in the Southwest.
The Southwest's Mesoamerican connections feed directly into the
[[north-american-states-debate|debate over whether states existed north of Mexico]]. This
process runs alongside [[mesoamerican-state-formation]] and [[andean-state-formation]] as a
third, distinct New World pathway.

Charles Mann's [[mann-1491-2005|*1491*]] sets the mound-builders within the wider
[[pristine-myth|managed-landscape]] argument: [[cahokia|Cahokia]] (at its c. 1100 CE peak larger than
contemporary London) and the engineered bottomland of the American Bottom were a **humanized landscape**,
and the eastern woodlands the colonists found "natural" were in part shaped by Indian **fire-management**
(clearing underbrush, creating game-rich parklands). The depopulation of these societies by
[[virgin-soil-epidemics|introduced epidemics]] — and the consequent regrowth — was, on Mann's account,
part of what produced the "[[pristine-myth|virgin wilderness]]" of the European imagination.
