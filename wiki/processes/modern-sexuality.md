# Modern Sexuality

**Modern sexuality** is the transformation of sexual life, norms, and identities in the
modern world (c. 1800–present) under the pressure of **capitalism, the state, science, mass
media, and social movements** — the period in which sexuality became at once **medicalized**,
**commodified**, **politically governed**, and a central axis of public conflict. From
[[cambridge-world-history-of-sexualities-v4|*The Cambridge World History of Sexualities*,
Vol. 4]] — the modern endpoint of the [[history-of-sexuality|history of sexuality]].

## Definition and Scope

The process is the **reconstruction of sexuality by modern institutions** — markets,
medicine, the state, media, and political movements — c. 1800 to the present. It is not the
"liberation" of a fixed natural sexuality but its remaking, and it includes both
emancipatory currents (rights, the [[sexual-revolution]]) and disciplinary ones (eugenics,
commodification, surveillance).

## Causal Drivers

- **Capitalism and consumerism** — sex became a commodity and commodities erotic
  ("parasexuality": desire "deployed but contained" to sell goods).
- **Sexual science** — sexology (Krafft-Ebing, Ellis, Freud) recast sex as medical-scientific
  knowledge and produced the modern taxonomy of "sexualities."
- **The modern state** — eugenics, public health, and population/reproductive policy.
- **Mass media** and, from the mid-20th century, **feminist and LGBTQ+ movements**.

## Major Phases

1. **Medicalization (19th c.):** sexology and the classification of "sexualities"; the
   regulation of prostitution and venereal disease.
2. **State and population (late 19th–mid 20th c.):** eugenics, birth-control struggles, the
   sexual disruptions of the **World Wars**, and the sexual politics of dictatorships.
3. **Liberalization (1960s–70s):** the **[[sexual-revolution]]** in the post-war democracies.
4. **Politicization and backlash (1980s–present):** rights movements, AIDS, and the
   contemporary culture wars.

## Geographic Spread

Originating in the industrializing **West**, the process spread globally — unevenly and often
through **colonialism** (which used sexuality to define and rule the "other") and through
global media and markets — interacting everywhere with local traditions (see
[[sexuality-in-world-belief-systems]]).

## Interaction

Modern sexuality is entwined with **[[family-and-biopolitics|biopolitics]]** (state control
of reproduction), with **[[gender-history|gender]]**, with **capitalism**, and with the
[[urban-sexual-cultures|modern city]] as its theatre; its scholarly study *is* the
[[history-of-sexuality]].

## End Conditions

The process is **ongoing**: commodification and rights, liberalization and backlash continue
to advance together, so that the modern history of sexuality remains, in Vol. 4's reading, an
**ambivalent transformation** rather than a completed liberation.
