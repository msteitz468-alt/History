# Modern Sexuality

**Modern sexuality** is the transformation of sexual life, norms, and identities in the
modern world (c. 1800–present) under the pressure of **capitalism, the state, science,
mass media, and social movements**. It is the period in which sexuality became at once
**medicalized**, **commodified**, **politically governed**, and finally a central axis of
public conflict. From [[cambridge-world-history-of-sexualities-v4|*The Cambridge World
History of Sexualities*, Vol. 4]] — the modern endpoint of the
[[history-of-sexuality|history of sexuality]].

## Major drivers and phases

- **Medicalization and sexual science.** From the 19th century, **sexology**
  (Krafft-Ebing, Havelock Ellis, Freud) reframed sex as an object of medical-scientific
  classification, producing the modern taxonomy of "sexualities" and the
  [[history-of-sexuality|"homosexual" as a type of person]].
- **Capitalism and commodification.** Vol. 4's central thesis: modern sexuality is
  inseparable from the market. Sex became a **commodity** and commodities erotic — what
  Peter Bailey called **"parasexuality"** (desire "deployed but contained" to sell goods,
  shopping spaces, and leisure), from advertising to cosmetics, contraception, and
  pornography. This complicates "liberal assumptions about agency, liberation, and progress."
- **The state, eugenics, and reproductive control.** Modern states made sex a matter of
  **public health and population**: eugenics, the regulation of prostitution and venereal
  disease, and — contested across the century — **birth control and reproductive rights**
  (see [[family-and-biopolitics]]).
- **War.** The **World Wars** disrupted gender and sexual orders (mass mobilization,
  women's work, wartime intimacy and its policing).
- **The [[sexual-revolution|sexual revolution]]** of the 1960s–70s — contraceptive
  technology, decriminalization, feminism, and gay liberation — loosened norms in much of
  the world, even as commodification advanced.
- **Politics and backlash.** Sexuality became central to modern **politics**: persecuted
  under 20th-century dictatorships, liberalized in post-war democracies, mobilized by
  feminist and LGBTQ+ movements — and the target of recurring conservative and religious
  **backlash** ("sexuality under attack now").

## Significance

The modern era did not simply "liberate" a natural sexuality; it **reconstructed** it —
through markets, medicine, and the state — and made it a defining field of identity and
political struggle. Vol. 4's critical edge is its insistence that **commodification and
control advanced alongside liberation**, so that the modern history of sexuality is one of
ambivalent transformation rather than linear progress.
