# Desert Archaic

## Definition and Scope

Long-duration hunter-gatherer adaptation of the **Great Basin / Interior West**, type-sited at **Danger Cave, Utah**. Part of [[paleoindian-and-archaic-north-america]]; analogous to Eastern Archaic trends (sedentism pressures, small-game maximization, storage) but in arid environments without farming as the dominant trajectory until late, temporary Fremont intrusion. Contrasts with [[southwest-agricultural-development]].

## Causal Drivers

- Post-Pleistocene drying and the Anathermal–Altithermal–Medithermal climatic sequence
- Seed grinding and small-game (esp. rabbit) intensification
- Marsh and piñón seasonal rounds; caching manos/metates at base camps
- Late introduction of bow and arrow; temporary horticultural (Fremont) competition

## Major Phases

### Early Archaic (~9000 B.C. onward)

**Danger Cave** (type site): 34 m above Great Salt Lake; occupation from **9000 B.C.**; 4 m deposit, five major zones; dry preservation of fiber, bone, wood, leather. Other key sites: Hogup, Sudden Shelter (UT); Gatecliff, Leonard Rockshelter, Lovelock Cave (NV). Early deposits still mix big-game hunting with seed grinding.

### Middle Archaic (after Altithermal peak, ~2000 B.C. horizon)

Rabbit bones and grinding stones abundant; “the Archaic tendency toward maximization of small game in large numbers had progressed further in the Great Basin than in most regions by the beginning of European contact.” Marshes drying after **1200 B.C.**; seasonal rounds including piñón.

### Late Archaic (~A.D. 500)

Begins as horticulture spreads in East and Southwest. **Bow and arrow** introduced (possibly by Na-Dene from Canada), replacing the spear thrower. Temporary ~**900-year Fremont** horticultural intrusion from the south — but “the most successful adaptation remained an Archaic hunter-gatherer one.”

### Numic expansion (late precontact)

Ancestral Shoshones, Utes, Paiutes, and other **Numic** (Uto-Aztecan) speakers expanded NE from a SE California / S Nevada core. Ecologically “less advanced” yet replaced Fremont and semisedentary Klamath ancestors — “a rare case of an adaptive radiation filling in behind the ecological collapse of sedentary communities.”

## Geographic Spread

Great Basin core (Utah, Nevada) with related Desert Culture variants feeding Southwest Archaic sequences (San Dieguito, Cochise, Oshara — nested under [[southwest-agricultural-development]]).

## End Conditions

No single end: Archaic HG remained viable into historic contact; horticultural alternatives were episodic (Fremont) or peripheral (SW farming frontiers). Historic Numic peoples continue related lifeways under colonial pressure.
