# Mesoamerican Preclassic (Formative)

## Definition and Scope

The **Preclassic** or **Formative** stage of [[mesoamerica-culture-area|Mesoamerica]] —
from established agricultural villages to the threshold of Classic urban civilizations.
"Formative" and "Preclassic" are treated as interchangeable in Adams's CHNPA framework
([[mesoamerican-periodization]]): Preclassic ~1500 B.C.–A.D. 150, with Olmec "civilization"
by ~1350 B.C. and most defining Mesoamerican features in place by A.D. 150.

David C. Grove (CHNPA II.1 Ch. 3) treats central-highlands Preclassic as fully Neolithic-like
(agricultural villages + ceramics). Agriculture and settled life largely *preceded* pottery
(Archaic); the advent of ceramics is the common Preclassic marker, but pottery's start date
in the altiplano remains unclear, so Grove leaves the Preclassic beginning open-ended.

## Causal Drivers

- Maize-based farming (with beans, squash, and many other cultigens) after
  [[paleoindian-and-archaic-mesoamerica]] and [[maize-domestication]].
- Expanding exchange of obsidian, jadeite/greenstone, shell, iron-ore mirrors, feathers.
- Competitive ceremonial centers generating hereditary elites and early states across
  **multiple regions simultaneously** — empirical basis for multi-lineal rather than purely
  Olmec-diffusion models ([[olmec-mother-vs-sister-culture]]).
- Resource inequality among valleys underwriting village craft specialization and regional
  exchange (Grove).
- Population growth, forest clearance, and eventual irrigation/nucleation under Holocene
  climatic stress in the Late Preclassic highlands.

## Major Phases

### Early Preclassic / Early Formative (roughly 2000/1500–900 B.C.)

**Basin of Mexico and southern river valleys (Grove).** Preceramic sedentary/semisedentary
lakeshore societies may have been present by ~4000 B.C. (Zohapilco/Tlapacoya teosinte
5500–3500 B.C. remains equivocal for agriculture). ~3000 B.C. volcanic pumice may have
made the southern Basin unlivable for nearly half a millennium. Niederberger at Zohapilco:
occupation 2500–2000 B.C. with possible house foundations; earliest conclusive ceramic
date for Basin agricultural communities **1400 B.C.** (assemblage 1400–1200 B.C.),
including red-rimmed tecomates shared with southern lowlands — early interregional idea
diffusion via exchange rather than Classic-style long-distance trade.

By **1200 B.C.** the Basin and southern/eastern river valleys saw population growth under
maize agriculture (rainfall/humidity; some simple irrigation); 1200–900 B.C. forest
clearance slowly altered pine–oak environments.

- **Tlatilco** (western Basin piedmont): best-known Early Preclassic model village;
  surface refuse ~50 ha over >300 years (momentary village smaller); wattle-and-daub
  houses, some on raised platforms; hundreds of figurines and ~500 excavated graves
  c. 1200–900 B.C. Tolstoy: possible two endogamous moieties with rank continuum;
  highest rank = deeper extended graves + ≥13 objects including iron-ore mirrors from
  southern trade.
- **Coapexco** (2,600 m on Iztaccíhuatl): ~100 years, ~200 houses / ~1,000 people;
  specialized manos/metates and prismatic blades; distant-source obsidian trade
  (Boksenbaum).
- Optimal agriculture south of the Basin (Puebla, Morelos, Guerrero): three-tier
  settlement hierarchy in every valley (one large village, several smaller, numerous
  hamlets) → minor chiefdoms focused on largest villages as exchange/redistribution
  centers. Ceramic "ethnicity": Tlatilco–western Basin–Morelos shared pattern does *not*
  extend to Puebla/Guerrero/eastern Basin (Zohapilco); Grove argues deliberate ethnic
  markers while remaining economically interactive.
- Shared Early Preclassic pottery motifs ("were-jaguar," "fire serpent," "paw-wing")
  symbolize supernatural spirits/forces (not gods) of earth, sky, and infraworld; Tlatilco
  males practiced groin-area auto-sacrifice with sharks' teeth and other tools.
- Public architecture rare: Chalcatzingo early 2 m earthen platform; Teopantecuanitlan
  (Guerrero) clay-faced sunken patio c. 1400–900 B.C. unique then — Grove reads
  indigenous Guerrero precocity.

**Oaxaca (Marcus & Flannery, CHNPA Ch. 8).** House 20 at [[san-jose-mogote|San José Mogote]]
~1900–1400 B.C.: oldest known sedentary house and oldest ceramics (plain buff-brown bowls
and jars). Maize, beans, squash already cultivated for millennia; year-round residence by
best alluvium established de facto land rights. By 1400–1150 B.C. ~18–20 hamlets; ~1350
B.C. first public building (lime-stuccoed "Men's House" analog). Ranking 1150–850 B.C.:
greater SJM 791–1,976 persons over >70 ha with no peer; status continuum (jade,
slab-lined graves, magnetite mirrors traded to Morelos and **San Lorenzo**).

**Pacific coast (Sharer, CHNPA Ch. 10).** Chantuto bridges Archaic/Early Preclassic ~2000
B.C.; hiatus then **Barra** pottery c. 1500–1350 B.C.; **Ocos** c. 1300–1150 B.C.
(red-rimmed tecomates, shell impression, rocker stamping) from Chiapas to W El Salvador
with related Gulf Coast traditions. Large wattle-and-daub buildings at San Carlos and
Paso de la Amada (up to 10×20 m) — elite residences or communal lineage houses (undecided).

**Maya lowlands (Hammond, CHNPA Ch. 5).** Settled life before 1200 B.C.; pollen suggests
maize possibly mid-third millennium B.C. **Cuello** first sedentary occupation ~1200 B.C.
(Swasey 1200–900 B.C.): maize already adapted to humid lowlands (earlier highland
introduction); manioc, squash, beans, chile; ceramics distinctly lowland Maya with
**no close ties to Gulf Coast Olmec**.

**SE frontier (Sheets, CHNPA Ch. 9).** Early Formative 2500–1000 B.C. (Henderson);
Ocos-related ceramics in western El Salvador averaging 1590 ± 150 B.C. Marcus
speculates Pacific coast Chiapas–Guatemala–El Salvador may have developed complex society
coeval with or prior to the Olmec. Chalchuapa sedentary village ~1200 B.C.; Ixtepeque
obsidian provisioning route across Tehuantepec into Olmec area (obsidian at San Lorenzo).

### Middle Preclassic / Middle Formative (roughly 900–500/400 B.C.)

**Central highlands (Grove).** Rapid population growth; poorer northern/eastern Basin
settled despite drier conditions; chiefly rank intensified. Over ~a century, elaborate
Early Preclassic iconographic vessels and baby-like figurines replaced by plainer pottery
while jadeite/greenstone beads, pendants, figures, and bloodletting lancets appear —
iconography formerly on ceramics now elite-restricted on jade, legitimizing chiefly status.
After ~900 B.C.: ceramic griddles + processed lime → maize tortillas; nutritional
advantage and women's labor reorganization.

- **Chalcatzingo**: terraces; 70 m earthen platform; stone-faced sunken patio with
  bas-relief earth-supernatural altar; elite jadeite burials; C.8 "portrait" figurines
  (≥20 individuals, possibly chiefs/ancestors); cliffside ritual bas-reliefs and
  political stelae (often "decapitated").
- **Teopantecuanitlan**: stone-faced sunken patio by 800 B.C. with inverted-T monolithic
  faces; only Chalcatzingo and Teopantecuanitlan share stone-faced patios + bas-relief
  monumental art → likely alliance (possible marriage commemorated by Monument 21 —
  only identified woman in Middle Preclassic monumental art).
- **Grove on highland–Olmec links:** stone monumental art atypical of highlands and
  characteristic of Gulf [[olmec|Olmec]]; presence at Chalcatzingo/Teopantecuanitlan
  indicates significant associations (Gulf need for Guerrero greenstone) — limited elite
  alliance/symbol adoption in "frontier" style, **not** blanket Olmec domination of
  highland villages. Chapter deliberately written without once mentioning "Olmec
  influences" on central Mexican farming villages.

**Oaxaca.** Middle Formative chiefdoms 800–350 B.C.: adobe, early irrigation, three-tier
hierarchy headed by SJM 60–70 ha; Monument 3 (oldest Zapotec hieroglyphs) — sacrificed
prisoner with 260-day name "1 Earthquake"; raiding among rank societies.

**Maya lowlands.** Mamom sphere (600–400 B.C.) ceramic uniformity across the lowland
zone indicates high interaction; Xe/Mamom Maya fit Middle Preclassic shared-idea
landscape with Olmec as a **set of regional traditions**; earliest nondomestic public
architecture ~600 B.C. (Altar de Sacrificios, Tikal); large Mamom structures at Nakbe,
Rio Azul, Wakna suggest complex chiefdom by 400 B.C.

**Southern Maya Area.** Complex chiefdoms; Olmec-style traits concentrate at larger
sites as long-distance elite symbols rather than wholesale colonization; monumental
sculpture, status tombs with sacrifices.

**West Mexico.** Chupícuaro (Lerma, Guanajuato) Late Preclassic florescence; El Opeño
tombs ~1200 B.C.; Teuchitlán tradition San Felipe (~1000–300 B.C.) → El Arenal
(~350/300 B.C.–A.D. 150/200 tomb complex peak). Amuco (Balsas): Olmec figurine fragments
in Early Sésame raise West Mexico–Gulf questions.

### Late Preclassic / Late Formative (roughly 500 B.C.–A.D. 150/300)

**Central highlands.** By/soon after 500 B.C.: Chalcatzingo and many Middle Preclassic
villages abandoned; population nucleated around large "central place" villages with
pyramidal public architecture despite aridity (implies intensive irrigation). Cultural
focus shifts for ≥next millennium to the previously marginal [[basin-of-mexico|Basin of
Mexico]]. **[[cuicuilco|Cuicuilco]]**: by 300 B.C. largest Basin settlement — 27 m circular
pyramid, ≥11 rectangular pyramids, maybe ~400 ha / ~20,000 people; irrigation canals
under lava support "Mesoamerica's first city-state." **Teotihuacan Valley**: by 300 B.C.
600–800 ha / 20,000–40,000 inhabitants rivaling Cuicuilco; ~100 B.C. eruption devastates
Cuicuilco, leaving urbanization to Teotihuacan. Other centers: Amalucan irrigation;
Cholula Preclassic under later pyramid; Xochicalco settlement; eastern Basin piedmont
centers; Tlapacoya/Zohapilco pyramid.

**Oaxaca — Monte Albán foundation.** ~500 B.C. [[monte-alban|Monte Albán]] founded on
previously unoccupied mountain (Blanton confederacy / Greek **synoikism** analogy);
3,600–7,200 by 400 B.C.; 10,000–20,400 by 250 B.C. Building L *danzantes* gallery
(>300 slain captives). Protoclassic after 200 B.C.: kingship (palace, royal tombs),
standardized two-room temples, ballcourt, Building J conquest slabs (subject territory
radius ~80–150 km; documented Cuicatlán military takeover ~200–100 B.C.); four-tier
settlement hierarchy.

**Maya lowlands.** Late Preclassic (400 B.C.–A.D. 250) revised from "simple villages":
masonry superstructures, vaulted roofs, polychrome ceramics, stelae, hieroglyphic writing,
bar-and-dot numerals already present before third century A.D. Cuello massive platform
with human sacrifices and *pop* (woven mat) motif; Cerros ceremonial precinct and
1,200 m canal; Edzna canal 12 km / ~1.7 million person-days; El Mirador colossal
(El Tigre and Monos pyramids ~150 B.C., up to 55 m, >250,000 m³ each; Danta arguably
tallest Maya construction); possible Tikal 25 km defensive ditch; Calakmul Structure 2;
Lamanai N10-43. Stelae complex collocation by early second century A.D.; "Protoclassic"
debate marginalized.

**Southern Maya highlands/Pacific.** Late Preclassic peak at [[kaminaljuyu|Kaminaljuyu]]
(most powerful southern center; El Chayal obsidian hub) and Chalchuapa; Maya art style
and writing system appear to develop in the southern Maya area (origins debated).
**Ilopango** eruption ~A.D. 200–250 (Sheets ~A.D. 200; Sharer ~A.D. 250) devastates
central-western El Salvador and disrupts Pacific coastal Guatemala — major natural
endpoint for SE Preclassic trajectories.

**SE frontier Late Formative.** Dense Salvadoran landscape of large independent chiefdoms
after Olmec-influence decline; Quelepa founded ~400 B.C.; Los Naranjos Jaral phase
(800–400 B.C.) with massive defensive ditch.

**Gulf Coast.** After 600 B.C. complex societies spread west of Olmec heartland; Tres
Zapotes Epi-Olmec with Izapa-style influence; La Mojarra Stela 1 (A.D. 143, 156) Epi-Olmec
script (Justeson & Kaufman Zoquean decipherment).

## Geographic Spread

Gulf Coast Olmec heartland; Central Highlands ([[basin-of-mexico]], Morelos, Puebla,
Guerrero); [[oaxaca-valley|Oaxaca]] and Mixteca; Maya lowlands (Belize, Petén, Yucatán);
southern Maya highlands and Pacific slope (Chiapas–Guatemala–El Salvador); West Mexico
(Jalisco lake system, Chupícuaro, Balsas); SE frontier into Honduras.

## End Conditions

Regional transitions into Classic periodization — not a single pan-Mesoamerican event:

- Basin: Cuicuilco destroyed by lava; Teotihuacan urban takeoff (Patlachique–Tzacualli)
- Oaxaca: Protoclassic/Classic Monte Albán state
- Maya lowlands: Early Classic historicity on monumental inscriptions from c. A.D. 250
  (Tikal Stela 29 = A.D. 292 still earliest legible lowland Initial Series)
- Southern Maya: Ilopango disaster and Terminal Preclassic southern collapse/reordering
- Gulf: Epi-Olmec → Early Classic coastal centers under Teotihuacan commercial shadow

## Historiography (CHNPA II.1)

Until the 1970s highland Preclassic chronology rested mainly on Zacatenco, El Arbolillo,
and Tlatilco wrongly treating Tlatilco and Zacatenco as contemporaneous Middle Preclassic;
Tolstoy & Paradis (1970) reassigned Tlatilco burials to Early Preclassic and El Arbolillo
to Middle with Zacatenco — foundation of the modern sequence. Grove's sister-culture
lean and deliberate avoidance of blanket "Olmec influence" language (Sharer & Grove 1989)
marks a scholarly shift. Hammond revises Late Preclassic Maya from "simple villages" to
near-Classic complexity. Diehl inventories mother- vs sister-culture positions without
adjudication. Sheets and Sharer both push against Olmec-as-sole-mother while affirming
significant Gulf–SE/southern interaction.
