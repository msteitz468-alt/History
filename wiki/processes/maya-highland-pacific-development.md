# Maya Highlands and Pacific Coast Development

## Definition and Scope

Robert Sharer (CHNPA II.1 Ch. 10) defines the **southern Maya Area** as the Maya highlands of Guatemala (and adjacent Chiapas highlands) plus the Pacific coastal plain of Chiapas, Guatemala, and western El Salvador — a linked developmental theater distinct from, but interacting with, the [[maya-civilization|Maya lowlands]]. Chronology (Sharer): Preclassic c. 2000 B.C.–A.D. 250 (Early 2000–1000; Middle 1000–400; Late 400 B.C.–A.D. 100); Terminal Preclassic A.D. 100–250; Classic A.D. 250–800; Postclassic A.D. 900–1500. Research long lagged the lowlands; synthesis remains unbalanced and provisional.

## Causal Drivers

- **Environment:** Pacific plain of Quaternary sediments from Tehuantepec through Chiapas/Guatemala into W El Salvador — avenue for migration and commerce; rich alluvial/volcanic soils; highest rainfall in the Maya area (>3,000 mm on Chiapas/W Guatemala Pacific slopes). Highlands north of the plain: temperate above ~800 m; southern volcanic belt densely occupied (Valley of Guatemala = largest basin; Quetzaltenango; Ahuachapán & Zapotitán in W Salvador). **El Chayal** and **Ixtepeque** obsidian; jadeite/serpentine on Sierra de las Minas / middle Motagua. Quetzal plumes from Alta Verapaz rainforest as elite symbols.
- **Economy:** littoral food and salt; **cacao** production and trade-route control; highland minerals (obsidian, jadeite) to lowlands via Usumacinta–Chixoy and Motagua.
- **Hazards:** Ilopango ~A.D. 250 destroyed life within 20–30 km and rendered ~100 km radius uninhabitable up to ~200 years; [[ceren-salvador|Cerén]] buried ~A.D. 600; historical earthquakes (Antigua, 1918 Guatemala City, 1976 Motagua fault).
- **Interaction:** Gulf Coast Formative networks; lowland Classic politics; Teotihuacan-related elite symbols; Postclassic Pipil and Mexicanized Maya movements.

## Major Phases

### Early–Middle Preclassic

Pacific littoral: Chantuto preceramic; **Barra** pottery c. 1500–1350 B.C.; **Ocos** c. 1300–1150 B.C. from Chiapas to W El Salvador. Large Ocos buildings at San Carlos and Paso de la Amada (up to 10×20 m) — elite residences or communal lineage houses (undecided). Middle Preclassic: complex chiefdoms; “Olmec style” traits as long-distance elite-authority symbols rather than wholesale colonization; Escuintla piedmont site hierarchy growth 800–400 B.C.; monumental sculptures of leaders Chiapas–W El Salvador. [[kaminaljuyu|Kaminaljuyú]] emerges from smaller Valley of Guatemala settlements; Salamá Valley elite tombs (Los Mangales sacrifices); El Portón stela-altar pair and early glyphs ~400 B.C.

### Late Preclassic peak

Full expression of complex society with distinctive **Maya art style and writing** — both appear to have developed in the southern Maya area (origins debated). Larger sites shift from single platforms to complexes of monumental architecture. Economy: cacao and crops plus highland mineral trade under regional elite control. [[kaminaljuyu|Kaminaljuyú]] dominant (El Chayal hub); peers: [[izapa|Izapa]], Abaj Takalik, El Baúl, Chalchuapa, Chiapa de Corzo, El Portón. **Usulután** ware unites the south. Earliest Long Count and stela-text packages concentrated in southern (and Mixe-Zoquean western) centers. Mosaic of independent **petty states** (Sharer).

### Terminal Preclassic decline (~A.D. 100–250)

Cessation of carved hieroglyphic stelae; abandonment of many Pacific and inland centers — **far more drastic in the south** than in the lowlands. Trigger: **Ilopango** ~A.D. 250; chain reaction of trade-route collapse; rise of **Balberta** on central coast without old political/religious symbols; fusion of southern and lowland Terminal Preclassic traditions contributes ingredients of Classic lowland civilization (Sharer).

### Classic

Early Classic research meager vs lowlands; Kaminaljuyú principal highland referent. Older Teotihuacan-colonial model rejected by Sharer in favor of local elite appropriation of foreign traits; Balberta grows without significant Teotihuacan contact. Northern highlands (Alta Verapaz, Nebaj, Cotzal): renewed growth; possible lowland elite colonization. Late Classic: population maximum in Valley of Guatemala; Cotzumalhuapan sculptural style on cacao coast (Bilbao, El Baúl, El Castillo) — proposed Mexican/Pipil-related intrusion; Asunción Mita ties to [[copan|Copán]]; Chalchuapa/Tazumal and San Andrés commercial ties to Copán; Cerén everyday life window; Chixoy polities then abandonment with Fine Orange and Plumbate at terminal Classic.

### Postclassic

Early Postclassic knowledge heavily ethnohistoric (*Popol Vuh* etc.). Chontal Mexicanized Maya expansion; Pipil migration waves A.D. 900–1000 and 1200–1350 (Fowler) colonizing coastal plain. Highland settlement **dramatic shift** to fortified hilltops/promontories with ditch-and-wall works — capitals of competing conquest states. Late Postclassic: Quiché and Cakchiquel segmentary conquest states (consensus favors Mexicanized elite intrusion + segmentary state, contested by internal-process models); major centers Mixco Viejo, Chinautla Viejo, Atitlán, Zaculeu, Utatlán/Gumarcaaj, Iximché. Quiché consolidation ~1350–1400; Cakchiquel revolt ~1470; Spanish conquest ends independent cultural development (Sharer).

## Geographic Spread

Guatemalan and Chiapas highlands; Soconusco and Pacific piedmont; linkages into El Salvador and the [[southeast-mesoamerican-frontiers|southeastern frontiers]] (Sheets Ch. 9); Motagua and Chixoy corridors to the lowlands.

## Interaction

- Lowland Classic politics and commodity demand (obsidian, jade, feathers, cacao).
- Central Mexican (Teotihuacan, later Nahua/Pipil) elite symbols and migrations.
- Gulf Coast Formative and Terminal Classic Chontal networks.
- Mixe-Zoquean Pacific/Chiapas writing-calendar experiments shared with early Maya.

## End Conditions

Spanish conquest of highland kingdoms (16th c.); continuous Maya populations and ethnohistoric memory ([[popol-vuh|Popol Vuh]], Annals of the Cakchiquels). Independent political development ceases with conquest (Sharer’s closing frame).

## Colonial–modern continuation (bridge)

This process ends where independent highland political development ends. The **same highland populations and places** — Guatemalan highlands, Chiapas highlands, Pacific piedmont labor zones — enter Spanish rule, demographic collapse, congregación, Liberal coffee labor, civil war, and diaspora under the long-duration process [[highland-maya-colonial-modern]] (Lovell, CHNPA II.2 Ch. 21).
