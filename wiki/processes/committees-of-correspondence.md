# Committees of Correspondence

## Definition and Scope

Standing **committees of correspondence** were local and provincial bodies that exchanged political intelligence, resolutions, and constitutional arguments among towns and colonies, especially from Boston’s **2–3 November 1772** committee outward. [[schiff-revolutionary-samuel-adams-2022|Schiff]] presents them as a daringly original institution under an inoffensive name—“news service, alarm system, to some a proto-terrorist cell”; later “engines of a revolution,” “furnaces of propaganda,” “the germ of a government.” They are the core mechanism by which [[adams-samuel|Samuel Adams]] nationalized a Boston quarrel.

## Causal Drivers

Imperial crises (Townshend duties, occupation, residual tea duty, crown pay for justices August 1772 “like thunder”) plus the limits of sporadic town meetings and newspapers alone. Adams had floated intercolonial correspondence earlier (Candidus; letter to Arthur Lee). True design, as he later put it: circulate earliest intelligence of importance to country friends; establish a union formidable to adversaries—“Where there is a spark of patriotic fire, we will enkindle it”; assault on one = assault on all.

## Major Phases

1. **Creation (2–3 Nov 1772):** ~10 p.m. vote; 21 men sworn to secrecy; Cushing sidled off; Hancock pleaded private business. Otis made titular head of the “grand committee for stating their rights” (Hutchinson: Adams taming Otis). Boston rights pamphlet **20 November** (Adams with Otis, Quincy Jr.—natural-rights section).
2. **Slow uptake then surge:** by **21 Dec 1772** only Roxbury, Plymouth, Cambridge, Marblehead; by early **March 1773** about one-third of Massachusetts towns; Virginia appointed after Boston encouragement. A Loyalist later noted the colonies had been “13 disunited bodies” until the committees.
3. **Hutchinson letters (June 1773)** and House hijack: illegal entities “rooted in half of Massachusetts”; promoted “disturbers of the peace” to “usurpers of government.”
4. **Tea-crisis instrument:** 21 Oct letter on “true design”; **6 Nov** outreach to every Massachusetts town; first alarm-system use when Adams alerted neighbors after Castle soldiers brandished weapons; committee with five neighboring towns directed the tea matter.
5. **Post-tea:** Lord Dartmouth ordered elimination of “unusual and unconstitutional” committees (they displeased the king); via the same entities Adams heard a countryside chorus (Newbury, Sandwich, Colrain, South Hadley, Portsmouth, etc.). Wedderburn at Franklin’s Privy Council reprimand (29 Jan 1774): committees + the “great twelve-penny book” as ready-drawn declaration of revolt, approved by 60–70 towns. Tracks led to the Philadelphia gathering—Adams repeatedly floated a congress (minimal expense, great advantages).

## Geographic Spread

Massachusetts interior towns first; then sister colonies along the Atlantic seaboard; Virginia’s appointment a critical intercolonial hinge.

## Interaction

**enabled:** [[boston-tea-party-1773]] coordination and rapid intercolonial messaging (Revere rides). Concurrent with [[sons-of-liberty|Sons of Liberty]] activism (some prominent Sons edged away from the committees). Absorbed into provincial congresses and the Continental Congress system once open war began—`part_of` the political infrastructure of [[american-revolution]].

## End Conditions

Absorbed into provincial congresses and the Continental Congress apparatus after 1774–75; wartime committees of safety and later state governments superseded the correspondence form.
