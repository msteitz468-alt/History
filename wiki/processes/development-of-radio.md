# Development of Radio

The **development of radio** — wireless transmission of intelligence by electromagnetic waves — emerged in the 1890s from the convergence of Hertz's experimental confirmation of Maxwell's electromagnetic theory, the invention of tuned resonant circuits, and commercial and naval demand for communication without wires.

## Definition and Scope

Radio depends on a transmitter that generates oscillations, an antenna and ground system, tuning to select frequencies, and a detector at the receiver. [[actors/tesla-nikola|Nikola Tesla]]'s high-frequency work and his 1893 lectures demonstrated the principles of tuned wireless transmission and reception, and he held foundational patents; [[actors/marconi-guglielmo|Guglielmo Marconi]] built the first commercially successful systems and made the first claimed transatlantic transmission (1901).

## Priority and Interaction

The Tesla–Marconi priority dispute (see [[events/marconi-radio-priority-dispute-1943]]) ran for decades and was decided in Tesla's favor by the U.S. Supreme Court in 1943. Other contributors included Oliver Lodge, Reginald Fessenden, and Edwin Armstrong. The process culminated in the rise of broadcasting and corporate wireless (RCA) in the 1910s–1920s.
