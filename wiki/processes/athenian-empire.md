# Athenian Empire (Delian League)

The transformation of the anti-Persian Delian League (founded 478/7 BCE) into an Athenian-dominated empire. Members paid tribute (phoros) to Athens in exchange for protection; Athens enforced compliance with its fleet, installed garrisons/cleruchies, and controlled league finances and policy. At its height under Pericles it funded the Parthenon, radical democracy (jury pay), and cultural flourishing, but also provoked resentment leading to the Peloponnesian War.

## OHCW (Boardman/Griffin/Murray 1986) — Hornblower (Ch.6) / Boardman (Ch.12)

[[sources/boardman-oxford-history-classical-world-1986|OHCW (1986)]] supplies Hornblower’s mid-1980s synthesis of the empire’s rise, instruments, and cultural pay-off:

**Phases.** 478 naval empire foundation under Athens; 470s–460s offensive against Persia culminating at [[cimon|Cimon]]’s Eurymedon; Thasos revolt mid-460s; Peace of Callias ~449 ending formal Persian hostilities; 446 settlement recognizing the Naval Empire while Athens abandoned Boeotia and other mainland gains; post-446 expansion (Amphipolis 437; Samos 440/39) and path to the Great [[peloponnesian-war|Peloponnesian War]] of 431–404.

**Instruments of control (seven).** Economic (Hellespont guards/taxes; ships then money tribute); administrative/military garrisons and fleet; judicial concentration of serious cases at Athens; religious propaganda (Ionian mother-city; Athena precincts); territorial cleruchies and elite land-holding in allied territory; social (451 citizenship law); political interference (generally but not always supporting democrats against oligarchs).

**Oppression “was” vs “became.”** That the fifth-century empire was or became oppressive is not disputed: the 377 Second Athenian Naval Confederacy inscription explicitly repudiates tribute, territorial encroachments, garrisons, and governors. Sparse pre-450 evidence may create an illusion of qualitative change after 449; Tribute List arrears suggest disaffection. Hornblower accepts economic necessity (grain, metals, timber) without minimizing “naked aggressiveness and greediness.”

**Empire pays for democracy and culture.** Imperial revenue, greatly increased by Cimon’s early-460s operations, made possible the 462 democratic changes of Ephialtes and [[pericles|Pericles]] — jury pay, then pay for boule and festivals — so that democracy was “paid for by the allies.” Pericles’ diversion of League funds to rebuild Athens (Parthenon as war memorial to Athens/Athena more than pure cult; Propylaea; Erechtheum; countryside temples) was the greatest state patronage program until Hellenistic capitals (Boardman). After 404 Spartan “liberation,” a Second Confederacy (378) abjured fifth-century abuses but by 373 “contributions” and garrisons reappeared. Hornblower’s closing verdict after Chaeronea: “Imperialism had after all proved incompatible with democracy.”
