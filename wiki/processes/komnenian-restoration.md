## Definition and Scope

The **Komnenian restoration** is the century-long recovery of the Byzantine empire
under the **Komnenos dynasty** (1081–1185), beginning with **Alexios I Komnenos**
(1081–1118) and continuing under **John II** (1118–1143) and **Manuel I** (1143–1180).
[[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] (Michael Angold, Chs 8 and — for the twelfth century —
Paul Magdalino, Ch. 20) calls it an "astonishing" reversal of the near-collapse that
followed [[battle-of-manzikert-1071|Manzikert (1071)]]: from a state "struggling for its
existence," Byzantium became once again the leading power of the eastern Mediterranean.

## Causal Drivers

- **The crisis after Manzikert.** Civil war, the loss of Anatolia to the Seljuks, the
  Pecheneg occupation of the Danube provinces, and the Norman conquest of Byzantine
  Italy brought the empire to the brink.
- **The seizure of power by the military aristocracy.** [[alexios-i-komnenos|Alexios I]]
  came to the throne (1081) as the candidate of the great military families and rebuilt
  the state around a **ruling family group** — a militarised, kin-based regime that
  displaced the old civil bureaucracy and bound the aristocracy to the dynasty by
  marriage and title.
- **Fiscal and military reorganisation**, debasement and then restabilisation of the
  coinage, and the skilful use of **diplomacy, alliances, and the Italian naval powers**
  (the trade privileges granted to Venice) to compensate for diminished resources.

## Major Phases

1. **Survival and consolidation under Alexios I (1081–1118).** Alexios beat off the
   Normans (Robert Guiscard and Bohemond), crushed the Pechenegs (Levounion, 1091), and
   exploited the **[[first-crusade-1095|First Crusade]]** (which he had partly solicited)
   to recover the western coast of Anatolia — while struggling to control the crusaders'
   ambitions.
2. **Reconquest under John II (1118–1143).** A capable soldier-emperor who recovered
   more of Anatolia and asserted Byzantine suzerainty over the crusader principality of
   Antioch.
3. **The apogee and overreach under Manuel I (1143–1180).** Manuel restored Byzantine
   prestige to its twelfth-century height — intervening in Italy, Hungary, the crusader
   states, and against the Seljuks — but his defeat at **Myriokephalon (1176)** ended
   hopes of reconquering the Anatolian interior, and his Latin-friendly, overextended
   policies left the empire dangerously dependent on, and resented by, the West.

## Interaction

The restoration is inseparable from the [[crusades|Crusades]] (which Byzantium both used
and feared) and from the rise of the [[commercial-revolution|Italian maritime
republics]], whose trade privileges and resentments would culminate, a generation after
Manuel's death, in the [[fourth-crusade-1204|Fourth Crusade's sack of Constantinople
(1204)]].

## End Conditions

The Komnenian system depended on strong individual emperors and on managing the Latin
relationship. Manuel's death (1180) was followed by dynastic collapse, anti-Latin
reaction (the massacre of the Latins in Constantinople, 1182), and the [[angelos-dynasty|
Angelos]] decline — the prelude to **1204** (Cycle 9 / Magdalino, Ch. 20).
