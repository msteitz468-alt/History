# Indian Ocean Slave Trade (19th Century)

## Definition and Scope
Maritime and overland trade in enslaved Africans (primarily from East Africa) across the western Indian Ocean, distinct in scale, conditions, and routes from the Atlantic Middle Passage but equally traumatic. Focused on East Africa to Zanzibar, Arabia, the Gulf, Mascarenes, and beyond.

## Causal Drivers
Demand from Busaidi clove plantations (Zanzibar/Pemba using slave labor), Gulf pearling and date plantations, Arabian needs, and broader colonial commodity production. British antislavery treaties (1822 limiting range, 1873 final on Barghash) complicated enforcement but did not immediately end trade while demand existed.

## Major Phases
- Betsimisaraka (Madagascar) fleets raiding Comoros and East Africa (1785–1817).
- Peak dhow trade mid-century.
- Slow decline after 1873; legal end ~1902 in many areas.
- Slavery persisted in Arabia into the late 20th century; smuggling continued.

## Geographic Spread
From Mozambique and northern Mozambique coast, Kilwa, Zanzibar to Arabia, Gulf, India, Comoros, Madagascar, Mascarenes, South Africa, and occasionally Brazil (Portuguese/Brazilian ships).

## Interaction
Tied to Omani/Busaidi empire, British naval patrols, transition to indentured labor, Indian merchant networks, and piracy suppression. Victims often children, young people, and women; conditions on dhows included overcrowding, thirst, and deception.

## End Conditions
British naval enforcement and treaties; shift to indentured labor systems; eventual legal abolition (varied by region).

## Historiography / Collection Coverage Note
Grounded in Alpers via Royal Navy interviews, liberated captives’ testimonies (e.g., Swema Yao girl 1865, Makua boy Petro Kilekwa), and contemporary reports.
