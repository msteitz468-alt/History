# Roman Colonization (Republic)

The systematic Roman Republican practice of founding new settlements (coloniae) on annexed or conquered land, extending Roman military presence, agricultural exploitation, and — over time — legal and cultural Romanization throughout Italy and eventually the wider Mediterranean. Colonies took two principal legal forms: "Latin colonies" (coloniae Latinae), whose settlers held the intermediate "Latin Rights" short of full Roman citizenship, and later "citizen colonies" (coloniae civium Romanorum), whose settlers retained full Roman citizenship.

## Definition and Scope

Distinct from the roughly contemporary [[greek-colonization|Greek]], [[phoenician-colonization|Phoenician]], and later [[seleucid-colonization|Seleucid]] colonization movements, Roman colonization was tightly bound to territorial annexation following conquest — colonies were typically planted on land confiscated from defeated Italian peoples, such as the [[roman-commonwealth-formation-338bce|ager Veientanus]] distributed after the fall of Veii, and served simultaneously as garrisons, land grants to citizens and veterans, and instruments of political control over newly subjugated regions.

## Major Phases

Early colonization concentrated on securing strategic points in Latium and central Italy in the fourth and early third centuries BCE; a second wave following the wars against the Samnites, Etruscans, and Pyrrhus extended colonies across the peninsula; the practice was later adapted (and became politically contentious, notably under the Gracchi) for large-scale veteran and citizen resettlement in the late Republic.

## Geographic Spread

From an initial concentration in Latium and central Italy, colonization extended progressively into Etruria, Campania, Samnium, and eventually across the length of the Italian peninsula, forming the network of allied and citizen communities that underpinned the "commonwealth" structure of the Roman confederation before the Social War extended citizenship universally in 90–89 BCE.

## Interaction with Other Processes

Colonization was closely bound up with Rome's broader military expansion and with the [[struggle-of-the-orders|Struggle of the Orders]], since access to newly distributed colonial land was a recurring point of contention between patricians and plebeians.

## End Conditions

The practice as a mechanism of Italian expansion effectively ended once Rome had absorbed the whole peninsula and extended citizenship to its Italian allies after the Social War; colonization continued thereafter chiefly as a tool for provincial settlement and veteran resettlement under the late Republic and Empire, in a substantially altered form.
