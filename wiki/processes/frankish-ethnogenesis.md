# Frankish Ethnogenesis

**The process by which the Franks coalesced as a people** out of a loose cluster of
Rhine-mouth tribes during the third to sixth centuries — a "West Germanic" variant of
[[ethnogenesis]] markedly different from the Gothic case, and, in Geary's reading, a
creation of the Roman frontier rather than an ancient migrating nation.

## Definition and Scope

The name *Frank* first appears in Roman sources of the **mid-third century**, designating a
variety of Istwaeonic tribes (Chamavi, Chattuari, Bructeri, Ampsivarii, Salii, and others)
"so loosely connected that some scholars have denied altogether that they formed a
confederation." The name meant "the hardy" or "the brave," only later acquiring the sense
the Franks themselves preferred — "the free."

Unlike the Goths, the western confederations (Franks, Alemanni, Bavarii) "did not for the
most part preserve ancient tribal traditions" and "were not organized into stable *regna*,
or kingdoms, prior to their entry into the Empire." Frankish identity was assembled late and
largely inside the Roman orbit.

## Causal Drivers

- **Roman frontier policy and service.** [[barbarization-of-the-roman-army|Roman military
  service]] fused Frankish and Roman identity directly. A third-century Pannonian funerary
  inscription reads *"Francus ego cives, miles romanus in armis"* — "I am a Frank by
  nationality, but a Roman soldier under arms." **Trier** was where many Frankish
  and Alemannic chieftains first entered Roman service, returning as "imperial Germans" to rule
  their peoples in Rome's interest.
- **Settlement as laeti.** The **Salians** were settled as *laeti* in Toxandria (near the modern
  Campine, Netherlands) to return land to cultivation and supply recruits.
- **Elite careers.** From Constantine's reign a Frank, **Bonitus**, became a Roman general; by the
  late fourth century Frankish commanders (Arbogast, Bauto, Richomer) dominated the western army.

## Major Phases

1. **Third-century confederation** of Rhine-Weser ("West Germanic") tribes under the new name *Frank*.
2. **Imperial-German leadership** (4th–5th c.): Frankish commanders as *magistri militum*; [[childeric|Childeric]]
   (d. 482), buried at Tournai with Byzantine, Hunnic, Germanic, and Gallo-Roman grave goods, was the last
   to serve as a traditional imperial German.
3. **Ethnogenesis completed under [[clovis|Clovis]]** (d. 511): conquest, conversion, law, and the
   elimination of rival kinglets fused the disparate groups into the single *gens Francorum* of the
   [[merovingian-franks|Merovingian kingdom]].

## The poverty of Frankish origin-myth

Tellingly, the Franks had **no genuine ancient tradition of their own**. [[gregory-of-tours|Gregory of
Tours]] preserves competing legends (a Pannonian and a Trojan origin), which Geary reads as betraying
"both the fact that the Franks knew little about their background and that they may have felt some
inferiority" beside peoples with glorious origin myths. The Trojan legend was a borrowed Roman literary
form, not an inherited memory.

## Interaction and Outcome

Frankish ethnogenesis is the western counterpart to [[gothic-peoples|Gothic ethnogenesis]] and is driven
throughout by [[barbarization-of-the-roman-army]] and the [[comitatus]] tradition. Its end-condition is
the durable [[merovingian-franks|Merovingian Frankish]] kingdom — and, because the Franks adopted the
**orthodox Christianity** of the Gallo-Roman population rather than Gothic Arianism, the most successful
amalgamation of Roman and barbarian of all the successor states.
