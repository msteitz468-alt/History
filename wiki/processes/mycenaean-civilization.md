# Mycenaean Civilization

The **first high civilization of the Greek mainland** — the Late Bronze Age palace
culture named for **[[mycenae|Mycenae]]**, "the city of Agamemnon." Its speakers
were **Greeks** (proved by [[aegean-scripts|Linear B]]); it arose from the fusion of
the [[coming-of-the-greeks-1900bce|Middle Helladic]] mainland with
**[[minoan-civilization|Minoan]]** influence, conquered Crete, and supplied the
**heroic age** that classical Greece remembered in epic. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (Ch. XIV, Stubbings; the tablets in
Ch. XIII, Chadwick).

## Definition and Scope

"Mycenaean" (= Late Helladic, LH I–III) denotes the civilization of mainland Greece
and its Aegean reach from ~1600 to ~1100 BCE. This page covers its **rise** (LH I–II,
~1600–1400), the period of CAH Vol. II.1; the Amarna-age zenith and the
[[late-bronze-age|Late Bronze]] collapse belong to later volumes. Stubbings stresses
that, uniquely for the prehistoric Aegean, **Greek legend preserves a broadly valid
historical memory** of this age — anchored to surviving monuments (the walls of
Tiryns, the *tholoi*) and to localized dynastic traditions.

## Causal Drivers: the Shaft Graves problem

The rise is visible above all in the **[[mycenae|Shaft Graves of Mycenae]]**
(Grave Circles B and A, ~1650–1500), where the modest Middle Helladic world
suddenly blazes with **gold** — masks, inlaid weapons, Minoan-style cups. The
sources of this wealth and the new culture are debated:

- a **fusion** of native Helladic forms (Minyan pottery, the *kantharos*, the
  megaron) with imported **[[minoan-civilization|Minoan]]** art and craft (the
  inlaid daggers, marine motifs, signet-rings, faience);
- an **Egyptian connexion** — Egyptian imports in the graves, the first
  **horse-drawn chariots** in Greece, carved grave-stelai, and a metal-inlay axe in
  Queen Ahhotep's tomb (the name of **Ahmose**, expeller of the Hyksos) — dating the
  graves firmly to the early sixteenth century;
- new **warrior overlords**, whose broad bearded faces (the gold masks) and martial
  art are "quite unlike the elegant, smooth-chinned Minoans."

Stubbings ties the new wealth and the chariot to the **expulsion of the
[[hyksos|Hyksos]] from Egypt**, and links it to the Greek legend of **Danaus**, the
king "out of Egypt" who took the Argolid — reading the Mycenaean efflorescence as
one limb of the same westward movement that produced the Hyksos and Minoan MM III.

## Major Phases

- **LH I (~1600–1500):** the Shaft Graves; the rise of Mycenae; the first **tholos
  ("beehive") tombs** (Messenia, Thessaly) replacing the shaft grave.
- **LH II (~1500–1400):** a true **palace civilization** spreads across central and
  southern Greece — Mycenae, Tiryns, Pylos, Thebes, Orchomenus (the "Treasury of
  Minyas"), Iolcus — with Minoanizing architecture (ashlar, columns, gypsum,
  fresco) over the native **megaron**. Pottery (LH II) rivals and surpasses its
  Minoan models (the Vaphio cups; the Ephyraean goblet). The **Mycenaean takeover
  of [[knossos|Knossos]]** (~1450) makes Greece, not Crete, the Aegean centre.

## Geographic Spread and Interaction

Mycenaean trade and settlement pushed to **Melos, Rhodes (Ialysos), Miletus, and
[[troy|Troy]]** (contact from LH II), to **Cyprus** and the copper trade, to
**Egypt, Palestine and Syria** (where Mycenaean pottery now outstrips Minoan), and
west to the **Lipari Islands** and Italy. The legends of overseas adventure —
**Bellerophon** in Lycia, the **Argonauts** to Colchis, Perseus and Andromeda at
Joppa — plausibly echo this Late Helladic II reach.

## End Conditions

Beyond this volume, the palace world reaches its height in the Amarna age and is
then shattered in the **~1200 BCE [[late-bronze-age|Bronze Age Collapse]]** — the
horizon the Pylos Linear B "coast-guard" tablets seem to anticipate, and which the
Egyptians record as the **Sea Peoples**. Mycenaean Greece passed into the Dark Age,
its memory preserved in the **Homeric epics**.

## Significance

Mycenaean civilization is the **bridge between Minoan Crete and historical Greece**:
it Hellenized the Aegean Bronze Age, produced the earliest **Greek written records**
([[aegean-scripts|Linear B]]), conquered and inherited Minoan Crete, and bequeathed
to classical Greece its **heroic tradition** — the matter of Mycenae, Pylos and
Troy that became the *Iliad* and *Odyssey*.
