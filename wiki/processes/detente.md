# Détente

**Détente** (French, "relaxation") was the easing of [[cold-war|Cold War]] tensions between
the superpowers from the early 1960s to the late 1970s — a period of negotiation, arms
control, and managed coexistence that stabilized, without ending, the conflict. From
[[leffler-westad-cold-war-v2|*The Cambridge History of the Cold War*, Vol. II]].

## Definition and Scope

Détente denotes the **deliberate relaxation and management of superpower rivalry** through
negotiation rather than its resolution: arms-control agreements, summitry, expanded
East–West trade and contact, and the normalization of the European status quo. It is a phase
*within* the Cold War, not its end — the underlying rivalry persisted, especially in the
[[decolonization|Third World]].

## Causal Drivers

It grew from the shock of the **[[cuban-missile-crisis-1962|Cuban Missile Crisis]]** (mutual
fear of accidental war); from rough **[[nuclear-arms-race|nuclear parity]]** by the late
1960s (which made "victory" meaningless); from the **[[sino-soviet-split]]** (letting the US
play Moscow against Beijing); and from **economic pressures** on both sides (the cost of the
arms race, Soviet need for Western grain and technology, US strain from [[vietnam-war|Vietnam]]).

## Major Phases

1. **Foundations (1963–68):** the hotline, the Partial Test Ban Treaty (1963), and the NPT
   (1968) following the 1962 crisis.
2. **High détente (1969–75):** Brandt's **Ostpolitik**; the Nixon–Brezhnev summits and **SALT
   I / ABM Treaty (1972)**; Nixon's opening to China; the **Helsinki Final Act (1975)**.
3. **Erosion (1975–79):** Third World rivalry (Angola, the Horn), the SS-20 deployments, and
   stalled SALT II.

## Geographic Spread

Centred on the Washington–Moscow axis, détente was most institutionalized in **Europe**
(Ostpolitik, Helsinki, the CSCE), extended to **East Asia** via the US–China opening, but
conspicuously **failed to reach the Third World**, where superpower competition intensified
even as it relaxed in Europe.

## Interaction

Détente capped the **[[nuclear-arms-race]]** (SALT/ABM), exploited the **[[sino-soviet-split]]**
(triangular diplomacy), and, through **Helsinki's** human-rights "Basket III," planted a seed
that dissidents and the [[revolutions-of-1989|1989]] movements would later use against the
communist regimes — even as it coexisted with continued proxy conflict.

## End Conditions

Détente collapsed at the end of the 1970s under continued Third World rivalry, the SS-20s, US
domestic backlash, and decisively the **Soviet [[soviet-war-in-afghanistan-1979|invasion of
Afghanistan]] (1979)**, which killed SALT II ratification and ushered in the confrontational
"**second Cold War**" of the early 1980s.
