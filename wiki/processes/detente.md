# Détente

**Détente** (French, "relaxation") was the easing of [[cold-war|Cold War]] tensions between
the superpowers from the early 1960s to the late 1970s — a period of negotiation, arms
control, and managed coexistence that stabilized, without ending, the conflict. From
[[leffler-westad-cold-war-v2|*The Cambridge History of the Cold War*, Vol. II]].

## Drivers

Détente grew from the shock of the **[[cuban-missile-crisis-1962|Cuban Missile Crisis]]**,
which frightened both Washington and Moscow into reducing the risk of accidental war; from
the achievement of rough **[[nuclear-arms-race|nuclear parity]]** by the late 1960s (which
made "victory" meaningless and stabilized deterrence); from the **[[sino-soviet-split]]**
(which let the US play Moscow and Beijing against each other); and from **economic
pressures** on both sides (the costs of the arms race; Soviet need for Western technology and
grain; US strain from the [[vietnam-war|Vietnam War]]).

## Major achievements

- **Ostpolitik** — West German chancellor Willy Brandt's normalization with the East
  (treaties with the USSR, Poland, and East Germany, 1970–72), defusing the German question.
- **The Nixon–Brezhnev summits and SALT I (1972)** — the first strategic-arms-limitation
  agreement and the **ABM Treaty**, capping the central nuclear competition; flanked by
  Nixon's opening to **China (1972)**.
- **The Helsinki Final Act (1975)** — the Conference on Security and Co-operation in Europe:
  the West accepted Europe's postwar borders, the East accepted **human-rights commitments**
  (Basket III) that dissidents would later wield against communist regimes.

## End conditions

Détente eroded in the later 1970s: continued superpower rivalry in the [[decolonization|Third
World]] (Angola, the Horn of Africa, Nicaragua), the Soviet deployment of SS-20 missiles, US
domestic backlash, and finally the **Soviet [[soviet-war-in-afghanistan-1979|invasion of
Afghanistan]]** (1979), which killed SALT II's ratification and ushered in the confrontational
"**second Cold War**" of the early 1980s.

## Significance

Détente neither ended the Cold War nor reversed the arms race, but it institutionalized
**arms control** and **coexistence**, defused the German flashpoint, and — through Helsinki —
planted the human-rights seed that helped undermine the Soviet bloc in 1989. Its collapse
showed how fragile superpower accommodation remained while the underlying rivalry endured.
