The Viet Minh (Việt Nam Độc Lập Đồng Minh Hội, League for the Independence of Vietnam) was the broad national front organization created by the ICP at the 8th Plenum (Pac Bo, May 1941) under Ho Chi Minh. It subordinated explicit communist class language to anti-colonial, anti-Japanese, anti-French nationalism to build a mass base beyond the small party core. Organizational genius lay in decentralized "committees of liberation" at village level (matching traditional Vietnamese structures) and National Salvation Associations.

Key phases: base-building in Viet Bac (1941–44); OSS liaison 1944–45; exploitation of 1945 Japanese coup and famine; August Revolution seizures; early DRV coalition government. Transitioned after 1951 into Lien Viet and later Fatherland Front structures. Model of broad-front united nationalism proved durable against two successive powers.
