# First Intermediate Period (Egypt)

The roughly 120-year era of **political fragmentation** between the
[[fall-of-old-kingdom-2160bce|collapse of the Old Kingdom]] (~2160 BCE) and the
[[reunification-of-egypt-2040bce|Theban reunification]] (~2040 BCE) — Egypt's first
"intermediate period," when central authority broke and the country split between
rival royal houses and quasi-independent provincial lords. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]] (Ch. XX, W. C. Hayes, internal; Ch.
XXI, Posener, external).

## Definition and Scope

After the [[old-kingdom-egypt|Old Kingdom]] dissolved (the ephemeral Seventh and
Eighth "Memphite kinglets"), two power centres emerged:

- **The Heracleopolitan kingdom** (Ninth and Tenth Dynasties, the "House of
  Achthoes/Khety"), ruling the north from **Heracleopolis** near the Faiyum, with
  **Memphis** still its administrative and burial centre. Its political testament
  is the **Instruction for King Merykare**, one of the finest Egyptian didactic
  texts.
- **The Theban kingdom** (the Eleventh Dynasty, the **Inyotef** and **Mentuhotpe**
  line) rising in the south from **Thebes**, gradually absorbing the "Head of the
  South" up to This and Abydos.

The two fought a long, intermittent war over the **Thinite Nome (This/Abydos)**
until the Theban king [[mentuhotpe-ii|Nebhepetre Mentuhotpe II]] destroyed
Heracleopolis and reunited the Two Lands.

## Causal Drivers

The Old Kingdom's structural decay (hereditary, tax-exempt nomarchs; fragmentation
of crown land) left the **nomes as small independent states** — each governor
levying his own armies, dating by his own years, building his own tombs. Repeated
**famine**, "the inevitable companion of political disunity," stalked the land:
nomarchs from Asyut to El-Mi'alla (Ankhtify) to Thebes boast in their tomb
biographies of feeding their people when "all Upper Egypt was dying of hunger" —
imagery a generation of scholars (and Barbara Bell) read as evidence of the
**low-Nile / climatic** crisis associated with the
[[societal-collapse-and-environmental-determinism|4.2-kiloyear event]]. On the
eastern frontier, **Asiatic (Amu) nomads** infiltrated the weakly-defended Delta —
the situation lamented in the *Admonitions of Ipuwer* and addressed in the
*Instruction for Merykare*.

## Major Phases

1. **Heracleopolitan ascendancy** (Dyn. IX–X) — Achthoes I imposes a harsh northern
   kingship; the loyal nomarchs of Asyut anchor the regime.
2. **Theban rise** (early Dyn. XI) — the Inyotefs expand north; the sack of the holy
   Thinite nome by the Heracleopolitans (admitted as a fatal blunder in the
   *Instruction*) turns the south solidly against them.
3. **Reunification** — [[reunification-of-egypt-2040bce|Mentuhotpe II takes
   Heracleopolis]] (~2040 BCE), founding the [[middle-kingdom-egypt|Middle Kingdom]].

## Cultural Consequence: "Democratization of the Afterlife"

The collapse of the Memphite state had a profound religious effect. Royal funerary
privilege devolved: the **Pyramid Texts**, once the king's alone, were adapted as
the **Coffin Texts** for any Egyptian wealthy enough to commission them — the
"**democratization of the Hereafter**." Simultaneously the cult of **Osiris** (god
of the dead, with his promise of a moral judgment and resurrection at **Abydos**)
rose to overshadow the old solar theology of Heliopolis. Art decentralized into
vigorous, unpolished **provincial schools** whose realism later fed the
distinctive Middle Kingdom style.

## Interaction

The FIP is Egypt's counterpart to the contemporaneous Mesopotamian breakdown — the
[[gutian-invasion|fall of Akkad and the Gutian interlude]] — and the Levantine
[[first-intermediate-period|EB–MB]] de-urbanization, all clustered in the
~2200–2000 BCE horizon. Its external face (Ch. XXI) is the **interruption of the
Byblos timber trade** ("no one sails to Byblos today," laments Ipuwer) and the
Asiatic infiltration of the Delta, both reversed under the Middle Kingdom.

## End Conditions

Ended by the [[reunification-of-egypt-2040bce|Theban military reunification]] under
[[mentuhotpe-ii|Mentuhotpe II]], which inaugurated the
[[middle-kingdom-egypt|Middle Kingdom]] — the pattern (collapse → intermediate
period → reunification under a new dynasty) that would recur in the Second and
Third Intermediate Periods.
