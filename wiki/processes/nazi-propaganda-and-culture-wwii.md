# Nazi Propaganda and Culture at War (1939–1945)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]], "Cultures of Destruction." Goebbels's governing doctrine reversed WWI practice: entertainment first ("It's important for the war to keep our people in a good mood . . . We failed to do that during the [First] World War, and we had to pay for it with a terrible catastrophe").

## Media

- **Press:** paper shortages closed ~1,450 newspapers (including the *Frankfurter Zeitung*) while total circulation *rose* 20.5 → 26.5 million (1939–44); the *Racial Observer* (1.19m), Goebbels's *Das Reich* (1.5m), the SS *Black Corps* (750k). Goebbels fought Dietrich for control until 1944–45.
- **Radio:** total state monopoly from August 1939; by 1944 only 32 of 190 weekly hours were political. Listening to the BBC was a capital "radio crime" (~4,000 arrests in year one) — yet the BBC German service reached ~15 million daily by August 1944. The Hamburg "Swing Youth" were raided, beaten, and up to 70 sent to camps (Himmler's order of 26 January 1942) for jazz, Anglophilia, and contempt for the Hitler Youth.
- **Cinema:** attendance quintupled to over a billion tickets (1942); the Weekly Review newsreel reached ~20 million. Entertainment (*Request Concert*, *The Great Love*) outdrew ideology, but the antisemitic canon mattered: *Jew Süss* (Harlan, 1940 — compulsory for the SS, screened before deportations) and *The Eternal Jew* (Hippler). The war ended with *Kolberg* (8.5m RM, 187,000 soldiers as extras while the front collapsed), premiered 30 January 1945 to empty, ruined cinemas — and the real Kolberg's fall censored.
- **Theatre and literature:** theatre took the largest arts subsidy (40 million tickets, 1940) until Goebbels closed it all in August 1944; 45,000 front libraries; *Mein Kampf* at 6 million copies; "inner emigration" authors banned once their print runs sold out.

## Music

Hitler's Bruckner cult and Bayreuth "war festivals" gave way after Stalingrad to *The Merry Widow*; Goebbels humiliated Richard Strauss over his Jewish daughter-in-law while Strauss remained the most-performed living opera composer; Lale Andersen was arrested and "Lili Marleen" eventually banned despite (because of) its popularity on both sides of the front. The era's most enduring German music was written in Theresienstadt — Viktor Ullmann, Kurt Gerron, and Ilse Weber, who sang her own lullaby "Wiegala" to the children she voluntarily accompanied into the Auschwitz gas chamber (6 October 1944).

## Art Looting

Hans Posse (then Hermann Voss) spent 70 million RM and gathered 8,000+ objects for Hitler's planned Linz Führermuseum; Göring stocked Carinhall through Rosenberg's Paris Task Staff (the Jeu de Paume as clearing-house for the Rothschild and other Jewish collections, Göring choosing 600+ items personally); Mühlmann stripped Poland (the Czartoryski Leonardo and Rembrandt turning up in Frank's possession in 1945); SS units took the Amber Room east Prussia-ward. Sculptor Arno Breker drew a 250,000 RM tax-free honorarium as the regime's laureate.
