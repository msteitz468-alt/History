# Denazification (1945–c. 1955)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: over 3.6 million Germans in the western zones were processed through questionnaires and tribunals into five categories; under 5% were judged seriously incriminated (1,667 "chief culprits"; ~23,000 "incriminated"), 27% nominal Party members, a third exonerated; by the 1948 wind-up, 783,000 cases were simply dropped and 358,000 amnestied. The Soviet zone dismissed 300,000+ from their posts. Popular approval collapsed from 57% (1946) to 17% (1949) as Germans "wanted above all to forget"; polls into the 1950s still found majorities calling Nazism "a good idea, badly carried out."

Evans's verdict is double-edged: superficial, and largely a failure at changing minds — yet "overall, despite everything, the action was a success": it made open Nazism taboo, kept neo-Nazi parties marginal (and legally suppressed), and reopened public life to Social Democrats, Catholics, and liberals. The real reckoning waited for the [[nuremberg-trials-1945-1949|Frankfurt Auschwitz Trial]] generation and 1968.
