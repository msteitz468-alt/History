# Persecution of German Jews (1933–1939)

The peacetime escalation from discrimination to expropriation to violence:
the April 1933 boycott and civil-service purge; creeping professional and
social exclusion; the [[nuremberg-laws-1935|Nuremberg Laws]] (1935); the
tactical pause of the [[berlin-olympics-1936|Olympic year]]; accelerating
"Aryanization" of Jewish businesses 1937–38; the November 1938 pogrom
([[kristallnacht-1938]]) and its aftermath of total economic exclusion,
mass arrests, and forced emigration. By September 1939 roughly half of
Germany's 1933 Jewish population had emigrated; those remaining were
segregated, pauperized, and rightless.

## Definition and Scope

Covers Reich territory (incl. annexed Austria and Sudetenland) 1933–39;
distinct from the wartime genocide ([[the-holocaust]]) it enabled.

## Causal Drivers

Ideological antisemitism at the regime's core; party-base pressure from
below; material plunder interests (state, party, business) in Aryanization;
foreign-policy calendar setting the tempo.

## The 1933 Onslaught in Detail (Evans, 2003)

Vol. I ([[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003]]) deepens phase 1:

- **Violence preceded power**: brownshirt bomb attacks on Jewish shops and
  synagogues were already under way in autumn 1932. After 5 March 1933:
  the Kurfürstendamm rampage; in Breslau a Jewish theatre director kidnapped
  and beaten nearly to death and Jewish judges and lawyers assaulted in the
  courthouse (11 March — practising Jewish lawyers admitted there fell from
  364 to 17); the Königsberg synagogue burned and a Jewish businessman beaten
  to death; the Straubing trader Otto Selz abducted and shot dead (15 March);
  in Niederstetten and Creglingen two of eighteen Jewish men beaten died.
  Evans's count: 43 Jews murdered by stormtroopers by end June 1933 —
  "doubtless incomplete." Hitler's 10 March halt order on "individual
  actions" (prompted by Reichsbank complaints) shows the pattern of
  intervention only when violence threatened economic or diplomatic
  interests.
- **The 1 April boycott**: ordered by Hitler on 28 March (after Berchtesgaden
  with Goebbels, Himmler, Streicher), publicly framed as retaliation for
  foreign "atrocity propaganda" but "long contemplated in Nazi circles." The
  public response contradicted Goebbels's triumphant diary ("An imposing
  spectacle!"): largely passive, with pre-boycott rush purchases and scattered
  counter-demonstrations in Munich and Hanover; the biggest firms (Tietz,
  14,000 employees) were exempted for economic reasons. Evans: "The general
  lack of public opposition to the action was striking, but so too was the
  general lack of public enthusiasm for it."
- **The legal cage**: the 7 April civil-service and lawyers' laws ("non-Aryan"
  defined 11 April as one Jewish grandparent; Hindenburg's veteran
  exemption); the 25 April school/university quota (5% stock, 1.5% of new
  entries); the 14 July denaturalization of Eastern European Jewish
  immigrants. Exemptions kept many working — 336 of 717 Jewish judges and
  prosecutors, 3,167 of 4,585 Jewish lawyers — but Evans's verdict: "this
  bundle of different measures meant the end of the civil equality of the
  Jews that had existed in Germany since 1871."
- **Registered Jewish population**: 525,000 (Jan 1933) → just under 500,000
  (end June); 37,000 emigrated in 1933.
- **Witness**: [[klemperer-victor|Klemperer]]'s diary — 10 March a "mild
  prelude"; 30 March "a pogrom in the depths of the Middle Ages"; shame
  rather than fear ("The defeat of 1918 did not depress me as deeply as the
  present situation"); guilt at his veteran exemption; and on 30 June 1933
  the first entry in his private lexicon of Nazi language: "protective
  custody."
- **Northeim micro-pattern**: a half-hearted boycott, then exclusion of the
  town's 120 Jews from clubs and associations by late summer 1933 — Evans's
  representative case of social death preceding legal death.

## Major Phases (Evans, 2005)

1. **1933 — seizure-of-power violence and legal exclusion.** SA street terror
   (40+ Jews murdered by end June); the 1 April boycott (called off within
   days for lack of enthusiasm); the 7 April civil-service law (Hindenburg's
   front-veteran exemption); ~1,600 of 5,000 university teachers out by 1934;
   37,000 Jews emigrated in 1933.
2. **1934–35 — pressure from below.** SA boycott waves; the spring–summer
   1935 violence and signage campaign, driven partly by the regime's
   flagging popularity, culminating in the [[nuremberg-laws-1935|Nuremberg
   Laws]].
3. **1936 — the Olympic pause.** Tactical throttling ordered from the top
   ([[berlin-olympics-1936]]); Aryanization nonetheless continued and
   accelerated with the [[four-year-plan-1936|Four-Year Plan]] — Göring's
   Foreign Currency Search Office (under Heydrich, August 1936) seized firms
   on capital-flight pretexts; the Gestapo destroyed Jewish cattle-dealing by
   end 1937.
4. **1937–38 — radicalization.** Opened by Hitler's 13 September 1937 rally
   speech; Goebbels's diary (29 November 1937): "The Jews must get out of
   Germany, indeed out of Europe altogether... The Leader is firmly resolved
   on it." Of ~100,000 Jewish firms in 1933, ~70% were Aryanized or closed by
   July 1938 (only 9,000 of ~50,000 shops left). Evans's buyer typology: ~20%
   bought protectively at fair prices, ~40% took the bargain without qualms,
   ~40% — many party members — actively drove prices down with boycotts or
   Gestapo help. The Reich Flight Tax (threshold cut to 50,000 RM) and the
   Gold Discount Bank transfer fee (90% by June 1938) plundered emigrants.
   1938's legal avalanche: asset registration (26 April), doctors (25 July),
   lawyers (27 September), compulsory "Israel"/"Sara" names (17 August). The
   June 1938 arrests of 1,500 Jews with any criminal record ("Juni-Aktion")
   equated Jews with criminality and pressured emigration. The
   [[anschluss-1938|Anschluss]] radicalized everything — Vienna's instant
   pogrom and Eichmann's forced-emigration Central Agency became the SD's
   model; "without the Austrian example... it is impossible to understand the
   upsurge of violence... that culminated in the pogrom of 9–10 November"
   (Evans). The Polenaktion (27–29 October) deported ~18,000 Polish Jews to
   the border.
5. **November 1938 — the pogrom.** [[kristallnacht-1938]] and the 12 November
   Göring conference: the 1-billion-RM fine (1.127bn collected), confiscated
   insurance payouts, total exclusion from economic life — >2 billion RM
   plundered in 1938–39 before Aryanization profits.
6. **1939 — pauperization and forced labour.** Blocked accounts (21
   February), tenancy rights stripped (30 April, paving ghettoization),
   forced-labour drafting (20,000 on motorway construction by summer),
   emigration-or-camps under Heydrich's Reich Centre (24 January). Hitler's
   30 January Reichstag "prophecy" reframed Europe's Jews as hostages against
   American intervention (see [[the-holocaust]]).

**Numbers.** 437,000 Jews-by-faith (1933) → ~350,000 (end 1937) → ~207,000 in
the old Reich by September 1939, increasingly elderly (over-50s: 20% in 1933,
over half by 1939). Emigration: ~23,000/year through the mid-1930s; 115,000
in the ten months after the pogrom; ~400,000 total 1933–39 (132,000 to the
USA, ~60,000 to Palestine — the Haavara Transfer Agreement of 27 August 1933,
personally endorsed by Hitler, moved ~140m RM of emigrant assets there —
40,000 to the UK incl. 9,000+ Kindertransport children, 9,000 to Shanghai).
The Evian conference (6 July 1938, 32 nations) liberalized nothing.

**Jewish responses.** The Reich Representation under Leo Baeck (September
1933); community schools (167 schools, ~24,000 pupils by 1937) as children
were driven from state schools; the Jewish Culture League (180,000 members,
run from above by the Nazi Hans Hinkel, progressively banned from the German
repertoire — Schiller 1934, Goethe 1936, Beethoven 1937, Mozart 1938) — a
creeping cultural ghettoization completed after November 1938. Mixed
marriages (35,000 couples in 1933) faced systematic Gestapo divorce pressure;
Göring's 28 December 1938 "privileged" categories split families' fates. The
Klemperer and Solmitz diaries anchor Evans's account of the interior
experience ("we are annihilated").

## Geographic Spread

Reich-wide; radicalized fastest in Austria after the [[anschluss-1938|Anschluss]]
(the "Vienna model" of forced emigration under the SD).

## Interaction

Interlocks with [[nazi-economic-mobilization-and-rearmament-1933-1936]]
(Aryanization funding rearmament-strained finances) and with the broader
racial-hygiene program (sterilization, persecution of "asocials", Sinti and
Roma).

## End Conditions

Transformed, not ended: the war closed emigration routes and shifted policy
from expulsion to ghettoization and murder.
