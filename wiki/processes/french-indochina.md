# French Indochina (Union of Indochina)

The French colonial federation (1887–1954) comprising Cochinchina (colony in southern Vietnam), the protectorates of Annam and Tonkin (central and northern Vietnam), Cambodia, and (from 1893) Laos. It represented the culmination of piecemeal French conquest beginning in the 1850s and served as the administrative and economic framework within which Vietnamese anticolonial nationalism, including the Viet Minh, developed.

## Definition and Scope

Not a single "Vietnam" but a multi-ethnic colonial construct that dissolved pre-colonial Vietnamese polities (Nguyen empire) while preserving some monarchical facades in the protectorates. Cochinchina was directly ruled; Annam and Tonkin retained nominal Nguyen emperors under French Residents. Economic extraction focused on rice exports (Mekong delta), rubber plantations, mining, and infrastructure serving French interests. Cultural policy oscillated between assimilationist republican rhetoric and pragmatic indirect rule.

## Causal Drivers

- 19th-c. French imperialism: gunboat diplomacy, missionary protection (persecution of Catholics used as pretext), rivalry with British in Asia.
- Economic: desire for markets, raw materials, and settler opportunities after losses elsewhere.
- Ideological: mission civilisatrice — bringing French republican values, education, and administration, though in practice heavily racialized and extractive (see Goscha on failure of colonial republicanism).
- Strategic: counter British influence in Siam/China; later Japanese threat in WWII.

## Major Phases

- Conquest (1858–1887): Capture of Da Nang, Saigon, Cochinchina (1860s); Tonkin campaigns (1880s); Sino-French War; establishment of the Union 1887.
- "Pacification" and development (1887–1914): Suppression of resistance (e.g., Can Vuong, Duy Tan movements); economic transformation; limited education and elite formation (Franco-Vietnamese schools).
- Interwar and Popular Front: Economic crises, rising nationalism (VNQDD Yen Bay 1930, early communists); some reforms under Varenne etc.
- WWII and Japanese occupation (1940–1945): Vichy French administration under Japanese oversight; famine 1944–45; Viet Minh growth in highlands.
- First Indochina War (1946–1954): French attempt to reassert control after 1945; Viet Minh guerrilla-to-conventional transition; Dien Bien Phu; Geneva partition.

## Geographic Spread

Core in Vietnam (divided into three *kỳ*); extended to Cambodia and Laos. Highland minorities (Montagnards, etc.) often administered separately or used as buffers/recruits. Mekong and Red River deltas as economic engines.

## Interaction with Other Processes

- Produced or accelerated Vietnamese nationalism (both non-communist and communist strands).
- Interacted with Chinese and Japanese imperial projects.
- Fed into global decolonization and Cold War (US support for French as containment).
- Contrasts with British Malaya or Dutch Indonesia in terms of republican rhetoric vs practice.

## End Conditions

Military defeat at Dien Bien Phu (1954) and international pressure led to Geneva Accords. Formal end of the Union; Vietnam partitioned; Cambodia and Laos gained independence. French cultural/economic influence lingered, but political control passed to Vietnamese states (DRV north, RVN south).
