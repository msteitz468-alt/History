# The Atlantic Revolutions (c. 1775–1825)

> **Transition page** (CLAUDE.md standing list: "1789 and the Atlantic Revolutions").
> A genuine discontinuity opening the [[long-19th-century|Long 19th Century]].

## Definition and Scope

The **Atlantic revolutions** are the interconnected wave of upheavals — the
**American** (1775–1783), **French** (1789), **Haitian** (1791–1804), and **Spanish
American** independence revolutions (c. 1808–1825) — that, in [[mcneill-cwh-v7-2015|CWH
VII]]'s account, reordered the politics of much of the world and introduced the
political forms that dominate it today: the **large-scale republic** and "in some
sense, the national state itself." Jaime Rodríguez's dedicated treatment sits in the
volume's absent Book 2; the editors and several Book 1 chapters (Pomeranz & McNeill,
Roshwald, Tyrrell, Charlip) nonetheless make the revolutions a load-bearing turning
point.

## Causal Drivers

- **The Seven Years' War (1754–63)** — "the first global war" — left Britain, France,
  and Spain with crushing debts and drove the imperial centralizing and taxation
  measures that provoked colonial resistance ("no taxation without representation").
- **Enlightenment ideas of popular sovereignty and natural rights**, circulating
  across the Atlantic as "a more intense and consequential circulation of news and
  ideas" (Pomeranz & McNeill), supplied the language of revolt.
- **Imperial overreach and elite grievance**: criollo resentment of peninsular
  privilege (see [[latin-america-modern]]); settler resistance to metropolitan control.

## Major Phases

1. **American Revolution (1775–1783):** the first successful anti-colonial revolt,
   establishing a large-scale republic.
2. **French Revolution (1789):** popular sovereignty proclaimed ("the Third Estate is
   a complete nation"); the democratic and national ideals fused (see [[nationalism]]).
3. **Haitian Revolution (1791–1804):** slaves and free blacks overthrew French rule —
   the first independent state born of a slave colony, and the revolution that most
   frightened Atlantic elites and hardened pro-slavery reaction.
4. **Spanish American independence (c. 1808–1825):** triggered by Napoleon's conquest
   of Iberia; the criollo wars ending at Ayacucho (1824).

## Consequences

- **The first retreat of European colonialism** (begun, in the editors' long view,
  with the capture of Ceuta in 1415) — shifting the focus of European empire from the
  western hemisphere to the eastern.
- **Icons and precedents** (Washington, Bolívar, Toussaint, the Declaration of the
  Rights of Man) that would inspire the twentieth-century second wave of
  [[decolonization]].
- The **large-scale republic and the national state** as new, globally diffusing
  political forms; the rise of **abolitionism** along the same circuits.

## Interaction

The Atlantic revolutions open the [[long-19th-century|Long 19th Century]] and seed
modern [[nationalism]], [[decolonization]], and the abolition of the
[[atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic slave trade]]. They are the political hinge between
the [[age-of-expansion|Age of Expansion]] and the industrial-national world that
follows.
