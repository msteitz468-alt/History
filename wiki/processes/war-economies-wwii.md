# War Economies in the Second World War

In total war, states strive to extract as much as possible from the economy to serve the war effort, limited only by the amount of resources available, the capacity of the civilian population to accept tougher working conditions and reduced living standards, and the need to strike a balance between military mobilization and economic production.

## Causal Drivers

Raw resources do not determine outcomes; organization, central direction, efficiency of use, forced labor, and Allied coordination vs. Axis fragmentation matter far more.

- US: Henry J. Kaiser example of rationalization. At start of program, each ship took 1.4 million man-hours and 355 days; by 1943, 500,000 man-hours and 41 days. Robert E. Peary built in 4 days. Prefabricated sections on assembly lines.
- Germany: Failure to establish clear political/administrative structure. Hoarding, overcharging, constant design changes preventing mass production. Albert Speer (1942) centralized with main committees and central planning; output tripled 1941-1944 despite bombing, but more might have been done without oil/transport focus in 1944 bombing.
- USSR: Rigorous control; high proportion to war production + efficiency despite losses/evacuation.
- Japan: "Guns before rice" extreme prioritization.

## Major Phases

Early Axis reliance on Blitzkrieg/short-war and captured resources (inefficient). 1941-42 German rationalization under Speer. US mass production ramp (Liberty Ships). Soviet "more from less." 1942-44 Allied Lend-Lease enabling multi-theater. Axis peaks (Germany 1944) then collapse.

## Geographic Spread

US (assembly lines, shipyards). Germany (dispersal, occupied Europe). USSR (evacuation). UK/Commonwealth. Japan (severe civilian extraction).

## Interaction

War economies enabled (or failed to enable) military operations. Bombing targeted oil/transport/chemicals had outsized effects. Lend-Lease concurrent_with and enabled Soviet/UK survival.

## End Conditions

Axis overextension + Allied material superiority + coordination produced tipping point. German/Soviet extremes of coercion sustained fighting longer but could not overcome structural gaps.

## Historiography

Debates on "Speer miracle" (reassessment: realization of pre-existing potential). Efficiency metrics (workers/steel per output). Role of bombing vs. internal mismanagement. Lend-Lease significance (sustaining but not sole decider).

Grounded in The West Point History of World War II, Volume 1 (Overy chapter on People and Economies at War + supporting).

## From West Point Vol. 1 Range 5 (Overy + Rogers) - Detailed Claims

**Soviet production revival despite contraction**
After initial invasion losses (steel halved, coal more than halved, labor -35%), "the only course left for the rump Soviet economy, now based on the industrial areas of the central Urals region, was to focus all production on guns, to the virtual exclusion of butter. Output in the civilian industrial sector... fell by 1943 to only 46 percent of what it had been in 1937..." Gosplan under Voznesensky restructured with "slenderest of margins for civilian consumption." Result: between 1942 and 1945 "the Soviet Union exceeded German production of almost all major classes of army weapons and aircraft, despite possessing an economy only a fraction of the size."

**US guns and butter + superabundance**
" Of all the combatant nations, only the United States proved able to provide both guns and butter in plenty for the war effort. The American experience resulted from what might be called the economics of superabundance." GNP expanded 87% 1938-1945. "By the middle of the war, the United States was producing more war goods than the rest of the world put together." By 1943 Ford alone more than Italy total.

**War Production Board and efficiency**
Roosevelt government relied on federal agencies, "most important of which was the War Production Board established under the Sears Roebuck director, Donald Nelson" to supervise resources and reduce waste. Traditions of mass production, management training, competition for contracts drove results. "So effective did these qualities prove that by 1943, the Ford Motor Company alone was producing more military equipment than Italy."

**Lend-Lease volumes and impact**
USSR $10.7 billion (food large share in 1944-45, plus industrial goods, railroad equipment, trucks, field telephones, radios). "American supplies were vital to the Soviet war effort." Stalin (post-Communism evidence): "without Western aid, the Soviet Union 'could not have continued the war.'" Britain principal beneficiary $30 billion (64% military, 12% food). Without it, stricter consumption limits. Reciprocal $7.5B from Empire.

**German production paradoxes and Speer**
Pre-Speer: "the absence of a central committee or office for issuing commands fostered widespread confusion. In the absence of firm direction... industrialists pursued their own interests... hoarding stocks, retaining labor, overcharging... military leadership insisted that the tactical demands of the front should take priority... constant design changes preventing mass production." Consumption down 25% by 1942. "Guns will make us powerful; butter will only make us fat" (Göring).

Speer (Feb 1942) + Milch: rationalization, central committees, dispersal. "This was, as historian Adam Tooze has argued, not so much a production miracle as the final realization of what had up until now been a large but inefficiently mobilized war potential." Peak 1944.

Efficiency comparison: "For every tank or plane Germany produced in 1942, it drew on a national resource base of 655 industrial or foreign workers and 1,161 tons of steel production. For the Soviet Union... 162 workers and 145 tons of steel. American factories too were much more efficient."

**Home front / coercion contrasts**
Soviet: coercive + patriotic. Germany: coercion to bitter end even with defeat inevitable. US/UK: moral commitment, fewer sacrifices, debate allowed.
