# The Aegean Neolithic (Stone Age Greece and Crete)

The arrival and development of farming life in the Aegean — the Greek mainland, the
islands, and Crete — from the first Neolithic settlers (~7000 BCE) to the threshold
of the Bronze Age. It is the substrate on which the Helladic and **Minoan**
civilizations would later rise, and so the deep foundation for the CAH's Aegean
Bronze Age volumes. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p1-1970|CAH Vol. I, Part 1]]
(Ch. X, S. S. Weinberg).

## Palaeolithic and Mesolithic Background

Weinberg opens with the sparse Palaeolithic and Mesolithic record of the region
before turning to the decisive event: the introduction of an essentially
**Near Eastern farming package** — emmer and einkorn wheat, barley, sheep and goats
— carried into the Aegean by colonists, an instance of the
[[early-agriculture-europe|spread of agriculture into Europe]] by sea and land.

## The Earliest (Aceramic / Early Neolithic) Farmers

The first farmers arrived **without pottery**. Weinberg is careful that this does
not necessarily make their culture "aceramic" by conviction — rather "they had not
yet set up the means for producing pottery." Two type-sites anchor the picture:

- **Argissa Magoula** (Thessaly) and other Thessalian *magoulas* — preceramic and
  Early Neolithic levels on the fertile mainland plains.
- **Knossos** (Crete) — the deep basal Neolithic beneath the later palace. The
  earliest level was a **camp of first arrivals**: pits with ash, charcoal and a
  mass of **carbonized hexaploid wheat, emmer and barley**; child burials laid
  contracted in the earth without grave goods; tools of stone, bone, and imported
  **obsidian** (from Melos), with no permanent structures yet. Crete's entire
  Neolithic sequence begins here, in a single deep mound.

## The Mainland Neolithic Sequence

Weinberg sets out the Greek mainland succession that became standard:
- **Early Neolithic** — monochrome and early painted wares; **Nea Nikomedeia**
  (Macedonia), one of Europe's earliest known farming villages, with substantial
  houses and figurines.
- **Middle Neolithic** — the **Sesklo** culture of Thessaly, with fine red-on-white
  painted pottery and well-built houses.
- **Late Neolithic** — expansion and regional diversification across the mainland,
  islands, and Crete.

## Trade: Melian Obsidian

A defining feature of the Aegean Neolithic is the **maritime obsidian trade** from
the island of **Melos**, present even in the earliest levels at Knossos — evidence
of seafaring and exchange networks from the outset, the Aegean counterpart to the
Anatolian obsidian trade that linked [[catalhoyuk|Çatal Hüyük]] and
[[jericho|Jericho]]. See [[trade-network-formation]].

## Interaction and Significance

The Aegean Neolithic ties the European and Near Eastern Neolithic worlds together:
its crops, animals, and likely its first farmers came from Anatolia and the Levant,
while its later development is distinctively Aegean. It is the demographic and
cultural foundation on which the **Early Helladic / Early Minoan** Bronze Age and,
ultimately, [[urbanization|Aegean urbanism]] were built — the point at which the
wiki's Aegean thread begins, to be continued in later CAH volumes.

## End Conditions

Merges into the **Early Bronze Age** of the Aegean (~3300–3000 BCE) with the spread
of metallurgy and the rise of the Early Helladic, Cycladic and Early Minoan
cultures — the start of the trajectory toward Minoan Crete.

## Historiography

Weinberg's synthesis rests on mid-century excavation (Knossos under Evans and J. D.
Evans; Nea Nikomedeia; the Thessalian magoulas) and uncalibrated dates; the
relative sequence is durable, absolute dates superseded by calibrated radiocarbon.
See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
