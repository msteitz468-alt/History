# Indo-European Migrations (3rd–2nd Millennium BCE)

The set of late-prehistoric movements by which speakers of **Indo-European
languages** — recognisable as a family by deep correspondences of vocabulary and
inflexion (*father / pater / pitar-*; *I am = esmi / asmi*) — spread from a
still-unlocated "homeland" into Anatolia, Greece, Iran and India during the third
and second millennia BCE. This page treats the **process** (who moved, when, and
with what effect); the **homeland question** itself is a standing controversy at
[[indo-european-origins]]. The classic, deliberately cautious statement is R. A.
Crossland's **"Immigrants from the North"** in [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I,
Part 2]] (Ch. XXVII).

## Definition and Scope — what this is *not*

Crossland frames the chapter as a **reassessment against romanticism**. The
Indo-Europeans had been "portrayed too often as the incarnation of northern
virility sweeping down in massed chariots." He insists:

- "Indo-European" is a **linguistic**, not a racial, term; communities sharing a
  language need not share a culture or physical type. The "**Aryan race**" is a
  myth, and "Aryan" should be used only of the proto-Indic/proto-Iranian peoples.
- A change of *language* need not leave an archaeological trace, and a destruction
  layer need not mean ethnic newcomers — so migrations are genuinely hard to prove.
- Their *practical* contribution to the civilised lands was slight: **"no important
  practical innovation … except the use of the horse."** Their real impact was
  **political** — more effective tribal leadership and state-formation.

## Major Phases (the earliest attestations)

- **Anatolia — the Hittite-Luwian group.** The Bogazköy/[[hattusha]] archives
  revealed **Hittite** as Indo-European; **Luwian** and **Palaic** are close
  cognates, alongside non-IE **Hattic** (a surviving substrate). Indo-European
  personal names already appear in the [[kultepe|Kültepe]] trade tablets by the end
  of the **20th century BCE**, so immigration predates that; the **Anitta text**
  shows IE-speaking city-dynasts (see [[old-assyrian-trade]]). Anatolia itself
  cannot be the homeland (the Hattic substrate; no IE influence southward in the
  3rd millennium). The Hittite kingdom — "almost certainly the first large,
  coherent state north of the Taurus" — is the political fruit of this immigration.
- **Northern Mesopotamia and Syria — the "Aryans" of Mitanni.** Not a mass
  migration but a **ruling minority**: Indo-Iranian throne-names (Artatama), the
  gods **Mitra, Varuna, Indra, the Nasatya** invoked in a Mitanni treaty, and
  **Kikkuli's** horse-training manual with Sanskrit numerals (*panza-, -vartanna*).
  "Aryans" led Hurrian-speaking conquerors; their kin took Indo-European speech
  into **northern India** late in the 2nd millennium.
- **Greece.** **Linear B**, deciphered by **Ventris (1952)**, proved **Greek** in
  the Peloponnese by the 13th century BCE; the language arrived earlier, the
  standard view placing the first Greek-speakers ~**1900 BCE** at the start of the
  Middle Helladic (Minyan ware, destructions). See
  [[coming-of-the-greeks-1900bce]].
- **The rest of the family** (Italic, Celtic, Germanic, Baltic, Slavonic, Indic,
  Iranian, Armenian, Tocharian) is attested only much later (Latin from the 6th
  c. BCE; the Veda ~1000 BCE; Celtic, Germanic, Slavonic later still).

## The European / Balkan Branch (CAH III.1)

Crossland's chapter looks east and south; [[boardman-cah-v3p1-1982|CAH Vol. III,
Part 1]] (Ch. 3, M. Garašanin) supplies the **westward, European limb** of the same
process — the Indo-Europeanization of the **Carpatho-Danubian and Balkan** lands,
seen from the receiving end:

- **Mechanism — the Kurgan / ochre-grave intrusions.** From the later Eneolithic,
  successive waves of **steppe pastoralists** ("Kurgan culture," named for tumulus
  burial) moved off the **North Pontic steppe** into the lower Danube, bringing
  tumulus and ochre-grave rites, **corded ware**, and stone battle-axes. Identifiable
  pulses include the **Cernavoda** groups, the **Gorodsk–Usatovo** group (→ the
  Romanian **Foltești** culture), the **Globular Amphorae** culture, and finally the
  **Yamnaya-type barrow** burials. The newcomers' herding economy carried "a powerful
  patriarchal organization."
- **Outcome — fusion, not annihilation.** Dumitrescu and Garašanin both stress the
  **local Eneolithic populations were assimilated, not destroyed** ("various cultural
  elements peculiar to the old cultures … were transmitted to the newcomers'
  cultures," confirmed by skeletal evidence). The **Palaeo-Balkan peoples**
  (Thracians, Illyrians, Daco-Moesians) emerge from this long fusion — ethnogenesis
  *in place*, beginning in the Eneolithic.
- **Effect on Old Europe.** The intrusions coincide with the **breakdown of the Old
  European tell world** and a temporary contraction of metal use (see
  [[old-europe-copper-metallurgy]] and [[chalcolithic]]).

This is, in essence, the **Gimbutas Kurgan scenario** stated by contemporaries
working in the target region — and the very expansion (Yamnaya → Corded Ware
Europe) that **ancient DNA** would later confirm as a massive genetic influx. The
homeland debate it feeds is at [[indo-european-origins]]; the regional sequence is
on [[balkans]].

## Causal Drivers

- **The horse.** At the end of the 3rd millennium the horse "was still a novelty";
  even inefficient horsemen could raid, terrorise, and migrate fast, defeating
  settled peoples far more numerous than themselves. The **war-chariot came later**
  (early 2nd millennium, probably devised in the Hurrian/Iranian zone) — Crossland
  pointedly denies the Indo-Europeans arrived already chariot-borne.
- **Tribal war-society.** A recurrent pattern — a king chosen for war-leadership,
  advised by a council of elders and an assembly of free men (Hittite *pankus* and
  *tuliyas*; Spartan *gerousia*/*apella*; Roman *senatus*/*comitia*; the Germanic
  assembly) — made for effective leadership in conquest, though much of it may be
  **convergent**, developed *during* the migrations rather than inherited.
- **Religion.** A shared **sky/storm father-god** (*Dyeus* → Zeus, Juppiter,
  Dyaus) and "heavenly twins" (Dioskouroi / Nasatya), but no transferable body of
  cult — incomers generally adopted the religion of the peoples they conquered.

## Geographic Spread and the Homeland

Crossland locates the origin only **within wide limits** — a steppe/temperate zone
from central Europe to the Urals — and leans, "from the linguistic standpoint,"
toward the **Pontic region and the steppe to its north**, while judging the
homeland still **unidentified**. He weighs the contemporary archaeological models:
**Gimbutas's** rapid Kurgan dispersal (~2500–2400 BCE) from the Volga–Pontic
steppe, with battle-axes, ochre-grave barrows (*kurgans*), horses and Corded Ware;
**Merpert's** longer Lower-Volga "Pit Grave" chronology (which Crossland finds fits
the linguistics better, allowing time for dialect differentiation); and **Thieme's**
north-central-European homeland (which he rejects). For the full modern debate —
the steppe model's vindication by ancient DNA, Renfrew's Anatolian-farming
alternative, and the Indo-Hittite question — see [[indo-european-origins]].

## Interaction — the dialectology argument

Crossland's distinctive contribution is methodological: he treats the **archaic,
"peripheral" features shared by Hittite-Luwian, Italic, Celtic and Tocharian** (the
*-r* medio-passive; *kwi-* relative pronouns; weak feminine gender) as evidence
that these branches were **carried out of the continuum earliest**, with Greek,
Indo-Iranian, Baltic, Slavonic and Germanic remaining in contact (and innovating,
e.g. the *centum→satem* shift) later. This **radial-dispersal** reading supports a
**centrally placed homeland** and an *early* Anatolian split — an early cousin of
today's "Indo-Anatolian" phylogenies.

## End Conditions

The process is not bounded by a single end: it continues through the Iron Age
expansions (Medes and Persians, the Indic settlement of the Ganges) treated in
later CAH volumes. Within this volume's horizon it culminates in the **Hittite
state** and the **Greek-speaking Middle Helladic mainland**.

## Historiography

A landmark of **restraint**: Crossland repeatedly refuses to convert linguistic
inference into ethnic narrative, and explicitly demotes the chariot and the "Aryan"
mystique. Written before the radiocarbon calibration revolution and decades before
ancient DNA, his Pontic-leaning agnosticism reads as remarkably modern; where he
differs from the now-dominant steppe model is mainly in **caution about mechanism**
and in taking the **early Anatolian divergence** seriously. See
[[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]] and the full debate at
[[indo-european-origins]].
