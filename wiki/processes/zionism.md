## Definition and Scope

The modern Jewish national movement holding that Jews constitute a nation entitled to self-determination in a homeland in the historic Land of Israel/Palestine. It is distinct from religious attachment to Zion as such, though it drew on that longstanding religious and cultural connection; as a political movement it emerged specifically in the context of nineteenth-century European nationalism and antisemitism.

## Causal Drivers

Theodor Herzl's 1896 pamphlet *Der Judenstaat*, written partly in response to the Dreyfus Affair and rising European antisemitism, catalyzed the movement's political organization; the 1897 First Zionist Congress at Basel established the World Zionist Organization and its founding platform (the Basel Program) calling for a publicly and legally secured homeland for the Jewish people in Palestine.

## Major Phases

Early organizational phase (1897–1917); the [[processes/zionist-movement-early-20c|early twentieth-century movement]] and the 1917 Balfour Declaration, in which Britain expressed support for a Jewish national home in Palestine; the Mandatory period (1920–1948) of institution-building and successive waves of immigration amid growing friction with the Arab population; and the 1948 establishment of the State of Israel, after which the movement's core institutional goal was achieved and Zionism's subsequent history became bound up with the state's own politics.

## Geographic Spread

Centered organizationally in Europe (initially Central and Eastern Europe) before shifting its practical focus to Ottoman and then British Mandatory Palestine; later developed a substantial support base in the United States.

## Interaction

Intersects directly with the broader [[processes/arab-israeli-conflict|Arab-Israeli conflict]], European antisemitism and the Holocaust (which dramatically accelerated both international sympathy for and Jewish demand for a secure national homeland), and the decolonization-era reorganization of the Middle East following Ottoman collapse.

## End Conditions

Achieved its core founding goal with the 1948 establishment of Israel; the movement and its ideology continue in modified form (support for and development of the Jewish state) rather than having a clear historical endpoint.
