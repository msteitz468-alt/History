# The Orientalizing Revolution

The **orientalizing revolution** is the wholesale transformation of Greek material and
intellectual culture, between roughly the ninth and sixth centuries BCE, by sustained
contact with the older civilizations of the **Near East and Egypt** — the importation
not only of goods but of techniques, motifs, the **alphabet**, and a new sense of the
wider world. It is the cultural counterpart of the [[greek-colonization|colonial
expansion]] and the precondition of Archaic Greek civilization.
[[boardman-cah-v3p3-1982|CAH Vol. III, Part 3]] (Ch. 36a, vi, Braun) treats the
linguistic and mythological dimensions; the artistic side is in CAH III.1 Ch. 19 and
III.3 Ch. 45.

## Definition and scope

This is **not** a wave of eastern conquest or settlement of Greece — Braun is emphatic
that "the Greeks knew their place." When Greeks entered the eastern world they did so
**by royal favour**, as traders, mercenaries or raiders, in a setting of unquestioned
**oriental cultural and political superiority**. The flow of influence was therefore
asymmetric: the East had little interest in Greek art for its own sake (Greek pottery
in the Levant marks the presence of *Greeks using it*, not native demand — unlike
Etruria, which avidly bought Greek vases), while Greece was transformed by what it
imported. The process is the channel, not a fusion of equals.

## Causal drivers

- **Trade.** The renewal of Greek commerce with the Levant in the ninth century —
  centred on [[al-mina|Al Mina]] and Tell Sukas — brought eastern metalwork (Urartian
  and north-Syrian bronze cauldrons with siren and griffin attachments), ivories,
  gold and silver plate, jewellery, and dyed textiles into Greece. [[cyprus]] was the
  great intermediary.
- **Piracy and slaving.** Trade and raiding were inseparable; Greeks supplied the
  eastern markets with **slaves** (Ezekiel's "Yawan... traded the persons of men" at
  Tyre).
- **Mercenary and settler service.** From [[saite-egypt|Psammetichus I]] onward Greeks
  and Carians served and settled in Egypt ([[naucratis|Naucratis]], the Stratopeda),
  bringing home a direct knowledge of Egyptian monumental art.
- **The Phoenicians.** [[phoenicians|Phoenician]] seamen and craftsmen carried eastern
  goods (and, in Greek legend, Cadmus and the alphabet) into the Aegean.

## What it produced

- **The alphabet.** The single most consequential transfer: the Greek adaptation of
  the Phoenician consonantal script — the letters called *phoinikeia*, "Phoenician
  things" — into the first true alphabet with vowels (see [[alphabet]]). Braun calls
  it "one of the most important events in world history."
- **Orientalizing art.** Geometric styles gave way rapidly to **orientalizing** art —
  eastern animal friezes, floral motifs, monsters, the techniques of metalwork and
  ivory-carving — most likely under the hand of **immigrant eastern craftsmen** as
  well as imported models.
- **Monumental sculpture and architecture.** The mid-seventh-century Greek influx into
  Egypt inspired the **kouros** (life-size standing statue, derived from Egyptian
  models but rendered nude and on principles of proportion rather than the Egyptian
  grid — hence the anecdote of [[saite-egypt|Theodorus of Samos]]'s two-halves statue)
  and the first **monumental stone architecture** (early Doric colonnades echoing
  Egyptian ones; palm capitals borrowed directly).
- **Loan-words.** A listable but limited set of **Semitic loan-words** entered Greek —
  almost all naming **traded goods** (gold *chrysos*, ivory, tunic *chiton*, cumin,
  sesame, myrrh, the *mina* weight, the writing-tablet *deltos*). Tellingly, the
  Greeks borrowed **no** abstract, political, philosophical or musical *concepts* — the
  loan-words attest commerce, not intellectual dependence. ("The Levantines did not
  teach the Greeks how to play.")
- **Mythological geography.** Greeks wove the peoples they met into **genealogical
  myth** — Cadmus the Phoenician, Danaus from Egypt, Europa, the eponyms Belos, Mopsus,
  Medeios — and so came to imagine kinship between Greek cities and "barbarian"
  peoples, even as they began (only after the Persian wars) to define themselves
  *against* the "barbarian."

## Interaction and end conditions

The orientalizing revolution runs **concurrent with** the [[greek-colonization|
colonization movement]] and the rise of the [[city-state|polis]]; the same Euboean
enterprise that planted [[al-mina|Al Mina]] and [[pithecusae|Pithecusa]] carried the
alphabet and eastern art. It **transforms into** mature Archaic Greek civilization
around 600 BCE, by which time the borrowed forms have been thoroughly Hellenized; the
Egyptian and Near Eastern "debt," once absorbed, gave way to a confident and
distinctively Greek art and thought. Later Greek belief in deep Egyptian *intellectual*
influence (Herodotus's claims that Egypt gave Greece its gods, geometry and
metempsychosis) is, on Braun's assessment, largely mistaken — the real debt was
technological and artistic.
