# Predynastic Egypt (Badarian to the Road to Unification)

The long cultural trajectory in the Nile valley — from the earliest farming
communities to the eve of the First Dynasty (~3100 BCE) — out of which the
Egyptian pharaonic state emerged. Where Mesopotamia's road to the state runs
through [[eridu|temple towns]] and [[uruk|Uruk]], Egypt's runs through a sequence
of **Upper Egyptian cemetery cultures** whose grave goods track increasing wealth,
craft specialization and social ranking. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p1-1970|CAH
Vol. I, Part 1]] (Ch. IX(a), Elise Baumgartel).

## The Cultural Sequence

CAH adopts the **Naqada** terminology (after the largest cemetery, where Petrie
first found these cultures), noting "no total break" between phases but "a grafting
of something new on to an old stem":

- **Badarian** (~4400 BCE; identified by Brunton and Gertrude Caton-Thompson near
  El-Badari, and stratified at El-Hammamiya below Naqada I) — the **oldest known
  predynastic culture**, with fine rippled pottery and early evidence of social
  differentiation in burials.
- **Naqada I (Amratian)** — characterized by **white cross-lined pottery**;
  village farming, hunting, and developing craft.
- **Naqada II (Gerzean)** — marked by **wavy-handled pottery**, increasing
  contact with the Near East, metal, and the wealth concentration and proto-urban
  centres (Hierakonpolis, Naqada, Abydos) that prefigure the unified state.
- **Protodynastic / "Semainian"** — the immediate run-up to Dynasty I.

## Sequence Dating (Method)

Egypt's predynastic chronology rests on **Sir Flinders Petrie's "Sequence Dating"
(S.D.)** — a pioneering **relative-dating** technique that seriated graves by the
typological development of their pottery (notably the white cross-lined and
wavy-handled wares). CAH stresses its limits: the S.D. stages "do not correspond
with any calendar or absolute dates," are of unequal length, and rest almost
entirely on **cemetery** evidence needing supplement from stratified settlements;
several of Petrie's type-ranges have since been revised. Sequence dating is a
landmark in archaeological method and a counterpart, for prehistoric Egypt, to the
king-list apparatus of [[ancient-near-eastern-chronology|Mesopotamian chronology]].

## Toward the State

The Naqada II concentration of wealth and the emergence of regional centres in
Upper Egypt set up the **[[unification-of-egypt-3100bce|unification]]** of Upper
and Lower Egypt (traditionally under [[narmer|Narmer]]/Menes) at the start of
Dynasty I — the foundation of the pharaonic state ([[early-dynastic-egypt|Early
Dynastic Egypt]]) and, soon after, of [[memphis|Memphis]] as its capital. CAH
Vol. I, Part 2 ([[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971]], Ch. XI) takes up the story precisely
here, with **Scorpion** and **Narmer** leading a confederation of Upper Egyptian
nomes against the north. Compare the independent [[state-formation|state-formation]]
trajectories elsewhere.

## Interaction

Runs parallel to, and in growing contact with, the [[chalcolithic|Chalcolithic]]
Near East; the **Naqada II / Gerzean** phase shows Mesopotamian (Uruk-period)
influence — cylinder seals, niched façades — that bears on the debate over whether
the *idea* of [[writing-and-record-keeping|writing]] reached Egypt by stimulus
diffusion. The whole sequence is conditioned by the [[postglacial-environment-near-east|Nile
floodplain]] and the post-Neolithic-Subpluvial desiccation that concentrated
population along the river.

## End Conditions

Predynastic Egypt "ends" with political unification and the start of dynastic
history (~3100 BCE) — the threshold of the [[early-bronze-age|Early Bronze Age]] in
Egyptian terms.

## Historiography

Baumgartel's account is mid-century and pottery-typological; absolute dates are
uncalibrated and the Petrie S.D. framework has been substantially revised. The
*cultural sequence* (Badarian→Naqada I→II) remains standard; treat specifics as of
their period. See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
