# Andean State Formation

## Definition and Scope

This page tracks the **independent rise of complex societies in the central Andes** (coastal
and highland Peru and Bolivia) from the Preceramic to the Middle Horizon, as set out in
[[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (Begun & Brashler, Ch 20). The Andes are South America's one
clear cradle of **state-level** development — the only region of the continent where CWH IV
recognizes true states, culminating (beyond this volume) in the **Inca Empire**.

## A Distinctive Foundation

Uniquely, Andean monumentality **preceded ceramics**. By **3000 BCE** coastal sites raised
public mounds and terraced "temple" platforms supported by rich **Pacific marine resources**
plus early agriculture — the **maritime foundations** hypothesis (Moseley). **Caral** in the
Supe Valley (c. 2627–2020 BCE) and **El Paraíso** on the coast, with **Kotosh** in the
highlands, attest the **Norte Chico** florescence and the highland **Kotosh Religious
Tradition** of fire-offering chambers. Long-distance exchange already linked coast (shell,
fish, salt, cotton) and highland (obsidian, potatoes, camelids).

## Major Phases

- **Preceramic (3200–2000 BCE):** Norte Chico/Caral monumentality; cotton **twined textiles**
  bearing motifs that anticipate later Chavín iconography.
- **Initial Period (1800–900/600 BCE):** ceramics adopted; a shift from maritime to **agrarian**
  reliance; proliferation of **U-shaped pyramid–plaza** complexes.
- **Early Horizon (c. 800–200 BCE):** **[[chavin|Chavín de Huántar]]** spreads a shared
  religious art style (the fanged humanoid-jaguar figure) across the region — "the first
  Andean state?"
- **Early Intermediate / First Horizon (200 BCE–600 CE):** regional states in the coastal
  valleys, above all the **[[moche|Moche]]** (and the Nazca).
- **Middle Horizon (600–1000 CE):** the highland empires of **[[tiwanaku|Tiwanaku]]** and
  **Wari**, whose collapse opened the way to the Late Intermediate.
- **Late Intermediate and Late Horizon (1000–1533 CE):** [[kedar-cwh-v5-2015|CWH V]]
  (MacCormack, Ch 24) carries the sequence to its culmination — the coastal **Chimú**
  state and then the **[[inca-empire|Inca]]** (Tawantinsuyu), the largest pre-Columbian
  empire, assembled from Cuzco after Pachacuti's victory in the Chanca war (1438) and
  legitimated through the Sun cult and the ritual claim to have founded ordered Andean
  society — until the Spanish conquest of 1533.

## Interaction

Andean state formation ran **parallel to and independent of** its Mesoamerican counterpart —
the two American civilizational cores never connected. With [[mesoamerican-state-formation]]
and [[north-american-mound-building]] it makes up CWH IV's trio of autonomous New World
trajectories. The Andean reliance on **camelids** (llama/alpaca) and the **absence of writing**
(record-keeping via Moche ceramics and, later, the *khipu*) distinguish it sharply from both
Mesoamerica and the Old World.

## End Conditions

No terminal end within this volume: each horizon's collapse seeded the next, a pulsing rhythm
of integration and fragmentation that continued to the **Inca** and was cut short only by the
Spanish conquest.

## Fagan & Durrani (2020): pre-agricultural coastal centers and the two enduring poles

Fagan and Durrani's *World Prehistory* (10th ed., 2020) corroborates the Preceramic
monumentality already recorded above with added site detail: **Aspero** (Supe River mouth), a
coastal settlement of 15 ha, flourished by **3055 BCE** — among the earliest complex coastal
societies known anywhere with no agricultural base. **Caral**, in the Supe Valley, was a major
kingdom by **2600 BCE** with 17 associated centers in the Supe region and six large stone
platforms (the largest 152 × 137 m, 18 m high); it grew cotton (not maize or potato) for
fishing nets and subsisted heavily on anchovies (confirmed via desiccated feces at the site),
before being abandoned c. 2000–1500 BCE (see [[caral]]). **El Paraíso**, on the Chillón River
near Lima (c. 1800 BCE), is the oldest U-shaped ceremonial complex on record, involving the
movement of roughly 100,000 tons of rock, and is interpreted as a ceremonial/pilgrimage center
rather than a residential settlement.

Fagan & Durrani frame Andean civilization around **two enduring poles**: the north coast (the
desert river valleys of Peru) and the south-central highlands (the Titicaca Basin) — noting
that only the Inca ever united both into a single empire, Tawantinsuyu. Both **Tiwanaku** and
**Wari** are described as marking "a turning point" in the Middle Horizon: small regional
states integrating into larger political units, a precedent for Inca-scale unification. El
Niño/ENSO events are repeatedly implicated in Andean civilizational collapses (the Moche
above; Tiwanaku's post-1200 CE drought) — comparable, in the authors' view, to Old World
climate-driven collapses (Egypt, the Khmer, the Maya).

On the Late Intermediate coastal states, Fagan & Durrani note that **split inheritance** — the
institution by which a deceased ruler's estate and mummy remained his *panaqa*'s in perpetuity,
forcing each successor to conquer fresh land — is directly identified as a shared, transmitted
institution running from the [[chimor|Chimú]] to the Inca, making it a load-bearing causal link
in the terminal phase of this process (see [[inca-empire]] for the fuller Pachakuti-era
mechanism).

The [[inca-empire|Inca]] are the culmination D'Altroy's [[daltroy-incas-2004|*The Incas*]] makes legible:
Tawantinsuyu **synthesised** the deep Andean repertoire — [[norte-chico|Norte Chico's]] non-market
urbanism, the [[tiwanaku|Tiwanaku]]/Wari highland-state and road traditions, and the pan-Andean ethic of
**[[mita-and-andean-reciprocity|reciprocity, the ayllu, and vertical complementarity]]** — and scaled it
into empire through the [[khipu|khipu]], the [[qhapaq-nan|road system]], and the
[[split-inheritance-and-royal-mummies|mummy-driven imperative to conquer]]. The Andean pathway thus
reached statehood on principles — labour-tribute, redistribution, verticality — wholly distinct from the
market-and-coin logic of Afro-Eurasia.
