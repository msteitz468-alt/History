## Definition and Scope

**Sedentism** is "the business of living together in one place for all or most
of the year" (Barker & Goucher). It is closely associated with — but **logically
independent of** — [[agricultural-origins]]: sedentism can precede farming, and
farming need not be sedentary.

## Sedentism without agriculture

Sedentism developed among some late-Pleistocene and early-Holocene *foragers*
where resources were rich and predictable enough to support year-round (or
multi-season) residence:
- **Ohalo II** (Sea of Galilee, ~19,000 BP) — occupied for several months a year.
- **Natufian** villages (Levant) — semi-sedentary wild-grain harvesters, the
  proximate antecedents of SW Asian farming (see [[mesolithic]]).
- Resource-rich estuaries on the **Atlantic seaboard of Europe** (e.g. Ertebølle).
- **Jomon** Japan — perhaps the most striking case of durable
  forager sedentism, with pottery and substantial settlements (see
  [[mesolithic]] and [[early-agriculture-japan]] *(Cycle 4)*).

## Agriculture without sedentism

Conversely, not all early farmers were sedentary — especially where cultivation
was combined with hunting, gathering, or herding (mobile and shifting
cultivation; pastoralism — see [[pastoralism]]). The "Neolithic package" decoupled
into its components (see [[neolithic-revolution]]).

## Why it matters

A *developed commitment* to farming in many regions was nonetheless tied to
permanent agglomerated settlement, and sedentism was the hinge for cascading
consequences: it made bulky storage and heavy material possessions viable;
permitted higher fertility and faster population growth (the mobile forager
mother "could only carry one baby"); but also concentrated refuse, vermin,
contaminated water, and disease (see [[agriculture-and-human-health]]). Crucially,
long-term **investment in a fixed place and its land** created issues of
ownership and inheritance — "the seedbed for emergent inequalities" (Bogaard).

## End Conditions

Ongoing and now near-universal; sedentism became the dominant human settlement
mode and the platform for villages, towns, and cities.
