# Western Medieval Church

## Definition and Scope

The **western medieval church** is the long process by which Latin Christianity became the dominant religious institution of western Europe from the end of antiquity to c.1500: converting new peoples, building a papal and episcopal hierarchy, generating monastic and mendicant forms of life, defining orthodoxy against dissent, and binding disparate societies through shared rite and Latin learning. This page is the process spine for [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan's survey]]; it is **not** a history of Eastern Orthodoxy or of Islam except where they shape the Latin story.

## Causal Drivers

- Mission from Rome and the Irish/English monasteries
- Alliance and conflict with kings and emperors ([[gregorian-reform]], [[investiture-contest]])
- Urban growth, schools, and [[medieval-university|universities]]
- Lay demand for apostolic poverty ([[waldensians]], [[francis-of-assisi]], [[mendicant-orders]])
- Catastrophe and consolation in the fourteenth century ([[black-death]], [[purgatory]])

## Major Phases

1. **Late antique foundations** — Constantine to Gregory I
2. **Conversion and Carolingian order** — missions; Franco-papal alliance; Viking disruption
3. **Reform, schism, crusade** — eleventh–twelfth centuries
4. **Papal high tide and mendicants** — Innocent III; friars; Lateran IV
5. **Crisis and redefinition** — Avignon; Great Schism; Constance; fifteenth-century piety

## Geographic Spread

From Mediterranean core to Ireland, Anglo-Saxon England, Germany, Scandinavia, and the Iberian Reconquista frontier; always with local custom under Latin norms.

## Interaction

Interacts with [[crusades]], [[reconquista]], state formation, and [[conciliarism]]; contrasts with Byzantine Christianity after [[east-west-schism-1054]].

## End Conditions

No sharp end: Logan closes pedagogically at **1492** inside a longer transition toward Reformation and global mission.
