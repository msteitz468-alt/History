# Struggle for Baltic Supremacy (dominium maris baltici)

The long contest between [[denmark-norway|Denmark]] and [[swedish-empire|Sweden]]
(with Poland-Lithuania, Russia, and the maritime powers as further players) for
control of the Baltic Sea, its trade, and its tolls — the *dominium maris
baltici* — from the Northern Seven Years' War (1563–70) to the
[[great-northern-war-1700|Great Northern War]] (1700–21). The struggle drove the
[[militarisation-of-scandinavia|militarisation]] and fiscal transformation of
both states, the rise of the [[swedish-empire|Swedish power state]], and the
eventual eclipse of both by Russia. Control of the Sound and its toll revenue was
a central stake.

## Definition and Scope

*Dominium maris Baltici* — combined rule over the Baltic's territorial waters and
coastal lands — was the prize. "For almost 200 years to come the struggle for
supremacy in the Baltic, dominium maris Baltici, became the dominant factor in the
foreign policy of both states" (L11082–11084). Three factors decided mastery:
control of the Sound and the Danish Belts; naval strength; and the extent of
control over the Baltic coastlines (L11074–11076). Denmark held the Sound gateway
and levied the **Sound Due** from 1429 (L11078–11079).

## Causal Drivers

The dissolution of the [[kalmar-union-1397|Kalmar Union]] left two rival monarchies
contesting the same sea; the wealth of the Sound tolls, the grain and naval-stores
trade (see [[baltic-grain-trade]]), and the ambitions of [[swedish-empire|Sweden]]
to encircle the Baltic drove repeated war. The contest was also shaped decisively
by outside powers — the Netherlands, France, Brandenburg, and ultimately Russia —
which intervened to preserve a balance favourable to their own trade.

## Major Phases

Olesen periodises seven wars across three phases (L12039–12041):

- **Phase 1 (1563–1629): Danish ascendancy.** The **Northern Seven Years' War
  (1563–70)** ended in the status quo *Peace of Stettin*; Sweden paid 150,000
  thalers for the Älvsborg fortress. The **Kalmar War (1611–13)** under
  [[christian-iv-of-denmark|Christian IV]] ended at *Knäröd* with another Älvsborg
  ransom — "the last successful attempt to defend [Danish] mastery in Scandinavia
  and in the Baltic" (L11475–11476). Christian IV's intervention in the
  [[thirty-years-war-1618|Thirty Years' War]] (1625–29) ended in defeat at Lutter
  and the *Peace of Lübeck* (1629).
- **Phase 2 (1630–1660): Swedish breakthrough and Danish near-collapse.** The
  **Torstensson War (1643–45)**, launched by [[axel-oxenstierna|Oxenstierna]],
  ended at *Brömsebro* with major Danish cessions (Gotland, Saaremaa, Jämtland,
  Härjedalen) and Sound-Due exemption. *Westphalia* (1648) gave Sweden Pomerania,
  Bremen and Verden — "Sweden had almost succeeded in establishing an encirclement
  of the Baltic" (L11706–11707). The **Karl X Gustav wars (1657–60)**: the Swedish
  king's winter march across the frozen straits forced the *Peace of Roskilde*
  (1658) — "the hardest peace treaty ever … in the history of Denmark" (L11751) —
  ceding Skåne, Halland, Blekinge, Bohuslän and Trondheim; "Sweden achieved the
  greatest territorial expansion in its history" (L11765). The *Peace of
  Copenhagen* (1660) returned Bornholm and Trondheim under maritime-power pressure
  and made the Sound an international waterway shared by the two coasts.
- **Phase 3 (1660–1721): mutual decline, Russian rise.** The **Scanian War
  (1675–79)** ended in a French *dictat* at St Germain restoring the status quo —
  "this illustrates the influence of foreign powers concerning conflicts in the
  Baltic region" (L11869–11872). The **[[great-northern-war-1700|Great Northern
  War]] (1700–21)**: Poltava (1709) was "a turning point … [pointing] to the coming
  end of Sweden's position as a Great Power" (L11957–11959); the *Peace of
  Frederiksborg* (1720) restored the Sound Due against Sweden but did not return
  the eastern provinces lost in 1660.

## Geographic Spread

From the Sound and the Danish straits to the eastern Baltic littoral (Estonia,
Livonia, Ingria), the southern German coast (Pomerania, Bremen-Verden), and the
Norwegian border. After 1660 Sweden "Swedified" the conquered east-Danish
provinces (Skåne et al.), founding the naval base of **Karlskrona** (1680) to
project power into the Baltic earlier in the season than Stockholm allowed
(L14427–14429).

## Interaction

The struggle drove the [[militarisation-of-scandinavia|militarisation]] and the
[[fiscal-military-state-scandinavia|fiscal-military transformation]] of both
realms, and was a principal catalyst for [[scandinavian-absolutism|absolutism]]
(the 1658–60 catastrophe precipitated the Danish coup of 1660). It steadily drew in
the Dutch and French as balancers.

## End Conditions

The *Peace of Nystad* (1721) transferred Ingria, Estonia, Livonia and Saaremaa to
Russia, which "benefited most"; "both Sweden and Denmark were reduced to
middle-ranking states" (L12071–12072). The era of the Scandinavian power states —
and of the Baltic as a Scandinavian lake — was over, replaced by Russian
predominance.
