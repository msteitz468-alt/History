# Industrial Revolution (First / British-led)

The cluster of technological, organizational, and social changes, beginning in Britain in the mid-18th century, that transformed production (especially textiles, iron, and energy) from artisanal and organic sources to mechanized, fossil-fuel-based, factory-centered systems. Often dated c. 1760–1840 for the "First" Industrial Revolution; subsequent waves in other countries and sectors.

## Causal Drivers and Debates
- Supply-side: coal, steam engine (Watt), spinning jenny/mule, puddling and rolling for iron, machine tools.
- Demand and institutions: secure property rights, relatively high wages, large domestic and colonial markets, Atlantic trade, scientific culture (Royal Society, etc.).
- Why Britain first / why not elsewhere at the same time (Great Divergence debates).

Consequences: sustained per-capita growth, urbanization, new social classes and conflicts, environmental transformation, and the technological foundation for later global power imbalances.
