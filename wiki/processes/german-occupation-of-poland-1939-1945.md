# The German Occupation of Poland (1939–1945)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]], the occupation regime built after the [[invasion-of-poland-1939|September 1939 campaign]] was the laboratory of Nazi imperialism: terror, racial reordering, and colonial exploitation without legal restraint.

## Definition and Scope

By early October 1939 Hitler abandoned any rump Polish state. Annexed territory (90,000 km², ~10 million people, 80% of them Poles) formed the new Reich Districts of Danzig-West Prussia (Albert Forster) and the Wartheland (Arthur Greiser), with additions to East Prussia and Silesia. The remainder — the **General Government** (11+ million people) under [[frank-hans|Hans Frank]] — was, in Evans's phrase, "a colony, outside the Reich and beyond its law." Hitler's 17 October 1939 briefing set the terms: "a hard ethnic struggle that will not permit any legal restrictions"; the region an "advanced jumping-off point" for the future invasion of the USSR (`enabled: [[operation-barbarossa-1941]]`); the intelligentsia "prevented from forming itself into a ruling class"; the population a labor reservoir.

## Terror Apparatus

The **RSHA** (created 27 September 1939 under [[heydrich-reinhard|Heydrich]], merging SD, Gestapo, and criminal police) directed five (later seven) [[einsatzgruppen|Einsatzgruppen]] (~2,700 men, led disproportionately by educated Freikorps-generation radicals such as Bruno Streckenbach), armed with pre-war lists of ~60,000 Polish professionals and intellectuals marked for death — Heydrich (8 September 1939): "the aristocrats, the Poles and Jews must be killed." Alongside them the Ethnic German Self-Protection militia under Ludolf von Alvensleben killed roughly half of the ~65,000 Poles and Jews murdered in the last quarter of 1939 (Klammer/Kulm ~2,000; Mniszek gravel pits 10,000; Karlshof 8,000). Army complicity was extensive (Bock's village-burning order; 531 settlements burned, 16,376 executed by 26 October 1939), though some commanders (Küchler, early on) court-martialled perpetrators, and Colonel-General **Blaskowitz's** memoranda to Hitler condemning the killings — "boundless brutalization and moral depravity" — stand as the major internal protest; Hitler called them "childish" and removed him. Frank's May 1940 "pacification action" (AB-Aktion) killed ~4,000 resistance suspects and intellectuals plus 3,000 convicted criminals. Roland Freisler's Bromberg show trials had passed 100 death sentences by the end of 1939.

## Exploitation

Göring's decree of 25 January 1940 demanded a million Polish workers; volunteering collapsed within weeks under the reality of treatment in Germany, and from April 1940 Frank used compulsion — over 1 million Poles working in the Reich by September 1941, only ~15% voluntarily (see the wider process in [[forced-labor-nazi-germany]]). Property confiscation ran through the Head Office of the Trustees for the East: 205,000+ businesses by February 1941; half of all businesses and a third of large estates in the annexed territories seized without compensation by June 1941. Food was rationed by race — Warsaw 1941 daily calories: Germans 2,613, Poles 669, Jews 184 — with the black market supplying an estimated 80%+ of Polish needs by 1943. Frank himself looted spectacularly (the Potocki estate; a Berghof imitation near Zakopane); a 1942 corruption investigation documented warehouses of furs and food sent home, but with Göring's backing he survived, and in 1943 even won the replacement of his SS rival Krüger.

## Interaction and End Conditions

The occupation incubated [[jewish-ghettoization-in-occupied-poland-1939-1942|ghettoization]] and then [[operation-reinhard-1942|extermination]]; its expulsions fed [[germanization-of-occupied-poland-1939-1944|Germanization]]; its terror bred the underground state and Home Army (see [[warsaw-uprising-1944]]). It ended with the Soviet offensives of 1944–45. `contrasts_with` the Soviet occupation of eastern Poland (class revolution vs. ethnic revolution — Evans's explicit comparison; see [[molotov-ribbentrop-pact-1939]] and [[katyn-massacre-1940]]).
