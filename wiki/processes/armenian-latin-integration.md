## Definition and Scope

The **integration of the Cilician Armenians with the Latins** is the long process — the
organizing thesis of [[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian (2000)]] — by which
the Armenian communities of [[cilicia|Cilicia]] and northern Syria became woven into the
political, military, dynastic, legal, and ecclesiastical fabric of the
**[[crusades|Crusades]]** and the **[[crusader-states|Latin East]]**. It is **not** religious
conversion (the Armenians kept their non-Chalcedonian Church) and **not** simple alliance:
it was a structural interpenetration that, on Ghazarian's reading, simultaneously **made**
the Armenian kingdom possible and **unmade** it through the union question.

The process cuts both ways. The Armenians **enabled** the crusaders; the Latins **crowned**
the Armenians. And the Latin embrace carried within it the demand — union with Rome — that
would fracture Armenian society.

## Causal Drivers

- **Strategic necessity.** After [[battle-of-manzikert-1071|Manzikert (1071)]] and the loss
  of the [[armenia|Bagratid homeland]], displaced Armenians in Cilicia needed a counterweight
  to [[byzantine-empire|Byzantium]] and the [[seljuk-empire|Seljuks]]; the crusaders were the
  obvious instrument.
- **Demographic reality.** Frankish numbers in the Levant were tiny (fewer than 150,000
  Westerners, under 1,000 noble families after a century); they could not rule without the
  sedentary Christian — largely Armenian — population.
- **Marriage.** Repeated intermarriage bound the houses (Baldwin of Boulogne to Arda,
  daughter of Toros I; Joscelin to a daughter of Constantine I; the founding of Armeno-Frankish
  lines in Jerusalem, Antioch, Tripoli, and Cyprus).
- **Commerce.** The port of **Ayas** and charters to Genoa, Venice, and Pisa tied Cilicia to
  the Italian maritime economy.
- **Papal leverage.** Rome made **church union** the price of military aid and of Levon I's
  crown — the driver that turned integration into internal division.

## Major Phases

1. **Enabling the First Crusade (1096–99).** Cilician Armenians supplied the marching armies
   with food, intelligence, guides, secured roads, and seized forts; **Constantine I** was
   made a marquis by the Franks for provisioning the siege of [[antioch|Antioch]]; the
   Armenian **Firouz** opened a tower of Antioch (1098); the Armenians of Edessa made
   **Baldwin of Boulogne** their ruler, founding the first crusader state.
2. **Dynastic interweaving (12th c.).** Armeno-Frankish marriage alliances multiplied across
   Outremer even as the Roupenid barons fought [[byzantine-empire|Byzantine]] reconquest.
3. **The crown (1198/99).** [[coronation-of-levon-i-1199|Levon I's coronation]] by the
   archbishop of Mainz bound the kingdom formally to the Latin West and to the union question.
4. **Legal and institutional Latinization (13th c.).** [[hetum-i-of-cilician-armenia|Hetum I]]
   translated the **Assizes of Antioch** into Armenian; the kingdom ran a multilingual
   chancery and adopted Frankish feudal forms.
5. **The Cyprus fusion and Lusignan succession (14th c.).** Dense marriage links with the
   **[[lusignan-dynasty|Lusignans]]** of [[cyprus|Cyprus]] culminated in the Cypriot house
   taking the Armenian throne after 1341 — integration completed to the point of dynastic
   absorption.

## Geographic Spread

Centred on Cilicia, the process radiated to **Edessa, Antioch, Tripoli, and Jerusalem** (via
marriage and rule) and to **Cyprus** (via refuge, church foundations, and dynastic union),
making the Armenian diaspora a connective tissue of the Latin East.

## Interaction with Other Processes

It is a sub-current of the **[[crusades|Crusades]]** and feeds directly into the
**[[armenian-church-union-question|union controversy]]**; in the 13th century it ran parallel
to the **[[mongol-latin-crusade-alliance|Mongol–Latin alliance]]**, with Cilician Armenia the
hinge between the Frankish and Mongol fronts against the [[mamluk-sultanate|Mamluks]].

## End Conditions

Integration did not so much end as **consume its host**: the Lusignan succession (1341) and
the Mamluk conquest of [[fall-of-cilician-armenia-1375|Sis (1375)]] extinguished the kingdom,
after which the Latin Lusignans held only the empty title "King of Armenia." Ghazarian's
verdict is that the very integration that built the kingdom — by importing the union
question — also hollowed out its social cohesion and hastened its fall. This is a
**thesis-driven, pro-Armenian** interpretation and is recorded as such; see
[[armenian-church-union-question|the union controversy]] for the dispute over how decisive
the religious factor really was.
