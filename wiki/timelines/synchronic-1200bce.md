# Synchronic Snapshot — c. 1200 BCE

> A cut across the whole world at a single moment. Around 1200 BCE the interconnected
> palace civilizations of the eastern Mediterranean and Near East unravelled within a
> couple of generations — the **[[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]]** — while
> other regions followed their own, independent rhythms. This page reads *horizontally*
> across space, where the [[master-timeline|Master Timeline]] reads *vertically* through time.

## The interconnected core: eastern Mediterranean & Near East

The zone that **collapses**. A tightly networked system of palace economies, royal
diplomacy (the [[amarna|Amarna]] correspondence), and long-distance bronze/tin trade.

- **Egypt** — the [[new-kingdom-egypt|New Kingdom]] at its late imperial height; [[ramesses-ii|Ramesses II]]
  then [[ramesses-iii|Ramesses III]], who repels the [[sea-peoples|Sea Peoples]] but presides
  over the empire's contraction.
- **Anatolia** — the [[hittites|Hittite Empire]], a great power, vanishes; its successor
  [[neo-hittite-states|Neo-Hittite states]] persist in the southeast.
- **The Levant** — [[ugarit|Ugarit]], the great entrepôt, is destroyed and never reoccupied;
  [[alalakh|Alalakh]] likewise. The [[phoenicians|Phoenicians]] and [[philistines|Philistines]]
  (themselves linked to the Sea Peoples) emerge from the wreckage; early [[kingdom-of-israel|Israel]] forms.
- **Aegean** — the [[mycenae|Mycenaean]] palace centres burn; literacy (Linear B) is lost,
  opening the [[greek-dark-age|Greek Dark Age]]; the [[dorians|Dorians]] of later tradition.
- **Mesopotamia & Iran** — [[assyria-middle|Middle Assyria]] and the [[kassites|Kassite]] dynasty
  of Babylon weaken; [[elam|Elam]] active to the east; the [[neo-assyrian-empire|Neo-Assyrian]]
  and [[neo-babylonian-empire|Neo-Babylonian]] orders lie in the future.

→ See the dedicated controversy: causes of the **[[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]]**
(earthquake storm, drought, systems collapse, the Sea Peoples, or all at once).

## Beyond the collapse zone — independent trajectories

- **East Asia** — the [[shang-dynasty|Shang dynasty]] rules the Yellow River valley, untouched
  by the western collapse; the [[zhou-dynasty|Zhou]] conquest that founds China's longest dynasty
  is roughly a century or two away (trad. 1046 BCE).
- **South Asia** — the mature Indus cities are long gone; this is the early/middle
  [[vedic-period|Vedic Period]], an oral, pastoral-agrarian society with no cities or writing.
- **Nubia** — under New Kingdom Egyptian control; the independent kingdom of [[kush-meroe|Kush]]
  rises only after Egypt withdraws.
- **Mesoamerica** — the [[olmec|Olmec]], the "mother culture," are in their formative period at
  San Lorenzo — monumental sculpture but a wholly separate hemispheric clock.
- **Temperate Europe** — Urnfield bronze cultures; no states, no writing, but dense metal networks.

---

## The pattern

The collapse was **regional, not global**. Its severity tracks **connectedness**: the more a
society depended on the international bronze-age system (royal exchange, tin from afar, palace
redistribution), the harder it fell. Peripheral and unconnected societies — Shang China, the
Olmec, Vedic India — simply kept their own time. This is the central lesson a synchronic cut
teaches that a vertical timeline hides: **simultaneity is not connection**
(`concurrent_with` is not `caused_by`).
