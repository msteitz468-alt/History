# Council of Florence
## Narrative
In **1439 CE**, the **Council of Florence** (an ecumenical council of the Catholic and Orthodox churches) was convened in Italy. Facing imminent conquest by the Ottoman Turks, the Byzantine Empire and the Greek clergy signed an agreement recognizing papal supremacy and Latin theological formulas in exchange for Western military aid against the Turks.

The Russian delegation was led by **Metropolitan Isidore**, a Greek appointed by Constantinople to head the Russian Church. Isidore actively participated in the Council and signed the union document. 

Upon his return to Moscow, Isidore celebrated a solemn liturgy in the Cathedral of the Assumption, read the Union of Florence decree, and offered prayers for the Pope. Grand Prince **Basil II** immediately rejected the union, ordered Isidore’s arrest, and imprisoned him in a monastery (from which Isidore later escaped to the West). 

A council of Russian bishops convened in 1443 CE, formally condemned the Church union, and deposed Isidore. In **1448 CE**, the Russian bishops elected **Metropolitan Jonas** without seeking the confirmation of the Patriarch of Constantinople. This act established the **autocephaly** (independence) of the Russian Orthodox Church (l. 1543).

## Causal Analysis
* **Ottoman Threat:** The military expansion of the Ottoman Turks in the Balkans and their siege of Constantinople forced the Byzantine Empire to seek union with Rome as a desperate plea for crusader help.
* **Muscovite Defiance:** Grand Prince Basil II and the Russian clergy were deeply suspicious of the Latin Church and rejected any compromise on Orthodox dogma, viewing the Greek signature as a betrayal of the true faith.

## Consequence Analysis
* **Russian Church Autocephaly:** The rejection of the Union of Florence led directly to the independent, self-governing status of the Russian Orthodox Church, ending its administrative dependence on Constantinople.
* **Spiritual Isolation and Self-Importance:** The fall of Constantinople in 1453 CE, coming shortly after the Greeks "betrayed" Orthodoxy at Florence, convinced many Russians that Moscow was the sole remaining guardian of the true Christian faith. This directly inspired the **Moscow the Third Rome** doctrine.
* **Separation from the West:** The rejection of the union reinforced Russian suspicions of Western Catholicism, contributing to the cultural and religious isolation of the Muscovite state.

## Actors
* **Metropolitan Isidore:** The Greek Metropolitan of Kiev and All Russia who signed the union and was subsequently deposed.
* **Basil II (the Blind):** The Grand Prince of Moscow who rejected the union and ordered Isidore's arrest.
* **Metropolitan Jonas:** The first autocephalous Metropolitan of the Russian Orthodox Church, elected in 1448 CE.

## Historiography
Historians emphasize the Council of Florence as a critical turning point that decoupled the religious history of Russia from Byzantium. It transformed the Russian Orthodox Church into a national institution closely aligned with the rising autocracy of the Grand Princes of Moscow.

## From F. Donald Logan, *A History of the Church in the Middle Ages* (2013)

### Latin–Greek negotiations and *Laetentur coeli*

A ~**700-strong** Greek delegation led by Emperor **John VIII Paleologus** with the patriarch of Constantinople and representatives of Antioch, Alexandria, and Jerusalem met Latins at **Ferrara** then **Florence** under [[eugenius-iv|Eugenius IV]]. Four issues settled: *filioque* (saints of both sides mean the same); [[purgatory]] (East yielded to Latin geography of afterlife and suffrage); Eucharist (dominical words essential; leavened/unleavened and liturgical custom free); Roman primacy (“true vicar of Christ, head of the whole church”). Bull ***Laetentur coeli*** proclaimed **6 July 1439**. Unions followed with Armenians (22 Nov 1439), Copts (4 Feb 1442), and efforts with Nestorians, Syrians, Cyprus.

### Failure of union and 1453

Failure was not merely 1453: King Albert’s death (Oct 1439) delayed Western aid; Greeks reached Constantinople only 1 Feb 1440 amid John VIII’s grief for his wife, giving anti-union forces free rein; Western army defeated at **Varna** (Sept 1444); union solemnly proclaimed in Hagia Sophia **12 Dec 1452** mere months before the city’s fall (defenders outnumbered ~20:1)—with **1453** the reunion quest ended. “And so it stands.” Logan also notes national responses (Pragmatic Sanction of Bourges 1438; German neutrality at Mainz; England supporting Eugenius) that paralleled the council struggle with Basel.

### Historiography (Logan)

Gill on Florence and Eugenius IV; Philippides/Hanak on 1453; Oakley’s *Conciliarist Tradition*; Pastor. Florence’s success against Basel’s rump is a chief agent in the demise of effective [[conciliarism]].
