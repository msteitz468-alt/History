# Third Crusade (1189–1192)

## Narrative

The **Third Crusade** was Latin Christendom’s response to **[[battle-of-hattin-1187|Hattin]]** and the **[[fall-of-jerusalem-1187|loss of Jerusalem (1187)]]**. **Frederick Barbarossa** died en route in Anatolia; **Philip II of France** and **Richard I of England** prosecuted the war in Palestine. The long **siege of Acre** (ended 1191) restored a coastal bridgehead; Richard’s victory at **Arsuf** and advance toward Jerusalem never retook the city. The **Treaty of Jaffa** (1192) left Jerusalem under **[[saladin]]** while granting Frankish coastal holdings and pilgrimage access. Saladin died in 1193; the **[[ayyubid-dynasty|Ayyubid]]** inheritance fractured among his kin.

[[cobb-race-for-paradise-2014|Cobb]]’s operational sequence from Muslim sources:

**Prelude and Acre siege.** During winter 1188/89 Saladin received word of a European expedition to avenge Jerusalem. After small post-Hattin triumphs (Latakia, Safad, Karak, Shawbak/Montreal May 1189, Belvoir early 1189), **Guy of Lusignan** (title disputed after Sybil’s death; released after Hattin under vow to desist) put **Acre** under siege in late **August 1189** with Italian/German/French/Low Countries reinforcements. Main battle **4 October**: stalemate — Franks stopped to plunder the Muslim camp after routing troops; Saladin rallied. A Damascene coppersmith’s Greek fire destroyed Frankish siege towers (vinegar-steeped hides having failed). **Frederick Barbarossa**: into Anatolia late March 1190; mid-May Germans defeated Qilij Arslan’s son and took Konya; **10 June 1190** Barbarossa died bathing in the Göksu (stroke); remnant thinned by capture, hunger, and disease. Saladin sought Almohad naval help and wrote Baghdad — little came of either.

**Kings and fall of Acre.** Philip arrived **20 April 1191**; Richard **8 June** (having taken Cyprus). Muslim sources: Richard inferior in rank but “more famous in war and for courage.” On **12 July** Franks stormed Acre; the Muslim commander negotiated surrender — **200,000 gold dinars**, return of prisoners and the True Cross relic from Hattin, three monthly installments (biographers: Saladin had not authorized the terms). Cobb’s thesis: Acre was not a great strategic loss (Franks already had Tyre/Tripoli harbors); “the real casualties at Acre were Saladin’s prestige and Muslim morale.” On **20 August** Richard massacred some **three thousand** Muslim prisoners on the plain before Acre after Saladin sought to renegotiate the first installment — brutality “not forgotten even in the final days of the Frankish presence.”

**Arsuf to truce.** Richard marched south toward Jaffa; Saladin shadowed. At **Arsuf** a reckless Frankish charge knocked Muslims off balance; Franks settled Jaffa; Saladin ordered Ascalon’s fortifications dismantled lest Richard use it against Jerusalem/Egypt. Late July 1192 Saladin struck Jaffa during Richard’s absence — town surrendered, citadel resisted; Richard returned and ousted the Muslims (“last Frankish triumph of the Crusade”). **September 1192** truce of **three years and eight months**: Franks hold coast Tyre to Jaffa; Saladin retains Ascalon (walls demolished jointly); free passage in Palestine for Christians and Muslims. Richard sailed from Acre **9 October**. Saladin strengthened Jerusalem’s walls and established the Salihiyya madrasa (formerly Church of St. Anne), a hospital, and a sufi house. He died **4 March 1193** in Damascus.

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` Muslim recovery of Jerusalem and collapse of the first kingdom’s army.
`contributed_to:` Western monarchical crusading as prestige politics; Frankish retention of Tyre as a bridgehead after Hattin.
`preceded_by:` Ayyubid unification of Egypt and Syria.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` a reduced Second Kingdom of Jerusalem on the coast (Tyre–Jaffa).
`did not produce:` recovery of Jerusalem.
`enabled:` continued Ayyubid–Frankish diplomacy and later crusades (including Damietta); memory of Richard’s Acre massacre shaped later Muslim refusal to surrender (e.g. Mt. Tabor garrison in the Fifth Crusade).
`contributed_to:` Ayyubid appanage succession after Saladin’s death (al-ʿAdil preeminent by 1200).

## Islamic perspective (Cobb 2014)

See Narrative. Cobb’s assessment of Saladin after Hattin: military record often “miserable” (defeats, prolonged field alienating commanders); geopolitics usually gifted except Tyre; hard to claim he “won” the Third Crusade — a costly stalemate. Stronger diplomatically and politically as pious *mujahid* image-heir to Nur al-Din. Greatest achievement remained the reconquest of Jerusalem (Muslim hands more or less until the end of WWI).

## Historiography

Western tradition elevates Richard vs. Saladin as chivalric duel; Arabic sources (Ibn Shaddad, Imad al-Din) stress logistics, holy war, and Saladin’s exhaustion. [[cobb-race-for-paradise-2014|Cobb]] embeds the crusade in Ayyubid politics, supplies Acre terms and the prisoner massacre, and judges the outcome a prestige disaster for Saladin more than a strategic loss of a harbor.
