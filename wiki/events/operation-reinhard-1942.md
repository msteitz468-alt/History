# Operation Reinhard (1942–1943)

**Operation Reinhard** (Aktion Reinhardt) was the SS program to murder the Jews of the General Government in three purpose-built extermination camps — **Bełżec, Sobibór, and Treblinka** — run by Odilo Globocnik's staff with personnel drawn from [[aktion-t4-1939|Aktion T4]]. Roughly 1.7 million people were gassed there in under two years, the deadliest single phase of [[the-holocaust|the Holocaust]]. Documented for the wiki from [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)]].

## Narrative

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: before the [[wannsee-conference-1942|Wannsee Conference]], [[himmler-heinrich|Himmler]] appointed Odilo Globocnik — Austrian Nazi, convicted of murdering a Jew in 1933, restored by Himmler as SS and Police Leader in Lublin — to kill all the Jews of the General Government. On 13 October 1941 Himmler agreed with Globocnik and Krüger that a camp be built at **Bełżec** "created for the sole purpose of killing people"; construction began 1 November 1941. Staff came from [[aktion-t4-1939|Aktion T4]] — still paid by the Führer Chancellery — including Christian Wirth (first commandant of Bełżec, later inspector of all three camps) and Franz Stangl (commandant of Sobibór, then Treblinka); guards were Trawniki-trained Ukrainian auxiliaries.

- **Bełżec** (first gassings 17 March 1942): wooden gas chambers using engine exhaust; 75,000 killed in the first four weeks; rebuilt in concrete (capacity 2,000) mid-1942; ~414,000–600,000 dead by December 1942. Stangl on his first visit: "the pits . . . full, they were full . . . not hundreds, thousands, thousands of corpses."
- **Sobibór** (operational mid-May 1942): brick gas chambers reached through the 150-metre "tube"; ~250,000 killed. Prisoner revolt on **14 October 1943**: inmates killed most of the SS staff with axes and knives; 300+ of 600 escaped, ~200 survived the pursuit.
- **Treblinka** (first gassings 23 July 1942): built to "improve" on the others; 312,000 gassed by end of August 1942, chiefly from the Warsaw ghetto's Great Deportation. Under first commandant Irmfried Eberl the camp collapsed into open chaos — trains standing for days, mass shooting on the platform, unburied bodies everywhere — until Wirth and Globocnik dismissed him and installed Stangl, who built the notorious fake railway station with painted clock and thought of the victims, by his own later confession, as "cargo." Prisoner revolt on **2 August 1943**: ~half of 850 inmates broke out; perhaps 100 escaped for good.

Victims were told they were in transit camps and driven naked into "showers"; gold teeth, hair, and valuables were harvested — Reinhard plunder to 15 December 1943 was valued at nearly 180 million Reichsmarks, processed by Pohl's SS Economy and Administration Head Office. From late 1942 – 1943 the buried corpses (~700,000 at Treblinka alone) were exhumed and burned on rail grilles under Blobel to destroy the evidence; the camps were razed, planted over, and disguised as farms. After the revolts, Himmler ordered the remaining Jewish laborers of the Lublin district shot: **Operation Harvest Festival** (3–4 November 1943) killed 42,000 in a single coordinated action at Majdanek, Trawniki, and Poniatowa — 18,000 at Majdanek in one day, dance music broadcast over loudspeakers to drown the shooting. A report to Eichmann of 11 January 1943, intercepted by the British, counted nearly 1.25 million killed in the Reinhard camps by the end of 1942; the Korherr report of 23 March 1943 (a large-print version prepared for Hitler's 54th birthday) put Jews "sluiced through the camps" at 1,873,539. Modern estimates: **~1.7 million murdered at Bełżec, Sobibór, and Treblinka**. Related sites: gas vans at **Chełmno** (from 6 December 1941; >360,000 killed, mostly from the Łódź ghetto) and gas chambers at **Majdanek** (~180,000 dead, up to 120,000 of them Jews).

Evans quotes the Polish physician Zygmunt Klukowski's diary on the deportations through Szczebrzeszyn (April–May 1942) — police shooting Jews "like ducks . . . in their own houses — men, women, and children, indiscriminately," and his shame at being forbidden to treat the wounded — as well as postwar scenes of local treasure-hunters digging through the ash at Treblinka until state memorials were built.

## Historiography

Evans's account rests on perpetrator testimony (Stangl's interviews, Höss's memoir), survivor accounts (Oskar Berger), the intercepted Höfle telegram, and the Korherr report. He rejects the "industrialized, impersonal murder" image as half-true at best: the camps were inefficient, breakdown-prone, and saturated with personal sadism; the perpetrators were "hardened antisemites," not faceless bureaucrats. The T4 → Reinhard personnel continuity is central to his argument that the "euthanasia" program was the technical and human incubator of the extermination camps.

## Shirer (1960) additions

Hoess's Nuremberg affidavit as quoted by Shirer supplies the perpetrator comparison of the Reinhard camps with Auschwitz: Treblinka's commandant told him he had "liquidated 80,000 in the course of half a year" using monoxide in ten 200-person chambers, methods Hoess judged "not very efficient" against his Zyklon B and 2,000-person chambers — and at Treblinka "the victims almost always knew that they were to be exterminated," driving Auschwitz's delousing deception. Shirer's chapter also documents the commercial infrastructure (Topf & Sons crematoria correspondence; C. H. Kori's Belgrade bid citing "four furnaces for Dachau and five for Lublin") and the "Max Heiliger" Reichsbank deposits of victims' gold. Source: [[shirer-rise-fall-third-reich-1960]]
