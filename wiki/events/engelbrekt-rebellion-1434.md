# Engelbrekt Rebellion

The Swedish rising of 1434, led by the miner-noble **Engelbrekt Engelbrektsson**,
that began in **Dalarna** among miners and freeholding peasants and spread across
Sweden, becoming the pivotal crisis of the [[kalmar-union-1397|Kalmar Union]]. Per
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]], it ended the
strong union monarchy of [[erik-of-pomerania|Erik of Pomerania]]: the **Swedish
council of the realm sided with the opposition**, sending Erik a letter withdrawing
allegiance in August 1434.

## Causes and Significance

Grievances combined heavy union taxation, Erik's appointment of **non-native
bailiffs**, and the damage done to Swedish (especially mining) trade by his Schleswig
war and the Hanseatic blockade. The rebellion fused peasant and noble discontent and
articulated a **constitutionalist programme** (1435–36): the king to rule Sweden
according to the Landlaw, assisted by native men, **with the council of the realm as
the real governing body**. It thus marked the transfer from Act-of-Coronation
monarchism to **council constitutionalism**, and inaugurated the long pattern of
Swedish resistance — running through [[battle-of-brunkeberg-1471|Brunkeberg (1471)]]
and the [[stockholm-bloodbath-1520|Stockholm Bloodbath (1520)]] to the union's final
breakup. It coincided with the Council of Basel and the Uppsala archiepiscopal
controversy, producing a brief period of Nordic ecclesiastical autonomy.

## Historiography

The Cambridge History (Olesen) treats Engelbrekt as the hinge between
Act-of-Coronation monarchism and council constitutionalism; later Swedish national
tradition idealised him as a popular liberator, a framing modern scholarship
qualifies by stressing the leading role of the council aristocracy. See
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]].
