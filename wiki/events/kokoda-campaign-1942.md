# Kokoda Campaign

The Kokoda Campaign (mid-1942 to early 1943) was the critical land defence of Papua New Guinea against Japanese forces attempting to reach Port Moresby overland across the Owen Stanley Ranges. The campaign produced some of the most gruelling fighting Australians experienced in the Pacific War and became one of the foundational myths of Australian national memory.

## Context

Having failed in May 1942 to reach Port Moresby by sea (the **Battle of the Coral Sea** turned back the Japanese fleet), Japan landed troops on the north coast of Papua with the aim of crossing the Owen Stanley Ranges to capture Port Moresby by land. Port Moresby was the "seat of administration and vital southern port" — its loss would have compromised Australia's capacity to serve as the Allied southern base.

The terrain was extreme: "dense tropical rainforest and steep mountains, with malarial swamplands along the coast; temperatures soared during the day, and the nights were bitterly cold."

## Course of the Campaign

Australian troops advanced north from Port Moresby "along an immensely difficult single-file track to the airstrip at Kokoda." They were "forced to retreat until by September [1942] the enemy was within sight of Port Moresby, but then was driven back."

In August 1942, Japanese marines landed to the west at **Milne Bay** — and were repulsed. This was the first Allied land defeat of Japanese forces in the Pacific War.

In a costly battle, Australians and Americans then drove the Japanese from their fortified beachheads at **Buna, Gona, and Sanananda** on the northern coast of Papua.

**Papuan contribution**: The campaign was aided by Papuan carriers and guides ("aided by Papuans") — a contribution that has received increased historical recognition in recent decades.

## Significance

"The heroism of Australian soldiers on the Kokoda Track, aided by Papuans, became one of the key images of the Pacific war." The campaign demonstrated:
1. Australia could be directly threatened and successfully defended
2. Japanese forces were not invincible in land combat
3. The MacArthur–Curtin partnership could deliver operational results

By the end of 1942 "the Japanese advance had ended" in Papua. The attention then shifted to the anomaly of the dual army structure (volunteer AIF vs. conscript Militia), which Curtin resolved in January 1943 by extending conscript service to the South-West Pacific theatre.

## Historiography

Paul Ham (*Kokoda*, HarperCollins, 2004) is a major popular-scholarly account. The campaign occupies a central place in Australian war memory — arguably replacing Gallipoli as the defining national military experience for some generations. The chapter (Darian-Smith, in [[bashford-macintyre-cha-v2-2013]]) treats it as one of the "key images of the Pacific war" without extended tactical analysis.
