# The Conversion of Vladimir I and the Christianisation of Rus′ (988)

In **988** the Rus′ Grand Prince **Vladimir I** ("the Great") accepted **Orthodox Christianity** from Byzantium and imposed it as the state religion of [[kievan-rus|Kievan Rus′]] — one of the decisive religious-political events of the long tenth century, binding the east Slavs permanently to the Byzantine cultural world. [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]] (Noonan) treats it within the history of European Russia, noting in particular that **Vladimir sacked the Byzantine city of Cherson c. 988** in the course of the affair.

## Narrative

The conversion arose from a **military-diplomatic bargain with Byzantium** and a staged "test of the faiths." Per Primary Chronicle (Laurentian, ll. 5029–5999+):

Pagan context: 983 Vladimir sacrifices after conquests; lot on Christian Varangian (from Greece, house by future Virgin Church site) and son. Father: "These are not gods, but only idols of wood. Today it is, and tomorrow it will rot away... The God whom the Greeks serve... has made heaven and earth... I will not give up my son to devils." Killed; "For at this time the Russes were ignorant pagans. The devil rejoiced thereat, for he did not know that his ruin was approaching" (ll. 5038–5071).

986 missions: Bulgars (Mohammedan): circumcision, no pork/wine, 70 wives in paradise; Vladimir: "Drinking is the joy of the Russes. We cannot exist without that pleasure." Germans (Pope): fasting/glory of God; "Depart hence; our fathers accepted no such principle." Jewish Khazars: circumcision/Sabbath; Vladimir on dispersion: "How can you hope to teach others while you yourselves are cast out and scattered abroad by the hand of God?" Greek scholar: exhaustive narrative — creation (Satan fall as Sathanael, Adam/Eve/serpent, Cain/Abel birds bury, Noah, Babel 72 nations, idolatry from Serug/Abraham fire test, Isaac/Jacob/Moses plagues/Red Sea/Manna/Sinai/golden calf, judges/kings/David/Solomon split kingdoms, prophets of Jewish rejection + Gentile calling, incarnation prophecies, life/miracles/baptism/crucifixion (darkness 6th-9th hr, veil rent, dead arise), resurrection (3rd day, 40 days, ascension, Pentecost), judgment (canvas shown: righteous right, sinners left). Vladimir: "Happy are they upon the right, but woe to those upon the left!" "I shall wait yet a little longer" (ll. 5113–5727).

987 boyars/elders: envoys test. Bulgars: "stand ungirt... no happiness... dreadful stench. Their religion is not good." Germans: "many ceremonies... we beheld no glory there." Greeks (Basil/Constantine + Patriarch array clergy, incense, hymns in "wide space"): "we knew not whether we were in heaven or on earth. For on earth there is no such splendor or such beauty... God dwells there among men... Every man, after tasting something sweet, is afterward unwilling to accept that which is bitter." Boyars: "If the Greek faith were evil, it would not have been adopted by your grandmother Olga who was wiser than all other men." Vladimir: decision his (ll. 5728–5795).

988: "Vladimir proceeded with an armed force against Kherson, a Greek city." Siege; earthwork stolen via tunnel; Anastasius arrow: "There are springs behind you to the east, from which water flows in pipes. Dig down and cut them off." Vow: "if this hope was realized, he would be baptized." Cuts supply; thirst surrender. Messages to Basil/Constantine: captured city; "Unless you give her [unwedded sister Anna] to me to wife, I shall deal with your own city as I have with Kherson." Emperors: "It is not meet for Christians to give in marriage to pagans. If you are baptized, you shall have her to wife...". Vladimir: "willing to accept baptism, having already given some study to their religion". Anna: "It is as if I were setting out into captivity; better were it for me to die at home." Brothers: "Through your agency God turns the land of Rus’ to repentance... Do you not see how much harm the Russes have already brought upon the Greeks?" Anna sails; Vladimir eye disease ("could see nothing"). "The Princess declared... if he desired to be relieved... he should be baptized with all speed". Baptized Church of St. Basil (Kherson square); "as the Bishop laid his hand upon him, he straightway received his sight." "I have now perceived the one true God." Followers baptized. Marries Anna. "Those who do not know the truth say he was baptized in Kiev, while others assert this event took place in Vasil’ev". Takes Anna/Anastasius/priests/relics (Clement + Phoebus disciple)/vessels/images. Founds church on Kherson mound (earth from his earthwork). Priests instruct: full creed (Father unbegotten, Son born, Spirit emanation; one Baptism, True Body/Blood, 7 councils Nicaea–Nicaea II, reject Latins: wafers, earth mother, trample cross, simony etc.). (ll. 5796–5999+). 

He returned (implied) to impose on Kiev (Perun idol thrown in Dnieper per other accounts; mass baptism). The "test of the faiths" (Bulgars/Germans/Jews/Greeks) and Cherson siege + healing miracle are central to the Laurentian narrative.

Direct from Primary Chronicle (Cross & Sherbowitz-Wetzor, Laurentian Text, lines 6001-6062): Upon return, Vladimir "appropriated two bronze statues and four bronze horses, which now stand behind the Church of the Holy Virgin, and which the ignorant think are made of marble." He then "directed that the idols should be overthrown, and that some should be cut to pieces and others burned with fire. He thus ordered that Perun should be bound to a horse’s tail and dragged down Borichev to the stream. He appointed twelve men to beat the idol with sticks, not because he thought the wood was sensitive, but to affront the demon who had deceived man in this guise". Cast into Dnieper with command "If it halts anywhere, then push it out from the bank, until it goes over the falls"; it lodged on "Perun’s sandbank". Heralds proclaimed all (rich or poor) must go to river or "risk the Prince’s displeasure". "On the morrow, the Prince went forth to the Dnieper with the priests... and a countless multitude assembled. They all went into the water: some stood up to their necks, others to their breasts, and the younger near the bank, some of them holding children in their arms, while the adults waded farther out. The priests stood by and offered prayers." There was "joy in heaven and upon earth to behold so many souls saved. But the devil groaned, lamenting, 'Woe is me! how am I driven out hence!'"

Vladimir then "ordained that wooden churches should be built and established where pagan idols had previously stood. He thus founded the Church of St. Basil on the hill where the idol of Perun and the other images had been set". He "began to found churches and to assign priests throughout the cities, and to invite the people to accept baptism in all the cities and towns." He also "took the children of the best families, and sent them for instruction in book-learning". (See also tithe and Church of Holy Virgin construction in 989 entries, lines 6155-6161.)

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **Byzantine alliance** and the unprecedented offer of the **purple-born Anna**, which gave Vladimir a prestige no other route to legitimacy could match.
- `contributed_to:` the search, common to the new tenth-century rulers (compare [[boleslaw-i-chrobry|Poland]], [[hungary-arpad|Hungary]]), for a **unifying state religion** to sacralise dynastic power; and the precedent of **Olga's** earlier personal baptism.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **Orthodox Christianisation of the east Slavs** and the founding of the Rus′ church (metropolitan of Kiev under the patriarch of Constantinople).
- `produced:` the adoption of **Church Slavonic** liturgy and literacy (the Cyrillo-Methodian inheritance), shaping Russian, Ukrainian, and Belarusian culture.
- `enabled:` the cultural apogee under Yaroslav the Wise and the integration of Rus′ into the Byzantine commonwealth — while setting eastern Europe on a **confessional path distinct from the Latin west**, a divide that long outlasted the Middle Ages.

## Historiography

NCMH III emphasises that the **Primary Chronicle's** account (compiled generations later) is the dominant but unreliable source; the "choice of faiths" narrative is a literary construction, and the precise sequence of the Cherson campaign, the marriage, and the baptism is debated. What is secure is that Rus′ became, from the late tenth century, a **Byzantine-rite Christian polity**.

From this source (Laurentian Text, ll. 5000–6000 range): detailed "test of the faiths" (986) with Greek scholar's creation-to-judgment catechism (Satan fall, flood, Abraham, Moses/Red Sea, prophets of rejection + Gentiles, incarnation/crucifixion/resurrection details, judgment canvas); envoys' 987 report (Bulgars "dreadful stench", Germans "no glory", Greeks "we knew not whether we were in heaven or on earth"); Olga cited as precedent; 988 Kherson specifics (Anastasius water betrayal, Vladimir vow "if this hope was realized, he would be baptized", eye disease cure upon baptism in St. Basil church "I have now perceived the one true God", Anna's reluctance "as if I were setting out into captivity", post-baptism creed/7 councils/Latin warnings). Chronicle presents as divine turning point ("the devil... did not know that his ruin was approaching"). Cross/Sherbowitz-Wetzor intro (source page) flags legendary material/chronological issues for 9th–10th c.; use critically with archaeology/Byzantine sources (e.g., no direct Anna mention in some external records). Recent scholarship debates exact date/sequence but accepts political alliance + Cherson pressure leading to baptism and state Christianisation. Collection now supplies primary voice grounding the legendary elements.

The Primary Chronicle itself (this range, lines 6006-6047) supplies vivid eyewitness-style detail on the Kiev implementation: deliberate humiliation of Perun ("to affront the demon"), mass immersion ("countless multitude"), and the chronicler's dramatic devil's lament framing the event as cosmic defeat of paganism. It also records immediate institutional steps (wooden churches on former idol sites, St. Basil foundation, clerical assignment, child education in "book-learning" fulfilling prophecy) and Vladimir's prayer for the "new people" (6048-6055). Later entries (6497/989) detail Greek artisans for the tithe-church of Holy Virgin, images/vessels/crosses from Cherson given to Anastasius of Kherson, and the explicit curse on tithe-violators (6155-6231). These passages ground the "top-down" character and Byzantine material link. Source criticism (per source page): composite annalistic work with monastic redaction ca. 1113/1116; strong pro-Rurikid/Kievan/Christian bias; valuable for 988+ but retrospective. Cross-check with archaeology (no contemporary Rus' texts) and Byzantine sources essential. Recent scholarship treats the Perun/Dnieper narrative as stylized but core event reliable in outline.
