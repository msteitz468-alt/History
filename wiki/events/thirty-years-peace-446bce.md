# The Thirty Years' Peace, 446/5 BCE

## Narrative

The **Thirty Years' Peace** (winter 446/5) ended the [[first-peloponnesian-war|First
Peloponnesian War]] between Athens and the [[peloponnesian-league|Peloponnesian League]].
[[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH Vol. V]] (Ch. 6, D. M. Lewis) sets it in the crisis of **446**: the
loss of Boeotia at **Coronea**, the revolt of **Euboea**, and the defection of **Megara**
left Attica exposed to a Peloponnesian invasion under the young king **Pleistoanax**, who
reached Eleusis and then unexpectedly **withdrew** (his commanders, Athenians said, bribed
by [[pericles|Pericles]]; both were later exiled by Sparta). With his land empire gone,
Pericles suppressed Euboea and negotiated peace, the Athenian **Callias** again the chief
agent.

## Terms

- Athens **surrendered its mainland gains** — Nisaea and Pegae (the ports of Megara),
  Achaea, and Troezen — but **kept its naval empire**; otherwise each side **kept what it
  held**.
- Neither side was to **attack the other if the latter offered arbitration** — the
  essential clause, and (Lewis judges) the one least likely to work.
- **Lists of allies** were appended, with provision for further adhesions, but Athens was
  **barred from a full alliance with Argos**. The duration was fixed at **thirty years**.

## Significance

The Peace is the formal recognition of the **bipolar order** that the [[peloponnesian-war|
Peloponnesian War]] would later shatter: as Lewis puts it, "the dualism of Cimon's
aspirations — Sparta to dominate by land, Athens by sea — was being accepted on both
sides." Crucially, **no ancient source says Sparta thereby "recognized the Athenian
empire,"** but in effect it confined Athens to its naval sphere while the freeing of the
Megarid and Boeotia made Attica far more vulnerable to future invasion. In the years of
peace Athens consolidated: the **Coinage Decree** and other instruments of empire, the
foundation of **Thurii** (444/3) and **Amphipolis** (437/6), the suppression of the
**Samian revolt** (440), and the completion of the **[[parthenon|Parthenon]]**.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the **collapse of the Athenian land empire** in 446 and the demonstrated
vulnerability of Attica. produced: a fourteen-year peace; the consolidation of the mature
[[delian-league|empire]]; and a settlement whose **arbitration clause failed** in 432–431,
when Athens declined to arbitrate the Corcyra and Potidaea disputes — the legalistic
trigger of the [[peloponnesian-war]].

## Historiography

The Peace's terms survive mainly through Thucydides' later allusions (the Athenian
admission that they had been "under the heavier pressure") and the Peace of Nicias' echo of
its clauses; its exact wording on autonomy and Aegina is uncertain. Its breakdown is the
spine of Thucydides' analysis of the war's causes (see [[peloponnesian-war]] and
[[classical-greek-historiography]]).
