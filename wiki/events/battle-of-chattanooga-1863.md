# Battle of Chattanooga

## Narrative

Following the Union defeat at Chickamauga in September 1863, Confederate forces under Braxton Bragg besieged the Army of the Cumberland in Chattanooga, Tennessee. [[grant-ulysses-s|Ulysses S. Grant]], given overall command of the western theater, reopened supply lines (the "Cracker Line") and brought in reinforcements before launching a coordinated assault from 23–25 November 1863. Union troops captured Lookout Mountain on 24 November ("The Battle Above the Clouds") and, in a famous episode on 25 November, unexpectedly stormed and carried the seemingly impregnable Confederate positions on Missionary Ridge in an assault that outran its own generals' original, more limited orders.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** the Confederate army's overextended and poorly coordinated siege position after Chickamauga; contributed_to by declining Confederate morale and Bragg's strained relations with his subordinate commanders.

## Consequence Analysis

**produced:** the collapse of Confederate military presence in Chattanooga and much of eastern Tennessee, opening the route into Georgia that Sherman used for the 1864 Atlanta Campaign; Grant's promotion to General-in-Chief of all Union armies in March 1864.

## Historiography

The Missionary Ridge assault is a frequently cited case study in the "friction" and unpredictability of battle: participants' own accounts (including Grant's memoirs) note that the final charge exceeded the scope of the original orders, and historians continue to debate how much credit belongs to deliberate Union tactical planning versus spontaneous initiative among the advancing troops.
