# First Indochina War (1946–1954)

War fought between France and the Viet Minh (Democratic Republic of Vietnam, led by Ho Chi Minh and militarily directed by [[actors/giap-vo-nguyen|Vo Nguyen Giap]]) over the future of French colonial rule in Indochina. It began after Japan's wartime occupation ended and France moved to reassert control against the Viet Minh's September 1945 declaration of independence; low-level conflict escalated into full war after the French bombardment of Haiphong and Viet Minh attacks on French forces in Hanoi in late 1946.

## Narrative

French forces, including commanders such as [[actors/de-lattre-de-tassigny|Jean de Lattre de Tassigny]] (who stabilized the French position in 1951 before his death in 1952) and later [[actors/navarre-henri|Henri Navarre]], fought a war increasingly shaped by Giap's development of a conventional Viet Minh army capable of large-unit operations, supplied and advised by the People's Republic of China after 1949. French strategy sought to draw the Viet Minh into set-piece battles where superior firepower could prevail; this culminated in the fortification of Dien Bien Phu in late 1953 as bait, which instead became a decisive Viet Minh siege victory in the spring of 1954 after Giap moved heavy artillery onto the surrounding high ground, a logistical feat the French had judged impossible. The fall of Dien Bien Phu (7 May 1954) broke French political will to continue the war.

## Causal Analysis

The war was `caused_by` France's attempt to restore prewar colonial control against a Vietnamese nationalist movement already in possession of the machinery of an independent state, and `contributed_to` by the wider Cold War alignment of the conflict after 1949–1950, when Chinese and Soviet recognition of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam (and American recognition of the French-backed State of Vietnam) internationalized what had begun as a colonial war.

## Consequence Analysis

The war `produced` the July 1954 Geneva Accords, which partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel pending elections that were never held, and `enabled` the American-backed State of Vietnam (later Republic of Vietnam) in the south. French withdrawal left a vacuum of Western commitment that the United States increasingly filled through the 1950s, making the First Indochina War the direct institutional and military precursor to the [[hubs/warfare/cold-war/vietnam-war/vietnam-war|Vietnam War]].

## Historiography

The war is treated by most historians (e.g., Fredrik Logevall's *Embers of War*) as inseparable from the subsequent American war in Vietnam — a single continuous conflict over Vietnamese self-determination interrupted by a change of principal external power rather than two unconnected wars. Sourcing on the war remains heavily French- and Vietnamese-language in its primary documentation; English-language synthesis (Logevall, Fall) is the collection's main window onto it.
