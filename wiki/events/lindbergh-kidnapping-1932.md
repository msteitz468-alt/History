# Lindbergh Kidnapping (1932)

## Narrative
On the night of March 1, 1932, 20-month-old Charles Augustus Lindbergh Jr. was abducted from his nursery in the Lindbergh home in Hopewell, New Jersey. A ransom note demanding $50,000 was left. Despite ransom payment, the baby's body was found on May 12, 1932, a short distance from the home.

## Causal Analysis
- **Extortion Plot:** Driven by a criminal plan to extort ransom from the wealthy and famous Lindbergh family.
- **Opportunity:** The newly built home was isolated and lacked security systems or guards, allowing the intruder to use a wooden ladder to enter the nursery.

## Consequence Analysis
- **Federal Legislation:** The tragedy led to the passage of the Federal Kidnapping Act of 1932 (the "Lindbergh Law"), which made kidnapping across state lines a federal offense and authorized the FBI to investigate.
- **European Exile:** The sensational trial of Bruno Richard Hauptmann and relentless media harassment forced the Lindbergh family to flee the United States in December 1935, seeking safety and privacy in England and France.
