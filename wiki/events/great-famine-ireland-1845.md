# The Great Famine (Ireland, 1845–1852)

The catastrophic failure of the Irish potato crop to the blight *Phytophthora infestans*, which killed
roughly a million people and drove a comparable number to emigrate — the central trauma of modern
Irish history. Its severity reflected the extreme dependence of a large cottier and labouring
population on the potato, compounded by a British relief response constrained by laissez-faire ideology
and by the structure of the Irish land system (see [[irish-land-question]]).

The Famine permanently altered Ireland: it began a century-long demographic decline, normalized mass
[[irish-diaspora|emigration]] (especially to America), and fed the bitterness that radicalized both
[[irish-nationalism]] and the [[irish-land-war|land agitation]] of later decades. Whether British
policy amounted to negligence or something closer to culpability remains contested — part of the wider
[[irish-historiography-revisionism|revisionism debate]].
