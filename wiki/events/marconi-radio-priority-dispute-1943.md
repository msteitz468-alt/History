# Marconi Radio Priority Dispute & the Seizure of Tesla's Papers (1943)

This page covers the long contest over who invented radio and the events around [[actors/tesla-nikola|Tesla]]'s death in January 1943.

## Narrative

[[actors/tesla-nikola|Tesla]] held foundational U.S. patents on tuned wireless transmission that predated [[actors/marconi-guglielmo|Guglielmo Marconi]]'s commercial systems, but Marconi won fame, the 1909 Nobel Prize, and lucrative contracts. The priority question was litigated for decades. On **21 June 1943**, months after Tesla died penniless in the Hotel New Yorker (7 January 1943), the **U.S. Supreme Court** (in *Marconi Wireless Telegraph Co. v. United States*) upheld key prior patents — including Tesla's — over Marconi's, a ruling widely read as posthumous vindication of Tesla's radio priority (though the case also turned on government patent-liability questions).

On Tesla's death the U.S. **Office of Alien Property** seized his papers and effects; the **FBI** took an interest, in part because of his 1930s claims of a particle-beam **[[concepts/death-ray-teleforce|"death ray" / teleforce]]** weapon. No working superweapon was found, but the seizure and secrecy fed enduring conspiracy mythology.

## Historiography

The priority question is among the most litigated in the history of technology, and the historiography is correspondingly contested. Seifer (*Wizard*, 1996) marshals the patent record — Tesla's foundational tuned-circuit patents (notably no. 645,576 / 649,621), the 1900 Patent Commissioner's disqualification of Marconi's first application as resting on a "pretended ignorance of the nature of a 'Tesla oscillator' [that is] little short of absurd," the French Bonjean ruling for Tesla, and the 1916 *Marconi v. U.S. Navy* suit — to argue Tesla's priority. The 1943 Supreme Court decision (*Marconi Wireless Telegraph Co. v. United States*, 320 U.S. 1) is frequently cited as vindicating Tesla, but scholars caution that the ruling turned substantially on government patent-liability questions and the prior art of Lodge, Stone, and others, not on a simple finding that "Tesla invented radio." The FBI/Office of Alien Property seizure is documented through the declassified files Seifer obtained; the death-ray dimension of the government's interest, and the later conspiracy literature, are treated by Seifer as reconstruction from fragmentary evidence rather than established fact.
