# Woman Suffrage Procession (3 March 1913)

Mass suffrage parade down Pennsylvania Avenue the day before [[wilson-woodrow|Woodrow Wilson]]'s first inauguration, organized by [[paul-alice|Alice Paul]] and the Congressional Committee of NAWSA. Cox (and suffrage historiography) treat it as the public opening of the Wilson-era federal campaign — "greatest since Seneca Falls" in contemporary and later framing.

## Narrative

Organized by [[paul-alice|Alice Paul]] and [[burns-lucy|Lucy Burns]] for NAWSA’s Congressional Committee after a permit fight with District authorities. Contemporary estimates: **5,000+** marchers before a crowd on the order of **~250,000** inaugural visitors. Inez Milholland led on horseback in the iconic image of the movement. Police failure amid assault and obstruction produced a national scandal and Senate investigation; Harriot Stanton Blatch’s telegram traffic and press coverage made Paul a national figure overnight. [[wells-ida-b|Ida B. Wells]] and other Black suffragists contested segregation within the march order — an early race flashpoint Cox links to the Jim Crow climate the new administration would deepen. Wilson, arriving for the inauguration, was not an organizer but became the presidency the procession was designed to confront.

## Historiography

Standard suffrage narrative centerpiece; Cox re-centers it as the moment Wilson *could have* embraced universal democracy rhetoric and instead began a presidency of delay and opposition. Race order-of-march debates (Wells et al.) are now first-class in the literature.
