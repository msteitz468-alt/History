# The Battle of Bunker Hill (17 June 1775)

The first major engagement of the **[[american-revolution|Revolution]]** and one of its bloodiest
hours for the British — fought, despite its name, mainly on **Breed's Hill**. As the British army
under siege in [[siege-of-boston-1775|Boston]] prepared to seize the Charlestown Peninsula,
American militia under Colonel **William Prescott** (diverted by **Israel Putnam**) fortified the
heights overnight, building a redoubt and breastwork to "bait the British into making an attack."

## Narrative

General **[[howe-william|William Howe]]**, rejecting [[clinton-henry|Clinton]]'s proposal for an
amphibious envelopment of the peninsula's neck, chose a frontal assault. Roughly **2,300 British**
advanced against about **1,500 Americans**. Prescott's men and Colonel John Stark's troops behind a
rail-and-stone fence held their fire to lethally close range, shattering the first two assaults.
Only on the third assault — the Americans now out of powder — did the redcoats storm the redoubt
with the **bayonet**, breaking the militia, who had few. The British held the field but at
crippling cost: **226 killed and 828 wounded** to the Americans' **139 killed, 278 wounded, 31
captured**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the contest for the **high ground commanding Boston harbor** during the siege.
- `contributed_to:` Howe's decision to **assault frontally** rather than cut off the peninsula —
  "gross ignorance of the most common … rules of the profession," in subordinates' words.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` a lasting British **reluctance to storm fixed defenses** (visible in Howe's caution at
  [[battle-of-long-island-1776|Long Island]] and his evacuation of Boston).
- `produced:` American **self-confidence** — but also an "overreliance on fixed defenses." Gage
  warned London the rebels were "now spirited up by a rage and enthusiasm … you must proceed in
  earnest or give the business up."

## Historiography

Long mythologized in American memory ("Don't fire till you see the whites of their eyes") as a moral
victory; the *West Point History* stresses the technical lesson — disciplined infantry with bayonets
will eventually carry earthworks held by ill-equipped militia, but at a price that can be strategically
decisive.
