# Túpac Amaru II Rebellion

The largest anti-colonial uprising in the Spanish Americas before independence: a wave of
Andean rebellion (1780–1783) that began in November 1780 when José Gabriel Condorcanqui,
the *curaca* of Tinta who took the name [[tupac-amaru-jose-gabriel|Túpac Amaru II]], seized
and executed the corregidor Antonio de Arriaga. It rapidly fused with a parallel Aymara
rising led by Túpaj Katari (Julián Apaza), which besieged La Paz. Glave treats it in
[[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-2|CHNPA III Part 2]] as the climax of an
eighteenth-century cycle of Andean revolt — the "[[republic-of-indians-in-revolt|republic
of Indians" in revolt]].

## Narrative

The rising spread across the southern Peruvian and Upper Peruvian highlands, drawing in
hundreds of thousands. Túpac Amaru II was captured in April 1781 and executed with his
family in Cusco in a deliberately spectacular display; the Aymara phase under Túpaj Katari
continued the siege of La Paz until late 1781. Sporadic resistance persisted into 1783.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` [[bourbon-reforms]] — new taxes, the spread of the *repartimiento de
  mercancías* (forced sale of goods by corregidores), and tighter fiscal extraction.
- `contributed_to:` [[mita]] — the Potosí labor draft remained a grievance fusing material
  and moral complaint.
- `contributed_to:` an Andean **Inca-revivalist** political imagination that gave the
  movement legitimacy and a program beyond local protest.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` ferocious repression, the banning of the Inca nobility's titles and of
  Garcilaso's *Royal Commentaries*, and the abolition of the hereditary [[curaca|curaca]]
  class in many areas.
- `enabled:` a long-term transformation of Andean ethnic politics; its memory became a
  touchstone for later indigenous and national movements ([[ethnogenesis]]).

## Actors

[[tupac-amaru-jose-gabriel]] · Túpaj Katari (Aymara phase) · [[bourbon-reforms|Bourbon

## Historiography

Scholars debate whether the rebellion was a "national" Inca-restorationist project, a
defensive reaction to Bourbon extraction, or a coalition of distinct regional and ethnic
movements (Quechua vs. Aymara) with divergent aims. Glave stresses its character as the
peak of a longer rebellion cycle rather than an isolated explosion.
