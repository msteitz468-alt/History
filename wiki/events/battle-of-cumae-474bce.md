# The Battle of Cumae, 474 BCE

## Narrative

The **Battle of Cumae** (474 BCE) was the naval victory of **[[syracuse|Syracuse]]** under
the tyrant **[[hiero-i|Hiero I]]** over the **[[etruscans|Etruscans]]** off the Bay of
Naples. [[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH Vol. V]] (Ch. 7, D. Asheri) sets it in Hiero's drive to make
"the southern Tyrrhenian Sea a Syracusan lake." When the Campanian Greek city of **Cumae**,
under Etruscan pressure, appealed for help, Hiero's fleet sailed at once, joined the local
forces, and **destroyed many Etruscan ships**. An abortive attempt was made to plant a
Syracusan garrison on the island of **Pithecusae (Ischia)** opposite Cumae.

## Significance

- **The Etruscan decline.** Modern scholars regard Cumae as "the turning point in the
  Etruscan decline" at sea — though, Asheri cautions, this was not yet perceptible to
  contemporaries. Coming after the costly Phocaean fight at Alalia (c. 540) and the
  expulsion of the Tarquins from [[rome|Rome]] (509), the defeat left the
  [[etruscans|Etruscans]] "in no state to fight another day," and opened Campania to the
  Samnites and the Po Valley to the Celts.
- **Syracusan hegemony.** The victory confirmed Syracusan dominance of the southern
  Tyrrhenian; the democratic republic would resume the same policy with raids on Elba and
  Corsica in 453.
- **Panhellenic propaganda.** Hiero advertised the win as a Greek victory over barbarians —
  bronze helmets dedicated at **Olympia** (one survives, inscribed "from the Tyrrhenian
  spoils at Cumae"), a tripod at **Delphi** beside Gelon's, and odes by **Pindar** (*Pythian*
  1) and Simonides — assimilating Cumae to [[battle-of-himera-480bce|Himera]] and the Persian
  Wars as a triumph of "civilization over barbarism."

## Consequence Analysis

caused_by: Etruscan pressure on the Campanian Greeks and Hiero's Tyrrhenian ambition.

balance of the western Mediterranean) and Syracusan supremacy in the region; revived
panhellenic ideology in the Deinomenid court.
