# The Berlin Blockade and Airlift (1948–1949)

The **Berlin Blockade** was the first great crisis of the [[cold-war|Cold War]]: from June
1948 to May 1949 the Soviet Union cut off land and water access to the Western-occupied
sectors of **Berlin** — an island deep inside the Soviet zone — in a failed attempt to force
the Western powers out. The West's response, the **Berlin Airlift**, became the iconic early
symbol of Western resolve. From [[leffler-westad-cold-war-v1|*The Cambridge History of the
Cold War*, Vol. I]].

## Narrative

The crisis grew directly out of the **division of Germany**. As the Western powers moved to
rebuild and unify their zones — culminating in a **currency reform** (the Deutsche Mark) in
June 1948 that signaled a separate West German state — **Stalin** retaliated by blockading
West Berlin, gambling that the West would either abandon the city or abandon its plans for a
western Germany. Rather than force a corridor by arms (risking war) or withdraw, the US and
Britain mounted a round-the-clock **airlift**, flying in food, coal, and supplies to over two
million West Berliners for **nearly eleven months**. Unable to stop the airlift without
shooting down aircraft, Stalin **lifted the blockade in May 1949**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **division of Germany** and the Western powers' move to a separate West
  German state — above all the June 1948 **currency reform** (the Deutsche Mark).
- `contributed_to:` **Stalin's gamble** to force the Western powers out of Berlin (or abandon
  a western Germany) by blockading the isolated city.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the founding of **[[nato]]** (April 1949), as the crisis crystallized Western
  European demand for an American security guarantee.
- `produced:` the **creation of two German states** in 1949 — the Federal Republic (West)
  and the German Democratic Republic (East) — institutionalizing the division the blockade
  had been meant to prevent.
- A major **propaganda victory** for the West: West Berlin became the showcase of the "free
  world" behind the Iron Curtain, and the airlift recast the recent enemy (the Germans) as a
  protected ally.

## Actors

- The **Soviet Union** (Stalin) vs. the **United States, Britain, and France** (the Western
  occupying powers), with the **West Berliners** as the population sustained by the airlift.
  See [[united-states]], [[nato]], [[marshall-plan-1948]].

## Significance

The blockade was the moment the Cold War's lines in Europe **hardened into permanence**: it
accelerated the [[marshall-plan-1948|Marshall Plan]]'s Western bloc, produced NATO and the
two Germanies, and established **Berlin as the central flashpoint** of the Cold War — a role
it would play again in the crisis of 1958–61 and the building of the Berlin Wall.
