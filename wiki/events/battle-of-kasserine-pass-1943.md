## Historiography
This page remains a stub pending fuller ingestion; the fullest tactical treatments are found in official US Army histories, Rick Atkinson's *An Army at Dawn*, and Carlo D'Este's operational studies of the North African campaign. Primary material includes unit after-action reports, Rommel's own account, and Ultra intercepts (declassified decades later, which reshaped understanding of German intentions). Historians have long debated how much of the initial American defeat reflects Lloyd Fredendall's specific command failures versus the structural inexperience of a US Army fighting its first major armored engagement of the war; the battle is now conventionally read as a costly but formative learning experience rather than a near-disaster with lasting strategic consequences.
