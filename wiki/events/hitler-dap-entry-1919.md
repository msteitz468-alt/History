# Hitler Joins the German Workers' Party (DAP) (September 1919)

## Narrative

While employed by the Reichswehr as an "education officer" and informant to counter Bolshevik and "Jewish" revolutionary ideas among troops, Corporal Adolf Hitler was ordered to attend a meeting of the German Workers' Party (DAP) in the Sterneckerbräu beerhall in Munich as an observer. The DAP was a tiny völkisch-nationalist group (roughly 40 members) founded earlier in 1919 by Anton Drexler.

Hitler spoke against Bavarian separatism. Anton Drexler was struck by the performance: "Goodness, he’s got a gob. We could use him." Drexler invited him to join. Hitler accepted and was issued membership card (photographed in sources; sequential number in the small party).

This was the moment Kershaw identifies as the "discovery of a talent."

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` the specific post-revolutionary Munich army environment (Reichswehr political section under Karl Mayr retaining and "talent-spotting" Hitler after Lechfeld speeches); the existence of the marginal DAP as one of many radical circles; Hitler's own post-Pasewalk decision to enter politics and his demonstrated oratorical power. `contributed_to:` the DAP's need for effective speakers in a competitive radical-right milieu.

`preceded_by:` [[events/hitler-pasewalk-1918]], [[events/munich-revolution-1918-1919]], Lechfeld speaking success.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` Hitler's first formal political affiliation and platform. Within weeks he became the DAP's star agitator; the party (soon NSDAP) became his personal vehicle. The "nobody" from Vienna failure had found his vocation.

`enabled:` the entire subsequent career — propaganda mastery, SA formation, 1923 putsch, refoundation on Führerprinzip, 1933 seizure of power.

`followed_by:` rapid growth of the movement, alliance with Röhm and paramilitaries, [[beer-hall-putsch-1923]].

## Historiography

Kershaw (Range 01, Ch. 4) frames the entry as accidental in form but decisive in substance: the army's use of Hitler as anti-Bolshevik educator placed him in the right room at the right time. The DAP was insignificant; Hitler's oratory transformed it. The event marks the end of the "fantasy and failure" / "drop-out" / "elation and embitterment" phases and the beginning of "discovering a talent." Hitler's own later accounts emphasize destiny; Kershaw stresses structural opening + personal aptitude discovered late.

## Evans (2003): From Entry to Dictatorial Chairmanship

Evans ([[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003]]) extends the arc from the entry
through the party's early consolidation:

- **Formation**: Vienna gave Hitler [[schonerer-georg-von|Schönerer]]'s
  Pan-Germanism and [[lueger-karl|Lueger]]'s lesson in antisemitic mass
  demagogy; Evans's judgment (attributed): his antisemitism then "had an
  abstract, almost theoretical quality... his hatred of Jews only became
  visceral, personal and extreme at the end of the First World War." From
  *Mein Kampf* Evans extracts the Vienna lessons Hitler claimed: mass
  mobilization technique from the Social Democrats, terror met with "equal
  terror," and "a deep contempt for the state and the law" — "If the species
  itself is in danger... the question of legality is reduced to a subordinate
  rule."
- **The indoctrination courses** (June 1919, under Karl Alexander von Müller
  and Gottfried Feder) revealed his oratory; his 16 September 1919 letter
  articulated an "antisemitism of reason" aiming at "the removal of the Jews
  altogether" — his earliest documented programmatic statement.
- **DAP entry**: sent to observe the meeting of 12 September 1919; enrolled as
  member 555 (numbering began at 501 — he was roughly the seventh member).
  Full-time agitator by end of March 1920; party renamed NSDAP February 1920;
  the 25-Point Programme (24 Feb 1920) mixed Pan-German, antisemitic, and
  pseudo-socialist planks. Evans explicitly rejects reading Nazism as an
  outgrowth of socialism: a race-based "counter-ideology to socialism." Hitler
  designed the flag (red for socialism, swastika for racist nationalism,
  black-white-red evoking the imperial flag) and secured dictatorial
  chairmanship on 29 July 1921 by twice threatening resignation over merger
  schemes.
- **Escalating exterminationist rhetoric**, verbatim-documented by Evans: Jews
  "to be exterminated" (6 Apr 1920); Jews as "racial tuberculosis" (7 Aug
  1920); "solution of the Jewish question" only by "brute force" (Apr 1921);
  and January 1923: "if they come to power, our heads will roll in the sand;
  but... when we get our hands on power: 'Then God have mercy on you!'"
- **Violence and models**: a month in Stadelheim gaol (1922) for the
  Ballerstedt beating; the NSDAP banned in most states after the
  [[walther-rathenau|Rathenau]] assassination — but not Bavaria; and from
  [[mussolini-benito|Mussolini]]'s March on Rome (Oct 1922) the party took
  the "Führer" title, the stiff-armed salute, and the march-on-the-capital
  tactic that failed at the [[beer-hall-putsch-1923|Beer-Hall Putsch]].

## Shirer (1960) Additions

Shirer (1960) supplies documentary/eyewitness-adjacent detail on the party's earliest institutional life:

- Hitler's own account of the founding committee meeting he attended: "In the dim light of a grimy gas lamp four young people sat at a table... the association possessed seven marks and fifty pfennigs... Terrible, terrible! This was club life of the worst manner and sort."
- 24 February 1920, Hofbräuhaus Festsaal: Hitler unveiled the 25-point NSDAP program before ~2,000; party renamed NSDAP 1 April 1920. Points 11–16, 18 (the "socialist" planks — abolition of unearned income, nationalization of trusts, profit-sharing, death penalty for usurers/profiteers) were quietly abandoned once industrialist funding arrived, though Hitler declared the 25 points "unalterable" in 1926 for tactical reasons.
- Swastika/flag design (summer 1920): red field, white disk, black hakenkreuz, personally designed by Hitler, adapted from symbols the Ehrhardt Brigade free-corps had painted on their helmets fighting in the Baltic 1918–19. Hitler: "In red we see the social idea of the movement, in white the nationalist idea, in the swastika the mission of the struggle for the victory of the Aryan man."
- July 1921 Führerprinzip seizure: after a committee revolt led by Drexler tried to curb Hitler and merge with Streicher's rival group, Hitler forced through statute changes abolishing the committee by threatening resignation plus a libel suit against the rebels — corroborates and dates the "dictatorial chairmanship" episode.
- Party finance: the Völkischer Beobachter was purchased December 1920 for 60,000 marks, likely via Army secret funds channeled through Maj. Gen. Ritter von Epp; became a daily January 1923, further funded by wealthy patrons (Helene Bechstein, Gertrud von Seidlitz) and a $1,000 loan (March 1923) from Ernst "Putzi" Hanfstaengl against a Völkischer Beobachter mortgage — a fabulous sum amid hyperinflation.
- Early cadre, briefly: Ernst Röhm (Army District Command VII Munich staff) organized the first Nazi strong-arm squads, basis of the SA, and brought Army protection to the fledgling party; Dietrich Eckart, "spiritual founder of National Socialism" per Shirer, funded and mentored Hitler and is the dedicatee of Mein Kampf's closing line.
