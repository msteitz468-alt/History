# Hitler Joins the German Workers' Party (DAP) (September 1919)

## Narrative

While employed by the Reichswehr as an "education officer" and informant to counter Bolshevik and "Jewish" revolutionary ideas among troops, Corporal Adolf Hitler was ordered to attend a meeting of the German Workers' Party (DAP) in the Sterneckerbräu beerhall in Munich as an observer. The DAP was a tiny völkisch-nationalist group (roughly 40 members) founded earlier in 1919 by Anton Drexler.

Hitler spoke against Bavarian separatism. Anton Drexler was struck by the performance: "Goodness, he’s got a gob. We could use him." Drexler invited him to join. Hitler accepted and was issued membership card (photographed in sources; sequential number in the small party).

This was the moment Kershaw identifies as the "discovery of a talent."

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` the specific post-revolutionary Munich army environment (Reichswehr political section under Karl Mayr retaining and "talent-spotting" Hitler after Lechfeld speeches); the existence of the marginal DAP as one of many radical circles; Hitler's own post-Pasewalk decision to enter politics and his demonstrated oratorical power. `contributed_to:` the DAP's need for effective speakers in a competitive radical-right milieu.

`preceded_by:` [[events/hitler-pasewalk-1918]], [[events/munich-revolution-1918-1919]], Lechfeld speaking success.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` Hitler's first formal political affiliation and platform. Within weeks he became the DAP's star agitator; the party (soon NSDAP) became his personal vehicle. The "nobody" from Vienna failure had found his vocation.

`enabled:` the entire subsequent career — propaganda mastery, SA formation, 1923 putsch, refoundation on Führerprinzip, 1933 seizure of power.

`followed_by:` rapid growth of the movement, alliance with Röhm and paramilitaries, [[beer-hall-putsch-1923]].

## Historiography

Kershaw (Range 01, Ch. 4) frames the entry as accidental in form but decisive in substance: the army's use of Hitler as anti-Bolshevik educator placed him in the right room at the right time. The DAP was insignificant; Hitler's oratory transformed it. The event marks the end of the "fantasy and failure" / "drop-out" / "elation and embitterment" phases and the beginning of "discovering a talent." Hitler's own later accounts emphasize destiny; Kershaw stresses structural opening + personal aptitude discovered late.
