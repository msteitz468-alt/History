# The Conversion of Khan Boris I (864)

## Narrative

Around 864 **Khan Boris** of [[first-bulgarian-empire|Bulgaria]] accepted Christian baptism,
taking the name **Michael** (after his godfather, the Byzantine emperor Michael III). The
conversion was as much a diplomatic as a spiritual act: Boris had been negotiating
simultaneously with **Louis the German** and the Franks (to whom he signalled an intention to
convert in 864) and with **Byzantium**, and in the event received baptism from Constantinople.
He then sought to maximise his church's independence by playing **Rome** against
**Constantinople**, addressing a famous set of questions to **Pope Nicholas I** (866).

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Bulgaria's exposed position between the Frankish and Byzantine spheres and Boris's
  search for political stability and legitimacy
- `contributed_to:` Byzantine military and diplomatic pressure (one tradition claims Boris converted
  under duress, which NCMH II reads as a Byzantine gloss on a decision "essentially of Boris's making")
- `enabled:` the rivalry of Rome and Constantinople, each eager to claim a new Christian people

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the integration of Bulgaria into Christendom and, ultimately, into the **Byzantine-Orthodox**
  ecclesiastical sphere
- `contributed_to:` the **Photian schism** between the eastern and western churches, of which the dispute
  over jurisdiction in Bulgaria was a principal flashpoint
- `enabled:` Bulgaria's later role as the refuge of the disciples of [[cyrillo-methodian-mission|Cyril and
  Methodius]] and the cradle of **Slavonic Christian literature**

## Historiography

The date (variously given as 863–866) and circumstances are contested (NCMH II, Shepard, Ch. 9). The key
sources — Byzantine chronicles, the Boris–Nicholas correspondence, and the Frankish annals — are partisan,
each assimilating the conversion to its own side's claims.
