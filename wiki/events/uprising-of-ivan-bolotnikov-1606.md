# Uprising of Ivan Bolotnikov

## Narrative
The **Uprising of Ivan Bolotnikov** (1606–1607) was the largest social rebellion of the Time of Troubles, threatening the boyar government of Tsar Basil Shuisky (L2276-L2284).

Following the assassination of the First False Dmitry in May 1606, Prince Gregory Shakhovskoy and other opponents of Basil Shuisky aroused southern Russian cities against the new boyar tsar (L2266, L2282). Ivan Bolotnikov, a former slave, emerged as the commander of the rebel forces (L2284). In October 1606, Bolotnikov's southern armies advanced to the gates of Moscow, where they were checked by government forces under Prince Michael Skopin-Shuisky (L2284). 

The rebel camp soon split along class lines: the gentry armies of Ryazan (led by Procopius Liapunov) and Tula (led by Philip Pashkov) broke with the radical social rebel Bolotnikov and went over to Shuisky's side (L2286). In 1607, a government army invested the remaining rebels in Tula (L2286). After a bitter four-month siege and the flooding of the city, Bolotnikov surrendered; he was subsequently exiled and killed, while the pretender "False Peter" was hanged (L2286).

## Causal Analysis
The uprising was caused by:
* **Agrarian Bondage:** Peasants, serfs, and slaves sought to overthrow the oppressive Muscovite social and economic system that had increasingly restricted their freedom in the late 16th century (L2220-2224, L2374-2376).
* **Boyar Rule Resentment:** The elevation of Basil Shuisky was viewed as the victory of a narrow boyar clique in Moscow, lacking popular legitimacy (L2280-2282).
* **Pretender Legitimization:** Bolotnikov claimed to act in the name of the "true" Tsar Dmitry, who had allegedly escaped assassination in Moscow, and later adopted "False Peter" to legitimize his movement (L2286-2288).

## Consequence Analysis
The rebellion produced:
* **Gentry Alignment:** The radical social demands of Bolotnikov's peasant followers frightened the service gentry, prompting them to abandon the rebellion and align with the autocracy to protect their land and serf holdings (L2286, L2366).
* **Continued Instability:** Although Bolotnikov was defeated, the social forces of unrest remained active, immediately transferring their allegiance to the Second False Dmitry (the "Felon of Tushino") when he appeared in late 1607 (L2288-L2292).
