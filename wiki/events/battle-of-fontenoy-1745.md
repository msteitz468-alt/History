# Battle of Fontenoy (11 May 1745)

## Narrative

The emblematic pitched battle of the [[war-of-the-austrian-succession-1740|War of
the Austrian Succession]] and of eighteenth-century battle culture generally.
Maurice de Saxe, covering the French siege of Tournai with Louis XV present in
person, defeated the Anglo-Dutch-Hanoverian army under the Duke of Cumberland in a
single day's fighting. The battle is fixed in memory by the (embellished) courtesy
exchange between the English and French Guards — "Messieurs les Anglais, tirez les
premiers!" — cited ever since as the epitome of ritualized, honor-bound
eighteenth-century combat.

Not to be confused with [[battle-of-fontenoy-841|Fontenoy-en-Puisaye (841)]],
the Carolingian succession battle.

## Causal analysis

- caused_by: the operational logic of siege-and-relief within
  [[war-of-the-austrian-succession-1740]]; Saxe deliberately offered battle on
  prepared ground, and Cumberland accepted — a textbook mutual
  [[verdict-of-battle|acceptance of the wager of battle]].

## Consequence analysis

- produced: the fall of Tournai and the French overrunning of the Austrian
  Netherlands (largely returned at Aix-la-Chapelle 1748 — the classic case of
  battlefield winnings traded as legal counters at the peace table under the
  [[jus-victoriae]]).

## Actors

Maurice de Saxe and Louis XV (France) vs. the Duke of Cumberland (Britain/allies).

## Historiography

Fontenoy's politesse anecdote is probably literary embroidery (Voltaire is a main
transmitter), but Whitman ([[whitman-verdict-of-battle-2012]]) argues the underlying
ceremonialism was real and load-bearing: formal invitations to fire, drawn-up
lines, and honors of war were part of the legal-ritual apparatus that made battles
recognizable verdicts. Skeptics read the same rituals as aristocratic theater with
little restraining force ([[jus-victoriae]] carries the debate).
