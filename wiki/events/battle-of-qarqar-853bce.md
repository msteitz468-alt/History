# Battle of Qarqar (853 BCE)

A pitched battle on the **Orontes** at **Qarqar**, where **[[neo-assyrian-empire|Shalmaneser
III]]** of Assyria, pressing south into Syria, met a broad **coalition of Levantine
states**. Recorded on the Assyrian **Kurkh Monolith** (written soon after the event),
it is one of the most important documents of the Early Iron Age Levant — less for its
military outcome than for the **synchronisms** it fixes. [[boardman-cah-v3p1-1982|CAH
Vol. III, Part 1]] (Ch. 6, Grayson) treats it within Shalmaneser's western campaigns.

## Narrative

On his sixth campaign (853 BCE) Shalmaneser crossed the Euphrates, took tribute from
the northern states, burned cities of Hamath, and at Qarqar faced an allied army led
by **Hadadezer (Adad-idri) of Damascus** and **Irhuleni of Hamath**. The Kurkh
Monolith lists a "coalition of twelve kings" with — by Assyrian count — nearly
**4,000 chariots, ~2,000 cavalry, over 40,000 infantry, and 1,000 camels**. Among the
contingents: **2,000 chariots and 10,000 troops of [[kingdom-of-israel|Ahab the
Israelite]]**, 1,000 camels of **Gindibu the Arab**, and forces from Byblos, Arvad,
Egypt, and the [[arameans|Aramaean]] states. Shalmaneser claims a great victory and a
march to the sea — but the absence of any follow-up conquest, and the need to fight
the **same coalition again in 849, 848, and 845**, show the battle was in truth
**indecisive**.

## Causal Analysis

- **caused_by:** [[neo-assyrian-empire|Assyrian expansion]] beyond the Euphrates under
  Shalmaneser III, which threatened the independence of the Syro-Levantine states.
- **contributed_to:** the unusual cohesion of the **Damascus coalition** — normally
  rival states (Damascus, Hamath, Israel) banding together against a common existential
  threat.

## Consequence Analysis

- **produced:** a **decade of inconclusive warfare**; the coalition held until it
  dissolved on its own when **[[hazael|Hazael]]** usurped Damascus (the pact being a
  personal bond between rulers). Assyria's decisive penetration of southern Syria came
  only later.
- **enabled:** the wiki's **chronological backbone** for the period. Qarqar gives the
  **first contemporary, externally-dated mention of a king of [[kingdom-of-israel|Israel]]**
  (Ahab) — anchoring biblical chronology to the Assyrian calendar — and the **earliest
  datable reference to the Arabs** (Gindibu and his camels). Twelve years later the
  **Black Obelisk** shows **Jehu of Israel** paying Shalmaneser tribute (841).

## Historiography

Known almost entirely from **one biased Assyrian source**, so the claimed victory is
discounted by historians; the battle's true value is as a **fixed synchronism**
linking Assyrian, Israelite, and Arab history. It is a textbook case of why Assyrian
royal annals must be read critically (Grayson: "notoriously biased and occasionally
untruthful") — and of how even propaganda can yield priceless chronological data.
