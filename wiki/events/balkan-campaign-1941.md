# The Balkan Campaign (October 1940 – June 1941)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]].

## Narrative

**Italy's Greek fiasco.** Mussolini invaded Greece from Albania on 28 October 1940 without telling Hitler; ill-equipped Italian troops were thrown back into Albania by the Greek counter-offensive (~39,000 Italian dead), while Taranto (November 1940) and Cape Matapan (March 1941, via Bletchley decrypts) crippled the Italian fleet and the British took 130,000 Italian POWs in Egypt and destroyed Italian East Africa. The Axis balance shifted decisively: after January 1941 Hitler held Mussolini "in some contempt."

**Romania.** Germany locked up Romanian oil (monopoly from 27 May 1940); territorial amputations forced King Carol's abdication for General Ion Antonescu, who crushed his Iron Guard partners' January 1941 uprising — a pogrom in which some 200 Bucharest Jews were forced through an animal slaughter-line and hung from meat-hooks labeled "fit for human consumption" — and became Hitler's favored military dictator (his regime's later mass murder of Jews is treated on [[einsatzgruppen]]).

**Yugoslavia and Greece.** When Serb officers overthrew the pact Prince Regent Paul had signed under Berghof pressure (coup of 27 March 1941), Hitler ordered Yugoslavia "smashed . . . in a lightning operation" and "with merciless harshness": invaded 6 April, Belgrade taken 12 April, capitulation 17 April — 344,000 POWs for 151 German dead. Greece fell by 27 April; ~50,000 British troops were evacuated. The airborne assault on **Crete** (20 May 1941) succeeded despite Ultra forewarning, but 3,352 of 17,500 paratroopers died — no large German airborne operation was ever attempted again.

**Occupation.** Yugoslavia was partitioned (German Slovenia, Italian coast, Hungarian and Bulgarian annexations, Pavelić's "Independent State of Croatia" — see [[ustasha-genocide-1941]]); Serbia's reprisal regime under General Böhme crossed into the systematic murder of Jews and Roma (see [[einsatzgruppen]]). In Greece, looting and requisitioning produced famine — the Red Cross estimated a quarter-million Greeks dead of hunger and disease 1941–43 — and Bulgaria's occupation of Macedonia/Thrace expelled 100,000+ Greeks and killed 45,000–60,000 suppressing the 1941 rising.

## Causal Analysis

The campaign was `caused_by` Mussolini's Greek adventure and the Belgrade coup, and `contributed_to` the delay of [[operation-barbarossa-1941|Barbarossa]] from mid-May to 22 June 1941 — a link long debated (the spring 1941 rasputitsa would have constrained an earlier start regardless), which Evans registers as contributing rather than decisive. It `produced` the partisan wars of the Balkans (Chetniks under Mihailović, Tito's Communists — the first general uprising in occupied Europe, June–July 1941).

## Historiography

The old claim that the Balkan detour fatally delayed Barbarossa is treated by Evans as contributory at most (the wet spring constrained an early start regardless); the campaign's deepest consequences were occupational — the Ustashe genocide, Greek famine, and the first partisan war — rather than operational.
