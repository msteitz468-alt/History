## Narrative

Sellasia (222 BCE), in the hills of northern Laconia, was the decisive battle of the
**[[cleomenean-war-229|Cleomenean War]]** — the engagement in which
**[[antigonus-iii-doson|Antigonus III Doson]]** of Macedon, leading the forces of the
**Hellenic Symmachy** (including the [[achaean-league|Achaeans]]), destroyed the power of
**[[cleomenes-iii|Cleomenes III]]** of [[sparta|Sparta]]. Doson's army numbered nearly
29,000 (13,300 Macedonians, with Galatian, Agrianian, Cretan, and allied troops);
Cleomenes could muster only about 20,000 — Spartiates, *perioikoi*, liberated helots, and
mercenaries — having, at the last, been **abandoned by [[ptolemaic-egypt|Ptolemy III]]**,
who suspended his subsidies just ten days before the battle.

A mainly infantry encounter: a Macedonian ambush dislodged the Lacedaemonian left under
Cleomenes' brother and co-king Eucleidas, but the decision came on the right, where the
"superior weight of Macedonian numbers" eventually broke the Spartan phalanx. Cleomenes'
losses were heavy (Eucleidas fell); he escaped with a handful of men and fled to Egypt.

## Consequence Analysis

Sellasia was as sudden and total a collapse as the Achaean disintegration had been the
year before. **Antigonus took Sparta** — the first time in its history a foreign army had
done so — cancelled Cleomenes' revolution, restored the ephorate, and left the kingship
vacant; the social and economic measures were only partly reversed. He appointed a Boeotian
military governor (*epistates*), and Sparta was (eventually) brought into the **Hellenic
Symmachy**. The battle **ended Sparta's last bid for independent greatness** and sealed the
**restoration of Macedonian hegemony** over the Peloponnese — the order Philip V inherited
and tested in the [[social-war-220|Social War]].

## Historiography

Polybius (using a Megalopolitan source, perhaps Philopoemen, who fought there) and Plutarch
(via [[hellenistic-historiography|Phylarchus]]) describe the battle; its site and tactics
have been much studied. It marks the military superiority of the Macedonian phalanx even
over Cleomenes' reformed Spartan one. See [[cleomenean-war-229]], [[antigonus-iii-doson]],
[[cleomenes-iii]].
