# Battle of Antietam (17 September 1862)

**War hub:** [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/american-civil-war/american-civil-war|American Civil War War Hub]] · **Tactical analysis:** [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/american-civil-war/battle-of-antietam-1862|Battle of Antietam — Tactical Analysis]]

## Narrative

The Battle of Antietam was fought on 17 September 1862 near Sharpsburg, Maryland, along Antietam Creek. It was the bloodiest single day in American history. Union forces under Maj. Gen. George B. McClellan confronted Confederate forces under Gen. Robert E. Lee who had invaded Maryland. 

Grounded in source: Pre-battle situation captured in maps "Situation at 1800 Hours, 16 September 1862". Key terrain features documented in the source's gatefold: "Bloody Lane" and "Burnside Bridge".

## Causal Analysis

Lee's Maryland Campaign (part of broader Eastern operations 1862) aimed to relieve pressure on Virginia, possibly influence border states and European recognition. McClellan, with numerical superiority, pursued after the discovery of Lee's invasion plans (Special Orders 191).

## Consequence Analysis

- Heavy casualties forced Lee to withdraw south of the Potomac.
- Provided the political opportunity for the Emancipation Proclamation.
- Strategic stalemate but operational success for Union in halting the invasion.
- Impact on morale and foreign policy.
