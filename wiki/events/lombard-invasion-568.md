# The Lombard Invasion of Italy (568)

The **Lombard Invasion of Italy** began in 568 CE when the Lombards, a Germanic people led by King **[[alboin|Alboin]]**, migrated from Pannonia into Northern Italy. The invasion permanently shattered the brief Byzantine restoration achieved by [[justinian|Justinian's]] Gothic War, dividing Italy into a patchwork of Lombard and imperial (Byzantine) territories and creating the geopolitical conditions that enabled the Roman papacy to emerge as an independent political force.

---

## Narrative

### Background and Steppe Pressures
During the mid-sixth century, the Lombards were settled in Pannonia (modern Hungary) as allies (*foederati*) of the Byzantine Empire. In 567, the Lombards, allied with the **Avars**, destroyed their long-standing rivals, the Gepids. However, Alboin grew wary of the rising power of the Avars and decided to lead his people into Italy, which had been left undefended and depopulated after the **[[gothic-war-535-554|Gothic War]]** and the **[[justinianic-plague-541|Plague of 541]]**.

### The Migration (568)
In the spring of 568, Alboin led a heterogeneous host consisting of Lombards, Saxons, Gepids, and Danube Suebi across the Julian Alps into Venetia. The Byzantine military, under-resourced due to Justin II's fiscal cuts and occupied with Sasanian Persia, could not offer coordinated resistance. 

The Lombards occupied Friuli, Aquileia, Vicenza, and Verona. Pavia resisted but fell in 572 after a three-year siege, becoming the capital of the Lombard Kingdom. Alboin pushed south, but was assassinated in 572 in Verona in a conspiracy led by his wife, Rosamund.

### Fragmentation and the Interregnum (574–584)
Alboin's successor, Cleph, was murdered after a brief reign (572–574), prompting the **Rule of the Dukes** (574–584)—a ten-year interregnum in which the Lombards lacked a king. Power fragmented among thirty-five autonomous dukes. During this period, Lombard bands pushed south to establish the independent **Duchy of Spoleto** and **Duchy of Benevento**. The threat of Byzantine-Frankish alliances eventually forced the dukes to restore the monarchy in 584, electing **Authari** (and later **Agilulf**) as king.

```

     ┌─────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
     │      Lombard Zone       │     Byzantine Zone      │
     ├─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
     │ • Po Valley (Langobardia│ • Ravenna (Exarchate)   │
     │   Maior)                │ • Rome (Pentapolis)     │
     │ • Duchy of Spoleto      │ • Naples & Liguria      │
     │ • Duchy of Benevento    │ • Calabria & Sicily     │
     └─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

The invasion split Italy into two distinct, interlocking administrative spheres:
*   **The Lombard Kingdom (Langobardia):** Concentrated in the Po Valley, Tuscany, and the southern duchies of Spoleto and Benevento.
*   **The Byzantine Enclaves:** Controlled by the **Exarchate of Ravenna**, including Ravenna, Venice, Liguria (until 643), Naples, Calabria, and Sicily, connected by a narrow corridor of land between Rome and Ravenna.

## Historiography

*   **Break from Roman Institutions:** Unlike the Ostrogoths, who preserved Roman civil administration, the Lombards expropriated Roman estates, abolished Roman municipal offices, and established a militarized landholding aristocracy. The Roman senatorial aristocracy collapsed.
*   **Rise of the Papacy:** The physical separation of Rome from the Exarch of Ravenna forced the Roman bishop to assume civil and military defense of the city. **Gregory the Great (590–604)** negotiated independent treaties with the Lombards, managed papal estates (*patrimonium Petri*) to feed the city, and initiated missions to convert the Lombards from Arianism to Catholicism, laying the foundation for the temporal power of the medieval papacy.
*   **Gibbon** (below) stresses Byzantine court failure (Narses’ recall) over pure Lombard military superiority as the hinge of 568.

## Gibbon, *Decline and Fall* (1776–1788) — Ch. XLV

[[sources/gibbon-decline-and-fall-1776|Gibbon]]’s causation for 568 stresses Byzantine court failure over pure Lombard military superiority.

- **Narses’ recall:** “They might have failed, if Narses had been the antagonist of the Lombards”; Sophia’s distaff insult and court weakness ruined Italy; tradition that Narses invited Alboin.
- **Unopposed inland takeover:** “Alboin neither expected nor encountered a Roman army in the field”; terror preceded him; inland from Trentine hills to Ravenna/Rome became Lombard patrimony without battle or siege.
- **Dual Italy for two centuries:** “During a period of two hundred years, Italy was unequally divided between the kingdom of the Lombards and the exarchate of Ravenna,” whose exarchs held residual civil, military, even ecclesiastical power.
