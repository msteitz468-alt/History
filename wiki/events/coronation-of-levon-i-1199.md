# The Coronation of King Levon I (6 January 1199)

On **6 January 1199**, in the Church of **Sourp Sophia** (Holy Wisdom) at **Tarsus**, the
Roupenid baron **Levon II** was consecrated **[[levon-i-of-cilician-armenia|King Levon I]]**,
transforming the Armenian baronies of [[cilicia|Cilicia]] into the crowned
**[[cilician-armenian-kingdom|Cilician Armenian Kingdom]]** — the only Armenian monarchy ever
to sit on the Mediterranean.

## Narrative

The crown was the fruit of a decade of diplomacy. **Frederick I Barbarossa** had promised
Levon a royal crown in return for support of the [[crusades|Third Crusade]]; Frederick's
death (1190) passed the obligation to his son **Emperor Henry VI**, while Popes **Clement
III** and **Celestine III** negotiated the religious terms. Celestine sent **Conrad of
Wittelsbach, archbishop of Mainz**, bearing a crown. The price was ecclesiastical: three
liturgical conformities to Roman usage and a gesture of **union with the Roman Church**,
sealed in the **Act of Union of 1198**, to which Levon and twelve bishops swore. The
ceremony was performed **jointly** by the Armenian patriarch **Grigor VI Pahlavuni** and the
archbishop of Mainz — a deliberate symbol of the kingdom's new orientation. In 1199 **Pope
Innocent III** sent Levon the **banner of St Peter**.

## Causal Analysis

- `contributed_to: [[armenian-latin-integration]]` — the coronation is the institutional
  capstone of a century of Armenian–Latin interpenetration.
- `caused_by:` Levon's strategic need to escape vassalage to Antioch and the lingering claims
  of [[byzantine-empire|Byzantium]]; a Western crown gave him a patron and legitimacy neither
  Greek nor Muslim neighbours could deny.
- The **Act of Union (1198)** was the enabling precondition Rome demanded — making the crown
  and the union question inseparable from the outset.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced: [[cilician-armenian-kingdom]]` as a recognized monarchy.
- It **severed the Byzantine connection** and reoriented Cilician Armenia toward the Latin
  West and (after 1204) away from Constantinople.
- It planted the **[[armenian-church-union-question|union controversy]]**: the promised
  subordination to Rome never materialized in substance, Levon was excommunicated in 1211,
  and the unresolved question divided Armenian society for two centuries.

## Actors

- [[levon-i-of-cilician-armenia|Levon I]] — the crowned king
- Grigor VI Pahlavuni — Armenian patriarch, co-consecrator
- Conrad of Wittelsbach, archbishop of Mainz — papal/imperial envoy and co-consecrator

## Historiography

The event is central to [[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian (2000)]], who
reads the union commitment as **political rather than doctrinal** — a recurring Armenian
tactic for enlisting Western help. That is a sympathetic, thesis-driven reading; the sincerity
and significance of the union pledge is itself the matter of
[[armenian-church-union-question|the union controversy]].
