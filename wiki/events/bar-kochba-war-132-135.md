# The Bar Kochba War (132–135 CE)

The **second and last great Jewish revolt against Rome** in Judaea — a united, carefully prepared
uprising under **Simeon Bar Kochba** that [[hadrian|Hadrian]] crushed only by a grinding war of
attrition, ending in the catastrophic depopulation of the province and the definitive break of
the Jews from Jerusalem. Treated in [[bowman-cah-v11-2000|CAH XI]] Chs. 3 and 22.

## Causes

CAH XI attributes the revolt to **Hadrian's philhellenism** colliding with Jewish religion:
his decision (during his eastern tour, c. 130) to **refound Jerusalem as the Roman colony *Aelia
Capitolina*** with a temple to **Jupiter Capitolinus**, and his **ban on circumcision** ("repugnant
to the Greeks") — an attempt, the volume suggests, at the *hellenization of Jewry* that recalled
Antiochus Epiphanes. Against the unhealed trauma of the **destruction of the Temple (70)** and
the recent [[trajans-parthian-war-114-117|diaspora revolt]], these provoked a determination "to
expel their Gentile rulers once and for all."

## Narrative

Unlike the Great Revolt of 66, the Jews were now **united under a single leader**, **Bar Kochba**
("son of a star"), backed by the foremost religious authority, **Rabbi Akiba**. The rising
(beginning **132**) was so well-prepared that the legate of Judaea, with two legions, "could not
cope"; many Roman soldiers were killed, and Bar Kochba controlled much of Judaea for over two
years, administering it as **"prince over Israel"** (his coins reasserting Jewish sovereignty).
Hadrian summoned his **best general, Sex. Julius Severus**, from Britain — the empire "prepared to
send the right man from one end to the other" — who waged a deliberate war of **attrition**:
"intercepting small groups... slowly but surely crushing, exhausting and exterminating them."

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **catastrophic depopulation of Judaea** — Dio's figures (985 villages
  destroyed, 580,000 killed in action, "countless more by famine, disease, and fire") convey a
  demographic and cultural catastrophe.
- `produced:` the **renaming of the province** from *Judaea* to **Syria Palaestina** — "as if to
  symbolize that the loss of life had finally deprived it of its predominantly Jewish population."
- `produced:` **Aelia Capitolina** on the site of Jerusalem, from which Jews were henceforth
  **barred**.
- `enabled:` the decisive turn of Judaism toward the **rabbinic, diaspora-centred** form — the
  centre of gravity shifting from a land-based, Temple-oriented religion to the study of Torah in
  the diaspora and the surviving communities of the Galilee.

## Significance

The Bar Kochba War was the **end of Jewish political nationhood in antiquity** and a turning-point
in the history of Judaism. Hadrian, who took only one imperial salutation from the war and
celebrated little, recognized its grimness; his successors maintained the exclusion of Jews from
Jerusalem. The revolt also marks, with the destruction of the Temple, the deepening **separation
of Christianity from Judaism** (Bar Kochba's messianic claim being unacceptable to the Christians).
