# The Fall of Cilician Armenia (1375)

The **[[mamluk-sultanate|Mamluk]]** capture of the royal capital **Sis** in **1375**
extinguished the **[[cilician-armenian-kingdom|Cilician Armenian Kingdom]]** — the last
medieval Armenian state and the only Armenian monarchy ever to reach the Mediterranean.

## Narrative

The kingdom had been dying for half a century: Mamluk incursions from the 1320s, the
annexation of Adana and Tarsus by mid-century, crushing tribute (half the customs of
[[cilicia|Ayas]] and half the salt revenue paid to Cairo from 1322), and a corrosive
internal split between **pro-Roman "unionists"** and **traditionalists** that produced a
succession of royal murders. After [[levon-i-of-cilician-armenia|the Roupenid-Hetumid
line]] failed and Levon IV was assassinated (1341), the throne passed by marriage to the
Cypriot **[[lusignan-dynasty|Lusignan]]** house, whose pro-Latin kings (Constantine II–IV)
deepened the alienation.

The last king, **Levon V** — of mixed Lusignan and Armenian descent — was crowned at Sis
on **14 September 1374**, just as the Mamluk sultan **al-Ashraf Sha'ban** launched the final
assault. Sis was taken amid atrocities; Levon fled to **Gaban**, which surrendered after a
nine-month siege. On **13 April 1375** Levon, his family, and entourage were taken prisoner
to **Cairo**. Ransomed in 1382 (through Franciscan and Castilian efforts), Levon spent his
last years lobbying the courts of Europe for a restoration crusade, mediated in the
[[hundred-years-war|Hundred Years' War]], and **died in exile in Paris on 29 November 1393**,
buried at **Saint-Denis**. On his death the Lusignans of Cyprus assumed the empty title
"King of Cyprus, Jerusalem, and Armenia."

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` sustained **Mamluk military pressure** — the long reprisal for the kingdom's
  [[mongol-latin-crusade-alliance|Mongol alliance]] under [[hetum-i-of-cilician-armenia|Hetum I]].
- `contributed_to by:` the **internal religious division** ([[armenian-church-union-question|the
  union question]]) that, in [[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian's]] reading,
  hollowed out Armenian social cohesion; and the **dynastic instability** of the Lusignan
  succession.
- `contributed_to by:` the **failure of Western aid**, which the popes always conditioned on
  Armenian subordination to Rome.

## Consequence Analysis

- The **end of Armenian statehood** until the modern era.
- The **Armenian exodus** from Cilicia, accelerated by Timur's campaigns (Sis sacked 1401);
  many fled to Cyprus and Constantinople.
- The **catholicosate** eventually returned from Sis to **Etchmiadzin (1441)** in Greater
  Armenia, where, on Ghazarian's account, the Church preserved the national heritage the
  fallen monarchy could no longer protect.

## Historiography

[[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian (2000)]] frames the fall as the product
of **both** a secular cause (the rising tide of Turkic Islamic power — Seljuks, Mamluks,
Ottomans) **and** a deeper ecclesiastical cause (the union controversy). The second claim is
his thesis and is openly partisan; see [[armenian-church-union-question|the union
controversy]].
