# First Sacred War (tradition)

Late Classical tradition of an amphictyonic war that wrested Delphi from local Cirrhaean control in the early sixth century BCE. [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] Ch.12 treats it as a closing method case parallel to the [[events/lelantine-war-c700bce|Lelantine War]].

## Narrative

According to the developed tradition: Cirrhaeans (and/or Kragalidai) harassed Delphic pilgrims; the Pylaean Amphictyony — Thessalians under Eurylokhos, Athenians under Solon or Alcmaeon, and Sicyonians under [[actors/cleisthenes-of-sicyon|Cleisthenes of Sicyon]] — fought a war lasting ten years between 594 and 585 (Aristotle and his nephew Callisthenes, fr. 1). Solon is said to have diverted or poisoned the river Pleistos; Cirrha was destroyed and its land dedicated to Apollo and associated deities; the amphictyony reorganized the Pythian Games in 582.

This narrative is **not** attested in contemporary Archaic sources and fails Hall’s reliability tests (see [[controversies/first-sacred-war-historicity]]). The page records the tradition as tradition, not as established event history.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` **not asserted** in wiki voice for the full traditional war.
- Traditional aetiology: local harassment of pilgrims and control of the approaches to Delphi.
- `concurrent_with:` early sixth-century formalization of panhellenic festivals (Pythian, Isthmian, Nemean) discussed under [[concepts/panhellenism]].

## Consequence Analysis

- Tradition links the war to amphictyonic control of Delphi and reorganization of the Pythian Games.
- Secure archaeology/literature for amphictyonic administration and games formalization does **not** automatically validate the full war narrative.
- Fourth-century oratory (especially Aeschines) used the war as sacred-law precedent during later Sacred Wars.

## Actors

Amphictyonic coalition (tradition); Cirrhaeans as local antagonists (tradition); named leaders Solon/Alcmaeon, Cleisthenes of Sicyon, Eurylokhos — all source-problematic for 594–585.

## Historiography

Virtually no literary evidence predates the fourth century; fullest clusters fall in the 340s–330s during the Third and Fourth Sacred Wars. Herodotus is silent on Solon/Alcmaeon/Cleisthenes of Sicyon as participants. Hall prefers agnosticism over total denial: a minute “kernel” of multi-year amphictyonic hostilities against locals may underlie later bans on cultivating sacred land and amphictyonic (not purely local) administration of Delphi before 480 — but that kernel is “so minute as to be practically insignificant” relative to the full traditional war.
