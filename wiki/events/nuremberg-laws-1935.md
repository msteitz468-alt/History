# Nuremberg Laws (1935)

The Reich Citizenship Law and the Law for the Protection of German Blood and
German Honour, announced at the Nuremberg party rally on 15 September 1935:
Jews stripped of Reich citizenship; marriage and extramarital relations between
Jews and "citizens of German blood" criminalized. The laws translated Nazi
racial ideology into the legal architecture of persecution and forced the
regime to define "the Jew" bureaucratically (the November 1935 supplementary
decrees creating full-Jew/Mischling categories).

## Narrative (Evans, 2005)

The timing came from below and from the regime's troubles: by early 1935
Gestapo and Sopade agents alike reported sharp popular discontent (prices,
unemployment, party corruption), and the SA — purged and purposeless — needed
an outlet. A party-driven antisemitic wave ran through spring–summer 1935:
violent boycotts, "Jews not wanted here" signage, municipal bans, and
Streicher's *Stormer* made omnipresent by a Labour Front contract placing it
in every workplace (Streicher's Hamburg rally of 30 August drew 20,000 for
obscene race-defilement propaganda). The leadership objected to the disorder,
not the aim; Göring, Bormann, and Hess ordered wild actions stopped in
July–August while general regulations were prepared. At Nuremberg the trigger
for the flag law was New York dockers tearing the swastika from a German ship;
Hitler summoned the Reichstag to the rally and had the citizenship and blood
laws drafted overnight with Interior Ministry officials, presenting them on
15 September as a "once-and-for-all, secular solution," with Göring supplying
the raving justification speech.

**Definition problem.** Frick's decree made 3+ Jewish grandparents a Jew; the
14 November supplementary decree created *Mischling* first and second degree
and *Geltungsjuden* — with the grandparents' *religion* the operative
criterion, which, Evans notes, "made a nonsense of scientific claims" about
blood; every mixed-race solution was "entirely arbitrary," capped by Hitler's
personal exemption power. The Ancestry Proof (*Ahnennachweis*) made
genealogists the most sought-after experts in Germany. The Citizenship Law
also reduced *political* nonconformists of any race to mere "subjects of the
state."

**Enforcement.** "Racial defilement" prosecutions (~420 convictions/year
1936–39, two-thirds Jewish men) ran on denunciation and stretched to cover
embraces and kisses; from March 1938 Jews completing such sentences were
rearrested by the [[gestapo|Gestapo]] into camps (the Buchenwald murder of
20-year-old Julius Meier, who refused castration consent, is Evans's
exemplary case).

**Reception.** Party activists exulted; Gestapo *and* Sopade reported broad
popular criticism (four-fifths of the Palatinate disapproving, near-unanimous
working-class rejection) — yet intimidation worked, custom at Jewish shops
collapsed, and the majority settled into "indifference and passivity" while
propaganda seeped: "The feeling that the Jews are another race is general
nowadays" (Sopade, Berlin, January 1936). Many Jews felt partial relief,
reading the laws as promised stability after the violence.

**US race-law models as contributing factor (per Whitman 2017):** 

- contributed_to: [[events/nuremberg-laws-1935]] via the Reich Citizenship Law — Nazi study of American second-class citizenship techniques (literacy tests, poll taxes, grandfather clauses, "non-citizen national" status in territories, immigration national-origins quotas) supplied conceptual precedent and confirmation for the distinction between full Reich citizens (German blood) and mere nationals/subjects. Prussian Memorandum (1933) and June 5, 1934 meeting transcript record explicit discussion. Radicals saw US as having taken "tentative first steps" toward a healthy völkisch order.

- contributed_to: [[events/nuremberg-laws-1935]] via the Blood Law — American anti-miscegenation statutes (criminalization of interracial marriage/sex in ~30 states, with felony penalties, void marriages, and classification rules for mixed offspring) provided the leading modern Western statutory model for criminalizing "Rassenschande." Discussed in detail at the June 5, 1934 meeting (Freisler: "This jurisprudence would suit us perfectly..."); Prussian Memorandum cited Jim Crow separation as precedent (while calling its own proposal "limited"). No verbatim copy, but the criminal-law treatment of the sexual/reproductive boundary was the key borrowed technique.

- preceded_by: Prussian Memorandum (Sept 1933, radical drafting team) — explicit Jim Crow references and proposals for "Race Treason" and "Race Endangerment" crimes that fed directly into 1934 meeting agenda.

- The June 5, 1934 meeting (Gürtner chair, Freisler radical champion, Lösener draftsman, prepared Ministry memo on US law) was the pivotal documented policy-making moment where American models were weighed as operational material, not propaganda. Moderates (Gürtner, Lösener) resisted full radicalism on juristic "scientific" grounds; radicals prevailed on the need for unambiguous statutory criminalization.

## Narrative (US Race-Law Models and the June 5, 1934 Meeting; Whitman 2017)

**Background and sources of Nazi knowledge.** In the early 1930s Nazi lawyers and publicists (Hitler in *Mein Kampf*, Heinrich Krieger after study at University of Arkansas Law School, Otto Koellreutter, Herbert Kier in the *National Socialist Handbook*, Johann von Leers, Detlef Sahm, and others) repeatedly cited the United States as the leading example of racist legislation. They focused on three domains: (1) race-based immigration and naturalization (1790 "free white persons," Chinese Exclusion, 1921/1924 national-origins quotas); (2) second-class citizenship for Blacks (disfranchisement devices) and territorial "non-citizen nationals"; (3) anti-miscegenation statutes in ~30 states criminalizing interracial marriage and sex, with felony penalties and classification rules (fractions, outward appearance, social association). Krieger's 1934 article and 1936 book were especially influential vectors; a Justice Ministry "synoptic presentation" of North American race legislation was prepared for internal use.

**The June 5, 1934 meeting.** Chaired by Franz Gürtner, attended by Roland Freisler (radical champion), Bernhard Lösener (later principal draftsman), Hans von Dohnanyi, Fritz Grau, Karl Klee, and others. The meeting opened with Gürtner's presentation of the US memo. Participants returned repeatedly to American models. Radicals (Freisler, Grau) argued US practice proved criminalization of mixed relations and "primitive" (non-scientific) racial categories were workable and necessary; Freisler explicitly: "This jurisprudence would suit us perfectly, with a single exception... the Jews... are not reckoned among the coloreds." Moderates countered that US law was aimed at "coloreds," classified Jews as Caucasian, relied on covert "Umwege" (devious pathways) around the 14th Amendment, and was sometimes excessively harsh (one-drop rule). The meeting records genuine policy debate, not mere propaganda.

**Prussian Memorandum (1933) context.** Precursor radical program (Freisler-influenced) proposed "Race Treason" and related crimes, citing US Jim Crow separation while noting its own version would be "limited." Street-level "individual actions" and calls for "unambiguous laws" created pressure for statutory codification.

**Limits acknowledged by Nazis.** Some American practices (broad one-drop, classification of Jews as white) were viewed as too harsh or inapposite; the US constitutional tradition and liberal elements were despised. Influence was selective and inspirational rather than verbatim transplant.

## Historiography

The improvised drafting at Nuremberg (officials summoned overnight) is a
classic exhibit in the intentionalist/structuralist debate: ideological goal
fixed, path improvised.

Whitman (2017) supplies the missing comparative-legal chapter: primary evidence from the June 5, 1934 transcript and Prussian Memorandum shows sustained Nazi engagement with US models (immigration, second-class citizenship, anti-miscegenation) in genuine policy-making contexts. This challenges the prior consensus (Rethmeier 1995, Bernstein, Hanke, Guettel) that references were "insignificant," "fleeting," or "plain wrong" (because Jews were Caucasian). Whitman argues the evidence supports "direct American influence" on the *technique* of criminalizing mixed marriage and on the conceptual framing of second-class citizenship, while carefully noting no wholesale copy, Nazi ambivalence, and that responsibility for the crimes remains with the Nazis. The debate is recorded at [[controversies/american-influence-on-nazi-race-law]]. Evans and others provide the street-pressure and improvisational context; Whitman the foreign legal models. 

**Note on atrocity-dense material:** Verbatim quotes from Nazi planning (Grau on "quarantine," "severe criminal punishment"; Freisler on "primitive" methods) are reproduced only as load-bearing evidence with full attribution; the record is not softened.

## Shirer (1960) Addition

Shirer (1960), an [EYEWITNESS] living in Germany through this period, adds concrete social effects and a first-person consequence: by summer 1936 (Olympics year) roughly half of German Jews were without livelihood, from the combined legal and terror exclusion from public office (1933), the civil service, journalism, radio, farming, teaching, theater, and film (1933), stock exchanges (1934), and eventually law/medicine/business outright by 1938. He records shop signs reading "Jews Not Admitted" and a roadside sign near Ludwigshafen: "Drive Carefully! Sharp Curve! Jews 75 Miles an Hour!" Shirer also notes the Nazis temporarily hid antisemitic signage during the August 1936 Berlin Olympics to impress foreign visitors (corroborating the Evans account on [[berlin-olympics-1936]]) — and states he was himself "violently attacked in the German press and on the radio, and threatened with expulsion" for reporting the removal in a dispatch, an episode worth noting on the Olympics page as well.
