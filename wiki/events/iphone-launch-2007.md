# iPhone Launch (2007)

On 9 January 2007 at Macworld San Francisco, Steve Jobs unveiled the iPhone, describing it as “a revolutionary mobile phone… a widescreen iPod with touch controls… and a breakthrough internet communications device.” Three products in one. Released 29 June 2007.

**Narrative**: The project (codenamed internally) combined years of touch-interface work, the iPod click-wheel lessons, and a bet-the-company commitment to a full-screen glass device with virtual keyboard. Jobs insisted on software quality and carrier cooperation (exclusive with AT&T). The device sold out rapidly and reset expectations for mobile computing.

**Causal Analysis**: Enabled by prior iPod ecosystem success, multi-touch patents and engineering, and Jobs’s willingness to obsolete Apple’s own iPod in the phone space.

**Consequence Analysis**: The smartphone became the dominant personal computer. Created the app store economy. Shifted power from carriers and traditional phone makers (Nokia, Motorola, BlackBerry) to platform companies. Accelerated the post-PC era.
