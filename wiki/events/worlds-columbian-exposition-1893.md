# World's Columbian Exposition (1893)

The **World's Columbian Exposition** (Chicago, 1893) — the great world's fair marking the 400th anniversary of Columbus's voyage — was lit by [[actors/westinghouse-george|George Westinghouse]]'s **[[concepts/alternating-current|polyphase alternating-current]]** system using [[actors/tesla-nikola|Tesla]]'s patents, after Westinghouse underbid the Edison/General Electric DC interests for the lighting contract.

## Narrative

Nearly 100,000 incandescent lamps and Tesla's spectacular high-frequency demonstrations in the Electricity Building made the fair a triumphant public showcase of AC power. It was the largest, most visible demonstration to date that AC could safely and economically light a vast complex — a decisive propaganda victory in the [[processes/war-of-the-currents|War of the Currents]].

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` [[processes/war-of-the-currents|War of the Currents]] — the lighting contract was a front in the AC–DC commercial struggle.
- `contributed_to:` Westinghouse's competitive underbid and Tesla's patents.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` public confidence in AC and momentum that helped secure the [[events/niagara-falls-power-project-1895|Niagara Falls]] contract for AC.
- `enabled:` the broader [[processes/electrification|electrification]] of America on AC.

## Historiography

The Exposition's role in the [[processes/war-of-the-currents|War of the Currents]] is well documented through the surviving Westinghouse and General Electric corporate records, the contract bids, and the contemporary technical press. Seifer (*Wizard*, 1996) treats the lighting contract primarily as a business-and-publicity victory for AC rather than a purely technical one, emphasizing Westinghouse's willingness to underbid at a loss to secure the showcase. Older Tesla-centric accounts (O'Neill, 1944) foreground Tesla's personal demonstrations; business and technology historians stress the systemic transformer-economics advantage of AC. The fair's broader cultural meaning (the "White City," American technological self-fashioning) is a large separate literature.
