# Starship Integrated Flight Test 1 (IFT-1)

## Narrative

On 20 April 2023, [[spacex|SpaceX]] conducted the first integrated orbital-class test flight of its Starship launch vehicle from Starbase, Texas, combining the Super Heavy booster and Starship upper stage in a single stack for the first time. Several of the booster's 33 Raptor engines failed during ascent, the vehicle failed to separate its stages as planned, and it began to tumble before ground controllers triggered its flight termination system roughly four minutes into flight. The launch also caused significant damage to the launch pad itself, throwing concrete and debris over a wide area.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** SpaceX's iterative "test to failure" development philosophy, previously used for the Falcon 9 and Starship prototype hop tests, applied here to a fully stacked orbital-class vehicle for the first time.

## Consequence Analysis

**produced:** design changes to the launch pad (including a water-cooled steel flame deflector) and vehicle before subsequent test flights; increased regulatory review by the FAA of SpaceX's licensing and environmental compliance at Starbase.

## Historiography

Coverage of the flight split along a familiar divide in commentary on SpaceX's development approach: company and supportive commentators characterized it as a successful data-gathering test consistent with SpaceX's stated expectations, while critics pointed to the pad damage and unplanned early vehicle loss as evidence of inadequate pre-launch preparation.
