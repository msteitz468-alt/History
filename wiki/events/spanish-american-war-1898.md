# Spanish-American War (1898)

Brief "splendid little war" (John Hay) that transformed the United States into a colonial power with overseas possessions.

## Narrative

War declared April 1898 after the sinking of the USS Maine in Havana harbor (cause disputed then and now). U.S. naval victories at Manila Bay (Dewey) and Santiago. Land campaign in Cuba featured the Rough Riders. Fighting ended quickly; armistice August; treaty December.

## Causal Analysis

Immediate trigger: humanitarian concern for Cuba + explosion of Maine + sensational journalism. Deeper: strategic desire to remove Spain from the Caribbean; economic interests; ideological mix of altruism and Manifest Destiny.

## Consequence Analysis

U.S. emerged with Pacific and Caribbean colonies. Philippines required a brutal subsequent war against Filipino independence forces. Roosevelt's war service launched his national career. The war marked the shift from continental to global power projection.

## Actors

- United States (McKinley administration, Navy, Army)
- Spain
- Cuban and Filipino revolutionaries
- Key individuals: [[actors/roosevelt-theodore]], [[actors/mckinley-william]], [[actors/wood-leonard]], Dewey, etc.

## Historiography

Traditional view: short, popular, humanitarian war that launched American century. Revisionists emphasize economic motives, the role of the "yellow press," and the subsequent Philippine-American War as the real imperial conflict. Primary sources include official reports, soldier memoirs (TR's *The Rough Riders*), and diplomatic correspondence.
