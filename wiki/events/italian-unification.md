# The Unification of Italy (the Risorgimento)

The **unification of Italy** (the *Risorgimento*) was the process, climaxing in 1859–70, by
which the divided Italian peninsula was forged into the **Kingdom of Italy** under the House of
Savoy — driven by **Piedmont-Sardinia** and the *Realpolitik* of **Cavour**, the arms of
**Garibaldi**, and French intervention against Austria. Deep-read from
[[new-cambridge-modern-history-v10|*The New Cambridge Modern History*, Vol. X]].

## Narrative

After the failure of [[revolutions-of-1848|1848]] (when Piedmont's lone war against Austria
was crushed), the cause passed from Mazzini's republican idealism to the **constitutional
monarchy of Piedmont** and its minister **Count Cavour**, who pursued unification by diplomacy
and modernization. NCMH X stresses his calculated risks: Cavour brought Piedmont into the
**[[crimean-war|Crimean War]]** (1855) — "allié et non serviteur" — to win a seat at the peace
table and put "the Italian question" before the powers. At **Plombières (1858)** he secured
**Napoleon III's** alliance against Austria; the **war of 1859** (Magenta, Solferino) and
plebiscites brought Lombardy and the central duchies to Piedmont. In 1860 **Garibaldi's**
volunteer *Thousand* conquered Sicily and Naples (the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies), handing the
south to Victor Emmanuel. The **Kingdom of Italy** was proclaimed in **1861**; **Venetia** was
gained from Austria in 1866 and **Rome** taken in **1870**, completing unification.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` **Italian nationalism** (the Risorgimento) and the discrediting, in
  [[revolutions-of-1848|1848]], of unification by popular insurrection alone.
- `caused_by:` **Cavour's *Realpolitik*** and Piedmontese leadership — diplomacy, the Crimean
  gambit, and the French alliance — combined with **Garibaldi's** insurgent arms.
- `contributed_to:` **French intervention** (Napoleon III) against Austria in 1859.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **Kingdom of Italy** (1861; completed with Venetia 1866 and Rome 1870) and
  the **end of Austrian dominance** in the peninsula.
- `produced:` a celebrated **model of nationalist unification** (and a foil/parallel to
  [[german-unification|Germany's]]); and the **"Roman Question"** (the Papacy's loss of the
  Papal States, unresolved until 1929).
- Left deep **north–south** divisions and a gap between *legal* and *real* Italy.

## Actors

- **Piedmont-Sardinia** (Cavour, Victor Emmanuel II) and **Garibaldi**; **France** (Napoleon
  III) against the **Austrian Empire**; with **Mazzini's** republicans and the **Papacy**
  sidelined. See [[nationalism]], [[revolutions-of-1848]].

## Historiography

NCMH X foregrounds Cavour's diplomacy as the "measure of his statesmanship." Debate persists
over whether unification was a genuine national movement or a Piedmontese *conquest* of Italy
("piedmontization"), and over the marginalization of the democratic-republican (Mazzini,
Garibaldi) strand by the moderate monarchists.
