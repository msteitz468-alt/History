# Imperial Coronation of Otto I (962)

On **2 February 962** in **St Peter's**, **Pope John XII** crowned **[[otto-i|Otto I]]** emperor, reviving the western empire that had lapsed with the death of Berengar I (924) and inaugurating the line of German emperors that would become the **Holy Roman Empire**. [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]] (Müller-Mertens) treats it as the founding act of the German-Italian imperial nexus.

## Narrative

Having conquered the **[[kingdom-of-italy|kingdom of Italy]]** and married its dowager queen **Adelaide** (951), Otto answered Pope **John XII's** appeal for protection against **Berengar II** of Ivrea. Before crossing the Alps he secured the succession by having his son **Otto II elected and crowned co-king**. In Rome he received the imperial crown, and **Adelaide** was crowned and anointed alongside him. Otto confirmed the Carolingian donations to the Roman church (the *Ottonianum* / **Privilegium Ottonianum**), but also extracted from the Romans an **oath never to elect or consecrate a pope without imperial consent** — and made good on it, deposing the faithless John XII through a Roman synod and installing his own popes. He took the modest title *imperator augustus* (not Charlemagne's explicitly Roman style); the chancery honorific *magnus* gave him his epithet "the Great."

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Otto's prior **conquest of Italy** and **John XII's appeal** — the immediate trigger, on the exact model of the Carolingian popes' appeals to Pippin and Charlemagne.
- `contributed_to:` the **prestige of the [[battle-of-lechfeld-955|Lechfeld victory]]** (955) and Otto's hegemony over the post-Carolingian successor-states, which made him the obvious western emperor.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **revival of the western empire** and the permanent, fateful **union of the German kingdom with the *regnum Italiae*** — every German king would henceforth claim the imperial crown and be drawn south of the Alps (Müller-Mertens notes the Ottonians spent nearly 17 of 40 years on Italian expeditions).
- `produced:` a structural **imperial supremacy over the papacy** (the Ottonianum, control of papal elections) that lasted until the mid-eleventh century — and whose reversal by the reform papacy precipitated the **Investiture Contest**.
- `enabled:` Otto's foundation of the archbishopric of **Magdeburg** (papal consent secured at the coronation) and the eastern mission.

## Historiography

NCMH III situates the coronation within a contested **imperial ideology**: a *Roman* emperorship (stressed by the chancery and by Italian and some Saxon writers) coexisted with a *non-Roman*, victory- and acclamation-based idea (Widukind, deriving Otto's imperial rank from the Lechfeld). The act is read less as a deliberate "renewal of Rome" (that comes with [[otto-iii|Otto III]]) than as the regularisation of Otto's existing **hegemonial kingship** over the Carolingian successor world.
