# The Roman–Syrian War (192–188 BCE)

The war between Rome and **[[antiochus-iii-the-great|Antiochus III]]** that ended
Seleucid ambitions west of the Taurus and confirmed Rome as the arbiter of the
Aegean world. Treated in [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Ch. 8 (Errington).

## Narrative

While Rome fought Philip, **Antiochus III** — fresh from his eastern *Anabasis* and
the conquest of Coele-Syria from Egypt (Panium, 200) — re-occupied the coast of
**Asia Minor** and crossed to **Thrace** (196), rebuilding Lysimacheia. The
resulting diplomacy (the confrontation at **Lysimacheia**, 196; the clashes at Rome,
193) exposed **incompatible world-views**: Antiochus claimed Asia Minor as his
sphere (Italy being Rome's), with the Aegean/Balkans a no-man's-land; Rome, after
defeating Philip, claimed the Balkans to the Hellespont and treated Asia Minor as a
buffer. Flamininus' demand that Antiochus quit Europe and leave the Asian Greeks
"autonomous" was, like the demands on Philip, framed to be rejected.

Invited by the disaffected **[[aetolian-league|Aetolians]]** (who seized Demetrias),
Antiochus crossed to Greece (autumn 192) with a small force — fatally
overestimating Greek enthusiasm. Received coolly everywhere but at Demetrias and by
the Aetolians, he was crushed at **Thermopylae** (191) and fled to Asia. Rome,
under the consul **L. Cornelius Scipio** (with his brother **[[scipio-africanus|
Africanus]]** as *legatus*), won naval supremacy (Myonnesus) and marched into Asia.
The decisive battle came at **Magnesia ad Sipylum** (190): Antiochus, though
outnumbering the Romans two-to-one, was routed.

## The Peace of Apamea (188)

See [[peace-of-apamea-188bce]]. Antiochus was to **evacuate all Asia Minor north and
west of the Taurus**, pay a colossal **15,000-talent** indemnity, surrender his war
fleet and elephants, and give hostages (including a son) and the surrender of
enemies (Hannibal escaped). The vacated lands — Rome keeping **no territory** — were
divided between **[[pergamon|Eumenes II of Pergamon]]** and **[[rhodes]]**, with the
Greek cities that had joined Rome before Magnesia declared free. Cn. Manlius Vulso's
brutal Galatian campaign (189) and the settlement at Apamea completed the
reordering of Asia Minor.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the **incompatible spheres-of-influence** claims and Antiochus'
crossing to Europe, read by Rome as a threat to its Balkan settlement; the Aetolian
invitation provided the occasion.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the [[peace-of-apamea-188bce|Peace of Apamea]] — the
[[seleucid-empire|Seleucids]] confined to an Asiatic empire east of the Taurus, still
vast but barred from the west.
enabled: the **aggrandizement of Pergamon and Rhodes** as Rome's client-powers, and
the application to Asia Minor of [[flamininus|Flamininus' doctrine]] — gratitude and
client-freedom as a cheaper substitute for legions.

## Historiography

[[polybius]] and [[livy]]; the treaty text survives in part (the "Taurus line"
definition debated). The war is the clearest case of Roman *diplomacy by ultimatum*
and of the [[nature-of-roman-imperialism|systemic]] extension of Roman *imperium*.
