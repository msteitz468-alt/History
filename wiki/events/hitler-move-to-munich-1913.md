# Hitler's Move to Munich (May 1913)

## Narrative

In May 1913, aged 24, Adolf Hitler left Vienna for Munich. He registered as an "artist-painter." The move was presented as artistic, but Kershaw notes the "intensified longing to go to Germany, to where his 'childhood secret love' drew him," alongside the practical motive of evading Austrian military service (he had been liable since 1909 and had successfully avoided call-up).

He continued his marginal artistic existence (postcards/watercolours) in Munich until the outbreak of war in 1914.

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` cumulative failure and alienation in Vienna (Academy rejections, poverty, isolation) + ideological identification with the German Reich + concrete desire to escape conscription into the multi-ethnic Austrian army. `contributed_to:` small remaining inheritance and contacts from Vienna artistic sales.

`preceded_by:` Vienna years 1907–1913 and crystallization of antisemitic/völkisch worldview.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` presence in Munich at the moment of the Augusterlebnis (2 August 1914 Odeonsplatz crowd photo). Enlistment in a Bavarian regiment (List Regiment) rather than Austrian forces — a decision with lasting identity consequences.

`enabled:` the specific post-1918 return to Munich (rather than Vienna or Linz) as the site of his political debut in the DAP. The 1913 move placed him inside the Reich at the critical 1914–1919 juncture.

`followed_by:` WWI service, Pasewalk, 1918–19 Munich revolution, DAP entry.

## Historiography

Kershaw (Range 01) presents the move as the logical endpoint of Vienna failure rather than a dramatic break. Artistic pretext is accepted alongside the conscription-evasion motive; no evidence of grand political plan in 1913. The "childhood secret love" for Germany is quoted from Hitler's later self-mythologizing but treated as consistent with his pan-German formation.
