# The Warsaw Ghetto Uprising (April–May 1943)

When SS units entered the Warsaw ghetto on 19 April 1943 to deport its last inhabitants, several hundred young Jewish fighters of the ŻOB and ŻZW resisted with smuggled pistols, grenades, and petrol bombs, holding out for nearly a month against SS forces under Jürgen Stroop, who burned the ghetto block by block. Documented for the wiki from [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)]].

## Narrative

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: the Great Deportation began 22 July 1942, when SS deportation specialist Hermann Höfle informed the Jewish Council that 6,000 Jews a day would be deported — the Blue Danube waltz playing from an SS gramophone in the street as the minutes were typed by the young Marcel Reich-Ranicki. Ghetto Elder Adam Czerniaków, refusing to sign the order because it included the children, swallowed his cyanide tablet ("I can no longer bear all this. My act will show everyone the right thing to do."). By 12 September 1942 more than 253,000 ghetto inhabitants had been gassed at Treblinka, over 10,000 more shot during round-ups; ~36,000 remained. The diarist Chaim Kaplan, who chronicled the deportations ("Every day Polish Jewry is being brought to slaughter"), was murdered at Treblinka around the end of 1942; Emanuel Ringelblum posed the anguished question that gave Evans's chapter its title — "Why did we allow ourselves to be led like sheep to the slaughter?" — and answered it with German terror, reprisals against bystanders, the role of the Jewish police, scarce weapons, starvation, and the will to hope.

On 18 January 1943 insurgents attacked a deportation column and freed the deportees; Himmler ordered the ghetto's final liquidation on 16 February. When SS units entered on **19 April 1943** they met organized fire from the ŻOB and ŻZW — young fighters certain of death who "became convinced that it would be better to go down fighting and die with dignity." Stroop, ordered by Himmler to act with "the greatest harshness," burned the ghetto block by block; his own report describes Jews jumping from burning upper storeys and crawling with broken bones toward unburned buildings, and fighters smoked out of the sewers. He declared the action over on 16 May 1943 by dynamiting the Great Synagogue, reporting 7,000 "annihilated" in the fighting, up to 6,000 in the burning buildings, the rest deported to Treblinka, against an implausibly low fifteen German dead. Himmler ordered the ruins razed on 11 June 1943, and the uprising prompted his 21 June 1943 order liquidating **all** remaining eastern ghettos (Białystok's five-day resistance in August 1943 was crushed by Globocnik with tanks; Łódź, the last major ghetto, was emptied to Chełmno and Auschwitz in summer 1944, its Elder Chaim Rumkowski — "I must amputate limbs in order to save the body!" — gassed at Birkenau with his family from the final transport of 28 August 1944). Over 90 per cent of Poland's 3.3 million Jews were dead by then.

Ringelblum, hidden in a bunker after escaping Trawniki, was betrayed on 7 March 1944, tortured, forced to watch his wife and son killed, and executed; his buried ghetto archive was recovered in 1946 and 1950, part of it sealed in a milk-churn.

## Historiography

The Stroop Report (with its photographs) and the Ringelblum archive are the foundational sources; Evans supplements them with the diaries of Czerniaków, Kaplan, and the German officer Wilm Hosenfeld ("This is the way we intend to win the war. These beasts."). Evans also debunks the Rosenstrasse legend (February–March 1943): the intermarried Berlin Jews detained there were never slated for extermination, and the gathered relatives staged no explicit protest.
