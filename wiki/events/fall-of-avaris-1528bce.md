# The Fall of Avaris (Ahmose's Conquest of the Hyksos)

The capture and destruction of the Hyksos capital **[[places/avaris|Avaris]]** by
[[ahmose-i|Ahmose I]] between his regnal years 18 and 22 (c.1532–1528 BCE), which ended the
[[hyksos|Hyksos]] kingdom, closed the
[[processes/second-intermediate-period|Second Intermediate Period]], and founded the
[[new-kingdom-egypt|New Kingdom]]. The reunification it achieved "was not to be broken again
for over 400 years."

## Narrative

Building on the earlier offensives of [[actors/seqenenra-taa|Seqenenra Taa]] (killed in
battle) and [[kamose|Kamose]] (who blockaded but did not take Avaris), Ahmose mounted the
decisive campaign. His **strategy** was to bypass Memphis, seize **Heliopolis**, then — when
the inundation receded — take **Tell el-Habua** to cut the Hyksos line of retreat across
Sinai, before assaulting Avaris itself. The eyewitness biography of **Ahmose son of Ibana**
(Elkab) records siege, a fleet blockade, and finally the sack: "Avaris was despoiled and I
brought spoil from there." Ahmose then pursued the enemy into southern Palestine, besieging
**Sharuhen** for three years.

## Causal & Consequence Analysis

- `caused_by:` [[processes/second-intermediate-period|the Theban war of liberation]] — three
  generations of Theban kings (Seqenenra, Kamose, Ahmose) pressing against Hyksos rule.
- `produced:` the [[new-kingdom-egypt|New Kingdom]] and a reunified Egypt under the 18th
  Dynasty.
- `enabled:` the **imperial turn** — Ahmose's pursuit into the Levant and his Nubian
  campaigns inaugurated the aggressive foreign policy of the New Kingdom (see
  [[processes/egyptian-imperialism]]).

## Historiography

Three contemporary sources converge: the **biography of Ahmose son of Ibana**, the
**archaeology of Tell el-Dabʿa**, and **Ahmose's Abydos temple reliefs** (discovered 1993,
showing horses, chariots, and a battle fleet). They sharply revise Manetho's lurid account
(via Josephus) of a 480,000-strong siege and negotiated departure: at Tell el-Dabʿa "a clear
cultural break is visible between the latest Hyksos stratum and that of the earliest 18th
Dynasty," consistent with a **mass exodus** rather than a massacre. Ahmose razed the Hyksos
citadel and built a new palace on the ruins — the structure that yielded the celebrated
**Minoan-style frescoes** that "revolutionized ideas of the relations between Egypt and
Crete."
