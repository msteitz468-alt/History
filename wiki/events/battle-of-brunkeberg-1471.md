# Battle of Brunkeberg

The battle of 10 October 1471 on the Brunkeberg ridge outside Stockholm, in which
the Swedish regent **Sten Sture the Elder** decisively defeated King
[[denmark|Christian I of Denmark]]. Per
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]], the victory
not only strengthened Sten Sture's position but **paralysed union policy for some
time** and became a founding moment of Swedish proto-national identity.

## Analysis

The battle was **not a simple national showdown** between Swedes and Danes: the
**Oxenstiernas and Vasas sided with Christian I**, while Sten Sture was backed by the
**Axelssons (Thott)**, the **Dalecarlian miners and peasants**, and the **burghers of
Stockholm**. It thus cut across aristocratic families and social groups. In its
aftermath the clause of Magnus Eriksson's Townlaw requiring half of Stockholm's
councillors to be German was revoked, and Germans disappeared from the city council.
Karl Knutsson's *Karlskrönikan* propaganda later cast the conflict as Danish
oppression, embedding a national interpretation the event did not originally bear.
Brunkeberg belongs to the long arc of Swedish resistance to the
[[kalmar-union-1397|Kalmar Union]], from the [[engelbrekt-rebellion-1434|Engelbrekt
rebellion]] to the [[stockholm-bloodbath-1520|Stockholm Bloodbath]].

## Historiography

The Cambridge History (Schück, Olesen) stresses that the "national showdown"
reading is a later construction propagated by Karl Knutsson's *Karlskrönikan*, which
cast the union as Danish oppression; modern scholarship (Olesen, Enemark) instead
emphasises that union allegiance ran along family and estate lines rather than ethnic
ones. See [[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]].
