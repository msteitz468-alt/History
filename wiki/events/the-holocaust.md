# The Holocaust (1933–1945)

The **Holocaust** (Hebrew *Shoah*) was the systematic, state-sponsored persecution and murder of
about **six million European Jews** by Nazi Germany and its collaborators, alongside the mass
killing of Roma, the disabled, Soviet prisoners, Poles, and others — the central atrocity of the
[[second-world-war-1939|Second World War]] and the paradigm case of modern [[genocide]]. This page
treats the historical event; the analytical category is on [[genocide]]. Framed here through the
[[cambridge-history-second-world-war-v3|Cambridge History of the Second World War, Vol. III]]'s
account of the war as ideological war and total destruction, with established Holocaust
scholarship.

## Narrative

- **Persecution (1933–39).** From Hitler's accession the regime stripped German Jews of rights —
  boycotts, the 1935 **Nuremberg Laws**, "Aryanization" of property, and the **Kristallnacht**
  pogrom (November 1938) — driving emigration and escalating exclusion.
- **Ghettoization (1939–41).** The conquest of **Poland** brought millions of Jews under German
  control; they were concentrated in sealed, starving **ghettos** (Warsaw, Łódź) — part of the
  wider Nazi project of demographic reordering and **displacement** in the East.
- **Mass shooting (1941).** The invasion of the USSR (**Operation Barbarossa**) — explicitly a
  *Weltanschauungskrieg*, a war of annihilation — unleashed the **Einsatzgruppen**, mobile killing
  squads that shot well over a million Jews in pits behind the front (Babi Yar, 1941).
- **The "Final Solution" (1942–45).** Coordinated at the **Wannsee Conference** (January 1942), the
  regime moved to continent-wide industrialized murder in **extermination camps** in occupied
  Poland (**Auschwitz-Birkenau**, Treblinka, Sobibór, Bełżec, Chełmno) — deportation by rail,
  gassing, and "destruction through labour" in the camp-and-slave-labour system — until the camps
  were overrun in 1944–45.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Nazi **racial antisemitism** and the ideology of a racial war of annihilation
  (see [[communism-and-fascism]]).
- `contributed_to:` the **radicalizing dynamics of conquest and occupation** in the East — the
  cumulative escalation from persecution to ghettoization to mass shooting to extermination (the
  ground of the **intentionalist vs. functionalist** debate over how centrally pre-planned the path
  to genocide was).
- `enabled:` the **[[second-world-war-1939|war]]** itself, which furnished the territory (millions
  of eastern Jews), the secrecy, and the climate of mass violence that made continent-wide murder
  possible.

### The path to extermination (Paxton, 2004)

Paxton's synthesis in [[paxton-anatomy-of-fascism-2004|*The Anatomy of
Fascism*]] (ch. 6) reads the Holocaust as the outermost reach of fascist
radicalization, developing **incrementally**: "Both impulses ratcheted each
other up in an ascending spiral, in a way appropriate to a 'dual state'" —
local party vigilantism repeatedly channeled into orderly state policy
(street actions of spring 1933 → the 1 April boycott → Nuremberg Laws;
Kristallnacht → Göring's "Aryanization": "It is not the Jew they harm but
me, as the final authority for coordinating the German economy").
**Expulsion**, not murder, remained official policy until late 1941 —
Eichmann's Vienna exit-permit system (1938), the Madagascar plan (1940),
the hope that conquering the USSR would open expulsion space. The line to
total extermination was crossed in the Baltic states and eastern Poland as
early as August–September 1941, "apparently on local initiative (confident,
of course, of Berlin's approval)"; Wannsee "looks more like further state
coordination of local extermination initiatives than the initiation of a
new policy from above." No written Führer order exists, but "no serious
scholar doubts Hitler's central responsibility" — most likely a verbal
order in fall 1941 (rage at the failure before Moscow) or the secret speech
of 12 December 1941 after American entry, fulfilling the 30 January 1939
"prophecy." Three "dress rehearsals" lowered inhibitions and trained
personnel: the T-4 euthanasia program (70,000+ killed from September 1939,
halted after family and clergy protests, its experts transferred east); the
Einsatzgruppen (Polish elites 1939, the Commissar Order 1941 — this time
"the army raised no objections"); and the intentional death of millions of
Soviet POWs, on 600 of whom Zyklon-B was first tested at Auschwitz on
3 September 1941. The six death factories — Auschwitz, Chełmno, Treblinka,
Sobibór, Majdanek, Bełżec, all "outside the reach of the German normative
state and of German law" — accounted for 60% of the Jews murdered. On
Goldhagen: widespread murderous Jew-hatred was necessary "but does not tell
us why the line was crossed in certain places at certain times, and not
others" — the best accounts show Mommsen's "cumulative radicalization."
Knowledge inside Germany was "fairly widespread"; soldiers photographed
mass executions and sent the pictures home. Paxton's closing judgment: the
murder of the Jews diverted vital resources from the war itself — "fascist
radicalization… cannot be understood as a rational way to persuade a people
to give their all to a war effort."

### Payne's synthesis (1995) — the Holocaust in the history of fascism

[[payne-history-of-fascism-1995|Payne]] anchors his fascism survey on the
judgment that "fascism was one of the two most atrocious political movements
of modern times, and its greatest single atrocity was the mass liquidation
of the Jewish population of German-occupied Europe, a process unique in
human history" — "a direct and logical consequence of the extreme racial
anti-Semitism of Hitler and most of the Nazi core group." His chronology
matches the incremental account above: limited early persecution (more than
half of German Jewry fled before the war), Kristallnacht (9–10 Nov 1938),
radicalization with the conquest of Poland, the Madagascar speculation of
1940 (Himmler then calling Soviet-style mass extermination "repugnant to
German culture"), ghettoization, the 1941 "war of racial extermination"
bringing six million Jews under German control, over a million killed by
the Einsatzgruppen and auxiliaries by end-1941, the decision by early 1942
for six Vernichtungslager in Poland, and the central killing phase 1942–44:
more than four million murdered in the camps, ~5.5–6 million Jews in all —
"the greatest single act of genocide in world history." He tallies the
wider democide at ~10 million direct liquidations (several hundred thousand
Roma; nearly three million Poles) plus 3.3 million Soviet POWs and nearly
four million slave laborers — deaths that "served no military purpose and
in fact weakened the German war effort." His comparative chapters add the
collaborator genocides: the Ustasha state's murder of perhaps 250,000 Serbs
and virtually all of Croatia's 30,000–40,000 Jews (Jasenovac as "a sort of
Ustashi Auschwitz"; village massacres by burning locked churches; Jewish
losses in Yugoslavia 77.9 percent), Romania's "nonautomated holocaust" in
Transnistria (200,000–300,000 Jews killed by Romanian soldiers and police,
"by far the greatest liquidation of Jews by non-German forces," operationally
separate from the Final Solution), and Hungary 1944 (~437,000 deported in
seven weeks under Sztójay; ~50,000 more plus Danube-bank shootings under the
[[arrow-cross|Arrow Cross]]). Counterpoint: Fascist Italy's 83 percent
Jewish survival rate, second only to Denmark, with the Italian army actively
sheltering Jews in its occupation zones until September 1943 (7,682 of
Italy's Jews were killed, by the Germans, after the occupation). Payne also
notes the genealogical prehistory (Gobineau → Vacher de Lapouge →
Chamberlain's race-war "scenario"; the Protocols forged by the Russian
secret police in Paris, 1894–99) and quotes Hitler's 1939 invocation of the
unpunished Armenian genocide: "Who now remembers the massacre of the
Armenians?"

### The Holocaust in France (Paxton, 1972)

France is the paradigm case of **collaborator-state participation**.
[[vichy-france]] enacted its own [[vichy-anti-jewish-laws-1940|anti-Jewish
statutes]] (October 1940, June 1941) without German demand; its internments,
census, and "Aryanization" then "made the Final Solution much easier" when
Himmler's quotas reached France (June 1942; Vél d'Hiv roundup 16 July 1942).
**~60,000–65,000 Jews were deported from France** (mostly foreign refugees;
~6,000 French citizens; ~2,800 returned) — a 26% loss of the prewar Jewish
population, against Holland's 86% and Belgium's 55%, but Paxton's reframing
governs the responsibility question: not whether France's percentage was
lower than the totally-occupied countries', but "whether more Jews were
deported from France because of Vichy preparations and assistance than would
have been the case if the Germans had had to do it all alone. Vichy bears a
heavy burden of responsibility, seen in these terms." Contrasts he presses:
the Danish boat rescue and the Italian occupation zone's active shelter of
Jews that Vichy refused. See [[vichy-shield-thesis]].

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the murder of **~6 million Jews** — two-thirds of European Jewry — and the
  annihilation of the centuries-old Jewish civilization of eastern and central Europe, plus the
  killing of Roma (the *Porajmos*), disabled people (the "euthanasia" programme), and others.
- `produced:` the legal and moral response — Raphael Lemkin's coining of "**[[genocide]]**," the
  **Nuremberg trials**, and the **UN Genocide Convention** (1948) — and impetus to the founding of
  **Israel** (1948).
- `produced:` a permanent reference point for ethics, memory, and the history of modernity (the
  debate over the Holocaust's relation to modern bureaucracy, science, and industry).

## Actors

The **Nazi regime and the SS** (the perpetrators) and the **Jews of Europe** (the victims), with
the **Einsatzgruppen**, **local collaborators**, the targeted **Roma and disabled**, and the
**bystanders** and Allied governments who did little to stop it. See [[second-world-war-1939]] and
[[genocide]].

## Historiography

Beyond the **intentionalist/functionalist** debate (a master plan vs. cumulative radicalization),
scholarship debates the Holocaust's **singularity vs. comparability** with other genocides (see
[[genocide]]), the degree of ordinary-German and collaborator complicity (Browning's *Ordinary
Men* vs. Goldhagen), and its relation to **modernity** (Bauman). The
[[cambridge-history-second-world-war-v3|CHSWW Vol. III]] situates it within the war's
*Weltanschauungskrieg*, its economy of slave labour and "destruction through labour," and the
massive **displacement** of the 1940s — and Part IV traces how German intellectuals and global
memory have since reckoned with it. (The series' dedicated *Politics and Ideology* volume, which
would treat the ideological causes most fully, is absent from the collection.)
Griffin ([[griffin-nature-of-fascism-1991]]) frames the genocide as "the
poisonous fruit of Utopia" — destruction as the necessary pole of Nazism's
"synergy of palingenesis and destruction," not gratuitous nihilism — leans
toward the contingent dating (systematic genocide "once the Blitzkrieg
against Russia had failed," following Mayer 1990), and reads Himmler's Posen
speech as the "purest form" of fascism's logic of heroic self-sacrifice
justifying extermination. His Koestler-derived point generalizes it: "it is
the perversion of self-transcendent emotions, not self-assertive ones, which
has been largely responsible for the chronicle of atrocities."

## Pre-war antecedents — Evans, *The Third Reich in Power* (2005)

Evans (ingested 2026-07-02) supplies the peacetime prehistory in detail (see
[[persecution-of-german-jews-1933-1939]], [[nuremberg-laws-1935]],
[[kristallnacht-1938]], [[nazi-racial-hygiene-program-1933-1939]]). His key
interpretive claims for this page:

- **Expulsion, not yet extermination.** Through 1939 the regime "did not at
  this stage intend, still less plan, to exterminate all Germany's Jews" —
  the aim was a Jew-free Germany (then Europe) via forced emigration; the
  Haavara Agreement and Eichmann's Vienna "assembly-line" model (terror +
  coerced Jewish administration + asset plunder, ~100,000 Austrian Jews out
  by May 1939) were its instruments, and the SD's model for what followed.
- **The 30 January 1939 "prophecy"** (quoted in full in Evans): if
  "international finance Jewry" should plunge the peoples into a world war,
  the result would be "the annihilation of the Jewish race in Europe." Evans
  reads its 1939 meaning as **hostage-taking on the largest possible scale**
  — deterring US entry into the coming war — following private versions to
  Pirow (24 Nov 1938: the "protecting hand") and to the Czechoslovak foreign
  minister (21 Jan 1939: "The Jews among us will be annihilated... 9th
  November will be avenged").
- **Modernity, not regression.** The 1938 radicalization belonged to war
  preparation (expropriation partly driven by rearmament's cash hunger) and
  was justified by "the most modern, scientific criteria of racial fitness" —
  against any "regression into barbarism" framing. His comparative survey
  (Endek Poland, Iron Guard Romania, Horthy Hungary's Jewish Laws) shows
  Germany's distinctiveness: only there were race laws on marriage enforced,
  the robbery systematic, the pogrom state-organized nationwide, and more
  than half the Jewish population driven into exile.
- The sterilization program's machinery and desensitization ("state-sponsored
  violence against the body on a systematic scale") is flagged by Evans as
  the bridge to wartime murder — the real divergence from international
  eugenics came "when the Nazi regime turned from sterilizing social deviants
  to murdering them."

## Wartime mechanics and the decision debate — Evans, *The Third Reich in History and Memory* (2015)

From Evans's essays on the politics of genocide (chs. 24–25, ingested
2026-07-02):

- **Death toll by method** (Evans's breakdown of the ~6 million): ~3
  million in gas chambers, 1.3 million shot by SS Task Forces, police
  and army units, 700,000 murdered in mobile gas vans, up to 1 million
  by deliberate starvation, disease and maltreatment in camps, ghettos
  and transit.
- **Machinery timeline**: by end-December 1941 each of the four SS Task
  Forces had a mobile gas chamber; Belzec, Sobibor and Treblinka (the
  Reinhard camps) began mass extermination in March, May and July 1942
  respectively, using engine exhaust; Auschwitz-Birkenau operated from
  March 1942 with Zyklon-B, killing over a million. The Reinhard camps
  closed in **1943** — because their task of murdering the Polish
  ghetto populations was complete, not (pace Snyder) because the Red
  Army approached. The T-4 personnel supplied technical expertise, but
  Evans warns against a causal reading: had T-4 gassing continued, gas
  would still have been used against the Jews.
- **The decision debate**: the search for a single decision-moment
  "has long since been abandoned" in favor of a cumulative process —
  a ceaseless antisemitic propaganda barrage from Hitler and Goebbels
  from June 1941, implemented by Himmler/Heydrich and agents on the
  ground "in a relatively haphazard manner, though one always aimed at
  the goal of total annihilation." Gerlach's 12 December 1941 thesis
  (triggered by US entry) found little acceptance and Gerlach himself
  distanced from it; Evans notes Hitler's escalation is at least as
  plausibly tied to victory-euphoria as to frustration, and that the
  American dimension (Lend-Lease, Atlantic Charter) sharpened the
  "prophecy" logic alongside events in the east. Hitler's own repeated
  misdating of the prophecy from 30 January to 1 September 1939 shows
  that in his mind war and racial annihilation began the same day.
- **Distinctiveness within Nazi killing**: Jews ranked below every
  other group (Russians, Czechs, Ukrainians, Poles, even Roma) in
  rationing and work rules; the ritual humiliation inflicted on Jews
  from Poland 1939 onward had no equivalent in the (still murderous)
  treatment of Slavs; the autonomous Romanian (~380,000 killed) and
  Croatian genocides were inward-directed variants lacking the
  world-enemy construction. The death marches of 1945 — over half of
  715,000 camp prisoners dead — were genocidal though most victims
  were not Jewish (Blatman). See [[holocaust-uniqueness]] for the full
  comparative debate and [[colonialism-nazism-continuity]] for the
  colonial-precedent question.

Source: [[evans-third-reich-history-memory-2015]]

## The full wartime narrative — Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)

From the trilogy's concluding volume (Part 3, "The Final Solution", ingested 2026-07-02), which supplies the wiki's most detailed narrative treatment. The apparatus now has dedicated pages — [[einsatzgruppen]] (the shooting war, escalation to women and children July–August 1941, Babi Yar, the Romanian and Serbian killings), [[wannsee-conference-1942]] (the coordination meeting and Evans's process-not-decision argument), [[operation-reinhard-1942]] (Bełżec, Sobibór, Treblinka, Chełmno, Majdanek, Harvest Festival), [[auschwitz]] (Zyklon B, the selections, the toll, the SS town), [[warsaw-ghetto-uprising-1943]] (the ghettos, the Great Deportation, resistance and liquidation), [[aktion-t4-1939]] (the euthanasia incubator), and [[eichmann-adolf]]. Points held here:

- **Propaganda as launch mechanism.** Hitler personally ordered Goebbels on 8 July 1941 to focus the machine on "Jewish Bolshevism" and its supposed Anglo-American arm; the *Racial Observer*, which had run zero antisemitic front pages in 1940, ran a concentrated barrage July–November 1941 (Kaufman's crank pamphlet *Germany Must Perish* trumpeted as Roosevelt's genocide program). Evans dates the "genocidal mentality" to this convergence of Barbarossa, escalating US aid, and the prophecy rhetoric — with the memory of 1918 and security-paranoia as the master motive: "an ideologically pervasive mixture of fear and hatred," not the contempt reserved for Slavs.
- **The German-Jewish endgame.** The Klemperer and Solmitz diaries document the tightening vise (star decree 19 September 1941; death penalty for "virtually any offence" 4 December 1941; deportations from 15 October 1941 — the first Munich transport shot at Kovno's Fort IX). Up to 4,000 German Jews killed themselves 1941–43, nearly half of all Berlin suicides; Evans records the deaths of Jochen Klepper's family after Eichmann refused his wife an exit permit, and debunks the Rosenstrasse-protest legend.
- **Goebbels's 18 February 1943 Sportpalast slip** — "the most radical exter…[correcting himself] exclusion of Jewry!" to laughter and applause — as deliberate enlistment of the audience's complicity in the open secret.
- **Toll** (consistent with the 2015 breakdown): ~3 million in the camps, 700,000 in gas vans, 1.3 million shot, up to 1 million in ghettos and camps by hunger and disease; at least 5.5 million certain, ~6 million probable. The chapter closes with Wilm Hosenfeld's June 1943 diary: "With this terrible murder of the Jews we have lost the war . . . We deserve no mercy, we are all guilty."

Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## The Holocaust across occupied and allied Europe — Evans (2008), Part 4

Country-by-country outcomes as Evans gives them, keyed to the strength of native governments as buffers:

- **Netherlands** — the deadliest in the West: 102,000 of 140,000 Jews murdered (73%). Seyss-Inquart's Austrian-led civil administration, unchecked by any native government, even pursued sterilization of Jewish partners in mixed marriages, "discussed but never put into action in Germany itself." The February 1941 Amsterdam general strike was crushed; 47,000 went to Auschwitz on 53 trains (266 survivors) and 35,000 to Sobibór (19 survivors).
- **Belgium** — military administration, native buffers, strong resistance and Catholic sheltering of children: ~25,000 deported and killed, ~25,000 hidden (40% murdered).
- **France** — ~80,000 of 350,000 killed (just under a quarter); Vichy's own legislation, the Vél d'Hiv round-up by French police (16–17 July 1942; 8,160 penned in the velodrome days without water in the heat; a 24 August transport of 553 sick children gassed on arrival), Brunner at Drancy from 1943. Archbishop Saliège's 30 August 1942 protest ("like cattle") broke the hierarchy's silence. See [[vichy-france]].
- **Denmark** — the rescue: warned (partly by Reich Plenipotentiary Best's own leak), ~7,000+ Jews were ferried to Sweden on 1–2 October 1943; the 485 arrested went to Theresienstadt, not Auschwitz, and mostly survived.
- **Norway** — 770 deported under Quisling and Terboven (700 gassed); 930 escaped to Sweden. **Finland** refused: 8 foreign Jews deported after domestic outcry; the 2,000 native Jews untouched.
- **Greece** — ~45,000 of Salonika's 50,000 Jews to Auschwitz (March–April 1943), the islands in 1944.
- **Slovakia** — the Tiso government *offered* Jewish workers and paid Germany 500 RM per "unproductive" deportee; ~52,000 deported by June 1942; halted under Church/Vatican pressure and bribery; resumed after the 1944 uprising.
- **Romania** — after murdering 280,000–380,000 in 1941–42 (see [[einsatzgruppen]]), Antonescu refused the hand-over of the remaining 300,000 and repatriated Transnistria survivors from 1943, moved by Allied warnings and postwar calculation; Hitler's last appeal (5 August 1944) failed.
- **Bulgaria** — Boris III surrendered 11,000 Jews from annexed Thrace/Macedonia but, under parliamentary, popular, and Orthodox-Church protest, refused to deport the kingdom's own Jews.
- **Hungary** — Horthy and Kállay stalled deportation of 800,000 Jews through the 16–17 April 1943 Salzburg meeting, at which Hitler raged that Jews were "pure parasites" to be treated like "tuberculosis bacilli"; the German occupation of March 1944 delivered the catastrophe (~394,000 to Auschwitz — see [[auschwitz]] and the death-phase narrative on [[death-marches-1944]]).

Evans's structural point: where a native government or monarchy remained interposed between the SS and the Jewish population (Denmark, Bulgaria, Finland, post-1942 Romania, pre-1944 Hungary), most Jews survived; where German administration was direct (Netherlands, Poland, the occupied USSR), most died. Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## Knowledge, complicity, and the world's response — Evans (2008), Part 6

- **What Germans knew:** transmission ran through soldiers on leave, officials who read the Task Force reports, railway and housing personnel. Klemperer's diary tracks the rumor front: Jews "shot in Riga" (January 1942); Auschwitz "the most dreadful concentration camp" (16 March 1942); "a swift-working slaughterhouse" (October 1942). Reactions to the yellow star and the deportations were mixed but, Evans argues, predominantly negative among older and religious Germans; by 1943–44 Allied bombing was widely read as retribution for the Jews (Bishop Wurm to Lammers; SD Stuttgart, November 1944: "Have we not murdered thousands of Jews? . . . By doing all this we have shown the enemy what they can do to us if they win"). The full debate is filed at [[ordinary-germans-and-the-holocaust]].
- **The leadership said it aloud, internally:** Himmler's Posen speech (4 October 1943) — the extermination as "a laudable page in our history that will never be written," extended explicitly to women and children lest avengers grow up — repeated to Gauleiters, to Goebbels and Speer, and to army officers at Sonthofen (May 1944); Hitler to military personnel (26 May 1944): "By removing the Jews, I have removed from Germany the possibility of the construction of any kind of revolutionary cell . . . Humanitarianism would mean the greatest cruelty towards one's own people."
- **Alerting the Allies:** Schulte→Riegner (July 1942); Kurt Gerstein's eyewitness Bełżec report (2 August 1942 — a gassing that took 32 minutes after hours of waiting), pressed fruitlessly on Swedish, Vatican, and church channels; Karski's courier missions from the Warsaw ghetto led through the Albert Hall meeting to the joint Allied declaration of **17 December 1942** promising retribution. The Allies judged rapid victory the only effective response; bombing the Auschwitz rail lines was assessed as temporary respite at diversion cost. Goebbels ordered no denials, only tu-quoque.
- **Hungary 1944:** after occupying Hungary (19 March 1944), Eichmann's commando with full Hungarian police cooperation deported **438,000 Jews to Auschwitz in under two months** (from 15 May), ~394,000 gassed on arrival — the crematoria running so continuously one broke down. Horthy stopped the deportations on 7 July under Allied, Swedish, and papal pressure (Pius XII's appeal named neither the Jews nor their fate); the Budapest remnant then endured the Arrow Cross terror (see [[arrow-cross]] and [[death-marches-1944]]).
- **Rescuers:** Schindler's 1,100; Wilm Hosenfeld (Szpilman's protector, revealed only in the 1950s); the hiding networks of Berlin, Warsaw, and Amsterdam; the OSE children's rescue in France.

Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## Griffin (2007): The "Ecology" of Genocide

*Modernism and Fascism* (ch. 11, "The Third Reich's Biopolitical Modernism") supplies the modernist-studies reading, written with a "consciously 'anti-revisionist' stress on the crimes against humanity" and aligned with Bauman's *Modernity and the Holocaust* and Traverso's *The Origins of Nazi Violence*.

**Biopolitics in the extended sense.** Nazism conceived the Third Reich "as the product of a new form of politics shaped by and grounded in the forces of life itself, by a revolution conceived biologically" — a vitalist Lebensphilosophie that branded equality and human rights "decadent" and anti-life. The Nuremberg Laws fused völkisch (Lagarde) and eugenic/racial-hygiene (Ploetz, German Society for Racial Hygiene, 1905) criteria into one juridical-mental framework; the two rationales' tensions were "still detectable in the discussions at the Wannsee Conference and during the execution of the Final Solution." Hitler's Volkskörper discourse (Germany as "a single organism," himself as Physician curing "the disease") gave racial hygienists "carte blanche." Context Griffin insists on: eugenic sterilization was international social modernism (adopted at some point by the US, USSR, Sweden, Canada, Switzerland, and a dozen others; Churchill's rejected sterilization bill and his memorandum to Asquith on "the feeble-minded and insane classes... a national and race danger which it is impossible to exaggerate") — but Nazism implemented its version "with a systematic ruthlessness, bureaucratic efficiency, and technological modernity impossible in liberal democracies" (~400,000 sterilized 1933–45 under the July 1933 Law for the Prevention of Genetically Diseased Offspring, vs 64,000 in the US 1930–70). Schwarz's study shows Third Reich euthanasia advocates saw themselves as "the vanguard of the nation's modernization."

**Technocracy in service of extermination.** The SS tracked prisoners, forced labourers, and slaves with custom IBM/Dehomag punch-card machines, with the full collaboration of IBM New York (1.5 billion cards in 1943 alone); "in the summer of 1943 all non-Germans at Auschwitz were tattooed with five-digit IBM Hollerith numbers." A Zyklon B pesticide advertising film ran in Reich cinemas. The Generalplan Ost technocracy (Konrad Meyer's "work of art"; Heydrich's wall of German peasants against "the storm floods of Asia") planned the East as the Reich's "'Wild West'," with wetlands eyed by "the planners of genocide" for working slaves to death and "disappearing" massacre victims. A slave workforce of some 7.6 million by 1945 served the Todt Organization, the Hermann Goering Works, and Krupp, Daimler Benz, Opel, BMW, IG Farben, and AEG; Primo Levi's Dr Pannwitz at the IG Farben Buna works stands for the thousands of technicians "quietly going about their business." Topf & Söhne of Erfurt built the Auschwitz ovens to SS specifications "so as to be able to incinerate up to 4,765 corpses a day when used efficiently."

**Genocide as purification.** Traverso: Germany was "the laboratory of the West," synthesizing "nationalism, racism, anti-Semitism, imperialism, anti-Bolshevism, anti-humanism — all of which existed throughout Europe but which elsewhere either remained muted or else never entered into toxic combination"; "the revolt against the decadence of modernity appropriated the means of that very modernity." Hans Kohn (1938): Nazi racial theory "amounts to a new naturalistic religion for which the German people are the corpus mysticum and the army the priesthood"; genocide became "a disinfection, a purification — in short an 'ecological' measure." Perpetrator self-understanding, verbatim: the Red Cross inspector who visited the camps in 1944 — "These people were proud of their work. They were convinced of being engaged in an act of purification. They called Auschwitz the anus of Europe... If you cannot get your head round that you will understand nothing at all"; SS doctor Fritz Klein to Auschwitz survivor Dr Ella Lingens-Reiner, on his Hippocratic oath — "I am cutting out a festering appendix. The Jews are the festering appendix in the body of Europe. And this is why they must be cut out." Griffin's summation: within the Nazi mindset Auschwitz was "a vast biopolitical sewage works, a technocratic installation where human waste products were disposed of once anything of value... had been extracted for recycling"; Primo Levi called it "the ultimate drainage point of the German universe." Killing the "life unworthy of life" was, "in cosmological terms, a 'holy act' paralleled by the Aztecs' ritual slaughter of their enemies to placate the Sun God." Richard Evans is quoted: "The Third Reich was engaged in a vast experiment in human engineering, both physical and spiritual, that recognized no limits in its penetration of the individual's body and soul." Griffin's verdict, against any incidentalist reading: "The resulting crimes against humanity were thus not incidental, but integral to Nazism's version of modernity" — though mass murder was "not the primary goal of the Third Reich, but... the inevitable 'by-product' of the 'perverse logic' of its war on biological and moral decadence" (Etlin). The chapter closes with Kafka's *Penal Colony* as the botched redemption machine: "No sign was visible of the promised redemption." (Source: [[griffin-modernism-and-fascism-2007]].)

## From Paxton and Hessler, *Europe in the Twentieth Century* (2011)

This account gives the wiki's clearest three-stage model of the process — segregation, expulsion, extermination — and adds a distinct wartime narrative thread. Source: [[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011]] throughout this section.

**Stage model.** Paxton and Hessler read Nazi biological antisemitism as "potentially murderous from the beginning" — the regime deemed the most assimilated, most integrated Jews the most dangerous, precisely because assimilation blurred the racial line the regime needed to police. The persecution proceeded in fits and starts rather than a straight line — the 1936 Berlin Olympics saw a deliberate, temporary softening of public antisemitic display for foreign consumption — with the introduction of the compulsory yellow star in September 1941 marking segregation's climax, the point past which the policy shifted decisively toward expulsion and then extermination.

**Expulsion phase.** Before the war, and into its first two years, expulsion rather than murder was the operative policy. Eichmann's Vienna scheme (1938) was a self-financing forced-emigration apparatus — wealthy Jews' confiscated assets funded the emigration of poorer Jews, extracting them from German-controlled territory as efficiently as possible. Theresienstadt was built and marketed as a "model" ghetto for propaganda purposes, to disguise the fate awaiting deportees. The Madagascar plan (and a fainter Kenya variant) were emigration schemes rendered moot once the territories in question proved out of reach; more broadly, neutral states were, in Paxton and Hessler's phrase, "shockingly reluctant" to admit Jewish refugees fleeing Germany and, later, occupied Europe.

**The turning point: Barbarossa.** The invasion of the Soviet Union in June 1941 was the hinge on which the policy turned from expulsion to extermination. The Einsatzgruppen's orders called for the liquidation of Communist Party officials and of Jews together, collapsing the distinction between political and racial enemy. At Kiev's Babi Yar ravine, nearly 34,000 Jews were shot over two days in September 1941 — among the largest single mass-shooting actions of the war. The killing method itself evolved under pressure: mass shooting gave way to mobile gas vans, adopted in part because of the psychological toll shooting was taking on the killers themselves, and gas vans in turn gave way to Zyklon-B, a technology inherited from the 1939 euthanasia program, first tested on Slavic prisoners of war before being turned on Jews. Paxton and Hessler's judgment on the autumn of 1941: "the ultimate threshold was crossed" — the shift to industrialized extermination of an entire people, a project without precedent in human history.

**Decision historiography.** Paxton and Hessler lay out the functionalist/intentionalist debate in compact form: functionalists point to local zealots competing for the Führer's approval and to the practical pressures of wartime crowding, disease, and supply shortages in the occupied East as drivers that pushed policy toward extermination from below; intentionalists trace every major decision back to Hitler himself. Their own position: "Hitler's primary responsibility is undeniable," but the operation as actually carried out required the mass complicity of a very large administrative and military apparatus — no single office or decision-maker could have produced the Holocaust alone. They cite Philippe Burrin's dating of the decisive turn to late 1941, tied to the failure of the German drive on Moscow. The entry of the United States into the war (Hitler declared war on the US on 11 December 1941, reading it as a "Jewish" power) heightened the "racial-global" frame in which the regime cast the killing.

**Machinery.** Dedicated extermination camps began operating in the General Government (occupied Poland) in spring 1942. Auschwitz, roughly 30 miles west of Cracow, combined the extermination function with forced-labor branches operated by Krupp and IG Farben, integrating genocide with the slave-labor war economy.

**Wannsee.** At the Wannsee Conference (20 January 1942), Reinhard Heydrich coordinated the bureaucratic machinery of the "Final Solution" across the relevant ministries and agencies. Heydrich's own words, quoted verbatim by Paxton and Hessler: the survivors of the harshest labor conditions would have to be dealt with, since they would represent "natural selection" and, if released, would constitute "the germ cell of a new Jewish revival."

**Geography of survival.** Survival rates varied sharply by country, reflecting the presence or absence of a native administrative buffer between the SS and the Jewish population. Poland saw the most complete destruction. In the Netherlands and Belgium, more than 75% of Jews were killed, with local police cooperation identified as decisive in enabling German round-ups. Italy's deportation rate was markedly lower, at 16%. Denmark mounted a near-total rescue, ferrying its Jewish population to safety in Sweden. In France, roughly 25% of the Jewish population was deported — a rate Paxton and Hessler attribute in part to active Vichy assistance, which they argue made the French toll higher than it would have been had the Germans been left to act alone in France (see [[vichy-shield-thesis]] below). Hungary and Bulgaria, for a time, blocked the deportation of their native-born Jews while permitting the deportation of foreign-born Jews on their territory. Throughout occupied Europe, the Jewish Councils (Judenräte) forced into administering ghettos faced what Paxton and Hessler call "excruciating choices" between limited cooperation and complete abdication.

**Warsaw ghetto uprising.** The uprising began on 19 April 1943, the eve of Passover, triggered by the ghetto's third major deportation roundup. SS losses ran as high as 700 killed and wounded; roughly 50,000 Jews were killed in suppressing the revolt. SS commander Jürgen Stroop dynamited the Great Synagogue on 16 May 1943 to mark the uprising's end, reporting: "The Jewish quarter of Warsaw is no more." The Western Allies, informed of the killing at Auschwitz and urged to bomb the rail lines feeding it, declined to do so.

**Toll.** Paxton and Hessler give a total of more than 5 million Jews killed by 1945 — roughly two-thirds of prewar European Jewry — and note that Roma victims were killed alongside Jews in the same extermination process. **Note on figures:** this page's other sections (Evans, Payne) converge on a total closer to ~6 million; Paxton and Hessler's ">5 million, two-thirds of prewar European Jewry" figure sits at the lower end of the range published across Holocaust scholarship. This is treated here as source variation in a genuinely difficult tally (complicated by incomplete records, contested regional counts, and differing cutoff dates), not as a factual contradiction between sources.

## The documentary record as first assembled — Shirer (1960)

Shirer's ch. 27 ("The New Order") was the first mass-audience synthesis of the perpetrator documents, written from the Nuremberg record while most participants were alive; it remains valuable for its verbatim documentary payload and for marking the 1960 state of knowledge. Distinctive holdings:

- **The no-written-order problem, stated in 1960**: the "Fuehrer Order on the Final Solution" "apparently was never committed to paper — at least no copy of it has yet been unearthed"; all evidence pointed to verbal transmission to Göring, Himmler and Heydrich in summer–fall 1941 (Lammers at Nuremberg: "I knew that a Fuehrer order was transmitted by Goering to Heydrich"). Shirer thus anticipated the evidentiary terrain of the later intentionalist–functionalist debate, while himself reading the January 1939 "prophecy" as a literal blueprint.
- **Göring's 31 July 1941 directive** to Heydrich ("a total solution [Gesamtlösung]… the intended final solution [Endlösung] of the Jewish question") — with Shirer's note that a Nuremberg mistranslation ("desired solution") let Göring deny under Jackson's cross-examination that he had ever used the term.
- **Einsatzgruppen arithmetic from the reports themselves**: Ohlendorf's courtroom "Ninety thousand!"; Stahlecker's Group A report of 229,052 killed by 31 January 1942 with its coffin-symbol map (Lithuania 136,421; Estonia "Jew-free"); the one-day liquidation of the Minsk ghetto's remaining 16,200 (October 1942); Himmler's November 1942 report to Hitler of 363,211 Jews killed August–October; the Korherr report (23 March 1943) of 633,300 "resettled" in Russia — which Shirer, adding ~100,000 for 1943–45, judged the soundest total for the shooting operations. He noted the SS habit of inflating figures "to please Himmler and Hitler" against Eichmann's 2-million claim.
- **The gas-van genesis**: Himmler's near-collapse at a demonstration shooting of a hundred prisoners at Minsk (Bach-Zelewski's account, 31 August 1941) leading to the van order for women and children; Dr. Becker's service correspondence on "correct adjustment of the levers."
- **The Graebe affidavit** (Dubno, 5 October 1942), read by Shawcross at Nuremberg and reproduced by Shirer in full — the family groups at the pit, the old woman with the year-old child, the girl who said "twenty-three years old" — still among the most-cited eyewitness texts of the shooting Holocaust.
- **Hoess's affidavit** on Auschwitz: commissioned June 1941; the Treblinka comparison (carbon monoxide, 200-person chambers) against Zyklon B (2,000-person chambers, "three to fifteen minutes… We knew when the people were dead because their screaming stopped"); the two SS doctors making "spot decisions" at the siding; "children of tender years were invariably exterminated"; the Waldsee postcards, the BATHS signs, the inmate girls' orchestra playing operetta at the selections; 250,000–300,000 Hungarian Jews killed in 46 days in summer 1944, the crematoria (built by I. A. Topf and Sons) falling behind the gas chambers.
- **Commerce of extermination**: the Topf and C. H. Kori crematorium correspondence ("full satisfaction in practice… Heil Hitler!"); Tesch & Stabenow and Degesch as Zyklon B suppliers (Tesch and Weinbacher hanged by a British court, 1946); the Krupp fuse factory at Auschwitz; the "Max Heiliger" Reichsbank account for dental gold and valuables under the Himmler–Funk agreement, the Berlin pawnshop "overwhelmed" by early 1944.
- **1960 tolls, for calibration**: Nuremberg indictment 5.7m; Eichmann (via SS witnesses) 5–6m; Reitlinger 4.19–4.58m; Auschwitz then bracketed between Hoess's 2.5m+ and Reitlinger's ~900k (modern consensus ~1.1m sits inside Shirer's spread). Shirer: of ~10 million Jews in Hitler's reach in 1939, "nearly half" were murdered.

Shirer's framing sentence — the New Order was destroyed "not by any revolt of the German people against such a reversion to barbarism but by the defeat of German arms" — belongs with [[ordinary-germans-and-the-holocaust]]; his Vienna-gutter-origins causal reduction is the 1960 intentionalist reading, superseded by the process accounts above. Source: [[shirer-rise-fall-third-reich-1960]].

## The Camps and the Holocaust (Wachsmann, *KL*, 2015)

Wachsmann's history of the [[nazi-concentration-camps|concentration-camp system]]
sharpens how the KL fit into the genocide. He distinguishes the **single-purpose
Operation Reinhard death camps** (Bełżec, Sobibór, Treblinka — run by Globocnik's
Lublin office with T-4 "euthanasia" veterans under Christian Wirth, using carbon
monoxide, ~1.5 million dead in 1942) from the two SS concentration camps that
*also* became death camps under the WVHA: [[auschwitz|Auschwitz]] and
[[majdanek|Majdanek]]. Key correctives:

- **Auschwitz was not built for the Holocaust.** Its Zyklon B gas chamber was
  invented in autumn 1941 to murder Soviet POWs and infirm prisoners; its
  genocidal role emerged gradually in 1942 and became central only in 1943 as
  the Reinhard camps wound down. Selection on the ramp spared, on average, only
  ~20% of arriving Jews for labour.
- **"Jews into the KL."** After the [[wannsee-conference-1942|Wannsee
  Conference]] (which did not put the camps on the agenda), Himmler on 26 Jan
  1942 ordered Jews sent to the KL as slave-labour substitutes for the dead
  Soviet POWs — the trigger for mass deportations to Auschwitz and Majdanek and
  for [[annihilation-through-labor|"annihilation through labour"]].
- **Precedents of the mechanics.** The structural elements of industrialized
  murder — deportation straight to death, transport schedules, camouflage,
  poison gas, purpose-built crematoria, gold-tooth extraction — were pioneered
  in the KL against invalids (Action 14f13) and Soviet POWs in 1941, though
  Wachsmann insists these were **not a dress rehearsal** for the Holocaust.
- **Majdanek and Aktion Erntefest.** The largest single massacre at any SS
  camp: ~18,000 Jews shot at Majdanek on 3 November 1943.

Source: [[wachsmann-kl-concentration-camps-2015]].
