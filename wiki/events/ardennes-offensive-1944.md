# The Ardennes Offensive (December 1944 – January 1945)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: Hitler's last western gamble, launched 16 December 1944 under the cover of weather that grounded Allied air power — 200,000 men, 600 tanks, and 1,900 guns against 80,000 defenders, advancing 65 miles toward the Meuse before the fuel ran out. American armor held by Christmas Eve; 5,000 Allied aircraft joined once the skies cleared; Montgomery (over-cautious, in Evans's account) failed to pinch off the salient while Patton counter-attacked from the south. The supporting air strike of 1 January 1945 destroyed as many German planes as Allied (~280 each), and the Alsace subsidiary attack failed. On 17 December 1944 SS panzer troops murdered American POWs at **Malmedy**. Each side lost ~700 armored vehicles and ~70,000–80,000 men — losses the Americans replaced and the Germans could not. Hitler pulled back the main force on 3 January and returned to Berlin by train on the 15th, telling his adjutant: "I know the war is lost . . . We'll not capitulate. Never. We can go down. But we'll take a world with us."

## Historiography

Evans's account follows the post-archival consensus: the offensive was strategically hopeless from conception (his generals said so at the 3 November 1944 briefing), and its chief effect was to strip the eastern front of reserves before the January Soviet offensive; the "what if Antwerp fell" counterfactual finds little scholarly support.
