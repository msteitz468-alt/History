# Pequot War (1636–1637)

## Narrative

Combined [[mohawk]] triumph in the Hudson trade and English immigration transformed southern New England in the 1630s. By 1633, ~3,000 European settlers overwhelmed ~200 Massachusetts and Pawtuckets. Smallpox 1633–34 claimed most of these and many western Natives, undermining fur/wampum traffic.

[[pequot]] were resented for controlling lower Connecticut trade by [[narragansett]], Mohegans, and Dutch. They allied with Massachusetts Bay, which extracted tribute, deference, and land; Connecticut was founded where Pequots had recently dominated. When Pequots balked at tribute and at blame for English deaths, Massachusetts and Connecticut with Mohegans and Narragansetts launched a surprise predawn attack on **Mystic village**: “In all, several hundred villagers of all ages and both sexes were killed. The remaining Pequots were hunted down; those whose lives were spared were put under the supervision of Narragansetts and Mohegans, or enslaved by the English.” A few who sought Mohawk support with wampum were killed by Mohawks already gifted more by Narragansetts.

**Result:** Pequot autonomy crushed; English secure on Connecticut; Narragansetts became the primary wampum source for Mohawks—feeding [[beaver-wars-17th-century]] circuits.

## Consequence Analysis

- produced: mass killing at Mystic; enslavement and supervised subordination of survivors; end of Pequot political autonomy
- produced: English security on Connecticut; Narragansett–Mohawk wampum–fur linkage
- enabled: mid-century New England clientage pattern (Narragansett as tense English clients supplying wampum)
- contributed_to: structural conditions later feeding [[king-philips-war-1675]] (Mohegans and residual Pequots as English allies)
- part_of: [[eastern-woodlands-colonial-encounter-1600-1783]]; Indian enslavement as institutional feature (Salisbury)

## Actors

[[pequot]]; Massachusetts Bay and Connecticut; [[narragansett]] and Mohegan co-attackers; [[mohawk]] (killed fugitives seeking support); Dutch commercial background.

## Historiography

Long a centerpiece of New England colonial mythology (Puritan “just war”); modern ethnohistory stresses massacre of noncombatants, enslavement, and inter-Native politics (Narragansett/Mohegan/Mohawk). Salisbury integrates the war into the continental wampum–fur system rather than treating it as a local New England episode only. See also Jennings *Invasion of America*; Salisbury’s own southern New England work in the bibliographic essay.
