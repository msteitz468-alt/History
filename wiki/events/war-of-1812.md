# War of 1812

U.S.-British war (1812–1815) often called the "Second War of Independence." Triggered by maritime rights disputes and frontier pressures. Inconclusive militarily (burning of Washington, successful defense of Baltimore, Jackson's victory at New Orleans after peace signed) but politically transformative for the young republic.

See: [[james-monroe]], [[declaration-of-independence-1776]], [[long-19th-century]].

## Historiography

The war is documented through U.S. and British official and military records, congressional debates (including the "War Hawks'" speeches), and Native American oral tradition and later ethnohistory concerning Tecumseh's confederacy, though the Native American side remains comparatively underrepresented relative to American and British sources. Historians continue to debate the war's causation and significance: an older U.S. nationalist historiography emphasized maritime rights and impressment as the war's causes and celebrated it as a "second war of independence," while other historians have stressed American expansionist ambitions toward Canada and Native lands in the Old Northwest as at least equally important drivers. The war's near-total omission from British historical memory (as a minor theater of the Napoleonic Wars) versus its prominent place in American and Canadian national memory is itself a notable historiographical asymmetry.
