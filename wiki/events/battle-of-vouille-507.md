# Battle of Vouillé (507)

The decisive battle, fought at **Vouillé northwest of Poitiers in 507**, at which
[[clovis|Clovis]]'s Franks defeated the [[visigoths|Visigoths]] and killed their king
**Alaric II**, shattering Visigothic power in Gaul and leaving the [[merovingian-franks|Franks]]
masters of nearly all the country between the Loire and the Pyrenees.

## Narrative

Clovis coordinated the campaign with **Burgundian contingents** and his Rhenish kinsman
Chloderic. "At Vouillé, northwest of Poitiers, the Goths were soundly defeated, Alaric II
killed." The expedition was timed against **Byzantine fleet movements off the Italian coast**,
which "effectively prevented [[theoderic-the-great|Theodoric the Ostrogoth]] from coming to the
aid of the Visigoths." In the following year the Frankish forces took the Gothic capital of
**Toulouse**, and "the Gothic presence north of the Pyrenees [was] reduced to a narrow stretch
of coastline as far east as Narbonne."

## Causal Analysis

- `contributed_to: [[clovis|Clovis's Catholic conversion]]` — Geary argues Clovis's orthodoxy
  "increased the likelihood that the Gallo-Roman aristocracies within these neighbouring kingdoms
  would be inclined to collaborate with him" against their Arian Visigothic rulers, giving the
  invasion a fifth column.
- `enabled: [[visigoths|the religious gulf within the Visigothic kingdom]]` — Arian rulers over a
  Catholic Gallo-Roman population.
- Diplomatic isolation of the Visigoths (Byzantine pressure on Theodoric) removed the one power
  able to relieve them.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the end of the Visigothic kingdom of **Toulouse**; the Visigothic centre of gravity
  shifted permanently into Spain.
- `enabled:` Frankish control of Aquitaine and the south, completing [[clovis|Clovis]]'s creation
  of a Gallic kingdom; shortly afterward the emperor **Anastasius** sent emissaries to Tours
  recognising Clovis as an honorary consul, who appeared in purple and placed a diadem on his head.
- Confirmed the strategic value of the Frankish embrace of **orthodox** Christianity over Gothic
  Arianism — a structural reason the Frankish kingdom outlasted its rivals.

## Actors

- [[clovis|Clovis]] · [[merovingian-franks|the Franks]] (with Burgundian allies)
- [[visigoths|Visigoths]] under Alaric II
- [[theoderic-the-great|Theodoric the Ostrogoth]] (prevented from intervening)

## Historiography

The narrative descends largely from [[gregory-of-tours|Gregory of Tours]], whose chronology of
Clovis's campaigns is notoriously compressed and partisan; the coordination with Byzantine naval
movement is Geary's reconstruction of the wider strategic context. See [[geary-before-france-germany-1988]].
