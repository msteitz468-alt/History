# The Korean War (1950–1953)

The **Korean War** was the first **hot war of the [[cold-war|Cold War]]** — a three-year
conflict on the divided Korean peninsula that drew in the United States (under UN
authority), the new People's Republic of **[[china|China]]**, and, behind the
scenes, the Soviet Union. It transformed the Cold War from a primarily European,
political-economic contest into a **global, militarized** confrontation. From
[[leffler-westad-cold-war-v1|*The Cambridge History of the Cold War*, Vol. I]].

## Narrative

Korea had been divided at the **38th parallel** in 1945 (Soviet occupation north, American
south), hardening into the rival regimes of **Kim Il Sung's** DPRK and **Syngman Rhee's**
ROK. Until late 1949 Stalin restrained Kim from invading, warning it would bring US
intervention. But after the Communist victory in China and the Soviet atomic test, **Stalin
changed his mind in January 1950** — tempted, Leffler and Zubok suggest, to recoup the
Soviet retreat in Europe, to gain a springboard against Japan, and to lock China against the
US — and gave Kim a qualified green light, conditional on Mao's support.

North Korea **invaded on 25 June 1950**, nearly overrunning the South. The US (with the UN
Security Council boycotted by the absent USSR) intervened; **MacArthur's** amphibious landing
at **Inchon** (September 1950) routed the North and drove its army back. Truman and Acheson
then made the fateful decision to **cross the 38th parallel** to unify the peninsula,
betting China would not intervene. The bet failed: as UN forces neared the **Yalu**, **China
entered massively** (October–November 1950), throwing them back. The front stabilized near
the original border, and the war ground on for two more years of attrition until the
**armistice of 27 July 1953** — leaving Korea **divided** along almost the same line, with
no peace treaty.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **division of Korea (1945)** and Kim Il Sung's ambition to reunify by
  force, enabled by **Stalin's January 1950 authorization** and Chinese backing.
- `contributed_to:` **[[containment]]** — the US read the invasion as a global test of
  resolve (a "second Munich") and as proof of the militarized vision of NSC-68.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **militarization of containment**: NSC-68 was funded, US defense spending
  roughly tripled, [[nato|NATO]] became an integrated military command with a US troop
  commitment to Europe, and West German and Japanese rearmament/recovery were accelerated.
- `produced:` the **permanent division of Korea** (the DMZ) and a generation-long
  **Sino-American freeze** (Beijing and Washington as direct battlefield enemies; the US
  shielding Taiwan with the Seventh Fleet).
- A vast human cost (several million dead, mostly Korean civilians) and the entrenchment of
  Kim Il Sung's regime.

## Actors

- **North Korea** (Kim Il Sung) and **South Korea** (Syngman Rhee); the **United States** and
  the UN coalition; the **People's Republic of China** ([[mao-zedong|Mao Zedong]]); and the
  **Soviet Union** (Stalin) behind the scenes. See [[united-states]], [[china]], [[containment]].

## Historiography

Once read through the lens of monolithic Communist aggression directed from Moscow, the war
is now understood — with the opening of Soviet and Chinese archives — as a **conflict with
deep Korean roots** that Kim drove, Stalin reluctantly enabled, and Mao felt compelled to
join. Debate continues over whether the US decision to cross the parallel (turning a war of
containment into one of rollback) was the war's gravest error.
