# Siege of Kazan

## Narrative
The **Siege of Kazan** took place in late summer 1552 and resulted in the storming and capture of the capital of the Kazan Khanate by the forces of Tsar Ivan IV (the Terrible) (L2088-2090). 

In 1551, the Russians launched an offensive, conquering vassal tribes of the Khanate and constructing the fortress of Sviiazhsk near Kazan to serve as a base of operations (L2088). In 1552, as the main army advanced, the Crimean Tartars launched a diversionary invasion toward Moscow, but were checked and forced to withdraw (L2088). 

Tsar Ivan IV's troops subsequently surrounded Kazan by land and water (L2090). After a bitter six-week siege, Russian forces successfully stormed the city on October 2, 1552, utilizing powder to blow up sections of the stone walls (L2090). Key commanders in the assault included Prince Michael Vorotynsky and Prince Andrew Kurbsky, who led the first units into the city (L2090). It took another five years of local fighting to establish complete Russian rule over the entire territory of the former Khanate (L2090).

## Causal Analysis
The siege was caused by:
* **Border Security:** Muscovy suffered from frequent, large-scale raids by the Tartar armies of Kazan, Astrakhan, and the Crimea, which devastated the southeastern frontier and drained state finances to maintain defense lines (L2088).
* **Volga Control:** The Muscovite state sought to control the entire course of the Volga River to secure commercial trade routes to the Caspian Sea and Persia (L2090, L2512).
* **Military Reforms:** The campaign was enabled by Ivan IV's recent military reforms, which standardized gentry service, placed new emphasis on artillery and engineering, and added regular musketeer (*streltsy*) regiments to the army (L2082-2086).

## Consequence Analysis
The capture of Kazan produced:
* **Imperial Expansion:** The conquest marked the first time Muscovy annexed a non-Russian, Islamic state, transforming the grand duchy into a multi-ethnic empire (L2048, L2792).
* **Further Annexations:** It directly enabled the conquest of Astrakhan in 1554–1556, securing the entire Volga trade route (L2090).
* **Siberian Conquest:** The subjugation of Kazan removed the buffer state between Muscovy and the Ural Mountains, enabling the Stroganov family and Ermak to launch the conquest of Siberia in 1582 (L2138).

## Actors
* **Tsar Ivan IV:** Personally led the campaign, consolidating his title as "Tsar" and "Autocrat" (L2048, L2088).
* **Prince Andrew Kurbsky:** A leading commander who broke into the city, later a critic of the Tsar (L2090, L2116).
* **Prince Michael Vorotynsky:** Acclaimed hero of the siege who led the final assault (L2090).
