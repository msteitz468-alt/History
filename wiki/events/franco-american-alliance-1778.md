# The Franco-American Alliance (1778)

The treaties of **Alliance** and of **Amity and Commerce**, signed in Paris on **6 February
1778** between the Continental Congress's commissioners and the France of **Louis XVI**, which
turned the **[[american-revolution|American Revolution]]** from a colonial rebellion into a
global war. France recognized the United States and entered a defensive military alliance,
prompting Britain's declaration of war on France on 17 March 1778.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the American victory at **[[battles-of-saratoga-1777|Saratoga]]** (October 1777),
  which convinced Versailles the rebels could win and was the proximate "catalyst in negotiations
  for French intervention."
- `contributed_to:` **Benjamin Franklin**'s diplomacy in Paris, and France's strategic hunger to
  "regain power and influence they lost in the [[seven-years-war-1756|Seven Years' War]]."

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the entry of France — and soon Spain (1779) and the Dutch Republic — into the war,
  "severely overstretch[ing]" British resources across the Caribbean, Gibraltar, India, and home
  waters, and forcing Britain onto the strategic defensive in America.
- `produced:` the decisive instrument of victory — the **French navy**, whose appearance "completely
  changed the nature of the war." Conway: "the vital ingredient … without their presence and victory
  over the Royal Navy, [[siege-of-yorktown-1781|Yorktown]] would not have been possible."
- `enabled:` the near-bankruptcy of France that helped trigger the [[french-revolution-1789|French
  Revolution]].

## Actors

The **United States** (Franklin) and **France** (Louis XVI, later Rochambeau and de Grasse in the
field) against **Great Britain**. [[lafayette|Lafayette]] became the alliance's human symbol.
