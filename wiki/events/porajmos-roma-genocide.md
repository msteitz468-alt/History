# The Porajmos (1938–1945)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: the Nazi persecution and mass killing of Europe's Roma and Sinti.

## Narrative

Himmler spoke of a "final solution of the Gypsy question" as early as December 1938; Robert Ritter's Reich Health Office team racially registered 23,822 German and Austrian "Gypsies and part-Gypsies" by March 1944 — many already dead by the project's completion. Deportations to the General Government began in May 1940 (~2,500). Killing began in earnest in 1942: 5,000 Burgenland Roma confined in a sealed section of the Łódź ghetto were gassed at Chełmno (over half of them children); the [[einsatzgruppen]] shot Roma across the occupied USSR as "asocials" and "saboteurs" (Task Force D reported the Crimea "free of Gypsies" by March 1942); in Serbia Böhme's hostage-shooting ratios consumed Roma men wholesale, and the women and children were gassed at Sajmište; the Ustashe murdered most of Croatia's Roma (see [[ustasha-genocide-1941]]); Antonescu deported ~25,000 Romanian Roma to Transnistria, where only about half survived.

Himmler's decree of 16 December 1942 sent Germany's Roma to the **Auschwitz "Gypsy family camp"** — ~20,000 arrivals including decorated war veterans and even part-Roma Nazi Party members, held in the camp's worst conditions. The first liquidation attempt (May 1944) was abandoned when the forewarned inmates armed themselves and refused to leave; on **2 August 1944** the camp was destroyed, ~3,000 gassed. Auschwitz's Roma dead exceed 20,000; Höss called them, "unbelievably" (Evans), "my best-loved prisoners." In the Reich itself, policy after 1943 ran through sterilization (2,000–2,500 during the war) — what Evans calls a "delayed genocide."

## Historiography

Classification is genuinely contested, and this page does not adjudicate. **Evans's own position** is that Roma were persecuted primarily as "asocial" and criminal rather than as an existential racial enemy, and that this "does not make the mass murder of German and European Gypsies a genocide in the same way as the mass murder of German and European Jews was." **Against this**, scholars such as Ian Hancock and much of the Romani-studies literature argue the racial-biological registration (Ritter), the family-camp deportations, and the 2 August 1944 liquidation constitute genocide on any definition, and that the "asociality" framing reproduces the perpetrators' categories. West Germany recognized the genocide officially only in 1982. Total European Roma deaths are estimated anywhere from ~200,000 to 500,000; the documentation is far thinner than for the Jewish Holocaust — itself a legacy of non-recognition.
