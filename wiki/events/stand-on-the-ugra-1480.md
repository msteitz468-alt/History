# Stand on the Ugra River
## Narrative
In **1480 CE**, a major military standoff took place on the opposite banks of the **Ugra River** (a tributary of the Oka River) between the Muscovite forces of Grand Prince **Ivan III** and the army of the Golden Horde under **Khan Ahmad**. 

After decades of decreasing Muscovite compliance, Ivan III publicly renounced all allegiance to the Golden Horde and discontinued the payment of tribute. In response, Khan Ahmad mobilized an all-out punitive expedition, forming a military alliance with King Casimir IV of Poland-Lithuania. 

Ivan III aligned with Mengli-Geray, the Crimean Khan, and deployed his forces along the Ugra River crossings to block the Mongol advance. The two armies reached the opposite banks and remained there facing each other. 

The Mongols failed to cross the river before the Russian forces arrived. Furthermore, the expected Polish-Lithuanian reinforcements failed to join Ahmad, as Casimir IV had to concentrate his forces to repel a massive Crimean Tartar raid into Lithuania. When the Ugra River froze, which would have allowed the Mongol cavalry to cross, the Russian forces began to withdraw to defensive positions. However, the Mongols suddenly broke camp and fled back into the steppe, apparently frightened by a surprise Russian-Tatar raid on their home capital of Sarai. The standoff ended without a major pitched battle (l. 1571-1575).

## Causal Analysis
* **Renunciation of Tribute:** Ivan III's formal and public refusal to pay tribute to the Golden Horde precipitated the conflict.
* **Crimean Alliance:** The Russian alliance with the Crimean Khanate successfully neutralized the Polish-Lithuanian coalition by launching a diversionary raid into Casimir IV's territories.
* **Capital Raid:** A coordinated Russian-Tatar naval raid down the Volga to attack the Horde’s capital, Sarai, threatened Ahmad’s rear, forcing his sudden retreat.

## Consequence Analysis
* **End of the Mongol Yoke:** The stand marks the formal and final end of 240 years of Mongol domination over the Russian lands.
* **Sovereign Autocracy:** The event established the full sovereign independence of Muscovite Russia, enabling Ivan III to assume the titles of Tsar and Autocrat (*gosudar*).
* **Collapse of the Horde:** Shortly after his retreat, Khan Ahmad was killed in internal strife, and the Golden Horde itself dissolved around 1500 CE.

## Actors
* **Ivan III:** The Grand Prince of Moscow who led the Russian forces.
* **Khan Ahmad:** The last effective ruler of the Golden Horde who launched the expedition.
* **Mengli-Geray:** The Crimean Khan who supported Moscow and distracted the Polish-Lithuanian forces.

## Historiography
In Russian historical tradition, the Stand on the Ugra River represents the peaceful, almost miraculous lifting of the "Tatar yoke," contrasting sharply with the bloody conflicts of the past. Historians emphasize that by 1480, the shift in political, financial, and military power from the steppe to the forest was so complete that the Horde could no longer enforce its suzerainty.
