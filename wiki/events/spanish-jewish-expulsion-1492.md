# Spanish Expulsion of the Jews (1492)

## Narrative

Three months after the **[[fall-of-granada-1492|fall of Granada]]**, the triumphant **Catholic Monarchs** ordered the **expulsion of all Jews** from their kingdoms by decree at **Granada, 31 March 1492**. [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]] states the terms without softening: Jews had **three months** to leave **without gold, silver, horses, or arms**, or to convert. Many converted, including the **chief rabbi**; others left in a new exodus—“their numbers impossible to know but no doubt in the **tens of thousands**.” Sincerity of **conversos** would incur **Inquisition** suspicion. The decree sits in a longer pattern of medieval expulsions (Gascony 1289, England 1290) and was followed by **Portugal 1497**.

Logan places the expulsion among 1492 events chosen as a pedagogical end-point of his medieval church survey—not as the true end of the Middle Ages (“historical heresy”) but as a year whose events reflect the past and portend the future, amid confessional triumphalism after the completion of the [[reconquista|Reconquista]].

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` royal policy of religious uniformity after military triumph over Muslim Granada.
- `contributed_to:` crusading and messianic ideology of the Catholic Monarchs; pre-existing anti-Jewish pressures and converso anxiety in late-medieval Iberia.
- `preceded_by: [[fall-of-granada-1492]]` (2 January 1492 surrender; 31 March expulsion decree).
- `analogous_to:` earlier expulsions from England (1290) and Gascony (1289); Portugal (1497).

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` mass conversion under duress and a forced diaspora of tens of thousands; long-term Sephardic communities across the Mediterranean and beyond.
- `produced:` intensified Inquisition scrutiny of converted Jews (*conversos*) whose sincerity was doubted.
- `concurrent_with:` Columbus’s voyage (Logan’s epilogue pairs Granada, expulsion, Savonarola’s Florence, Alexander VI’s election, and the Caribbean landing as 1492’s cluster).
- `part_of:` the closing of medieval Iberian multi-confessional political space and the opening of early modern confessional states.

## Actors

- **Ferdinand and Isabella** — issuers of the Granada decree.
- **Spanish Jewish communities** — forced to convert or leave stripped of portable wealth and arms.
- **Chief rabbi** — among those who converted (per Logan).
- **Inquisition** — later instrument of suspicion against conversos.

## Historiography

Numbers of expellees remain debated (Logan refuses precision beyond “tens of thousands”). Relationship to the 1391 massacres, the *conversos*, and the Inquisition’s foundation (1478) is the standard scholarly frame; Logan’s contribution is brief, epilogue-level, and unsentimental. Full fidelity to the decree’s terms (no gold/silver/horses/arms; convert or leave) is required: the event is not to be euphemized as mere “emigration policy.”
