# The Battle of Carrhae (53 BCE)

The catastrophic defeat in which **[[crassus|Crassus]]'** invasion of **Parthia** was
destroyed in the Mesopotamian desert — one of Rome's worst military disasters, the
death of the triumvirate's third man, and a wound to Roman prestige that lasted
generations. Treated in [[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]] Ch. 10 (Wiseman).

## Narrative

Granted **Syria** in his second consulship (55), the aging **Crassus** sought the
conquest of Parthia (and perhaps, like his son, Bactria and India) to **rival the
glory of Pompey and Caesar**. He left Rome under a tribune's formal curse; plundered
the temples of **Jerusalem and Hierapolis** to finance the war; and in spring **53**
led some seven legions across the Euphrates at **Zeugma**.

Misled by the Arab dynast **Abgar of Osrhoene** (who betrayed him) and abandoned by
the Armenian king **Artavasdes**, Crassus marched into open country near **Carrhae**,
where the Parthian general the **Surenas** met him with **mailed cataphracts and
horse-archers** — a style of war the legions could not answer. His son **Publius**,
breaking out with the Gallic cavalry, was surrounded and killed, his head paraded on
a spear. The legions held out a day but were broken; the survivors fled to Carrhae
and piecemeal to Syria. **Thirty thousand men did not return.** Crassus himself was
killed under a false truce; his head and hand were sent to **King Orodes** in Armenia,
and a prisoner impersonating him was paraded into Seleucia "in derisive triumph," his
mouth (legend said) filled with molten gold.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: **Crassus' ambition** for military glory equal to his partners' — an
**unprovoked war of conquest** — compounded by **strategic blunders** (trusting
Abgar, marching into open desert against cavalry).

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the **destruction of a Roman army** and Crassus' death; a **lasting Roman
trauma** and the establishment of **Parthia** as the Republic's formidable eastern
rival on the Euphrates.
enabled: the **collapse of the [[first-triumvirate|First Triumvirate]]** — with
Crassus gone (and **Julia** dead the year before), nothing balanced **Caesar** and
**Pompey**, who slid toward [[roman-civil-war-49-45bce|civil war]] (Cycle 6).

## Historiography

**Plutarch's *Crassus*** (the fullest narrative) and **Cassius Dio**; Wiseman
follows Sherwin-White on the campaign. The moralizing tradition reads Carrhae as the
just punishment of avarice and impiety (the plundered temples) — Crassus the type of
the greed that destroyed the Republic.
