# The Voyages of Zheng He (1405–1433)

The **voyages of Zheng He** were seven great state-sponsored maritime expeditions sent by the
[[ming-dynasty|Ming]] **Yongle Emperor** across the Indian Ocean — the largest wooden fleets the
world had yet seen, reaching Southeast Asia, India, Arabia, and the **East African coast** decades
before the Portuguese. Their launch and then **abandonment** make them a classic case in the debate
over why China did not pursue overseas expansion. From the [[cambridge-history-of-china|Cambridge
History of China]] (Ming volumes).

## Narrative

Between **1405 and 1433** the Ming dispatched seven armadas under the command of **Zheng He**, a
Muslim eunuch admiral from Yunnan. The "treasure fleets" were vast — hundreds of vessels and tens
of thousands of men, the largest "treasure ships" far bigger than contemporary European craft. They
sailed to **Champa, Java, Melaka, Sri Lanka, Calicut**, the **Persian Gulf (Hormuz)**, Aden, and
the **Swahili coast (Malindi, Mombasa)**, returning with envoys, exotica (including a giraffe), and
the submission of rulers to the Ming **tribute system**. The voyages projected Ming prestige,
intervened in Indian Ocean politics (installing rulers, suppressing piracy at Palembang), and
enrolled distant states as tributaries — but they sought **prestige and recognition, not conquest
or commercial empire**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **Yongle Emperor's** drive to legitimize a usurped throne and to display Ming
  grandeur by drawing the maritime world into the tribute order.
- `contributed_to:` China's inherited **shipbuilding and navigational capacity** (from Song and
  Yuan maritime trade), which made fleets of this scale possible.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` a brief, spectacular extension of Ming **prestige and the tribute network** across the
  Indian Ocean — but **no colonies, forts, or lasting trade monopolies**.
- `produced:` after Yongle's death, **abandonment**: the voyages were halted, the records partly
  destroyed, and the Ming turned to continental defence and **maritime restriction (haijin)** that
  forbade private overseas trade.
- The contrast with the **Portuguese** entry into the same ocean a few decades later (small,
  armed, commercial, permanent) is a staple of the **"why not China?"** / [[great-divergence|Great
  Divergence]] debate.

## Actors

The **Ming dynasty** under the **Yongle Emperor** and admiral **Zheng He**, engaging the polities
of the Indian Ocean rim — including the [[swahili-coast|Swahili city-states]] of East Africa. See
[[ming-dynasty]] and [[indian-ocean-trade]].

## Historiography

The CHC and recent scholarship stress that the voyages were **state-prestige and tribute
operations**, not the seed of an aborted Chinese age of discovery; their cancellation reflected
court politics (Confucian-official hostility to costly eunuch-run enterprises and the priority of
the northern frontier), not a failure of capacity. They figure large in debates about Chinese vs.
European expansion and in modern Chinese maritime memory.
