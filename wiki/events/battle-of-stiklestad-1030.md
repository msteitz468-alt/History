# Battle of Stiklestad

The battle (traditionally 29 July 1030) in the Trøndelag at which the exiled king
[[olaf-haraldsson|Olaf Haraldsson]], returning to reclaim [[norway|Norway]], was
defeated and killed by a coalition of Norwegian magnates acting in the interest of
[[cnut-the-great|Cnut the Great]].

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: [[olaf-haraldsson|Olaf's exile and bid for return]] · contributed_to:
[[cnut-the-great|Cnut's]] support of Olaf's enemies and magnate hostility to royal
centralisation and Christianization.

## Consequence Analysis

Militarily a defeat, Stiklestad became a spiritual and political victory:
miracles reported at Olaf's grave led to his swift canonisation, and the **cult of
St Olaf** at Nidaros made the dead king the patron and *Rex Perpetuus* of Norway.
Within a few years Danish overlordship collapsed and Olaf's line was restored —
making Stiklestad a foundational moment of the Norwegian national monarchy and of
[[christianization-of-scandinavia|Norwegian Christianity]].

## Historiography

The main narrative is Snorri Sturluson's 13th-century *Heimskringla*, written two
centuries later and shaped by the developed saint's cult; the precise date,
numbers, and course are uncertain. See [[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]].
