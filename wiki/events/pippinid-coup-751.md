# The Pippinid Coup of 751

## Narrative

In 751 **[[pippin-iii|Pippin III]]**, mayor of the palace and effective ruler of the Franks,
deposed the last **Merovingian** king, the powerless Childeric III, and was elevated to the
kingship himself — anointed by the Frankish bishops and his act endorsed by **Pope Zacharias**.
Childeric was tonsured and confined to a monastery. Three years later, in 754, **Pope Stephen II**
crossed the Alps in person and re-anointed Pippin together with his sons [[charlemagne|Charles]]
and Carloman, declaring that future Frankish kings must come from this family alone. The coup ended
the Merovingian dynasty that had ruled the Franks since Clovis and founded the **Carolingian**
royal line.

## Causal Analysis

The deposition was the constitutional ratification of a power shift long since accomplished on the
ground. Since [[charles-martel]], the Pippinids had monopolised military leadership, treasure, and
king-making while the Merovingians reigned without ruling.

- `caused_by:` [[carolingian-empire|the accumulated dominance of the Pippinid/Arnulfing family]]
- `contributed_to:` Frankish church reform under [[boniface]], which burnished Carolingian religious
  legitimacy
- `enabled:` [[the-papacy-eighth-ninth-centuries|the papacy's search for a northern protector]] against
  Lombard pressure on Rome — though NCMH II stresses Rome *endorsed* rather than *enabled* the act

NCMH II (Fouracre) cautions that "no event of the eighth century has been more intensively explained,"
and warns against the tidy later narrative (Boniface anointing Pippin at papal command): Boniface's role
is uncertain and the deeper cause was a convergence of Frankish, Lombard, Byzantine, and papal interests.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the Carolingian royal dynasty and, in 754, the formal **Frankish–papal alliance**
- `produced:` the introduction of **royal anointing** — an Old Testament–flavoured sacral theory of
  Christian kingship — into the western political tradition
- `enabled:` Pippin's Italian campaigns and the **Donation of Pippin (756)**, founding the Papal State
- `preceded_by:` [[battle-of-tertry-687]] (the original Pippinid seizure of Frankish hegemony)
- `followed_by:` [[coronation-of-charlemagne-800]]

## Historiography

The sources for 751 and 754 were written under Pippin's descendants and work hard to justify a usurpation,
making motive and sequence difficult to recover. Noble's *The Republic of St Peter* is the standard guide
to the Franco-papal background. The relationship between the 751 episcopal anointing and the 754 papal
anointing — and which was decisive for Carolingian legitimacy — remains debated.
