# The Siege of Boston (1775–1776)

> **⚔️ Staff-college tactical analysis:** see **[[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/siege-of-boston|Siege of Boston — Operational Analysis]]** in the warfare hub for the full order of battle, the phased conduct of the action, and the principles-of-war assessment.

The eleven-month investment of British-held Boston that opened the **[[american-revolution|
Revolutionary War]]** and gave [[washington-george|Washington]] his first command. After
[[battles-of-lexington-and-concord-1775|Lexington and Concord]], New England militia penned General
Gage's garrison inside the city; the bloody check at [[battle-of-bunker-hill-1775|Bunker Hill]]
(June 1775) confirmed the stalemate.

## Narrative

### Occupied town and countryside (Schiff layer)

After Lexington, nearly half of Boston was missing; Gage sealed the town against attack. Associates sat in prison; [[adams-samuel|Samuel Adams]]’s son tried and failed to escape under an alias before succeeding later. Daily gunfire and cannonades; salt pork and beef; a selectman “invited… to dine upon rats.” The Old South became a riding school carpeted with dung (hog-sty pew, officers’ bar); the 129-year-old **Liberty Tree** was felled into fourteen cords of firewood. Gage: “I wish this cursed place was burned.” Fall 1774 prelude had already featured sabotage (bricks sunk, straw ignited, artisans vanished, 2,000 sheep gone, brass cannon stolen by night) and desertions as Gage’s reinforcements grew.

**Bunker Hill** (June 1775): British twice American casualties but killed **Joseph Warren** (35)—Adams’s closest friend and post-Massacre partner—by a shot to the head. Young Josiah Quincy died of tuberculosis returning from London.

### Continental command and evacuation

Washington arrived at Cambridge on 2 July 1775 to find a "ramshackle" provincial army with no
sanitation, drill, or supply system, and set about forging a true **[[continental-army|Continental
Army]]** — chains of command, discipline, and administration. Congress diverted him into the
disastrous winter invasion of Canada under **[[arnold-benedict|Benedict Arnold]]**, which "came to
grief before the walls of Quebec." The deadlock at Boston broke when **[[knox-henry|Henry Knox]]**
hauled the guns captured at Ticonderoga overland through the winter; on the night of 4–5 March 1776
the Americans seized and fortified **Dorchester Heights**, training artillery on the city. Perhaps
"reminded of Bunker Hill," [[howe-william|Howe]] declined to assault and evacuated — the last
British troops leaving on **17 March 1776**. Washington, who had wanted a bloody, war-shortening
victory, privately called the bloodless win a "disappointment."

Schiff on the city left behind: a heap of ruins, looting, furniture in the harbor, grassy streets and refuse, fences/steeples/wharves destroyed. Adams pronounced himself delighted with “the removal of the barbarians” / departure of what he termed “the rebel army,” and lobbied fortifications for the defenseless town. He never again slept at his Purchase Street home, uninhabitable on return.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the spontaneous **militia mobilization** after Lexington and Concord.
- `contributed_to:` Knox's artillery feat and the seizure of **Dorchester Heights**, which made the
  British position untenable.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **first major American strategic success** and the liberation of a major city.
- `produced:` a tested Continental Army and command structure that Washington carried into the
  [[battle-of-long-island-1776|New York campaign]].

## Historiography

Documented through Washington's extensive wartime correspondence (the single richest primary source for the Continental Army's early organization), congressional records, and British officers' accounts of the evacuation decision, the siege is a well-studied case in the historiography of army-building — how an ad hoc militia mass became a functioning military institution under Washington's administrative reforms. Historians differ on how much weight to give Washington's frustration at a "bloodless" resolution (he wanted a decisive battle) versus the strategic soundness of Howe's decision to avoid a second Bunker Hill; the Canadian expedition under Arnold, launched concurrently, is sometimes treated as a costly distraction and sometimes as a reasonable attempt to open a second front. Knox's artillery expedition is well documented and rarely disputed in its broad outline.

[[schiff-revolutionary-samuel-adams-2022|Schiff]] supplies the civilian/occupation texture (sealed town, Liberty Tree fuel, Old South riding school, rats, Adams’s family trapped or escaping) and treats Warren’s death as a personal as well as military hinge for the Boston leadership network.
