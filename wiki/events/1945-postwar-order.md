# 1945 and the Post-War Order

> **Transition page** (CLAUDE.md standing list: "1945 and the post-war order"). Marks
> the discontinuity between the [[world-wars-era|World Wars Era]] and the
> [[cold-war|Cold War]].

## Narrative

The end of the Second World War in **1945** inaugurated a new global order. In
[[mcneill-cwh-v7-2015|CWH VII]]'s reading it is a triple threshold: the **nuclear age**
(Hiroshima and Nagasaki — a "golden spike" candidate for the geological
[[anthropocene|Anthropocene]]); the **bipolar division** of the world between the
United States and the Soviet Union; and the eve of the **[[great-acceleration|Great
Acceleration]]** — the post-1950 surge in population, energy use, and economic output.
The 1945 settlement created the **United Nations** (and the [[world-health-organization|WHO]],
the Bretton Woods institutions) and set the stage for the worldwide wave of
[[decolonization]].

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the defeat of the Axis; the economic and military **exhaustion of the
  European imperial powers** (Duara stresses Britain's sterling crises); the
  ascendancy of the US and USSR as superpowers.
- `contributed_to:` the **atomic bomb**, which (Josephson notes) may have been used as
  much to demonstrate power as from military necessity.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the [[cold-war|Cold War]] bipolar order and the nuclear arms race.
- `enabled:` the rapid post-war **[[decolonization]]** (at least forty countries, a
  quarter of humanity, independent between 1945 and 1960); the internationalist
  institutions that made the [[smallpox-eradication|smallpox eradication]] possible;
  and the [[great-acceleration|Great Acceleration]] and the rapid diffusion of
  [[industrialization]] to East Asia.

## From Shirer, *The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich* (1960): the German surrender sequence

Admiral Dönitz's Flensburg "rump government" broadcast news of Hitler's death at 10:20 p.m. on 1 May 1945, falsely claiming he "fell for Germany" that afternoon "fighting to the last breath against Bolshevism" — a lie Shirer notes was necessary to preserve troop morale and the succession narrative; Dönitz publicly framed continued resistance as anti-Bolshevik. The surrenders followed in sequence: Italy, unconditional, 29 April 1945 (the news never reached Hitler, owing to the communications breakdown in the bunker); Northwest Germany, Denmark, and Holland to Montgomery, 4 May; Army Group G (1st and 19th Armies, south of the Alps) surrendered to Kesselring's opposite number, 5 May. Admiral Hans von Friedeburg arrived at Eisenhower's Reims headquarters on 5 May to negotiate; Jodl arrived the next day to help stall for time, moving German troops and refugees westward away from the Soviets, until Eisenhower threatened to close the entire Allied front to German refugees. Dönitz radioed full surrender authority to Jodl at 1:30 a.m. on 7 May.

Germany signed the unconditional surrender at Reims at 2:41 a.m., 7 May 1945 — signed for the Allies by General Walter Bedell Smith, witnessed by General Ivan Susloparov (USSR) and General François Sevez (France), and for Germany by Friedeburg and Jodl. Jodl's closing statement, quoted verbatim: "With this signature the German people and the German Armed Forces are, for better or worse, delivered into the hands of the victors... I can only express the hope that the victor will treat them with generosity" — met with no response from the Allied side; Shirer notes the parallel to the French general's similarly futile plea at the 1940 Compiègne surrender. Fighting formally ceased at midnight, 8–9 May 1945.

[SHIRER-THESIS] Shirer's own summary framing contrasts the totality of German collapse in 1945 with 1918: in 1918 "the Kaiser had fled, the monarchy had tumbled, but... a government chosen by the people had continued to function," whereas in 1945 "the Third Reich simply ceased to exist. There was no longer any German authority on any level" — the condition of total occupation and four-power control that structures the settlement covered on this page and on [[events/division-of-germany-1945-1949]].

Source: [[shirer-rise-fall-third-reich-1960]].

## Historiography

The postwar settlement is documented in conference protocols (Yalta, Potsdam) and the surrender instruments; interpretive debate centers on whether the division of Europe was agreed, improvised, or imposed (traditionalist vs. revisionist Cold War-origins historiography — see [[berlin-blockade-1948]] and the Cold War period page). Shirer's contribution here is the German-side surrender sequence from the captured record and his eyewitness Nuremberg coverage. Collection coverage: strong (Cambridge histories, Cold War surveys, Shirer).
