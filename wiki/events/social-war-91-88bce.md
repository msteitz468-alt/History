# The Social War (91–88 BCE)

The revolt of Rome's **Italian allies** (*socii*) to win Roman citizenship — the war
that the allies lost militarily but won politically, transforming Rome from a
city-state into the unified citizen-state of **Italy**. Treated in
[[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]] Ch. 4 (Gabba).

## Causes

The "allied question" (see [[roman-italian-alliance]]) reached its crisis through the
**Gracchan agrarian programme**, which threatened the *ager publicus* worked by
Italian elites and "gravely compromised" the long-closening ties between the Roman and
Italian upper classes. Over two generations the allies — increasingly assimilated in
law, culture, and prosperity, yet **subjects unable to share imperial power** and
still paying local taxes Romans had escaped — came to demand citizenship "in order to
be partners in empire instead of subjects." Roman exclusivism hardened: the
**Lex Licinia Mucia (95)** purged illegally-enrolled allies, "one of the principal
causes" of the war. The crisis came to a head with the tribune **M. Livius Drusus**
(91), whose programme (senatorial juries, land, and finally citizenship) won the
allies' hopes — and whose **murder** was "the signal for the outbreak of the revolt."

## Narrative

The rising began at **Asculum** (late 91) with the massacre of resident Romans. Most
of the central and southern Apennine peoples joined — **Marsi, Paeligni, Samnites,
Lucani**, Picenes, and others (the **Latin colonies stayed loyal**, save Venusia). The
rebels organised as a counter-Rome: a capital at **Corfinium** renamed **Italica**, a
federal senate of 500, two consuls and twelve praetors, and their own **coinage**
(the Sabellian bull goring the Roman wolf). They fielded ~100,000 men — trained in
Rome's own service.

The war (90–88) was hard-fought and bloody: rebel successes in Campania (Papius
Mutilus) and Picenum, Roman recovery under **Marius**, **Sulla**, and **Pompeius
Strabo** (the siege of Asculum). But Rome's imperial reserves were decisive — and so
was **concession**: facing an armed demand for citizenship, Rome granted it.

## The enfranchisement of Italy

- **Lex Iulia de civitate (90)** — citizenship to allies who had stayed loyal or laid
  down arms promptly (the Latins, then Etruscans/Umbrians), undercutting the revolt.
- **Lex Plautia Papiria (89)** — completing the incorporation, extending citizenship
  to *adscripti* domiciled in Italy who applied within sixty days (with the Samnites
  and Lucanians, still under arms, excepted — which merged the Social War into the
  civil wars).
- **Lex Pompeia (89)** — granting **Latin status** to the communities of Transpadane
  Gaul (full citizenship only in 49).

The new citizens were at first confined to a few extra **tribes** voting last (to
limit their weight) — itself a faultline of the 80s.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: [[roman-italian-alliance|the unresolved allies' question]] inflamed by the
[[agrarian-crisis-roman-republic|Gracchan land programme]] and Roman exclusivism;
trigger: the murder of Drusus.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the **enfranchisement of all Italy south of the Po** and the transformation
of the Roman state **from a city-state into a state of *municipia*** — Italy now "the
territory of the city of Rome," requiring a complete rethinking of how the state
functioned (the municipal charters, the *municipalization* of Italy).
enabled: the **civil wars of the 80s** (the intransigent Samnites/Lucanians carried
the fighting on into Sulla's war) and the integration of the Italian elites into the
Roman governing class — the demographic and political foundation of the late Republic
and Empire.

## Historiography

**Appian** (Book I of the *Civil Wars*, with Gabba's commentary) is the central
narrative, sympathetic to the allied cause; **Velleius** (himself of Italian
notable descent) thought the cause "fundamentally just." The chronology of Drusus'
laws and the exact content of the citizenship laws are much debated.
