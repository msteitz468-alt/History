# Tea Party Movement (2009–2010)

Populist conservative grassroots movement that emerged in 2009 in opposition to Barack Obama's economic stimulus, Affordable Care Act, and broader federal spending/tax policies following the 2008 financial crisis. Named after the 1773 Boston Tea Party.

## Narrative
Sparked by a February 2009 rant by CNBC's Rick Santelli on the floor of the Chicago Mercantile Exchange against "subsidizing losers' mortgages." Rapidly organized via social media, local groups, and national figures (Glenn Beck, Sarah Palin, Ron Paul influences). Major rallies on tax day 2009 and 2010; focused on limited government, lower taxes, debt reduction, and constitutional originalism.

## Causal Analysis
Contributed to by post-2008 bailout anger (TARP under Bush/Obama), stimulus package scale, ACA passage, and cultural/economic anxiety in middle America. Built on longer antitax and states-rights traditions (see Williamson tax history).

## Consequence Analysis
2010 midterm "shellacking" for Democrats, large Republican House gains, new cohort of fiscally hawkish (and often socially conservative) members who constrained leadership. Shifted GOP internal dynamics toward populism and anti-establishment sentiment that later fed into Trump 2016 support. Symbol of resistance to Obama agenda; criticized as astroturf or racially tinged by opponents.

## Actors
- Grassroots organizers and local chapters
- Media amplifiers: Fox News, talk radio
- Political beneficiaries: new Republican candidates and incumbents
- Targets: Obama administration, Nancy Pelosi-led Congress

## Historiography
Contemporary coverage polarized (populist awakening vs. corporate-backed obstruction). Later analyses tie it to longer arc of antitax politics, white working-class grievance, and media ecosystem changes. Jenkins and other syntheses treat it as key marker of 21st-century polarization and constitutional originalist revival. Primary: rally footage, movement manifestos, election data.
