# The Italian Campaign and the Fall of Fascism (1943–1945)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]. Operational battle detail lives in the warfare hub (Husky, Salerno, etc.); this page carries the political collapse and the occupation's crimes.

## The Fall of Mussolini

North Africa ended at Tunis (mid-May 1943, 250,000 Axis POWs; Rommel's own verdict: "the war in North Africa was decided by the weight of Anglo-American material"). Sicily was invaded 10 July 1943. On 24–25 July the Fascist Grand Council — meeting for the first time since 1939 — passed Dino Grandi's motion stripping Mussolini of powers, 19–7 (Grandi carried a live grenade throughout); the King dismissed and arrested him the next day, Badoglio took office, and the Fascist Party dissolved almost without resistance. The armistice of Cassibile (signed 3, announced 8 September 1943) triggered the German seizure of the peninsula and the disarming of the Italian army. Skorzeny's glider raid freed Mussolini (12 September) for the puppet **Salò Republic**, which executed the Grand Council "traitors" including his son-in-law Ciano and "soon degenerated into a morass of violence, corruption and terror."

## German Reprisals and Crimes in Occupied Italy

- **Cephalonia (September 1943):** after the island's Italian garrison resisted for a week, German forces executed more than 6,000 Italian soldiers and sailors, "shooting almost all the Italian officers in batches over a four-hour period of cold-blooded carnage." Of ~650,000 Italian troops seized as "military internees" and sent to the Reich as forced labor, up to 50,000 died — the highest death rate of any Western POWs in German hands (Goebbels: "a Gypsy people gone to pot").
- **The Holocaust in Italy:** Himmler ordered deportation on 12 September 1943. In Rome, SS chief Kappler extorted 50 kg of gold with a false promise of safety; Dannecker's round-up of **16 October 1943** seized 1,259 Roman Jews (200 children under ten) — 15 of the deportees survived Auschwitz. Kesselring, Möllhausen, and Weizsäcker tried to divert the Jews to labor, fearing a public protest from Pius XII "under whose windows" the deportation took place; Hitler overrode them, and the papal protest never came — the Vatican newspaper's vague article was, Weizsäcker noted, unrecognizable as referring to the Jews at all. The Salò regime interned Jews on its own initiative; ~3,800 more went to Auschwitz in 1944. Overall ~80% of Italy's 43,000 Jews survived, "not least thanks to help from ordinary, non-Jewish Italians."
- **The Pontine marshes (1943–45):** German medical officers (Martini, Rodenwaldt — both tied to Himmler's Ahnenerbe) deliberately re-flooded Mussolini's drained marshes with brackish water to reintroduce malaria as biological warfare against the "treacherous" Italians, confiscating quinine stocks: registered cases rose from ~1,200 (1943) to ~55,000 (1944).

## Historiography

Evans frames Italy 1943 as the Axis's political unraveling: "if Mussolini's regime could collapse overnight, then so too could Hitler's" — the fear that drove both the rescue and the German radicalization at home. The failed papal protest of October 1943 is central evidence in the Pius XII debate.
