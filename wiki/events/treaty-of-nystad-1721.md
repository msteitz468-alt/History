# Treaty of Nystad

## Narrative
The **Treaty of Nystad** was signed on August 30, 1721, between the Russian Tsardom and the Swedish Empire in the town of Nystad (Finnish: Uusikaupunki), ending the Great Northern War of 1700–1721 (L2900-2902). 

Unable to reverse its military decline following the death of Charles XII in 1718, and directly threatened by Russian naval raids into Sweden proper, the Swedish government under King Frederick I agreed to peace (L2900). 

Under the provisions of the treaty:
* **Territorial Cessions:** Russia acquired Livonia, Estonia, Ingermanland, part of Karelia, and several Baltic islands (including Ösel and Dagö) (L2902).
* **Restorations and Compensation:** Russia returned the bulk of occupied Finland to Sweden and paid two million rix-dollars (L2902).
* **Strategic Control:** The acquisition of the Baltic coast and strategically located fortress towns like Viborg gave Russia complete control over the Gulf of Finland and secured the new capital, St. Petersburg (L2902).

## Causal Analysis
The treaty was caused by:
* **Swedish Exhaustion:** Following the destruction of its main army at Poltava (1709) and the loss of its Baltic territories, Sweden was financially exhausted and politically isolated (L2890, L2900).
* **Russian Naval Raids:** To force a settlement, Peter the Great constructed a Baltic fleet and launched amphibious military expeditions into the Swedish mainland between 1719 and 1721, demonstrating Sweden's vulnerability (L2898-2900).

## Consequence Analysis
The Treaty of Nystad produced:
* **The Russian Empire:** At the peace celebrations in October 1721, the Senate prevailed upon Peter I to accept the titles of "Great," "Father of the Fatherland," and "Emperor," formally transforming the Tsardom of Russia into the Russian Empire (L2902-L2906).
* **Baltic Window:** Russia secured its long-sought direct maritime access to Western Europe, enabling trade and rapid Westernization (L2902, L2908).
* **Northern Hegemony:** Russia replaced Sweden as the dominant power in Northern Europe, obtaining a preponderant influence over Poland and German affairs (L2908).
