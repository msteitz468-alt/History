# The Revolt of Jehu (841 BCE)

The **military coup of 841 BCE** in which the army commander **Jehu ben-Nimshi**
overthrew and exterminated the [[omride-dynasty|House of Omri]], seized the throne of
[[kingdom-of-israel|Israel]], and inaugurated the longest-lived Israelite dynasty. A
religiously-charged purge of the Tyrian Baal cult, it also nearly destroyed the
Davidic line in [[kingdom-of-judah|Judah]]. It closes the period covered by Ch. 10 and
opens Ch. 11 of [[boardman-cah-v3p1-1982|CAH Vol. III, Part 1]] (T. C. Mitchell).

## Narrative

Anointed by an agent of the prophet **Elisha** while the Omride king **Jehoram** lay
wounded from the Aramaean wars, Jehu launched a lightning coup at **Jezreel**: he
killed Jehoram, had the queen-mother **Jezebel** thrown from a window, and — because
the [[kingdom-of-judah|Judahite]] king **Ahaziah** (Jezebel's kinsman) was visiting —
killed him too. He then slaughtered the rest of the Omride house, the Baal priesthood,
and the worshippers gathered in the Baal temple at Samaria, assisted by the ascetic
Yahwist **Rechabites** under Jonadab. In Judah, the killing of Ahaziah let his mother
**Athaliah**, an Omride, seize the throne and try to wipe out the Davidic heirs — the
infant Joash was hidden in the Temple, and the dynasty survived only by a hair (see
[[kingdom-of-judah]]). Later in the same year Jehu submitted to **[[neo-assyrian-empire|Shalmaneser
III]]**, paying tribute depicted on the **Black Obelisk**.

## Causal Analysis

- **caused_by:** the [[omride-dynasty|Omride]] dynasty's patronage of the **Tyrian
  Baal cult** (Jezebel) and the consequent **Yahwist prophetic reaction** (Elijah,
  Elisha), which sanctioned Jehu's revolt as a religious purge.
- **contributed_to:** **military discontent** amid the costly, inconclusive wars with
  [[hazael|Aram-Damascus]] — Jehu was a field commander who turned the army inward.

## Consequence Analysis

- **produced:** the **extermination of the House of Omri** and the founding of the
  **dynasty of Jehu**, which ruled Israel for nearly a century.
- **produced:** the **near-extinction of the Davidic dynasty** in Judah (Athaliah's
  usurpation) — averted only by the hiding of Joash.
- **enabled:** Israel's **diplomatic isolation**: by destroying the Omride alliances
  (Tyre, Judah) Jehu left Israel exposed, and [[hazael|Hazael of Damascus]] promptly
  stripped it of its Transjordanian territory, reducing it to near-vassalage for a
  generation.
- **enabled:** a precise **synchronism** — Jehu's tribute on the **Black Obelisk**
  (841) is the only surviving contemporary depiction of an Israelite king and a fixed
  chronological point (he is named "son of Omri," i.e. of the land of Omri, though not
  of the dynasty).

## Historiography

The biblical account (II Kings 9–10) is a vivid, theologically committed narrative
that **approves** Jehu's bloodshed as zeal for Yahweh — yet the eighth-century prophet
**Hosea** (Hos. 1:4) later **condemns** "the blood of Jezreel," showing that even
within the tradition the purge was contested. The external anchor is the **Black
Obelisk of Shalmaneser III**, which independently fixes Jehu's tribute to 841 BCE and
makes the revolt one of the most securely dated events of Israelite history. Mitchell
reads the narrative critically while using the Assyrian evidence to date it; the
episode is a centrepiece of the period's text-versus-inscription historiography.
