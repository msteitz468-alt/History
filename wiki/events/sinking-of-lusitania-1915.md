# Sinking of the RMS Lusitania (7 May 1915)

Torpedoing and sinking of the Cunard liner RMS *Lusitania* by German U-boat U-20 (Kapitänleutnant Walther Schwieger) off the Irish coast. Among the dead was [[actors/vanderbilt-alfred-gwynne|Alfred Gwynne Vanderbilt]], the focus of Cooper & Howe's Ch. 8.

## Narrative

On the morning of 7 May 1915 fog slowed the ship; when fog lifted, speed resumed. U-20 sighted *Lusitania* after midday, submerged, and at 2:10 p.m. fired a single gyroscopic torpedo that struck just behind the bridge; a second internal explosion followed (boiler, coal dust, or cargo — contemporaneously misreported as a second torpedo). Power failed within minutes; the ship listed heavily; many lifeboats could not be launched. *Lusitania* sank in about eighteen minutes roughly ten miles south of the Old Head of Kinsale. Cooper & Howe follow period figures of more than 1,100 dead, including the large majority of U.S. citizens aboard (they cite 129 of 139 lost), among them Alfred. Press accounts — passenger Slidell, crew Bernard and steward/Ratcliffe — celebrated Alfred's reported efforts to give away his lifebelt and help others. The book also notes munitions/cargo and auxiliary-cruiser framing in wartime argument.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` German submarine campaign against Allied shipping in a declared war zone
- `contributed_to:` *Lusitania*'s mixed passenger/cargo profile and routing
- `concurrent_with:` broader WWI attrition at sea; U.S. still neutral

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` mass civilian casualties and an American opinion crisis
- `contributed_to:` long erosion of U.S. neutrality (full entry still nearly two years later)
- Within Vanderbilt family history: loss of a prominent fourth-generation male heir and a press martyrdom narrative

## Actors

German naval command (U-20); Cunard; passengers including Alfred Gwynne Vanderbilt.

## Historiography

Diplomatic and military literature debates munitions cargo, warnings, and British political use of the incident. Cooper & Howe do not re-litigate war-causes debates; they narrate one passenger's death and its mythologization. Keep those layers distinct. (Ch. 7 of the same book is Alva's suffrage march of 4 May 1912 — not the *Titanic*.)
