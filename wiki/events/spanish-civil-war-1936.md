# Spanish Civil War (1936–1939)

War between the elected Popular Front government of the Second Spanish
Republic and a Nationalist military-civilian coalition led from October 1936
by [[franco-francisco|Francisco Franco]], ending in Nationalist victory and
a dictatorship that outlived every other regime of the fascist era. The most
internationalized civil war of the interwar period: [[nazi-germany|Germany]]
(see [[german-intervention-spanish-civil-war-1936]]) and Fascist Italy armed
the Nationalists; the Soviet Union armed the Republic; the democracies chose
Non-Intervention.

## Narrative

A partially failed military rising of 17–18 July 1936 split Spain
territorially and detonated parallel revolutions: a worker/anarchist social
revolution in the Republican zone and a counter-revolutionary terror in the
Nationalist zone. The war was decided by superior Nationalist military
cohesion, unified command under Franco, and steadier Axis supply against the
Republic's internal fragmentation (culminating in the Barcelona May Days of
1937) and dependence on Soviet aid. Madrid fell 28 March 1939; Franco declared
victory 1 April.

## Causal Analysis

`contributed_to:` European polarization after the [[great-depression]];
`caused_by:` the domestic breakdown of Republican politics 1934–36 (Asturias
rising, Popular Front election, spiraling political violence) — Payne
emphasizes internal Spanish causation over any fascist-takeover script: the
[[falange|Falange]] was electorally negligible before 1936 and grew only
inside the wartime Nationalist coalition, where Franco subordinated it.

**Payne (Civil War in Europe):** The 1934 insurrection (Oct 4) was the key precursor: Socialists justified revolt against CEDA "fascists" entering government (despite CEDA's legalism). Only Asturias succeeded; revolutionaries committed numerous atrocities (murdered >50 priests/civilians, arson, looted ≥15M pesetas). Military repression: ~1,400 killed (majority revolutionaries), 15,000 arrested; only 2 executions; PSOE not outlawed; leaders had privileges; amnesty after ~1 year. Repression "historically unprecedented in its leniency" compared to France 1871, Weimar 1918-23, Italy 1920-22 — fell between two stools: infuriated left without deterring them, failed to uphold law. 1935 was the Republic's most peaceful period; fascism support "almost nonexistent". Left propaganda (Comintern-funded) exaggerated repression.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` the Franco regime; `enabled:` Axis military experimentation
(Condor Legion, aerial bombing of cities — Guernica) and the perception of
democratic weakness before [[second-world-war-1939|WWII]];
`contrasts_with:` the German and Italian cases — in Payne's typology the
outcome was an authoritarian, not fascist, regime.

## The Rising and the Nationalist War Effort (Payne 1987)

Payne's regime history ([[sources/payne-franco-regime-1987|1987]]) adds granular detail on the Nationalist side. **Pre-war violence**: between 17 February and 17 July 1936, 213 attempted assassinations and 269 political killings (Madrid 45, Barcelona only 3); the murder of opposition leader Calvo Sotelo by state police (12–13 July) — "a crime unprecedented in the annals of west European parliamentary government" — decided the waverers, including Franco. Payne finds no documented leftist plan to seize power by a set date (the "Communist plot" documents were fakes), and no contact between the conspirators and Berlin or Rome before the war's first week (Viñas's "definitive" refutation of the fascist-conspiracy thesis). **The rising**: joined by only 7 of 27 major generals and perhaps 1,000 activist officers, it succeeded in about a third of peninsular garrisons and failed in four of six major cities — "a revolt that failed in more than half the national territory can scarcely be called a success." Payne's central causal judgment: "a preemptive counterrevolutionary coup set off the revolution," and "main responsibility" lies with the Azaña-led Republican Left, "more the accomplice than the victim of the revolutionary left." The rising was conducted under the Republican flag ("¡Viva la República!"); Sanjurjo, its designated head, died in a plane crash on 20 July, opening Franco's path. **War effort**: Franco's airlift of the Army of Africa from 20 July 1936 was "the first airlift in military history"; his diversion to relieve the Alcázar of Toledo instead of striking a weakly held Madrid (September 1936) was possibly "Franco's major error of the war." 6,832 clergy were killed in the Republican zone — the framing of the war as a religious Crusade (see [[concepts/national-catholicism]]) followed the atrocities rather than preceding the rising, whose original Mola program had kept church–state separation. For the terror on both sides and the casualty accounting (Salas Larrazábal's ~70–72,000 executions per side; total violent deaths under 300,000, the *millón de muertos* a myth), see [[events/francoist-repression-1936]].

## Historiography

Vast and politically charged: Republican-sympathetic historiography (war of
fascism vs. democracy) against revisionist emphasis on Republican disorder
and revolutionary violence; Payne (1995) and his Spanish-history corpus
represent the latter tendency's scholarly wing. See
[[fascism-and-the-political-left]] for adjacent debates.
