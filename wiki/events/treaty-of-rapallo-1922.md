# Treaty of Rapallo
## Narrative
In the spring of 1922, a Soviet delegation led by George Chicherin attended the Genoa Economic Conference—the first international conference in which the Bolshevik government participated. When the conference bogged down over Allied demands that Russia pay tsarist war debts and compensate foreigners for confiscated property, Soviet and German representatives met secretly in the nearby resort town of Rapallo. On April 16, 1922, they signed the Treaty of Rapallo. The agreement established normal diplomatic relations, mutual recognition, and economic cooperation, and was supplemented by secret agreements allowing Germany to test weapons and train military personnel in Soviet territory.

## Causal Analysis
- `caused_by:` the **diplomatic and economic isolation** of both Soviet Russia and Weimar Germany, who were treated as outcasts in the post-Versailles world (Line 6209); and the **failure of the Genoa Conference** to resolve Russia's debts and reparations issues.

## Consequence Analysis
- `produced:` the **normalization of diplomatic relations** and economic cooperation between the two powers, breaking the diplomatic blockade of Soviet Russia.
- `produced:` the **mutual renunciation of war claims** and nationalization compensations, resolving outstanding financial disputes.
- `enabled:` **secret military cooperation**: the German military (Reichswehr) established aviation, tank, and chemical weapon training bases in Soviet territory, bypassing the restrictions of the Treaty of Versailles.
- `preceded_by:` a wave of diplomatic recognitions for the Soviet Union in 1924, including Great Britain, France, and Italy (Line 6213).

## Actors
- **George Chicherin**, the Soviet Commissar for Foreign Affairs who negotiated and signed the treaty; and the representatives of the **Weimar Republic**. See chicherin-george.

## Historiography
Riasanovsky notes that Rapallo had nothing to do with ideological sympathy between the signatories, but was a classic example of *Realpolitik* driven by mutual advantage in the face of shared isolation (Line 6209).
