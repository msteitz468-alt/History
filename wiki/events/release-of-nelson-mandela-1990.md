# Release of Nelson Mandela (11 February 1990)

On 11 February 1990, after 27 years of imprisonment, Nelson Mandela walked out of Victor Verster Prison hand-in-hand with Winnie Madikizela-Mandela. The release, announced by President F.W. de Klerk on 2 February along with the unbanning of the ANC and other organizations, was one of the defining images of the late twentieth century. Meredith opens the biography with this moment and returns to it as the pivot from the prison years to the negotiated settlement.

## Historiography

Martin Meredith's biography draws on Mandela's own memoir (*Long Walk to Freedom*),
contemporary international press coverage (the release was globally televised), and
interviews with ANC and National Party figures. The event is heavily documented and
undisputed in its basic facts, but historians differ on how to weigh the relative
contributions to de Klerk's decision — international sanctions and isolation, internal
mass mobilization and armed struggle, or secret prison negotiations between Mandela and
the government dating to the mid-1980s — a causal question tied to the broader
historiographical debate over what actually ended apartheid. South African archival
material (National Party and security-service records) has become more available since
the 1990s transition, though some intelligence-related records remain restricted.
