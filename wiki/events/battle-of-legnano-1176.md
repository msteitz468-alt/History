# The Battle of Legnano (1176)

## Narrative

At **Legnano**, north-west of Milan, on 29 May 1176, the militia of the
**[[lombard-league|Lombard League]]** defeated the army of the Emperor
**[[frederick-i-barbarossa|Frederick Barbarossa]]**. [[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]]
(Benjamin Arnold, Ch. 14; Giovanni Tabacco, Ch. 15a) treats it as the decisive military
verdict on Barbarossa's two-decade attempt to make imperial authority a reality in
northern Italy. The League's infantry — the citizen militias of the communes, famously
including the Milanese "Company of Death" defending the *carroccio* (the communal
war-altar on wheels) — withstood and broke the charge of the imperial knights;
Barbarossa was unhorsed and briefly believed dead.

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` Frederick Barbarossa's assertion of the imperial *regalia* over the
Lombard cities (Roncaglia, 1158) and the destruction of Milan (1162), which drove the
communes into the **Lombard League (1167)**.
`caused_by:` the **papal schism**, which aligned **Pope Alexander III** with the League
against the emperor and his antipopes.
`contributed_to:` the tactical strength of the **communal infantry** against feudal
cavalry, and the exhaustion of Barbarossa's resources after years of Italian campaigning.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` the collapse of Barbarossa's Italian policy and his turn to negotiation —
the **Peace of Venice (1177)** with the papacy and the **Peace of Constance (1183)** with
the cities, which recognised communal **self-government** under nominal imperial
overlordship.
`enabled:` the lasting practical **independence of the Italian communes** and the
failure of the medieval empire to dominate Italy.
`produced:` an enduring symbol of **civic liberty** against imperial power (later
mythologised in Italian national memory).
