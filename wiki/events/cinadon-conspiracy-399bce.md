# The Conspiracy of Cinadon, c. 399 BCE

## Narrative

Early in the reign of **[[agesilaus-ii|Agesilaus II]]**, a man named **Cinadon** — not a
full Spartiate but one of the **hypomeiones ("Inferiors")**, citizens who had lost or
never attained full status — was detected organizing a revolt against the Spartan ruling
class. According to **[[xenophon]]** (*Hellenica* iii.3), whose Peloponnesian contacts
let him report "such a dangerous and — for the Spartan authorities — embarrassing
episode," Cinadon led an informer to the agora and pointed out how **few the true
Spartiates were** among the crowd: against perhaps a few thousand full citizens stood the
**helots, perioikoi, neodamodeis and Inferiors**, of whom Cinadon said that they would
gladly "eat the Spartiates raw." The ephors, learning of the plot, had Cinadon arrested
on a pretext, interrogated under torture, and executed with his fellow conspirators.

## Significance

[[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 2, Lewis) places the affair among the structural
**disqualifications of Sparta** for the imperial role thrust on it by victory. At the very
moment of the [[spartan-hegemony|Spartan hegemony]]'s greatest external power, the Cinadon
conspiracy laid bare the **demographic crisis** beneath it: the relentless shrinkage of
the Spartiate body (*oliganthropia*), the resentment of the disfranchised "Inferiors," and
the perpetual danger of the huge subordinate population that Spartan society rested upon.
It is the domestic counterpart to the external overreach that would end at Leuctra — and
the helots' grievance that [[epaminondas]] would later exploit by liberating **Messenia**.

## Consequence Analysis

caused_by: the structural narrowing of Spartan citizenship and the exclusion of the
*hypomeiones*. produced: a brief, brutally suppressed crisis that exposed the fragility of
the Spartan system — a fragility that [[thebes-hegemony-371bce|Thebes]] would exploit a
generation later.
