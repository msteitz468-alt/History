# The Napoleonic Wars (1803–1815)

The **Napoleonic Wars** were the series of conflicts by which **[[napoleon|Napoleonic France]]**
fought, and ultimately lost, a bid for mastery of Europe — the climax of the wars begun by the
[[french-revolution-1789|Revolution]], fought "from the Urals to the Atlantic." They remade the
map of Europe, spread revolutionary institutions by conquest, provoked modern nationalism, and
ended in the Restoration settlement of 1815. From
[[cambridge-modern-history-v9-napoleon|*The Cambridge Modern History*, Vol. IX]].

## Narrative

Renewed war with **Britain (1803)** set the pattern: Napoleon supreme on land, Britain
supreme at sea. **Trafalgar (1805)** destroyed the Franco-Spanish fleet and ended invasion
plans, but on land Napoleon crushed the coalitions — **Austerlitz (1805)** over Austria and
Russia, **Jena-Auerstedt (1806)** over Prussia, and **Friedland (1807)**, ending the Holy
Roman Empire (1806) and producing the **Treaty of Tilsit (1807)** at the empire's height. To
strangle Britain economically he imposed the **Continental System**, whose enforcement drew him
into the draining **Peninsular War** in Spain (from 1808) and, fatally, the **invasion of
Russia (1812)**, where the Grande Armée was destroyed. A revived coalition beat him at
**Leipzig (1813)**; Paris fell and he abdicated (1814). His return (the Hundred Days) ended at
**Waterloo (18 June 1815)**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **expansion of revolutionary/Napoleonic France** and Napoleon's drive for
  **European hegemony**, threatening the [[balance-of-power|balance of power]].
- `caused_by:` the **Anglo-French commercial and naval rivalry**, which made Britain the
  paymaster and organizer of successive coalitions.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **redrawing of Europe** — the end of the Holy Roman Empire (1806), the
  consolidation of German and Italian states, and the spread of the **Napoleonic Code** and
  revolutionary administrative models across the continent.
- `produced:` the rise of modern **[[nationalism]]** — German, Spanish, and Russian resistance
  to French domination forged national feeling.
- `produced:` the **Congress of Vienna (1815)** and the **Concert of Europe** — a restored
  [[balance-of-power|balance of power]] and a conservative order that kept general peace for a
  generation.
- Confirmed **British naval and commercial supremacy** for the nineteenth century.

## Actors

- **Napoleonic France** and its satellites (the Confederation of the Rhine) vs. the shifting
  coalitions of **Britain, Austria, Prussia, and Russia** (with Spain, Portugal, and Sweden).
  See [[napoleon]], [[balance-of-power]].

## Historiography

The wars are central to the [[military-revolution|military]] history of mass armies (the
*levée en masse*, the corps system, decisive battle) and to debates on whether Napoleon was the
heir or the betrayer of the Revolution, and whether his empire spread liberation or exploitation.
