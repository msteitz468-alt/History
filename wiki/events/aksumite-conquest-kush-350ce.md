# Aksumite Conquest of Kush (c. 350 CE)

## Narrative

The campaign against the **Noba** (Nubians) and **Kasu** (Kush) is documented primarily in Ezana's stone inscriptions **RIE 190** (Ge'ez, ASAM-derived script) and **RIE 271** (Greek), and probably also in RIE 189. The inscriptions record that the expedition departed Aksum on the eighth day of the Aksumite month **Magabit** — which both texts specify fell on a Saturday. The most widely accepted dates for 8 Magabit falling on a Saturday in the relevant period are **AD 349, 355, or 360**, though the first or second remain most favoured.

March (mid-Magabit by Gregorian reckoning) was, as Phillipson notes, a good season for such an expedition: dry enough for march but before the harshest heat, and before the wet season.

The Greek text (RIE 271) frames the campaign explicitly in Christian terms: Ezana invokes the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit and designates Christ as "the god in whom I have believed." The Ge'ez text (RIE 190) is less explicitly Christian, suggesting the Greek version was amplified for a foreign audience.

The campaign destroyed the remnants of Kushite political power at Meroe — the last phase of a civilisation that had dominated the Middle Nile valley since c. 750 BCE. Ezana's inscription names both the Noba and Kasu as targets, probably distinguishing between the Nubians (Noba) who had settled the former Meroitic heartland and the residual Kushite (Kasu) polity itself.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Aksumite westward expansion over preceding century — Aksumite trade and tribute networks had been encroaching on Kushite territory; Aksumite caravan routes to Egypt bypassed the Nile and drained commerce away from Kush.
- `contributed_to:` weakening of Meroe by the Noba — the Noba (Nubians) had been settling in the former Meroitic heartland, fragmenting what remained of Kushite political authority before Ezana's campaign.
- `contributed_to:` Ezana's inscription cites Noba and Kasu raiding or failure to maintain subordinate status as the immediate pretext.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` end of the Aksumite-era Kingdom of Kush; Meroe's political remnants destroyed.
- `produced:` Aksumite hegemony to the western borderlands; tribute collection from Sudanese peoples.
- `preceded_by:` the prior Bega campaign (Ezana's trilingual inscriptions RIE 185/270, 185bis/270bis).
- `followed_by:` fragmentation of the Nile valley into successor polities — eventually Nubian Christian kingdoms (Nobatia, Makuria, Alodia) emerged in the following centuries.

## Actors

- **Ezana of Aksum** — campaign commander; the inscriptions are royal first-person accounts.
- **Noba** — the Nubian people settled in the former Meroitic heartland; primary opponents.
- **Kasu** — residual Kushite political entity; almost certainly the Kush of the Nile valley (Meroe).
- **Beja (Bega)** — a related pastoral people east of the Nile; subject of Ezana's earlier inscribed campaigns (RIE 185/270 series).

## Historiography

The primary sources are Ezana's own inscriptions (RIE 189, 190, 271). There has been debate over whether RIE 190/271 and RIE 189 refer to the same campaign; Phillipson considers them probably distinct, noting that the stone slabs differ significantly in size and textual detail. The precise dating — 349, 355, or 360 CE — is contested and depends on calendrical calculation (Saturday/Magabit coincidences) combined with other evidence for Ezana's reign length. The identification of "Kasu" with Kush is accepted by most scholars (Lusini 2007). The Noba are identified with the Nubians who succeeded Kush in the Middle Nile.

The conquest marked the end of a Kushite civilisation that had lasted, in various forms, from c. 750 BCE — nearly a thousand years. Its significance within Aksumite history is sometimes overshadowed by the religious dimension of Ezana's reign.
