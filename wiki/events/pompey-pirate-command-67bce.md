# Pompey's Pirate Command and the Lex Manilia (67–66 BCE)

The pair of tribunician laws that armed **[[pompey|Pompey]]** with **unprecedented
Mediterranean-wide commands** — first against the pirates, then against
**[[mithridates-vi|Mithridates]]** — the template for the extraordinary command that
would carry the dynasts to supremacy. Treated in [[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]] Ch. 8a
(Sherwin-White).

## The Lex Gabinia and the pirate war (67)

Piracy, swollen by decades of eastern war and based in **Cilicia** and **Crete**, had
grown into a strategic menace: it cut Rome's **grain supply**, raided the Italian
coast, and even seized praetors on the Appian road. In **67** the tribune **A.
Gabinius** carried, over fierce optimate resistance, a law granting **Pompey** — then
a private citizen after his consulship of 70 — a **command over the entire
Mediterranean and 50 miles inland**, with *imperium* equal to any proconsul's and a
staff of **15 (then 20) legates each holding praetorian imperium** — an innovation
that let subordinates act independently across vast distances.

Pompey **cleared the seas in three months**, sweeping the western Mediterranean
sector by sector and breaking the Cilician fleet at **Coracesium**. His treatment of
the surrendered pirates was characteristically shrewd: rather than mass execution he
**resettled them in inland cities** (Soli became Pompeiopolis), turning a maritime
plague into a settled population.

## The Lex Manilia and the eastern command (66)

With the disaster of **[[third-mithridatic-war-73-63bce|Zela]]** discrediting
Lucullus, the tribune **C. Manilius** in **66** carried a second law **transferring
the whole war against Mithridates and Tigranes to Pompey**, extending his Gabinian
powers and adding the right to **make war, peace, and alliances** at his own
discretion. It was supported by the praetor **Cicero** in the *De Imperio Cn.
Pompei* — a speech Sherwin-White calls "skilful misrepresentation," silent on the
five or six legions already in the field and on the war of conquest and plunder that
actually followed.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the genuine **piracy crisis** and grain shortage, the **failure of
Lucullus**, and the political weight of the **equestrian financiers** (and the urban
plebs) backing Pompey.
contributed_to: by the **restored tribunate** (see
[[pompey|Pompey and Crassus, cos. 70]]) — the instrument that made these popular laws
possible against the Senate's wishes.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the swift end of organized piracy and **Pompey's eastern conquests** and
[[pompeian-eastern-settlement-63bce|settlement]].
enabled: the **model of the great extraordinary command** — Mediterranean in scope,
conferred by plebiscite, with delegated *imperium* — that Caesar, Crassus, and the
triumvirs would inherit; "that excessive growth of individual power" the oligarchy
rightly feared (see [[optimates-populares]]).

## Historiography

Cicero's *De Imperio* (a primary document of the debate), Plutarch's *Pompey*,
Appian, and Dio; the inscription of Lentulus Marcellinus confirms the power of
Pompey's legates. Sherwin-White's theme: the laws were dressed as emergency measures
but effected a **war of imperial expansion**.
