# Hurricane Katrina (2005)

One of the deadliest and costliest natural disasters in U.S. history, a major hurricane that made landfall on the Gulf Coast on 29 August 2005, causing catastrophic damage across Louisiana, Mississippi, and Alabama and, most consequentially, breaching the federal levee system protecting New Orleans, which flooded roughly four-fifths of the city.

## Narrative

Katrina strengthened to Category 5 over the Gulf of Mexico before weakening somewhat and making its final landfall near the Louisiana-Mississippi border as a strong Category 3 storm. The storm surge overwhelmed and breached multiple levees and floodwalls around New Orleans, engineered and maintained by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, flooding large portions of the city — including low-lying, predominantly poorer and Black neighborhoods hit hardest — for weeks. Evacuation efforts, particularly for residents without private transportation, were widely judged inadequate, and thousands sheltered in dire conditions at the Superdome and Convention Center awaiting rescue and supplies. The Federal Emergency Management Agency's slow and disorganized response drew intense criticism, and [[actors/bush-george-w|President George W. Bush's]] administration faced sustained political fallout over perceived failures of leadership and preparedness, symbolized by his remark praising FEMA director Michael Brown's performance amid widely-televised chaos.

## Causal Analysis

The disaster's scale was `caused_by` a combination of an intense hurricane making landfall near a below-sea-level city and the catastrophic, `contributed_to`-by-design-and-maintenance-failure breach of the levee system meant to protect it; a later Army Corps of Engineers investigation acknowledged flaws in the levees' design and construction as a major contributing factor beyond the storm's raw intensity.

## Consequence Analysis

Katrina `produced` long-term demographic and economic disruption of New Orleans (a substantial and lasting population decline, especially among displaced Black residents who did not return) and `contributed_to` a broader national reassessment of disaster preparedness and levee infrastructure. Politically, it `contributed_to` a sharp decline in public approval of the Bush administration, compounding the political costs of the concurrent [[events/iraq-war-2003|Iraq War]].

## Historiography

Katrina generated extensive journalistic, governmental (including a bipartisan congressional investigation and an Army Corps of Engineers review), and scholarly analysis, with continuing debate over the relative weight of natural disaster versus infrastructure and governance failure, and over the disaster's disproportionate impact along race and class lines in New Orleans — a debate that remains active in urban planning, disaster policy, and American political historiography.
