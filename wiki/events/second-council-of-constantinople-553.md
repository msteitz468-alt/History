# The Second Council of Constantinople, 553

The **Fifth Ecumenical Council**, convened and dominated by the emperor **[[justinian|Justinian I]]** to
settle the **[[three-chapters-controversy|Three Chapters controversy]]** — the most problematic of the
early councils and a turning point in the empire's long, failing effort to heal the schism left by
**[[council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedon (451)]]**.

## Narrative

To win back the **Miaphysites**, Justinian sought to condemn the "**Three Chapters**" — the person and
works of **Theodore of Mopsuestia**, the anti-Cyrilline writings of **Theodoret of Cyrrhus**, and the
**Letter of Ibas to Mari** — as carriers of the Nestorian (Antiochene) theology that the Miaphysites
believed Chalcedon had sheltered. The council, meeting at Constantinople in **553**, **anathematized**
the Three Chapters and also condemned **Origen** and Origenism. **Pope Vigilius**, refusing to attend
and issuing a contrary *Constitutum*, was pressured by the emperor and eventually **capitulated**,
endorsing the condemnations.

## Consequence Analysis

caused_by: [[justinian|Justinian's]] determination to reconcile the Miaphysites and his domination of the
church (a high point of **caesaropapism**). produced: the **condemnation of the Three Chapters** and of
Origen, and the formal status of the council as the Fifth Ecumenical. enabled: the **Schism of the Three
Chapters** in the West — Africa, Aquileia and Istria broke with Rome over Vigilius's surrender, dividing
northern Italy until the late seventh century. failed_to: win back the **Miaphysites** (who were
unappeased), and it **deepened the breach** with the **Church of Persia**, which revered Theodore of
Mopsuestia, and strained relations between the Greek East and Latin West.

## Historiography

The acts are now ingested as **[[price-acts-constantinople-553|The Acts of the Council of Constantinople
of 553]]** (Richard Price). The older view condemns the council for distorting Chalcedon and for its
coercive methods and doctored dossiers; **Price** defends its **Neo-Chalcedonian** theology (Chalcedon
read through Cyril of Alexandria) as constructive. See **[[three-chapters-controversy]]**.
