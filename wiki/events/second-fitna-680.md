# Second Fitna (680–692)

The **Second Fitna** (680–692 CE) was the second major civil war in the early Islamic caliphate. It began following the death of the first Umayyad caliph, Mu'awiya I, in response to his controversial nomination of his son, Yazid I, as successor. The conflict involved several factions: the ruling **Umayyads**, the supporters of Ali's household led by **Husayn ibn Ali**, and an independent caliphate established in Mecca by **Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr**. The war resulted in the victory of the Marwanid branch of the Umayyads, the destruction of the Meccan counter-caliphate, and the permanent consolidation of the Shi'a sect following the martyrdom of Husayn at Karbala.

## Narrative

### 1. Karbala and the Rise of Discontent (680 CE)
Upon Mu'awiya's death in 680, Yazid I assumed the throne. Husayn ibn Ali (grandson of Muhammad and son of Ali) and Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr refused to pledge allegiance. Husayn left Medina for Kufa, where the population had promised him support. However, his small party was intercepted by a large Umayyad army at Karbala (modern Iraq) on October 10, 680. Husayn and nearly all of his family and followers were slaughtered. The **Battle of Karbala** shocked the Muslim world, sparking rebellions in Kufa (the *Tawwabin* / Penitents revolt) and Medina.

### 2. Ibn al-Zubayr’s Counter-Caliphate (683–692 CE)
In Mecca, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr declared himself caliph, gaining control of the Hijaz, Egypt, Iraq, and parts of Syria. In response, Yazid sent an army that sacked Medina (683) and besieged Mecca, burning the Ka'ba, before the siege was lifted upon Yazid's sudden death in late 683. Yazid's successor, Mu'awiya II, died after a few months, leaving the Umayyad dynasty on the brink of collapse.

### 3. The Marwanid Recovery and Victory (684–692 CE)
The Umayyad loyalist faction in Syria rallied around **Marwan I** (r. 684–685), defeating the pro-Zubayrid tribes at the Battle of Marj Rahit (684) and reclaiming Syria and Egypt. 

Marwan's son, **[[abd-al-malik|Abd al-Malik]]** (r. 685–705), succeeded him. Abd al-Malik systematically crushed the rebel groups:
- He defeated the pro-Alid rebellion of Mukhtar al-Thaqafi in Kufa (687).
- He defeated Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr (governor of Iraq) at the Battle of Maskin (691).
- In 692, Abd al-Malik's general, **al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf**, besieged Mecca for seven months. Al-Hajjaj bombarded the city and the Ka'ba with catapults. Ibn al-Zubayr was killed in the final assault, ending the Second Fitna.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced: [[abd-al-malik|Marwanid Consolidation]]`: The Umayyad dynasty secured absolute central control from Damascus, establishing a highly militarized state.
- `produced: [[schism-in-islam|Shi'a Consolidation]]`: Husayn's martyrdom at Karbala became the central religious narrative of the Shi'a sect, transforming a political party (*Shi'at Ali*) into a distinct religious movement centered on redemptive suffering, martyrdom, and the annual commemoration of *Ashura*.
- `contributed_to: [[abd-al-malik|Centralization and Reforms]]`: To prevent future civil wars and integrate the ethnically diverse empire, Abd al-Malik launched sweeping administrative and monetary reforms, including the Arabization of the bureaucracy and the introduction of epigraphic Islamic coinage (see [[abd-al-malik|Abd al-Malik]]).
