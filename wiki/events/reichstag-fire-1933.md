# Reichstag Fire (27–28 February 1933)

The burning of the Reichstag building, set — on the scholarly consensus Evans
endorses — by the Dutch anarcho-syndicalist Marinus van der Lubbe acting
alone, and exploited within hours by the new Hitler government as proof of a
Communist rising. The Reichstag Fire Decree signed by Hindenburg the next
day suspended civil liberties indefinitely and became the legal foundation of
"protective custody" and the permanent state of emergency.

## Narrative

From Evans ([[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003]], Part 5). **The arsonist**:
Marinus van der Lubbe, b. 13 January 1909 in Leiden into the direst poverty
(father deserted; mother dead when he was twelve), a mason drawn to the
Communist youth movement, who left it in 1931 for anarcho-syndicalism and
"propaganda by the deed." Eyesight damaged in a work accident, he had trekked
toward the USSR (reaching only Poland) and returned to Berlin on 18 February
1933, where the left's passivity under Nazi terror appalled him. On 25
February he attempted arson at a Neukölln welfare office, the town hall, and
the former royal palace — all failed and barely reported. On 27 February,
after spending his last money on matches and firelighters, he entered the
empty Reichstag around 9 p.m., fired the chamber's curtains, and the dome
acted as a chimney. He was caught inside; the building was gutted.

**The balcony scene** (Rudolf Diels's postwar memoir, quoted at length by
Evans): Göring — "This is the beginning of the Communist uprising! Now they'll
strike out! There's not a minute to waste!"; Hitler, face "flaming red with
excitement": "There will be no more mercy now; anyone who stands in our way
will be butchered... Every Communist functionary will be shot where he is
found. The Communist deputies must be hanged this very night." Diels — the
non-Nazi head of the Prussian political police, who judged van der Lubbe "a
madman" needing no helpers — called the scene a "mad-house." Police worked
from pre-existing lists that night: 4,000 arrests; Diels's men "quietly
ignored Göring's instruction that they should be shot."

**The decree**: Gürtner had proposed a draconian public-order law to cabinet
on 27 February *before* the fire; Grauert suggested the emergency decree;
Frick inserted the crucial clause 2 letting the **cabinet** (not the
President) take over the federated states. The decree of 28 February suspended
constitutional protections of expression, press, assembly, and association and
allowed indefinite "protective custody" without court order. Hitler to
cabinet: "The psychologically correct moment for the confrontation has now
arrived"; the struggle "must not be made dependent on judicial
considerations." Göring embellished with fabrications — Communist plans for
"poisoning of public kitchens," kidnapping ministers' families, sabotage.
Hindenburg signed "despite the fact that it ceded a significant part of his
powers to the Hitler government."

## Causal Analysis

- The van-der-Lubbe-alone finding vs. Nazi-arson theories is a long-running
  dispute; Evans treats lone arson as established ("Subsequent investigation
  turned up a mass of documentary evidence confirming his story that he had
  been acting alone") while stressing that the regime's response was prepared
  opportunism, not conspiracy proof — the repression instruments predated the
  fire.
- Contemporary suspicion of Nazi arson was immediate and reached into the
  DNVP itself: floor leader Ernst Oberfohren, who investigated, was hounded
  out and found dead on 7 May 1933 (see
  [[dissolution-of-german-parties-1933]]).

## Consequence Analysis

- produced: the Reichstag Fire Decree (28 February) — "the legal pretext for
  everything that was to follow" (Evans): 10,000 Communists in custody by 15
  March; Thälmann arrested 3 March; the KPD effectively outlawed from 28
  February (formally from 6 March) while still allowed to contest the 5 March
  election — Hitler's deliberate choice, Evans notes, both to avoid provoking
  an uprising and to keep Communist candidacies siphoning votes from the SPD;
  the 81 elected KPD deputies were arrested as found.
- enabled: the [[enabling-act-23-march-1933|Enabling Act]] (Göring's quorum
  manipulation excluded the legally elected KPD deputies) and the terror wave
  of [[nazi-seizure-of-power-1933]]; over 200 telegrams from local Nazi groups
  demanded the "sub-humans" be "shot out of hand, or publicly strangled in
  front of the Reichstag building."
- The stormtroopers read the fire as release: "the beacon in Berlin... Finally
  the relief of the order: 'Go to it!'" (SA memoir quoted by Evans) — though
  Evans stresses the SA violence had "no coordinated plan," being the venting
  of long-nurtured personal hatreds.

## Historiography

The Tobias–Mommsen lone-arsonist thesis vs. recurring Nazi-complicity
revivals (Bahar/Kugel); Evans sides with the former.
