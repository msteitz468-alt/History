# Kharijite Revolt of Abu Yazid (943–947)

The **revolt of [[abu-yazid-al-nukkari|Abu Yazid al-Nukkari]]**, "the Man on the Donkey," was the gravest
crisis of the [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] Ifriqiyan period — a **Kharijite Berber uprising** that overran
almost all of Ifriqiya and nearly extinguished the dynasty before its defeat by [[al-mansur-fatimid|al-Mansur]].

## Narrative

A Nukkari **Kharijite** preacher educated at Tahert, Abu Yazid spent some fifteen years — backed by the
**Umayyads of Córdoba** — building a coalition of **Zanata Berber** tribesmen against Fatimid rule. From
**943** he launched attacks, and in October 944 seized **[[qayrawan|Qayrawan]]**, where the disaffected Sunni
**Maliki** scholars allied with him. His armies drove Fatimid authority back to the coast, leaving only
**[[mahdiyya|Mahdiyya]]** and a few strongholds. He personally led the **siege of Mahdiyya** (20 January – 16
September 945), but al-Mahdi's walls held. The dying caliph [[al-qaim-bi-amr-allah|al-Qa'im]] praised the
Kutama defenders as "like the Apostles of Jesus," named his son heir, and died in May 946 with the outcome
still uncertain. As crown prince, al-Mansur relieved Sousse, recovered Qayrawan (whose people now shut their
gates against Abu Yazid), and in a series of battles in **947** routed the rebels. Abu Yazid was cornered in
the **Hodna mountains** and died of his wounds in August 947.

## Causal Analysis

- **caused_by:** long-standing **Kharijite** doctrine among the [[berbers|Zanata Berbers]], hostile to both
  Umayyad and Alid claims that leadership required Qurashi or Prophetic descent.
- **contributed_to:** **Berber tribal rivalry** — the Zanata resented the dominance of the Fatimid-allied
  Kutama.
- **contributed_to:** **Sunni Maliki grievance** at Qayrawan, whose scholars had lost standing under Shi'i rule.
- **enabled:** **Umayyad sponsorship** from Córdoba, extending the wider Fatimid–Umayyad proxy war.

## Consequence Analysis

- **produced:** the **survival and re-legitimation** of the dynasty under a victorious new Imam-caliph.
- **produced:** the new capital **[[mansuriyya|Mansuriyya]]**, built on the battlefield of victory.
- **enabled:** al-Mansur's **conciliatory settlement** — a year's tax remission for all communities and the
  appointment of a **Sunni Maliki chief judge** over Qayrawan — shaping Fatimid governance of the Sunni
  majority thereafter.

## Historiography

The revolt is known mainly through **pro-Fatimid sources** (Qadi al-Nu'man, Idris Imad al-Din) that cast Abu
Yazid as a diabolical adversary and the Kutama as the faithful "Ansar," and through later Sunni chroniclers;
Jiwa notes an **internal dating tension** in the tradition, with the campaign's launch given as both **943** and
"summer 944." Modern scholarship treats the revolt as a revealing episode in the interplay of Berber tribal
politics, Kharijite religiosity, and Sunni–Shi'i tension in early Fatimid Ifriqiya. This wiki follows the
book's own second reference point (943) for the start date; the siege of Mahdiyya (945) and Abu Yazid's death
(947) are firmly fixed.
