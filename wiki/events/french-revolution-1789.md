# The French Revolution (1789–1799)

The **French Revolution** was the upheaval that destroyed France's *ancien régime* of
[[absolutism|absolute monarchy]] and privileged orders and inaugurated **modern ideological
politics** — popular sovereignty, citizenship, the nation-state, and the language of left and
right. The central event of the [[atlantic-revolutions|Atlantic revolutions]] and a hinge of
modern history. From [[new-cambridge-modern-history-v8|*The New Cambridge Modern History*, Vol.
VIII]].

## Narrative

A **fiscal crisis** — the bankruptcy of the monarchy, deepened by aid to the
[[american-revolution|American Revolution]] — forced Louis XVI to convene the **Estates-General
(1789)** for the first time since 1614. The Third Estate declared itself a **National Assembly**;
the storming of the **Bastille (14 July 1789)** and rural panic broke royal control. The
Assembly abolished feudalism and issued the **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the
Citizen (1789)**. A constitutional monarchy (1791) gave way, amid war with the European powers
(1792) and the king's flight, to the **Republic (Sept 1792)** and the **execution of Louis XVI
(1793)**. The radical phase — the **Jacobins**, the *sans-culottes*, and the **Terror**
(1793–94) under Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety — was followed by the
Thermidorian reaction and the Directory (1795–99), ending with **Napoleon's coup of 1799**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **fiscal collapse** of the absolutist state (war debts, an inequitable tax
  system the privileged orders blocked from reform).
- `caused_by:` **social grievances** — the bourgeoisie's exclusion from power, peasant burdens,
  and (in 1788–89) harvest failure and bread prices.
- `contributed_to:` **[[enlightenment|Enlightenment]]** ideas of reason, rights, and popular
  sovereignty, and the **[[american-revolution|American]]** example.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **end of the *ancien régime*** — feudalism, legal privilege, and absolute
  monarchy abolished; the **Declaration of the Rights of Man** as a charter of modern rights.
- `produced:` **modern ideological politics** — nationalism, the citizen-army and *levée en
  masse*, the left/right spectrum, and the revolutionary tradition (and its conservative
  reaction, Burke).
- `produced:` the **[[napoleon|Napoleonic]]** era and the **[[napoleonic-wars]]** — a
  generation of European war; the export of revolutionary law (the **Civil Code**) and the
  inspiration (and repression) of the wider [[atlantic-revolutions|Atlantic revolutions]]
  (Haiti, Latin America).
- Conventionally marks the opening of the [[1789-atlantic-revolutions|"long" 19th century]] /
  modern politics.

## Actors

- The **Third Estate / National Assembly**, the **Jacobins** (Robespierre) and **sans-culottes**,
  vs. the **monarchy** (Louis XVI) and the privileged orders; the **European coalitions** and
  **émigrés** against the Republic. See [[absolutism]], [[enlightenment]], [[american-revolution]].

## Historiography

The most contested event in modern historiography: the classic **social interpretation**
(a bourgeois revolution against feudalism — Lefebvre, Soboul) was challenged by **revisionists**
(Cobban, Furet) who deny a rising capitalist bourgeoisie and stress political culture, ideology,
and contingency. Debate continues over the Terror (circumstance vs. ideology) and over whether
the Revolution was the birth of liberty or of modern totalitarian politics.
