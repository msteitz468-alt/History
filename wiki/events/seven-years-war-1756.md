# The Seven Years War (1756–1763)

The **Seven Years War** (1756–63) was the first genuinely **global war** — fought across
Europe, North America, the Caribbean, West Africa, India, and the world's oceans — pitting a
British–Prussian alliance against a Franco–Austrian–Russian–Spanish coalition. It established
**British global supremacy**, confirmed **Prussia** as a great power, and set in motion the
fiscal crises that led to the American and French revolutions. From
[[new-cambridge-modern-history-v7|*The New Cambridge Modern History*, Vol. VII]].

## Narrative

Two rivalries fused into one war. In **Europe**, Frederick the Great's **Prussia** — having
seized **Silesia** from Austria in the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–48) — faced
Austria's determination to recover it; the **"Diplomatic Revolution" of 1756** reversed old
alliances (France with Austria; Britain with Prussia). Overseas, **Britain and France** were
already clashing over **North America** (the Ohio country) and **India**. From 1756 Prussia,
nearly overwhelmed by Austria, France, and Russia, survived through Frederick's generalship,
British subsidies, and the lucky "Miracle of the House of Brandenburg" (Russia's 1762
withdrawal). At sea and overseas, British naval and financial power proved decisive: **1759**,
the *annus mirabilis*, brought victories at Quebec (the fall of New France), Minden, Lagos, and
Quiberon Bay; in **India**, Plassey (1757) and later victories gave the **East India Company**
dominance in Bengal. The **Peace of Paris (1763)** confirmed the outcome.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **Anglo-French colonial and commercial rivalry** in North America and India.
- `caused_by:` the **Austro-Prussian struggle over Silesia** and the European
  [[balance-of-power|balance of power]], realigned by the Diplomatic Revolution of 1756.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` **British global supremacy** — France ceded Canada and its claims east of the
  Mississippi, and was eclipsed in India, making Britain the leading colonial and naval power.
- `produced:` the **survival and arrival of Prussia** as a recognized great power (keeping
  Silesia), shifting the German and European balance.
- `produced:` enormous **war debts** on all sides: British attempts to tax the American
  colonies to pay them helped spark the [[american-revolution|American Revolution]], while
  French bankruptcy fed the crisis of 1789 ([[french-revolution-1789|French Revolution]]).
- Accelerated the East India Company's transformation from trader to territorial ruler in India.

## Actors

- **Great Britain** and **Prussia** (Frederick the Great) vs. **France**, **Austria** (Maria
  Theresa), **Russia**, and **Spain**; with the American colonists and Native nations, and the
  **East India Company** and Indian powers, as theatre participants. See [[balance-of-power]].

## Historiography

Often called the real "first world war," it is central to debates on the
[[military-revolution]], the **fiscal-military state** (Britain's superior credit as the key to
victory), and the origins of the Atlantic revolutions. Churchill's description of it as the
first truly worldwide conflict captures its global scale.
