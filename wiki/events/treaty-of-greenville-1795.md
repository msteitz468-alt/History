# Treaty of Greenville (1795)

## Narrative

Treaty signed **3 August 1795** ending the Northwest Indian War phase after U.S. forces under Gen. Anthony Wayne defeated the western Native alliance. Opened most of present Ohio to settlement, recognized Indian rights to unceded lands (repudiating pure right-of-conquest title), and set a pattern for subsequent Midwestern purchase cessions within [[indian-removal-eastern-dispossession-1780-1880]]. Michael D. Green, CHNPA I.1 ch. 8.

### Alliance and Ohio River boundary

By mid-18th century Ohio Valley/Great Lakes opinion divided between **Nativist** (pan-Indian spiritual power/ritual/prophecy) and **accommodationist** (particularistic cooperation with Europeans) strategies. Despite factional struggle, **1775–1795** achieved a degree of pan-Indian unity for defense of territory and autonomy “never before and never again” west of the Appalachians (Green). English support (guns, powder from Canada) critical; British retained Great Lakes posts in defiance of Paris 1783.

Grand Council at **Sandusky** (fall 1783): delegates from more than thirty nations agreed the **Ohio River** should be the boundary with the U.S. (per Fort Stanwix 1768) and only Council representatives would negotiate. Congress refused sole Council authority; mid-1780s dictated treaties (Fort Stanwix 1784, Fort McIntosh 1785, Fort Finney 1786) with small unrepresentative gatherings. Grand Council **Detroit Dec 1786** denounced those treaties as invalid. **Fort Harmar 1789** reaffirmed right-of-conquest cessions (violating Ohio boundary) but introduced the principle that the U.S. should **pay** for lands ceded.

### War: Harmar, St. Clair, The Glaize, Fallen Timbers

Wabash–Maumee villages (Shawnees, Miamis, Delawares and others) led militant defense. **Sept 1790** Brig. Gen. Josiah Harmar ~1,500 men: burned five empty villages; ~250 killed in ambushes; retreat. **Oct 1791** St. Clair ~2,000 men ambushed by **Little Turtle** (Miami)—killed or wounded almost half the army; **630 dead**—most costly U.S. army defeat at Native hands. “**The Glaize**” at Auglaize–Maumee junction densified to ~2,000-person militant center by 1792.

**Summer 1793** Detroit talks: U.S. offered $50,000 immediately + $10,000 annually if Council accepted whole Fort Harmar cession (except land-company grants). Council: “Money, to us, is of no value, and … no consideration whatever can induce us to sell our lands”—proposed dividing the sum among poor settlers instead.

**20 August 1794, Fallen Timbers:** Wayne advanced; more than half of ~2,000-man alliance army had left to find food (Wayne advanced slowly; warriors fasted before battle). Battle two hours, few killed either side, but **British barred Fort Miami gates and refused support**. Wayne burned towns/fields. Defeat + British refusal cracked the alliance. Winter 1794–95 Wayne at Greenville shattered remaining faith in British; **Jay’s Treaty** required British evacuate posts on American soil—ending a decade of encouragement and supplies vital to the alliance.

### Terms (3 Aug 1795)

Indians ceded **all but the northwestern corner of present Ohio** plus many Great Lakes military-post sites. U.S. recognized rights to unceded lands (repudiation of right of conquest) and paid money, goods, and services. Peace; American trade extended; [[us-civilization-program|“civilization”]] policy made available. Hard fact: the **Ohio River was no longer a boundary**—the new line was a surveyors’ line on a map, unable to keep out settlers and livestock.

## Causal Analysis

- caused_by: military defeat at Fallen Timbers and collapse of British material support
- preceded_by: pan-Indian western alliance defense of Ohio River boundary; Harmar and St. Clair campaigns
- contributed_to: by U.S. land program (Land Ordinance 1785, Northwest Ordinance 1787) requiring supportive Indian policy
- concurrent_with: Knox–Washington expansion-with-honor doctrine and [[treaty-sovereignty-native-north-america]]

## Consequence Analysis

- produced: major land cession in Ohio; temporary NW peace; template of paid cession + civilization offers
- enabled: accelerated U.S. settlement north of the Ohio; later Harrison cessions 1803–1809
- followed_by: Nativist–accommodationist fracture; [[tecumseh]] and Tenskwatawa’s challenge to unrepresentative treaty chiefs
- contributed_to: [[indian-removal-eastern-dispossession-1780-1880]] long arc

## Actors

Gen. Anthony Wayne and U.S. commissioners; western alliance nations (Shawnee, Miami, Delaware, Wyandot, Ottawa, Chippewa, Potawatomi, and others); Little Turtle; British agents at Fort Miami (refusal of aid); Knox/Washington policy frame; Joseph Brant’s earlier coalition role.

## Historiography

Interpreted as coerced settlement after military defeat **and** as diplomacy recognizing Native collective agency and occupancy rights. Green stresses Greenville’s repudiation of pure conquest title for unceded lands within expansion-with-honor. Guides: Horsman *Expansion and American Indian Policy 1783–1812*; White *Middle Ground*; Dowd *Spirited Resistance*; Edmunds on later Tecumseh phase.
