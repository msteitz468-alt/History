# Darius' Scythian Expedition (c. 513 BCE)

About **513 BCE** **[[achaemenid-empire|Darius I]]** led a grand expedition across the
**Bosporus** into Europe and beyond the **Danube** against the **European
[[scythians|Scythians]]** of the south Russian steppe. Militarily an "expensive
stalemate," it nonetheless established Persia's **European satrapy of [[thrace|Skudra]]**
and the foothold from which the invasions of Greece would later be launched.
[[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 3f, Fol & Hammond) reconstructs it — almost
entirely from a difficult [[herodotus-reliability|Herodotus]].

## Narrative

Darius bridged the **Bosporus** with a pontoon of some 200 ships (an "amazing feat,
unrivalled until 1973"), commemorated with inscribed columns recording his vast host, and
watched his army cross. He marched **inland through Thrace**, subduing the **Getae** ("the
first people he subdued") and securing the rich Hebrus plain as a base, while the **Ionian
fleet** sailed into the Black Sea to bridge the **Danube** and await him. Crossing the
river, Darius drove **eastward through the Scythian lands**, burning the city of the Budini
and beginning a line of **eight frontier forts** at the edge of the desert, then turned
**westward** in pursuit of the elusive Scythian divisions, who fouled wells and removed
stock but **refused battle**. Harassed by Scythian cavalry and short of water, Darius
finally **slipped away by night** (leaving the sick and braying donkeys to mask his
retreat), regained the Danube bridge — held loyal by the Ionians despite (so Herodotus)
Miltiades' urging to destroy it — and withdrew through Thrace. He left **Megabazus** as
"general in Europe" with a large army.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the expansive momentum of [[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid]] power and a real
**strategic-economic interest** — Persians and Greeks shared a stake in controlling the
**Black Sea trade** (Scythian gold, grain, hides, furs) and in checking the **steppe
nomad** continuum that ran from the Ukraine to Central Asia (where Darius had already
fought the [[scythians|Saka]] c. 519).
contributed_to: Herodotus' framing makes the campaign a "precedent" for Xerxes' later
invasion of Greece — a parallel Fol & Hammond treat with caution.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the **satrapy of [[thrace|Skudra]]** in Europe (Megabazus then reduced the
Hellespontine cities, Thrace and the Paeonians) and the **submission of Macedon** under
**Amyntas I**.
enabled: the **invasions of Greece** — Thrace and Macedon became the land bridge for
Mardonius (492) and Xerxes (480).
contributed_to: the **permanent abandonment** of any Persian attempt to subdue the European
Scythians; Darius "inflicted widespread damage" and upset the steppe balance of power but,
failing to bring the enemy to battle, **secured no territory** beyond the Danube.

## Historiography

The campaign is a notorious crux of **Herodotean** source-criticism. Fol & Hammond reject
several of Herodotus' "precedents" and Hellenocentric touches (the revenge motive, the
exaggerated failure of Darius, the Miltiades-at-the-bridge story — which cannot be true,
since Darius afterwards left Miltiades in the Chersonese), while salvaging the **route and
strategy** from the Scythian-told account. The **date (c. 513)** and the **aims** of the
expedition both remain disputed.
