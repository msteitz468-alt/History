# The Lagash–Umma Border War

A multi-generational conflict (c. 2500–2350 BCE) between the neighbouring
Sumerian city-states of **[[lagash|Lagash]]** and **Umma** over a disputed
border-territory (the **Gu'edena**) — **the earliest war documented in
contemporary written sources**, and the earliest recorded **international boundary
dispute and arbitration**. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]]
(Ch. XIII, C. J. Gadd).

## Narrative

The fields in dispute were claimed by Lagash as the property of its god
**Ningirsu**, but repeatedly seized by Umma. Generations earlier, **Mesilim, king
of Kish**, had arbitrated and set up a boundary stele favouring Lagash — but
whenever Umma "imagined an opportunity" it overthrew the markers and occupied the
land.

- **Eannatum** of Lagash crushed Umma in the field, "slaying thousands," sacked
  the city, and imposed terms: Umma must not cross the boundary canal and must pay
  an indemnity of grain. He commemorated the victory with the **Stele of the
  Vultures** (so named for the birds shown devouring the dead) — showing the king
  leading his **phalanx** and the god Ningirsu's "great net" ensnaring the men of
  Umma. It is the earliest surviving historical war monument.
- His nephew **Entemena** had to re-impose the settlement after a fresh incursion
  by **Urlumma** of Umma, who burned the markers and sanctuaries; Entemena
  defeated him and installed a client, **Il**, a former priest.
- The struggle finally ended not by resolution but by the destruction of **both**
  contestants' independence: **Lugalzaggisi of Umma** sacked Lagash under
  [[urukagina|Urukagina]], then was himself conquered by
  [[naram-sin|Sargon of Akkad]].

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by: [[temple-economy]]` — the casus belli was framed entirely in terms
  of **divine land-ownership**: an affront to Ningirsu, which the ensi as the
  god's agent was bound (in a dream) to avenge.
- `contributed_to: [[unknown]]` — the **material** stake was the boundary
  **canal** and its water; control of irrigation was among "the fruitful causes of
  strife between neighbours."

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the brief hegemony of Lagash under Eannatum across much of Sumer.
- `produced:` the Stele of the Vultures — a foundational document of both military
  history and royal commemorative art.
- The endemic inter-city warfare it exemplifies `enabled` the eventual
  consolidation of Sumer under a single imperial power (Lugalzaggisi, then Akkad) —
  the [[naram-sin|transition to empire]].

## Historiography

Known almost entirely from the **Lagash** side (the inscriptions of Eannatum and
Entemena, the Stele of the Vultures); Umma's perspective survives only
fragmentarily. The conflict is the prime case study for Sumerian
[[early-dynastic-sumer|warfare]] and for the theology that drove it. Dates follow
the [[mesopotamian-chronology|middle chronology]].
