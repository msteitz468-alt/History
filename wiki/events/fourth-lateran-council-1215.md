# Fourth Lateran Council (1215)

## Narrative

The **Fourth Lateran Council**, summoned by [[innocent-iii|Pope Innocent III]] and held at the Lateran in **November 1215**, was the most ambitious general council of the high Middle Ages. Hundreds of bishops and abbots, with secular envoys, enacted a programme of **doctrine, discipline, crusade, and reform** that shaped Latin parish life for centuries — including the famous requirement of annual confession and Easter communion.

- `produced:` annual confession/communion discipline; anti-heretical definitions; reform canons for clergy and laity
- `enabled:` tighter parish surveillance and the later pastoral work of [[mendicant-orders]]
- `followed_by:` implementation uneven across dioceses; long afterlife in canon law

## Historiography

Standard accounts (Logan ch. 10; NCMH and specialist Lateran studies) treat 1215 as the legislative summit of Innocent's pontificate. Debates centre on implementation rates and whether the council's pastoral canons or its political/crusading agenda mattered more.
