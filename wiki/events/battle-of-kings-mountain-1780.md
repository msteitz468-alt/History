# The Battle of Kings Mountain (7 October 1780)

A rare and decisive **militia triumph** in the Carolina backcountry civil war. Major **Patrick
Ferguson**, inspector of Loyalist militia, commanded ~1,100 Loyalists on [[cornwallis-charles|
Cornwallis]]'s western flank — "unsupported by regular troops" — and threatened the trans-mountain
settlements with destruction.

## Narrative

In answer, ~900 enraged frontier riflemen — the **"Overmountain Men"** — crossed the Great Smoky
Mountains (a rare long-range militia offensive) and cornered Ferguson atop **Kings Mountain**. Shooting
from cover up the wooded slopes "in one of the most potent displays of marksmanship of the war," they
**killed Ferguson and ~240 of his men at a cost of perhaps 30**, capturing nearly all the rest. Officers
had to stop their men firing into the surrendering Loyalists; a week later several Loyalist officers were
executed in reprisal for **Tarleton's "massacre" at the Waxhaws**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Ferguson's **threats and depredations**, which provoked the independent-minded backcountry.
- `part_of:` the **Loyalist–Patriot civil war** that Clinton's paroles proclamation and British plundering
  had inflamed.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the destruction of Cornwallis's **Loyalist wing** and a decisive "shift of advantage toward
  the rebels," forcing Cornwallis to abandon his advance and retire to Winnsboro.
- `contributed_to:` the momentum [[greene-nathanael|Greene]] would exploit at
  [[battle-of-cowpens-1781|Cowpens]].
