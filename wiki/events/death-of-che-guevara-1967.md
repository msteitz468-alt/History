# Death of Che Guevara (8–9 October 1967)

Capture of Ernesto "Che" Guevara, wounded, near La Higuera, Bolivia on 8 October 1967; execution by Bolivian army the following day (9 October) on orders of the military high command, in the presence of CIA operative Félix Rodríguez. Body displayed, hands amputated for identification, secretly buried under the Vallegrande airstrip to prevent a shrine.

## Narrative

After months of isolation, supply failure, desertions, and the loss of half his force (including Tania at Vado del Yeso), Che's small band was cornered. Wounded in the leg/foot, he was taken alive. Despite some officers' preference for keeping him alive for propaganda and ransom, the high command ordered execution. He was shot in a schoolhouse at La Higuera. The body was flown to Vallegrande, displayed to the press and public (iconic Christ-like photographs), then secretly disposed of.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** Failure of the Bolivian foco (no peasant base, Monje/Bolivian CP refusal of support, terrain and logistics, rapid Bolivian response aided by US Special Forces and CIA). 
**contributed_to:** Debray and Bustos captures that exposed the operation and cut communications.

## Consequence Analysis

The death instantly mythologized Che. Photographs of the corpse became global icons. The 1997 exhumation and repatriation (facilitated in part by Anderson's reporting) closed one circle but amplified the legend. Bolivia's regime gained short-term prestige; foco theory suffered a major practical defeat.

**produced:** Enduring revolutionary martyr symbol detached from the man.
**enabled:** Commodification of the image alongside continued inspiration for insurgents.

## Actors

Che Guevara (captured and killed); Bolivian officers (Vargas Salinas, Selich et al.); Félix Rodríguez (CIA); Fidel Castro (strategic patron who could not save the mission).

## Historiography

Anderson's on-the-ground interviews (including Vargas Salinas confession) and later forensic work provide the most detailed non-official account. Bolivian and CIA participants gave varying stories; discrepancies remain on exact sequence of shots and who gave the final order. The event is treated as both the end of Che's personal revolutionary project and the birth of his global symbolic power.
