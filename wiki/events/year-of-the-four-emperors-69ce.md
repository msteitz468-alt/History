# The Year of the Four Emperors (68–69 CE)

The civil war that followed the fall of **[[nero|Nero]]**, in which **four emperors —
Galba, Otho, Vitellius, and Vespasian** — held power in little over a year, ending the
[[julio-claudian-dynasty|Julio-Claudian dynasty]] and founding the **Flavian**. Treated in
[[bowman-cah-v10-1996|CAH X]] Ch. 6 (Wiedemann).

## Narrative

**68 — Vindex, Galba, Nero's fall.** The Gallic legate **C. Julius Vindex** circulated letters
calling for Nero's overthrow; in Spain **Servius Sulpicius Galba** let himself be acclaimed
(April), styling himself "legate of the Senate and Roman People." Vindex's Gallic levies were
crushed by the Rhine army under **Verginius Rufus** at **Vesontio** (whose troops then offered
Rufus the purple, which he refused). But Nero lost his nerve, was abandoned by the praetorians
(bribed by **Nymphidius Sabinus**) and declared a public enemy, and **killed himself (9 June
68)**. The Senate recognised **Galba** as Augustus.

**Galba's failure.** Old, austere, and tied to the Julio-Claudians "so tenuously as to be
worthless," Galba alienated everyone: the guard (he refused the promised donative — *"he levies
his soldiers, he does not buy them"*), the freedmen of the *domus Caesaris* (whom he executed
and replaced with his own men), and **Otho**, his early supporter, whom he passed over as heir
by adopting **L. Calpurnius Piso Licinianus** (10 January 69).

**69 — Otho, Vitellius.** Five days later **Marcus Salvius Otho** — Nero's old friend and
Poppaea's former husband — had himself acclaimed by the praetorians; **Galba and Piso were
killed in the Forum (15 January)**. Meanwhile the **Rhine legions** had already (1–2 January)
proclaimed their new commander **Aulus Vitellius**, marching on Italy under **Caecina** and
**Fabius Valens**. Otho, though recognised by the Senate, the East, and most provinces, was
defeated at the **First Battle of Bedriacum** (near Cremona, April) and **took his own life** —
a restrained end that won him posthumous respect. **Vitellius** entered Rome as emperor.

**The Flavian victory.** On **1 July 69** the legions of Egypt, Judaea, and Syria proclaimed
**Titus Flavius Vespasianus** — the general prosecuting the Jewish War — emperor; the eastern
governor **Mucianus** and the Danube legions under **Antonius Primus** invaded Italy. At the
**Second Battle of Bedriacum / Cremona** (October) the Vitellians were broken and **Cremona
sacked**. As Flavian forces neared Rome, the **Capitol was burned** and Vespasian's brother
**Flavius Sabinus** killed; **Vitellius was murdered (20 December 69)**. Vespasian, recognised
by the Senate, founded the **Flavian dynasty**.

## Causal & Consequence Analysis

- `caused_by:` **the fall of [[nero]]** and the **end of the [[julio-claudian-dynasty]]**,
  which left no legitimate heir to the *domus Caesaris*.
- `caused_by:` the exposure of what Tacitus called the ***arcanum imperii*** — "the secret of
  empire, that an emperor could be made elsewhere than at Rome," by the armies.
- Wiedemann's analytic theme: **military power was necessary but not sufficient**. Otho's fall
  shows an emperor needed to *control* his armies; **Vitellius' fall shows that armies alone
  could not hold the empire** — he never won the client kings, freedmen, and procurators of the
  *domus Caesaris*, nor the eastern and Danubian provinces. Legitimacy still required mastery of
  the **whole** apparatus of patronage, Senate, and provinces, not just a battlefield.
- `produced:` the **Flavian dynasty** (Vespasian, Titus, Domitian) and a Principate refounded
  on a broader provincial base — the subject of CAH XI.

## Historiography

The narrative of 69 rests on **Tacitus' *Histories*** (Books I–III, surviving), supplemented by
Plutarch's *Galba* and *Otho*, Suetonius, and Cassius Dio — all shaped by **pro-Flavian**
tradition and hindsight (Tacitus wrote under Nerva and Trajan). Wiedemann cautions against the
sources' over-emphasis on **warfare** and against the old "native revolt" reading of Vindex
(and Civilis): these were Roman senators reacting to Nero's tyranny, invoking the **"Liberty of
the Roman People"** — meaning the right of Senate and People to *recognise* who held power, not
to restore the Republic. The events are a standing **transition page** in the wiki's scheme:
the first demonstration that the Principate's succession crisis could fracture the empire.

## Beard (SPQR, 2015)

Mary Beard, drawing on the same Tacitean formula as the *arcanum imperii* passage above,
frames 69 CE as the moment "the secret of imperial rule was revealed, that an emperor could
be made somewhere other than Rome" — each of the four claimants backed by a different
regional army bloc. She stresses that **Vespasian** ultimately prevailed with no
Julio-Claudian bloodline at all, instead using reported miracle-working (restoring a blind
man's sight, curing a withered hand) to bolster his legitimacy — an early instance of the
imperial-cult theology she discusses more broadly on [[roman-empire]].
