# The Liberation of Thebes, 382–379 BCE

## The seizure of the Cadmea (382)

In **382 BCE**, while marching north to the Olynthus campaign, the Spartan commander
**Phoebidas** — at the invitation of the pro-Spartan Theban faction-leader **Leontiades**
— **seized the Cadmea**, the citadel of Thebes, in time of nominal peace.
[[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 6, Seager) treats this as the most flagrant of the
abuses by which [[agesilaus-ii|Agesilaus]] turned the [[kings-peace-386bce|King's Peace]]
into an instrument of [[spartan-hegemony|Spartan]] domination. Sparta fined Phoebidas as a
gesture but **kept the garrison on the Cadmea**, installed Leontiades' junta, and
executed the captured democratic leader Ismenias — Agesilaus declaring that "initiative
might be tolerated if exercised for the good of the city." Some 300 democrats fled to
**Athens**.

## The liberation (winter 379/8)

The exiles, led by **Pelopidas**, returned secretly to Thebes and, in a daring coup,
**killed the polemarchs of the puppet regime** and roused the city; with help from
Athenian volunteers and the besieging citizens, the **Spartan garrison on the Cadmea
surrendered** and was allowed to march out. Theban **democracy was restored** and
**boeotarchs** were at once elected — the signal that Thebes meant to rebuild the
**Boeotian Confederacy**. Seager notes that the liberators even sent an embassy to Sparta
offering a return to the old alliance, which Sparta's harsh terms rebuffed.

## Significance

The liberation of Thebes is the hinge of the period. It exposed the
[[spartan-hegemony|Spartan hegemony]] as a tyranny that the autonomy clause was supposed
to forbid, drove **Athens** to found the **[[second-athenian-confederacy|Second Athenian
Confederacy]]** (378/7), and launched the partnership of **Pelopidas and
[[epaminondas]]** that would carry Thebes to victory at **[[battle-of-leuctra-371bce|
Leuctra]]** (371) and a brief hegemony over Greece. The botched Spartan **Sphodrias raid**
on the Piraeus (378), bribed (says Xenophon) by the Thebans to bind Athens to their
cause, sealed the new Athenian–Theban alliance.
