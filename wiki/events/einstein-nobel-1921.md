# Einstein Awarded 1921 Nobel Prize (Announced 1922)

**Nobel Prize in Physics for 1921 awarded to Albert Einstein "for his services to theoretical physics, and especially for his discovery of the law of the photoelectric effect," announced September 1922 (ceremony December).**

## Narrative

After years of nominations (mostly for relativity) blocked by experimentalist bias on the committee and opposition from Philipp Lenard, Carl Wilhelm Oseen engineered a compromise: award the delayed 1921 prize for the photoelectric law (a "fundamental natural law, fully proven by experiment") and pair it with Bohr for 1922. The citation explicitly excluded relativity/gravitation "after these are confirmed in the future." Einstein, traveling in Japan, did not attend the December ceremony; the German ambassador accepted. Einstein later gave his acceptance speech in Sweden on relativity and unified fields.

Prize money (121,572 kronor ≈ $32,250) largely sent to Mari; used to purchase Zurich rental properties.

## Consequence Analysis

Einstein never received a Nobel for relativity. The award ironically rested on work building on Lenard's observations. Money helped stabilize family finances amid German inflation. Einstein used the platform to discuss his ongoing quest.
