# Jewish Ghettoization in Occupied Poland (1939–1942)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]. Poland in 1939 held ~3.5 million Jews — 10% of the population, Europe's largest Jewish community, over 75% urban, 85% Yiddish- or Hebrew-speaking; in nearly a third of General Government towns Jews were the majority.

## Narrative

**Immediate violence.** From the first days: Udo von Woyrsch's Task Force murdered ~500 Jews in Będzin (8 September 1939, synagogue burned with flamethrowers); Jews were burned alive in the Dynów synagogue; troops looted, raped (the Nuremberg Laws no "hindrance in practice," as the diarist Chaim Kaplan bitterly recorded), cut off beards, forced men to smear one another with excrement and carved stars into foreheads — regular army as well as SS. Hitler toured the Kielce Jewish quarter (10 September): "They live in inconceivable filth"; Goebbels after Łódź: "These are no longer people . . . the task is not humanitarian but surgical," commissioning *The Eternal Jew* (1940). Shirer already concluded in November 1939 that "Nazi policy is simply to exterminate the Polish Jews."

**Failed expulsion schemes.** Eichmann's **Nisko Plan** (October 1939) dumped ~1,800 deportees from Ostrava and Vienna at a facility-less "reservation" on the San, guards driving them toward the Soviet lines ("Go over there to your Red brothers!"); Evans reads the episode as intentional cruelty behind resettlement euphemisms, not incompetence. [[frank-hans|Frank]]: "A pleasure finally to be able to tackle the Jewish race physically. The more that die, the better." The Madagascar Plan's afterlife paused the Warsaw wall in mid-1940.

**The ghettos.** Łódź: boundaries drawn 10 December 1939, sealed 30 April–1 May 1940 with ~162,000 Jews, most dwellings without water or sewage; Elder Chaim Rumkowski built an autocratic ghetto-state integrated into the war economy. Warsaw: concentration ordered 19 May 1940 (typhus the pretext), sealed 16 November 1940 — ~445,000 people on 2.4% of the city's area, 6–7 per room. From 22 December 1939 Jews over ten wore the yellow star; Jewish POWs (50,000) were worked and starved, 25,000 dead by spring 1940; the September 1940 medical report on the **Bełzec labor camps** (distinct from the later extermination camp) found men shoeless, 75 to a room. Mortality: Warsaw 10.7/1,000 by 1941 (~140,000 deaths over the ghetto's existence); Łódź 75.9/1,000 in 1941; a quarter of the children in Warsaw's shelters died in June 1941 alone. Deportations from the Reich, Stettin (February 1940, a third dying en route), Alsace, Baden, and Luxembourg poured more people in.

**Witness and response.** Emanuel Ringelblum's Oyneg Shabbos archive, Kaplan's and Dawid Sierakowiak's diaries, and Klukowski's record from Szczebrzeszyn ground Evans's account; cultural life persisted (theatre, Rumkowski's schools, Czerniaków contemplating *Carmen*). ~28,000 Jews found hiding outside the Warsaw ghetto, most surviving thanks to Polish helpers — "a small minority" against the blackmailers and profiteers; Evans notes that neither the Home Army, the government-in-exile, nor the Polish Catholic Church took a clear stance against the persecution, quoting a semi-official 1941 Church report that the Germans "have shown that liberation of Polish society from the Jewish plague is possible" (attributed precisely as Evans gives it).

## Causal Analysis

Ghettoization was `caused_by` Nazi racial policy and Heydrich's concentration orders, `contributed_to` by the failure of the expulsion schemes, and `enabled` [[the-holocaust]]: the ghettos concentrated, registered, impoverished, and weakened the victims whom [[operation-reinhard-1942|Operation Reinhard]] then murdered. The deportations and liquidations of 1942–44 are narrated on [[warsaw-ghetto-uprising-1943]].

## Historiography

The Ringelblum archive (recovered 1946/1950) and the survivor diaries are the documentary core; Evans stresses that mass death in the ghettos was policy, not by-product — a "slow death" regime preceding the fast one.
