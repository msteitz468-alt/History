# The Great Leap Forward (1958–1962)

The **Great Leap Forward** was Mao Zedong's radical campaign to transform China into a communist
industrial power almost overnight through mass mobilization — and the cause of the **deadliest
famine in human history**. From the [[cambridge-history-of-china|Cambridge History of China]] (PRC
volume, 14).

## Narrative

Rejecting the gradualist Soviet model, **Mao** in 1958 drove the merger of collective farms into
giant **people's communes** (organizing hundreds of millions of peasants for collective labour,
mess halls, and even **backyard steel furnaces**), and set wildly utopian production targets. Local
cadres, terrorized into compliance after the **Anti-Rightist** purge, reported fictitious bumper
harvests; the state requisitioned grain against those false figures while labour was diverted to
useless industrial projects and the backyard steel proved worthless. The result was the **Great
Chinese Famine (1959–61)** — mass starvation across the countryside.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` **Mao's voluntarist ideology** — the belief that revolutionary will and sheer numbers
  could leap over the normal stages of development.
- `contributed_to:` the **suppression of accurate information** in a totalitarian system (inflated
  reports, punishment of skeptics — Marshal **Peng Dehuai** was purged for criticizing the Leap at
  Lushan in 1959) and the **requisitioning of grain** on the basis of fabricated harvests.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` a **famine that killed tens of millions** (estimates commonly range from c. 15 to 45
  million) — the central catastrophe of the Maoist era.
- `produced:` Mao's **temporary political retreat**: pragmatists (**Liu Shaoqi, Deng Xiaoping**) took
  over economic management and quietly dismantled the communes' worst features.
- `enabled:` the elite rupture that drove Mao to launch the **[[cultural-revolution|Cultural
  Revolution]]** to recover his authority and purge his critics.

## Actors

**Mao** and the **CCP** versus the realities of the **rural communes**; the pragmatist leaders who
wound it down and whom Mao would later destroy. See [[maoist-china]].

## Historiography

Long understated, the famine's scale was established by later demographic work and archival research
(Yang Jisheng's *Tombstone*; Dikötter). The CHC, written closer to events, treats the Leap within the
Party's internal struggles and the failure of Maoist economics; it is now the paradigm case of
**ideologically-driven, information-blind catastrophe** in a totalitarian state.
