# Battle of Megiddo and the Death of Josiah (609 BCE)

The encounter at **Megiddo** in **609 BCE**, where Pharaoh **Necho II** killed King
**[[josiah|Josiah]]** of [[kingdom-of-judah|Judah]], ended the reign of Judah's last
great king and its brief revival of independence — the hinge between Josiah's reform
and the slide to the [[fall-of-jerusalem-587bce|destruction of Jerusalem]]. It is
treated by [[boardman-cah-v3p2-1991|CAH Vol. III, Part 2]] (Ch. 30, T. C. Mitchell).

## Narrative

By 609 [[neo-assyrian-empire|Assyria]] had all but fallen: [[fall-of-nineveh-612bce|
Nineveh had been sacked (612)]], and the last king **Ashur-uballit II** held out at
**Harran** awaiting Egyptian aid, for **Egypt now saw Babylon as the greater threat**
and backed the Assyrian remnant. When **Necho II** marched north "to the king of
Assyria, to the river Euphrates" (II Kings 23:29), his route crossed Josiah's recently
**reannexed northern territories**. Josiah — for whom a resurgent, Egyptian-propped
Assyria was the worst outcome — **intercepted Necho at the strategic pass of Megiddo**.
Chronicles says Necho sent word disclaiming hostility, but Josiah came to fight; he was
**mortally wounded by a bowshot**, transferred to a second chariot, and carried the
~100 km back to Jerusalem, where he died. Kings says simply that Necho "killed him at
Megiddo." **Jeremiah lamented** him.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the **collapse of [[neo-assyrian-empire|Assyria]]** and Egypt's decision to
prop up its remnant against [[nabopolassar|Babylon]]; Josiah's strategic interest in
preventing an Assyrian recovery and in holding his northern gains.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the **death of [[josiah|Josiah]]** and the abrupt end of his reform-era
independence. Necho deposed Josiah's son **Jehoahaz** after three months and installed
**Jehoiakim** as an Egyptian vassal — Judah passing under **Egyptian, then (after
[[battle-of-carchemish-605bce|Carchemish, 605]]) Babylonian** domination.
enabled: the chain leading to the [[fall-of-jerusalem-587bce|fall of Jerusalem (587)]]
and the [[babylonian-exile|Exile]] — the judgement the prophetess Huldah had said would
be deferred only until after Josiah's death.

## Historiography

Known from **Kings and Chronicles** (which differ on whether Josiah died at Megiddo or
on the road) and the **Babylonian Chronicle** (for the Egyptian–Assyrian alliance and
the campaigns of 610–609). The episode is a textbook case of a **small state's fatal
miscalculation** amid the realignment of great powers — and, in the biblical framing, the
moment Judah's reprieve ran out.
