# Congo Crisis (1960-1967)

## Narrative
The Belgian Congo became independent on June 30, 1960, marked by King Baudouin's speech and Prime Minister [[lumumba-patrice|Patrice Lumumba]]'s sharp rebuttal. An army mutiny broke out within days; Belgium sent 1,200 paratroopers on July 10. Katanga province seceded on July 11 under Moïse Tshombe, backed by the mining company Union Minière and Belgian (later French) officers; South Kasai also seceded under Albert Kalonji. The UN Security Council authorized 16,000 peacekeepers on July 14, 1960 under Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld.

McCoy periodizes the resulting seven-year crisis (1960–67) into four phases the CIA itself tracked: removing Lumumba; ending the Katanga/Kasai secessions; suppressing the eastern Simba rebellion; and consolidating [[mobutu-sese-seko|Mobutu]]'s dictatorship. President Kasavubu dismissed Lumumba on September 5, 1960, but Lumumba won a parliamentary confidence vote; he was then ousted in a CIA-backed coup led by army chief of staff Mobutu on September 14, 1960 — the CIA had given Mobutu $5,000 the day before. Lumumba was captured fleeing to Stanleyville on November 27, 1960, flown to Katanga on Mobutu's orders on January 17, 1961, and executed by a firing squad led by a Belgian police officer the same day. Hammarskjöld himself died in a September 1961 plane crash while flying to negotiate a Katanga ceasefire — McCoy characterizes this as "likely shot down by a Belgian mercenary pilot flying for the Katangans," though the cause remains contested and unresolved in the broader historical record.

Katanga and Kasai secession was reintegrated by January 1963. The Simba rebellion (1964–65) was suppressed with the help of white mercenaries — including "Mad Mike" Hoare's roughly 300-man force — and a CIA-recruited Cuban-exile air force of 56 aircraft; the Belgian-CIA Operation Dragon Rouge (November 1964) parachuted 320 Belgian troops into Stanleyville to rescue roughly 300 Western hostages held by Simba rebels. Mobutu seized full power again in November 1965.

## Causal Analysis
- `caused_by:` abrupt Belgian decolonization that left the Congo without a trained administrative or officer corps, precipitating the army mutiny and the collapse of central authority.
- `contributed_to:` Katanga's mineral wealth, which gave Union Minière and Belgian officers a direct interest in secession.
- `caused_by:` CIA Director Allen Dulles's August 26, 1960 cable ordering Lumumba's removal as "a high priority," backed by a $100,000 discretionary fund for station chief Larry Devlin — reflecting US Cold War fears that Lumumba would align with the Soviet Union.

## Consequence Analysis
- `produced:` Lumumba's assassination and the defeat of the Katanga/Kasai secessions.
- `produced:` Mobutu's consolidation of dictatorial power by 1965, sustained through the Cold War with substantial CIA financial and military support.
- `contributed_to:` (per McCoy's interpretive thesis — see [[mccoy-covert-empire-thesis]]) the First Congo War (1996–97), in which Laurent Kabila's coalition toppled Mobutu, and the Second Congo War (1998–2003), which McCoy describes (citing UN sources) as killing an estimated 5.4 million people and displacing 2 million — the deadliest conflict since World War II.

## Actors
- **[[lumumba-patrice|Patrice Lumumba]]** — first prime minister of independent Congo, assassinated January 1961.
- **[[mobutu-sese-seko|Mobutu Sese Seko]]** — CIA-backed army chief who seized power in 1960 and 1965, ruled 1965–1997.
- **[[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]]** — station chief Lawrence "Larry" Devlin ran what one scholar called "the largest covert operation in the agency's history" to that point, costing $11.7 million by 1967 (McCoy's figure).
- Mike Hoare — mercenary commander for Tshombe; later self-reported killing "between five and ten thousand Congolese rebels" (his own claim, quoted directly, not adopted in wiki voice).

## Historiography
McCoy's narrative draws heavily on Larry Devlin's own memoir, including a verbatim exchange in which CIA scientist Sidney Gottlieb reportedly told Devlin that a botulinum-poison delivery for Lumumba's assassination came on President Eisenhower's own request ("Yes, President Eisenhower has requested"). This is a single-source memoir claim implicating a sitting US president by name in an assassination order and should be treated with appropriate caution — Devlin states he did not personally use the poison or execute Lumumba, though he explored other assassination plots. The cause of Hammarskjöld's death remains a genuinely contested historical question, not resolved by McCoy's account alone. Source: Alfred W. McCoy, *Cold War on Five Continents* (2024).
