# Shoshenq I's Campaign in Palestine (925 BCE)

The invasion of Palestine by **Shoshenq I** (biblical **Shishak**), founder of Egypt's
Libyan **Twenty-second Dynasty**, in the fifth year of [[kingdom-of-judah|Rehoboam of
Judah]] — **925 BCE**. The earliest external event securely datable in the history of
the divided Hebrew monarchy, it is recorded both in the **book of Kings** and in
Shoshenq's own **topographical relief at Karnak**. [[boardman-cah-v3p1-1982|CAH Vol.
III, Part 1]] (Ch. 10, T. C. Mitchell) reconstructs its route.

## Narrative

For most of Shoshenq's reign Egypt was quiescent toward Asia — indeed he had earlier
**sheltered the dissident [[kingdom-of-israel|Jeroboam]]** during his flight from
Solomon. But the **[[hebrew-kingdom|division of Solomon's kingdom]]** in 931 left
Palestine fragmented and exposed, and in **925 BCE** Shoshenq struck. The Old
Testament reports only that "Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem" and
carried off the treasures of the Temple and palace, Rehoboam buying him off. But the
**Karnak list** of conquered place-names shows the campaign ranged far wider than
Judah: it swept through the **Negev**, up into the territory of **Israel** itself
(Jeroboam's kingdom was not spared despite the earlier asylum), and reached **Megiddo**
in the Jezreel — where a fragment of a **victory stele of Shoshenq** was actually
found. A horizon of **destruction levels** at numerous Palestinian sites is plausibly
attributed to this campaign.

## Causal Analysis

- **caused_by:** the **[[hebrew-kingdom|schism of 931]]**, which replaced Solomon's
  consolidated state with two weaker, mutually hostile kingdoms — a target of
  opportunity.
- **contributed_to:** the revival of Egyptian assertiveness under the new Libyan
  dynasty; Shoshenq sought booty and prestige, not permanent conquest.

## Consequence Analysis

- **produced:** the **stripping of Jerusalem's Temple and palace treasures** (the
  Solomonic gold), and a wave of destruction across Palestinian towns.
- **enabled:** a **chronological anchor** — synchronizing Egyptian and biblical
  history at 925 BCE and helping date the archaeological destruction horizon and the
  reigns of Rehoboam and Jeroboam I.
- Notably **did not** produce lasting Egyptian control: there was no follow-up, and
  Egypt lapsed back into inactivity, leaving the two Hebrew kingdoms to their rivalry.

## Historiography

A rare case where an **Egyptian monument and the Hebrew Bible record the same event**,
allowing each to correct the other: Kings gives only the Judahite, Jerusalem-centred
view, while the Karnak topographical list reveals the campaign's true northern and
Negev sweep — though the list's geography and order are debated, and not every named
place is securely located. The convergence makes 925 BCE one of the firmest dates in
early first-millennium Levantine history. (Egypt's Libyan dynasties are treated more
fully in Ch. 13 of this volume.) The collection is strong on the biblical and
Egyptological sides alike for this episode.
