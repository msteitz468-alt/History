# Battle of Xuân Lộc

The last major stand of the Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN), fought
9–21 April 1975 at the district town of Xuân Lộc, roughly 60 km northeast of
Saigon on Route 1. The ARVN 18th Division, reinforced by airborne and
ranger units, held the town against repeated PAVN assaults for nearly two
weeks — far longer than any other formation in the 1975 collapse — before
being outflanked and withdrawing.

## Narrative

After the collapse of the Central Highlands (March 1975) and the fall of Huế
and Đà Nẵng, PAVN's high command under Văn Tiến Dũng pushed south along
multiple axes toward Saigon. Xuân Lộc blocked the most direct approach. ARVN
resistance there was unexpectedly fierce, inflicting heavy PAVN losses and
briefly reviving hopes of a stabilized defensive line. PAVN eventually
bypassed the position with a wider envelopment, forcing an ARVN withdrawal
on 21 April.

## Causal Analysis

Xuân Lộc did not reverse the strategic collapse under way since the loss of
Phước Long (see [[phuoc-long-1974]]) and the Central Highlands, but it
demonstrated that South Vietnamese units could still fight effectively under
competent leadership and adequate supply — underscoring, by contrast, how
badly other formations had disintegrated.

## Consequence Analysis

The delay bought roughly ten days, during which the US evacuation of Saigon
was accelerated and President Nguyễn Văn Thiệu resigned (21 April 1975).
PAVN entered Saigon on 30 April, nine days after Xuân Lộc fell.

## Historiography

American and Vietnamese accounts broadly agree on the battle's facts;
interpretive disagreement centers on whether Xuân Lộc shows what a properly
resourced and led ARVN could have achieved throughout 1975 (a view stressed
in some US military-history accounts) versus historians who treat it as an
isolated exception within an otherwise structurally collapsing army and
state.
