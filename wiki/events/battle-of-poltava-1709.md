# Battle of Poltava

## Narrative
The **Battle of Poltava** was fought on July 8, 1709 (June 27, OS), and resulted in the decisive destruction of the main Swedish army under Charles XII by the Russian forces under Peter the Great (L2888-2890). 

After a freezing winter in Ukraine, during which the Swedes were cut off from their supply bases, Charles XII laid siege to the small Russian fortress of Poltava (L2884-L2890). The main Russian army came to the rescue of the fortress, confronting the Swedes in the open field (L2888-2890). 

The Swedish force (numbering 22,000 to 28,000) put up a fierce fight but was overwhelmed by the Russian army (over 40,000 strong) and its vast superiority in light and heavy artillery (L2890). The Swedish lines broke; the majority of the Swedish troops and generals surrendered on the field or days later near the Dnieper River, which they could not cross (L2890). Charles XII and his ally, the Ukrainian Hetman Ivan Mazepa, escaped to Ottoman territory (L2890).

## Causal Analysis
The battle was caused by:
* **The Ukrainian Campaign:** Charles XII's decision to bypass Moscow in 1708 and swerve south into Ukraine to rest his troops and join Mazepa's Cossacks (L2884).
* **Mazepa's Defection:** Hetman Mazepa secretly turned against Peter the Great, promising Cossack support (L2884). However, his calculations failed; he brought only a few thousand Cossacks, while Menshikov's rapid countermeasures kept Ukraine loyal to Russia (L2884).
* **Lesnaia Supply Loss:** The Russian capture of the Swedish supply train at the Battle of Lesnaia in October 1708 left Charles XII's forces short of ammunition, food, and artillery at Poltava (L2884-L2888, L2890).

## Consequence Analysis
The Battle of Poltava produced:
* **Geopolitical Revolution:** The defeat destroyed Sweden's reputation as an invincible military power and ended its hegemony in Northern Europe (L2908).
* **Coalition Revival:** The victory led to the immediate revival of the anti-Swedish coalition, with Saxony, Poland, Denmark, Prussia, and Hanover joining Russia to partition Sweden's Baltic provinces (L2898).
* **Russian Empire:** The victory demonstrated the success of Peter the Great's military reforms and discipline, directly enabling the acquisition of the Baltic coast and the proclamation of the Russian Empire in 1721 (L2890, L2902).
* **Turkish War:** The presence of Charles XII in Turkey instigated the Ottoman Empire to declare war on Russia, leading to the disastrous Pruth River Campaign of 1711 (L2892).
