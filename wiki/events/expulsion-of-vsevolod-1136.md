# Expulsion of Prince Vsevolod
## Narrative
In **1136 CE**, the citizens of Novgorod, assembled in their **veche** (town assembly), launched a popular revolution that resulted in the arrest and expulsion of their ruling prince, **Vsevolod Mstislavich** (a grandson of Vladimir Monomakh). 

According to *the Chronicle of Novgorod*, the townspeople were deeply dissatisfied with Vsevolod's leadership. They drew up a list of grievances: he did not care for the common people, he attempted to secure an alternative seat in Volynia against Novgorod's interests, and during a disastrous battle against the Suzdalians, he was the first to flee the field. 

The veche imprisoned Vsevolod and his family in the archbishop’s palace for several weeks under armed guard, before formally showing him "the road," declaring: "We do not want thee, go whither thou wilt" (l. 1263, 1287).

## Causal Analysis
* **Princely Neglect:** Vsevolod's attempts to treat Novgorod merely as a personal appanage and his neglect of local interests alienated the town's wealthy boyars and middle-class merchants.
* **Military Incompetence:** His flight from the field of battle destroyed his moral authority as commander-in-chief, which was the primary functional justification for a prince's presence in Novgorod.
* **Veche Power:** The existence of a strong, well-developed town assembly enabled the Novgorodians to coordinate popular action and enforce their collective will against the prince.

## Consequence Analysis
* **The Hired-Prince System:** Following 1136 CE, the Prince of Novgorod was stripped of sovereign power, becoming in essence a hired official of the city-republic. His powers were strictly circumscribed by detailed contracts: he could not own land in Novgorod, could not trade directly with Western merchants, and could not dismiss officials or hold court without the presence of the *posadnik* (the elected mayor) (l. 1321).
* **The Commercial Republic:** The expulsion accelerated the transformation of Novgorod into an independent "commercial republic" dominated by its boyar Council of Notables and the veche.
* **Relocation of Princely Headquarters:** The prince and his retainers (*druzhina*) were forced to relocate their headquarters outside the city walls to the Gorodishche settlement.

## Actors
* **The Novgorod Veche:** The democratic town assembly that organized the revolution.
* **Prince Vsevolod Mstislavich:** The deposed ruler, who subsequently fled to his father in southern Russia.

## Historiography
Historians of Russia analyze the expulsion of 1136 CE as a key turning point in the political fragmentation of the Kievan state. It represents the moment when the democratic and municipal element of the Kievan political heritage triumphed completely over the monarchical element in northern Russia.
