# Dissolution of Standard Oil (1911)

In *Standard Oil Co. of New Jersey v. United States* (May 15, 1911), the U.S. Supreme Court found [[actors/standard-oil|Standard Oil]] in violation of the Sherman Antitrust Act and ordered the holding company broken into 34 independent successor firms (ancestors of Exxon, Mobil, Chevron, Amoco, Conoco, and others), articulating the "rule of reason" standard for antitrust analysis. The decision was the culmination of decades of public agitation — Henry Demarest Lloyd, the Hepburn Committee, [[actors/tarbell-ida|Ida Tarbell]]'s *History*, and the antitrust drive of Theodore Roosevelt's administration. Ironically, the breakup sharply increased [[actors/rockefeller-john-d|Rockefeller]]'s fortune, as shares in the rising successor companies appreciated far beyond the value of the combined trust.
