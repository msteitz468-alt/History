# The Battle of Guilford Courthouse (15 March 1781)

> **⚔️ Staff-college tactical analysis:** see **[[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-guilford-courthouse|Guilford Courthouse — Tactical Analysis]]** in the warfare hub for the full order of battle, the phased conduct of the action, and the principles-of-war assessment.

The **Pyrrhic victory** that broke Cornwallis's army in the South. After the "Race to the Dan,"
**[[greene-nathanael|Greene]]** — reinforced to over 4,000 and outnumbering [[cornwallis-charles|
Cornwallis]]'s ~1,400 by more than two to one — chose to stand at Guilford Courthouse.

## Narrative

Borrowing **Morgan's [[battle-of-cowpens-1781|Cowpens]] scheme**, Greene deployed in **three lines**:
militia who fired once or twice and broke, a second militia line that "fell back in good order," and
Continentals who "counterattacked strongly." The British ground forward at escalating cost; at the
crisis Cornwallis "took the desperate step of ordering his artillery to fire into the confused melee" of
intermingled friend and foe. Greene withdrew in good order, conceding the field and his cannon — but the
British had suffered **~500 casualties (nearly a quarter of the army) to Greene's 264**. Cornwallis's
own officer Dundas called it "a victory … without any very brilliant advantages arising from it."

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Cornwallis's relentless **pursuit after Cowpens** and Greene's calculated choice to give
  battle on favorable ground.
- `contributed_to:` Greene's **defense-in-depth** that traded ground for British blood.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` an army too weak to hold the Carolinas; Cornwallis retreated to **Wilmington**, then
  marched into **Virginia** — the road to [[siege-of-yorktown-1781|Yorktown]].
- `produced:` the collapse of the Loyalist project: after the battle "they were no more inclined to come
  forward," and Greene returned to clear South Carolina.
