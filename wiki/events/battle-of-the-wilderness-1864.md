# Battle of the Wilderness (May 5–7, 1864)

The opening battle of Grant's Overland Campaign, fought in dense woods that negated Union advantages.

## Narrative

See detailed tactical analysis at [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/american-civil-war/battle-of-the-wilderness-1864]].

Heavy fighting in the thickets; fires added to the horror. Grant refused to retreat and moved south.

## Causal Analysis

Terrain favored defense; Lee attacked aggressively; Grant's persistence.

## Consequence Analysis

Set the pattern for the campaign; led to Spotsylvania.


### From Ellsworth, *Midnight on the Potomac* (2025)

Dense roadless thicket (visibility 20–30 paces); cavalry/artillery nearly useless; three bitter days; forest fires burned dead and wounded between lines. Ellsworth figures: **>17,000** Union killed/wounded/captured/missing; Rebel losses **upward of 12,000**. Combined with Spotsylvania called "largest battle ever to occur in the Americas." Press blackout; Henry Wing's report; Monday 9 May Washington celebrated a false victory while Lincoln cautioned "a great deal still to be done."

Source: [[sources/ellsworth-midnight-on-the-potomac-2025]]. Prefer warfare-hub OOB for tactical depth.
