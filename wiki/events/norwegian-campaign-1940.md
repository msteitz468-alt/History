# The Norwegian Campaign (April–June 1940)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]. Background: the Soviet–Finnish **Winter War** (30 November 1939 – 13 March 1940) — Soviet losses of 126,000+ dead against Finnish 50,000, the "Molotov cocktail" christened in mockery — exposed the purge-hollowed Red Army and fed Hitler's contempt for Soviet arms, while Allied schemes to aid Finland fixed attention on Scandinavia.

## Narrative

Vidkun Quisling, leader of Norway's tiny fascist National Unity party, urged a coup on Hitler in early 1940 and convinced him a British landing was imminent; days later Hitler ordered invasion planning. On **9 April 1940** Denmark fell in under two hours; in Norway the landings succeeded despite the loss of the cruiser *Blücher* to coastal batteries and ten destroyers at Narvik — the Kriegsmarine's surface fleet was crippled for the rest of the war. Quisling's self-proclaimed puppet government was rejected even by his own nominal ministers; his name became the generic word for traitor. Allied counter-landings (with French Foreign Legion and Polish units) retook Narvik on 29 May but withdrew by 8 June amid the collapse in France, the carrier *Glorious* sunk with all her aircraft; King Haakon went into exile on 7 June, maintaining the state of war.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced` German naval and air bases for the Atlantic war and Sweden's accommodation (troop transit, iron ore, materiel). The Commons "Norway debate" (Attlee: "Everywhere the story is 'too late'") cut Chamberlain's majority from 213 to 80; he resigned, "a broken man," and Churchill became Prime Minister on **10 May 1940** — the day the western offensive began (see [[fall-of-france-1940]]).

## Historiography

Shirer called the Allied operation a "debacle" of delayed mine-laying and confused planning. Evans treats the campaign chiefly as prologue: strategically useful to Germany but bought with the fleet that Sea Lion would have needed (see [[battle-of-britain-1940]]).

## From Shirer, *The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich* (1960)

Shirer's captured-document record shows the operation's origins lay with the German Navy (Raeder, Admiral Carls), first raised with Hitler on 10 Oct 1939, revived by winter iron-ore logistics and the 30 Nov 1939 Soviet attack on Finland, which raised German fears of Allied transit through Scandinavia. Quisling's collaboration is documented from Rosenberg's own Nuremberg-produced memoranda: a Dec 1939 Berlin visit proposing an Anschluss-style coup, naming Narvik commander Col. Konrad Sundlo as sympathetic — Sundlo later surrendered Narvik without a shot. The "Weserübung" directive (1 March 1940, quoted verbatim by Shirer) ordered the operation to "appear as a peaceful occupation." [EYEWITNESS] Shirer calls the German ultimatum delivered to Denmark and Norway at 5:20 A.M. on 9 April 1940 "perhaps the most brazen document yet composed by Hitler and Ribbentrop"; Denmark accepted under protest, Norway refused within 32 minutes. King Haakon VII's refusal to appoint Quisling as Prime Minister, delivered to his Council of State at Nybergsund (10 April), is quoted at length ("I cannot appoint Quisling Prime Minister... if so, abdication will be the only course open to me"); the Luftwaffe then bombed Nybergsund believing it had killed the King and government (a captured airman's diary read "Nybergsund. Oslo Regierung. Alles vernichtet"), but they had already fled to a wood. The cruiser *Blücher* was sunk in Oslo Fjord by the WWI-vintage guns of Oskarsborg fortress before dawn on 9 April, with the loss of 1,600 men including the Gestapo/administrative officials tasked with arresting the King and government — the single action that let the royal family, government, and gold reserves escape by train. German losses: 1,317 killed, 2,375 missing, 1,604 wounded; naval losses were disproportionate (10 of 20 destroyers, 3 of 8 cruisers sunk; *Scharnhorst*, *Gneisenau*, and *Lützow* damaged for months), leaving the fleet unfit for Sea Lion (Shirer 1960).
