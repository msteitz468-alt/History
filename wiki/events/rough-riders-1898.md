# Rough Riders (1898)

The 1st United States Volunteer Cavalry, popularly known as the Rough Riders, organized and led in combat by Theodore Roosevelt (and initially Leonard Wood). Iconic volunteer unit of the Spanish-American War.

## Narrative

Raised in May 1898 after TR resigned as Assistant Secretary of the Navy. Recruited from the West (cowboys, ranchers) and East (Ivy League athletes, clubmen). Trained in San Antonio. Shipped to Cuba. Fought dismounted at Las Guasimas (24 June) and in the assault on San Juan Heights (1 July), where TR personally led the charge up Kettle Hill. Suffered significant casualties from Spanish fire and disease. Returned to the U.S. in August as national heroes.

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` TR's ambition for field command combined with Wood's military professionalism and McKinley's political willingness to authorize volunteer units. The regiment's composition deliberately mixed social classes and regions for symbolic effect.

## Consequence Analysis

The unit's exploits (especially TR's personal leadership on 1 July) made Roosevelt the most famous American of his generation. The "Rough Rider" image became central to TR's political brand. Militarily the campaign demonstrated both the value and the logistical problems of hastily raised volunteers.

## Historiography

TR's own *The Rough Riders* (1899) is the primary narrative, vivid but self-serving. Morris (1979) reconstructs the fighting with attention to conflicting accounts and the gap between legend and terrain/reality. Later scholarship questions the decisiveness of the San Juan charge and notes high disease losses.
