# Invasion of Poland (1 September – 6 October 1939)

**Detailed tactical/operational analysis:** [[hubs/warfare/world-wars-era/second-world-war/invasion-of-poland-1939|Invasion of Poland — Tactical Analysis]] (full template: OOB, phases, 9 principles, quotes, Bzura, Warsaw).

## Narrative

German forces launched Operation Case White (Fall Weiss) across Polish borders from East Prussia, Pomerania, Silesia, and occupied Slovakia at dawn 1 September 1939. Air strikes on airfields preceded ground assaults. Two army groups executed concentric pincer: Army Group North (Bock) cleared the Polish Corridor then advanced; Army Group South (Rundstedt) with main effort (Tenth Army mobile corps) thrust toward Warsaw. Polish forces under Rydz-Śmigły deployed in linear border defense, including exposed Poznan salient. By 6 Sep Krakow fell; 8 Sep Panzers reached Warsaw outskirts. Poznan Army counterattack along Bzura (9 Sep) achieved initial surprise but was encircled by German armor redeployed from Warsaw drive; >100k Poles surrendered. Soviet invasion from east 17 Sep met minimal resistance. Warsaw surrendered 27 Sep after sustained bombardment. "Eighteen Days' Campaign." Polish government fled. ~100k Poles escaped to Romania.

From range (Kershaw Nemesis): "The Germans had won this campaign in its opening days." Five of seven Polish armies mauled/encircled. "Blitzkrieg" term entered vocabulary. Occupation immediately followed: Heydrich 21 Sept plans (Führer-approved) for Jewish deportation into 'foreign-speaking Gau' (Cracow), ghettos, leadership 'rendered harmless', Poles as seasonal migrant workers. Selbstschutz (Hitler-directed ethnic German militia) and Einsatzgruppen carried out mass shootings of Jews, clergy, nobility ('this matter had already been decided by the Führer' — Keitel to Canaris). Hitler to Rosenberg: Poles 'a thin Germanic layer, below that dreadful material. The Jews, the most horrible thing imaginable.' To Goebbels: 'more animals than human beings'; 'Asia begins in Poland.' 17 Oct meeting: General Government 'not to become part of the Reich'; 'We only want to get labour supplies from there'; 'hard ethnic struggle that will not permit any legal restrictions'; 'purify the Reich area too of Jews and Polacks'; 'the devil’s work.' Army protests (Blaskowitz) met with Brauchitsch defence of 'ethnic-political tasks... ordered by the Führer' and Hitler's 'You can’t wage war with Salvation Army methods.' 'Working towards' exemplified by Greiser (Warthegau): invoked 'special plenipotentiary powers personally given to me by the Führer'; demanded 'brutal, harsh, and again harsh'; competed with Forster to Germanize fastest. Hitler: report after ten years only whether 'purely German'; methods immaterial if successful. Parallel: backdated 1 Sept euthanasia order (Bouhler/Brandt) launched inside Reich as 'gangway' to larger extermination. Near-genocidal traits already present; training-ground for 1941 USSR.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** Nazi-Soviet Pact removing two-front risk + secret protocols dividing Poland; prior unopposed expansions proving Western weakness.

**contributed_to:** Polish geographic vulnerability + linear deployment choice (political necessity not to yield territory pre-invasion).

**preceded_by:** Munich appeasement, full Czechoslovakia occupation March 1939, Polish guarantee.

## Consequence Analysis

**produced:** Rapid German victory validating mobile doctrine; Soviet territorial gains; immediate global war (UK/FR declaration 3 Sep).

**enabled:** Subsequent Scandinavian (Weserübung Apr 1940) and Western campaigns.

**followed_by:** Phony War period.

## From Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)

**Campaign.** 60 German divisions (~1.5 million men), five armoured divisions spearheading, the mass of infantry on foot with 300,000+ horses; 897 bombers and 426 fighters (many Condor Legion veterans) against a Polish air force of ~313 mostly obsolete aircraft and a 15:1 German edge in armoured/motorized divisions; Slovak units joined for promised territory. Warsaw's garrison of 120,000 surrendered 27 September; the last units on 6 October. Losses: ~70,000 Poles killed against Germany, ~50,000 against the USSR; ~1 million POWs between the two occupiers; 150,000 escaped abroad. German dead 11,000; Soviet dead ~700.

**Premeditated annihilation.** Hitler to his generals, 22 August 1939: "the aim of the war lies not in reaching particular lines but in the physical annihilation of the enemy . . . Poland will be depopulated and settled with Germans." Evans's thesis: the mass murder that followed was not "barbarization of warfare" — Poland fell with "contemptuous ease" — but the pre-planned extension of prewar racial ideology; "almost everything that was to happen in the invasion of the Soviet Union from June 1941 onwards was already happening on a smaller scale in the invasion of Poland nearly two years before" (`enabled: [[operation-barbarossa-1941]]`).

**Propaganda pretext.** ~2,000 ethnic Germans died in Polish arrests, panic marches, and the Bromberg "Bloody Sunday" violence; Goebbels's ministry inflated the figure from 5,800 to an arbitrary 58,000 (probably on Hitler's order) to license the ethnic cleansing that followed — a retrofitted pretext (`preceded_by`, not `caused_by`). Terror began immediately: 531 towns and villages burned and 16,376 Poles executed by the end of military administration (26 October 1939); ~65,000 Poles and Jews murdered in the last quarter of 1939, about half by the Ethnic German Self-Protection militia ("You are now the master race here . . . Don't be soft, be merciless" — von Alvensleben); reprisal massacres (Wawer: 122 shot over a knife wound), POW shootings (Ciepielów: 300 machine-gunned), and Heydrich's liquidation of the intelligentsia from pre-war lists of 60,000 names. Blaskowitz's written protests ("boundless brutalization and moral depravity that will spread through valuable German human material like an epidemic") were dismissed and cost him his command in May 1940. See [[german-occupation-of-poland-1939-1945]], [[germanization-of-occupied-poland-1939-1944]], and [[jewish-ghettoization-in-occupied-poland-1939-1942]] for what followed; [[katyn-massacre-1940]] and the Evans section of [[molotov-ribbentrop-pact-1939]] for the Soviet zone.

## From Paxton and Hessler, *Europe in the Twentieth Century* (2011)

**Pretext and force ratios.** The immediate trigger was a staged Gleiwitz-style provocation: SS men in Polish uniforms, using murdered concentration-camp inmates as props, faked a Polish frontier raid on a German radio station to justify the invasion — the propaganda companion to Hitler's rhetoric about Poland's "inferior cultural value." Anglo-French diplomatic pressure delayed Polish mobilization by 24 hours in a vain last effort to avert war. Germany fielded 63 divisions (6 of them Panzer) against Poland's 40.

**Allied non-support.** Britain and France declared war but gave Poland no material help: the promised 16th-day ground offensive never materialized, replaced by a token French raid into the Saar and RAF leaflet drops over Germany. Warsaw fell 27 September; Stalin's Red Army entered eastern Poland on 17 September under the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact's secret protocol, following a second Ribbentrop visit to Moscow. Hitler's proposal for a rump Polish state was rejected by Stalin, who preferred outright partition. Source: [[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011]].

## From Shirer, *The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich* (1960)

**The false start.** Hitler set the initial attack for Saturday, 26 August 1939, 4:30 a.m. (Halder diary: "There will be no more orders regarding Y Day and X Hour. Everything is to roll automatically.") He postponed it at roughly 6:30–8:30 p.m. on 25 August after (1) the formal signing of the Anglo-Polish Mutual Assistance Treaty (5:35 p.m. London time) and (2) Mussolini's letter declining Italian military participation unless Germany supplied vast war materiel. Halder's diary at 7:30 p.m.: "Treaty between Poland and England ratified. No opening of hostilities. All troop movements to be stopped." Halting the Army required frantic recall efforts — General Petzel's I Corps (East Prussia) received the countermand at 9:37 p.m.; General von Kleist's motorized columns were physically halted at the frontier by a staff officer who made a quick landing in a small scout plane; in some sectors shooting had already begun before the order arrived. Hitler reissued the attack order at 3:22 p.m. on 26 August: "Get everything ready for morning of 7th Mobilization Day. Attack starts September 1" (Shirer 1960).

**Directive No. 1.** Signed by Hitler in Berlin on 31 August 1939 (Top Secret), it set the invasion for 1 September with attack time 4:45 a.m. (inserted in red pencil), and ordered that "the responsibility for the opening of hostilities should rest squarely on England and France" in the west, where German forces were to hold; the neutrality of Holland, Belgium, Luxembourg, and Switzerland was to be "scrupulously observed." The formal decision was issued shortly after 12:30 p.m. on 31 August — before Halifax's démarche to the Poles, before Ambassador Lipski's meeting with Ribbentrop, and before Germany's public "sixteen points" were released — demonstrating that the afternoon's diplomatic theater was pure stalling (Shirer 1960).

**Gleiwitz.** Shirer's account of the false-flag incident matches and extends the wiki's existing (Paxton/Hessler-sourced) coverage: SS officer Alfred Naujocks led the staged attack on the German radio station at Gleiwitz on the Polish border on 31 August 1939, under the codename "Canned Goods" for the drugged concentration-camp inmates left as fake casualties. Naujocks's Nuremberg affidavit states he received the order at noon from Heydrich ("report to Mueller for Canned Goods"); a prisoner was delivered "alive but completely unconscious... a lot of blood was smeared across his face"; the station was seized, a Polish-language broadcast made, and pistol shots fired. General Lahousen of the Abwehr testified at Nuremberg that all SS men who wore Polish uniforms in the simulated attacks were afterward "put out of the way" — killed to erase evidence. Hitler's Reichstag speech that morning invoked the fiction directly: "This night for the first time Polish regular soldiers fired on our own territory. Since 5:45 A.M. we have been returning the fire, and from now on bombs will be met with bombs." In the same speech Hitler declared himself "from now on just the first soldier of the German Reich," vowing not to remove his old army coat "until victory is secured, or I will not survive the outcome" — [SHIRER-THESIS] Shirer notes this is the one moment of truth in an otherwise dishonest speech, and that Hitler "would prove as good as his word," a deliberate foreshadowing of the 1945 suicide (Shirer 1960).

[EYEWITNESS] Shirer's Berlin diary for 24–25 August records the city's feverish atmosphere ("It looks like war"; "War is imminent"): antiaircraft guns being emplaced, bombers flying continually toward Poland. His 27 August entry records the government's announcement of food, soap, shoe, textile, and coal rationing beginning the next day — which, more than anything else, "woke up the German people to the imminence of war," provoking audible grumbling; on 28 August Berliners watched troops moving east in requisitioned moving vans and grocery trucks. [EYEWITNESS] On 1 September itself, the day of the invasion, Shirer describes Berlin as apathetic — laborers at the I.G. Farben building kept working, no one bought newspaper extras, no crowds gathered — a stark contrast he draws with the "wild enthusiasm" of August 1914. [SHIRER-THESIS] Shirer reads this "dazed" mood as evidence the German people did not want this war but followed Hitler anyway, a recurring theme in his account of German public docility under propaganda control (Shirer 1960).

**Weizsäcker's cover story and first Allied casualty.** State Secretary Weizsäcker's 1 September circular telegram instructed German missions abroad to describe the invasion not as "war, but merely as engagements which have been brought about by Polish attacks" — the High Command's first communiqués likewise called it a "counterattack." German submarine U-30 torpedoed and sank the British liner SS *Athenia* without warning at 9 p.m. on 3 September 1939, roughly 200 miles west of the Hebrides, en route Liverpool–Montreal with 1,400 passengers aboard; 112 were killed, including 28 Americans — Shirer marks this as the war's opening naval act: "World War II had begun" (Shirer 1960).

**Soviet complicity, first day.** On 1 September 1939, at German request, the Soviet Minsk radio station continually identified itself and extended its broadcast hours to aid Luftwaffe bomber navigation over Poland, recorded via Ambassador von der Schulenburg's cable to Berlin — an early instance of the covert cooperation detailed further on [[molotov-ribbentrop-pact-1939]] (Shirer 1960).
