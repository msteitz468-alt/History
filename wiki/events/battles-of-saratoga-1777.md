# The Battles of Saratoga (September–October 1777)

The **turning point of the [[american-revolution|Revolution]]**: the surrender of an entire British
army that brought **France into the war**. General **[[burgoyne-john|John Burgoyne]]** invaded from
Canada down the Hudson corridor to sever New England, but the supporting prongs failed —
[[howe-william|Howe]] sailed for Philadelphia instead of marching to meet him, and St. Leger's
column was turned back in the Mohawk Valley.

## Narrative

Burgoyne's advance bogged down: a mile-a-day crawl against American obstruction, the **Jane McCrea
atrocity** that cost him his Indian scouts and inflamed the militia, and the loss of nearly 900 men
in the **Bennington** raid (16 August). Facing **[[gates-horatio|Horatio Gates]]** in a strong
position at **Bemis Heights** (sited by the engineer Kościuszko), Burgoyne was checked at
**Freeman's Farm** (19 September), where [[morgan-daniel|Morgan]]'s riflemen and
[[arnold-benedict|Arnold]]'s aggression cost him "casualties he could ill afford." With his army
dwindling to ~6,600 against nearly twice that number, Burgoyne gambled on a second assault at
**Bemis Heights** (7 October) and was broken — General Fraser killed by Morgan's marksmen, the
Breymann Redoubt stormed. Cut off, Burgoyne surrendered **~5,900 men** on **17 October 1777**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Burgoyne's strategically isolated, logistically overstretched **invasion plan** and its
  uncoordinated supporting columns.
- `contributed_to:` American **numerical concentration** (militia streaming to the colors), Gates's
  sound defensive position, and the tactical aggression of Arnold and Morgan.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **[[franco-american-alliance-1778|Franco-American alliance]]** (February 1778) —
  Saratoga "served as a catalyst in negotiations for French intervention" — turning a colonial revolt
  into a **global war**.
- `enabled:` the strategic conditions that, four years on, "set the stage for the ultimate victory at
  [[siege-of-yorktown-1781|Yorktown]]."

## Historiography

A nationalist tradition crowns [[arnold-benedict|Arnold]] the hero of Saratoga; the *West Point
History* is more measured — his aggression "contributed to British defeat … but hardly turned the
tide, since the core of the American defenses was never tested." The victory was collective:
Schuyler's logistics, Gates's position, militia numbers, and Burgoyne's own errors.
