# Vichy Anti-Jewish Legislation (Statut des juifs), 1940–1941

French laws excluding Jews from public life, enacted by [[vichy-france]]
**without German demand** — one of the pivotal findings of
[[paxton-vichy-france-1972]]: "It is vital to expose the French roots of
early Vichy anti-Jewish measures, for nowhere else has the claim of German
pressure and French passivity been more insistent… Long before the Germans
began to apply any pressure, the Vichy government began setting up a purge
and quota system." Paxton found no direct German order for any of the 1940
legislation; Germany at that point "cared little enough about France's
internal composition to 'dump' German Jews there" (6,000 expelled into France
over French objections, 23–24 October 1940).

## The laws

- **27 August 1940** — repeal of the 1939 law penalizing antisemitic press
  excess.
- **First Statut des juifs, 3 October 1940** — excluded Jews (defined by
  three Jewish grandparents) from elected bodies, senior civil service, the
  judiciary and officer corps, teaching, the press, film, and radio.
- **4 October 1940** — prefects empowered to intern foreign Jews or assign
  forced residence; by summer 1942 ~20,000 were behind barbed wire in
  unoccupied-zone camps under this law.
- **7 October 1940** — repeal of the Crémieux decree (1871), stripping
  Algerian Jews of citizenship. Alibert's denaturalization commission (from
  22 July 1940) reviewed 500,000 post-1927 naturalizations and revoked
  15,154, of which 6,307 were Jews.
- **Second Statut, 2 June 1941** (under [[darlan-francois|Darlan]], Xavier
  Vallat as Commissioner-General from March 1941) — expanded bans, imposed 2%
  professional quotas and a 3% student quota, and ordered a census of Jewish
  persons and property. The law of **22 July 1941** extended forced
  "Aryanization" sales to the unoccupied zone.

## Causal analysis

`caused_by:` [[national-revolution-vichy]] — the "France for the French"
strand of indigenous reaction, forty years of Maurras's "anti-France"
rhetoric, and 1930s refugee xenophobia (Third Republic internment camps at
Le Vernet and Gurs predate Vichy). `contributed_to:` [[the-holocaust]] in
France — Paxton's careful formulation: Vichy antisemitism was "cultural and
national… in a French assimilationist tradition" rather than racial (veteran
and old-family exemptions; Vatican consultation), and Vichy "had not planned
to turn discrimination into genocide" — but "the French laws of 1940 and 1941
made the Final Solution much easier": the internments, the census, the UGIF,
the destitution by Aryanization, and the official respectability lent to
antisemitism. When the deportations began (Vél d'Hiv roundup, 16 July 1942;
~60,000–65,000 deported from France, ~2,800 returned), the machinery was
French-built. "Vichy's share of responsibility for the bestial deportations
of 1942–44 was probably greater than the German documents themselves would
suggest."

## Historiography

The German-pressure alibi ran through every postwar trial and memoir
(Peyrouton blamed German threats; Laval blamed German insistence) and rooted
itself in public opinion until Paxton's archival demolition. His account of
the paradox that Vichy's own attempt to reassert sovereignty over Jewish
policy (Vallat's mission "to harmonize the two zones") became "a major avenue
for German influence over Vichy" is now standard. See [[vichy-shield-thesis]]
for the comparative "lesser evil" dispute this feeds.
