# White House Screening of *The Birth of a Nation* (18 February 1915)

Private White House showing of D. W. Griffith's *The Birth of a Nation*, arranged through [[dixon-thomas|Thomas Dixon Jr.]], Wilson's Johns Hopkins-era acquaintance and author of the source novel *The Clansman*. Cox places the screening inside the continuum of [[federal-segregation-wilson-1913|federal resegregation]] and the administration's comfort with Lost Cause / Klan mythology.

## Narrative

[[dixon-thomas|Thomas Dixon Jr.]] — Johns Hopkins acquaintance, author of *The Clansman*, résumé fabricator (Wake Forest LL.D. story in Cox) — secured a private White House showing on **18 February 1915**, with other official Washington screenings. Griffith’s intertitles quoted Wilson’s *History of the American People* on Reconstruction and the Klan. Tumulty later managed damage-control alibis; the NAACP organized national protest while the film became a commercial phenomenon associated with the second Klan’s revival climate. Apocryphal praise (“history written with lightning”) remains a quotation dispute; the political fact of the screening does not. Cox places the event inside continuous administration racial practice, not as an isolated gaffe.
