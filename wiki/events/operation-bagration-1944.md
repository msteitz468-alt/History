# Operation Bagration (June–August 1944)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: the Soviet destruction of Army Group Centre — "one of the greatest and most spectacular victories of the war." Maskirovka convinced the Germans the summer blow would fall in Ukraine (Model drew reserves away from Busch's bulging central sector); from 19–20 June partisans blew hundreds of rail lines behind the front; then 1.5 million Soviet troops executed the encirclement style the Wehrmacht had patented in 1941. Hitler refused Busch's appeal to withdraw; in under two weeks ~300,000 German troops were killed or captured — 57,000 of them paraded through Moscow on 17 July 1944 "in a kind of Roman Triumph," many having surrendered rather than face another Stalingrad. The advance ran 200 miles before pausing to regroup; follow-on blows reached the Baltic (cutting off Courland), knocked Finland out of the war (Mannerheim's armistice, 4 September 1944), and threw Model's Army Group North Ukraine back to the Carpathians — Soviet superiority now resting on massed artillery (the Katyusha "Stalin Organ") and air supremacy bought by the Reich's fighter redeployment against the bombing (see [[strategic-bombing-campaign]]). Busch was sacked for the catastrophe; Zeitzler broke down and abandoned the General Staff; the arrival of the Red Army at the Vistula set the stage for the [[warsaw-uprising-1944|Warsaw Uprising]].

## Historiography

Bagration inverted 1941: deception, encirclement, and annihilation executed against the encirclers. Evans pairs it with Normandy as the point where the two-front war became terminal — German casualties in summer 1944 exceeded any comparable period of the war.
