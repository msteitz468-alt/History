# The Nuremberg Trials (1945–1949)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]].

## Narrative

The International Military Tribunal tried the "major war criminals" November 1945 – October 1946. Death: [[frank-hans|Frank]], Frick, [[goring-hermann|Göring]] (suicide the night before hanging), Jodl, Kaltenbrunner, Keitel, Ribbentrop, Rosenberg, [[sauckel-fritz|Sauckel]], Seyss-Inquart, Streicher, and Bormann in absentia. Hess got life (suicide in Spandau, 1987, "the last of the Nazi suicides" — Evans); [[speer-albert|Speer]] and Schirach twenty years; Dönitz ten; Schacht, Papen, and Fritzsche were acquitted (Papen was later sentenced by a German court). Evans notes the tribunal's legitimacy was "undermined to a degree by the participation of Soviet judges and by the Western Allies' carpet-bombing of German cities," and the conspiracy charge strained — but it "established a crucial precedent for the future."

**Successor and national trials.** The twelve US trials (184 defendants) included the Doctors' Trial (Brack and Brandt hanged for [[aktion-t4-1939|T4]] and the [[human-experimentation-nazi-camps|camp experiments]]; Mennecke died under sentence). Poland hanged Höss at Auschwitz (16 April 1947) and tried 40 Auschwitz staff at Cracow (23 death sentences); Jeckeln, Ohlendorf, Stroop, Pohl, Greiser, and Forster were executed elsewhere; the Tesch & Stabenow Zyklon-B suppliers were hanged by a British court while Degesch's Peters went free. The **Frankfurt Auschwitz Trial** (1963–65), driven by prosecutor Fritz Bauer, was for Evans "a crucial turning-point in bringing the attention of the younger generation of West Germans to the crimes of the Third Reich."

**The escaped.** Eichmann fled through the Rome "ratline" of Bishop Alois Hudal to Argentina (kidnapped 1960, hanged in Israel 31 May 1962); Stangl by the same route to Brazil (life sentence 1970, for 900,000 Treblinka dead); Mengele drowned unpunished off Brazil in 1979, identified from his grave in 1985.

## Historiography

The trials created the documentary spine of the field (Shirer built *The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich* on them) and the crimes-against-humanity precedent; the standing critiques — victor's justice, the Soviet bench (Katyn charged to Germany), retroactivity — are inseparable from their achievement. See [[denazification-1945-1955]] for the mass-level counterpart.

## From Paxton & Hessler, *Europe in the Twentieth Century*

[[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011|Paxton & Hessler]] corroborate the verdict
tally above (trial November 20, 1945 – October 1946) and add that contemporary German
public reaction was largely skeptical, regarding the tribunal as "victors' justice" —
consistent with the collapsing approval ratings for denazification generally (see
[[denazification-1945-1955]]). They frame the trial's larger significance as
establishing a durable jurisprudence of "crimes against humanity" later invoked in West
Germany's own 1960s Auschwitz-guard prosecutions — i.e., the Frankfurt Auschwitz Trial
already discussed above.
