# Treaty of Andrusovo

## Narrative
The **Treaty of Andrusovo** (Truce of Andrusovo) was signed on January 30, 1667, between the Russian Tsardom and Poland-Lithuania, establishing a 13.5-year truce that ended the Russo-Polish War of 1654–1667 (L2456-2458). The Russian delegation was led by the diplomat Athanasius Ordyn-Nashchokin (L2456).

By the terms of the treaty:
* **Territorial Partition:** Ukraine was partitioned along the Dnieper River. Russia obtained Left-bank Ukraine (east of the river) and Poland retained Right-bank Ukraine (west of the river) (L2456-2458).
* **Smolensk:** Russia officially recovered Smolensk, which had been lost to Poland during the Time of Troubles (L2406, L2458).
* **Kiev:** Located on the right bank, Kiev was to be left under Russian administration for a transitional period of two years (L2458). However, Russia never returned the city, and its permanent possession was confirmed by the **Treaty of Eternal Peace of 1686** (L2458).

## Causal Analysis
The treaty was caused by:
* **Military Standoff:** Thirteen years of continuous warfare, complicated by Swedish intervention in Poland and Ottoman threats, had economically and militarily exhausted both Poland and Russia (L2456-2458).
* **Ukrainian "Ruin":** Following the death of Bogdan Khmelnitsky in 1557, Ukraine fell into a chaotic civil war ("the Ruin"), with competing Cossack factions allying with Poland, Russia, or Turkey (L2458). This instability made a negotiated partition attractive to both major powers (L2458).

## Consequence Analysis
The Treaty of Andrusovo produced:
* **Partition of Ukraine:** The treaty permanently divided Ukraine, frustrating Cossack attempts to build a unified autonomous state and leading to decades of conflict ("the Ruin") (L2458).
* **Rise of the Russian Empire:** The acquisition of Smolensk, Kiev, and Left-bank Ukraine laid the territorial foundation for Russia's emergence as the dominant power in Eastern Europe (L2458, L2908).
* **Alliance against Turkey:** The treaty initiated a long-term geopolitical realignment, bringing Russia and Poland together as allies against the Ottoman Empire, which was confirmed by the 1686 peace treaty (L2802-2804).
