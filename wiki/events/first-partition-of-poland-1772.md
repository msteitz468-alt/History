# First Partition of Poland

## Narrative
The **First Partition of Poland** took place in 1772, representing the first stage in the dismemberment and eventual destruction of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth by its three powerful neighbors: Russia, Prussia, and Austria (L3440-3444).

In 1764, Russia and Prussia had agreed to cooperate in Polish affairs, supporting the election of Catherine II's former lover, Stanislaw Poniatowski, to the Polish throne (L3440). When Poniatowski tried to introduce reforms and granted equal rights to religious dissidents (Orthodox and Protestants) under Russian pressure in 1766–1768, Catholic nobles formed the Confederation of Bar and launched a civil war (L3440). 

Russian troops intervened to suppress the Confederates, provoking Turkey to declare war on Russia in 1768 (L3440). To resolve the resulting European diplomatic crisis, Frederick the Great of Prussia proposed a partition of Polish lands (L3442).

Under the treaty signed in 1772:
* **Russia:** Obtained White Russian and Latvian Lithuania up to the Dvina and Dnieper rivers, containing 1,300,000 inhabitants (L3442).
* **Austria:** Annexed Galicia (Red Russia and parts of Podolia and Little Poland) with 2,650,000 inhabitants (L3442).
* **Prussia:** Took Royal (Polish) Prussia (excluding Danzig and Thorn), which connected East Prussia with Brandenburg and contained 580,000 people (L3442). 

In total, Poland lost about one-third of its territory and more than a third of its population (L3442).

## Causal Analysis
The partition was caused by:
* **Polish Institutional Weakness:** The Commonwealth suffered from extreme political decentralization, characterized by the *liberum veto* which allowed a single deputy to dissolve the sejm, making the central government impotent (L3430-3432).
* **Austrian Alarm:** Russia's overwhelming military successes in the Russo-Turkish War alarmed Austria (L3442). Frederick the Great proposed partitioning Poland to satisfy Russia's expansionist drive, compensate Austria, and secure Prussian territorial integration without triggering a war between the three monarchies (L3442).

## Consequence Analysis
The First Partition produced:
* **Polish Reform Movement:** The shock of the partition galvanized the Polish nation to enact sweeping internal reforms, culminating in the constitution of May 3, 1791 (L3444-3450).
* **Partition Precedent:** The success of the dismemberment established a precedent of diplomatic collusion among the three eastern monarchies, leading directly to the Second and Third Partitions in 1793 and 1795 when Poland was completely destroyed (L3454-3456).
