# Sulla's Civil War (83–82 BCE)

The war by which **Sulla**, returning from the East as a declared public enemy,
overthrew the **Cinnan–Marian regime** and seized control of the Roman state —
the Republic's first civil war fought to a finish on Italian soil. Treated in
[[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]] Ch. 6 (Seager).

## Narrative

Sulla landed at Brundisium in spring **83** with five legions, his veterans, and
the prestige of the Mithridatic victory, declaring he came to avenge his murdered
friends but bore no grudge against the citizen body — a calculated bid to split
the regime from the new Italian citizens, whose rights he now promised to uphold.
He was joined by men who would define the next generation: the young
**[[pompey]]**, who raised three private legions in Picenum, and **M. Licinius
Crassus**.

The Marian government (the consuls Carbo, Norbanus, Scipio, and the younger
Marius) commanded larger forces but was hollowed by defections — at Teanum
Scipio's whole army went over. Through 83–82 the war raged across Italy:
**Metellus Pius** and Pompey secured the north (Faventia, the betrayal of
Norbanus); the younger Marius was bottled up and besieged at **Praeneste**. The
Samnites — still unreconciled since the Social War — fought on as the regime's
fiercest allies. In a last desperate stroke they marched on Rome itself, meeting
Sulla at the **battle of the Colline Gate (1 November 82)**, fought into the
night: Sulla's left broke, but Crassus' right won, and the Samnite army was
annihilated. Pontius Telesinus' reported cry — to destroy "the wolf in its lair"
— made it a war for Rome's survival.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: Sulla's **outlawry** by the Cinnan regime and the irreconcilable rival
legitimacies created by his [[sulla|march on Rome in 88]]; each side held the
other's acts void.
contributed_to: by the **[[marian-army-reforms|client army]]** — soldiers loyal to
their general's promise of land and booty rather than to the state — which made
private armies like Pompey's possible.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: **total Sullan victory** and, with it, the
**[[sullan-proscriptions-82bce|proscriptions]]**, the **dictatorship**, and the
**[[sullan-constitution|reconstruction of the constitution]]**. The prisoners of
the Colline Gate and Praeneste were massacred; **Samnium and Etruria** were
savagely punished and settled with **veteran colonies** (Sulla settled as many as
23 legions). Pompey was sent to recover Sicily and Africa (executing Carbo and
Domitius) and extorted a **triumph at 24, still an *eques***.
enabled: the careers of [[pompey]] and Crassus; the surviving Marians fought on
in Spain under **Sertorius** (the [[sertorian-war-80-72bce|Sertorian War]]).

## Historiography

Appian's *Civil Wars* I and Plutarch's *Sulla* and *Pompey*, all coloured by
Sulla's *Memoirs*. The scale of the Samnite role — and whether Sulla's later
near-extermination of Samnium was vengeance or policy — is much discussed.
