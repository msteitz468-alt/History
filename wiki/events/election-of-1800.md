# Election of 1800

The presidential election of 1800 resulted in a tie between Thomas Jefferson and Aaron Burr in the Electoral College, which was resolved by the House of Representatives in Jefferson's favor after 36 ballots in February 1801. It marked the first transfer of power between opposing parties in American history.

## Narrative

Incumbent John Adams sought re-election against the Republican ticket of Jefferson and Burr. The campaign was bitter, with Federalists attacking Jefferson's character and religion, and Republicans portraying Federalists as monarchists and warmongers.

The electoral vote ended in a 73-73 tie between Jefferson and Burr (due to the original constitutional rules where electors cast two votes without distinguishing president and vice president). The decision went to the House, where Federalists initially blocked Jefferson. Alexander Hamilton's influence helped break the deadlock in Jefferson's favor.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** Deep partisan divisions exacerbated by the Alien and Sedition Acts and foreign policy disputes.

**contributed_to:** The constitutional design that did not anticipate organized parties.

## Consequence Analysis

The election demonstrated that power could change hands peacefully. Jefferson called it the "Revolution of 1800." It led to the 12th Amendment. The Federalist Party never regained the presidency.

## Historiography

Malone's account in *Jefferson and the Ordeal of Liberty* emphasizes the high stakes for republican government and Jefferson's restraint during the crisis. Modern treatments stress the contingency of the House vote and Hamilton's role in the outcome.
