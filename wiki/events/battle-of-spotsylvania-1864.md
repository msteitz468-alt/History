# Battle of Spotsylvania Court House (8–21 May 1864)

Major engagement of the [[events/overland-campaign-1864|Overland Campaign]] following the [[events/battle-of-the-wilderness-1864|Wilderness]]. Ellsworth calls Spotsylvania "the largest battle ever to occur in the Americas" and notes its relative under-memory compared with Gettysburg — no *Killer Angels*, thinner public pilgrimage — despite horrific losses.

## Narrative

After the Wilderness, Grant and Lee raced to Spotsylvania Court House. Entrenched Confederate lines (including the Mule Shoe salient) met repeated Union assaults; the Bloody Angle fighting of 12 May became synonymous with close-quarters slaughter. Neither army broke; Grant sidled southeast again. Combined with Wilderness and Cold Harbor, Spotsylvania defined the 1864 casualty shock that nearly unseated Lincoln politically.

## Historiography

Operational literature (Rhea and others) is deep; popular memory lags Gettysburg and even the Wilderness. Ellsworth's claim of "largest battle in the Americas" is a popular-scale judgment — treat as his framing, cross-check definitions (duration, engaged strength, casualties) against specialist orders of battle. ## Ellsworth Detail (*Midnight on the Potomac*, 2025)

- **Race:** After Wilderness, Grant wheeled left; Lee arrived first and fortified.
- **10 May:** Emory Upton's column assault (~12 regiments, no stop to fire) briefly breached then withdrew when supports failed; ~1,000 Union losses in the assault.
- **12 May Bloody Angle:** Hancock massed ~20,000 for "the largest body of men ever organized on the continent for a single blow" (Bruce); ~22 hours continuous fighting; oak 22" diameter felled by rifle fire; dead piled five deep.
- **Scale (Ellsworth figures):** Spotsylvania ~18,000 Union / ~12,000 Confederate casualties; with Wilderness, "the largest battle ever to occur in the Americas." May 1864 = deadliest month of the war. Treat magnitude claims as Ellsworth's framing; cross-check Rhea / warfare hub for OOB.
