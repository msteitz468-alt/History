# Battle of Kiev (1941)

Enormous encirclement battle in Ukraine during Operation Barbarossa. After initial advances, Hitler overruled his generals and ordered Army Group Center to divert panzers south to assist Rundstedt's Army Group South. The resulting pocket around Kiev trapped four Soviet armies. Enormous material and human losses for the Red Army; significant delay to the drive on Moscow. Classic example of Hitler's operational meddling and the tension between economic/ideological objectives and pure military concentration.

## Stahel via Evans (2015)

From Evans's review of Stahel, *Kiev 1941* (in
[[evans-third-reich-history-memory-2015]], ch. 21):

- **Encirclement**: Guderian's panzers from the north-east met
  Rundstedt's forces across the Dnieper on 15 September 1941; four Soviet
  armies trapped; the city fell 19 September. The German claim of 665,000
  prisoners is, per Stahel, likely a propagandistic "manipulation of the
  facts," though the scale of the defeat stands.
- **Soviet command failure**: Zhukov urged abandoning Kiev and was
  dismissed as Chief of Staff (29 July); Budennyi backed him and was also
  fired; Tupikov warned of "catastrophe — a matter of a couple of days"
  and was dismissed as "panicky." Stalin held the city partly for
  symbolism and Allied optics.
- **Pyrrhic victory**: nearly 500,000 German casualties by 16 September
  1941 overall; Guderian's Panzer Group 2 lost 32,000 men; Model's 3rd
  Panzer Division fell from ~200 tanks to 10 — attrition that crippled
  the subsequent Moscow offensive.
- **Atrocities**: after "Stalin's socks" mutilation killings of captured
  Germans, 4,000 Red Army POWs were summarily shot in reprisal. Following
  NKVD-planted bombs in the fallen city, the occupiers blamed Kiev's Jews
  and on 29 September marched 34,000 men, women and children to **Babi
  Yar**, shooting them all over two days — "the largest single
  antisemitic mass extermination of the war to date."
- **Starvation policy**: "Kiev must starve" (agricultural planners'
  confidential meetings); population fell from ~815,000 to 295,000 by
  October 1943; Hitler had wanted the city razed outright. See
  [[hunger-plan-1941]].
