# The Battle of the Chesapeake (5 September 1781)

> **⚔️ Staff-college tactical analysis:** see **[[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-the-chesapeake|The Chesapeake — Naval Tactical Analysis]]** in the warfare hub for the full order of battle, the phased conduct of the action, and the principles-of-war assessment.

The naval action that decided the **[[siege-of-yorktown-1781|Yorktown]] campaign** — and, with it, the
war. As [[washington-george|Washington]] and Rochambeau marched south, the French admiral **de Grasse**
brought **26 ships of the line** from the West Indies to the Chesapeake; the British **Thomas Graves**
arrived with **19** to contest control.

## Narrative

"For two hours the two sides blasted away at each other." No ship was lost, but both fleets were badly
damaged — and the British rear under **Samuel Hood** "failed to bring the ships of the rear division
fully into the battle," for which Conway assigns Hood "a larger share of the blame." Outnumbered and
with several ships in poor condition, Graves judged he had "no choice but to return to New York to
refit," abandoning the bay. Though **tactically inconclusive**, the battle "precluded any resupply or
naval withdrawal of Cornwallis's army."

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the campaign-long contest for **command of the Chesapeake**, and French **numerical
  superiority** off the capes.
- `contributed_to:` Hood's failure to engage and the poor material state of the British fleet.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **loss of British sea control**, which trapped [[cornwallis-charles|Cornwallis]] at
  Yorktown without relief, resupply, or escape. Conway: "Perhaps this was the decisive moment of the
  whole campaign."
