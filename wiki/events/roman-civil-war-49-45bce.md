# Caesar's Civil War (49–45 BCE)

The war between **[[julius-caesar|Caesar]]** and **[[pompey|Pompey]]** (and the
Senate) that destroyed the Republic's free politics and left Caesar master of the
Roman world. Treated in [[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]] Ch. 11 (Rawson); the primary
account is Caesar's own **Bellum Civile**.

## Narrative

**The Rubicon and Italy (49).** When the Senate (7 Jan. 49) authorized Pompey to
defend the Republic and the tribunes (Antony, Cassius) fled to him, **Caesar crossed
the Rubicon** with the Thirteenth Legion ("the die is cast"). His celebrated
**clemency at Corfinium** (releasing Domitius Ahenobarbus and his men) reassured
Italy; **Pompey abandoned Rome and Italy** for the East, crossing from Brundisium —
a decision Rawson (with Cicero and Napoleon) judges politically disastrous, however
militarily rational. Caesar seized the treasury (threatening the tribune Metellus),
then secured the West.

**Spain and the periphery (49).** Caesar defeated the Pompeian legates **Afranius
and Petreius at Ilerda** and took Spain; **Massilia** was besieged and reduced. But
**Curio**, sent to Africa, was destroyed with his army by **King Juba**.

**Pharsalus (48).** Made dictator to hold his own election as consul, Caesar crossed
to Greece, was checked at **Dyrrachium**, then — when Pompey, pressed by his
over-confident optimate allies, gave battle — **crushed him at Pharsalus (9 Aug.
48)** despite inferior numbers. Caesar burned Pompey's correspondence and forgave
suppliants (including **Brutus**). **Pompey fled to Egypt and was murdered as he
landed.**

**Egypt and Zela (48–47).** Caesar followed to **Alexandria**, was caught in the
**Alexandrian War**, installed **[[ptolemaic-egypt|Cleopatra]]** (his mistress, who
bore **Caesarion**), then crushed **Pharnaces** at **Zela** ("*veni, vidi, vici*").

**Thapsus and Munda (46–45).** The Republican coalition regrouped in **Africa** under
**Metellus Scipio**, **Cato**, **Labienus**, and **Juba**; Caesar destroyed it at
**Thapsus (46)**, after which **Cato committed suicide at Utica** rather than accept
Caesar's pardon. The last Pompeian armies under Pompey's sons and Labienus were
beaten in a desperate battle at **Munda (45)** in Spain — Caesar said he fought "for
his life." The civil war was over.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the **optimate *factio*** (Cato, the Marcelli, Lentulus) driving to strip
Caesar of his command before he could pass to a safe second consulship; and Caesar's
refusal to be destroyed in the courts. contributed_to: by the dissolution of the
**[[first-triumvirate|triumvirate]]** (Julia's and Crassus' deaths) that left Caesar
and Pompey unmediated rivals.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: **Caesar's total victory** and **[[caesars-dictatorship|dictatorship]]**;
the deaths of **Pompey, Cato, Metellus Scipio, Labienus**, and the Republican cause's
leaders.
enabled: the end of free Republican politics, Caesar's autocracy, and — through the
resentment it bred — the **[[julius-caesar-assassination-44bce|Ides of March]]** and
the further civil wars (CAH X).

## Historiography

Caesar's **Bellum Civile** (an apologetic propaganda document, of disputed date) and
the *Corpus Caesarianum* (the Alexandrian, African, Spanish Wars by lesser hands),
checked against **Cicero's letters** — the richest contemporary record, often
exposing Caesar's bias — and the later Plutarch, Suetonius, Appian, and Dio. Rawson
stresses both Caesar's self-justification and the genuine uncertainty of the war's
outcome.

### OHCW (Boardman/Griffin/Murray 1986) — Griffin; Stockton (R7)

Griffin (Ch. 19): structural question is why oligarchs destroyed the system that
benefited them — moral degeneration from imperial wealth; rising success standards;
laws “bent and ignored” too often by 49 to command obedience. Rubicon dispute: popular
grants and tribunician vetoes vs Optimate control of foreign affairs; Caesar crossed in
defence of tribunes’ rights and his honour; Pompey went east (“Sulla did it: why not
I?”); Cicero lamented both aimed only at *dominatio*. Victory destroyed the system in
which Caesar wanted primacy; reforms preceded Ides of March 44. Stockton (Ch. 21, R7)
treats post-Caesar wars 44–30 as continuum (triumviral proscriptions, Philippi,
Perusia, Sicilian war, Actium) — “rule of law was set aside” for thirteen years after
Matius’ bleak question.

Source: [[boardman-oxford-history-classical-world-1986]] (Ch. 19 Griffin; Ch. 21 Stockton).

## Flower, *Roman Republics* (2010)

Harriet I. Flower ([[flower-roman-republics-2010|*Roman Republics*]]) reframes January
49 as **aftermath of functional republican end ~60**, not the first failure of a still-
working traditional republic ([[roman-republics-periodization]];
[[fall-of-roman-republic]]). Civil war resulted from breakup of the “Big Three”
(Pompey, Crassus, Caesar) after Crassus’s death, persistent chaos in Rome, and Pompey’s
sole consulship — “the final disintegration of republican government took place in
60/59 even though it was not signaled by immediate civil war.” There was no functional
republic in 51–50 to negotiate Caesar’s return; Caesar’s invasion targeted a community
that had already lost integrity and shape. Clash developed because no mediating group of
leaders (in the senate or outside) had authority to stop client armies. Destabilization
in the 50s: election cycle disrupted especially after Luca 56; years beginning without
consuls; system broken by 54–52; after Clodius’ murder the mob burned Sulla’s senate
house as funeral pyre before Pompey’s sole consulship. On Flower’s map the 50s “no longer
belong to a period of republican history.”

## Woolf, *Rome: An Empire's Story* (2012)

Source: [[woolf-rome-empires-story-2012]].

After Crassus’ death, Pompey–Caesar war from ~50 fought mostly in Balkans; Pompey defeated at Pharsalus (48), murdered in Egypt; Caesar tracked Pompeians until assassination without institutionalizing his position. Woolf’s arc: whenever only one super-general remained, Roman action briefly cohered; “From the chaos of the Republican empire, Rome had sleepwalked into military autocracy, and it worked.”
