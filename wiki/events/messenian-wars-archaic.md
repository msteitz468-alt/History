# Messenian Wars (Archaic)

Traditional First and Second Messenian Wars by which Sparta conquered Messenia and reduced much of its population to dependent (helot) status. [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] Ch.7 treats conquest, territory, and exploitation with heavy attention to source limits and to the complexity of Archaic dependency.

## Narrative

**Modern consensus model** (contra Spartan aetiology of maiden-rape at Artemis Limnatis): the First Messenian War was driven by territorial ambition; Messenians were enslaved on their land either after the First War or after a failed Second War rebellion; they were forced to deliver a substantial share of produce (helots, like southern Laconians); guaranteed agrarian income enabled Spartan full-time military training needed to police the helots; a Third War followed in the 460s; liberation came in 369 by Epaminondas after Leuctra, with a new capital Messene on Ithome.

Hall notes that archaeological field survey data “seem to indicate that the Eurotas valley in the heart of Laconia was not extensively exploited until the end of the sixth century,” undercutting simple early territorial-ambition models for the first conquest.

**Dating is fragile.** Pausanias dates the First War 743–724 and the Second 685–668 — probably from Tyrtaeus fr. 5 (Theopompus conquers Messene) plus Herodotus’ eight generations before the Persian War. Nikhoria destruction ~750 and Olympic victor absence after 736 are weak confirmations. Second War dating often rests on Tyrtaeus’ “fathers of our fathers” plus the assumption that Tyrtaeus is a single mid-seventh-century author (Suda/Jerome ~640–633). If the phrase means “forefathers” generally, or if Tyrtaean verses are a cumulative tradition, “all hopes of accurately dating the Second Messenian War founder.”

**Second War as possible post-Epaminondan creation.** No explicit “Second Messenian War” before the fourth century; some see propagandistic invention for liberated Messenia’s national history (Aristomenes romance). Yet Tyrtaeus papyri mention Messenians and probably Argives/Arcadians in battle. Hall’s working picture: First War theater focused on capture of Messene/Ithome; subsequent progressive subjugation across the late eighth to ~600; later literature telescoped resistance into a discrete Second War.

**Tyrtaeus as primary witness.** Pausanias 4.14.4–5 quotes Tyrtaeus frs. 6–7: Messenians “just like asses oppressed by heavy burdens, bringing to their masters out of hateful necessity half of all the fruits that the earth bears,” and an obligation of men and wives to bewail masters at deaths of kings/officials — key primary evidence for economic and ritual dependency, not a campaign narrative.

**Scope of conquest.** Tyrtaeus fr. 5 speaks of capturing Messene, not annexing all Messenia. Classical Messene is a fourth-century foundation, but excavations show eighth-century activity at Epaminondas’ site under Ithome (also named by Tyrtaeus). Initial Spartan intervention was likely concentrated on the lower Pamisos Valley.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` not reducible to a single documented *casus belli*; modern scholars prefer territorial ambition over the Limnatis aetiology.
- `contributed_to:` Spartan need for dependent labor and agricultural surplus; elite competition and regional warfare patterns of Archaic Peloponnese.
- `preceded_by:` Dark Age / early Archaic settlement reconfiguration in Laconia and Messenia (see also [[controversies/dorian-migration-historicity]]).
- Hall’s broader claim: Archaic conflicts often prioritized **exploitation of populations reduced to dependency** over full territorial annexation.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` [[concepts/helotage|helotage]] as structural base of Classical Spartan society (with Laconian helots and perioikoi as related spectrum).
- `enabled:` full-time Spartan military culture and the ideology of *eunomia* filtered through the [[concepts/spartan-mirage|Spartan mirage]].
- Long-term: Messenian diaspora identity and Theban-backed liberation (369); new Messene on Ithome.

## Actors

Sparta (Spartiates, kings such as Theopompus in tradition); Messenians as free opponents then dependents; possible Arcadian/Argive allies in poetic tradition; perioikic communities in parts of Messenia (Thouriatai, Aithiaieis; coastal Asine/Methone refugees in some accounts).

## Historiography

Literary tradition is late and ideological. Tyrtaeus is the primary near-contemporary poetic witness for Spartan warfare ideology and dependency conditions more than for campaign chronology. Pausanias synthesizes much later romance. Hall stresses spectrum of free and unfree labor rather than simple annexation-and-enslavement; sanctuary dedications suggest perioikoi in parts of Messenia; Tyrtaeus may imply some Messenians abandoned plots and fled.
