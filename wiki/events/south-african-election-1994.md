# 1994 South African General Election

## Narrative

Held from 26 to 29 April 1994, this was South Africa's first election held under universal adult suffrage, ending centuries of white-minority political monopoly and formally dismantling the apartheid state. Long queues of voters, many casting a ballot for the first time in their lives, became an enduring image of the transition. The African National Congress, led by [[mandela-nelson|Nelson Mandela]], won an outright majority (just under two-thirds of the vote), with the National Party of outgoing president [[de-klerk-fw|F. W. de Klerk]] and the Inkatha Freedom Party as the next-largest parties. The resulting Government of National Unity, mandated by the interim constitution, incorporated de Klerk and Inkatha leader Mangosuthu Buthelezi as deputy president and cabinet ministers alongside the ANC.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** the negotiated settlement reached through the CODESA process (1991–1993) between the apartheid government and liberation movements, itself made possible by Mandela's 1990 release and the unbanning of the ANC, PAC, and South African Communist Party.

## Consequence Analysis

**produced:** Mandela's presidency (1994–1999) and the formal legal end of apartheid; a Government of National Unity intended to manage the transition and reassure the white minority; the beginning of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission process to address apartheid-era human rights abuses.

## Historiography

The election is generally treated in the historiography as the negotiated, comparatively peaceful culmination of the anti-apartheid struggle, though scholars debate how much credit belongs to elite negotiation (Mandela–de Klerk) versus decades of internal resistance, international sanctions, and the structural unsustainability of apartheid's economic and demographic arrangements. The relatively low level of large-scale violence during the transition, given widely feared civil-war scenarios beforehand, remains a subject of comparative political-transition scholarship.
