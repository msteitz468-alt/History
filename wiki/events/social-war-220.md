## Narrative

The Social War (220–217 BCE) — the "war of the allies" (*symmachoi*) — was fought by the
young king **[[philip-v|Philip V]]**, as *hegemon* of the **Hellenic Symmachy** founded by
[[antigonus-iii-doson|Doson]], and the **[[achaean-league|Achaean League]]** against the
**[[aetolian-league|Aetolian League]]** and its allies (Sparta, Elis). CAH VII.1 ch. 12
(Walbank) treats it as the volume's final episode.

The Aetolians, alarmed at being hemmed in after [[battle-of-sellasia-222|Sellasia]] and
true to their tradition of **raiding for plunder**, attacked Achaea's neighbours (Messenia,
Elis) and committed outrages — the massacre at **Cynaetha** the most notorious — that let
[[aratus-of-sicyon|Aratus]] marshal the Symmachy behind a war he had largely engineered.
The allies declared war (220) with sweeping aims: to recover all that Aetolia had seized
since Demetrius II, restore the cities' governments, and **free Delphi from the Aetolian
yoke**. Philip proved a fast and ruthless general — his lightning raid sacked the Aetolian
federal centre of **Thermum** (in revenge for Aetolian temple-plundering at Dium and
Dodona), and he descended on Sparta — while purging the guardian-Friends Doson had left him
and falling increasingly under the influence of **Demetrius of Pharos**, who pointed his
ambitions westward.

## The Peace of Naupactus (217) and *Symploke*

The war was ended at the conference of **Naupactus (217)**, where the Aetolian **Agelaus**
delivered his famous speech urging the Greeks to settle their quarrels before the "**cloud
in the west**" — the war between Rome and Carthage — broke over Greece. The trigger was news
of **Hannibal's victory at Lake Trasimene** (June 217), which reached Philip at the Nemean
games and prompted his abrupt decision to make peace and turn west. The Peace restored the
*status quo*; Philip kept the best gains (Triphylia, Phthiotic Achaea, Zacynthos) and stood
"the darling of the Greeks."

## Significance

The Social War is **the terminal event of CAH VII.1**, chosen because 217 is the year
[[polybius|Polybius]] singled out as the beginning of ***symploke*** — the interweaving of
the affairs of Greece, Macedon, Africa, and Italy into a single connected history. The
volume ends with the self-contained Hellenistic world on the brink of the age of Roman
intervention: Philip's turn toward the west, encouraged by Demetrius of Pharos, set Macedon
on the road to the Macedonian Wars with Rome. See [[philip-v]], [[aetolian-league]],
[[hellenistic-world]].

## Historiography

The war's principal source is **Polybius**, writing within a lifetime or two of the
events and drawing partly on his own Achaean family's political tradition (his father
Lycortas was an Achaean statesman), which gives him a strongly pro-Achaean, pro-Aratus
vantage point and a correspondingly hostile view of the Aetolians, whom he consistently
frames as opportunistic brigands rather than a legitimate federal power — a bias modern
historians must correct for when assessing Aetolian conduct and motives. Polybius's
account is also shaped by his larger thesis about *symploke*, the "interweaving" of
Mediterranean history from 217 onward; his selection of this war as the volume's closing
episode, and his placement of Agelaus's "cloud in the west" speech at its climax, serve
his overarching argument as much as they report the Nemean-games diplomacy itself, and
some modern historians have questioned how much of Agelaus's specific wording is
Polybian retrospective construction rather than a preserved record of the speech.
