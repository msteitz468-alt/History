# The An Lushan Rebellion (755–763)

The **An Lushan Rebellion** was the revolt of the frontier general **An Lushan** against the
[[tang-dynasty|Tang dynasty]] at the height of its power — the catastrophe that broke the high Tang
and is conventionally treated as the **great turning point of the dynasty and of mid-imperial
Chinese history**. From the [[cambridge-history-of-china|Cambridge History of China]] (Sui-Tang
volume).

## Narrative

By the 750s the Tang had concentrated its armies on the frontiers under powerful military governors
(**jiedushi**). **An Lushan**, of Sogdian-Turkic origin and a favourite at the court of the aging
**Emperor Xuanzong**, held three northeastern commands. In **755** he rebelled, seized the eastern
capital **Luoyang** and then the capital **Chang'an**, forcing the emperor to flee (and, on the
road, to allow the execution of his favourite consort Yang Guifei). The Tang survived only by
recalling frontier armies and hiring **Uyghur** cavalry; the war dragged on until **763**, leaving
the north devastated.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **over-concentration of military power** in the frontier jiedushi commands,
  which gave a single general the means to challenge the centre.
- `contributed_to:` **court factionalism** and complacency under the late Xuanzong. (The CHC notes
  that older readings of the revolt as essentially a "foreign" rising — because An Lushan and many
  followers were non-Han — and as simple Hebei regional alienation are **unpersuasive**; it was a
  failure of the imperial military-political structure itself.)

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` a **collapse of the registered population** in the census (from c. 53 million to far
  fewer households recorded) — partly real catastrophe, partly the breakdown of central
  registration.
- `produced:` the **permanent weakening of central authority**: the post-war Tang governed through a
  patchwork of **autonomous provincial governors**, especially in Hebei, that it never fully
  recovered.
- `produced:` deep **fiscal and social transformation** — the move to the **twice-a-year tax**
  (*liangshui*, 780), the decline of the old equal-field landholding system, and the long-term
  shift of economic weight to the **commercializing south**, setting up the very different world of
  the [[song-dynasty|Song]].

## Actors

The **Tang dynasty** under **Xuanzong** (and his successors) against **An Lushan** and his
successors (the rebellion outlived An Lushan himself, murdered by his own son in 757). See
[[tang-dynasty]].

## Historiography

The rebellion is the classic divide between "early" and "late" Tang. The CHC sets aside the
"barbarian rebellion" and pure-regionalism explanations in favour of a structural account (the
militarized frontier and a brittle court), and stresses how the post-755 fiscal and provincial
changes — not the formal end of the dynasty in 907 — mark the real transition toward
mid-imperial China.
