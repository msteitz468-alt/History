# Second Battle of Adobe Walls (27 June 1874)

## Narrative

In late June 1874 a multi-tribal force of [[actors/comanches|Comanches]], Cheyennes, and others — spiritually primed by [[actors/isa-tai|Isa-tai]] and including war leaders in [[actors/parker-quanah|Quanah Parker]]'s circle — attacked the hide-hunter settlement at **Adobe Walls** in the Texas Panhandle. A small number of professional buffalo hunters with long-range rifles held the post. The failure shattered confidence in Isa-tai's invulnerability claims and helped trigger the army's full [[events/red-river-war-1874|Red River War]] response. (Distinct from Kit Carson's **1864** fight near the earlier Adobe Walls trading site — do not merge the two.)

### Mobilization (Isa-tai and Quanah)

Isa-tai (name euphemized variously; Gwynne: more accurate "wolf's vulva" / "coyote vagina"): Quahadi medicine man ~23; winter–spring 1873–74 claimed healing, raising dead, bullet immunity; "raised from his stomach a wagonload of cartridges"; four times ascended beyond the sun overnight; correctly predicted a comet vanish in five days; claimed control of hail/lightning/thunder. With Quanah: vision of purifying / leaving the white man's road / final war restoring Comanche glory; recruited Comanche then Cheyenne/Kiowa/Arapaho; revenge-raid tradition (both lost relatives to Hudson/Buell). Quanah's pipe recruitment: "If he smoke he go on war path… God kill him, he afraid." May: Isa-tai sent runners for the **first all-Comanche council/Sun Dance ever** — no prior whole-tribe council, Sun Dance not Comanche tradition. Gathered on Red River west of the reservation (near present Texola). ~half the tribe agreed to follow; Penatekas/most Nokonis (Horse Back)/many Yamparikas left under threat. Elders overruled young men's Texas-settlement plan → **first target: buffalo hunters at Adobe Walls**.

### The fight (Gwynne)

- **Force:** ~250 warriors mainly Comanche/Cheyenne (+ few Kiowa; 22 Arapaho under Yellow Horse) under Quanah; Isa-tai naked, sage-stem cap, painted yellow (invulnerable) as was his horse.
- **Plan:** club sleeping whites (28 men, 1 woman).
- **Warning:** James Hanrahan's fake ridgepole crack (gunshot mid-night) + free 4 a.m. drinks kept many awake — he had known an attack was coming days earlier.
- **Assault:** attack at gallop; prairie-dog holes tumbled riders; sod walls (~2 ft) would not burn; Quanah backed horse into a door, climbed roof, picked up a wounded comrade from horseback.
- **Firepower:** Sharps "Big Fifty" — .50-cal 600-grain / 125 gr powder, could drop a 2,000-lb buffalo at 1,000 yards; merchants had cases of rifles + ≥11,000 rounds. Quanah's horse shot at ~500 yards; Quanah hit by ricochet off powder horn (shoulder blade/neck — not serious). Indians picked off at ~¾ mile; **Billy Dixon** famous shot ~1,500 yards on day 3.
- **Medicine failure:** Cheyenne whipped Isa-tai; neighbor's horse shot; excuse that Cheyennes had skinned a skunk the day before.
- **Casualties (Gwynne):** ~15 Indians killed, many more wounded; 3 whites killed early (+1 accidental death later). Whites later **beheaded** dead Indians and stuck heads on stakes.
- **Operational judgment:** white siege logic (starve / cave walls) vs Indian inability to accept siege losses.

### Date inconsistency in Gwynne (flag)

Gwynne's text once places the early-morning hours of **May 26, 1874** then closes the account at dawn **June 27**. Standard history and Gwynne's own "Second Battle… June" framing use **27 June 1874**. This page retains **27 June 1874** as `date_start`/`date_end` and records the May 26 slip as an internal inconsistency in the source.

### Aftermath

Enraged warriors raided Colorado→Texas; hide hunting stopped; frontier "forted up"; ~190 whites killed summer 1874. Exhausted peace advocates. **26 July:** Grant put agencies under military control; Davidson at Fort Sill enrollment/roll call; cascade into the Red River War.

## Causal Analysis

- **caused_by:** Ecological-economic assault of the hide trade on the buffalo foundation of plains life.
- **contributed_to:** Prophetic revitalization politics under Isa-tai; Quanah's recruitment pipe and multi-band war path.
- **part_of:** [[processes/southern-plains-indian-wars]] final phase.

## Consequence Analysis

- **produced:** Tactical defeat of the coalition; propaganda disaster for millenarian war; public beheading atrocity by whites after the fight.
- **enabled:** Army justification for multi-column offensive through fall 1874.
- **contributed_to:** Shift toward Quanah's pragmatic leadership trajectory after eventual surrender (1875).

## Historiography

Hunter memoirs vs. Indian oral tradition; exact numbers of attackers inflated in some white accounts (Gwynne ~250; older lore often higher). Gwynne places Adobe Walls as the spiritual-military hinge of 1874. Billy Dixon's long shot is part of popular lore — treat as attributed anecdote. Do not confuse with **First Adobe Walls (1864)** — Kit Carson from Santa Fe nearly destroyed by Comanche/Kiowa force at the trading-post ruins. Atrocity fidelity: post-battle beheading of Indian dead is load-bearing evidence of the mutual total-war climate, not optional color.
