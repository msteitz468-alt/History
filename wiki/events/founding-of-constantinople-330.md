# The Founding of Constantinople

The **founding of Constantinople** — Constantine's refoundation of **Byzantium** as **"New Rome,"**
dedicated on **11 May 330** — created the eastern imperial capital that would anchor the Roman world
for over a thousand years. Treated in [[bowman-cah-v12-2005|CAH XII]] Ch. 4 (**Averil Cameron**).

## Narrative

After defeating Licinius at nearby **Chrysopolis** (324), [[constantine-the-great|Constantine]]
renamed **Byzantium** — superbly sited on the Bosphorus controlling the straits between Europe and Asia
— as the **"city of Constantine."** He endowed it with the apparatus of an imperial centre: a great
oval **forum** with his statue on a porphyry column, the **Augusteum** square, a **senate house**, a
**hippodrome** adjoining the **palace**, walls, and a **bread distribution** like Rome's to attract
settlers. He also built churches and a **mausoleum** ringed with twelve empty sarcophagi for the
apostles — and the (later completed) S. Sophia and Holy Apostles. The city was dedicated on 11 May 330.

## Causal Analysis

Cameron stresses that Constantinople was, in essence, a **tetrarchic capital** in the mould of
Thessalonica or Serdica — the natural product of the [[roman-tetrarchy|tetrarchic]] system of mobile
emperors ruling from frontier "sub-capitals," now given permanent and monumental form at a strategic
site near the eastern and Danubian frontiers.

## Consequence Analysis

Cameron is careful to **deflate the myth** of a city "founded as a Christian capital": its
Christianization was **gradual** (pagan temples remained, even new ones per Zosimus), and Constantine
was at least as concerned to provide the **secular accoutrements of an imperial centre** as to build
churches. Paradoxically it was **Rome** — which Constantine never visited after 326 — that received the
great Constantinian churches, while at Constantinople building concentrated on the **secular and
imperial**. In the long run, however, "New Rome" became the enduring capital of the Eastern Roman
([[byzantine-empire|Byzantine]]) Empire and a Christian metropolis without rival.

## Significance

The foundation shifted the empire's centre of gravity decisively **eastward** and created the city
that would outlast the Western empire by a millennium — a permanent imperial residence that completed
the late-Roman eclipse of Rome and the senate.
