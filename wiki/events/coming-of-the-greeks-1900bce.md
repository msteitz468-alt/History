# The Coming of the Greeks (~1900 BCE)

The disputed horizon at which **Indo-European speakers of an early form of Greek**
first entered the Greek mainland — the founding migration of Greek-speaking Europe.
It is not a dated event but a **problem of inference**, hung on a destruction layer
and a kind of pottery. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]]
(Ch. XXVII, Crossland; the archaeology in Ch. XXVI(a), Caskey).

## Narrative

For decades the standard view placed the arrival "**c. 1900 BCE**," at the opening
of the **Middle Helladic** period, when many settlements in central and southern
Greece were destroyed and a characteristic new **grey or yellow "Minyan" ware**
appeared, along with apsidal houses and cist graves. "An invasion is the obvious
cause," Crossland writes, and since **no later major intrusion** is visible before
Greek is securely attested (Linear B, 14th–13th c. BCE), the Minyan-ware people are
the natural candidates for the first Greek-speakers.

The picture was complicated by the [[lerna|Lerna]] excavations: the **severe
destruction there fell earlier, at the end of [[early-bronze-age-aegean|Early
Helladic II]]** (~2200 BCE), and Minyan-style ware appears already in the latest
EH III level. So the immigration may have come in **more than one phase** — an
earlier wave (proto-Greek, or a different Indo-European people) destroying Lerna,
and the main body arriving ~1900 BCE.

## Causal Analysis

- **caused_by:** [[indo-european-migrations]] — part of the broader Indo-European
  dispersal from the north/steppe; the proximate trigger is `unknown`.
- The **mechanism** is contested. The older "invasion from the north" account
  founders on the fact that **no convincing northern prototype of Minyan ware has
  been found** in Macedonia or Thrace.
- **Mellaart's** alternative: Minyan-type grey wares developed in **north-western
  Anatolia** (Troy V horizon); "proto-Greeks" settled there, learned the ware and
  seafaring, then crossed to Greece ~1900 BCE bringing both pot and language.
- **Palmer's** alternative: the Minyan-ware folk were **Luwians**, and the first
  Greek-speakers came only later (16th–15th c. BCE). Crossland presents these as
  "mutually exclusive theories" that can be evaluated only provisionally.

## Consequence Analysis

Whatever its exact date and route, the outcome is decisive: by the Late Bronze Age
Greek is the language of the **[[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean]]** palaces
([[aegean-scripts|Linear B]] at Pylos, Mycenae, [[knossos|Cnossus]]). The migration
thus seeds the entire later trajectory of **Greek civilization** — the Mycenaean
world, its collapse, and the historical Greeks. In Crete, by contrast, there is **no
such rupture**: the [[minoan-civilization|Minoan]] palaces grow out of an unbroken
local development (see [[early-bronze-age-aegean]]).

## The CAH II.1 reassessment (Caskey)

[[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II.1]] (Caskey, Ch. IV(a)) reaffirms the equation
on a refined basis. From the [[lerna|Lerna]] sequence Caskey reconstructs **two
incursions**: a violent one at the end of EH II (the destruction of the Argolid),
and a second, **kindred and largely peaceful**, opening the Middle Helladic ~1900 —
the makers of grey/yellow **Minyan** ware and Matt-painted pottery, "hardy,
tenacious, conservative" farmers who were **related in culture (and probably race)
to the people of [[troy|Troy VI]]**, arriving at the Hellespont about the same time.
His decisive argument is **linguistic-toponymic**: following **Blegen & Haley
(1928)**, the un-Greek (hence pre-Greek) place-names in **-*nth*- and -*ss*-**
(*Korinthos*, *Tylissos*) belong to the **Early Bronze Age**, and since the MH→LH
development is continuous and unbroken, the **Middle Helladic people must be the
first true Greeks** in the land — Greek being a language that formed *in* Greece, not
imported ready-made. Caskey is pointedly cautious that "race" here means only a
**thoroughly mixed stock**, and that the rise of Mycenae was a **local flowering**,
not a fresh invasion.

## Historiography

A textbook case of the **limits of inferring migration from material culture**.
"Minyan ware = Greeks" is an equation the evidence underdetermines: the ware's
origin is unsettled, destruction layers are ambiguous, and the language is not
directly attested until centuries later. Crossland's own verdict is measured —
"the reasonable conclusion is that the first considerable body of Greek-speaking
immigrants moved into central and southern Greece c. 1900 BCE," while conceding the
Lerna evidence pushes part of the process earlier. The dating is therefore flagged
**disputed**. See [[indo-european-origins]] and
[[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
