# Siege of Caizhou (1233–34)

The **siege of Caizhou** was the **Jin dynasty's last stand** — isolated by Mongol **[[nerge]]**
tactics, starved, flooded, and finally taken with Song assistance in February 1234.

## Narrative

By 1233 the Jin held only eastern Honan. Emperor Ai-Tsung rejected counsel to relocate to
water-protected Kuei-te and moved to Caizhou near the Song frontier. Tachar Noyan approached in
**nerge** fashion (October 1233), isolating the city and over-stretching food supply. Cannibalism,
currency collapse, and Imperial Guard desertions followed. The Song joined with 10,000 men;
Mongols and Song breached walls by flooding (January 1234). Ai-Tsung abdicated 8 February; the
city fell 9 February. Mongol commander Sübedei had already been reassigned — demonstrating depth
of Mongol generalship.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` cumulative Mongol reduction of Jin territory since 1211.
- `contributed_to:` Mongol–Song cooperation that would become Mongol–Song war.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` destruction of the **Jin** — clearing northern China for Mongol–Song confrontation.
