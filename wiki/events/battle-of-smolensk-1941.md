# Battle of Smolensk (1941)

The **Battle of Smolensk** (10 July – 10 September 1941) was a major engagement of the
opening phase of Operation Barbarossa, in which German Army Group Center encircled and
destroyed several Soviet armies around the city of Smolensk on the road to Moscow. Despite
another catastrophic Soviet defeat — hundreds of thousands of prisoners taken — Soviet
forces mounted determined, costly counterattacks that slowed the German advance
considerably, buying roughly two months that Soviet historians and some Western analysts
(notably David Glantz) argue proved critical in allowing Moscow's defenses to be organized
before the autumn assault (Operation Typhoon) and before the onset of winter.

## Consequences

The battle is often cited as the first point at which the German timetable for a rapid
victory began to slip meaningfully, feeding directly into the [[events/battle-of-
moscow-1941|Battle of Moscow]] later that year. It also forced a contentious debate within
the German high command (reflected in Hitler's Directive 33 diversions) over whether to
continue toward Moscow immediately or divert forces south toward Ukraine — a decision that
delayed the assault on Moscow into the autumn.

## Historiography

Older Western accounts, drawing heavily on German general staff memoirs, tended to treat
Smolensk chiefly as a staging point before the "real" battle for Moscow. Glantz's
archival work using Soviet records has argued for reassessing Smolensk as a genuine,
prolonged battle of attrition in its own right, whose Soviet resistance — however costly —
meaningfully degraded German offensive strength ahead of the winter campaign.
