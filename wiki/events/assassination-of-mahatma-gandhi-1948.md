# Assassination of Mahatma Gandhi (1948)

On the evening of 30 January 1948, as Gandhi walked to his daily prayer meeting at Birla House in New Delhi, he was shot at point-blank range by Nathuram Godse, a Hindu nationalist with ties to the Hindu Mahasabha. Gandhi died within minutes, uttering "Hey Ram."

Guha's final chapters and Epilogue reconstruct the conspiracy, the motives (detailed in Godse's courtroom statement), the security lapses, Gandhi's own premonitions, and the immediate national and international reaction. The assassination occurred only months after Independence and Partition, at a moment when Gandhi was using his last fasts to protect India's Muslim minority and to insist that moral means still mattered.

## Historiography
The fullest reconstruction rests on trial records and Godse's own courtroom statement — a source shaped by his intent to justify the killing as political martyrdom, and therefore read critically by historians rather than taken at face value. Contemporary press accounts, police investigation files, and later biographical works (notably Guha's) draw out the intelligence failures that allowed a second, successful attempt after an earlier bomb attack on Gandhi failed weeks before. Debate continues over the extent of organizational planning behind the Hindu Mahasabha/RSS-linked conspirators versus Godse's own account emphasizing personal ideological motive, and over how directly the assassination should be read as a consequence of Partition violence versus longer-run Hindu-nationalist opposition to Gandhi's politics.
