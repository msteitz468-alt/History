# October Revolution (1917)

The **October Revolution** of 1917, officially designated in the Soviet tradition as the **Great October Socialist Revolution**, was an armed insurrection in Petrograd on November 7, 1917 (October 25, Old Style). Organized by the Bolshevik Party and led by Vladimir Lenin and Leon Trotsky, the revolution overthrew the Russian Provisional Government, bringing the Bolsheviks to power and leading directly to the establishment of the Soviet state.

## Narrative

By autumn 1917, the Provisional Government had lost almost all authority. Following the Kornilov affair, the Bolsheviks captured majoribilities in the Petrograd and Moscow Soviets. Operating incognito in Petrograd, **Vladimir Lenin** argued that the party must seize power immediately, before the elections to the Constituent Assembly and the meeting of the Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets. On October 23, 1917, he convinced the Bolshevik Central Committee to approve an armed insurrection.

**Leon Trotsky**, chairman of the Petrograd Soviet, coordinated the practical preparations through the Soviet's **Military Revolutionary Committee (MRC)**. The MRC took control of the Petrograd military garrison, directing soldiers to obey only its orders.

The insurrection began on the night of November 6–7. Armed units of Red Guards (factory workers), Kronstadt sailors, and garrison soldiers systematically occupied strategic points in the capital, including bridges, post offices, telephone stations, the state bank, and railway stations, encountering virtually no resistance.

On the afternoon of November 7, Trotsky announced the deposition of the Provisional Government. That night, Bolshevik-led forces surrounded the Winter Palace, the headquarters of the cabinet. Prime Minister Alexander Kerensky had escaped the capital in a car to seek loyal troops. Early on November 8, after a brief and largely bloodless siege, Red troops stormed the Winter Palace, arresting the remaining ministers of the Provisional Government. 

Simultaneously, the Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets met in Petrograd. Moderate socialists (Mensheviks and Right SRs) walked out of the congress in protest of the Bolshevik coup, leaving the Bolsheviks and Left SRs in control. The Congress approved the overthrow, passed the Decrees on Land and Peace, and established the **Council of People's Commissars** (*Sovnarkom*) under Lenin's chairmanship as the new government of Russia.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` **Collapse of the Provisional Government:** Kerensky's cabinet had alienated the Right (by arresting Kornilov) and the Left (by continuing the war and delaying reforms), leaving it without military defenders.
- `caused_by:` **Lenin's Tactical Drive:** Lenin recognized that the crisis of autumn 1917 was a temporary window of opportunity. He overrode his own party's doubts to insist on a pre-emptive coup before the Soviet Congress met.
- `caused_by:` **Trotsky's Coordination:** Trotsky successfully used the Military Revolutionary Committee of the Petrograd Soviet to take control of the garrison and execute the coup under the guise of defending the Soviet.
- `contributed_to:` **Popular Discontent:** The slogans "Peace, Land, and Bread" and "All Power to the Soviets" resonated deeply with war-weary soldiers, land-hungry peasants, and impoverished urban workers.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` **Bolshevik Seizure of Power:** The coup ended the democratic experiment of the Provisional Government, placing the Bolsheviks in control of the capital.
- `produced:` **Creation of the Soviet State:** The Second Congress of Soviets ratified the creation of the world's first socialist government.
- `produced:` **Dissolution of the Constituent Assembly:** When the Constituent Assembly finally met in January 1918 and refused to ratify Bolshevik decrees, Lenin dissolved it by force, initiating a one-party dictatorship.
- `produced:` **The Russian Civil War:** The violent overthrow of the government and the Bolsheviks' radical policies sparked a brutal civil war (1918–1921) between the Bolshevik "Reds" and the anti-Bolshevik "Whites."

## Actors

- **Vladimir Lenin:** The Bolshevik leader who provided the ideological will and tactical insistence for the coup.
- **Leon Trotsky:** Chairman of the Petrograd Soviet and head of the MRC, who planned and executed the armed seizure of Petrograd.
- **Alexander Kerensky:** Prime Minister of the Provisional Government, who fled the capital as his cabinet was arrested.
- **The Military Revolutionary Committee:** The Soviet body that served as the general staff of the insurrection.

## Historiography

Historiography on the October Revolution is deeply divided: liberal views see it as coup against democratic promise; Soviet as inevitable proletarian victory; recent (Freeze 2002 Orlovsky lines 7949–8230) stress objective conditions (war/economic collapse, govt weakness, Kornilov fiasco) plus Bolshevik exploitation, with Lenin "bombard[ing] the Central Committee with letters demanding... seize power" but party divided (Kamenev/Zinoviev 10-2 CC vote 10 Oct for uprising "without setting a timetable", lines 8219–8223); Trotsky/MRC key to execution "under the cover of soviet legitimacy" (lines 8225); "Bolsheviks brilliantly exploited the situation, but owed much to ‘objective conditions’" (lines 8227). Not pre-ordained; Kornilov "farce and fiasco" eroded Kerensky without guaranteeing Oct methods/timing (line 8085). "Strikes... main ‘form of participatory politics’" (Koenker/Rosenberg). Updated from Service/Riasanovsky with Freeze's emphasis on dual power erosion, popular radicalism (not just elite manipulation), and multi-ethnic/nationality strains accelerating crisis.

## Consequence Analysis (expanded from Freeze)

- `produced:` Bolshevik "Workers’ and Peasants’ Government"; Decrees on Peace and Land; immediate move to one-party rule (dissolution of Constituent Assembly Jan 1918 after SR plurality; lines 8283–8284 from range).
- `produced:` [[russian-civil-war-1918|Russian Civil War]] and War Communism as "crucible of communism" (lines 8235).
- `enabled:` Red Guards and armed worker militias as ongoing power base.
- `followed_by:` Rebuilding the state via party-state fusion, Cheka (Dzerzhinskii "sword of the revolution"), nomenklatura (lines 8251–8282).
- **Soviet and Marxist Historiography:** Depicts October as a heroic, popular socialist revolution of the Russian working class led by the vanguard Bolshevik Party, executing the laws of historical materialism.
- **Liberal and Totalitarian Historiography:** Analyzes October as a classic, minority coup d'état staged by a disciplined group of conspirators (the Bolsheviks) who hijacked a popular democratic revolution and imposed a dictatorship.
- **Social History Revisionism:** Focuses on the radicalization of the workers, sailors, and soldiers from below, arguing that the Bolsheviks succeeded because their slogans aligned with the spontaneous demands of the popular masses.
