# Richard I's Conquest of Cyprus (1191)

In May 1191, during the **Third Crusade**, **Richard I of England** conquered the
Byzantine province of **[[cyprus|Cyprus]]** from the usurper **Isaac Komnenos**, adding
the island to Latin rule — not from Muslims but from Greek Christians. Western control
lasted from that conquest until the Ottoman fall of Famagusta in August 1571 (380 years).
The conquest created the territorial basis of the later **[[kingdom-of-cyprus|Lusignan
kingdom]]**.

## Narrative

Richard had taken the Cross as early as November 1187; dynastic quarrels delayed his
departure until July 1190. After wintering in Sicily with Philip Augustus, his fleet left
on 10 April 1191, was caught in storms, and reached Rhodes (22 April), where Richard fell
ill. Setting out again on 1 May into more bad weather, several ships were wrecked on
Cyprus; survivors were imprisoned and maltreated on Isaac's orders. Joanna (Richard's
sister) and Berengaria of Navarre (his bride-to-be) anchored off Limassol; Isaac tried to
entice them ashore.

On the evening of 5 May Richard rejoined the women's ship; the next day he landed near
Limassol, brushed aside opposition, entered the town, and defeated Cypriot forces nearby
(one source places the skirmish at Kolossi). On 12 May his marriage to Berengaria was
solemnized at Limassol. Isaac then offered terms — to serve with Richard in Palestine —
which Richard, urgently awaited at Acre, prepared to accept; Isaac fled after the
settlement was reached, and (on Edbury's reconstruction) that flight decided Richard on
total subjugation. The English fleet sailed round the coast to Kiti or Famagusta; inland
toward Nicosia, Isaac was again defeated at Tremetousha; Richard occupied Nicosia and
Kyrenia (capturing Isaac's daughter). At the end of May Isaac surrendered from a northern
refuge (Buffavento, Kantara, or Cape St Andreas — sources differ) and was held in **silver
chains** because Richard had promised not to put him in iron.

Exactly one month after arrival, on **5 June 1191**, Richard left for Palestine. Less than
six weeks later Acre's Muslim garrison capitulated. He left custody to Richard of Camville
and Robert of Thornham; arrangements were short-lived. Within weeks — before Acre fell —
he sold his rights to the **Templars** for 100,000 Saracen bezants (40,000 down). Templar
rule (to April 1192) was rapacious and under-manned; on 4 April 1192 Nicosia rose against
the garrison; the Order surrendered the island back to Richard. He promptly sold it again
to **[[guy-of-lusignan|Guy of Lusignan]]** (further 60,000 bezants received; Guy never paid
the remaining 40,000), marking the start of the three-century Latin regime.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` storm diversion and Isaac's mistreatment of wreck survivors and of
  Joanna/Berengaria (Richard's own letter of 6 August frames revenge and rescue)
- `contributed_to:` once landed, recognition of Cyprus as a supply base after the long
  Sicilian delay; seizure of Isaac's Kyrenia treasury
- Edbury rejects as "probably baseless" chronicle claims of an Isaac–[[saladin|Saladin]]
  alliance as a motive
- `contrasts_with:` First Crusade conquests from Muslims — Cyprus was a Byzantine province
  peopled by Christians under Christian rule, a "new departure" later repeated on a larger
  scale at Constantinople in 1204

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` Latin disposal of the island → Templar interlude → sale to Guy →
  [[kingdom-of-cyprus]]
- `enabled:` Third Crusade logistics and finance ("a significant proportion of the costs of
  [Richard's] warfare in Palestine")
- `analogous_to:` Fourth Crusade seizure of Christian Byzantine territory (1204)
- Durability: after the April 1192 rising, no Greek island-wide insurgency for nearly two
  centuries; next full-island foreign conquest was 1570–71
- Philip of France's claim to a half-share (per crusade-division agreement) was firmly
  rejected; later English kings never seriously asserted suzerainty

## Actors

- **Richard I** — conqueror and seller; no design on the Jerusalem crown
- **Isaac Komnenos** — Byzantine usurper since 1184; held at Marqab after capture; released
  1193/94; died c. 1195; daughter later married Thierry of Flanders, who in 1203 fruitlessly
  claimed Cyprus from [[aimery-of-lusignan|Aimery]]
- **Templars** — interim purchasers, 1191–April 1192
- **[[guy-of-lusignan|Guy of Lusignan]]** — final purchaser, spring 1192

## Historiography

[[edbury-kingdom-cyprus-crusades-1991|Edbury (1991)]] re-reads the Continuations of William
of Tyre and the English crusade narratives. He stresses that creating a long-term Latin
regime formed no part of Richard's programme — the initial landing was revenge; exploitation
as supply base and sale for cash followed. Richard preferred to exploit existing
institutions (Roger of Howden: charter confirming laws of Manuel Komnenos in exchange for a
levy of half of Cypriot possessions) rather than destroy them. Isaac lacked full subject
support: Greek notables made their own peace; Constantinopolitan élites failed him;
Neophytus condemned him as a tyrant.
