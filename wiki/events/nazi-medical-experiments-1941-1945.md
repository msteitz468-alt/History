# Nazi Medical Experiments on Prisoners (1941–1945)

## Narrative

Under [[himmler-heinrich|Himmler's]] patronage, SS and Luftwaffe-affiliated physicians used concentration-camp inmates and prisoners of war as expendable experimental subjects. Shirer's summary judgment stands: fewer than two hundred "murderous quacks" conducted the experiments, but "their criminal work was known to thousands of leading physicians of the Reich, not a single one of whom, so far as the record shows, ever uttered the slightest public protest" — even Ferdinand Sauerbruch, later an anti-Nazi, sat through a 1943 lecture on gas-gangrene experiments objecting only that surgery beat sulfanilamide (Shirer 1960, ch. 27).

The catalog (perpetrator-documented at the Doctors' Trial): high-altitude decompression tests; typhus and jaundice infection; freezing experiments; poison bullets and mustard gas; gas-gangrene and bone-grafting operations on the Polish "rabbit girls" at Ravensbrück; salt-water survival tests on Roma at Dachau and Buchenwald; and mass-sterilization research (Dr. Adolf Pokorny to Himmler, on sterilizing three million Bolshevik prisoners: "the enemy must be not only conquered but exterminated").

**Dachau — Dr. Sigmund Rascher.** Rascher's 15 May 1941 letter to Himmler requested "professional criminals" for high-altitude research since "nobody volunteers"; Himmler supplied them "gladly." Some 200 prisoners went through the decompression chamber, about 80 killed outright, the survivors mostly executed afterward. Freezing experiments followed (ice-water immersion and naked exposure — ~400 experiments on 300 people, 80–90 direct deaths, most survivors "bumped off"), including the "animal heat" rewarming tests pressed on a skeptical Rascher by Himmler personally. Ninety-five German scientists heard the results presented at a Nuremberg medical conference in October 1942 without protest. Rascher and his wife were ultimately executed by the SS themselves — for the fraud of their kidnapped "children," not the murders.

**Strasbourg — the skeleton collection.** Professor August Hirt of the Reich University of Strasbourg, through Wolfram Sievers of Himmler's Ahnenerbe ("Institute for Research into Heredity"), had 115 people selected at Auschwitz and gassed at Natzweiler in August 1943 by Josef Kramer (later "Beast of Belsen") to furnish a Jewish skeleton collection; the partially processed corpses were found by the U.S. Seventh Army in late 1944 after a failed attempt to destroy the evidence ("The collection as such is inconspicuous" — Sievers to Himmler, 5 Sept 1944). Hirt disappeared; Sievers was hanged.

**Buchenwald/Dachau — the skins.** Tattooed prisoners were killed for decorative human-skin articles associated with Ilse Koch ("Bitch of Buchenwald") — testimony of inmates Pfaffenberger and Bláha at Nuremberg.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` [[nazi-racial-hygiene-program-1933-1939]] — the experiments extended the regime's biopolitics to its logical terminus once the camps supplied rightless subjects.
- `enabled:` Himmler's Ahnenerbe patronage network and the war's military-medical pretexts (aviation rescue, wound treatment, mass sterilization as demographic weapon).
- `produced:` the Doctors' Trial (US v. Karl Brandt et al., 1946–47): 7 hanged (incl. Gebhardt, 2 June 1948), Herta Oberheuser 20 years, Pokorny acquitted; and the **Nuremberg Code** on voluntary consent in human experimentation.

## Consequence Analysis

Scientifically near-worthless ("very little, if any, benefit to science was achieved" — Shirer; later assessments concur for most programs), the experiments' lasting consequence is ethical-institutional: the Nuremberg Code and the postwar architecture of research-consent law, plus a continuing debate on citing the hypothermia data.

## Historiography

Shirer's chapter is drawn from the Doctors' Trial record (Rascher's reports, Sievers's diary, inmate testimony — Neff, Pacholegg, Herypierre) and remains a reliable documentary digest; the definitive modern treatments (Lifton, *The Nazi Doctors*; Weindling) add the professional-culture analysis Shirer lacked — the perpetrators were not marginal quacks only, but included established academic physicians, which sharpens rather than softens the indictment. Kramer's Nuremberg answer stands as the chapter's epitaph on perpetrator psychology: "I had no feelings in carrying out these things because I had received an order… That, by the way, was the way I was trained."
