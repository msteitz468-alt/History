# Battle of Mantinea (418 BCE)

Major land battle during the Peloponnesian War. Spartan army under King Agis II defeated a coalition of Argos, Mantinea, Athens, and others. Despite some tactical confusion on the Spartan side, superior hoplite discipline and numbers carried the day. The battle demonstrated the limits of the Peace of Nicias and Spartan willingness to use force to maintain its position in the Peloponnese.

## Osborne (2000)

[[sources/osborne-classical-greece-2000|Osborne (ed., 2000)]] (Ch. 7, Kallet) cites Mantinea
as the clearest evidence of the **Peace of Nicias's "unpeaceful" nature**: alongside
continued Corinthian agitation against Athens, the battle of Mantinea (418) — pitting
Athens and Sparta and their respective allies against one another only three years into a
peace meant to last fifty — is precisely why Thucydides chose to treat the entire period
431–404 as a **single war** rather than two separate conflicts bridged by an interlude.

## Historiography

The battle is known through **Thucydides** (Book V), whose account of the peace's collapse
frames Mantinea not as an isolated Peloponnesian squabble but as proof that the Peace of
Nicias never produced genuine peace — a historiographical judgment (the "single war" thesis)
that has shaped how the whole 431–404 conflict is periodized and named. No serious modern
dispute attaches to the battle's outcome; the debate concerns instead its significance for
Thucydides's structuring of the war narrative.
