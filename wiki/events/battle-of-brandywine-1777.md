# The Battle of Brandywine (11 September 1777)

> **⚔️ Staff-college tactical analysis:** see **[[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-brandywine|Brandywine — Tactical Analysis]]** in the warfare hub for the full order of battle, the phased conduct of the action, and the principles-of-war assessment.

The battle that opened the road to **Philadelphia**, the rebel capital. Landing 15,000 men at Head of
Elk after a brutal sea voyage, [[howe-william|Howe]] advanced on the city; [[washington-george|
Washington]] stood to defend the **Brandywine Creek** crossings at Chadds Ford.

## Narrative

Howe executed "his favorite tactic": **Knyphausen** demonstrated against the ford with 6,800 men while
Howe and **[[cornwallis-charles|Cornwallis]]** marched 8,200 around the **unguarded upper fords** to
turn the American right — a maneuver Washington learned of too late and through conflicting reports.
The divisions of Stirling, Sullivan, and Stephen "fought stubbornly" at Birmingham Hill but were
flanked; only the rearguard stand of **[[greene-nathanael|Greene]]'s Virginians** "saved the army from
total destruction." British losses were ~89 killed and 488 wounded; American losses about 200 killed,
500 wounded, and 400 captured. Howe soon outmaneuvered Washington again and occupied **Philadelphia on
26 September**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Howe's **strategic decision to take Philadelphia** by sea and maneuver.
- `contributed_to:` American **failure to reconnoiter the upper Brandywine fords** — Lengel notes the
  Americans understood "less about their native terrain than did the invading British."

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **fall of Philadelphia** and the loss of the Trenton-Princeton "mystique."
- `produced:` a **crisis of command** — Congress and army "openly discussed [Washington's] removal and
  replacement by [[gates-horatio|Gates]]."
