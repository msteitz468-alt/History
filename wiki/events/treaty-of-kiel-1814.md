# Treaty of Kiel (14 January 1814)

The peace treaty that, as part of the settlement of the
[[napoleonic-wars|Napoleonic Wars]], obliged Denmark — which had sided with
Napoleon — to cede [[norway]] to the king of Sweden, while retaining the North
Atlantic dependencies (Iceland, the Faroes, Greenland). Norway resisted, adopting
its own liberal constitution at Eidsvoll (17 May 1814) before entering a personal
union with Sweden later that year. The treaty dissolved the four-century
[[denmark-norway|Danish–Norwegian union]] and is a pivotal moment in
[[scandinavian-nation-state-formation|Nordic nation-state formation]].

## Historiography

Danish and Norwegian historiography reads Kiel differently: in the Danish
national narrative it is the nadir of the catastrophe that followed alignment
with Napoleon, while Norwegian historiography treats it as the negative
precondition of the 1814 [[norwegian-constitution-1814|Eidsvoll constitution]] and
the founding moment of the modern Norwegian state. CHS Vol. II situates it within
the broader [[scandinavian-nation-state-formation|reordering of the Nordic states]]
set in motion by the Napoleonic crisis.
