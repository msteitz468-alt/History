# The Late Victorian Famines (1876–1879, 1896–1902)

Two clusters of catastrophic famine, in **1876–79** and **1896–1902**, struck the tropical world — above
all **British India** and **Qing China**, but also Brazil's Nordeste, Ethiopia, Korea and Java — during
exceptionally strong **El Niño** events. Documented for the wiki through
**[[davis-late-victorian-holocausts|Mike Davis's *Late Victorian Holocausts*]]**, they are the central
case of the **colonial conversion of drought into mass death** and, in Davis's thesis, of the **making of
the "Third World."**

## Narrative

In 1876–79 the monsoon failed across the Deccan and south India while, under Viceroy **Lord Lytton**,
grain continued to be exported to Britain and relief was rationed on deterrent principles (the **Temple
wage**, set below the ration of Nazi or Buchenwald inmates by Davis's grim comparison). Simultaneously
drought and the erosion of the Qing **ever-normal granary** system devastated north China (the "Incredible
Famine" of Shanxi). The pattern recurred in **1896–1902**, overlapping with plague and with the violence
of the **Boxer** crisis in China. Aggregate mortality is estimated at **30–60 million**, a range that is
genuinely uncertain (colonial demography is poor) and contested at the upper end.

## Causal Analysis

- `contributed_to: unknown` — a strong **ENSO/El Niño** oscillation failed monsoons across three
  continents at once (Davis's use of climatic "teleconnections").
- `caused_by: [[imperialism]]` — Davis's core claim is that drought became *famine* through political
  economy: **grain exports** from starving regions, **railways** serving paying demand, relief withheld or
  made punitive on **laissez-faire/Malthusian** grounds, and the dismantling of older indigenous relief
  capacity. The contrast of British India with the (declining but historically robust) Qing granary system
  is part of the argument.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` a demographic catastrophe and the entrenchment of the colonized world as an impoverished,
  deindustrializing **periphery** — Davis's "making of the Third World," one materialist account of the
  origins of modern **global inequality** (see [[industrialization|the Great Divergence]]).
- `enabled:` the **Indian nationalist economic critique** (the "drain of wealth"; the emergent
  [[imperialism|anticolonial]] argument) and the codification of **famine relief codes**.

## Historiography

Davis's synthesis of climatology and colonial political economy is influential; the **death tolls** and
the strongest causal claims (that policy, not nature, was the proximate killer) are debated, with critics
noting the limits of colonial demography and the role of genuine ecological shock. The wiki records the
mortality figures as estimates and the causal weighting as a live interpretive question. See also
[[genocide]] for the boundary between policy-driven mass death and intent.
