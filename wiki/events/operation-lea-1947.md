# Operation Léa (October–November 1947)

Operation Léa was the largest French colonial military action in Indochina to date. Its goal was to capture or kill Ho Chi Minh and Vo Nguyen Giap at their Viet Bac headquarters, destroy a significant portion of the Viet Minh regular army, and cut their links to China.

## Narrative

On 7 October 1947, General Valluy launched 17 battalions plus 950 paratroopers in a pincer movement against the Viet Bac. The airborne element (three trips) lost the element of surprise; warning reached the target just as the first troops landed. Ho and Giap escaped with minutes to spare (one associate, Nguyen Van To, was killed).

The ground columns advanced into extremely difficult terrain against constant ambushes, demolished bridges, and “piano-key” anti-vehicle ditches. The northern pincer never closed; the southern never arrived. The operation was called off on 8 November. French claims of 9,000 Viet Minh dead were almost certainly inflated.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** French high command’s belief that a single decisive conventional blow could break Viet Minh resistance while the movement was still relatively weak and before significant Chinese aid arrived.

**contributed_to:** Viet Minh superior intelligence, terrain mastery, and willingness to trade space for time.

## Consequence Analysis

Léa failed to achieve any of its strategic objectives. The Viet Minh leadership survived intact; the regular army was not destroyed; the Chinese border route remained open. The operation demonstrated both the limits of French conventional power in the northern mountains and the Viet Minh’s growing ability to survive and evade large-scale offensives.

It was followed by Operation Ceinture (the “Belt”), which cleared some territory and captured supplies but again produced no decisive battle; the French withdrew to the lowlands by late December and the Viet Minh immediately reoccupied the cleared areas.

## Actors

- French: General Valluy (overall command).
- Viet Minh: Ho Chi Minh and Vo Nguyen Giap (leadership); local and regional forces.

## Historiography

Logevall uses Léa as an early example of French over-optimism about quick military solutions. French accounts often emphasize the logistical nightmare and the skill of Viet Minh small-unit tactics. Vietnamese histories present the successful evasion as proof of the correctness of the protracted-war strategy.
