# The Siege of Charleston (1780)

The greatest British battlefield success of the war and "one of the worst American disasters." Having
finally committed serious resources to the South, [[clinton-henry|Clinton]] sailed from New York with
**7,600 troops** and laid formal siege to Charleston.

## Narrative

**Benjamin Lincoln** chose to defend the city from its fortifications, "waiting for reinforcements from
the North" rather than contesting the British landing. Clinton dug siege trenches across Charleston Neck;
**Banastre Tarleton** closed the noose by seizing Moncks Corner (14 April). When Clinton refused
honorable terms, the bombardment opened, and on **12 May 1780 Lincoln surrendered his entire army** of
more than 5,500. The British then overran the state; most South Carolinians initially "reconciled
themselves to the new regime." Tarleton's slaughter of surrendering Virginians at the **Waxhaws** (29
May) — "Tarleton's quarter" — soon poisoned that calm.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Clinton's decision to make the **southern strategy** his main effort.
- `contributed_to:` Lincoln's choice to be **besieged in a coastal city** he could not relieve.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **capture of an American field army** and the conquest of South Carolina — and relief
  for Lord North's beleaguered government at home.
- `produced:` the conditions for the backcountry **civil war** that Tarleton's brutality and Clinton's
  paroles proclamation would ignite, undoing the conquest (see [[battle-of-camden-1780|Camden]] and
  [[battle-of-kings-mountain-1780|Kings Mountain]]).
