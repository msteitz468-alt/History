# The Marshall Plan (1948–1952)

The **Marshall Plan** — officially the **European Recovery Program (ERP)** — was the
massive US aid program (c. $13 billion, 1948–52) that financed the reconstruction of
**Western Europe** after the Second World War. Announced by Secretary of State **George C.
Marshall** in June 1947, it was the **economic arm of [[containment]]** and a decisive step
in the **division of Europe** into two blocs. From [[leffler-westad-cold-war-v1|*The
Cambridge History of the Cold War*, Vol. I]].

## Narrative

By 1947 Western Europe faced a crippling **dollar deficit** — it needed American goods it
could not pay for — and the postwar Bretton Woods liquidity measures and UNRRA relief had
proved insufficient. Britain could no longer fund anti-Communist forces in Greece; the
French, Belgian, and Italian Communist parties were gaining among workers whose living
standards seemed threatened; Germany's currency had collapsed into barter. Marshall's
**Harvard speech (5 June 1947)** offered American aid to all of Europe — including, in form,
the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe — to rebuild a functioning continental economy.

The offer was structured so that **Stalin rejected it** (and forced the Eastern European
states, notably Czechoslovakia and Poland, to refuse) — seeing it, correctly, as a tool to
draw Europe into the American orbit. This, as Charles Maier shows, made the Marshall Plan a
principal mechanism of the **economic division of Europe**: it consolidated a Western bloc
built around a revived West Germany and accelerated the parallel **[[berlin-blockade-1948|crisis
over Germany]]**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Europe's postwar **dollar shortage** and economic collapse, which the Bretton
  Woods measures and UNRRA relief had failed to remedy.
- `contributed_to:` the political **danger of Communist advance** amid postwar misery (the
  French, Italian, and Belgian Communist parties) — and the strategy of [[containment]].

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **recovery of Western Europe** (industrial output surged), the
  **consolidation of the Western bloc**, and impetus toward **European integration** (the
  OEEC, the road to the European Coal and Steel Community).
- `produced:` the **economic division of Europe** and a sharpened Cold War — Stalin's refusal
  (and the USSR's countermoves, the Cominform of 1947 and later Comecon).

The Marshall Plan is the paradigmatic case of **economic statecraft as grand strategy**: it
revived the West European power centres that [[containment|Kennan]] deemed vital, bound them
to the United States, and drew the dividing line of the [[cold-war|Cold War]] across the
continent.
