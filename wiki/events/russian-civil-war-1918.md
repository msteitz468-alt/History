# Russian Civil War
## Narrative
Following the Bolshevik seizure of power in November 1917, political and social opposition erupted into a major military conflict in the summer of 1918. The counterrevolutionary forces, known as the **[[white-movement|White Movement]]**, drew support from army officers, cossacks, the bourgeoisie, and political groups ranging from monarchists to Socialist Revolutionaries. The conflict was further complicated by foreign Allied intervention, regional independence movements (in Ukraine, Transcaucasia, and the Baltic), and peasant guerrilla bands ("greens"). Despite initial White victories in 1918–1919 (under Admiral Kolchak in the east and General Denikin in the south), the Red Army, organized by Leon Trotsky, successfully defended the Bolshevik core. By late 1920, the main White forces were defeated, culminating in General Wrangel's evacuation from the Crimea in November 1920.

## Causal Analysis
- `caused_by:` the **Bolshevik seizure of power** and the **forcible dispersal of the Constituent Assembly** (January 1918), which closed off parliamentary avenues; the **humiliation of the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk**, which alienated nationalists and former Allies; and the **clash of social classes** over the redistribution of land and nationalization of industry.

## Consequence Analysis
- `produced:` the **consolidation of Bolshevik rule** and the formal establishment of the **[[soviet-union|Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR)]]** in December 1922.
- `produced:` **complete economic ruin**: industrial production fell to 20% of pre-war levels, and the country suffered a catastrophic famine in 1921–1922 that claimed millions of lives (Line 5827).
- `produced:` the **liquidation and flight of the traditional Russian elites** (gentry, bourgeoisie, clergy, and independent intelligentsia), with over two million emigrating (Line 5827).
- `followed_by:` the transition from the emergency measures of **[[war-communism|War Communism]]** to the market compromises of the **[[new-economic-policy|New Economic Policy (NEP)]]** in 1921.

## Actors
- **Vladimir Lenin**, head of the Soviet government; **Leon Trotsky**, who built and led the Red Army; and White commanders **Alexander Kolchak** and **Anthony Denikin**. See [[lenin-vladimir]], [[trotsky-leon]].

## Historiography
Riasanovsky notes that the Bolshevik victory was due to their control of the central Russian core (inner lines of communication and supply), unity of command, and the peasants' fear that a White victory would restore the gentry landlords, making the Reds the lesser of two evils for the rural majority (Lines 5809–5815).
