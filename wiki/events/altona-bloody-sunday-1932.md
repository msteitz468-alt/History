# Altona Bloody Sunday (1932)

The deadliest single day of the July 1932 campaign's street war, and the
pretext for the coup against Prussia.

## Narrative

On 17 July 1932 thousands of SA stormtroopers — the ban on the organization
lifted weeks earlier as part of [[papen-franz-von|Papen]]'s bargain with the
Nazis — marched through the Communist stronghold of Altona, a working-class
Prussian municipality bordering Hamburg. Red Front-Fighters resisted with
stones and missiles, with reports of rooftop sharpshooters. "Someone, nobody
was sure who, fired a shot" (Evans, [[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003]]), and
the police opened panicked, indiscriminate fire. Eighteen people were killed
and over a hundred injured; autopsies showed most of the dead were struck by
police bullets, not combatant fire.

## Causal Analysis

- caused_by: the SA-ban lift and the deliberately provocative tactic of
  marching through "red" districts during the July campaign — part of the
  record violence of June–July 1932 (in Prussia, 155 killed in political
  clashes in 1932, 105 of them in those two months).
- The first shot's origin is explicitly unresolved in the source (Evans).

## Consequence Analysis

- enabled: three days later, Papen cited the violence to depose the Social
  Democratic Prussian government of Braun and Severing (the *Preussenschlag*,
  20 July 1932) — per Evans the decisive blow against the SPD, after which
  "the only realistic alternatives were a Nazi dictatorship or a conservative,
  authoritarian regime backed by the army."
- contributed_to: the demonstration effect of SPD/union non-resistance, which
  convinced conservatives and Nazis alike that democratic institutions could
  be dismantled without opposition (see [[weimar-democracy-and-its-collapse]]).

## Historiography

Evans (2003) is the ingested account. The later Altona trial of 1933 —
executions of Communists under the new regime for deaths mostly caused by
police fire — is a standard exhibit in the Nazi-justice literature (not yet
ingested in detail).
