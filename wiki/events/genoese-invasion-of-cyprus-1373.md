# Genoese Invasion of Cyprus (1373–1374)

The **Genoese invasion of 1373–74** seized **[[famagusta|Famagusta]]**, the island's
principal port, and forced the **[[kingdom-of-cyprus|Lusignan kingdom]]** into a subordinate
commercial and political relationship with **[[republic-of-genoa|Genoa]]**. For Edbury this
is the **climacteric** ending the era in which Cyprus could act as a crusading power.

## Narrative

### Spark: Famagusta coronation riot (10 October 1372)

After majority (December 1371) and Nicosia coronation (January 1372), Peter II was crowned
king of Jerusalem at Famagusta. Venetian and Genoese representatives disputed the ceremonial
right to lead the king's horse; tumult followed; violence spread — inhabitants and Venetians
killed many Genoese and destroyed property. Peter blamed the Genoese and refused
conciliation; Genoa decided on force.

### War (1373–74)

Under Doge Dominic of Campofregoso, Genoa raised a *maona*-financed fleet under the doge's
brother Peter (demands included a castle for merchants — "fortress mentality"). Papal
mediation failed; Venice offered sympathy but **no military aid**. Damian Cattaneo's
advanced squadron (from March/April) forced the Cypriot evacuation of **Satalia** back to
the Turks, burnt Limassol, and took Paphos. Main fleet (~36 galleys + transports; Genoese
account: army over 14,000) arrived early October. After temporary Cypriot successes, talks
in the evacuated harbour castle became a trap: Genoese seized the fortress, then took
prisoner the king, Eleanor, and John of Antioch in "blatant violation" of host-sworn oaths.
Famagusta was occupied and sacked. James of Lusignan (future James I) became the focus of
resistance at Kyrenia; Eleanor escaped to join him; Kyrenia held.

### Terms (21 October 1374)

Armistice lifted the Kyrenia siege (15 March) on condition James hand over and go into exile
(Genoese later seized him at Rhodes despite safe-conduct). Terms: annual tribute **40,000
florins**; indemnity to the *maona* just over **two million florins** within twelve years;
**90,000 florins** galley expenses; Genoese occupation of **Famagusta** as security (from
1383, sovereignty); hostages in Genoa. Of forty-four hostage families, eighteen never
reappear in Cyprus; the Genoese beheaded the last Ibelin lord of Arsur.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Famagusta riot and Genoese demand for a fortress; century of bad relations
- `contributed_to:` post-1369 contention between John of Antioch's regency and Eleanor of
  Aragon, giving Genoa moral cover
- `preceded_by:` [[sack-of-alexandria-1365]] and Peter I's murder (fiscal and political
  overstretch)

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` effective end of Lusignan crusading initiative — "no way that Cyprus could
  play any positive role in the continuing conflict between Christianity and Islam"
- `produced:` long Genoese hold on Famagusta; commercial ruin (Martoni, 1390s, found the
  city in decay)
- `enabled:` later Mamluk pressure (1420s) and Venetian takeover (1489)
- Edbury's close: almost 200 years before 1373 had seen "remarkable… stability and
  prosperity," making Cyprus "one of the most successful western regimes established by
  the crusaders"

## Historiography

Edbury ends his detailed narrative here (title dates 1191–1374). Makhairas is the fullest
near-contemporary Cypriot narrative (pro-James). Hill and Arbel cover the Venetian epilogue.
