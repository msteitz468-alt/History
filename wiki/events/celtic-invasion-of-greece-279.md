## Narrative

In 280–277 BCE bands of **Celts (Gauls)**, part of a renewed wave of migration pushing
out of central Europe toward the Danube and the Balkans, poured into a Macedon left
defenceless by the collapse of [[lysimachus|Lysimachus']] frontier kingdom at
[[battle-of-corupedium-281|Corupedium]]. Will (CAH VII.1, ch. 4) stresses that the
defence of the Thraco-Macedonian border had depended not only on armies but on
**diplomacy with the mountain peoples** — a craft the usurper [[ptolemy-ceraunus|Ptolemy
Ceraunus]], ignorant of his stolen kingdom's frontiers, completely lacked. Failing to
come to terms with the **Dardanians**, he forced them to make common cause with the
invaders. Ceraunus met the Gauls in the open (early 279), was defeated, and was killed;
Macedon dissolved into anarchy.

One great band under **Brennus** drove south to **Delphi** (279). The later tradition of
a sack of the sanctuary, miraculously repelled by Apollo with a snowstorm, is (Will
notes) **late and false**: in fact the peoples of central Greece — Boeotians, Phocians,
and above all the **[[aetolian-league|Aetolians]]** — organised the resistance, harried
the Celts with guerrilla warfare in the mountains, and forced their retreat. The "miracle
of Delphi" was nonetheless commemorated with a great new festival, the **Soteria** (in
honour of Zeus Soter), and became a propaganda triumph for the Aetolian League.

The decisive military blow fell in 277, when **[[antigonus-ii-gonatas|Antigonus
Gonatas]]** lured and **destroyed a large Gaulish army by ambush near Lysimacheia** —
the only heavy Celtic defeat of these years. The victory opened Macedon to him and let
him claim the throne (276) as a true *Soter*.

## Consequence Analysis

- **Macedon stabilised under the Antigonids**: the invasion both created the vacuum and
  furnished Gonatas the victory that legitimised his accession — see
  [[antigonid-dynasty]].
- **The Aetolian League's prestige** soared as the saviour of Delphi, accelerating its
  rise as a great power and its growing weight in the Delphic Amphictyony.
- **Lasting Celtic settlements**: the kingdom of **Tylis** in Thrace (until c. 212) and,
  shortly afterward and in special circumstances, the **[[galatians|Galatian]]** settlement
  of central Asia Minor — the Celts whose raids terrorised the cities of Anatolia and against
  whom the Attalids of [[pergamon|Pergamum]] would build their reputation (see ch. 11).

## Historiography

Reconstructed from Justin, Diodorus, Pausanias, Memnon, and the Soteria inscriptions
(some contemporary, some later). The episode is a case study in how a later, miraculous
tradition (the divine defence of Delphi) overlay a more prosaic reality (Aetolian
guerrilla resistance) for propaganda purposes — see [[hellenistic-historiography]].
