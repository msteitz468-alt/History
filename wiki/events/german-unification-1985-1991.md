# The Unification of Germany, 1985–1991

The peaceful unification of the two German states within NATO and the European Community, achieved through the 2+4 process, was one of the decisive events closing the Cold War.

Key sequence (from CHCW Vol. III, Haftendorn chapter):
- Summer 1989 mass exodus of East Germans via Hungary/Austria and FRG embassies.
- Leipzig demonstrations (“Wir sind das Volk!” “Keine Gewalt!”) reached >100,000 on 9 October 1989; authorities did not use force.
- Honecker replaced by Krenz (18 October).
- Schabowski’s incomplete announcement on 9 November 1989 opened the Berlin Wall.
- Kohl’s “Ten Points” (28 November 1989) proposed a path to federation/confederation within European structures.
- 2+4 talks (agreed February 1990) handled external aspects.
- Monetary union effective 1 July 1990 (1:1 rate for many purposes).
- Unification treaty signed 31 August 1990.
- Treaty on the Final Settlement (2+4, 12 September 1990) defined borders, renounced ABC weapons, capped Bundeswehr at 370,000, ended four-power rights; Germany regained full sovereignty.
- Unification effective 3 October 1990.

Gorbachev accepted German unity and the right to choose alliances (February 1990 Moscow talks; confirmed in Caucasus July 1990). Financial package: DM 12 billion + DM 3 billion loan. Germany renounced ABC weapons and accepted force limits.

Western leaders (especially Bush support for rapid unity in NATO; Mitterrand linking to deeper EC integration; Thatcher caution) shaped the outcome. The peaceful character was made possible by Soviet tolerance and prior East-West agreements on reducing nuclear risk and respecting human rights.
