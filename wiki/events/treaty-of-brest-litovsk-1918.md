# Treaty of Brest-Litovsk
## Narrative
Following the October Revolution of 1917, the Bolsheviks declared their intention to make peace with the Central Powers. After the conclusion of an armistice in December 1917, peace negotiations began at Brest-Litovsk. The German representatives offered Draconian terms, demanding massive territorial cessions. Leon Trotsky, representing the Soviet government, refused these terms, proclaiming a policy of "no war, no peace!" (Line 5727). In response, German troops advanced rapidly, occupying further territories and threatening Petrograd. Faced with the imminent destruction of the Soviet regime, Vladimir Lenin used his authority to force the party to capitulate to the German terms. The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk was signed on March 3, 1918.

## Causal Analysis
- `caused_by:` the complete **collapse of discipline in the Russian army** (soldiers massacring officers and fleeing the front); the **advancing German military machine** which could not be resisted by the young Soviet government; and **Lenin's realistic determination** to save Communist rule by sacrificing territory.

## Consequence Analysis
- `produced:` the **cession of vast territories**: Ukraine, Poland, Finland, Lithuania, Estonia, and Latvia received their independence (effectively becoming German puppet states), and part of Transcaucasia was ceded to Turkey.
- `produced:` **staggering economic and demographic losses** for Russia: 26% of its population; 27% of its arable land; 32% of its average crops; 26% of its railway system; 33% of its manufacturing industries; 73% of its iron industries; and 75% of its coal fields (Line 5731–5735).
- `produced:` the **breakup of the Bolshevik-Left Socialist Revolutionary coalition** (the Left SRs resigned from the cabinet in protest and launched an abortive rebellion in July 1918).
- `enabled:` the **consolidation of Soviet power** in the Great Russian core of the country, which allowed the Bolsheviks to survive the initial challenges of the Civil War.
- `followed_by:` the **annullment of the treaty** in November 1918 following Germany's defeat in World War I, which enabled the Red Army to reclaim Ukraine and parts of the Baltic region.

## Actors
- **Vladimir Lenin**, who insisted on signing the peace; **Leon Trotsky**, who led the negotiations and proposed "no war, no peace"; and the **German Empire**, which dictated the terms. See [[lenin-vladimir]], [[trotsky-leon]].

## Historiography
Riasanovsky notes that Lenin's willingness to accept this humiliating peace was a key demonstration of his practical realism (Line 5727). Most historians agree that without the treaty, the Soviet government would have been overthrown by the German military within weeks.
