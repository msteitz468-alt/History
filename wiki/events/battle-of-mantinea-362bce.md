# The Battle of Mantinea, 362 BCE

## Narrative

The **Battle of Mantinea** (362 BCE) was the last great hoplite battle of independent
Greece and the death-throe of the **[[thebes-hegemony-371bce|Theban hegemony]]**. A split
in the **Arcadian League** — over the use of Olympia's treasure to pay the federal army,
and over peace with Elis — set a pro-Theban bloc (**Tegea, Megalopolis, Messene, Argos,
Sicyon, Boeotia**) against an anti-Theban one (**Mantinea, Sparta, [[athens]], Elis,
Achaea**). [[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 7, Roy) recounts how **[[epaminondas]]**
marched south, nearly surprised both **Sparta** and **Mantinea**, and then met the
coalition near Mantinea. Using once more his **deep oblique attack**, "**Epaminondas'
army broke the enemy line, but Epaminondas himself was killed**, and this major
confrontation produced no decisive outcome."

## The anticlimax

Mantinea is famous as the point at which **Xenophon ended his *Hellenica***, with the
celebrated confession that the result was the opposite of what everyone expected: "there
was even more **confusion and disorder** in Greece after the battle than before." A
victory that killed the only man who could exploit it settled nothing. In the aftermath
the mainland states made a **Common Peace** — achieved, notably, "by their own diplomatic
efforts... without Persian intervention" — from which only **Sparta** excluded itself, by
refusing to recognize the independence of **Messene**.

## Significance

Mantinea ended Theban supremacy not by defeat but by **decapitation** (compare the death
of [[pelopidas|Pelopidas]] at Cynoscephalae in 364). Thebes remained stronger than before
[[battle-of-leuctra-371bce|Leuctra]] but "had lost some of the capacity... to initiate
change in Greek inter-state politics," withdrawing from Thessaly and Macedon. The battle
is the emblem of the fourth century's central political fact: the **mutual exhaustion** of
Sparta, Thebes and Athens, each able to check the others but none able to lead — the
vacuum into which **Philip of Macedon** would advance within two decades.

## Consequence Analysis

caused_by: the split of the Arcadian League and Epaminondas' bid to reassert Theban
control. produced: the **end of the [[thebes-hegemony-371bce|Theban hegemony]]**; a
Persia-free **Common Peace** (362/1) excluding Sparta; and the exhausted multipolar Greece
that opened the way for **Macedon**.
