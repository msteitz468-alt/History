# The Sack of Rome (410)

The capture and three-day sack of the **city of Rome** by the Visigoths under **[[alaric|Alaric]]** on
**24–27 August 410** — the first time the city had fallen to a foreign enemy in **800 years** (since the
Gauls in 390/387 BCE), and the supreme psychological shock of late antiquity. Treated in
[[cameron-cah-v13-1998|CAH XIII]] Ch. 4 (**R. C. Blockley**).

## Narrative

The sack was the climax of three years of failed negotiation. After the execution of
**[[stilicho|Stilicho]]** (408) and the massacre of the federates' families drove 30,000 men to
**[[alaric|Alaric]]**, the anti-German government of **Olympius** at Ravenna proved unable either to
fight or to settle with him. Alaric **blockaded Rome** in late 408, extracting a huge ransom (5,000 lb of
gold, 30,000 lb of silver); the emperor **Honorius**, secure at Ravenna, repeatedly refused to ratify a
treaty. In 409 Alaric set up a puppet emperor, the pagan-leaning senator **Priscus Attalus**, then
deposed him; he moderated his demands to little more than grain and land in Noricum. Honorius' final
intransigence, and an attack on the Goths by Sarus, drove Alaric to a **third siege**. On **24 August 410**
the Visigoths entered by the **Salarian Gate** (allegedly opened from within) and **looted and burned for
three days** before marching south, carrying off Honorius' sister **Galla Placidia**. Alaric died within
months at Consentia.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **failure of the federate settlement** — the western government's refusal to grant
  Alaric the land and command his people sought (Blockley: the sack was "a confession of his failure to
  win a settlement").
- `caused_by:` the **fall of [[stilicho|Stilicho]]** (408) and the massacre of the federates' families,
  which swelled Alaric's army and removed the one Roman able to manage him.
- `contributed_to:` **Honorius' intransigence** and the dysfunction of the Ravenna court.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` an immense **psychological shock** across the Roman world — Jerome: *"In one city the whole
  world perished."*
- `produced:` **Augustine's *City of God*** — written to answer pagans who blamed the catastrophe on the
  abandonment of the old gods, and to detach Christian hope from the fate of the earthly Rome.
- `enabled:` the **Visigothic migration** into Gaul (under Ataulf) and the settlement in **Aquitaine
  (418)** — the first post-Roman barbarian kingdom on imperial soil.

## Historiography

Blockley stresses the gap between the sack's **symbolic** enormity and its limited **strategic** effect:
the eastern court reacted with indifference, and for Ravenna it was "one of a long list of reverses,
endurable provided its own stronghold remained secure." Modern scholarship (Heather, Matthews) reads it
less as the "fall of Rome" than as the failure of the federate experiment and of Theodosian policy. It
remains a standing reference point in the [[causes-of-the-fall-of-the-western-roman-empire|debate over
the fall of the West]].
