# Time of Troubles

## Narrative
The **Time of Troubles** (*Smutnoe Vremia*) (1598–1613) was a period of severe political, social, and national collapse in the Russian Tsardom, initiated by the extinction of the Rurikid dynasty and resolved by the election of Michael Romanov (L2198-2200). 

Following Sergey Platonov's classical analysis, the era can be divided into three consecutive phases based on the primary issues at stake:
1. **The Dynastic Phase (1598–1606):** Following the death of the childless Tsar Theodore in 1598, Boris Godunov was elected tsar (L2176, L2200-2202, L2235). His reign was destabilized by the Great Famine of 1601–1603 (L2237) and the appearance of the First False Dmitry, who claimed to be Ivan IV's youngest son (L2239-2245). Boris Godunov died suddenly in April 1605; his family was murdered, and False Dmitry I entered Moscow as tsar (L2250-2252). He was assassinated in May 1606 in a boyar coup led by Prince Basil Shuisky, who then proclaimed himself tsar (L2265-L2270).
2. **The Social Phase (1606–1610):** Shuisky's reign had little popular support and was immediately challenged by local rebellions (L2280-2282). Ivan Bolotnikov led a massive peasant and Cossack war that besieged Moscow in late 1606 (L2284). In 1607, a Second False Dmitry (the "Felon of Tushino") emerged, establishing a parallel government at Tushino near Moscow (L2292-2296). To counter him, Shuisky made a military alliance with Sweden in 1609 (L2300). This prompted King Sigismund III of Poland to declare war on Russia, besieging Smolensk (L2302-2303). In July 1610, Shuisky was deposed, and a council of seven boyars assumed control (L2306).
3. **The National Phase (1610–1613):** The seven boyars agreed to invite Polish prince Wladyslaw to become tsar, and Polish troops occupied Moscow (L2306, L2312). However, Sigismund III rejected the terms, seeking the throne for himself, and arrested the Russian embassy (L2314-2318). Sweden declared war, occupying Novgorod (L2320). In response to appeals from Patriarch Hermogen, a First National Army was formed in 1611 but dissolved after Cossacks killed its leader, Procopius Liapunov (L2322-L2326). A Second National Army was organized in Nizhnii Novgorod by Kuzma Minin and Prince Dmitry Pozharsky (L2328-2330). In November 1612, they stormed Moscow and liberated the Kremlin (L2334). In early 1613, a Zemsky Sobor elected sixteen-year-old Michael Romanov as tsar, ending the Troubles (L2336-L2340).

## Causal Drivers
* **Dynastic Collapse:** The death of Tsar Theodore in 1598 ended the Rurikid line, and the dubious death of Prince Dmitry of Uglich in 1591 left no natural heir, creating a succession vacuum (L2170-2176, L2200).
* **Social Strife:** The rapid expansion of the gentry *pomestie* system and the introduction of "forbidden years" to tie peasants to the land created a massive, dislocated underclass of runaway peasants and Cossacks who hated the Muscovite social order (L2210-2212, L2220-2226).
* **Geopolitical Opportunism:** Poland-Lithuania and Sweden sought to exploit Russia's sudden domestic collapse to secure territorial gains and place their own candidates on the Muscovite throne (L2202, L2320).

## Consequence Analysis
The Time of Troubles produced:
* **The Romanov Dynasty:** Proclaimed a stable hereditary monarchy that ruled Russia for 300 years until 1917 (L2336, L2340).
* **Territorial Loss:** To secure peace, Russia signed the Treaty of Stolbovo (1617) with Sweden, ceding its Baltic coastline, and the Truce of Deulino (1618) with Poland, ceding Smolensk (L2406-2408).
* **Strengthening of Autocracy:** Despite the collapse of the central government, autocracy survived unimpaired as the only perceived guarantee of internal order; local self-government institutions of the 16th century did not survive (L2364-2366).
* **Consolidation of Serfdom:** The victorious service gentry successfully defended its class interests, leading directly to the final legal binding of the peasantry in the Ulozhenie of 1649 (L2366, L2374-2376).
