# Battle of Morgarten (15 November 1315)

## Narrative

The founding victory of the [[swiss-confederation|Swiss Confederation]]. Duke **Leopold I of
Habsburg** invaded rebellious **Schwyz** with a knightly army; the confederates trapped his column
in the defile between Lake Ägeri and the Morgarten slope. A crossbow advance guard blocked and
jammed the head of the column while the concealed main body charged downhill — with rolling stones
and logs — into the helpless mass. Most knights were cut down or driven into the lake; Leopold barely
escaped. [[delbruck-medieval-warfare-vol-3-1982|Delbrück]] argues it was a **planned ambush**
requiring organised scouting and signalling, not a spontaneous peasant uprising, and dismisses the
chronicle figure of 20,000 as "meaningless" (real strengths ~2,000–3,000 Habsburg, ~3,000–4,000
Swiss).

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` Schwyz's defiance of Habsburg authority and Leopold's punitive campaign. The victory
was produced by the Swiss **choice and preparation of the ground** (the Letzi walls; the baited open
approach along the lake) and by terrain that made cavalry worthless.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` the founding legend and self-confidence of Swiss arms; `enabled:` the line of phalanx
victories from Laupen (1339) through the Burgundian Wars (1476–77) that would make Swiss infantry the
dominant foot of Europe.

## Actors

[[swiss-confederation|Swiss Confederation]] (Schwyz, Uri, Unterwalden; field direction Werner
Stauffacher) · Duke Leopold I of Habsburg.
