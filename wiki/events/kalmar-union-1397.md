# The Kalmar Union (1397–1523)

The dynastic union of the three Scandinavian kingdoms — **Denmark, Norway and Sweden** — under a
single monarch, founded at **Kalmar in 1397** and lasting (with interruptions) until 1523. Treated by
Herman Schück in [[allmand-ncmh-v7-1998|NCMH VII]] (Ch. 26). It was the achievement of one of the
ablest rulers of the age, **Margrete (Margaret) of Denmark**, and the dominant framework of Nordic
politics across the fifteenth century.

## Narrative

**Margrete** — regent of Denmark, then of Norway (through her husband Håkon VI), and finally of
Sweden after defeating its German-backed king at Åsle (1389) — gathered all three crowns and had her
great-nephew **Erik of Pomerania** crowned at **Kalmar in June 1397**. The union was a **personal
union of the crowns under the Danish-led monarchy**, each realm keeping its own laws and council; the
proposed Union Charter, which would have bound them more tightly, was never ratified.

Tension between the Danish crown and the Swedish aristocracy and commons recurred throughout the
century. The miners' and peasants' revolt led by **Engelbrekt Engelbrektsson (1434–36)** — provoked
by Erik's taxation and bailiffs — produced the first Swedish national assembly and a tradition of
Swedish resistance; thereafter Sweden oscillated between union kings (e.g. **Christian I** of
Oldenburg) and native regents (the Sture family). The union was finally broken when **Gustav Vasa**
led Sweden out in **1523** (after the "Stockholm Bloodbath" of 1520), while **Denmark and Norway**
remained joined.

## Causal & Consequence Analysis

`caused_by:` Margrete's **dynastic statecraft** and the high aristocracy's interest in a shared
monarchy that preserved their privileges; `contributed_to:` the **Hanseatic** commercial order of the
Baltic and North Seas, whose Lübeck-led merchants were a constant external factor (and, in 1534–6,
made a last bid to shape the Danish succession). `produced:` a century of Danish-dominated union,
chronic **Swedish separatism**, and ultimately the modern division of Scandinavia into a
**Sweden** and a **Denmark–Norway**.
