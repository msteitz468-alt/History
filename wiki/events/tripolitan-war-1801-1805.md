# First Barbary War / Tripolitan War (1801-1805)

## Narrative

In May 1801 the Pasha of Tripoli, Yusuf Karamanli, declared war on the United States after the Jefferson administration refused increased tribute demands. Jefferson, who had argued against tribute since his time in Europe, dispatched a small naval squadron under Commodore Richard Dale (later reinforced under Edward Preble). Operations included blockade of Tripoli, several inconclusive actions, and the dramatic loss and recapture/destruction of the frigate *Philadelphia* (Stephen Decatur's raid, February 1804).

In 1804-1805 William Eaton, former consul, led a small force of U.S. Marines and mercenaries with the deposed Hamet Karamanli on an overland march from Egypt to capture Derna (April 1805), the only significant land battle. The action helped pressure the Pasha to terms.

A treaty was concluded in June 1805 ending the war with Tripoli. The U.S. paid a ransom for prisoners but no ongoing tribute from Tripoli. Other Barbary powers were temporarily deterred but the problem was not fully solved until later decades.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** Barbary practice of tribute + piracy + Jefferson's refusal to continue the policy of his predecessors.

**enabled:** Small but professional U.S. Navy created under Adams; distance and European wars limiting European intervention.

## Consequence Analysis

- Demonstrated that the U.S. could project power and fight limited wars successfully.
- Established important precedents for presidential use of force (squadron dispatched before formal congressional authorization of full war).
- Mixed results: ended one threat at significant cost; the "shores of Tripoli" entered Marine Corps lore.
- Political: Boosted Jefferson's image as defender of commerce; costs and inconclusive nature fed Federalist and later Randolph criticism.

## Historiography

Malone (Vol. 4) provides detailed reconstruction of Jefferson's decision-making, the cabinet discussions, and the operational narrative. Modern naval historians emphasize Preble's professionalization of the squadron and the importance of the *Philadelphia* episode.

**Sources:** Malone, *Jefferson the President: First Term* (1970); naval records; Eaton's memoir.
