# The Hunger Plan (Hungerplan)

The deliberate Nazi policy, adopted in May 1941 before the invasion of
the USSR, of removing food from the occupied East to feed German troops
and civilians while leaving the local population — "umpteen million"
people, in the words of the May 1941 inter-ministerial meeting minutes —
to starve. Devised principally by Reich Food Ministry state secretary
**Herbert Backe** and folded into Himmler's **General Plan for the East**,
which in final form projected 30–45 million Slav deaths by engineered
hunger and withheld medical care (Evans corrects Collingham's 100-million
figure as exaggerated). It was never fully executed only because Germany
lost the war in the East.

## Narrative

Extracted from Evans, *The Third Reich in History and Memory* (2015),
chs. 20 and 23–25, reviewing Collingham (*The Taste of War*), Mazower and
Snyder; figures attributed:

- The motivation reached back to the First World War: over half a million
  German civilians had died of blockade-induced malnutrition, a trauma
  central to Hitler's strategic thinking; German fat rationing began as
  early as January 1937. About 40% of the bread and meat consumed by
  German forces and civilians during the war came from occupied
  territories or deported labor.
- **Soviet POWs**: ~3.3 million deliberately killed by starvation,
  disease, neglect or shooting (Evans's "generally agreed figure,"
  correcting Collingham's 2.35 million).
- **Leningrad**: German forces were ordered to starve the city rather
  than storm it; at least a million died, with widespread cannibalism
  reported.
- **Kharkov**: sealed by the occupiers; roughly a third of the remaining
  450,000 inhabitants dead by the end of 1942, "almost all... from
  starvation."
- **Kiev**: "Kiev must starve" — population fell from ~815,000 to 295,000
  by October 1943 (see [[battle-of-kiev-1941]]).
- **Warsaw Ghetto**: up to 100,000 dead (Collingham), though Evans notes
  many died of typhus rather than starvation directly.

## Causal Analysis

- caused_by: Lebensraum ideology — the plan was the logistical face of
  [[nazi-germany|the Nazi empire's]] racial reordering (Generalplan Ost).
- contributed_to: [[the-holocaust]] — "useless eaters" food-supply
  arguments were used to justify and possibly accelerate the murder of
  the Jews in spring 1942, though Evans argues this was "a convenient
  military rationalisation for an ideologically motivated policy" already
  in motion by autumn 1941.
- contrasts_with: Allied-sphere wartime famines (the 1943 Bengal famine,
  up to 3 million dead amid Churchill's shipping cuts and provincial
  protectionism; East African famine, ~300,000 dead in Rwanda) and
  Japan's starvation catastrophes (90% of 158,000 troops on New Guinea;
  ~400,000 of 500,000 in the Philippines) — Collingham's global
  "food as weapon" frame, which Evans warns against elevating into the
  war's master explanation.

## Historiography

Long subsumed under general occupation history; reconstructed by Aly &
Heim (*Architects of Annihilation*), Gerlach, Tooze (*Wages of
Destruction*) and Collingham (2012). The plan's relation to the Final
Solution — instrument, accelerant, or parallel program — remains debated;
see [[holocaust-uniqueness]] and [[the-holocaust]].
