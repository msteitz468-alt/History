# Battle of Kulikovo
## Narrative
On **September 8, 1380 CE**, a coalition of Russian forces led by Grand Prince **[[dmitry-donskoy|Dmitry Donskoy]]** of Moscow met the Mongol-Tatar army of the Golden Horde under the command of military strongman **Mamai** at **Kulikovo Field**, located where the Nepriadva River flows into the upper Don.

As Moscow grew in strength and Horde authority declined due to civil wars, the Mongol strongman Mamai launched a major expedition to reassert control. He formed a military alliance with Grand Prince Jagiello of Lithuania, assembling a force of approximately 200,000 troops. 

Dmitry Donskoy, determined to prevent the Mongol-Lithuanian linkup, crossed the Don River with a force of about 150,000 men to force a battle on hilly terrain intersected by streams, which limited the maneuverability of the Mongol cavalry. 

The battle was fought with desperate ferocity. Dmitry himself fought on foot in the frontlines, dressed as a common soldier, and was knocked unconscious during the combat. The turning point came when the last Russian reserve, hidden in ambush in a forest, struck the exhausted and unsuspecting Mongol flank. Mamai’s army was completely routed. Jagiello’s Lithuanian forces, which were only two days away, retreated without fighting (l. 1519).

## Causal Analysis
* **Defiance of Tribute:** Moscow’s refusal to pay tribute and its defeat of a minor Mongol force at the Vozha River (1378) forced Mamai to mount a punitive campaign.
* **Preemptive Russian Strategy:** Dmitry’s decision to cross the Don and engage Mamai before the arrival of the Lithuanian forces was the critical strategic move.
* **Selection of Terrain:** The choice of hilly, forested terrain restricted the flanking movements of the Mongol cavalry, forcing a frontal assault where Russian foot soldiers could hold their ground.

## Consequence Analysis
* **End of Mongol Invincibility:** The battle broke the 140-year-old myth of Mongol military invincibility.
* **Rise of Moscow's Moral Leadership:** Dmitry Donskoy emerged as the champion of the Russian people. The victory rallied some twenty princes under Moscow's banner in a campaign blessed by the Church as a crusade.
* **Temporary Reversal:** Although Khan Tokhtamysh sacked Moscow in 1382 and forced a resumption of tribute, the Mongol grip on Russia was permanently weakened and never regained its former absolute authority.

## Actors
* **[[dmitry-donskoy|Dmitry Donskoy]]:** Grand Prince of Moscow and overall Russian commander.
* **Mamai:** The military ruler of the Golden Horde who led the invasion.
* **Grand Prince Jagiello of Lithuania:** Foe, who failed to arrive in time.

## Historiography
The Battle of Kulikovo is celebrated as the epic beginning of Russian liberation. It inspired a rich literary cycle, including the *Story of the Massacre of Mamai* and the *Zadonshchina*. Modern historians analyze it as the moment when Moscow’s proprietary interests merged with the national cause of the Russian people.
