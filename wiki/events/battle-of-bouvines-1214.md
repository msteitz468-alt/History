# The Battle of Bouvines (27 July 1214)

**Bouvines** was the decisive battle of the early-thirteenth-century struggle for western
Europe, in which **[[philip-ii-augustus|Philip II Augustus]]** of France crushed a coalition
of **Otto IV of Brunswick** (the Welf emperor), the counts of **Flanders** and **Boulogne**,
and English contingents financed by **[[john-of-england|King John]]**.
[[abulafia-ncmh-v5-2000|NCMH V]] treats it as the hinge of three histories at once — French,
English and Imperial.

## Narrative

The battle was the climax of **John's great campaign of 1214** to recover Normandy and
Anjou. While John was outfaced in the south (Anjou), his **northern allies** met Philip
Augustus near Valenciennes in Flanders and were "decisively defeated." Otto IV fled; the
counts of Flanders (Ferrand) and Boulogne were captured. **Frederick of Hohenstaufen was not
even present** — the French king is said to have sent him the imperial eagle "left lying on
the battlefield."

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` **John's bid to reverse the loss of Normandy (1204)**, for which he had spent
  a decade and a treasure of £130,000 rebuilding the alliances of his brother Richard I.
- `contributed_to:` the **Welf–Hohenstaufen civil war** in Germany, which made Otto IV
  dependent on English subsidy and drew the Empire into the Anglo-French conflict; and the
  **Capetian–Hohenstaufen alliance** Philip Augustus renewed against the Welf.

## Consequence Analysis

Bouvines was triple in its effects:

- **France**: it **confirmed the Capetian conquest of Normandy and Anjou** permanently,
  "tipping the balance of power decisively" toward the Capetians and consummating the
  destruction of the [[angevin-empire|Angevin empire]].
- **England**: John "had no alternative but to agree to a six-year truce," his hopes of
  recovery over. Returning "humiliated, penniless and at the mercy of his domestic enemies,"
  he faced the rebellion that produced **[[magna-carta-1215|Magna Carta]]** — "the road from
  Bouvines to Runnymede was direct, short and unavoidable" (Holt).
- **The Empire**: the defeat finished **Otto IV**, whose remaining strongholds fell the next
  summer, and secured the triumph of **[[frederick-ii-hohenstaufen|Frederick II]]**, ratified
  at the Fourth Lateran Council (1215).
