# Granma Landing (2–5 December 1956)

The near-catastrophic landing of the yacht *Granma* carrying 82 members of the 26th of July Movement from Mexico to Cuba on 2 December 1956, followed by the ambush at Alegría de Pío on 5 December that reduced the force to a handful of survivors who reached the Sierra Maestra. The event marked the effective start of the guerrilla phase of the Cuban Revolution.

## Narrative

After months of training in Mexico, the expedition left Tuxpan on 25 November 1956 aboard the overloaded yacht *Granma*. The voyage took seven days instead of the planned five; the vessel struck a sandbar and the men waded ashore in daylight on 2 December near Playa las Coloradas, having jettisoned much of their equipment. Batista's forces were alerted immediately. On 5 December at Alegría de Pío the column was surprised in a cane field; heavy losses. Only about 22 ultimately regrouped in the sierra with Fidel and Che among the survivors.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** The decision to launch a small armed expedition from exile after the 1953 Moncada failure and imprisonment/amnesties. Poor navigation, overloading, and loss of surprise turned a high-risk plan into a near annihilation.

## Consequence Analysis

The disaster paradoxically created the legend of the Sierra Maestra and the tiny band that would grow into the Rebel Army. It forced reliance on local peasant networks ("Radio Bemba") and rapid adaptation. Che's personal moment at the ambush (reaching for ammunition rather than medical kit, neck wound and fatalistic reverie) cemented his identity as combatant.

**produced:** The Sierra Maestra base and the core leadership group that won the war.
**preceded_by:** Moncada attack and Mexican exile organization.

## Actors

Primary: Fidel Castro, Ernesto "Che" Guevara, the other Granma survivors (Almeida, Camilo Cienfuegos later arrivals, etc.).

## Historiography

Official accounts emphasize heroism and inevitability. Anderson uses Che's field diary and survivor testimony to stress the chaos, loss of weapons, thirst/hunger, and the role of peasant guides in the survival of the remnant. The "Granma myth" was later cultivated; the actual landing was a military fiasco redeemed by subsequent adaptation.
