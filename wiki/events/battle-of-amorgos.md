# Battle of Amorgos

A decisive naval defeat inflicted on the Athenian-led Hellenic League fleet
by a Macedonian fleet under Cleitus the White in 322 BCE, during the Lamian
War that followed Alexander the Great's death.

## Narrative

After early Greek successes besieging Antipater at Lamia, Macedonian
reinforcements under Craterus and Leonnatus, together with Craterus'
war fleet, turned the naval balance. Cleitus the White's Macedonian fleet
met and destroyed the Athenian fleet near Amorgos in the Cyclades. Coming
after an earlier Athenian naval defeat at the Hellespont, Amorgos ended
organized Athenian resistance at sea.

## Causal Analysis

The battle was a direct consequence of Athens' strategic decision, at the
outset of the Lamian War, to contest Macedonian naval superiority in order
to isolate Antipater's besieged army — a strategy that depended on Athens
maintaining the qualitative and numerical edge it had enjoyed decades
earlier, which Macedon's post-Alexander resources had eliminated.

## Consequence Analysis

With its fleet destroyed, Athens lost the ability to project power or
resist blockade, contributing directly to its defeat later in 322 BCE, the
dissolution of the democracy, and the imposition of a Macedonian garrison at
Munychia — the effective end of Athens as an independent naval power.

## Historiography

The battle is known mainly through Diodorus Siculus (see
[[diodorus-siculus]]), whose account preserves fragments of the
contemporary historian Hieronymus of Cardia; precise details of fleet sizes
and tactics are thin and debated, typical of the sparse source base for the
Lamian War compared to the Classical-period Aegean conflicts that precede
it.
