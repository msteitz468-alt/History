# The Peace of Nisibis (298)

The **most favourable settlement Rome ever won from Persia** — imposed on the Sassanian king **Narses**
by the Caesar **Galerius** and the Augustus **[[diocletian|Diocletian]]** after Galerius' crushing
victory of 297. It reversed the humiliations of the [[capture-of-valerian-260|Valerian]] generation and
fixed the eastern frontier for forty years. Treated in [[bowman-cah-v12-2005|CAH XII]] Chs. 14 (**Frye**)
and 15 (**C. S. Lightfoot**); the terms survive via the sixth-century fragment of **Petrus Patricius**.

## Narrative

After Narses invaded Roman Mesopotamia and **defeated Galerius** between Carrhae and Callinicum (296),
Diocletian — busy in Egypt — demanded a counter-stroke. In 297 Galerius led a field army into
**[[armenia|Armenia]]**, **surprised and routed** Narses' camp **east of Satala**, capturing the Persian
king's harem, sisters and children (Narses himself escaping). The ensuing treaty, negotiated at length and
concluded c. 298–299, ceded to Rome:

- all lands **west of the Tigris** north of Mosul — the **five *regiones Transtigritanae*** (trans-Tigris
  principalities) acknowledged as Roman vassals;
- additional territory to **Armenia** under the restored king **[[armenia|Tiridates IV]]**, extending its
  frontier east to Zintha in Atropatene;
- **suzerainty over Iberia** (whose king the Romans confirmed);
- the designation of **Nisibis as the sole authorized point of trade** between the two empires — the only
  place where tolls were levied and border control exercised.

In exchange Narses recovered his captured family.

## Causal & Consequence Analysis

- `caused_by:` **Narses' aggression** (the invasion of 296) and `caused_by:` **Galerius' decisive victory**
  at Satala — a rare instance of Roman field superiority over the Sassanians.
- `produced:` a **40-year peace** (held until Shapur II reopened hostilities c. 337), during which
  Diocletian **reorganized the eastern *limes*** (the *Strata Diocletiana*, new legions I/II Armeniacae and
  IV/V/VI Parthicae) and stabilized the frontier.
- `enabled:` the consolidation of **Christian Armenia** under Tiridates IV and the long Roman ascendancy in
  Mesopotamia that Constantine inherited.

## Historiography

The settlement is known chiefly through the **Petrus Patricius** fragment and is read against Galerius'
**Thessalonica arch** (where he is *Persicus Maximus, Armeniacus, Medicus, Adiabenicus*). Lightfoot notes
the difficulty of matching the **five *regiones*** of 298 to the regions Jovian surrendered in 363, and
that there is "scant evidence" for an actual Diocletianic fortification of the upper-Euphrates *limes*
despite the treaty's reorganization.
