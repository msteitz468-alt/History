# Battle of Lake Regillus

## Narrative

Roman tradition places this battle, fought near Lake Regillus southeast of Rome, in either 499 or 496 BCE, as the decisive engagement of a war between the fledgling Roman Republic and a coalition of Latin cities backing the deposed king Tarquinius Superbus's attempt to reclaim his throne. The dictator Aulus Postumius (or, in some versions, with Titus Aebutius as master of horse) led Roman forces to victory over the Latins under Octavius Mamilius of Tusculum. The battle became embedded in Roman religious memory through the legend of the Dioscuri (Castor and Pollux), said to have fought for Rome and then appeared, watering their horses, at the Spring of Juturna in the Forum to announce the victory — an episode commemorated by the Temple of Castor and Pollux.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** the Latin League's alliance with the exiled Tarquin dynasty against the new Republic; contributed_to by ongoing regional rivalry between Rome and the Latin cities over dominance in Latium.

## Consequence Analysis

**produced:** the negotiated [[foedus-cassianum-493bce|Foedus Cassianum]] (493 BCE), which formalized a mutual-defense alliance between Rome and the Latin League on terms of rough equality, later becoming the basis for Rome's expanding hegemony over Latium.

## Actors

(Roman tradition's named commanders — Aulus Postumius for Rome, Octavius Mamilius for the Latins — belong to the early, semi-legendary regal-to-early-republican period and are not treated here as independently verified historical actors with dedicated pages.)

## Historiography

Like most events from Rome's first century as a republic, Lake Regillus is known only through much later literary tradition (Livy, Dionysius of Halicarnassus), heavily overlaid with religious legend (the Dioscuri epiphany) and likely conflating or embellishing an underlying, genuine early conflict between Rome and the Latin League; both the precise date and the battle's true scale are treated by modern historians as uncertain.
