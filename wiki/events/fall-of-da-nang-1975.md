# Fall of Da Nang

The collapse of South Vietnam's second city, 28–30 March 1975, during PAVN's
Ho Chi Minh Campaign. Da Nang fell not to a set-piece battle but to a
disintegration of command and discipline: hundreds of thousands of refugees
and retreating soldiers from the Central Highlands and Huế converged on the
city, overwhelming its port and airfield before PAVN forces occupied it
against negligible organized resistance.

## Narrative

President Nguyễn Văn Thiệu's March 1975 order to abandon the Central
Highlands and redeploy forces toward the coast triggered a chaotic retreat
along Route 7B that destroyed much of the II Corps' cohesion. Refugees and
broken units then flooded north Coastal cities, including Da Nang, which had
been I Corps headquarters and a major American-built logistics base. Attempts
at evacuation by sea and air descended into disorder; PAVN units entered the
city on 29–30 March against essentially no defense.

## Causal Analysis

The fall was less a military defeat than an administrative and command
collapse cascading from the Central Highlands withdrawal — a case study cited
in South Vietnamese and American postmortems of how a single ambiguous
strategic order (a partial, unannounced retreat) can unravel an army faster
than direct combat.

## Consequence Analysis

Da Nang's loss, following Huế days earlier, eliminated organized South
Vietnamese resistance across the entire northern half of the country within
a single month and freed PAVN forces to concentrate on the drive toward
Saigon, culminating three weeks later in the [[phuoc-long-1974|test case]]
already passed at Phước Long and the fighting at [[xuan-loc-1975|Xuân Lộc]].

## Historiography

Accounts converge on the sequence of events; the central historiographical
question is whether Thiệu's withdrawal order was the decisive proximate
cause of the general collapse (the dominant view in American military
historiography) or whether it merely accelerated a disintegration already
underway given the erosion of US aid and airpower after 1973.
