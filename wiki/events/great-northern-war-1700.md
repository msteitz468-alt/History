# Great Northern War

## Narrative
The **Great Northern War** (1700–1721) was a monumental conflict waged by a coalition of Northern European powers, led by the Russian Tsardom under Peter the Great, against the Swedish Empire under Charles XII for control of the Baltic region (L2862-2866).

The war began in 1700 with a series of quick Swedish victories (L2866). Charles XII forced Denmark to surrender, then routed a much larger Russian army at the **Battle of Narva** in November 1700 (L2866-2870). Underestimating the Russians, Charles XII turned his main attention to Poland to depose King Augustus II, giving Peter I a crucial respite (L2872-2874).

Peter I utilized this time to rebuild his army, cast new cannons, and advance in Livonia and Estonia (L2874). In 1703, he founded St. Petersburg near the Neva River (L2874). After defeating Augustus II and forcing the Treaty of Altranstadt (1706), Charles XII launched a decisive campaign against Russia in 1708, advancing with 50,000 men (L2876-2880). 

Instead of marching on Moscow, Charles XII swerved south into Ukraine to join forces with the defecting Ukrainian Hetman Ivan Mazepa (L2884). However, Peter I intercepted and smashed Swedish reinforcements at the **Battle of Lesnaia** in October 1708, capturing their supply train (L2884-2888). The Swedish army was finally destroyed by Peter I at the **Battle of Poltava** on July 8, 1709, forcing Charles XII to escape to Turkey (L2888-2890).

Although the war was decided in 1709, it dragged on for twelve more years (L2894). Russia captured Viborg, Riga, and Reval (1710), occupied Finland, and won naval victories off Hangö (1714) (L2894-L2900). Following Charles XII's death in 1718, Sweden finally sued for peace (L2900). The war ended with the **Treaty of Nystad** on August 30, 1721 (L2900).

## Causal Analysis
The war was caused by:
* **The Baltic Window:** Peter I sought a direct maritime trade and communication route to Western Europe, bypassing Sweden's monopoly on Baltic ports (L2862, L2908).
* **Anti-Swedish Coalition:** The Baltic nobleman Johann Reinhold Patkul brought Russia, Denmark, and Poland-Saxony together in a coalition, hoping to exploit the accession of the fifteen-year-old Charles XII (L2864-2866).

## Consequence Analysis
The Great Northern War produced:
* **Treaty of Nystad:** Russia acquired Livonia, Estonia, Ingermanland, and Karelia, establishing itself firmly on the Baltic Sea (L2902).
* **Rise of the Russian Empire:** In October 1721, the Senate bestowed upon Peter I the titles of "Great" and "Emperor," officially inaugurating the Imperial Period of Russian history (L2902-L2906).
* **Decline of Sweden:** Sweden was reduced to a second-rate power, losing its hegemony in northern Europe (L2908).
* **Reforms and Cost:** The war acted as the primary driver for Peter the Great's internal reforms (conscription, administrative colleges, Governing Senate, and the poll tax), but it exacted a heavy toll in human lives and financial strain, exhausting the country's resources (L2922-2924, L3028-3030, L3040).
