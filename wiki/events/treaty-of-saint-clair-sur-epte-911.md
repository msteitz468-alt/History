# Treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte (Founding of Normandy)

The **Treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte** (911) was the agreement by which the west Frankish king **[[capetian-dynasty|Charles the Simple]]** settled the Viking leader **Rollo** (Old Norse *Hrólfr*) and his followers on the lower Seine — the founding act of what became the **duchy of Normandy**. [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]] (Dunbabin) treats it as a landmark of the period: a settlement of Vikings as defenders against future Vikings, and a demonstration that royal **peace-making authority** still counted even when the princes did the fighting.

## Narrative

After two generations of devastating Viking raids and winter camps along the Seine and Loire, a coalition of princes — **Robert of Neustria**, **Richard le Justicier** of Burgundy, and the count of Poitou — routed a Viking force near **Chartres** (c. 911), putting Rollo to flight. According to **Dudo of Saint-Quentin** (writing a century later, and the chief, tendentious source), the Franks then proposed that Rollo be granted the land "from the river Andelle to the sea" to settle, in return for accepting baptism and undertaking to defend the realm against further Viking incursions. The grant was carved from the lands of Robert and the king. Charles thereby converted an intractable military problem into a frontier lordship; he gained, too, a counterweight to Robertine dominance in Neustria.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the long failure to expel the **Seine Vikings** by force, and the **rout near Chartres** that gave the king a moment of leverage.
- `contributed_to:` Charles the Simple's need for an **independent power-base** against the "Robertine wall" excluding him from Neustria.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the nucleus of the **duchy of Normandy** — a polity that rapidly Christianised, adopted the Frankish language and institutions, and (under Rollo's successors) expanded to become one of the strongest principalities of west Francia.
- `enabled:` the later Norman conquests of **England (1066)** and **southern Italy/Sicily**, and a distinctive Norman fusion of Scandinavian dynamism with Frankish administration.
- `produced:` a precedent for the **assimilation of Scandinavian settlers** through grant, baptism, and military service — contrasted with the conquest-and-tribute pattern of the contemporary Danelaw in England.

## Historiography

NCMH III flags the **source problem**: the treaty's details survive chiefly through **Dudo of Saint-Quentin**, a court panegyrist writing c. 1015–1020 whose account is "richly juicy" but unreliable. The historicity of the ceremony (the famous tale of Rollo's man refusing to kiss the king's foot) and the precise terms are uncertain; what is secure is that a durable Norman principality emerged on the lower Seine in the early tenth century with royal sanction.
