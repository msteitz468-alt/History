# Battle of Tertry (687)

The **Battle of Tertry** (687 CE) was a major battle in Merovingian Francia. It pitted the forces of Austrasia, led by their Mayor of the Palace **Pippin II of Herstal**, against the forces of Neustria and Burgundy, led by their Mayor **Berchar** and the Merovingian king **Theuderic III**. Pippin’s victory established the absolute political ascendancy of the Austrasian Mayor of the Palace over the entire Frankish realm, marking the de facto end of Merovingian royal power and laying the foundations for the rise of the Pippinid (later Carolingian) dynasty.

## Narrative and Context

Following the reunification of the Frankish sub-kingdoms under Chlothar II in 613, the power of the central Merovingian monarchy declined. Control of the state’s resources and patronage was increasingly seized by the Mayors of the Palace in Neustria (the western realm) and Austrasia (the eastern realm).

In 680, Pippin II of Herstal became the Mayor of the Palace of Austrasia. Following a period of conflict, Berchar became the Mayor of Neustria in 686. Berchar's high-handed rule alienated many Neustrian nobles, who fled to Austrasia and urged Pippin to launch an invasion.

In 687, Pippin crossed the Somme River with an Austrasian army. The two forces clashed at Tertry. Pippin’s army routed the Neustrian forces. Berchar fled the field and was assassinated shortly after by his own followers, while King Theuderic III was captured by Pippin.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by: [[francia|Austrasian-Neustrian Rivalry]]`: The battle was the culmination of decades of political and military competition between the eastern (Austrasian) and western (Neustrian) Frankish elites for control of the unified Merovingian court.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced: [[pippinids|Pippinid Hegemony]]`: Following the battle, Pippin II did not depose King Theuderic III; instead, he retained him as a puppet monarch, using him to validate his own acts. Pippin assumed the title *Princeps Francorum* ("Prince of the Franks") and appointed his loyal supporters to Neustrian office, establishing de facto sole rule over all Francia.
- `enabled: [[carolingian-empire|Carolingian Rise]]`: The victory secured the wealth of the Neustrian fisc and the control of major bishoprics for the Pippinid family. This concentrated resources in Pippin's lineage, allowing his son Charles Martel and grandson Charlemagne to consolidate power and eventually overthrow the Merovingians in 751.

## Historiography

The primary sources for the battle are the *Liber Historiae Francorum* and the *Continuations of the Chronicle of Fredegar*. These texts, written under Pippinid and Carolingian patronage, frame the Battle of Tertry as a turning point that restored peace and order to a fractured Frankish realm. Modern historians (like Paul Fouracre) note that while Tertry was a decisive military victory, Pippin's control over Neustria and Burgundy remained contested, requiring decades of campaigning and family alliances to fully secure his family's hegemony.
