# Beaver Wars (mid–late 17th century)

## Narrative

Series of wars in which the [[haudenosaunee-confederacy]] (Five Nations) expanded against neighbors—most dramatically the [[huron-wendat]]—amid competition for furs, European manufactures, and captives. Salisbury (CHNPA I.1 ch. 7) places the conflict at the center of mid-seventeenth-century Eastern Woodlands transformation: “fierce competition between New France and the Five Nations Iroquois for control of the region’s furs… [shaping] much of eastern North American history for the rest of the century and beyond.”

### Setup

[[mohawk]] triumph over Mahicans (by 1630) and access to Fort Orange guns, plus southern New England wampum after [[pequot-war-1637]], gave the Iroquois unrivaled manufactures while depleting their own hunting territories. Hurons had exhausted own beaver and depended on northern Nipissings/Ottawas for thick pelts, delivering corn and European goods inland—but lacked European gun allies on the same scale. Structural contrast: Hurons had Indian allies with northern pelts; Iroquois had European allies who would provide guns.

### Huron crisis and climax (1630s–1649)

After English occupation of Canada (1629–32), French insisted Hurons accept Jesuits as price of reviving alliance. Epidemics culminating in massive smallpox 1639–40 cut Huron population of ~21,000 in half. From 1643 Christian Huron traders received special French post treatment (higher prices, gun permission); over five years >50% of traders converted but <15% of total population Christian—deep divisions in a consensus-oriented society.

With Dutch guns, Mohawks attacked Algonquins and Hurons carrying pelts to Montreal, diverting goods to Fort Orange; captives—some tortured to death, **most adopted** as replacements for disease/raid dead. After Dutch West India Company relinquished Fort Orange monopoly (1639), weapons flow escalated into coordinated full-scale warfare. From 1648 Iroquois destroyed whole villages while offering peace/trade via Dutch and reunion with captives. Within a year more than half the Huron nation was Christian—“part of the collective resignation that overtook the Hurons as the Iroquois utterly destroyed them as a political entity in the winter of 1648–9.” Survivors: some with Jesuits (Lorette near Quebec); many among Iroquois; others fled to Petun, Neutral, Erie.

### Cascade (early 1650s–)

Early 1650s Iroquois dispersed Petuns, Neutrals, Eries; drove most remaining Ottawas, [[shawnee]], and other Algonquians from Michigan and Ohio Valley (refugees to Wisconsin; some Eries/Shawnees south). 1653 Green Bay attack repelled by Potawatomi-led refugees—first suggestion Iroquois expansion had limits. Western ripple: Neutrals had already driven Central Algonquians (Sauk, Fox/Mesquakie, Potawatomi) west; pan-Algonquian Green Bay community became major pro-French, anti-Iroquois power center. Erie refugees later appear in Virginia piedmont as “Westos” (repelled Virginia expulsion 1656), then as heavily armed Carolina slave-raid clients.

### Mid-century limits and late wars to 1701

Susquehannocks emerged as major anti-Iroquois force with Swedish then Dutch/Maryland arms. 1663 ~800 Senecas, Cayugas, Onondagas attacked main Susquehannock village and were repulsed (with Delaware contingent)—“clear demonstration… that the Iroquois were not invincible.” English conquest of New Netherland (1664) cut Dutch arms; French raids burned Mohawk villages 1666; peace with France 1665–67. Renewed western war 1680s–90s met French western military alliance; estimated Iroquois losses 1689–98: 500 of 2,000 fighting men and 1,600 of total population 8,600; Mississaugas ([[ojibwe]] branch) occupied southern Ontario hunting territories. **Grand Settlement of 1701:** French opened hunting/trade as far as Detroit for Iroquois neutrality in future Anglo-French wars; English treaty kept Albany privileges; Covenant Chain redefined to exclude Iroquois military activity for English.

## Causal Analysis

- caused_by: [[eastern-north-american-fur-trade]] competition (New France vs Five Nations) and Dutch arms via Mohawk
- contributed_to: epidemic depopulation (Huron half lost by 1640; Iroquois mourning-war captive needs)
- contributed_to: captive adoption as demographic-military strategy—“most… adopted as replacements for Mohawks who had died from disease or in the escalating cycle of raids”
- concurrent_with: Dutch–English–French colonial rivalry; coastal wampum commercialization; Jesuit mission politics among Hurons

## Consequence Analysis

- produced: destruction of Huron confederacy as territorial/political entity (1648–49); dispersal of Petun, Neutral, Erie
- produced: refugee cascade into Wisconsin/Green Bay multi-ethnic pro-French centers; later Ohio “republican” coalescence
- enabled: Covenant Chain politics with English (1677+) and Grand Settlement neutrality (1701)
- contributed_to: eighteenth-century southern mourning wars vs [[catawba]] after Detroit peace removed western targets
- scale_consequential civilizational: reordered Great Lakes–Ohio demography and alliance structure for the rest of the colonial period

## Actors

[[haudenosaunee-confederacy]] (Five Nations; [[mohawk]] as gun/wampum conduit); [[huron-wendat]]; Petun, Neutral, Erie; French Jesuits, traders, and officials; Dutch at Fort Orange; Susquehannocks; Central Algonquian refugees; later French-allied Great Lakes nations and Mississauga/Ojibwa.

## Historiography

Older “economic” beaver-depletion model vs multi-causal interpretations emphasizing mourning wars, demography, diplomacy, and captive adoption (Salisbury synthesis). Trigger *Children of Aataentsic* remains foundational for Huron; Richter *Ordeal of the Longhouse* for Iroquois; White *Middle Ground* for pays d’en haut refugee coalition. Campaign numbers and exact village sequences debated; political destruction of Huronia as entity is not.
