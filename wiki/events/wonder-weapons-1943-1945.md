# The Wonder Weapons (1943–1945)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]].

- **V-1** (pulse-jet cruise missile; first ten launched at London 13 June 1944; 22,384 fired, a third to a half failing to arrive) and **V-2** (von Braun's ballistic rocket; ~3,200 fired, most at Belgium): together ~6,000 V-1s and 1,000+ V-2s hit Britain, killing ~9,000 and destroying 31,600 houses — meaningful terror, negligible strategy. Built underground at the Mittelwerk with Dora camp labor after the Peenemünde raid: Evans endorses Neufeld's verdict that the V-2 was "a unique weapon: more people died producing it than died from being hit by it" (~20,000 laborers vs. ~5,000 victims). See [[forced-labor-nazi-germany]] and [[speer-albert]].
- **The nuclear program** never passed laboratory scale — split between rival teams (Heisenberg/Hahn vs. Diebner/Ardenne), starved of uranium and priority, its scientists having talked themselves out of "Jewish physics" objections at a 1940 conference without ever getting the resources or, perhaps, the will.
- **Nerve agents** — Tabun, Sarin, Soman (IG Farben; 12,000 tonnes of Tabun by June 1944) — were never used: no defense against blowback, and fear of Allied retaliation in kind.
- **Jets and boats:** the Me262 lost a year to Hitler's bomber fixation; the Type XXI electro-boats arrived too late to sink a single ship. The V-3 supergun was bombed before completion; a multi-stage rocket's only strike killed "several chickens and a dog"; an April 1945 "death ray" championed by Ley and Bormann was an amateur's fantasy.

Evans's verdict: the regime's "inability to prioritize" and its institutional infighting doomed every program; their chief real function was propaganda — sustaining the home front's hope of a miracle into 1945 (and, at Dora, killing prisoners on a scale their targets never suffered).

## From Shirer, *The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich* (1960)

Shirer adds hard-data detail on the intelligence/bombing campaign against the program: RAF reconnaissance, tipped off by the Polish underground, photographed Peenemünde in May 1943, and an August 1943 British bombing raid damaged the installation, delaying V-1/V-2 research by months. By November 1943 the Allies had located 63 V-1 launch sites in the Pas-de-Calais; between December 1943 and February 1944 they destroyed 73 of 96 identified sites. The "V" designation stood for *Vergeltungswaffen* ("weapons of reprisal").

## Historiography

Neufeld (*The Rocket and the Reich*) anchors the V-2 literature and the "more died producing it" verdict Evans adopts; the German nuclear program's failure (resources and self-limitation vs. Heisenberg's postwar moral-reluctance narrative) remains its own debate (Walker vs. the *Farm Hall* readings), which Evans resolves firmly on the side of incapacity, not conscience.
