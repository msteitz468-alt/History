# The Gothic War (535–554)

The **Gothic War** was a protracted and highly destructive conflict waged by the eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire under [[justinian|Justinian I]] against the Ostrogothic Kingdom for control of Italy. Spanning nearly two decades, the war devastated Italy's urban infrastructure, decimated the Roman senatorial aristocracy, and ended Ostrogothic rule. The Byzantine victory was short-lived; the resulting administrative exhaustion and depopulation created a power vacuum that facilitated the **[[lombards|Lombard invasion of Italy (568)]]**, permanently dividing the peninsula.

---

## Narrative

### Casus Belli and Phase 1: Belisarius's Victories (535–540)
Following the death of the Ostrogothic king Theoderic in 526, Italy suffered dynastic instability. In 535, the pro-Byzantine regent **Amalasuentha** (Theoderic's daughter) was deposed and murdered by her cousin **Theodahad**. Justinian utilized this murder as a *casus belli* to launch a reconquest of Italy.

Justinian sent [[belisarius|Belisarius]] with a small, elite force. Belisarius swiftly took Sicily (535), captured Naples, and entered Rome (536). The Goths deposed the incompetent Theodahad and elected **Witigis** as king. Witigis gathered the Gothic host and besieged Rome (537–538). Despite Witigis cutting the city's aqueducts and launching massive assaults, Belisarius defended Rome successfully. Belisarius then marched north and captured the Gothic capital of **Ravenna** in 540 by feigning acceptance of the Goths' offer to crown him Western Roman Emperor. Belisarius returned to Constantinople with Witigis as prisoner.

### Phase 2: Gothic Resurgence under Totila (541–551)
Justinian's refusal to appoint a single commander in Italy, combined with the arrival of ruthless Byzantine tax collectors (*logothetes* like Alexander "the Scissors") and the outbreak of the **[[justinianic-plague-541|Justinianic Plague]]**, alienated the local population. 

In 541, the Goths elected **[[totila|Totila]]** (Baduila) as king. Totila launched a brilliant guerrilla campaign. To rebuild the Gothic army, he implemented radical social measures, freeing agricultural slaves, recruiting Roman *coloni* (tenant farmers), and promising land redistribution. Totila swept through Italy, captured Naples (543), besieged and took Rome (546), and built a Gothic navy. Belisarius was sent back to Italy but was starved of reinforcements and could not check Totila’s advance, leading to his final recall in 548.

### Phase 3: Narses's Victory (552–554)
In 551, Justinian appointed his elderly eunuch general **[[narses]]** to command the war, equipping him with a massive army of 35,000 men, including Herul, Lombard, and Hunnic mercenaries. Narses marched around the head of the Adriatic, bypassing Gothic strongholds, and confronted Totila.

*   **Battle of Busta Gallorum (Taginae, 552):** Narses utilized dismounted cavalry and archers to crush the Gothic charge. Totila was mortally wounded.
*   **Battle of Mons Lactarius (553):** Narses defeated Totila’s successor, Teias, near Mount Vesuvius, ending organized Gothic resistance.
*   Narses then repelled a massive Frankish-Alamannic invasion (553–554) at the Battle of the Volturnus (Casilinum), securing the peninsula.

## The Settlement: Pragmatic Sanction of 554

Justinian issued the **Pragmatic Sanction of 554** to settle Italy's administration:
*   **Restoration of Property:** Returned land and slaves to the Roman senatorial aristocracy, reversing Totila's social reforms.
*   **Fiscal Exaction:** Imposed back-taxes on the impoverished population to fund the military administration.
*   **Episcopal Authority:** Empowered local Catholic bishops to supervise municipal judges, grain distribution, and local finances, cementing the bishop's role as civic administrator.

## Historiographical Significance

In *The New Cambridge Medieval History, Vol. 1*, **John Moorhead** argues that the Gothic War was a structural catastrophe for Italy:
1.  **Urban Ruin:** The repeated sieges of Rome, Milan, and Naples ruined the country's physical landscape. Rome’s population plummeted from over 100,000 to a few thousand, and the destruction of the aqueducts ended Roman bath and villa culture.
2.  **Senatorial Decimation:** The senatorial elite, which had maintained Roman administrative traditions under Ostrogothic rule, was physically destroyed and fiscally ruined. The Senate ceased to exist as a functioning body by the end of the 6th century.
3.  **Fatal Separation:** By destroying the Ostrogothic kingdom—which acted as a political bridge between Roman civil traditions and barbarian military strength—the Byzantine reconquest created a weak, overtaxed administration unable to resist the **[[lombard-invasion-568|Lombards]]** in 568. The war ultimately accelerated the permanent division between East and West.
