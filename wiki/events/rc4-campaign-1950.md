# RC4 Campaign / Cao Bang Disaster (September–October 1950)

The RC4 (Route Coloniale 4) Campaign was the most devastating French defeat of the First Indochina War up to that point. In a series of actions centered on the isolated Cao Bang–Dong Khe–Lang Son corridor along the Chinese frontier, Viet Minh main-force divisions (with growing Chinese aid) annihilated two French columns and forced the abandonment of the entire northern border region.

## Narrative

French positions along the primitive one-lane dirt road RC4 (Cao Bang to Tien Yen) were vulnerable to constant ambushes. After the Chinese Communist victory, the Viet Minh gained access to modern weapons and advisers. In September 1950 Giap massed five battalions with artillery against Dong Khe; it fell after 52 hours of fighting (September 18).

A relief column (Le Page) and the Cao Bang garrison (Charton) were then ordered to withdraw southward. They were cut to pieces in a series of ambushes and blocking actions. Only about 600 men from the combined columns reached French lines. Total French Union losses: approximately 6,000 casualties, of whom 4,800 were listed dead or missing. Vast quantities of arms, ammunition, vehicles, and stores were abandoned (including at Lang Son, evacuated on higher orders without a fight).

By mid-October the French had been driven out of all of northern Tonkin from the sea to the Red River; the border with China was completely open.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** French decision to hold a string of isolated forts along a single vulnerable supply route after the 1949–50 shift in the regional balance (Chinese victory + Viet Minh re-equipment). Giap’s choice to accept a set-piece conventional battle for the first time on favorable terrain and with Chinese logistical support.

**contributed_to:** French command paralysis and intelligence failures (air reconnaissance defeated by camouflage and night movement).

**preceded_by:** Revers report (May 1949) recommending abandonment of most of Tonkin in favor of a defensible delta quadrilateral (ignored).

## Consequence Analysis

The disaster was “by far the most devastating defeat of the war to this point” and, in Bernard Fall’s estimation, “the greatest French colonial defeat since the loss of Montcalm at Quebec in 1759.” It opened the border, gave the Viet Minh a huge windfall of equipment, and produced a political shock in France that strengthened calls for negotiation while simultaneously prompting greater U.S. material commitment.

It demonstrated that the Viet Minh had transitioned from pure guerrilla warfare to the ability to mass and sustain division-level conventional operations.

## Actors

- Viet Minh: Vo Nguyen Giap (planning and command); Chinese advisers (CMAG) providing matériel and doctrine.
- French: Carpentier (overall); Charton and Le Page (column commanders).

## Historiography

Logevall treats the campaign as the moment the war was “internationalized” in a way that favored the Viet Minh. French accounts emphasize betrayal by higher command (Lang Son evacuation order) and the limits of airpower against disciplined infantry. Vietnamese histories present it as the first major conventional victory and the vindication of Giap’s shift in strategy. The exact death toll and the decision-making behind the Lang Son abandonment remain points of French controversy.
