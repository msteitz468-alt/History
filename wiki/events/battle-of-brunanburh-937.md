# Battle of Brunanburh (937)

The great battle in which **Æthelstan**, king of the English, defeated a coalition of
**Constantín son of Aed** ([[kingdom-of-alba|Alba]]), **Óláfr Guthfrithsson** (Hiberno-Norse
[[places/dublin|Dublin]]), and the Britons of Strathclyde — celebrated by a 73-line poem in
the [[places/england|Anglo-Saxon Chronicle]]. Its site remains unidentified.

## Narrative

In 937 Constantín allied with Óláfr against Æthelstan and invaded England. Æthelstan won; a
son of Constantín was killed. Yet the campaign ended in something closer to stalemate in
the longer run: Æthelstan's exceptional overlordship over Britain "was perhaps not attained
again by an English king until Edward I," but his power receded after his death in 939, and
Óláfr briefly recovered York.

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` the resistance of the northern and Hiberno-Norse powers to the
[[actors/kingdom-of-wessex|West-Saxon]] hegemony Æthelstan had asserted over the whole
island in the 930s.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` the high-water mark of tenth-century English imperial claim — but also a
demonstration of its fragility, since the coalition reformed and York changed hands again
within two years.

## Historiography

The battle is famous chiefly through the alliterative **Old English poem** embedded in the
Anglo-Saxon Chronicle — a West-Saxon panegyric that magnifies Æthelstan's triumph and must be
read as dynastic propaganda, not a battle report. The **site of Brunanburh remains
unidentified** despite long debate (Bromborough on the Wirral is a leading candidate),
leaving the campaign's geography conjectural. Modern scholarship (Insley, Woolf in
[[sources/stafford-companion-early-middle-ages-britain-ireland-2009|Stafford's *Companion*]])
stresses **contingency over destiny**: Æthelstan's island-wide overlordship "was perhaps not
attained again … until Edward I," yet it proved ephemeral, receding after his death in 939 —
a corrective to teleological readings that treat Brunanburh as a decisive step toward an
inevitable united England.
