# The Cleisthenic Reform of the Athenian State (508/7 BCE)

In **508/7 BCE** **[[cleisthenes|Cleisthenes]]** carried through the [[athens|Athenian]]
Assembly a sweeping reorganization of the state that **replaced the kinship basis of
politics with a territorial one** and gave the demos a decisive voice — the reform later
Athenians credited with founding their **democracy**. [[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]]
(Ch. 5, Martin Ostwald) gives the fullest analysis; its "genius lies in its being all of
one piece," each part integrated with every other.

## Narrative — the political crisis

After the [[pisistratidae|tyranny fell]] (510), dynastic rivalry erupted between Cleisthenes
(Alcmaeonid) and **Isagoras**, who won the archonship for 508/7. Cleisthenes responded by
"taking the people into partnership"; Isagoras called in **[[cleomenes-i|Cleomenes]] of
Sparta**, who expelled Cleisthenes and 700 cursed families and tried to install a board of
300. But **Council and people resisted**, besieged Cleomenes and Isagoras on the Acropolis,
and forced their withdrawal. Cleisthenes returned and the reform was enacted. A three-front
attack (Sparta, Boeotia, Chalcis) followed in **506**, but collapsed when the Corinthians
and the Spartan king Demaratus refused; the Athenians then **crushed Boeotians and
Chalcidians on a single day** and settled 4,000 cleruchs on Chalcidian land — a victory
Herodotus famously ascribed to the new energy of freedom (*isegoria*).

## The reform — structure

- **Demes.** The natural villages of Attica (≈139 of them) became the basic political and
  citizenship units. Membership was made **hereditary**; one registered in one's ancestor's
  deme regardless of later residence. This displaced the **phratry** (and the gené that
  controlled it) as the proof of citizenship, confining the old kinship bodies to religious
  matters.
- **Trittyes.** Attica was divided into three regions — **city (asty), inland (mesogeia),
  coast (paralia)** — each split into ten *trittyes* (30 in all). These cut deliberately
  across the old regional power-blocs; enclaves were engineered to **break up aristocratic
  cult-centres** (e.g. detaching Probalinthus from the Marathon Tetrapolis, weakening
  Isagoras' home shrine at Hecale).
- **Ten tribes.** Each new tribe combined **one trittys from each region**, so every tribe
  held "a cross-section of the whole of Attica." The trittyes were allotted to tribes **by
  lot** (probability of gross imbalance ~1/100), the tribes named for ten **eponymous heroes**
  chosen by Delphi. This "mixing" Aristotle regarded as Cleisthenes' greatest achievement.
- **Council of Five Hundred.** Each tribe sent **50 members**, each deme a fixed quota, to
  the new *boulé* — which prepared the agenda for the Assembly. Later refinements (the
  **prytany** system, the lot) post-date Cleisthenes.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the [[pisistratidae|fall of the tyranny]] and the renewed **dynastic rivalry**
of the gené, which Cleisthenes recognized as the structural cause of tyranny; his own
defeat by Isagoras, which drove him to the demos.
contributed_to: the reform's enactment was secured by the **popular rising** against
Cleomenes and Isagoras.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: a constitution that was "neither a democracy nor an aristocracy" but rested on
**[[isonomia]]** — no decision valid without the approval of Council and Assembly, in which
every citizen was an equal, even though only the wealthy held high office.
enabled: the institutional spine of **[[athenian-democracy|Athenian democracy]]** (the
deme–tribe–boulé structure lasted ~300 years); the army reorganization into ten tribal
regiments under ten elected **generals** (*str-* c. 501); the law of **[[ostracism]]**
(used from 488/7); and the surge of civic energy Herodotus credits for the **victory of
506**.

## Historiography

Built on **Herodotus** (V.66–78) and **Aristotle's Ath. Pol.** (which alone preserves the
constitutional detail), with the deme-topography reconstructed from inscriptions. Debate
turns on **Cleisthenes' motive** (factional self-interest vs. statesmanship), on whether
the whole package could really have passed in one archon-year (Ostwald: the outline passed,
details were worked out by a small committee afterward), and on how "democratic" the
*isonomia* of 507 actually was, given that office remained restricted by census class.
