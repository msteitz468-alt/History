# Battle of Kursk
## Narrative
In July 1943, the Germans launched their last major summer offensive on the Eastern Front (Operation Citadel), aiming to encircle Soviet forces in the strategic Kursk salient. Hitler massed some forty divisions, half of them armored or motorized (totaling half a million men and thousands of tanks). However, warned by intelligence, Soviet Marshal George Zhukov prepared deep defensive networks, mining the approaches and massing artillery and armor. The battle opened on July 5 and culminated in a massive clash of armored forces (most notably at Prokhorovka). The German drive was spent within ten days. The Red Army immediately launched a powerful counteroffensive, liberating Orel, Belgorod, and Kharkiv, and beginning the drive that would carry them to Berlin.

## Causal Analysis
- `caused_by:` **German delay in launching the offensive**, which allowed the Soviets months to prepare; **Soviet intelligence superiority** which pinpointed the exact date and target of the attack; and the **deep defensive lines** constructed by Zhukov, consisting of multiple belts of anti-tank minefields and dug-in artillery that successfully depleted German tank formations.

## Consequence Analysis
- `produced:` the **complete depletion of German armored reserves**, ending Germany's ability to launch large-scale offensives on the Eastern Front.
- `enabled:` the **Soviet liberation of Ukraine** and the rapid advance of the Red Army into Central and Eastern Europe (Marshal Konev crossed the Pruth into Romania in April 1944; Line 6295).
- `contributed_to:` the **moral collapse of Germany's satellite allies** (Romania, Hungary, Italy), who began seeking ways to exit the war.

## Actors
- **Joseph Stalin**, who approved the defensive strategy; **George Zhukov**, who designed the defense and counteroffensive; and German Field Marshal **Erich von Manstein**, who planned the German attack. See [[stalin-joseph]], zhukov-george.

## Historiography
Riasanovsky notes that Kursk demonstrated the qualitative and quantitative improvements of the Red Army, which out-fought the best German units in massed armor engagements (Line 6289). It remains celebrated as the largest tank battle in history.
