# Conquest of Astrakhan

## Narrative
The **Conquest of Astrakhan** occurred in two stages between 1554 and 1556, culminating in the annexation of the Astrakhan Khanate at the mouth of the Volga River by the Russian Tsardom under Ivan IV (the Terrible) (L2090).

Following the fall of Kazan in 1552, Russian forces advanced down the Volga River and seized Astrakhan for the first time in 1554, deposing the reigning khan and installing a vassal candidate, Yamgurchei, in his place (L2090). However, this vassal khan soon established clandestine contacts with the Crimean Khanate, violating his allegiance to Moscow (L2090). In response, Russian forces launched a second campaign, seizing Astrakhan once more in 1556 and formally annexing the territory directly into the Russian state (L2090).

## Causal Analysis
The conquest was caused by:
* **Securing the Volga trade:** Following the capture of the Middle Volga at Kazan, Astrakhan at the mouth of the river remained the final obstacle to total Russian control of the Volga commerce route to the Caspian Sea (L2090).
* **Ottoman and Crimean encirclement:** The Russian state sought to preempt Ottoman and Crimean influence in the region, as the Crimean Tartars, backed by their Ottoman suzerain, continuously raided the Russian borders and sought to establish vassal states along the Volga (L2090).

## Consequence Analysis
The conquest produced:
* **Geopolitical Consolidation:** Complete Russian control over the entire Volga River, securing the southern borders against encirclement (L2090).
* **Eastern Commerce:** Direct maritime access to the Caspian Sea, enabling the establishment of trade relations with Persia, Central Asia, and eventually India (L2090, L2512, L3594).
* **Steppe Encirclement:** The Crimean Khanate was isolated as the only remaining major Tartar threat to Russia, though it retained Ottoman support and launched retaliatory raids (including the burning of Moscow in 1571) (L2090, L2130-2132).
