# Degenerate Art Exhibition (Munich, 1937)

The regime's showcase condemnation of modernism: 650 confiscated works by 112
artists (Beckmann, Dix, Grosz, Kirchner, Kandinsky, Klee, Nolde among them),
opened 19 July 1937 and deliberately hung to repel — over two million visitors
by November 1937, making it, notoriously, the most-attended modern-art
exhibition in history. Its officially approved counterpart, the Great German
Art Exhibition, opened the day before in the new House of German Art.

## Narrative (Evans, 2005)

Goebbels — privately no enemy of modernism — organized the exhibition
cynically, to preempt Rosenberg and satisfy Hitler, whose hatred of the
"cubist-dadaist cult of primitivism" was personal and long-standing (his 1933
Nuremberg speech had assigned modernist artists to "medical care" or the
fraud courts; touring the exhibition he mused about sterilizing artists with
hereditarily "defective visual capacities"). The Reich Law for the
Confiscation of Degenerate Art (31 May 1938) legalized the seizure of ~5,000
paintings and ~12,000 works on paper from German museums; some were sold
abroad for currency, and on 20 March 1939 about 1,004 oil paintings and 3,825
works on paper were burned in the courtyard of Berlin's central fire station —
a book-burning for pictures.

## Historiography

The 1991 LACMA reconstruction and Peter-Klaus Schuster's work anchor the
literature; Evans uses the episode as the clearest case of Hitler's personal
intervention shaping cultural policy (contrast his indifference to film and
radio, left to Goebbels).
