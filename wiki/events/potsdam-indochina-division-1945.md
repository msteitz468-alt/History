# Potsdam Division of Indochina (16th Parallel, 1945)

At the Potsdam Conference the Allies resolved the command dispute over Indochina by dividing the country at the 16th parallel for the purposes of disarming Japanese forces and maintaining order. This temporary military division had profound long-term political consequences.

## Narrative

The theater boundary question (Mountbatten’s SEAC vs. Wedemeyer’s China theater) was settled so that SEAC would handle Indochina south of the 16th parallel and China would be responsible north of it. The United States also agreed to allow French participation in surrender ceremonies throughout Indochina (including the Chinese-controlled north) and to involve French forces in the Far East to speed the Japanese surrender.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** Practical Allied need to use available troops (British/Indian in the south, Chinese in the north) rather than waiting for French forces that were not yet in position.

**contributed_to:** Different occupation experiences north and south that shaped the immediate post-surrender power struggles.

## Consequence Analysis

The division facilitated the British return to the south (and Gracey’s pro-French actions) while the Chinese occupation in the north, though exploitative and chaotic, gave the Viet Minh more space and time to consolidate than they had in the south. It also created an early precedent for thinking of Vietnam in terms of north/south divisions.

## Actors

- Allies: Mountbatten, Wedemeyer, the Big Three at Potsdam.
- French: Leclerc (positioned to benefit from the arrangements).
- Chinese: Lu Han (occupying force in the north).

## Historiography

Logevall presents the Potsdam decision as a pragmatic wartime measure with enormous unintended political consequences for the future of Indochina. It is often cited as one of the early steps in the “internationalization” of what had previously been a Franco-Vietnamese colonial question.
