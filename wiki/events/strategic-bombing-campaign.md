# The Strategic Bombing Campaign (1940–1945)

The **strategic bombing campaign** of the Second World War was the sustained Allied (and earlier
Axis) effort to win the war from the air by attacking the enemy's **war economy and civilian
morale** behind the front — the fullest expression of [[total-war]]'s erasure of the line between
combatant and civilian. From the [[cambridge-history-second-world-war-v1|Cambridge History of the
Second World War]] (Vol. I, Anglo-American strategic bombing; Vol. III on its economic effects and
the destruction of cities).

## Narrative

Pre-war air-power theory ([[douhet-giulio|Douhet]], Trenchard, [[mitchell-william|Mitchell]], the
US "industrial web" school) held that fleets of bombers could be decisive by themselves — the
doctrine of [[strategic-bombardment]] and [[command-of-the-air]] (see the
[[hubs/warfare/naval-and-air-power-theory|Naval & Air Power Theory]] sub-hub). After the German *Blitz* on British cities (1940–41) and
the discovery that bombers could not hit precise targets by night, **RAF Bomber Command** turned to
**area bombing** of German cities (the dehousing of workers and the breaking of morale), while the
**US Army Air Forces** pursued daylight "precision" bombing of key industries (oil, ball-bearings,
aircraft, transport). The combined offensive grew enormously from 1943 — Hamburg (the 1943
firestorm), the Ruhr, Berlin, and **Dresden** (February 1945) — and in the Pacific culminated in
the **firebombing of Japanese cities** (Tokyo, March 1945) and the **atomic bombings of Hiroshima
and Nagasaki** (August 1945).

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the inter-war doctrine that **air power could win wars independently** by hitting the
  enemy's economy and will.
- `contributed_to:` the **technical inaccuracy** of early bombing, which drove the RAF from
  precision to area (city) attacks; and the [[total-war]] logic that made the enemy's whole society
  a legitimate target.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` immense **civilian death and urban destruction** — roughly 600,000 German and several
  hundred thousand Japanese civilians killed, and many cities largely destroyed.
- `produced:` by 1944, **severe disruption of the German war economy** — the oil and transport
  campaigns, and the diversion of huge German resources to air defence (flak, fighters, the "second
  front" in the air). Adam Tooze and the official surveys argue bombing's decisive economic effect
  came **late but was real**, capping German production at the moment of crisis (see
  [[war-economy]]).
- `produced:` the **atomic age** (Hiroshima/Nagasaki) and a **lasting moral controversy** over the
  deliberate killing of civilians.

## Actors

**RAF Bomber Command** (Arthur Harris) and the **US Army Air Forces** against the cities and
industries of **Germany** and **Japan**; the **civilian populations** and war-workers who bore the
cost. See [[second-world-war-1939]].

## Historiography

The campaign is among the most debated aspects of the war. Disputes turn on **effectiveness** (did
it justify the vast resources it consumed? — sceptics vs. those, like Tooze, who stress its real
late impact on oil, transport, and air defence) and **morality** (the deliberate targeting of
civilians in area bombing, Dresden, and the atomic bombings). The [[cambridge-history-second-world-war-v1|CHSWW]]
treats it both as a military instrument (Vol. I) and as part of the war's catastrophic destruction
of urban society and its [[war-memory-and-mourning|memory]] (Vol. III).

## From Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008) — the bombing of Germany

**Doctrine and escalation.** Harris took Bomber Command in early 1942 with 69 heavy bombers and area-bombing doctrine (night precision being impossible); Lübeck (28–29 March 1942) was the demonstration, Cologne the first thousand-bomber raid (30 May 1942), and Casablanca (January 1943) formalized the Combined Bomber Offensive: "the progressive destruction and dislocation of the German military, industrial and economic system, and the undermining of the morale of the German people." The Ruhr campaign (spring 1943, ~15,000 dead) stopped armaments growth cold — steel down 200,000 tons in a quarter, Schweinfurt cutting ball-bearings 38%, Speer warning Hitler production could halt.

**Hamburg, Operation Gomorrah (24 July – 3 August 1943).** The first "Window" (chaff) operation; the firestorm of 27–28 July reached 800°C with hurricane-force winds. Evans preserves the survivor testimony at full strength: the fifteen-year-old Traute Koch led through the flames in wet sheets; the nineteen-year-old milliner — "There were people on the roadway, some already dead, some still lying alive but stuck in the asphalt . . . They were on their hands and knees screaming"; corpses "black and shrivelled; some were lying in a mess of coagulated human body fat." ~40,000 dead, 900,000 homeless, 56% of dwellings destroyed. The civic order broke — Party functionaries fled, badges were torn off ("Let's get that murderer," of Göring) — yet industrial output was back to 80% within five months.

**Attrition and victory.** The air Battle of Berlin (November 1943 – March 1944) cost the RAF 3,300+ aircrew; Schweinfurt (14 October 1943) cost 60 of 220 B-17s; Nuremberg (30 March 1944) 95 of 795. The Merlin-engined, drop-tanked P-51 Mustang reversed the exchange from December 1943 — German fighter losses hit ~50% a month, and by June 1944 the Luftwaffe was effectively beaten, making [[d-day-1944|Overlord]] possible ("Had the German air force retained command of the skies, the invasion could not have taken place"). Of 1.42 million tons of bombs dropped on Germany, 1.18 million fell in the final year; by January 1945 bombing had cost the Reich a quarter of its planned tanks and nearly a third of its aircraft, and transport attacks had halved rail traffic.

**Cost and morality.** 400,000–500,000 German civilian dead (~10% of them foreign workers and POWs); ~40% of urban housing destroyed; ~80,000 Allied airmen killed (against 60,000 British civilians killed by German bombing). From 1944 German crowds, incited by Goebbels ("not to be protected from the people's anger"), lynched at least 350 downed Allied airmen; the Gestapo shot 50 recaptured Great Escape officers on Himmler's order. Evans's own assessment — the dead "were not mere 'collateral damage'"; morale-breaking and revenge were explicit aims, customarily war crimes, and the final year was "too indiscriminate to be justifiable," though the campaign shortened the war — is recorded with the debate at [[allied-strategic-bombing-morality]]. Postwar surveys: 91% of Germans called bombing the war's hardest burden; Evans judges it did "even more than the defeats at Stalingrad and in North Africa to spread popular disillusion about the Nazi Party." Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## 1945 and Dresden — Evans (2008), Part 7

The final winter brought the heaviest tonnage: Magdeburg (16 January, ~4,000 dead), Pforzheim (23–24 February — a 22-minute raid killing up to 17,000 of 79,000 inhabitants), Berlin's biggest raid (3 March), Swinemünde (12 March, ~5,000 refugees killed, at Soviet request), Würzburg (16–17 March), Potsdam (14–15 April, the last major British night raid). **Dresden, 13–15 February 1945:** with its flak withdrawn east and fighters grounded for fuel, two RAF waves and two US daylight raids ignited a firestorm that destroyed the city center; Evans gives **~35,000 dead** (a figure historically inflated by Nazi and later propaganda to 250,000+ — the modern Dresden Historians' Commission confirmed ~25,000; treat precise numbers as disputed). The raid's grim paradox: that same morning Victor Klemperer had been ordered to help deliver deportation notices to Dresden's last Jews, himself slated for the next list; in the firestorm ("Something hard and glowing hot struck the right side of my face . . . I felt for my eye. It was still there") the deportation lists burned, Eva Klemperer cut the yellow star from his coat with a pocket knife, and the Klemperers went underground and survived — a direct case of an Allied raid saving Holocaust victims. Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]
