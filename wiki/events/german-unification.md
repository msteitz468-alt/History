# The Unification of Germany

The **unification of Germany** was the process (1862–71) by which **Prussia**, under
**Otto von Bismarck**, united the German states (excluding Austria) into the **German Empire**
— achieved not by the liberal nationalism of [[revolutions-of-1848|1848]] but by Prussian
power and *Realpolitik* ("blood and iron"). From [[new-cambridge-modern-history-v10|*The New
Cambridge Modern History*, Vol. X]].

## Narrative

The Frankfurt Parliament's failure in [[revolutions-of-1848|1848–49]] discredited unification
"from below"; thereafter the question was whether Germany would be unified under **Prussia**
(*kleindeutsch*, excluding Austria) or **Austria** (*großdeutsch*). NCMH X traces how Prussian
statesmen sought to "assert Prussian leadership and win support from moderate liberal" national
feeling. **Bismarck**, Minister-President from **1862**, resolved the army-budget constitutional
crisis by governing without parliament and pursued unification through three wars:

- the **Danish War (1864)** over Schleswig-Holstein (Prussia and Austria vs. Denmark);
- the **Austro-Prussian War (1866)** — the decisive victory at **Königgrätz/Sadowa** expelled
  Austria from Germany and created the **North German Confederation** under Prussia;
- the **Franco-Prussian War (1870–71)** — the defeat of **Napoleon III** (Sedan) rallied the
  southern German states to Prussia.

The **German Empire** was proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors at **Versailles (18 January
1871)**, with William I as Kaiser and Bismarck as Chancellor.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` **German nationalism** and the failure of liberal unification in
  [[revolutions-of-1848|1848]] — leaving the field to **Prussian power**.
- `caused_by:` **Bismarck's *Realpolitik*** and the **Austro-Prussian rivalry** for leadership
  of Germany, backed by Prussian military reform (Moltke, the General Staff).

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **German Empire (1871)** — a powerful, industrializing new state at the
  centre of Europe — and the **eclipse of Austria** (pushed toward the Dual Monarchy) and of
  **France** (the loss of Alsace-Lorraine, a lasting grievance).
- `produced:` a fundamental shift in the **[[balance-of-power|balance of power]]**: a united
  Germany that would dominate continental politics and whose rise is central to the origins of
  the [[first-world-war-1914|First World War]].
- Entrenched a German polity combining constitutional forms with Prussian military-monarchical
  dominance.

## Actors

- **Prussia** (Bismarck, William I, Moltke) vs. **Austria**, **Denmark**, and **France**
  (Napoleon III); the **German states** drawn in; the **liberal nationalists** co-opted. See
  [[nationalism]], [[balance-of-power]].

## Historiography

The classic debate is whether unification was the triumph of **Bismarck's genius/Realpolitik**
or of deeper forces (Prussian economic and military weight, the *Zollverein*, nationalism), and
the "Sonderweg" question of whether 1871's authoritarian-militarist compromise set Germany on a
"special path" toward 1914 and 1933.
