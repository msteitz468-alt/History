# Trajan's Parthian War (114–117 CE)

The last and most ambitious of **[[trajan|Trajan's]]** wars of conquest — an invasion of the
**Parthian Empire** that briefly carried Roman arms to the Persian Gulf and created new eastern
provinces, but over-reached the empire's capacity and collapsed amid a great revolt. Treated in
[[bowman-cah-v11-2000|CAH XI]] Ch. 2 (and Ch. 22 on Judaea).

## Narrative

Exploiting the perennial dispute over **Armenia** (where Parthia had installed a king without
Roman sanction), Trajan invaded in **114**, annexing **Armenia**, then drove down through
**Mesopotamia (115–116)**, taking the Parthian capital **Ctesiphon** and reaching the **Persian
Gulf** — where, Dio reports, he gazed at a ship bound for India and lamented that he was too old
to follow **Alexander**. His coins proclaimed *Armenia et Mesopotamia in potestatem populi Romani
redactae*; new provinces of **Armenia, Mesopotamia, and Assyria** were declared.

## Collapse

The conquests proved **unsustainable**. As Trajan pushed south, a vast revolt erupted **in his
rear**: the **Jewish diaspora revolt of 115–117** convulsed **Cyrenaica, Egypt, Cyprus, and
Mesopotamia** with great violence and loss of life, while the newly conquered Mesopotamian
peoples rose behind the advancing army. Trajan could not hold the new provinces; he installed a
client king at Ctesiphon and withdrew, dying on the return journey at **Selinus in Cilicia
(117)**.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **brief annexation** of Armenia, Mesopotamia, and Assyria — the empire at its
  absolute **greatest extent**.
- `caused_by` / `concurrent_with:` the **Jewish diaspora revolt (115–117)** (see
  [[roman-judaea]]) — a catastrophe for the diaspora communities of Cyrene, Egypt, and Cyprus.
- `produced:` the recognition that the East had been **over-reached**: Trajan's successor
  **[[hadrian]]** at once **abandoned the trans-Euphrates conquests**, restoring the
  Euphrates frontier and the client-king settlement in Armenia — a decisive turn from Trajanic
  expansion to **consolidation**.

## Significance

The Parthian War marked both the **apogee and the limit** of Roman expansion. It demonstrated
that the empire could conquer the Parthian heartland but **could not hold it**, and Hadrian's
immediate reversal established the principle of **fixed, defensible frontiers** that governed the
High Empire (see [[augustan-imperial-expansion|the frontier debate]]). The accompanying diaspora
revolt devastated and transformed the western Jewish communities, a prelude to the Bar Kochba War.
