# Battle of Vinh Yen (January 1951)

The Battle of Vinh Yen was the opening engagement of Giap’s 1951 Red River Delta offensives. It marked the first time the Viet Minh committed large regular formations in a bid to seize the delta and was the first major test of de Lattre’s new command and the effectiveness of French (US-supplied) napalm against massed attacks.

## Narrative

After the RC4 victory, Giap launched a major offensive aimed at the French delta defenses. At Vinh Yen in mid-January 1951, Viet Minh forces (notably the 312th Division) assaulted in strength. French groupes mobiles, supported by artillery and aircraft, stood their ground. When Viet Minh troops massed for final assaults, French pilots dropped napalm in large quantities, “literally roasting thousands.” A last mass attack by the 312th was stopped by a curtain of fire.

Viet Minh losses were catastrophic (French claimed 6,000 dead and 8,000 wounded). Giap broke off the offensive.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by:** Giap’s belief (post-RC4 and with Chinese aid) that the time had come for the “equilibrium” or early “general offensive” phase in the populated delta. De Lattre’s determination to demonstrate that the French could and would hold the key economic and political heartland.

**contributed_to:** Surprise effectiveness of napalm against infantry in the open; superior French mobility and fire support in the delta terrain.

## Consequence Analysis

The battle was a tactical and operational victory for the French and a major morale boost after the humiliations of 1950. It convinced Giap that a direct conventional thrust into the delta was not yet viable; he shifted back to guerrilla and sapper tactics while preparing for future set-piece opportunities (ultimately Dien Bien Phu).

It also marked the first large-scale introduction of US napalm into the war and highlighted growing American material involvement.

## Actors

- Viet Minh: Vo Nguyen Giap (overall command).
- French: Jean de Lattre de Tassigny (strategic direction); various groupes mobiles and air units.

## Historiography

Logevall presents Vinh Yen as the moment de Lattre showed that French forces, properly led and supported, could still inflict shattering defeats on Viet Minh regulars. Vietnamese accounts emphasize the learning experience and the cost of premature conventional operations. The battle is less well known in the West than Dien Bien Phu but was critical in the 1950–51 campaign season.
