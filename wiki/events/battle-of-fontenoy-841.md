# Battle of Fontenoy (25 June 841)

## Narrative

At **Fontenoy-en-Puisaye** in Burgundy on 25 June 841, the sons and grandson of
[[louis-the-pious]] fought the decisive battle of the Carolingian civil war of 840–843.
**[[lothar-i|Lothar I]]**, asserting a unitary imperial claim over the whole empire on the
817 model, allied with his nephew **Pippin II of Aquitaine**; against them stood his brothers
**[[charles-the-bald|Charles the Bald]]** and **[[louis-the-german|Louis the German]]**, who
had bound themselves to remain united. Charles and Louis won, but at heavy cost, and peace
was slow to come.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the death of [[louis-the-pious]] (840), which "abruptly reopened the whole
  question of the empire's future"
- `caused_by:` [[lothar-i|Lothar's]] attempt to make good a unitary claim on the whole Frankish
  heartlands, breaking the *iura naturae* (the natural rights of his brothers, in the annalists' phrase)
- `contributed_to:` the unresolved structural tension between the 817 **Ordinatio imperii** (imperial
  unity) and Frankish **partible inheritance**

**Battle as judgment of God.** Whitman
([[whitman-verdict-of-battle-2012|The Verdict of Battle]], 2012) cites Fontenoy
as the earliest leading medieval example of the *arranged* battle functioning as
trial by combat: the brothers deliberately staged the encounter "so that their
case could be examined by the judgment of God," and its verdict established the
lines of succession to Charlemagne's inheritance — the paradigm of
[[verdict-of-battle|battle as legal proceeding]] alongside Hastings, Mühldorf
(1322), and Bosworth (1485). Not to be confused with
[[battle-of-fontenoy-1745|the 1745 Fontenoy]] in the Austrian Netherlands.

## Consequence Analysis

The victory did not settle matters immediately, but it discredited the unitary model.

- `produced:` the **Strasbourg Oaths** (February 842), in which Charles and Louis — and their men —
  swore mutual fidelity against Lothar; Nithard records the oaths in early **Romance** and **Old High
  German**, a landmark for the history of the two vernaculars
- `produced:` the [[treaty-of-verdun-843]], which abandoned the unitary "imperial" model of 817 and
  divided the Frankish heartlands three ways
- `enabled:` the long divergence of West Francia (France) and East Francia (Germany)

## Historiography

Fontenoy and its aftermath (840–843) are "the best documented of any in the early middle ages" (NCMH II,
Nelson, Ch. 4). The fullest account is by **Nithard**, a participant-observer and himself an illegitimate
grandson of Charlemagne, attached to Charles the Bald's camp — vivid but partisan. Nithard says less about
the fighting than about the efforts to avert it and the post-war reconciliation, revealing "the mechanics
of Carolingian diplomacy": the interplay of *affinity* (kinship and clientship) and *congruence* (a shared
sense of what was fair), and the existence of a class of lesser aristocrats whose loyalty kings had to win.
The battle is the empirical anchor of NCMH II's argument that aristocratic interest, not dynastic accident,
drove the empire's division.
