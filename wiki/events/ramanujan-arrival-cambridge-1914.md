# Ramanujan’s Arrival in Cambridge (April 1914)

Ramanujan arrived in England in April 1914 after the long sea voyage. He was met by the Cambridge world — cold, formal, at war. He stayed initially with the Nevilles, then moved into rooms at Trinity. The first meetings with Hardy and Littlewood began the intense period of joint work that would define his international reputation. The contrast with the temple town of Kumbakonam could hardly have been more complete.

## Historiography
The episode is documented mainly through Hardy's own recollections, Ramanujan's surviving letters, and later biographical reconstruction (notably Kanigel's *The Man Who Knew Infinity*, 1991), all written or compiled well after the fact and shaped by the participants' and biographers' interest in the "genius discovered" narrative. Less is directly recorded from Ramanujan's own perspective on his cultural dislocation than from Hardy's and the Nevilles' accounts, a source imbalance later historians have noted when assessing how the "English chill" and adjustment difficulties have been characterized.
