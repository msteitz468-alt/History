# The Battle of Berlin (April–May 1945)

The final Soviet offensive of the European war: some 2.5 million Red Army troops stormed the Oder line and encircled Berlin, fighting street by street against a scratch defense of broken divisions, Volkssturm militia, and Hitler Youth while [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]] directed phantom armies from the Reich Chancellery bunker before killing himself on 30 April. The city surrendered on 2 May; the Reich followed within a week. Documented for the wiki from [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)]].

## Narrative

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: Hitler abandoned Rastenburg for the Reich Chancellery on 20 November 1944; the Soviet pause at the German border (supply and rail-gauge friction) bought time for the failed [[ardennes-offensive-1944|Ardennes gamble]]. By mid-April 1945, 2.5 million Soviet troops stood ready; the assault opened on Hitler's 56th birthday (20 April — his last filmed appearance, decorating Hitler Youth boys none older than fourteen in the Chancellery garden). Zhukov and Konev closed the ring on 25 April — the day American and Soviet troops shook hands at Torgau on the Elbe — and the battle "degenerated into bitter, uncoordinated street fighting" that Evans compares explicitly to Stalingrad; more tanks were lost per day in Berlin than even at [[battle-of-kursk-1943|Kursk]]. Heinrici, commanding Army Group Vistula, resigned on 29 April rather than keep executing "increasingly meaningless commands"; Soviet troops entered the government quarter the same day.

In the bunker: Hitler's 22 April breakdown ("everyone had betrayed him, even the SS"); the "betrayals" of Göring's telegram and Himmler's Bernadotte feelers (Fegelein shot in reprisal); marriage to Eva Braun in the early hours of 29 April; the Political Testament; suicide at ~3:40 p.m. on **30 April 1945** (pistol; Braun by cyanide), the bodies burned in the Chancellery garden and later identified by Soviet investigators only through dental work. Goebbels and his wife murdered their six children on 1 May before their own suicides. The city commandant surrendered on **2 May**; Dönitz's rump government signed unconditional surrender at Reims (7 May) and Karlshorst (8–9 May). The civilian catastrophe — mass rape by the Red Army (Berlin's two largest hospitals estimated at least 100,000 victims in the capital), flight and expulsion — is treated at [[expulsion-of-germans-1945]].

## Historiography

Evans reads the final defense — Volkssturm, Hitler Youth, flying courts-martial hanging "defeatists," Himmler's order that "where a white flag appears, all the male persons of the house concerned are to be shot" — as the regime turning its annihilationist logic inward on the German population itself.
