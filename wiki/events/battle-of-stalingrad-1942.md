# Battle of Stalingrad
## Narrative
Following their defeat at Moscow, the Germans launched a major summer offensive in 1942 targeting the southern USSR to capture the oil fields of the Caucasus and cut off the Volga shipping route. In late August, the German Sixth Army reached Stalingrad, an industrial city of half a million people stretched along the Volga River. Under General Vasily Chuikov, the Soviet 62nd Army fought a brutal war of attrition for every house and street, reducing the city to rubble. Despite heavy casualties and German breakthroughs, the Soviets held. In November 1942, Soviet Marshal George Zhukov launched a massive counteroffensive (Operation Uranus) that encircled the German forces. Disregarding his generals, Hitler refused to permit a retreat. On February 2, 1943, German Field Marshal Friedrich Paulus and his remaining 120,000 troops surrendered, marking the end of the battle.

## Causal Analysis
- `caused_by:` **Hitler's strategic errors** (assumed personal command, divided his forces between the Caucasus and Stalingrad, and forbade Paulus to break out of the encirclement; Line 6287); **General Chuikov's defense tactics** (clinging to the German positions to minimize the effectiveness of Luftwaffe bombings); and **Marshal Zhukov's flanking counteroffensive** which targeted weak Romanian and Hungarian divisions guarding the German flanks.

## Consequence Analysis
- `produced:` the **annihilation and capture of the German Sixth Army**, resulting in over 200,000 German casualties and the surrender of 120,000 troops.
- `produced:` the **collapse of the German Caucasus offensive**, forcing a hasty withdrawal of German forces from Novorossiisk and Mozdok to avoid encirclement.
- `enabled:` the **Soviet Union to seize the strategic initiative** on the Eastern Front, ending any prospect of a German victory in Russia.
- `concurrent_with:` British victory at El Alamein in Egypt and the Allied landings in North Africa (Operation Torch), marking the general turning point of World War II (Line 6293).

## Actors
- **Joseph Stalin**, who ordered "Not a Step Back!"; **George Zhukov**, who designed the counteroffensive; **Vasily Chuikov**, who defended the city; and German Field Marshal **Friedrich Paulus**. See [[stalin-joseph]], zhukov-george.

## Historiography
Riasanovsky highlights that Stalingrad was a battle of extreme contrasts, showcasing both the brutal urban combat and the sophisticated Soviet strategic planning (Line 6287). It remains widely recognized as the single most important turning point of the war in Europe.
