# Battle of Stalingrad
## Narrative
Following their defeat at Moscow, the Germans launched a major summer offensive in 1942 targeting the southern USSR to capture the oil fields of the Caucasus and cut off the Volga shipping route. 

**Soviet context and preconditions (Glantz/House Ch. 2):** At end of winter 1941–42, Stalin overestimated German exhaustion and underestimated rebuild time, just as Hitler did for Soviets. Red Army "literally reinventing itself" via new tank corps (from 31 Mar 1942: 28 by Dec) and tank armies (May/Jul), backed by 4,500 tanks produced Jan–May. But rifle divisions remained weak (~7k effectives); new mobile units "too weak to conduct sustained operations" with inexperienced leaders. Strategic debate: Stalin and Timoshenko fixated on Moscow threat, planned dispersed local offensives including the "unnecessary gamble" Khar’kov offensive (10 Apr plan: two-pronged convergence by SW Front's 21st/28th/38th/6th Armies + new 21st/23rd Tank Corps and cavalry groups from Staryi Saltov and Barvenkovo salients). Despite reports of rifle divs at "51.2 percent of authorized rifles and less than 24 percent of machine guns," the plan proceeded over Shaposhnikov/Vasilevsky objections. "the limited offensives of May 1942 merely dissipated Soviet assets that would have been better used to stop the coming German offensive." 1 May 1942 OOB: SW Front (Timoshenko) and Southern Front (Malinovsky) with specific armies and commanders listed; overall Soviet strength ~11M but high turnover and leadership gaps (tank corps cmdrs far less experienced than German counterparts). These set conditions for the German southern success in Blau.

**Approach to the "gates" — Great Bend of the Don, 23–31 July 1942 (Ch. 6):** After Blau II and the AG split (A to Caucasus, B toward Stalingrad per Directive 45), Paulus’s reinforced Sixth Army (VIII AC, LI AC, XIV Pz Wietersheim with 3rd/60th Mot + 16th Pz; later XXIV Pz 24th Pz) advanced into the Great Bend of the Don (22–25 July) with double-pincer plan to encircle 62nd (Kolpakchi) and 64th (Chuikov) Armies west of the Don and seize Kalach/Trekhostrovskaia crossings for coup de main on Stalingrad. Terrain (steep west banks, steppe, balkas) canalized but favored west-east attack. OOB (22 Jul Table 16): Axis ~290 tanks vs Stalingrad Front (Gordov, ~23 Jul) 1,239 (1st TA Moskalenko ~350 incl. 13th/28th TC; 4th TA Kriuchenkin ~200 incl. 22nd TC; 62nd 320 + 64th 55 + 23rd TC 178 + front). Fuel shortages slowed (AG A priority); kampfgruppen advanced rapidly. By 24 Jul loose cordon encircled Group Zhuravlev (parts 62nd) Maiorovskii; 64th to Surovikino. 

Stalingrad Front counterstroke (Gordov 2000 hrs 26 Jul): 1st/4th TA + 21st/62nd/64th + 8th Air to destroy enemy Verkhne-Buzinovka/restore Don by 30 Jul. "Powerful and menacing on paper" (>450 tanks) but "tank armies were not ready for coordinated combat operations... Only six and one half hours... allocated"; poor C2/comms. 1st TA 27 Jul (13th TC wedges vs 16th Pz "armada of 200 tanks"; "tanks... fighting on the sandy seas of the steppe"); 4th TA delayed/fragmented (22nd TC no recon/infantry support). Luftwaffe 1,000+ sorties 27 Jul. By late 30 Jul aborted; "well more than half of their 1,239 tanks" lost (majority mechanical; XIV Pz alone 482 destroyed; e.g. 13th 86/152, 28th >2/3 178, 22nd ~80% 180). Stalin: "twelve tank brigades lost 326 out of 400 tanks, of which about 200 were lost to mechanical problems... covert sabotage and wrecking". Paulus northern pincer to Don NW Kalach but failed full encirclement; southern small Chir bridgehead. "Paulus’s attempt to encircle all Soviet forces west of the Don had failed; with that, Hitler’s ambitious hopes to seize Stalingrad by a coup de main from the march were also dashed." "Gordov’s four armies... fought Paulus’s spearheads to a standstill" at tremendous cost; front retained ~500 tanks + new arrivals 1 Aug. Concurrent Voronezh/Orel pinning (Briansk/Voronezh 17–26 Jul; Liziukov killed leading KV 23 Jul) tied German reserves (9th Pz etc.) but did not divert main. 

**Approach endgame 1–19 Aug 1942 (Ch. 7):** Hitler 30-31 Jul reallocated 4th Pz (Hoth: XXXXVIII Kempf 14 Pz Heim +29 Mot Fremerey ~150 tanks + Rom VI + IV AC) from AG A to AG B "to turn northeast and attack toward Stalingrad, converging with Sixth Army". 6th (Paulus) reinforced XI AC + Italian Eighth relief on Don (free 384/389/76/295 etc. east); ~330 tanks; pause 1-6 Aug fuel. 4th Pz 1 Aug from Tsimlianskaia: split 51st Army; Kotel'nikovo 2 Aug, Aksai 4 Aug, Abganerovo/Plodovitoe 5 Aug (14 Pz). Chuikov Southern Gp halted left wing 12 days on Aksai; 9-11 Aug Shumilov counter (38/157/204 RD +13 TC 68 tanks +133 TB 40 KV) pushed back 10 km N Abganerovo (4th Pz "broken"; stabilized 12 Aug). 6th to Kalach 7 Aug (16 Pz NW +24 Pz S link-up; XI/LI/XXIV compress); 8-11 mop-up (claims 8 RD +270 tanks/35k+ POW but ~half escaped). OKW bulletin "75,000 men... 1,000 tanks". Still faced 4th Tank bridgehead. 

Organizing defenses: 4 Aug Stavka split fronts (Stalingrad Gordov protect NW/W; Southeastern Eremenko halt S at all cost + prevent Volga penetration S of city); 9 Aug unified under Eremenko + troika. Detailed missions (62nd/4th Tank Don bank; 64th Abganerovo-Tinguta external; 57th Raigorod; 1G 2nd belt Ilovlia; 51st lakes Sarpa). Minefields/reserves. But "fatally flawed" (1G late; encircled weak; time denied); "logistical shoestring" (62nd 0.28 rifle loads; 265 vehicles; rail interdiction).

NE corner 15-19 Aug: Paulus 10 Aug plan 11 divs XIV/XXIV/XI/VIII/LI vs 4th Tank Kletskaia-Bolshenabatovskii (diversion 13 Aug XI; 15 Aug main 60/3 Mot +16 Pz smash center to Don S Sirotinskaia/Trekhostrovskaia; overran HQ; split army). Gordov 17 Aug counter (1G +62/21) failed. 4th Tank "no longer combat capable" 17 Aug (7 tanks left); Paulus mopped, expanded bridgeheads 18-19, refit panzers. "with Sixth Army’s forces finally lodged firmly on the western bank of the Don, Paulus could plan for the next stage—the decisive final drive to the Volga and Stalingrad."

Ch7 conclusions: >1 mo +4 separate offensives (not single bound); Soviets resilient (5 armies still faced); slow rates (1.5-1.75 km/day Aug); attrition (6th/4th Pz >50% down; 14 Pz 24 tanks post-Abganerovo); Great Bend "far more significant... seriously weakened Sixth Army" pre-city + Soviet strategic counteroffensive effort. 

**NOT A STEP BACK! (Order 227, 28 July):** "dated 28 July 1942, drafted by Vasilevsky, and extensively rewritten by Stalin. Although its official title was People’s Commissariat of Defense Order No. 227, the decree became infamous by its title—Ni Shagu Nazad! or “Not a Step Back!”" Verbatim: "The enemy is throwing new forces forward... " "some foolish people at the front are consoling themselves with discussions that we can retreat farther to the east... such talk is spurious and false..." "We must radically nip in the bud the talk... We must stubbornly defend every position and every meter of Soviet territory to the last drop of our blood..." "We are short of order and discipline... This is now our main shortcoming. We must institute the strictest of order and iron discipline..." "panic-mongers and cowards must be exterminated on the spot." Retreat w/o orders = "enemies of the Homeland." Fronts 1–3 penal battalions (800 men each, officers guilty cowardice); armies 5–10 penal companies (150–200); blocking "to shoot panic-mongers and cowards on the spot". "Order No. 227 became the ultimate expression of fear as a motivational tool." (lines 1263–1337, Ch. 6) Reinforced local fortification of Stalingrad defenses.

In late August, the (already weakened by overextension, counterstrokes, and incomplete rebuild from Blau I) German Sixth Army reached Stalingrad. Per Paulus 19 Aug order: occupy isthmus N of Kalach-Stalingrad rail and protect flanks. XIV Pz Corps (16th Pz +3rd/60th Mot) main dash from new Vertiachii bridgehead (LI Corps crossed 21 Aug, 74 dead/351 wounded) to Volga N of city 23 Aug. "At 4.30 hours the tanks of KG Sieckenius broke out... After a tough fight, 16th Panzer Division overcame the Tartar Ditch and crossed the Frolov-Stalingrad railroad south of Kotluban." ~1500 hrs reached suburbs; first tanks on Volga high bank 1835 hrs ("Pride, happiness, and astonishment glowed on the faces of the men"). 16th Pz in hedgehog Rynok–Spartanovka; "16th Panzer Division will hold its positions in all circumstances" (Hitler). But outran infantry; narrow corridor; heavy losses (up to 500/day); Wietersheim/Hube requested pullback but denied.

Eremenko counterstrokes (Groups Kovalenko/Shtevnev ~350 tanks 23–29 Aug from Kotluban/Orlovka) smashed some hedgehogs but failed to close (hasty prep, wide fronts, piecemeal, Stuka pounded; 4th TC to 23 tanks). 62nd Army (Lopatin) struggled on corridor S flank/Don left; LI Corps pressed it toward Rossoshka but 71st alone insufficient for full encirclement. 4th Pz Army (Hoth) advanced N in "spurts" from Abganerovo/Tinguta (20–29 Aug); breakthrough 29 Aug vs 126th RD to Basargino (24th Pz 41 tanks 31 Aug); linked 71st Inf + Romanian 20th at Voroponovo 2 Sep. "the Germans had no choice but to contemplate a major struggle to capture the city of Stalingrad. In this respect... the counterstrokes... ultimately reaped major benefits."

City: Defense Committee (10th NKVD Saraev + worker battalions + militias) + late-evacuated civilians; Luftwaffe "most concentrated aerial bombardment" from 23 Aug (firebombs, oil tanks, round-the-clock). Sideshows: Serafimovich (63rd/21st +3rd Gds Cav) 50x25 km Don bridgehead 20–28 Aug (1,200 mostly Italian POW); Kletskaia (1st Guards) 35x30 km vs XI Corps. "by year’s end they would become rallying cries to every Soviet and curses to every German." "Army Group B was stretched too thin—as early as August and September 1942—to liquidate these bridgeheads."

Under General Vasily Chuikov, the Soviet 62nd Army fought a brutal war of attrition for every house and street, reducing the city to rubble. Despite heavy casualties and German breakthroughs, the Soviets held. In November 1942, Soviet Marshal George Zhukov launched a massive counteroffensive (Operation Uranus) that encircled the German forces. Disregarding his generals, Hitler refused to permit a retreat. On February 2, 1943, German Field Marshal Friedrich Paulus and his remaining 120,000 troops surrendered, marking the end of the battle. "a force of well under 200 tanks was too weak... All the while, losing up to 500 men per day, Wietersheim’s XIV Panzer Corps was in danger of slowly bleeding to death." "the most important unanswered question was whether the army’s will would continue to prevail in the fall. The battle inside Stalingrad would answer this question."

**Blau I approach phase (28 June–12 July 1942, Glantz/House Ch. 4 exclusive):** Armeegruppe Weichs (XXIV Pz Langermann ~350 tanks + XXXXVIII Pz Kempf ~325) + Sixth Army (Stumme XXXX Pz ~360) opened with massive success against unprepared boundary between Briansk 13th/40th (Parsegov) and SW 21st (Danilov)/28th (Riabyshev). "Hoth’s armored spearheads rolled forward at 1000 hours, meeting minimal resistance." 24th Pz overran 40th HQ Bykovo 29 Jun ("army commander lost control of troop combat operations once and for all"); pincers closed at Staryi Oskol 3 Jul destroying organized resistance of 40th/21st (remnants ~half men reached Don "but most divisions and brigades no longer existed as organized combat formations"). Voronezh captured 6 Jul after GD seized intact Semiluki bridge ("Corporal Hempel removed the demolition charges ... with his bare hands"). Stalin's 5th Tank Army (Liziukov, 641 tanks) counterstroke 6–12 Jul (toward Zemliansk/Khokhol to sever rear) was defeated piecemeal despite initial gains; losses "roughly half of its 641 tanks, 261 of them permanently" + 7,929 men. "the intense fighting northwest of Voronezh indicated that Hitler’s new crusade east would be no cakewalk." 3 Jul Hitler/Bock conference + 7 Jul AG split (B retains elements of 4th Pz) delayed full south pivot. German armor superiority in maneuver/C2 negated Soviet numerical tank edge. (Verbatim + OOB from Ch. 4 lines 126-922, 1013-1152)

Prologue (23 Jul 1942 Sukhaia Vereika near Voronezh): Liziukov's 5th Tank Army (641 tanks) counterstroke vs German northern flank (2 Pz divs + inf + air sup) committed piecemeal ("headquarters... only six weeks" old; corps April-formed); delayed but "seemed to confirm the widespread German contempt for Soviet military staff work and leadership. Yet repeated Soviet counterstrokes of this kind delayed and weakened the Germans long before they reached Stalingrad." Liziukov killed leading rescue of encircled brigades. `preceded_by:` such actions + 1941 pattern of pocket escapes and mobilization capacity.

## Consequence Analysis
- `produced:` the **annihilation and capture of the German Sixth Army**, resulting in over 200,000 German casualties and the surrender of 120,000 troops.
- `produced:` the **collapse of the German Caucasus offensive**, forcing a hasty withdrawal of German forces from Novorossiisk and Mozdok to avoid encirclement.
- `enabled:` the **Soviet Union to seize the strategic initiative** on the Eastern Front, ending any prospect of a German victory in Russia. Ch7 endgame: 4th Pz/6th "offensive cutting edges dulling perceptively" (tanks/personnel >50% loss; 14 Pz 24 tanks post-Abganerovo); 6th still faced 5 Soviet armies mid-Aug after destroying 3; advance rates slowed to 1.5-1.75 km/day; "Great Bend... far more intense and costly... seriously weakened Sixth Army" pre-city. "Zhukov would employ 24th and 66th Armies, together with 1st Guards Army, to remind Weichs and Paulus that capturing Stalingrad would require a major battle outside as well as inside the city." (Ch. 8) 1st Guards/62/21 sideshow bridgeheads tied reserves.
- `enabled:` bridgeheads at Serafimovich and Kletskaia (from sideshow offensives 20–28 Aug) that became "rallying cries to every Soviet and curses to every German" by year-end.
- `concurrent_with:` British victory at El Alamein in Egypt and the Allied landings in North Africa (Operation Torch), marking the general turning point of World War II (Line 6293).
- From Ch. 10: arrival "already weakened" ("the Soviet Union had almost exhausted the invading Sixth Army before it even reached the city proper"; "Paulus’s Sixth Army lacked the strength necessary"; "Wietersheim’s dramatic losses—up to 500 men per day") + porous flanks + retained Soviet bridgeheads set conditions for Uranus. The three Soviet factors + "escape and fight another day" enabled the regeneration that produced the encirclement. `produced:` not just city fight but strategic overcommitment that decided Eastern Front tide.

## Historiography
Riasanovsky highlights that Stalingrad was a battle of extreme contrasts, showcasing both the brutal urban combat and the sophisticated Soviet strategic planning (Line 6287). It remains widely recognized as the single most important turning point of the war in Europe.
