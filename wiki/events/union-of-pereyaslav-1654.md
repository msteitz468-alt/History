# Union of Pereyaslav

## Narrative
The **Union of Pereyaslav** was concluded on January 18, 1654 (January 8, OS), when a general assembly (*rada*) of the Ukrainian Cossacks and representatives of the land met in Pereiaslav and decided to swear allegiance to the Russian Tsar Alexis (L2450-2452).

Under the leadership of Hetman Bogdan Khmelnitsky, the Ukrainians had waged a war of liberation against Poland since 1648 (L2448-2450). After several campaigns and abortive agreements, Khmelnitsky realized the Cossacks could not defeat Poland alone and appealed to Moscow for protection (L2450). Following the October 1653 decision of the Russian Zemsky Sobor to accept Ukraine under the Tsar's authority, a Russian embassy led by boyar Basil Buturlin arrived in Pereiaslav (L2450-2452). 

At the rada, the assembly rejected potential subjection to Catholic Poland or Muslim Turkey and swore allegiance to the Orthodox Tsar of Moscow (L2450-L2452).

## Causal Analysis
The union was caused by:
* **Socio-Religious Oppression:** Orthodox Ukrainians suffered intense social pressure under the Polish gentry and religious persecution from Polish authorities, who promoted the Uniate Church (established by the 1596 Union of Brest) over Orthodoxy (L2440-2442).
* **Cossack Autonomy Search:** The Zaporozhian Cossacks sought a powerful external protector to preserve their democratic military organization, elective offices, and land rights against the Polish gentry (L2442-2444, L2448).

## Consequence Analysis
The Union of Pereyaslav produced:
* **Russo-Polish War (1654–1667):** The alliance led directly to a protracted war between Moscow and Poland, ending with the Treaty of Andrusovo (1667) which partitioned Ukraine along the Dnieper River (L2456-2458).
* **Geopolitical Realignment:** Russia acquired Kiev and the Left-bank Ukraine, starting its rise as the dominant power in Eastern Europe (L2456-2458, L2908).
* **Loss of Autonomy:** Although the Cossacks viewed the union as a conditional agreement that preserved their autonomy, the Russian government interpreted it as unconditional integration (L2450-2452). In subsequent decades and centuries, Moscow gradually abrogated Ukrainian self-government, dissolved the Cossack hosts, and imposed serfdom on the Ukrainian peasantry (L2452-2454, L3388).
* **Cultural Influence:** More highly-educated Ukrainian scholars entered Muscovite service, playing a leading role in the Russian cultural revival and the reforms of Peter the Great (L2454-2456, L2684-2686).
