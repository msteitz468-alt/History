# Operation Barbarossa (June–December 1941)

German invasion of the Soviet Union, the largest military operation in history.

## Narrative

See detailed tactical analysis at [[hubs/warfare/world-wars-era/second-world-war/operation-barbarossa-1941]].

Three army groups advanced; initial successes but stalled at Leningrad, Moscow, with Soviet winter counteroffensives.

## Causal Analysis

**Decision mechanics (Kershaw *Nemesis* Range 03, grounded).** July 1940 Berghof: strategic pivot after Sealion deemed impossible. Halder: 'With Russia smashed, England’s last hope would be shattered... Decision: Russia’s destruction must therefore be made a part of this struggle. Spring 1941.' Primarily to compel British terms by removing last continental prop (USSR as 'the factor on which England is relying the most'); ideological secondary but ever-present. 'Working towards': Halder anticipates July 1940 by ordering study; Loßberg on own initiative drafts plans before formal order. Underestimation shared ('child’s play'). 

Directive 21 (18 Dec 1940): 'crush Soviet Russia in a rapid campaign' (code-name Barbarossa chosen by Hitler as crusade allusion). Molotov visit (Nov 1940) confirms clash. March 1941: explicit ideological framing — 'showdown of two different ideologies... The Jewish-Bolshevik intelligentsia... must be eliminated.' 30 March speech to generals: 'This is a war of annihilation... extermination of the Bolshevist commissars... Commissars... are criminals.'

**Complicity and orders.** Army translates without objection (Barbarossa Decree May 1941: suspends law/collective reprisals; Commissar Order June 1941: shoot commissars at once). Generals like Hoepner echo 'repulse of Jewish Bolshevism... with unprecedented severity.' Einsatzgruppen (Heydrich May 1941): 'Jewry was the source of Bolshevism... had to be eradicated... Jews... executed forthwith.' 'By 22 June the genocidal whirlwind was ready.'

Hitler's psychology: time obsession ('Time... was not on Germany’s side'), all-or-nothing ('I shall never survive the defeat of my people'), post-decision relief ('I again feel spiritually free'). 'Hitler had authorized more than initiated.' Broad mandate + 'working towards' produced mechanics.

**Launch (22 June 1941).** Proclamation frames as preventive crusade against 'Jewish-Anglo-Saxon warmongers and the equally Jewish rulers of the Bolshevik headquarters.' Early advances astonishing but 'Soviet calamity was immense – and avoidable' (Stalin ignored intel, exposed armies).

## Consequence Analysis

Opened two-front war Hitler sought to avoid; massive Soviet losses enabled initial gains but exposed overextension. Merged war with full Holocaust (Einsatzgruppen killings from day one; path to death camps by end 1941). 'By the end of 1941... the death camps were commencing operations.' 'The war in the East... was indeed Hitler’s war. But... acceded to, even welcomed... by all sections of the German élite' and large parts of population as 'crusade against Bolshevism.' 'The path to the Holocaust, intertwined with the showdown with Bolshevism, was prefigured.' Turned European war into world war of annihilation; set conditions for Allied victory and Soviet survival as superpower.

## Historiography (Kershaw emphasis)

Kershaw stresses structural 'working towards the Führer' + Hitler's broad licence over sole intentionalism. Army complicity ideological (anti-Bolshevism overlap + Poland precedent) not reluctant imposition. Decision primarily strategic in July 1940 (force Britain), with ideological 'Jewish-Bolshevism' crusade intensifying March 1941. Shared underestimation of 'Russian colossus' not just intel failure but racial contempt. 'The war in the East was indeed Hitler’s war. But it was more than that... acceded to... by all sections of the German élite.' Source: Kershaw *Nemesis* (primary Goebbels diaries, Halder notes, directives, Heydrich addresses).
