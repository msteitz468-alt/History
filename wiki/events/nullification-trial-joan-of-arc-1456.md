# The Nullification Trial of Joan of Arc

The Nullification Trial of Joan of Arc (also known as the Rehabilitation Trial) was opened in 1455 and concluded on 7 July 1456. Commissioned by Pope Callixtus III at the behest of Charles VII and Joan's family, the trial nullified the 1431 condemnation.

## Historiography

The trial produced an unusually rich documentary record: the full Latin dossier of witness testimony (over one hundred deponents, including childhood acquaintances, fellow soldiers, and clergy who had known Joan) is the primary source not only for the nullification proceedings but for much of what is known about Joan's early life and the original 1431 trial's irregularities. Because the process was explicitly commissioned to clear Charles VII's name and rehabilitate Joan, however, the testimony was gathered with a predetermined outcome in mind, and historians (led by the pioneering edition work of Jules Quicherat in the nineteenth century) have long cautioned that witnesses — some recalling events twenty-five years past — may have shaped their memories to fit the desired verdict, particularly regarding Joan's piety and the fairness (or lack thereof) of the original trial. The nullification record nonetheless remains, alongside the 1431 trial transcript itself, one of the best-documented judicial processes of the fifteenth century, and modern scholarship treats the two dossiers together as complementary rather than independently reliable sources.
