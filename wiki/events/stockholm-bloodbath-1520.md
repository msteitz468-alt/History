# Stockholm Bloodbath

The mass execution of 8–9 November 1520, in which [[christian-ii-of-denmark|Christian
II of Denmark]], days after his coronation as king of Sweden, had about **one hundred
people beheaded on Stortorget** in Stockholm. Per
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]], it is "a
classical issue in Nordic historical research" and the watershed that ended the
[[kalmar-union-1397|Kalmar Union]].

## Narrative and Analysis

After reconquering Sweden and defeating Sten Sture the Younger, Christian II was
crowned and then permitted **Archbishop Gustaf Trolle** to bring a charge of
**heresy** against the late Sten Sture's party for having deposed Trolle. Under cover
of this accusation the castle gates were closed and the victims — including **two
bishops**, secular counsellors, nobles, and Stockholm citizens — were executed, a
flagrant violation of clerical immunity (*privilegium canonis*).

The massacre provoked the rebellion of **Gustav Vasa**, whose success (1521–23) drove
out the Danes, made him king, and **permanently broke the union**. Historians debate
whether the bloodbath was a spontaneous explosion after the coronation or a
**premeditated joint operation** by Christian II and Trolle using the heresy charge as
a "legal disguise."

## Historiography

Called "a classical issue in Nordic historical research." The debate turns on
premeditation: Skyum-Nielsen (1964) argued for the deliberate "legal disguise"
interpretation — that the heresy charge was engineered in advance to give the purge
canonical cover — which the Cambridge History endorses. See
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]], [[engelbrekt-rebellion-1434]],
[[battle-of-brunkeberg-1471]].
