# Assassination of Malcolm X (February 21, 1965)

On February 21, 1965, **Malcolm X** (El-Hajj Malik El-Shabazz) was shot and killed while preparing to speak at the Audubon Ballroom in Harlem, New York City. He was 39 years old.

## Narrative

Malcolm had been speaking publicly despite multiple death threats following his break with the Nation of Islam. On the day of the assassination, he was scheduled to address a meeting of the Organization of Afro-American Unity. As he began his remarks, several men in the audience created a disturbance. Gunfire erupted; Malcolm was struck by multiple bullets and died shortly afterward at a hospital.

Three men associated with the Nation of Islam were arrested and tried. Talmadge Hayer (also known as Thomas Hagan) confessed to being one of the shooters and later identified other NOI members as accomplices. Norman 3X Butler and Thomas 15X Johnson were also convicted. Hayer later claimed that the other two were innocent and that the plot originated within NOI circles angry at Malcolm's defection and public criticism of Elijah Muhammad.

## Causal Analysis

The immediate context was the bitter split between Malcolm and the NOI leadership after his 1963 suspension and 1964 departure. Malcolm had publicly accused Elijah Muhammad of moral hypocrisy and had begun building rival organizations (Muslim Mosque, Inc. and the OAAU).

Broader factors:
- Intense FBI surveillance of Malcolm, the NOI, and virtually every Black radical or civil rights figure of the era (COINTELPRO program).
- The charged atmosphere of 1964–65, with urban unrest, white backlash, and intra-Black movement tensions.

Whether law enforcement agencies had advance knowledge, actively facilitated, or simply failed to protect Malcolm remains disputed. Some accounts suggest the NYPD and FBI had informants inside the NOI and were aware of threats.

`caused_by` and `contributed_to` relationships are complex and not fully resolved.

## Consequence Analysis

- Immediate national and international shock. Malcolm's death was mourned by many who had opposed him in life.
- His image and words became even more potent symbols for subsequent generations.
- The *Autobiography* (published later in 1965) became a massive bestseller and foundational text.
- Contributed to the radicalization of parts of the Black freedom movement (influence visible in SNCC, the Black Panther Party, and cultural nationalism).
- Persistent questions about the completeness of the official account have fueled decades of research, documentaries, and activism around the case.
- The event remains a touchstone in debates over state repression, intra-movement violence, and the costs of political reinvention.

## Actors

- **Primary victim**: Malcolm X
- **Convicted**: Talmadge Hayer (Hagan), Norman 3X Butler, Thomas 15X Johnson
- **Contextual**: Elijah Muhammad and NOI hierarchy; FBI and local police

## Historiography

The assassination has been examined in Marable's 2011 biography (drawing on new sources and interviews), earlier accounts, and numerous documentaries and books. Hayer's later statements implicating specific NOI members have been influential but not universally accepted as closing the case. Some researchers and family members continue to allege wider involvement.

Official resolution is incomplete in the eyes of many observers.
