# The Siege of Saguntum (219 BCE)

The eight-month siege that precipitated the [[second-punic-war-218-201bce|Second
Punic War]]. Treated in [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Chs 2–3 (Scullard,
Briscoe).

## Narrative

**Saguntum** was an Iberian city (of the Arsetani) on a plateau near the eastern
Spanish coast, **south of the Ebro** but bound to Rome by *fides* — a tie formed
"several years before" Hannibal's command, probably with Massiliote prompting.
After internal *stasis* in which the pro-Roman faction (with Roman arbitration)
executed pro-Punic leaders, [[hannibal]] — having conquered central Spain to the
Douro — used a Saguntine quarrel with a Carthaginian-protected tribe (the
Torboletae) as his occasion. He besieged the city in spring 219. Rome, with both
consuls committed to Illyria against Demetrius of Pharos and unwilling to fight on
two fronts, sent no help. Saguntum fell, after eight months of resistance, in the
late autumn of 219.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the collision between the **Ebro treaty** (226, which Carthage read as
freeing it to act south of the river) and **Rome's separate tie with Saguntum** —
the inconsistency analysed on [[causes-of-second-punic-war]].
contributed_to: Hannibal's determination, embittered (Scullard) by Carthage's
earlier humiliation over Sardinia, not to see Roman interference repeated.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the **Roman declaration of war**. On news of the city's fall Rome sent
an embassy to Carthage demanding the surrender of Hannibal; refusal meant war
(218). Whether the Senate had pre-committed to treat an attack on Saguntum as a
*casus belli* (Polybius) or debated the question afresh (the Lentulus–Fabius
debate in Dio/Zonaras) is itself disputed.

## Historiography

The Saguntine affair is the most propaganda-laden episode of the war's origins.
The **date and legal nature** of the Roman–Saguntine tie (a formal *foedus* or a
looser *deditio in fidem*?), its relation to the **Ebro treaty** (before or after
226?), and the geography (later writers, even Polybius once, wrongly placed
Saguntum *north* of the Ebro to brand Hannibal a treaty-breaker) are all contested
— see [[causes-of-second-punic-war]]. Livy's chronology of the affair is
"totally confused" (Briscoe); Polybius is the better guide.
