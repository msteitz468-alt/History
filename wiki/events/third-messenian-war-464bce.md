# The Third Messenian War (the Great Helot Revolt), 464–456 BCE

## Narrative

The **Third Messenian War** was the great revolt of the **Helots** — the Messenian serfs
of [[sparta|Sparta]] — touched off by a catastrophic **earthquake** at Sparta in **464
BCE**. [[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH Vol. V]] (Ch. 5, D. M. Lewis) treats it as the pivotal
internal crisis of mid-century Sparta and the trigger of the break with Athens.

The earthquake was severe — late sources speak of 20,000 dead and "all but five houses"
destroyed (figures Lewis treats cautiously, while accepting serious loss, with
demographic effects on the thin Spartiate citizen body). In the chaos the **Messenian
Helots**, joined by two perioikic communities (Thouria and Aethaea), rose and seized
**Mount Ithome**, the ancient stronghold of independent Messenia. The young king
**Archidamus II** was credited with rallying the city. The war was largely fought in
Messenia, and the Spartans only slowly drove the rebels back to their fortified base on
Ithome, which they could not storm.

## The rebuff of Cimon — a turning point

Unable to reduce Ithome's stockades, Sparta appealed to its allies, including **Athens**,
whose skill in siege-assault had been shown at Plataea. **[[cimon]]** carried the day
against [[ephialtes|Ephialtes']] opposition and led **4,000 hoplites** to the Peloponnese.
But the Spartans, "suspecting Athenian daring and *neoteropoiia*" (the revolutionary
spirit then triumphing in [[ephialtes-reform-462bce|Ephialtes' reform]], and perhaps
fearing the Athenians might sympathize with the rebels), **dismissed them alone of all the
allies**. Lewis calls the rebuff "a turning-point in Greek history": Athens renounced the
Spartan alliance, allied with Argos and Thessaly, and entered the
[[first-peloponnesian-war|First Peloponnesian War]].

## The settlement

The war ended (on the chronology adopted, **456/5**) with terms "notably favourable" to
the rebels — testimony to Spartan desperation and to a **Delphic oracle** "to release the
suppliant of Zeus of Ithome." The defenders and their families could leave the Peloponnese
freely (to be enslaved if they returned). Athens settled these **Messenians at
[[naupactus]]** on the Corinthian Gulf, where, "formally known as Messenians," they served
Athenian purposes for the rest of the century.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the **earthquake of 464** acting on the **structural fragility of the helot
system** — the permanent insecurity Sparta's whole militarized order existed to contain
(see [[sparta]]). contributed_to: long-standing Messenian aspirations to throw off serfdom.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the **rupture of Athens and Sparta** and the [[first-peloponnesian-war|First
Peloponnesian War]]; a **free Messenian community at Naupactus** that became an Athenian
instrument; and a deepened Spartan **demographic and strategic anxiety** (the *oliganthropia*
that would tell at Pylos in 425). It is the clearest case of the helot problem shaping
Greek high politics.

## Historiography

The war's chronology is one of the Pentekontaetia's cruces: Thucydides' statement that it
ended "in the tenth year" (i.103.1) conflicts with the surrounding events, prompting
emendation; the scale of the earthquake's demographic effect on Spartiate numbers is
debated. See [[classical-greek-historiography]].
