# Soweto Uprising (1976)

The Soweto Uprising began on 16 June 1976 when thousands of Black schoolchildren in Soweto protested against the government’s decision to enforce Afrikaans as a medium of instruction in schools. Police opened fire, killing at least 13-year-old Hector Pieterson and sparking months of nationwide unrest. The uprising marked a generational shift in the anti-apartheid struggle, brought Black Consciousness ideas to the fore, and severely damaged the apartheid regime’s international legitimacy. It is a pivotal moment in Meredith’s narrative of the internal resistance that eventually forced the regime to negotiate.

## Historiography

Meredith's account draws on contemporary press reporting, survivor testimony, and
government inquiry records (the official death toll of 176 is widely regarded by
historians as an undercount, with some estimates running several times higher, reflecting
apartheid-era suppression of casualty information and the chaotic, nationwide scope of
the unrest). Historiographical attention has shifted over time from a narrow focus on the
Afrikaans-medium policy as trigger to a broader structural account emphasizing Bantu
Education's cumulative grievances and the influence of Black Consciousness thought
(associated with Steve Biko) in politicizing a new generation independent of the
then-imprisoned ANC leadership. South African state archives on the police response have
become more accessible since the 1990s transition, though precise casualty figures remain
disputed.
