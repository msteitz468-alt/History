# The Maccabean Revolt (167–160 BCE)

The Jewish uprising against [[antiochus-iv-epiphanes|Antiochus IV]] that preserved
Judaism, rededicated the Temple (the origin of Hanukkah), and founded the Hasmonean
state. Treated in [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Ch. 10 (Habicht).

## Narrative

Habicht offers a **revisionist account** that complicates the traditional Jewish
version (Antiochus as sole villain). Since 200 the Jews of Seleucid Judaea had lived
by their own law. The crisis began **within Judaea**: in 174 **Jason**, brother of
the high priest, bought the high priesthood and the right to **transform Jerusalem
into a Greek polis** ("Antioch") with a gymnasium and ephebate — a Hellenizing reform
that split the nation. Outbid by the more radical **Menelaus**, Jason later attacked
the city on a false rumour of Antiochus' death.

Reading this factional violence as **revolt**, Antiochus — returning from the
[[day-of-eleusis-168bce|Egyptian campaign]] — plundered the Temple, garrisoned
Jerusalem, and by decree (autumn 168) **prohibited Judaism** and turned the Temple
into a sanctuary of Zeus. Resistance came from the **Hassidim** ("the Pious") and,
pragmatically, from **Mattathias and his sons** of Modein, whose son **Judas
Maccabaeus** led a guerrilla war. The rebels recovered Jerusalem and **rededicated
the Temple (164)**. The dying Antiochus, then his successor's regent **Lysias**,
withdrew the prohibition and restored religious freedom (163); the war for Jewish
worship was won, though Hasmonean political ambitions carried the fighting on.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the **Hellenizing reform initiated by the Jewish priesthood itself**
(Jason, Menelaus) and the factional strife it unleashed; Antiochus'
**misreading** of that strife as rebellion, and his over-reaction in banning the
religion (Habicht: the king's "one serious mistake").

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the **rededicated Temple** and restored religious freedom; the **Hasmonean
dynasty** and an eventually independent Judaea (its later history in CAH IX).
enabled: the **survival of Judaism** as a distinct faith and a foundational tradition
of martyrdom and religious resistance — and, in Christian reception, the casting of
Antiochus as a type of the Antichrist.

## Historiography

The sources — **1 and 2 Maccabees**, **Daniel**, Josephus — are all hostile to
Antiochus and shaped by the Jewish tradition. Habicht's key intervention is to
**shift the initiative** to the Jewish Hellenizers, against the older view (Bevan,
Tarn) of a royal programme of forced Hellenization, which he argues was alien to
Seleucid practice. The relationship of the religious revolt to the dynastic
ambitions of the Hasmoneans is much debated.
