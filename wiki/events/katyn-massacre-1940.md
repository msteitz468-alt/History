# The Katyn Massacre (April–May 1940)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: of the ~15,000 Polish officers, police, customs, and prison officials deported into the USSR after September 1939, the NKVD in April–early May 1940 took ~4,443 in batches to the Katyn Forest near Smolensk and shot them individually in the back of the head, burying them in mass graves — part of a wider killing of ~15,000 of the officer group (only ~450, mostly convertible Communists, spared) plus ~11,000 alleged counter-revolutionaries; total estimates run to ~20,000, and "the exact number may never be known." Many victims were reservists — the doctors, lawyers, teachers, and engineers of Polish civil society — making Katyn the Soviet counterpart to the Nazi liquidation of the Polish intelligentsia (`analogous_to` the AB-Aktion killings under [[german-occupation-of-poland-1939-1945]]).

The Wehrmacht discovered the Katyn graves in 1943, and Goebbels made them a propaganda centerpiece — attributing the massacre to "the Jews" — which precipitated Stalin's break with the Polish government-in-exile when it requested a Red Cross investigation.

## Historiography

The USSR blamed the Germans from 1943 (including at Nuremberg) and admitted responsibility only in 1990, with the Politburo execution order (5 March 1940, signed by Stalin and Beria) published from the Soviet archives in 1992. Now uncontested among scholars.
