# Little Rock Crisis (1957)

In September 1957, nine Black students ("the Little Rock Nine") attempted to enroll at the previously all-white Central High School in Little Rock, Arkansas, under a court-ordered desegregation plan following Brown v. Board of Education (1954). Governor Orval Faubus deployed the Arkansas National Guard to physically block the students' entry, defying federal court orders and triggering a direct confrontation between state and federal authority.

After weeks of standoff and mob violence against the students by segregationist crowds, President Dwight D. Eisenhower — initially reluctant to intervene in what he regarded as a matter for local and state authorities — federalized the Arkansas National Guard (removing it from Faubus's control) and ordered elements of the U.S. Army's 101st Airborne Division into Little Rock to escort the students into the school and maintain order, the first deployment of federal troops to enforce desegregation since Reconstruction.

## Causal Analysis

The crisis was **caused by** Faubus's direct defiance of federal court desegregation orders, itself **contributed to** by the broader pattern of Southern "Massive Resistance" to Brown v. Board across state legislatures and governors in the mid-1950s.

## Consequence Analysis

Eisenhower's intervention **produced** a landmark demonstration of federal supremacy over state resistance to court-ordered desegregation and **enabled** subsequent federal civil rights enforcement actions through the 1960s, while also **contributing to** international scrutiny of U.S. racial practices during the Cold War competition for global opinion.

## Historiography

The crisis is extensively documented through contemporary press coverage, federal records, and the personal accounts of the Little Rock Nine (notably Melba Pattillo Beals). Historians debate the degree to which Eisenhower's action reflected genuine civil rights commitment versus reluctant defense of federal constitutional authority and Cold War image management; most accounts emphasize the latter as his primary motivation.
