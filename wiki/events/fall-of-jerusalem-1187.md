# Fall of Jerusalem to Saladin (1187)

## Narrative

In October 1187, **[[saladin]]** accepted the surrender of **[[jerusalem|Jerusalem]]** after a short siege, following his annihilation of the kingdom’s army at **[[battle-of-hattin-1187|Hattin]]** (4 July 1187). **Balian of Ibelin** negotiated terms; unlike the Latin massacre of 1099, the Muslim takeover was largely orderly, with ransoms and safe-conducts — a contrast both medieval Arabic writers and later memory heavily stressed. The recovery of the city, lost to the Franks for eighty-eight years, made Saladin the central hero of Islamic crusade memory and triggered the **[[third-crusade-1189|Third Crusade]]**.

[[cobb-race-for-paradise-2014|Cobb]] fills the operational and ceremonial detail. By end of 1187 most First Crusade Frankish territory had been retaken under diverse circumstances: Acre often surrendered at sight of armies; Nablus Muslim villagers blockaded Franks; Jubayl exchanged for Hugh Embriaco’s ransom; Ascalon resisted despite a parade of Guy and the Templar master; Beirut and Jaffa resisted; **Tyre** was reconnoitered twice but left untouched — “an act of expediency [Saladin] would live to regret.”

The siege of Jerusalem began **20 September 1187**; the city was swollen with refugees. Five days later Saladin shifted to the weaker northern wall (mirroring the 1099 Frankish attack axis). Sappers and engines breached the walls; Balian threatened to kill all Muslim prisoners and destroy the Dome of the Rock and al-Aqsa if no quarter were given. Saladin accepted terms with the bloody 1099 precedent in mind. By **Friday 2 October** terms were settled; Muslim banners rose; the cross was removed from the Dome of the Rock amid *Allahu akbar*.

**Ransom terms:** forty-day grace — **10 dinars** man, **5** woman, **1** child; non-payers to slavery. Many were ransomed by donations; bribery of officials occurred; Reynald’s widow was released; the patriarch was allowed Holy Sepulcher treasure (~**200,000 dinars** contemporary valuation — hedge) despite church property not being covered — “We shall not leave them to accuse the people of the Faith of breaking faith.” Most ransom was redistributed. Post-conquest purification: al-Aqsa cleansed of Templar HQ fittings; Dome of the Rock cleansed of Frankish altar; perpetual Qurʾan reciters installed; debate over destroying the Holy Sepulcher ended with it left standing (Franks would come anyway; ʿUmar’s seventh-century precedent). **Nur al-Din’s** carved pulpit was brought from Aleppo as Nur al-Din had planned. First Friday *khutba* by Muhyi al-Din ibn al-Zaki framed Jerusalem as a “strayed camel” recovered after nearly a hundred years of polytheist abuse, urged continued *jihad*, prayed for the Abbasid caliph, and praised Saladin as if the thirty-three months spent fighting fellow Muslims had never happened.

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` [[battle-of-hattin-1187|Hattin]] and the destruction of the Frankish field army.
`caused_by:` Saladin’s prior unification of Egypt and Muslim Syria ([[ayyubid-dynasty]]).

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` Muslim restoration of Jerusalem and collapse of most inland Frankish holdings (Tyre excepted as the seed of later resistance).
`produced:` the Third Crusade and a negotiated coastal stalemate (1192).
`contributed_to:` Saladin’s enduring symbolic role in Islamic and Western memory ([[cobb-race-for-paradise-2014|Cobb]] prologue); a carefully managed public image that silenced critics of his earlier wars on Muslims.

## Islamic perspective (Cobb 2014)

See Narrative. Cobb’s theses: generosity and letter-campaigns recast prior anti-Muslim wars as unfortunate necessity for Frankish triumph; Jerusalem’s reconquest was conducted “full in the public eye”; for Muslim memory the orderly 1187 surrender stands as deliberate inverse of 1099; sparing Tyre twice was the operational regret that seeded the Third Crusade’s coastal bridgehead.

## Historiography

Arabic sources celebrate a pious, restrained conquest; Latin sources lament catastrophe. Modern scholarship debates how far Saladin’s clemency was principle, propaganda, or pragmatism. [[cobb-race-for-paradise-2014|Cobb]] supplies ransom scales, Balian’s Dome/al-Aqsa threat, purification rites, the Nur al-Din pulpit, and the first-Friday sermon’s political theology, framing the event within a longer Frankish–Muslim Mediterranean struggle rather than as a unique climax.
