# The Battle of Sepea (494 BCE)

At **Sepea**, near Tiryns in the Argolid, **[[cleomenes-i|Cleomenes I]]** of [[sparta|Sparta]]
won a "brilliantly successful" and savage victory over [[argos|Argos]] in **494 BCE** —
reportedly costing Argos **6,000 hoplites** and crippling the city for a generation.
[[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 6, L. H. Jeffery) treats it as the decisive
clash of the perennial Argive–Spartan feud.

## Narrative

Cleomenes' army was ferried up the east coast to land at **Nauplia** (some of the transport
ships supplied by [[aegina|Aegina]], which claimed it had been coerced). He defeated the
Argive army "thoroughly and savagely" inland at Sepea. The reported loss of 6,000 — if true,
"six *lochoi* completely wiped out" — points to a catastrophe for Argive manpower whatever
the exact figure.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the **long-standing feud** over the **Thyreatis and Cynuria**, won by Cleomenes'
father Anaxandridas a generation earlier; Cleomenes was "committed to hold that valuable
district for Sparta at all costs."
contributed_to (conjectural): the likely **expiry of a fifty-year truce** that may have been
signed when Argos lost the Thyreatis in the 540s — making a renewed clash due "as soon as a
later generation of Argives was old enough to fight."

## Consequence Analysis

produced: the **destruction of Argive military power** for a generation, and a domestic
crisis — the old aristocracy so depleted that a **more democratic government** emerged
(its enemies called it "government by slaves"). When 1,000 Argive volunteers (probably
disaffected aristocrats) later aided Aegina against Athens, they too were destroyed.
enabled: **Argive neutrality** in the Persian Wars — Argos cited its Sepea losses (and a
Delphic warning to beware "danger from its neighbours") to refuse the Greek coalition of
481/480, even cultivating a quiet understanding with Persia (the Perseus-ancestor myth).
This removed the one Peloponnesian counterweight to Sparta.

## Historiography

Known from **Herodotus** (VI.76–81; VII.148–50), whose casualty figure and account of the
battle's stratagems are condensed and not fully verifiable. The **lapsed-truce** hypothesis
is modern conjecture. The battle is also a key datum in debates over Argive constitutional
change and the politics of medism before Xerxes' invasion.
