# Constitutions of Clarendon (1164)

## Narrative

In **January 1164** at **Clarendon** (near Salisbury), **[[henry-ii-of-england|Henry II]]** demanded that the English bishops give solemn assent to the “customs” of his grandfather **Henry I**. **[[thomas-becket|Thomas Becket]]** and the bishops reluctantly **swore under threat**; Henry then ordered the customs **written down** — which [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]] calls a **blunder**, because a written text hardened positions. Sixteen **Constitutions** were issued; **six** were immediately unacceptable to the archbishop and (later) to Rome.

Becket **refused to seal** the document after swearing. **Pope Alexander III** later **condemned** the Constitutions and released Becket from his oath. The written customs framed the road to **Northampton (October 1164)**, Becket’s flight into a **six-year exile**, and the eventual murder of 1170 — even though, at the **Fréteval** reconciliation of 1170, the constitutions were “quietly buried” rather than formally repealed in the peace terms.

### Six unacceptable constitutions (Logan)

| # | Content (Logan’s summary) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Advowson disputes to be heard in the **king’s court** |
| 3 | **Criminous clerks** first appear in the king’s court; if guilty there, church trial, then return to the king’s court for **punishment** |
| 4 | Senior clergy may leave the kingdom only with the **king’s leave** |
| 7 | No **excommunication** of tenants-in-chief without royal leave |
| 8 | No **final appeal to the pope** without the king’s consent |
| 12 | **Royal income** from vacant sees and abbeys |

Constitution **3** (criminous clerks) and **8** (appeals) were the sharpest edges: dual jurisdiction and Rome’s appellate supremacy.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Henry II’s programme to reassert **ancestral customs** over a church recently strengthened by reform and papal justice
- `caused_by:` Becket’s post-1162 stance that clerical crime cases belonged **wholly** to ecclesiastical courts, with punishment only once (degradation/exile not doubled in secular court) — echoing current **canonist** teaching
- `contributed_to:` structural clash of two jurisdictions, “not one right and another wrong” (Logan)
- `preceded_by:` Becket’s resignation of the chancellorship and ascetic redefinition as archbishop

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` a written flashpoint that Becket would not seal and Alexander III would condemn
- `produced:` the **Northampton** confrontation (Oct. 1164), Becket’s appeal to the pope (violating Const. 8), and midnight escape into exile
- `enabled:` six years of diplomacy under an embattled Alexander III (anti-pope crisis; need for Angevin and French goodwill); Becket at Cistercian **Pontigny** then Benedictine **St Columba** near Sens
- `followed_by:` failed reconciliations (Montmirail 1169; Montmartre); young Henry’s **1170 coronation** by York; Fréteval peace without kiss of peace; martyrdom at Canterbury

## Actors

- **[[henry-ii-of-england|Henry II]]** — author of the customs programme
- **[[thomas-becket|Thomas Becket]]** — reluctant swearer, refuser of the seal, later exile and martyr
- **English bishops** — coerced into the oath; many later unsympathetic to Becket (Foliot, York)
- **Pope Alexander III** — condemned the Constitutions; released Becket’s oath; constrained by anti-pope politics

## Historiography

Logan’s Becket chapter presents Clarendon as the moment when oral “customs” became a **text**, freezing a compromise that might have remained flexible. He refuses a simple right/wrong reading of criminous clerks: two legal worlds with room for a settlement that never came. The Constitutions sit in the long European sequence of post-Gregorian church–state boundary fights (compare [[investiture-contest]], [[concordat-of-worms-1122]]), but in an **Angevin** key of written custom, royal courts, and papal appeal. Specialist Becket scholarship (and NCMH coverage of Henry II) elaborates source traditions (anonymous lives, John of Salisbury, Herbert of Bosham) beyond Logan’s textbook synthesis.
