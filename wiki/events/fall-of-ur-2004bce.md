# The Fall of Ur (Collapse of the Third Dynasty)

The destruction of the **[[third-dynasty-of-ur|Third Dynasty of Ur]]** (~2004 BCE)
under its last king **Ibbi-Sin** — the end of the last great
**Sumerian-language empire**, sacked by [[elam|Elam]] after being hollowed out by
[[amorites|Amorite]] invasion and internal defection. It became the archetypal
catastrophe of Mesopotamian memory, mourned in the great Sumerian
**Lamentations**. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]] (Ch.
XXII, C. J. Gadd).

## Narrative

The crisis that **Shu-Sin** had tried to wall off (the
[[third-dynasty-of-ur|"Wall against Amurru," Muriq-Tidnim]]) broke over his son
**Ibbi-Sin**. The [[amorites|Amorites]] breached the wall and spread through the
northern homeland; one fortress commander abandoned the key "Fort before the
Mountain" by "cowardice or treachery," turning the whole defensive flank. As cities
**defected**, the decisive figure was **Ishbi-Erra**, the "man of Mari" — sent by
Ibbi-Sin to buy grain, he instead seized **Isin** and the northern cities and set
himself up as an independent ruler, leaving the king penned in his shrinking
territory amid soaring **famine prices**. Their surviving correspondence captures
the collapse: Ibbi-Sin, unable even to find barges, bitterly concludes that the
gods must have "elected" the low-born Ishbi-Erra to rule.

Ibbi-Sin won real victories — over the Amorites and over Susa, Adamdun, and Awan —
but they were not decisive. In the end **[[elam|Elam]] (under a king of Simashki)
and the Subarians**, joined in memory by the Gutians, **sacked Ur** with a
ferocity "never forgotten," carrying **Ibbi-Sin into captivity at Anshan** and the
Moon-god's statue away. A lunar eclipse on the 14th of Adar was remembered as the
omen of "the desolation of Ur, the destruction of its walls."

## Causal Analysis

- **caused_by:** the convergent **[[amorites|Amorite]]** thrust from the northwest
  and **[[elam|Elamite]]** invasion from the east — the same double pincer
  (west + east) that had earlier destroyed [[akkadian-empire|Akkad]] under
  Shar-kali-sharri.
- **contributed_to:** the **defection of the cities**, above all Ishbi-Erra's
  seizure of Isin — an internal political collapse as much as an external conquest.
- **contributed_to:** **famine** and the failure of the over-extended defensive
  strategy (Shu-Sin's wall).

## Consequence Analysis

- **produced:** the **Isin–Larsa period** — Ishbi-Erra's dynasty at Isin and a
  parallel [[amorites|Amorite]] dynasty at Larsa divided the land, beginning the
  political world that [[amorites|Amorite]] **Babylon** (Hammurabi) would later
  unify.
- **produced:** the end of **Sumerian as a living imperial language**; thereafter
  Sumerian survived only as a learned and liturgical tongue under Akkadian- and
  Amorite-speaking dynasties.
- **produced:** the **Lamentations over the Destruction of Ur and of Sumer and
  Ur** — among the most influential works of Mesopotamian literature, and a model
  for the later "city-lament" genre.

## Historiography

Reconstructed from **date-formulae**, the **Ibbi-Sin / Ishbi-Erra correspondence**
(late school-copies), liver-omen models from Mari, and the literary **Lamentations**
— so the *sequence* of the collapse "cannot be accurately described." The relative
weight of Amorite vs. Elamite vs. internal causes is debated, and the lament-texts
conflate widespread regional ruin into a single Ur-centred catastrophe. The lunar
eclipse omen offers modern chronologers a possible anchor. Dates follow the
[[mesopotamian-chronology|middle chronology]]. See
[[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
