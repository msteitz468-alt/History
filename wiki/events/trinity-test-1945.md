# Trinity Test (16 July 1945)

The first detonation of a nuclear weapon, a plutonium implosion device code-named "the gadget," at the Alamogordo Bombing Range in New Mexico. Directed by J. Robert Oppenheimer for the Los Alamos laboratory under the Manhattan Project. Yield ~20 kt. Oppenheimer later recalled quoting the Bhagavad Gita: "Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds."

## Historiography
The test is documented through Manhattan Project technical records, participant memoirs (Groves's *Now It Can Be Told*, 1962; various Los Alamos scientists' recollections), and declassified government documents released in stages over subsequent decades, including material on fallout and downwind radiation exposure not disclosed at the time. Oppenheimer's own retrospective account of the "Bhagavad Gita" quotation is now understood to be a considerably later and possibly embellished recollection rather than a contemporaneous record, illustrating a broader source problem with participant memory in Manhattan Project historiography. Scholarly debate concerns both the test's technical significance (confirmation of the implosion design ahead of the Nagasaki bombing) and, more broadly, the ethics and necessity of the weapon's subsequent use against Japan — the latter a live controversy in nuclear history addressed more fully on the Hiroshima/Nagasaki event pages.
