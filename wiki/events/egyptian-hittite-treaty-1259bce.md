# The Egyptian–Hittite Treaty (the "Eternal Treaty")

The **first international peace treaty in history of which both parties' texts
survive** — concluded between [[ramesses-ii|Ramesses II]] of Egypt and
[[hattusili-iii|Ḫattušili III]] of Hatti in Year 21 of Ramesses (**1259 BCE**;
1284 by this volume's chronology), sixteen years after the
[[battle-of-qadesh-1274bce|battle of Qadesh]]. A landmark of Late Bronze diplomacy
and of the [[interpolity-law|great-powers system]]. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]] (Chs. XXIII–XXIV).

## The treaty

Both versions are preserved — the **Egyptian** (on the walls of Karnak and the
Ramesseum) and the **cuneiform** (the silver-tablet text found at
[[hattusha|Boğazköy]]) — the only such bilateral pair from the ancient world. Its
terms were **reciprocal**, treating the two kings as **equal great kings**:
- a mutual **renunciation of war** and recognition of the existing frontier (the
  line [[shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma I]] had drawn — i.e. **Egypt's final
  renunciation of its claim to north Syria**);
- a **mutual-defence pact** (each to aid the other against attack, internal or
  external);
- reciprocal **extradition** of fugitives, with humane-treatment clauses.

## Causes

caused_by: the **military stalemate** after Qadesh — sixteen years of war had proved
"that neither could overcome the other"; contributed_to: the **rise of Assyria**
under [[tukulti-ninurta-i|Adad-nirari I and his successors]], which threatened the
Euphrates flank of both powers and which Ḫattušili especially feared; and
**Ḫattušili III's need for legitimacy** after usurping the throne from his nephew
Urḫi-Teshub, which a great-power peace helped secure.

## Consequences

- produced: a **stable frontier and decades of genuine peace and alliance** —
  regular diplomatic correspondence (king to king *and* queen to queen, the Hittite
  **Queen Puduḫepa** prominent), a contemplated state visit, and (Year 34 /
  ~1246 BCE) a **dynastic marriage**, Ḫattušili's daughter wedding Ramesses;
- enabled: the resumption of trade and the prosperity of vassals like
  [[ugarit|Ugarit]] and Amurru in the peaceful decades;
- the peace **held for the rest of the Bronze Age** — "Khatti is at peace,"
  [[merneptah|Merneptah's]] Israel Stela could still say, and Egypt even shipped
  grain to relieve a Hittite famine. It lapsed only when Hatti itself fell in the
  [[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]].

## Significance

The treaty is the **classic document of ancient international law** — the founding
example of a parity treaty, defensive alliance, and extradition agreement between
sovereign equals, and the diplomatic capstone of the
[[interpolity-law|Late Bronze great-powers system]]. (A modern copy hangs at the
United Nations as a symbol of international peacemaking.) It marks the **definitive
limit of Egyptian empire in Asia** and the maturity of the balance of power that
[[shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma]] had set in motion.

## Historiography

Exceptionally secure because **both sides' texts survive and can be compared** — a
rarity that makes it a touchstone for ancient diplomatics. Its absolute date hangs
on the [[mesopotamian-chronology|Egyptian regnal chronology]] (Year 21 of Ramesses
II). See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
