# Outrage of Anagni (7 September 1303)

## Narrative

On **7 September 1303**, **Guillaume de Nogaret**, with **Sciarra Colonna** and a large Italian mercenary band (possibly ~1,000), forced into the papal palace at **Anagni**. They found **[[boniface-viii|Boniface VIII]]** sitting on the papal throne, clothed in papal robes and clutching a cross to his breast. Sciarra may have struck him; Nogaret intervened against murder—for a martyred Boniface was not in his plans. Townspeople turned against the attackers and Nogaret fled. Broken and weary of life, Boniface returned to Rome and died on **12 October 1303**, ending a troubled pontificate.

The assault came one day before Boniface’s planned explicit **excommunication of [[philip-iv-capetian|Philip the Fair]]** was to be nailed to the cathedral door at Anagni (8 September). It was the climax of a campaign that had already produced Louvre charges (12 May 1303: heresy, simony, “unspeakable sins”), a 29-count indictment before French prelates, and appeals for a general council.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Philip IV’s determination—through Nogaret and the Colonna—to destroy Boniface’s authority after *Clericis laicos*, the Pamiers arrest, the forged *Ausculta fili*, and *Unam sanctam*.
- `contributed_to:` the Colonna feud (money-convoy attack 1297; stripped cardinals claiming invalid election and murder of [[celestine-v|Celestine V]]).
- `preceded_by:` the Louvre assemblies and preparation of Boniface’s excommunication of Philip for 8 September.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the physical humiliation of the reigning pope; Boniface’s death weeks later; a long shadow over papal policy toward France.
- `enabled:` Clement V’s election and the provisional settlement at [[avignon-papacy|Avignon]] (from 1309); posthumous pressure on Clement to try Boniface for heresy; absolution of Nogaret (1311) under royal pressure.
- `followed_by:` Logan’s chain of papal trauma from 1303 through Avignon exile, [[great-schism-1378|Great Schism]], and humiliation at Pisa and Constance.

## Actors

- **[[boniface-viii|Boniface VIII]]** — captive in his own palace, then broken return to Rome.
- **[[philip-iv-capetian|Philip IV]]** — principal principal; “new Pilate” in Dante’s image.
- **Guillaume de Nogaret** — royal agent directing the raid; stopped murder to avoid a martyr.
- **Sciarra Colonna** — Roman enemy of the Gaetani; may have struck the pope.

## Historiography

Dante’s *Purgatorio* 20 fixed the event as a second passion of Christ in his vicar:

> “The fleur-de-lys I see Anagni invade, / And, in his vicar, Christ is made captive. / I see Him mocked a second time; / The vinegar and the gall again renewed, / And Him slain again between living thieves. / The new Pilate [Philip] I also see, / So cruel that he is not even sated by this.”

Yet the same Dante places Boniface among simoniacs. Logan refuses to rank Boniface among popes who polluted Peter’s tomb—“Almost certainly not”—while treating Anagni as the most vivid expression of national-monarchy pressure on the institutional church. Source detail (numbers of mercenaries, whether Sciarra struck) remains partly chronicle-dependent; the political meaning is not.
