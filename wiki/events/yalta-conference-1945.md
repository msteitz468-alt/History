# Yalta Conference, 4–11 February 1945

**Event page.** The final wartime summit of the Big Three (Roosevelt, Churchill, Stalin) at Livadia Palace, Yalta, Crimea. Discrete diplomatic event with profound consequences for postwar order.

## Narrative

The conference addressed final military coordination, the structure of the United Nations, the future of Poland and liberated Europe, German occupation/reparations, and Soviet entry into the war against Japan. Roosevelt traveled despite declining health; the setting was the Soviet Black Sea resort area.

## Causal Analysis

**caused_by**: Advancing Red Army control of much of Eastern Europe created facts on the ground; U.S./British desire for Soviet cooperation against Japan and in postwar institutions; FDR's personal commitment to the UN project.

**contributed_to**: Military momentum after Normandy and other fronts; prior conferences (Tehran, Dumbarton Oaks) setting agendas.

## Consequence Analysis

**produced**: Agreement on UN Security Council voting (critical to Soviet participation); Declaration of Liberated Europe (FRUS, Conferences at Malta and Yalta, 1945, 977–978); specific Polish government formula; Soviet pledge to enter Pacific war 2–3 months after German surrender; principles for German zones and reparations.

**enabled**: Creation of the United Nations (Bohlen: “There would hardly have been a United Nations” without the Soviet agreement on voting); immediate end of European war planning; but also seeds of Cold War disputes over Eastern Europe implementation.

**concurrent_with**: FDR's physical decline; final Allied push in Europe.

## Historiography (from Smith 2007 notes)

Heavily sourced from participant records and FRUS volumes. Smith cites:

- Charles E. Bohlen, *Witness to History* (key minutes and recollections).
- W. Averell Harriman and Elie Abel, *Special Envoy to Churchill and Stalin* (389, 405, 407).
- Edward R. Stettinius, *Roosevelt and the Russians: The Yalta Conference*.
- Admiral William D. Leahy, *I Was There*.
- Winston S. Churchill, *Triumph and Tragedy*.
- FRUS, *The Conferences at Malta and Yalta, 1945* (Washington: GPO, 1955).

Key grounded claims (verbatim from Range 08 endnotes for Ch. 26):

- Soviet surveillance: “We foresaw that Roosevelt would have himself wheeled into the park surrounding the palace to take the air, and so we could no longer be satisfied with microphones hidden in the rooms...” (Sergo Beria, *Beria, My Father* 104).
- UN voting: Without that agreement by the Soviets, wrote Bohlen, “There would hardly have been a United Nations.” (Bohlen, *Witness to History* 193–195).
- Declaration of Liberated Europe: text in FRUS, Malta and Yalta 977–978. Churchill added signature though “the problem was remote and secondary.”
- Far East / Japan entry: JCS advice Jan 23 1945: “Russia’s entry at as early a date as possible consistent with her ability to engage in offensive operations is necessary...” (Stimson and Bundy, *On Active Service* 619).
- FDR health during conference: Bruenn, “Clinical Notes” 589; Anna Boettiger letters; post-conference strain noted.
- FDR address to Congress, March 1, 1945: 13 *Public Papers and Addresses* 570–586 (report on Yalta). Delivery criticized by Rosenman for lacking fire (Rosenman, *Working with Roosevelt* 527–530). Mrs. Jackson: “I do not think we will ever see the President alive again” (Jackson, *That Man* 154).
- Post-conference: WSC to Clementine Churchill, Feb 12, 1945 (personal letters).

Controversies: Implementation in Eastern Europe (Declaration of Liberated Europe); extent of FDR health impact on bargaining (notes document BP readings and strain but do not adjudicate); Soviet gains in Far East vs. military necessity.

**Collection note**: Smith provides dense primary citations. Strong on U.S. perspective and documentary record; cross-reference Churchill and Soviet accounts for balance. Gaps in full Soviet archival access at time of writing.
