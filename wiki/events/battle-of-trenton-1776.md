# The Battle of Trenton (26 December 1776)

> **⚔️ Staff-college tactical analysis:** see **[[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-trenton|Trenton — Tactical Analysis]]** in the warfare hub for the full order of battle, the phased conduct of the action, and the principles-of-war assessment.

The stroke that saved the **[[american-revolution|Revolution]]** at its lowest ebb. With the army
shrunken and demoralized after the loss of [[battle-of-long-island-1776|New York]], Congress fled
to Baltimore, and most enlistments due to expire on New Year's Day, [[washington-george|Washington]]
gambled everything on a surprise attack across the **Delaware River** against the Hessian garrison
at Trenton.

## Narrative

On the night of Christmas, in a storm of "rain, sleet, and wet snow," **John Glover's Marblehead
fishermen** ferried 2,400 Continentals and 18 guns across the ice-choked river; the two supporting
columns never got across, but Washington pressed on. Striking at 8 a.m., his men achieved **complete
surprise** over Colonel **Johann Rall**'s brigade of ~1,500, worn down by constant patrolling.
**[[knox-henry|Knox]]'s artillery** raked the streets and drove the Hessians into the open, where
they were surrounded and surrendered. Within an hour Rall lay dying, **893 Hessians were captured**,
and Washington had lost just **2 killed and 4 wounded**.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **desperation of December 1776** — an army about to dissolve and a cause near
  collapse, demanding a victory before the enlistments lapsed.
- `contributed_to:` the **dispersed, complacent Hessian outposts** of British winter quarters.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` a surge of morale that, with new war powers and bounty money, let Washington **persuade
  his men to re-enlist** and rebuild the army.
- `produced:` the follow-on victory at **[[battle-of-princeton-1777|Princeton]]** and the recovery of
  most of New Jersey.
