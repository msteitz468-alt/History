# Niagara Falls Power Project (1895)

The **Niagara Falls power project** harnessed the falls for large-scale hydroelectric generation and, after long deliberation, adopted [[actors/tesla-nikola|Tesla]]'s and [[actors/westinghouse-george|Westinghouse]]'s **[[concepts/alternating-current|polyphase alternating-current]]** system. The Niagara Falls Power Company / Cataract Construction Company (backed by an international syndicate with [[actors/morgan-john-pierpont|Morgan]] interests) commissioned Westinghouse generators; power was first produced in 1895 and transmitted to Buffalo in 1896.

## Narrative

The choice of AC over DC and compressed air for one of the world's great power sources was the capstone confirmation that AC could capture and distribute energy at industrial scale over distance. The Adams Power Plant's Westinghouse generators became the model for hydroelectric development worldwide.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the technical superiority of [[concepts/alternating-current|polyphase AC]] for long-distance transmission.
- `contributed_to:` the [[events/worlds-columbian-exposition-1893|1893 Columbian Exposition]] demonstration and the outcome of the [[processes/war-of-the-currents|War of the Currents]].

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the first large-scale AC hydroelectric system and transmission to Buffalo.
- `enabled:` the global model of centralized hydroelectric generation and accelerated [[processes/electrification|electrification]].

## Historiography

The decision process of the International Niagara Commission (chaired by Lord Kelvin, who initially favored DC or compressed air before AC prevailed) is documented in the commission's records and the Cataract Construction Company archives. Seifer (*Wizard*, 1996) credits the Niagara contract as the capstone confirmation of AC and notes the syndicate's international financing (with Morgan and European backing) and General Electric's role in transformers and the transmission line. The relative weight of Tesla's specific patents versus the broader Westinghouse engineering effort and the contributions of others is a recurring point of emphasis in Tesla-centric versus business-history accounts.
