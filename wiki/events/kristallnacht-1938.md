# Reichskristallnacht (9–10 November 1938)

**Narrative**: The nationwide pogrom of 9–10 November 1938 (cynically dubbed 'Reich Crystal Night' or 'Reichskristallnacht' from the broken glass on pavements) was the culmination of a third wave of antisemitic violence that had escalated through spring–autumn 1938 as the domestic counterpart to the Czech crisis. It followed the shooting of German diplomat Ernst vom Rath in Paris by Polish-Jewish student Herschel Grynszpan on 7 November (revenge for deportation of his family and 18,000 Polish Jews). The death on 9 November coincided with the fifteenth anniversary of the 1923 Beerhall Putsch.

On the evening of 9 November, during the annual 'old guard' reception in Munich's Old Town Hall, Goebbels conferred agitatedly with Hitler. Goebbels had already orchestrated press incitement. Hitler decided: 'let the demonstrations continue. Pull back the police. The Jews should for once get to feel the anger of the people.' Goebbels immediately gave directives to police and party and delivered a short inflammatory speech to the leadership: 'Now the people will act.' Storms of applause followed; leaders rushed to telephones. The Stoßtrupp Hitler was launched in Munich; synagogues burned; Goebbels phoned direct instructions to demolish Berlin's Fasanenstraße synagogue.

Across the Reich, SA men (many celebrating the putsch anniversary and some drunk) were summoned and turned loose on Jewish property. Synagogues were set on fire or vandalized; shops smashed and looted; apartments wrecked; individuals beaten. Heydrich (informed after actions began) instructed police not to obstruct synagogue destruction and to arrest as many wealthy male Jews as prison space allowed (20–30,000 figure already in circulation). By morning: around 100 synagogues demolished or burned, several hundred more damaged, at least 8,000 Jewish shops destroyed, pavements littered with glass and merchandise, private homes trashed. ~100 Jews murdered that night; suicides commonplace. The 30,000 arrested faced indescribable sadism in camps; many later emigrated or perished.

Goebbels assessed with Hitler on 10 November. Hitler was 'in agreement with everything. His opinions are very radical and aggressive.' The action was halted (to prevent 'the mob' appearing), but severe economic measures followed. At the 12 November meeting in the Air Ministry (Göring presiding), a 1 billion Mark 'atonement fine' was imposed on the Jews; they were to be excluded from the economy by 1 January 1939 and pay for their own damage (insurance to the Reich). 'At any rate now a tabula rasa is being made. The radical view has triumphed.'

**Causal Analysis**:
- `caused_by:` Goebbels' initiative and orchestration, backed by explicit Hitler authorization at the decisive conference ('let the demonstrations continue').
- `contributed_to:` Summer 1938 violence and aryanization momentum; 'working towards the Führer' (Party activists, Aryan business interests, civil servants, Gestapo/SD all pressed forward assuming Führer approval; 'a classic case of "working towards the Führer"'); vom Rath shooting as immediate pretext and anniversary coincidence.
- `preceded_by:` Anschluss/Sudeten violence precedents (Vienna 'success' noted by Berlin radicals); Hitler's tacit support for Goebbels' Berlin 'clean-up' plans (with timing caveats for diplomacy).
- No single top-down order for the pogrom itself, but blanket verbal licence in the regime's unstructured decision style.

**Consequence Analysis**:
- `produced:` Immediate physical destruction and terror; economic 'solution' (expropriation, fine, exclusion); decisive shift of anti-Jewish policy to SS control (Heydrich Central Office Jan 1939); massive boost to forced emigration (~80,000 fled by war in traumatic circumstances).
- `enabled:` Transition from open pogrom antisemitism to systematic, 'rational' SS-driven measures (ghettos, later deportation/extermination logistics); dehumanization and exclusion as vital step toward genocide; Göring's economic exploitation interests aligned.
- `concurrent_with:` Ongoing Czech crisis; post-Munich triumphalism and radical tide.
- `part_of:` Broader radicalization process linking expansion, war preparation, and 'removal' of Jews. Contributed to Hitler's 30 Jan 1939 prophecy framing war as leading to Jewish 'annihilation'.

**Actors**:
- Primary: Hitler (licence and radical approval); Goebbels (instigator, speech, press orchestration; used event to regain standing).
- Secondary: Göring (post-pogrom economic measures and fine); Heydrich/Himmler (SS preference for 'rational' methods over SA 'excesses'; later takeover); local SA/Party leaders (improvised execution); police/Gestapo (arrest quotas); ordinary citizens (some joined looting; many appalled).

**Historiography**:
From Kershaw, *Hitler: Nemesis* (Range 02): The pogrom was not ordered in detail by Hitler but resulted from his green light in conversation with Goebbels; precise intentions left open in the regime's non-formalized style. Hitler knew from Hessen accounts what 'demonstrations' meant. Post-facto equivocation and blame-shifting onto Goebbels (even by close associates) reflected embarrassment at unpopularity and damage, not opposition to the aim. 'None of this has the ring of actions being taken against Hitler’s will.' The event cleared the impasse in anti-Jewish policy (emigration stalled post-Evian; uncoordinated measures) and handed coordination to SS 'rational' antisemites. 'Crystal Night' had profound impact on Hitler, reinforcing the war–Jewish destruction linkage already present in his worldview since 1918–19. Public condemnation (even within regime) ended open pogrom methods inside the Reich; the 'rational' phase began. Source quality strong (Goebbels diaries, Heydrich telexes, Göring meeting record, contemporary reports, Sopade summaries). Debates centre on degree of Hitler's foreknowledge vs. improvisation and on whether the pogrom was 'excess' or logical escalation. Collection strong on mechanics and 'working towards' via Kershaw; weaker on victim voices here.
