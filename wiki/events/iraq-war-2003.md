# Iraq War (2003–2011)

US-led invasion to remove Saddam Hussein, justified primarily on WMD and terrorism links (later disproven). Major phase of "War on Terror." Long-term instability and domestic political effects. (Stub.)

## Historiography

Primary sources include the official Iraq Survey Group report (2004, which found no
active WMD program), congressional and Senate Intelligence Committee investigations into
prewar intelligence, the UK's Chilcot Inquiry report (2016), and extensive journalistic
and memoir accounts from administration officials, military commanders, and Iraqi
participants. Interpretation split early between an orthodox administration narrative
(preemptive necessity against a WMD threat) and a rapidly dominant revisionist consensus,
solidified once no WMD stockpiles were found, that the war rested on flawed or manipulated
intelligence and unrealistic postwar planning assumptions. Ongoing scholarly debate covers
the relative weight of ideological (neoconservative) versus strategic motives, the sources
of the post-invasion insurgency and sectarian violence, and the war's causal role in the
later rise of ISIS. Kruse and Zelizer's *Fault Lines* (2025) treats the war within a
broader narrative of early-2000s American political polarization.
