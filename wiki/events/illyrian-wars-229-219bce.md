# The Illyrian Wars (229 and 219 BCE)

Rome's **first military interventions east of the Adriatic** — limited campaigns
that, without territorial annexation, planted Roman influence on the Balkan coast
and opened the long road to involvement in Greek affairs. Treated in
[[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Ch. 4 (Errington).

## Narrative

### First Illyrian War (229)

Through the 220s the Illyrian kingdom of the **Ardiaei** (King Agron, then his
widow **Teuta** as regent for the infant Pinnes) extended its raiding south,
sacking **Phoenice** in Epirus, where many **Italian traders** were killed or
enslaved. Their appeals — long ignored — were now heard, the more so as Phoenice
lay near the strategically vital **Straits of Otranto**. After the murder of a
Roman envoy (the Coruncanii), Rome sent both consuls with 20,000 men and 200
ships (229). **Demetrius of Pharos** betrayed Corcyra to Rome; Apollonia,
Epidamnus, the Parthini and Atintanes were received into Roman *fides*. Teuta was
confined to a rump; the Ardiaei paid tribute, and a "Lissus clause" barred Illyrian
warships from sailing south of Lissus. Rome then announced the settlement to the
Aetolian and Achaean Leagues and was present (deliberately) at the **Isthmian
Games** of 228 — in effect recognised as "honorary Greeks."

### Second Illyrian War (219)

The settlement's weak point was the ambition of **Demetrius of Pharos**, who
married Pinnes' mother, took the regency, allied with **Macedon** (sending troops
to Sellasia, 222), and in 220 sailed south of Lissus in breach of the treaty. In
219 Rome sent both consuls; Dimallum and Pharos fell, Demetrius fled to the court
of the young **[[philip-v|Philip V]]**, and the status quo of 228 was restored.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: **Illyrian piracy** and the appeals of Italian traders and of Issa;
above all Rome's settled instinct (Errington) to **weaken strong neighbours** near
its security perimeter — the same logic that had taken Sardinia and Corsica.
Errington stresses this was reactive and limited, with **no territorial ambition**
in the Balkans.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: a belt of **trans-Adriatic *amici*** (Corcyra, Apollonia, Epidamnus) and
Rome's first standing concern with the Greek world.
enabled: the **[[first-macedonian-war-214-205bce|First Macedonian War]]** — by
driving Demetrius to Philip's court, the war put Rome and Macedon on a collision
course over the same Illyrian coast.

## Historiography

A celebrated source problem: **Polybius** (drawing on Fabius Pictor) and
**Appian** give **incompatible accounts** of the First War's origins — the
dramatic Teuta interview vs. Appian's version (Agron's death, the appeal of Issa,
the envoy Cleemporus). Modern work (Petzold, Derow) has partly **rehabilitated
Appian** against Polybius, whose ignorance of Pinnes and "written-up" Teuta
stereotype Errington exposes. The episode is the test case for the debate over
early Roman eastern policy: passive/defensive (Errington, against Holleaux's
"defensive imperialism" in its older form) vs. systemically expansionist (see
[[nature-of-roman-imperialism]]).
