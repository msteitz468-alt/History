## Narrative

On 18 June 1815, near the village of Waterloo south of Brussels, Napoleon's Armée du Nord attacked the Anglo-allied army under the Duke of Wellington in a defensive position along the Mont-Saint-Jean ridge. Napoleon had defeated the Prussian army under [[actors/blucher-gebhard-leberecht-von|Blücher]] two days earlier at Ligny but failed to destroy it, and detached a substantial force under Marshal Grouchy to pursue the Prussians while the main French army engaged Wellington. Repeated French assaults — including massed cavalry charges and the eventual commitment of the Imperial Guard — failed to break the Allied line, which held through a costly afternoon of fighting centered on the fortified farmhouses of Hougoumont and La Haye Sainte. In the early evening, Blücher's Prussian army, having eluded Grouchy's pursuit, arrived on the French right flank in strength and joined the battle, tipping the balance decisively against the exhausted French forces, whose retreat collapsed into a rout.

## Causal Analysis

The battle was `caused_by` Napoleon's strategic gamble to defeat the Anglo-allied and Prussian armies separately before they could unite, and was `enabled` by his partial, not total, victory at Ligny two days prior, which left Blücher's army battered but intact and able to march to Wellington's relief.

## Consequence Analysis

Waterloo `produced` Napoleon's second and final abdication (22 June 1815) and his exile to Saint Helena, and `enabled` the consolidation of the Congress of Vienna's territorial and political settlement across Europe, ending more than two decades of nearly continuous war stemming from the French Revolution and Napoleonic era.

## Historiography

Anglophone popular narratives have traditionally centered Wellington's generalship, while Continental and more recent scholarship (e.g., Peter Hofschröer) has argued for restoring fuller weight to the scale and independence of the Prussian contribution; the tactical-level "who won it" debate remains a live historiographical dispute even though the outcome's strategic significance is not contested.
