# Einstein's 1921 United States Tour with Weizmann

**March–May 1921 two-month processional through eastern and midwestern United States to raise funds for Hebrew University in Jerusalem, accompanying Zionist leader Chaim Weizmann.**

## Narrative

Einstein initially resisted the invitation but accepted after Blumenfeld read Weizmann's telegram invoking organizational authority. The Einsteins sailed March 21; arrived New York April 2. The tour featured motorcades through Jewish neighborhoods, packed lectures (often in German), press conferences, White House visit (Harding), Senate/House interest, university lectures (Princeton technical series), parades, and fundraisers. Crowds numbered in the thousands; Einstein drew "tumultuous greeting" even when silent.

Famous exchanges: "Subtle is the Lord, but malicious he is not" (Princeton, re new Michelson-Morley claims). On "only twelve people understand" headline: "No... I think the majority of scientists who have studied it can understand it." Edison test quip on education. Weizmann quip on understanding relativity after shipboard explanations.

## Consequence Analysis

Unprecedented scientific celebrity: "the world had never before seen... such a scientific celebrity superstar". Strengthened Einstein's role as visible patron of Hebrew University and Jewish identity ("My relationship to the Jewish people has become my strongest human tie"). Financial results modest (~$750k vs hoped $4M) due to assimilated vs recent-immigrant donor split. Reinforced image as internationalist, not German; fueled domestic German backlash.

## Historiography

Isaacson (2007) uses contemporary New York Times, other press, Weizmann and Einstein letters, and later recollections to capture both the frenzy and Einstein's mixed amusement/aversion. Highlights the tour as the moment fame and Zionism converged.
