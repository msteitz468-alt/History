# Sack of Baghdad (1258)

In **1258** **[[hulegu|Hülegü]]** ended the **Abbasid Caliphate** with a systematic siege of
**[[baghdad|Baghdad]]** — engineering concentration on the Ajami tower, flooding of the Abbasid
field army's camp, and the execution of Caliph al-Musta'sim (carpet trampling).

## Narrative

After destroying the Ismaili fortresses (1256), Hülegü advanced from Azerbaijan. Caliph
al-Musta'sim, preoccupied and misled by his Shi'a wazir Ibn al-Alqami, failed to prepare.
Baiju crossed the Tigris on a bridge of boats; Abbasid forces were defeated on the plain when
dykes were broken and their camp flooded (possibly with wazir complicity). Siege began 29 January;
catapult fire breached the Ajami tower by 1 February. Al-Musta'sim surrendered; Hülegü executed
him and notables. The city was given over to thirty-four days of pillaging from 13 February.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Möngke's directive to subdue the Middle East; Abbasid failure to submit in person.
- `contributed_to:` [[kitbuqa|Kitbuqa]]'s Syrian operations and the Mamluk response.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **[[ilkhanate|Ilkhanate]]** as a Near Eastern power; end of Abbasid temporal rule.
- `enabled:` Berke's Muslim outrage and Golden Horde–Ilkhanid war; Mamluk–Ilkhanid alliance.
