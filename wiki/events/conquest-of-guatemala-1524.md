# Conquest of Guatemala (and Chiapas highlands), 1524–c. 1534+

## Narrative

After the fall of [[spanish-conquest-of-mexico-1519|Tenochtitlan]] (**1521**), Cortés’s forces turned to south and east lands where Aztec influence reached but authority generally did not. **Chiapas** and **Guatemala** were known at Tenochtitlan for cacao, cochineal, and quetzal feathers. Spaniards faced quarrelsome, disparate polities, not a cohesive state — conquest would be neither sudden nor sure (W. George Lovell, CHNPA II.2 Ch. 21).

**Guatemala (Alvarado).** Shortly before Luis Marín’s northern approach to Chiapas, **Pedro de Alvarado** crossed the **Soconusco** littoral into Guatemala. There was little resistance on the Pacific Coast; battles followed ascent into the highlands. Main opponents were the **K’iche’s**; after their defeat, Mam, Ixil, Ch’orti’ and others had to be dealt with one by one. **Kaqchikel** warriors sometimes fought alongside the Spaniards (e.g. against the **Tz’utujil** of Atitlán). Kaqchikel allegiance withered after barely **six months**, when excessive demands for tribute caused a rebellion lasting almost **four years**. The *Annals of the Cakchiquels* record Alvarado threatening burning/hanging and demanding **1,200 pesos** gold; half was paid when the Kaqchikel escaped and joined general war with horse-pits and stakes. **Q’eqchi’** and **Uspanteko** temporarily defeated invaders before later conquest. Not until some **ten years** later — in certain areas considerably longer — did Spaniards in Guatemala bring the natives to heel. Resistance made military conquest bloody and protracted.

**Chiapas (Marín reconnaissance; Portocarrero–Mazariegos pacification).** Luis Marín’s February **1524** expedition found several well-organized but not especially powerful societies. It passed Zoque country (middle Grijalva) easily; fought Chiapanecos at present **Chiapa de Corzo**; moved through **Zinacantán**; engaged Tzotzil at **Chamula**; returned toward Tabasco rather than push through Tzeltal/Coxoh (possibly Tojolabal) country. The expedition was strategic reconnaissance. Effective Spanish domination of the central highlands came almost four years later when **Pedro Portocarrero** and **Diego de Mazariegos** reentered with larger forces (Portocarrero met Mazariegos at **Comitán**, likely **1528**). Territory was initially under New Spain, then (**1530–1821**) under Guatemala, except self-government of “Chiapa” **1540–44**. The center of Spanish settlement in colonial Chiapas was **Ciudad Real** (present-day San Cristóbal de las Casas).

Large armies impeding Spanish entry indicate sizable highland populations at contact; conquest was more or less contemporaneous with catastrophic depopulation lasting well into the seventeenth century (and in Chiapas far beyond). See [[highland-maya-colonial-modern]] and [[virgin-soil-epidemics]].

## Causal Analysis

- caused_by: [[spanish-conquest-of-mexico-1519]] (Spanish military and political base in central Mexico enabling southward expansion)
- contributed_to: Spanish demand for tribute labor and highland/Pacific prestige and commercial products (cacao, cochineal, quetzal feathers)
- contributed_to: political fragmentation of highland Maya polities (K’iche’, Kaqchikel, Mam, Tzotzil, etc.) without a Triple Alliance–scale state
- concurrent_with: earliest virgin-soil epidemics, including the *Annals* pestilence of **1519–20** before Spaniards themselves reached Guatemala
- preceded_by: Postclassic highland conquest-state system ([[maya-highland-pacific-development]]; Quiché/Cakchiquel segmentary states)

## Consequence Analysis

- produced: Spanish colonial rule over the Guatemalan and Chiapas highlands, with colonial capital geography (Santiago/Antigua; Ciudad Real)
- produced: [[encomienda]] awards and later [[congregacion|congregación]] resettlement as twin institutions of rule
- contributed_to: highland Maya demographic collapse (Lovell & Lutz Guatemala series; Gerhard Chiapas series — see process page tables)
- enabled: NW highland **culture of refuge** and SE/Pacific **ladinoization** gradient under mature colonial economy (MacLeod regionalization)
- followed_by: mature colonial order, Tzeltal Revolt **1712–13**, independence partition (Chiapas to Mexico), Liberal coffee labor regimes — all under [[highland-maya-colonial-modern]]

## Actors

- **Spanish:** Pedro de Alvarado (Guatemala principal captain); Luis Marín (Chiapas 1524 reconnaissance); Pedro Portocarrero and Diego de Mazariegos (effective Chiapas pacification ~1528); early governors awarding encomiendas (e.g. Jorge de Alvarado **1527–29**: 86 awards / 56 encomenderos — Kramer via Lovell).
- **Highland Maya:** K’iche’ (main early opponents); Kaqchikel (allies then four-year rebels); Tz’utujil of Atitlán; Mam, Ixil, Ch’orti’; Q’eqchi’ and Uspanteko (temporary military successes); Chiapas: Zoque, Chiapanecos, Tzotzil (Chamula), Tzeltal/Coxoh country deferred in first expedition.
- **Civilizational frame:** [[maya-civilization]] · places [[guatemala-highlands]] · [[chiapas-highlands]].

## Historiography

Lovell synthesizes conquest as **protracted multi-polity war**, not a single battle-year; subjugation timelines differ by region (ten years or longer). Primary indigenous voice for Guatemala: *Annals of the Cakchiquels* (tribute demands, plague, congregación founding of Sololá). Chiapas operational detail: Lenkersdorf on Portocarrero–Mazariegos **1522–32** (bibliographical essay). Kramer’s work on pre-Cerrato encomienda reframes the immediate post-conquest decades as the most exploitative for Maya, correcting over-reliance on Cerrato’s mid-century assessment as a “starting point.” Contact population remains disputed (Denevan / Lovell & Lutz ~2M Guatemala vs lower highland-only estimates by Sanders & Murdy, Zamora, Solano) — related to [[pre-columbian-american-population]] and Black Legend debates, but Lovell stresses that **catastrophic depopulation contemporaneous with conquest** is less contested than absolute starting numbers.

English-language colonial entry points: Thomas Gage; modern landmark MacLeod *Spanish Central America* (**1973**). This event page rests primarily on Lovell, CHNPA II.2 Ch. 21, not on full operational reading of Alvarado’s letters or Bernal Díaz for Guatemala.

## From Evans & Webster 2001

Encyclopedia capsule statements (Maya Culture and History / highlands summaries) align with Lovell’s protracted multi-polity frame while compressing operational detail ([[evans-webster-archaeology-ancient-mexico-central-america-2001]]):

- In **1524** Pedro de Alvarado initiated the conquest of Central America with the defeat of the **Quiché Maya under Tecun** in western Guatemala; conquest of other Maya groups was protracted, with the last independent Maya polity (Itza of Petén) falling only in **1697**.
- Arrival of the Spaniards **interrupted the Quiché rise to dominance** among competing highland Maya groups.
- Spanish armies and highland Indian allies **quickly conquered the Quiché and Cakchiquel in the mid-1520s**; northern Yucatán Maya resisted more effectively until final subjugation in the **1540s** (contrast: [[conquest-of-yucatan-1527]]).

These encyclopedia statements simplify the Kaqchikel alliance-then-rebellion sequence that Lovell documents in full; both sources agree that highland military conquest was faster than Yucatecan, while demographic and institutional colonization remained multi-decadal.
