## Narrative

[[actors/timur|Timur]] had earlier backed Tokhtamysh's rise to leadership of the Golden Horde, but the two fell out once Tokhtamysh, secure in power, began attacking Timurid holdings in Transoxiana and the Caucasus in the 1380s. After an earlier inconclusive campaign, Timur launched a major punitive expedition in 1395, defeating Tokhtamysh decisively at the Battle of the Terek River. Timur then drove deep into Golden Horde territory, sacking its major cities, including the capital Sarai on the Volga, and devastating the trade infrastructure that had underpinned the Horde's wealth.

## Causal Analysis

The campaign was caused_by Tokhtamysh's repeated incursions into Timurid territory after Timur's earlier support had helped establish him; Timur's subsequent destruction of Sarai and other Horde cities was a deliberate act of economic and political devastation, not merely a battlefield victory.

## Consequence Analysis

The defeat and the accompanying destruction of Golden Horde infrastructure (produced) a severe and lasting weakening of Horde central authority, contributed_to the Horde's progressive fragmentation into successor khanates (Kazan, Crimea, Astrakhan, Sibir) over the following century, and removed a major check on the rising power of Muscovy, which the Horde had previously dominated.

## Historiography

The campaign is documented through Timurid court chronicles (naturally favorable to Timur) and later Golden Horde/Tatar historical tradition; its long-term significance for the Horde's decline is broadly accepted, though historians differ on how much weight to give Timur's campaign versus longer-run structural factors (succession disputes, the rise of Muscovy, plague) in explaining the Horde's eventual disintegration.
