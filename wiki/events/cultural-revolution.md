# The Cultural Revolution (1966–1976)

The **Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution** was the decade of political upheaval Mao Zedong
unleashed to reassert his authority and combat "revisionism" — mobilizing the youth against the
Communist Party itself and convulsing China in persecution, factional violence, and paralysis. From
the [[cambridge-history-of-china|Cambridge History of China]], Vol. 15 (titled for the "revolutions
within the Chinese revolution," 1966–82).

## Narrative

Sidelined after the [[great-leap-forward|Great Leap Forward]] disaster, **Mao** struck back in 1966
by calling on students — the **Red Guards** — to "bombard the headquarters" and overthrow those
"taking the capitalist road." Wielding the *Little Red Book* and a cult of personality, Red Guard
factions attacked teachers, officials, and the "Four Olds," destroyed cultural heritage, and fought
each other; top leaders were purged (**Liu Shaoqi** died in detention; **Deng Xiaoping** was sent
down). As chaos spread, Mao called in the **PLA** to restore order and dispersed youth "**up to the
mountains and down to the villages**." Radical leaders (the **Gang of Four**, including Mao's wife
Jiang Qing) contested the succession until Mao's **death in September 1976**, after which the Gang of
Four were arrested — ending the movement.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` Mao's **loss of authority** after the Great Leap and his determination to purge the
  pragmatist rivals (Liu, Deng) who had eclipsed him.
- `caused_by:` Mao's **ideological fear** of bureaucratic "revisionism" and a Soviet-style betrayal
  of the revolution — his theory of **continuing revolution under socialism**.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` a **decade of turmoil** — mass persecution (perhaps 1–2 million deaths and countless
  ruined lives), the near-destruction of the Party apparatus, closed universities, and economic and
  cultural devastation ("the ten lost years").
- `produced:` the ultimate **discrediting of Maoist radicalism**: its excesses paved the way for
  **Deng Xiaoping's** rehabilitation and the **"reform and opening"** that followed from 1978.
- The CCP's own 1981 verdict judged it a catastrophe and "grave error" of Mao's later years.

## Actors

**Mao** and the radical **Gang of Four** and **Red Guards** against the Party establishment (**Liu
Shaoqi, Deng Xiaoping**), with the **PLA** as the eventual restorer of order. See [[maoist-china]]
and [[mao-zedong]].

## Historiography

CHC Vol. 15, written in the immediate aftermath, frames the Cultural Revolution through the Party's
internal power struggle and the dynamics of Maoist mass mobilization. Later scholarship adds the
social history of factionalism, generational trauma, and regional variation. It remains a sensitive,
incompletely examined subject within China, and the paradigm of **ideological mass movement turned
self-destructive**.
