# Treaty of Perth

The 1266 treaty by which **Magnus Lagabøte** of [[norway|Norway]] ceded the
**Hebrides and the Isle of Man** to **Alexander III of Scotland** in return for 4,000
marks plus an annual 100 marks in perpetuity, while Scotland confirmed Norwegian
sovereignty over **Orkney and Shetland**. Per
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]], it followed the
inconclusive western campaign of [[hakon-hakonarson|Hákon Hákonarson]] (who died in
Orkney in 1263 after the Battle of Largs) and defined the outer limits of the
Norwegian western dominion (*Norgesveldet*). It marks the turning point from Norwegian
expansion to retrenchment in the Irish Sea; Orkney and Shetland would themselves be
pledged to Scotland in 1468–69.

## Historiography

The treaty is well documented from both Norwegian (the saga of Hákon Hákonarson) and
Scottish records. The Cambridge History reads it as marking the practical limit of the
*Norgesveldet* — the point at which Norway's North Atlantic dominion ceased to expand.
See [[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]].
