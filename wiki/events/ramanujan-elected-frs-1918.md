# Ramanujan Elected Fellow of the Royal Society (1918)

In 1918 Srinivasa Ramanujan was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society — the first Indian so honored for scientific work. The election was the direct result of the papers he had published since arriving in England and of G. H. Hardy’s persistent advocacy. It was a formal recognition that the “clerk from Madras” had entered the first rank of world mathematicians. The same year he was also elected a Fellow of Trinity College.

## Historiography
The election is documented through Royal Society records and Hardy's own correspondence and published tributes, which are the primary source for how strongly Hardy's advocacy is credited relative to the independent judgment of the wider Fellowship. Biographical accounts (Kanigel, and earlier Hardy's own writings) generally treat the election as confirmation of scientific merit rather than a controversial or contested honor, though some later commentary has noted the unusual speed and unanimity of Ramanujan's election as itself evidence of how exceptional his published output 1914–1918 was judged to be by contemporaries.
