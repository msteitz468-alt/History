# The Archidamian War, 431–421 BCE

## Narrative

The **Archidamian War** — named for the Spartan king **Archidamus II** — was the **first
phase** of the [[peloponnesian-war|Peloponnesian War]] (431–421). [[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH
Vol. V]] (Ch. 9, D. M. Lewis) follows Thucydides' narrative, which "determines our view of
the war even more firmly than of the preliminaries." Open war began in **431** with the
**Theban surprise attack on Plataea** in spring and the **Spartan invasion of Attica** eighty
days later — the Spartans later admitting they had "formally and illegally opened the war" by
taking up arms while Athens offered arbitration.

## Periclean strategy

The war tested **[[pericles|Pericles']]** revolutionary **defensive strategy**: instead of
meeting the invaders in the field, **evacuate Attica** behind the **Long Walls**; rely on the
**fleet** and the imperial **revenue** (beyond anything Peloponnesian finance could match);
keep the allies in hand; **raid the Peloponnesian coast** in retaliation; and **avoid
irrelevant offensive ventures**. The aim, never stated outright, was to make the Spartans
recognize they could not break Athens and accept the *status quo*. Lewis notes Pericles may
have had **more offensive designs** than Thucydides allows — above all the recovery of
**Megara**, key to barring Peloponnesian moves north of the Isthmus.

## The plague and Pericles' death

The strategy was overtaken by catastrophe: the **[[plague-of-athens-430bce|plague]]** that
struck the crowded city in **430** (recurring to 426) killed perhaps a quarter of the
population, wrecked the campaign to finish the **Potidaea** revolt, and carried off
**Pericles himself in 429**. Thucydides delivered Pericles' **Funeral Oration** over the
first year's dead — his idealized vision of Athens as "the education of Greece" — just before
the plague turned the city against the war and its author.

## The middle years

The war was marked by mounting **brutality** — the *stasis* (civil strife) Thucydides
anatomized at **Corcyra** (427), and the new acceptance of **massacre and enslavement** of
captured cities. Key episodes:

- **Plataea** — besieged from 429, it fell in **427** and its defenders were executed.
- **Mytilene** — the Lesbian city revolted (428); after its reduction the Athenian Assembly
  reversed its own decree to slaughter the population (the **Mytilenean Debate**, Cleon vs.
  Diodotus), sparing the *demos*.
- **Pylos and Sphacteria** (425) — Demosthenes fortified **Pylos** in Messenia; the
  resulting capture of **120 Spartiate prisoners** (a major shock, given Spartan
  *oliganthropia*) drove Sparta to **sue for peace**, which Athens, urged by
  **[[cleon]]**, **rejected** — the chance Thucydides thought it a mistake to miss.
- **Brasidas in Thrace** (424) — the energetic Spartan **[[brasidas]]** marched north and
  won over the Chalcidic cities, taking **Amphipolis** (the loss of which exiled its failed
  defender, the historian Thucydides). The Athenian counter-offensive into Boeotia ended in
  defeat at **Delium** (424).

## The Peace of Nicias

The **simultaneous deaths of [[cleon]] and [[brasidas]] at Amphipolis (422)** removed the
chief war-leaders on each side and made peace "irresistible." The **Peace of Nicias (421)**
restored gains on a status-quo basis (each side returning what it held) — Sparta abandoning
"all attempt to destroy the Athenian empire" and even cherished allied interests "for the
security of her own system," its allies (Corinth, Boeotia, Megara, Elis) refusing to ratify.
Lewis's verdict: "**Athens had won the war**" — though the peace would not hold.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the [[peloponnesian-war|structural causes of the war]] and the precipitating
attacks of 431. produced: an inconclusive peace that satisfied no one; a Sparta convinced
peace was necessary (the prisoners, the **Pylos/helot** threat); and the conditions — a
restless Alcibiades, an unstable settlement — that led to the
[[sicilian-expedition|Sicilian Expedition]].

## Historiography

The chronicle is Thucydides', and the chapter is "an attempt to clarify his thinking and call
attention to points he has obscured." Lewis flags the suspicion that Thucydides **played down
what did not happen** (Megara, the border forts) and saw allied loyalty through the eyes of
his own class (the *stasis* between *demos* and oligarchs). See [[classical-greek-historiography]].
