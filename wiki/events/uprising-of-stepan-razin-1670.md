# Uprising of Stepan Razin

## Narrative
The **Uprising of Stepan Razin** (1670–1671) was the largest social revolt in 17th-century Muscovite Russia, engulfing the Volga River basin and southern steppe regions (L2426-2428).

The movement began in the spring of 1670 when Stepan (Stenka) Razin, a Don Cossack commander who had won fame raiding Persian shores along the Caspian Sea, led his band up the Volga River (L2426-2428). "In the spring of 1670 Razin decided on a much bolder enterprise... an attack on the Russian heartland to eradicate the ‘traitor-boyars’ in Moscow. In May Razin and his cossacks crossed again from the Don to the Volga, and captured Tsaritsyn." (lines 17032–17034). "The cossacks’ capture of the fortress [Astrakhan] was facilitated by a popular uprising in the city. There ensued a massacre of the privileged elites of Astrakhan’: the governor, Prince I. S. Prozorovskii, was thrown to his death from the top of a tower" (lines 17034–17036). "As the cossacks moved up the Volga, they distributed ‘seditious letters’ in the surrounding villages, provoking a widespread peasant revolt. Estates were looted, manor houses burned and landowners murdered. Some of the non-Russian peoples of the Volga were also drawn into the rising, especially the Mordva, the Mari and the Chuvash." (lines 17039–17042). "The rebels’ triumphant advance was eventually arrested at Simbirsk." (line 17042). 

The rebellion grew rapidly to over 20,000 men as serfs, runaway peasants, slaves, and indigenous Volga tribes rose against their masters (L2428). However, the poorly organized rebel forces were defeated at Simbirsk in late 1670 by regular Muscovite troops, who utilized Western-trained regiments (L2428). Razin escaped to the Don but was arrested by loyal Cossack authorities in the spring of 1671 and handed over to Muscovite officials to be executed in Moscow (L2428). Astrakhan, the last rebel stronghold, surrendered in late 1671 (L2428). "Razin himself belonged to the more prosperous section of the cossacks, but most of his" followers were from lower strata (lines 17152–17153). "In his speech to the cossacks at Panshin Gorodok, Razin called on them all ‘to drive the traitors out of the Muscovite state and to give the common people freedom’." (lines 17343–17345). "Every where he promised Liberty, and a redemption from the Yoak... of the Bojars or Nobles" (contemporary account, lines 17348–17350). "in Astrakhan’, indicating that they destroyed the documents which registered slaves, thereby granting the bondsmen their freedom." (lines 17357–17359).

## Causal Analysis
The uprising was caused by:
* **Codification of Serfdom:** The Ulozhenie of 1649 had legally tied the Russian peasantry to their lands, removing all time limits on the recovery of fugitives (L2526-2528). This prompted thousands of peasants to flee to the Don region, creating a volatile, landless underclass of "unrecognized" Cossacks (L2224-2226, L2446-2448).
* **Fiscal Pressure:** The Muscovite state's financial crises and military exactions (such as the salt tax that provoked the 1648 Moscow riot and the copper coin debasement that led to the 1662 riot) created widespread discontent (L2425-2427).

## Consequence Analysis
The rebellion produced:
* **Agrarian Repression:** The defeat of the rebellion was followed by brutal state reprisals, further strengthening the legal and physical grip of the gentry over their serfs (L2528-2530).
* **Borderland Centralization:** Moscow tightened its grip on the Don Cossack territory, gradually eroding their independence and integrating the Cossacks into the state military apparatus (L2972-2974).
* **Folk Legacy:** Stepan Razin was immortalized in Russian folklore and songs, becoming a symbol of popular resistance against autocracy (L2426, L2696).
