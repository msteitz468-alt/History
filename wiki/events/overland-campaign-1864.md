# Overland Campaign (May–June 1864)

Grant's direct campaign against Lee from the Rapidan to the James River, consisting of the Battles of the Wilderness, Spotsylvania Court House, North Anna, and Cold Harbor, ending with the crossing to Petersburg.

## Narrative

See detailed tactical analysis at [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/american-civil-war/overland-campaign-1864]].

Grant took overall command and insisted on continuous pressure. After bloody fighting in the Wilderness and at Spotsylvania (including Upton's assault and the Bloody Angle), the armies maneuvered to Cold Harbor, where a disastrous Union assault occurred. Grant then executed a masterful crossing of the James.

## Causal Analysis

Grant's strategy of attrition and maneuver vs. Lee's defensive use of fortifications and interior lines. Political need to keep pressure before the 1864 election.

## Consequence Analysis

Lee's army was severely weakened proportionally and forced into the Petersburg lines. The campaign demonstrated the high cost of modern warfare but achieved the strategic goal of fixing Lee while Sherman operated in the West.


### From Ellsworth, *Midnight on the Potomac* (2025)

- Grant in Washington 8 Mar 1864; left Culpeper with ~120,000 — "largest army ever assembled in the Western Hemisphere" (Ellsworth).
- Rapidan crossing night of 4 May; five pontoon bridges; plan to swing behind Lee.
- Wilderness: terrain trap, forest fires cremating wounded; no victory for either side by 7 May; Stanton press ban; false "Victory!" papers 9 May; Lincoln moderated crowds.
- Spotsylvania race, Upton 10 May, Bloody Angle 12 May — see [[events/battle-of-spotsylvania-1864]].
- By ~9 June Army of the Potomac halted outside Petersburg for siege — "war wouldn't be ending anytime soon."
- Cold Harbor only as casualty add-on in Ellsworth's narrative pass (prefer warfare hub / specialist for phases).
- Cascading political cost: hospital ships, Armory Square nurses (Akin), capital crisis under Early, summer electoral despair.

Source: [[sources/ellsworth-midnight-on-the-potomac-2025]].

## Historiography

Standard operational accounts (Hattaway & Jones, West Point series, Rhea on Wilderness/Spotsylvania) treat the Overland as continuous contact warfare that traded Union numbers for pinning Lee and enabling simultaneous advances elsewhere. Ellsworth (*Midnight on the Potomac*, 2025) is not an operational monograph; he uses the campaign as Act One of a last-year national crisis narrative—hospitals, casualty lists, Early's raid as related political shock, and the road to the 1864 election.
