# The Zannanza Affair

The astonishing episode in which an **Egyptian queen offered the throne of Egypt to
a Hittite prince** — and the murder of that prince derailed it. A textbook case of
**contingency in history**, and the only point at which the Egyptian and Hittite
royal houses nearly merged. Recorded in the **"Deeds of Šuppiluliuma"** and treated
in [[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]] (Chs. XVII & XIX).

## Narrative

On the death of an Egyptian king whom the Hittite source calls **Niphururiya** —
almost certainly [[tutankhamun|Tutankhamun]], who died young and **heirless**,
ending the male line of [[new-kingdom-egypt|the Eighteenth Dynasty]] — his widow
(Ankhesenamun) wrote to [[shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma I]] the famous letter:

> "My husband has died, and I have no son. They say about you that you have many
> sons. You might give me one of your sons, and he might become my husband. I would
> not want to take one of my servants. I am loath to make him my husband."

The offer was so unexpected that Šuppiluliuma "called together his noblemen" and
sent an envoy to **investigate** before believing it. A second, more reproachful
letter from the queen — "I have written only to you... He will be my husband and
king in the country of Egypt" — finally convinced him, and he despatched his son
**Zannanza**. The prince **never reached Egypt: he was murdered on the way**,
probably by the Egyptian court faction "who did not wish a foreigner to ascend the
throne of the pharaohs."

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: [[tutankhamun]]'s death without a son and the **extinction of the
dynastic male line** — "whomsoever his widow married would *ipso facto* be the next
pharaoh." contributed_to: the long **tradition of Egyptian–Mitannian royal
marriage**, which the queen seems to have sought to transfer to the now-ascendant
Hittites; and the divine-kingship ideology that made a foreign king preferable to "a
native commoner."

## Consequence Analysis

- produced: the **murder of Zannanza** and the accession of the commoner-vizier
  **[[unknown|Ay]]** (who appears to have married the royal widow), then
  [[horemheb|Horemheb]];
- produced: a lasting **breach with Hatti** — Šuppiluliuma, enraged, attacked the
  Egyptian border province of **Amqa**;
- enabled: the **plague** carried home from Amqa by the returning Hittite army, which
  would kill Šuppiluliuma himself and his successor and ravage Hatti for years (the
  subject of [[unknown|Muršili II's plague-prayers]]).

## Counterfactual significance

The volume frames it explicitly as a missed turning point: "by over-cautious
hesitation Šuppiluliumash missed the chance of making one of his sons pharaoh of
Egypt." Had Zannanza lived, a Hittite would have sat on the throne of Egypt — a
union of the two greatest powers of the age. Its failure instead set Egypt and Hatti
on the collision course that ran to the [[unknown|battle of Qadesh]].

## Historiography

The episode is known almost entirely from the **Hittite** side (the "Deeds of
Šuppiluliuma"); the identity of the dead pharaoh ("Niphururiya") — Tutankhamun, or
possibly Akhenaten/Smenkhkare — and the queen are debated, and with them the absolute
date (this volume's high chronology places it ~1352 BCE; modern dating ~1323/1322).
See [[shuppiluliuma-i]] and [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
