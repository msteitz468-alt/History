# Ghost Dance Movement

Prophetic revitalization religion originating with Paiute **Wovoka (Jack Wilson)** in Nevada (1889) and spreading by rail across the West; among the Lakota in 1890 it became the proximate religious context of the [[wounded-knee-1890]] massacre—while continuing as ongoing practice into the twentieth century.

## Narrative

During a **total solar eclipse on New Year’s Day 1889**, Paiute man **Wovoka** (Jack Wilson) received a message of **peace and right living** from the Creator. His round-dance teaching, assisted by speedy rail service, spread to groups in **California, Arizona, Wyoming, Oklahoma, and the Dakotas**. During the **summer of 1890**, growing numbers of **Lakota** began to perform the round dance Wilson had prescribed; the Indian Office massed troops around Sioux camps.

On the [[pine-ridge-reservation]] and Rosebud, communities many times the size of Apache/Crow groups previously intimidated by troop massing, leaders were battle-hardened veterans of 1860s Montana campaigns and Little Bighorn 1876—not men who trusted U.S. Army officers. Land-loss tension after the **1889 Crook commission** opening of millions of acres west of the Missouri formed the political backdrop (contributed_to Wounded Knee context).

After Wounded Knee, the Ghost Dance **continued to draw followers**. Wovoka sold red paint emblematic of his vision and preached peace, hard work, and sobriety until his death in **1932**. Persistent followers included **Kiowas, Comanches, and Pawnees of Oklahoma**, **Sioux groups in Saskatchewan**, and **Shoshones in Wyoming**.

## Causal Analysis

- caused_by (religious origin): Wovoka’s eclipse vision and teaching of peaceful right living—not, in Hoxie’s presentation, a call to war.
- contributed_to (structural): reservation confinement, 1880s land reductions, buffalo collapse, and subsistence crisis making revitalization cosmologies compelling.
- enabled: industrial rail communication that carried ritual and news across tribal borders (same infrastructure enabling non-Indian intrusion).
- preceded_by: failed armed resistance elsewhere and deterrent lessons of Apache exile / Crow Sword Bearer incident—raising stakes of government “nervous reaction.”

## Consequence Analysis

- Proximate context of military response and mutual fear culminating in Sitting Bull’s killing (15 Dec 1890) and Big Foot’s flight → Wounded Knee (Hoxie: government nervous reaction + mutual fear; first shot still disputed).
- produced: deterrent signal after massacre that direct opposition would be met with force—but **not** extinction of the religion itself.
- followed_by: multi-decade continuity under Wovoka’s peace/hard-work/sobriety preaching; pan-tribal religious network analogous to later peyote / Native American Church expansion.
- contributed_to: intertribal cultural re-creation phase of reservation period.

## Actors

- Wovoka / Jack Wilson (originator; d. 1932).
- Lakota communities at Pine Ridge, Rosebud, Standing Rock, Cheyenne River (1890 crisis).
- Persistent non-Lakota followers: Kiowa, Comanche, Pawnee, Saskatchewan Sioux, Wyoming Shoshone.
- Indian Office agents and U.S. Army as repressive apparatus.

## Historiography

Hoxie carefully separates **origin teaching** (peace and right living) from **Lakota 1890 crisis dynamics** (troop massing, mutual fear) and refuses to settle the Wounded Knee first-shot dispute. He documents **post-1890 continuity**, correcting narratives that end the Ghost Dance at the massacre. Date end in frontmatter uses Wovoka’s death (1932) as terminal for the prophet-led phase while noting ongoing practice. Do not reduce the movement to “cause of Wounded Knee” alone—contributed_to as proximate context, not sole causation of Army violence.
