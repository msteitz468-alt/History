# Battle of Carchemish (605 BCE)

The **battle of Carchemish** (605 BCE) was the decisive clash between the rising
[[neo-babylonian-empire|Neo-Babylonian Empire]] and **Saite Egypt** for the corpse
of the [[neo-assyrian-empire|Assyrian Empire]] — the victory that delivered the
entire Levant to **[[nebuchadnezzar-ii|Nebuchadnezzar II]]** and ended Egyptian
power in Asia. [[boardman-cah-v3p2-1991|CAH Vol. III, Part 2]] (Ch. 27, Wiseman)
narrates it from the **Babylonian Chronicle**, "a precise and reliable source for
the major events until 594/3."

## Narrative

After Assyria's fall, **Necho II of Egypt** had pushed up into Syria to claim the
former Assyrian provinces, garrisoning the Euphrates crossings and using **Carchemish**
as a forward base. The Egyptians had besieged the Babylonian garrison at **Kimuhu**
and pressed Babylon back from posts on the river. In **605**, **Nebuchadrezzar** —
crown prince, his father [[nabopolassar|Nabopolassar]] aged and ailing at Babylon —
**took personal command of the whole army** and marched directly on Carchemish, where
the Egyptians had concentrated. He **crossed to the west bank to cut off the Egyptian
line of retreat** and force a battle; the tactic worked, and the retreating Egyptians
were "completely overwhelmed." The survivors were overtaken in the **Hamath** district
and destroyed, so that "**not a single man escaped to his own country**." The
annihilation of Necho's forces in **August** let Babylon impose its hold "swiftly over
the former Assyrian provinces and vassal territories in the west."

The battle is notable for **Greek mercenaries on both sides** — attested by finds at
Carchemish, the fort at Mesad Hashavyahu, and the report that **Antimenidas, brother
of the poet Alcaeus**, fought for Nebuchadrezzar.

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the [[fall-of-nineveh-612bce|destruction of Assyria]], which left its
western provinces a prize contested between Babylon and Egypt; Necho II's bid to
inherit Syria.
contributed_to: Nebuchadrezzar's smooth accession — news of Nabopolassar's death
reached him just after the victory, and he secured the throne while the west lay
prostrate.

## Consequence Analysis

produced: **Babylonian mastery of Syria and the Levant** to the Egyptian frontier
("the king of Egypt did not march out of his country again," II Kings 24:7); the
vassalage of **[[kingdom-of-judah|Jehoiakim of Judah]]**.
enabled: the chain of events leading to the [[fall-of-jerusalem-587bce|destruction of
Jerusalem]] and the Babylonian Exile — Judah's fatal oscillation between Babylon and
Egypt began here.
Though an indecisive open battle near **Pelusium in 601** later checked Babylon at
the Egyptian border, Carchemish had already settled the **strategic question**: the
Near East would be Babylonian, then Persian, not Egyptian.

## Historiography

The event is securely dated and narrated by the **Babylonian Chronicle**, corroborated
by the Hebrew Bible (Jeremiah 46; II Kings 24) and archaeology at Carchemish itself.
Its importance is structural: it marks the moment the [[neo-babylonian-empire|Chaldaean
empire]] became a Levantine power and sealed the fate of the small Syro-Palestinian
states caught between the great powers — the political context of the prophetic
literature treated in Cycle 5.
