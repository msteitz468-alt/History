# The Assassination of John the Fearless

The Assassination of John the Fearless, duke of Burgundy, took place on the bridge at Montereau on 10 September 1419. He was murdered by partisans of the Dauphin Charles (later Charles VII) during what was intended to be a reconciliation meeting.

## Historiography

The assassination is described in both Burgundian and Armagnac-aligned chronicles, and the two traditions diverge sharply. Georges Chastellain and Enguerrand de Monstrelet, writing from within the Burgundian orbit, present the killing as a premeditated ambush by the Dauphin's counselors (Tanguy du Châtel and Jean Louvet chief among the accused), staged under cover of a reconciliation conference; pro-Valois chroniclers such as Jean Juvénal des Ursins instead stress John the Fearless's own record of duplicity (including his complicity in the 1407 murder of Louis of Orléans) as provocation. Whether Charles himself ordered or merely tolerated the killing remains disputed — he was present on the bridge but claimed surprise — and the affair was still being litigated politically decades later, since Philip the Good made Charles's alleged guilt a precondition of the 1435 Treaty of Arras. The episode is textually well attested for a fifteenth-century political murder, but nearly every surviving account was produced by a partisan of one of the two rival Valois-descended factions, so motive and premeditation cannot be established from the chronicle record alone.
