# Stellinga Revolt (841–842)

The **Stellinga** rising of *frilingi* and *lazzi* in Saxony (841–2) is, in [[wickham-framing-the-early-middle-ages-2005|Wickham’s]] comparative peasant history, the **largest peasants’ revolt of the early medieval West**.

## Narrative

Charlemagne’s bloody conquest of tribal Saxony (772–804) and rapid aristocratic/ecclesiastical estate domination (c.790–840) compressed into roughly two generations transformations that England experienced over centuries (c.600–900). Exploiting the Carolingian civil war after 840, the Stellinga sought restoration of pre-Carolingian custom against Saxon aristocrats. Once elite unity returned, the rising was crushed.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` rapid advance of rent-extracting aristocratic/ecclesiastical power after conquest.
- `enabled:` political opportunity of 840–3 civil war.
- `analogous_to:` [[bagaudae-peasant-revolts]] as resistance when hegemony cracks under surplus extraction.

## Consequence Analysis

- Confirmed that regional-scale resistance was a real danger to “feudal” power and had to be crushed for that power to continue (Wickham’s formulation).
- Marks the outer edge of the book’s period but illustrates the **return of feudal dominance** after the high point of early medieval peasant autonomy (Ch. 9).

## Historiography

Treated in Wickham as evidence for the pace and violence of mode-of-production shift under Carolingian conquest, not as a free-floating ethnic rising. See [[peasant-mode-of-production]], [[carolingian-empire]], [[politics-of-land]].
