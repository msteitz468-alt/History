# Cameroons Campaign (1914–1916)

Germany's second West African colony, bounded by British Nigeria and French Equatorial Africa (plus Spanish Muni/ Fernando Po). The campaign exemplified supply crises, allied discoordination, and German preference for preserving colonial investment over European distraction.

## Narrative

German pre-war plan (24 Nov 1913): abandon coast (Duala) for interior northern plateau (Ngaundere); "the loss of the coastal strip... would not represent setbacks of strategic significance." "the stubborn resistance of the Cameroons was motivated not by any German desire to draw Entente troops from Europe... but by the fact that colonization mattered as an end in itself." Ebermaier (governor) assumed economic control 7 Aug 1914; markets kept open, cultivation intensified, self-sufficiency achieved.

Schütztruppen (~205 white + 1,650 black pre-war; expanded to ~1,460 white + 6,550 black) shifted to open-order, active defence, guerrilla style in enclosed terrain. "the askari now had to wage war as his tribal opponents had done." Limited ammunition (ersatz production at Jaunde/Ebolowa).

Allied operations: British (Dobell) maritime focus on Duala (seized early); French (Aymérich) territorial recovery + political primacy. Multiple columns uncoordinated (Morisson, Hutin, Le Meillour, Miquelard; Belgian support). Conferences (Brazzaville Feb 1915, Duala Mar/Aug 1915) set optimistic Jaunde/Lomie objectives never achieved on schedule. "Command was divided over three governments... four governors-general, six independent commanders-in-chief, and eight column commanders."

Major phases: Initial Entente coastal gains; German counter at Edea (Jan 1915); fall of Garua (Jun 1915) and Ngaundere; failed first Dobell advance on Jaunde (May–Jun 1915); second Duala conference; coordinated Oct–Nov 1915 advances; Jaunde entered 1 Jan 1916 (British) / early Jan (others); Germans evacuated south-west to Muni gap before arrival; Ebolowa and final pursuits; Mora surrendered 18 Feb (still had 37k rounds).

"the failure of the Campo and Gabon columns to cut off the German retreat was complemented by the delay in the allied pursuit from Jaunde." ~15,000 crossed to Spanish Muni (1k Germans, 6k askaris, 7k followers). "the loyalty of the Beti of Jaunde to the Germans was the major factor..."

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` pre-war frontier settlements (1911 French, 1913 British) left exposed colony; German decision to fight for colonial claim itself. `contributed_to:` porter crises ("two and three porters for each soldier"); French/British national priorities (territorial vs maritime) producing divergent axes; poor maps/intel; disease (French sickness double British).

`preceded_by:` 19th-c colonial small wars methods.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` German evacuation preserved Schütztruppen as coherent force (unlike total destruction sought); "If Germany had won the war in 1918 it might still have re-established its authority in the Cameroons." Provisional partition (north to British, rest French) split emirates/tribes; "The casualties were the tribes of the Cameroons." Education/medicine collapsed with German withdrawal.

`enabled:` French territorial gains; British focus elsewhere.

Part of global WWI opening; "the African campaigns... bore more relationship to the nineteenth-century campaigns of colonial conquest."

## Actors & Historiography

Ebermaier (civilian inspiration of defence); Zimmerman (frustrated central command); Dobell (British, limited by London strategy); Aymérich/Merlin (French ambitions). Strachan (Range 08) stresses discoordination and supply as decisive over battlefield heroics; "the true answer lay less in the munitions supply than in the higher direction of the campaign." African porters/askaris central; Beti loyalty key to German cohesion in retreat.
