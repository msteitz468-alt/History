# The Mission of Zhang Qian

## Narrative

In 139 BCE [[han-wudi|Emperor Wudi]], determined to break the [[xiongnu]] and seeking the
displaced [[yuezhi]] as an ally, issued an empire-wide summons; [[zhang-qian|Zhang Qian]]
answered and was appointed "envoy to the Yuezhi." The expedition left [[chang-an|Chang'an]]
c. 138 BCE through the Xiongnu-controlled Gansu Corridor and was almost immediately
captured. After **over ten years** in Xiongnu detention, Zhang Qian escaped westward to
**Dayuan (Ferghana)** — the first Chinese known to reach it — whose king, having heard of
Han wealth, provided guides to the Yuezhi. He reached the Yuezhi court c. 128 BCE, but the
king, now secure as ruler of [[bactria|Bactria]] and allied with Kangju, "considered the
Han too far away to bother with" and declined alliance. Zhang Qian spent ~a year gathering
the **first Chinese reports ever** on Bactria, Kangju, Parthia (Anxi), and India, was
recaptured on the return journey, and reached Chang'an c. 126/125 BCE.

In Bactra he had found Chinese goods (Sichuan bamboo and cloth) already on sale, arriving
via a pre-existing "**Great Indian Road**" through Burma and the Ganges — proof that
long-distance China–Central Asia commerce predated the formal Silk Roads. His report also
gave China its first knowledge of the **grapevine** and of the blood-sweating **"heavenly
horses" of Ferghana** (the red sweat now attributed to a parasitic nematode). Wudi's
desire for these horses — tied to an *I Ching* prophecy that "divine horses will appear
from the northwest" — drove two great campaigns to Ferghana in **104 and 102 BCE**; the
second (60,000 men) killed the king of Dayuan and brought back 3,000 horses, of which only
~a dozen were breeding quality. The *Han Shu* says the campaign "shocked and frightened"
the western states, most of which then sent envoys declaring vassalage.

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by: [[han-wudi]]` — Wudi's strategic decision to abandon defensive
[[han-dynasty|heqin]] tribute for an offensive western policy. `caused_by: [[yuezhi]]` —
their migration to Bactria created the would-be ally. `contributed_to:` Benjamin's
deeper chain: the [[xiongnu]] defeat of the Yuezhi c. 166 BCE displaced them, prompting
the search that produced the mission. [CONTROVERSY] Benjamin uses the episode for the
**[[great-man-vs-structural-causation|great-man vs. structural]]** debate — had Zhang Qian
not escaped, "Han expansion into Central Asia might have been stopped in its tracks."

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` the intelligence and diplomatic opening that drew Han power into the Tarim
oases; `enabled: [[silk-roads]]` — Benjamin treats the mission as effectively inaugurating
the eastern Silk Roads, even though the Yuezhi alliance failed. The mission's *failure* was
itself world-historical: had the Yuezhi gone east, "there would have been no
[[kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]]."

## Historiography

Documented in **Sima Qian's *Shiji* ch. 123** (Sima Qian perhaps an eyewitness to the
report) and the ***Han Shu* chs. 61, 96A**. Dates are approximate throughout. Benjamin
foregrounds the *Shiji*'s own candid source-criticism of the corrupt later envoys who
followed Zhang Qian.
