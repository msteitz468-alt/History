# Greek Civil War (1943–1949)

The final major civil war of the 1905–1949 European era, triggered by Axis occupation and developing through resistance infighting, the Dec 1944 Athens insurrection, interim violence, and the 1946–49 "third round" offensive by the Democratic Army (DAG). Communists (EAM/ELAS, later DAG) were defeated with British (1944) then US (Truman Doctrine 1947) intervention; parliamentary regime survived.

## Narrative

Axis occupation (German/Italian/Bulgarian zones) and British blockade caused catastrophic famine winter 1941–42 (~300–400k dead, ~5% population). The KKE organized EAM (National Liberation Front) and ELAS (National People’s Liberation Army); EAM narrower and more directly Communist-controlled than the Spanish Popular Front. First phase ("second round" per some accounts) erupted autumn 1943 when ELAS attacked smaller non-Communist resistance groups (victims nearly equaling German civilian killings in one region). ELAS created OPLA "Cheka" for executions of "reactionaries."

Dec 1944 Athens insurrection ("second round"): ELAS (strongest force) attempted to seize power; murdered at least 2,000 in Athens area (sometimes after torture), seized thousands of hostages. British veteran units from Italy subdued them. Varkiza agreement (Feb 1945): amnesty, ELAS disbanded. Interim 1945–46: National Guard + right-wing volunteers hunted Communists ("White Terror" ~3k leftists killed); remnants trained in Yugoslavia (~5k).

DAG/"third round" 1946–49: Larger operations with Macedonian/Greek Communist forces; conscription + coercion; program similar to Spanish Popular Front (democratic republic, private property). Truman Doctrine (1947) after British withdrawal: US aid decisive. Zahariadis ordered regular warfare 1948; heavy losses, draconian discipline, atrocities both sides. KKE endorsed secession; final retreat to Yugoslavia summer 1949. Last major atrocity: deportation of 25,000 children.

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` Axis occupation creating vacuum + KKE/EAM decision to seize power via superior ELAS numbers (Athens 1944); post-Varkiza repression pushing remnants to armed regrouping.

`contributed_to:` British intervention (1944) and US aid (1947); Communist shift to regular warfare; foreign backing.

`preceded_by:` Axis occupation famine and resistance formation.

## Consequence Analysis

`produced:` Communist defeat; preservation of parliamentary regime (unique among major 20th-c. European cases alongside Finland).

`produced:` Massive civilian toll (famine 300–400k; civil phases ~120k+ total deaths per estimates; greater among civilians than strictly military).

`followed_by:` Cold War alignment; "civil war" designation in 1989–90 reconciliation with equal pensions.

## Actors

- **EAM/ELAS/DAG (KKE-controlled)**: Dominant resistance then revolutionary force; narrower than Spanish Popular Front; OPLA terror apparatus.
- **Greek government + National Guard**: Backed by British (1944) then US (Truman Doctrine 1947).
- **Non-Communist resistance (EDES etc.)**: Attacked by ELAS 1943.

## Historiography

Pre-1974 right-wing dominance; post-1974 left revisionism (four of five books left-wing); later professionalism on violence role (Kalyvas). 1989–90 reconciliation officially termed it civil war with equal pensions. Payne notes Greece won by parliamentary regime (like Finland), last European case paralleling Indochina-style revolution/civil war.
