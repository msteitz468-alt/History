# The Battle of Leuctra, 371 BCE

## Narrative

At **Leuctra**, in Boeotia, in the summer of **371 BCE**, the Theban army under
**[[epaminondas]]** **destroyed the Spartan army** and with it the three-century-old myth
of Spartan invincibility — the single most consequential battle of the Greek fourth
century. [[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 6, Seager) sets it at the climax of the
Theban resurgence: when the **Common Peace of 371** required Thebes to leave the Boeotian
cities autonomous, Epaminondas refused to swear away the Boeotian Confederacy, and Sparta
— "as eager as [[agesilaus-ii|Agesilaus]] to force a show-down" — ordered King
**Cleombrotus**, already in Phocis, to attack.

## The tactics

Outnumbered, Epaminondas won by a revolutionary concentration of force. Against the
convention of putting one's best troops on the right, he **massed the Theban left into a
phalanx fifty shields deep**, aimed directly at Cleombrotus and his Spartiates on the
Spartan right — to "**crush the serpent's head**." Advancing **obliquely** (the rest of
his line held back), and led at speed by **Pelopidas and the Sacred Band**, the deep
column struck the Spartan right before it could complete its enveloping manoeuvre. A
preliminary cavalry skirmish, won by Thebes, disordered the Spartan hoplites further.
**Cleombrotus fell**, and the Spartans were driven back to their camp; their barely
engaged Peloponnesian allies "happily followed." Spartan dead numbered about **1,000,
including 400 of the 700 Spartiates present** — a catastrophic loss for so small a
citizen body.

## Aftermath

At Sparta the ephors ordered the **Gymnopaidia** festival completed and **forbade the
relatives of the fallen to mourn**. The Thebans' message to **Athens** met not joy but
"shock and then dismay" — the *boule* sent the herald away without answer — so Thebes
turned to **Jason of Pherae**, who marched to Leuctra but shrewdly **dissuaded both sides**
from a decisive sequel, preferring a Sparta weak but surviving as a check on Theban
expansion northward. The Athenian-sponsored peace conference that followed (the renewal
in which "the King's Peace had become no more than a name") confirmed that "**the
initiative in Greek affairs now lay... with Thebes**."

## Significance

Leuctra ended the [[spartan-hegemony|Spartan hegemony]] and opened the brief
**[[thebes-hegemony-371bce|Theban hegemony]]**. Its deeper consequence followed within a
year: [[epaminondas|Epaminondas]] invaded the Peloponnese, **liberated Messenia**, and
founded **Megalopolis**, stripping Sparta of the helot base that had underwritten its
power since the Archaic age (see [[sparta]]). Tactically, the **oblique order** and
**deep striking column** broke the symmetrical hoplite phalanx and were studied by Philip
of Macedon — a direct ancestor of the Macedonian battle line.
