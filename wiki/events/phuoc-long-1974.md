# Battle of Phước Long

A PAVN offensive (13 December 1974 – 6 January 1975) that overran Phước Long
Province northeast of Saigon, making it the first provincial capital captured
entirely by North Vietnamese forces since the 1973 Paris Peace Accords. Route
14 was cut, isolated outposts fell piecemeal, and the provincial capital
(Phước Bình) was in PAVN hands by 6 January 1975.

## Narrative

Hanoi framed the operation as a deliberate test case: a limited, reversible
probe of South Vietnamese defenses and, more importantly, of American
resolve. Congress had slashed aid to the Republic of Vietnam and the War
Powers Act constrained President Ford's options; when Washington responded
to the loss of Phước Long with statements of concern but no B-52 strikes, no
naval intervention, and no renewed combat deployment, the North Vietnamese
Politburo concluded the "American card" had been played out.

## Causal Analysis

The battle did not by itself alter the strategic balance — Phước Long was
thinly populated and militarily marginal — but it functioned as a diagnostic
event. Its outcome directly shaped the decision, taken within weeks, to
prepare a larger offensive against the Central Highlands (see
[[vietnam-war]]), which cascaded into the final campaign of 1975.

## Consequence Analysis

Confirmation of non-intervention removed the central variable South
Vietnamese planning had depended on since 1973. Within ten weeks PAVN forces
opened the Central Highlands campaign that led directly to the collapse of
South Vietnam.

## Historiography

Vietnamese Communist accounts (including Văn Tiến Dũng's memoir of the final
campaign) present Phước Long explicitly as a test of American resolve;
American historiography (e.g. Willbanks) treats it as the moment the
credibility of US commitments under the Paris Accords was finally and
irreversibly exposed as hollow. There is little dispute over the facts of
the battle itself; the debate concerns how much earlier US disengagement
(1973 troop withdrawal, the War Powers Act, aid cuts) had already made the
outcome foreordained.
