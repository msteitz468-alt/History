# Assassination of William McKinley (1901)

On 6 September 1901, President William McKinley was shot twice in the abdomen by Leon Czolgosz, a self-described anarchist, while greeting the public at the Pan-American Exposition in Buffalo. McKinley died on 14 September. Theodore Roosevelt was sworn in as President the same day.

## Narrative

McKinley, known for his affability and willingness to shake hands with ordinary citizens, was shot at close range with a revolver concealed in a handkerchief. Emergency surgery was performed; initial hopes faded as gangrene set in. Roosevelt, who had been vacationing in the Adirondacks, rushed to Buffalo.

## Causal Analysis

`caused_by:` Czolgosz's anarchist convictions (inspired in part by Emma Goldman and the assassination of King Umberto I). McKinley's personal decision to hold a public reception with minimal screening. Broader context of labor violence and radicalism in the 1890s.

`contributed_to:` The open, democratic style of the presidency itself.

## Consequence Analysis

The death of the popular, conservative McKinley brought the energetic, reform-minded, and youthful Roosevelt into power years earlier than the party machine intended. It marked a symbolic and substantive turning point toward the Progressive Era.

## Historiography

Contemporary accounts emphasized national mourning and the stability of the succession. Later analyses examine security failures, the medical treatment (X-rays were available but not used effectively), and the political consequences for Roosevelt's agenda. Primary sources include official reports, newspaper coverage, Czolgosz's statements, and TR's own recollections.
