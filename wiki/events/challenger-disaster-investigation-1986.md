# Challenger Disaster Investigation 1986

The presidential commission (Rogers Commission) investigation into the January 28, 1986 explosion of the Space Shuttle Challenger 73 seconds after launch, which killed all seven crew members. Richard Feynman served as a member. His appendix to the final report and his televised demonstration (O-ring in ice water) highlighted the temperature dependence of the seal failure and NASA’s unrealistic probability estimates (“one in 100,000”). His conclusion: “For a successful technology, reality must take precedence over public relations, for nature cannot be fooled.”

## Historiography

The Rogers Commission's published report and Feynman's own appendix and personal account
(later published in "What Do You Care What Other People Think?") are the primary sources;
these are supplemented by NASA internal memos and Thiokol engineer testimony (notably
Roger Boisjoly's warnings) that surfaced during the investigation. Gleick's *Genius*
(1992) provides the standard biographical account of Feynman's role and is generally
reliable but reflects Feynman's own retrospective framing of events. Scholarly and
organizational-behavior literature since has used the disaster as a case study in
"normalization of deviance" (a term associated with sociologist Diane Vaughan's later
archival study of NASA decision-making), which complicates the Commission's more
individualized narrative of managerial failure by emphasizing systemic and cultural
causes. There is little dispute over the technical cause (O-ring failure) but ongoing
debate over how much blame attaches to individual managers versus institutional culture.
