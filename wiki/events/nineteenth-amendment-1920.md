# Nineteenth Amendment (1920)

Constitutional amendment providing that the right to vote shall not be denied or abridged by the United States or any state on account of sex. Known throughout the campaign as the **Susan B. Anthony Amendment**. Proposed by Congress in 1919; ratified 18 August 1920 (Tennessee). Cox's *Light Withdrawn* makes its half-century path — and [[wilson-woodrow|Wilson]]'s long resistance and late, partial conversion — the spine of the Wilson presidency story.

## Narrative

From Reconstruction-era introductions (Sargent, 10 Jan 1878 — language of the 15th Amendment with “sex” substituted) through decades of congressional burial, the amendment reached final passage only after World War I politics, NWP pickets, NAWSA organization, and a belated Wilson appeal.

**House, 10 January 1918.** After a Green Room meeting (9 Jan) in which Wilson told a dozen House Democrats that suffrage was “a policy, not a principle” and “the time has come,” the House passed the Anthony Amendment **274–136** — two-thirds with no vote to spare. Republicans ~83% for; Democrats exactly 50%. Wilson issued no congratulations; the *Official Bulletin* omitted his change of position. Credit war began immediately between parties and between [[paul-alice|Paul]]/NWP and [[catt-carrie-chapman|Catt]]/NAWSA.

**Senate failures, 1918–19.** Expected “within a fortnight,” the Senate instead stalled. John Sharp Williams (D-MS), Wilson’s closest Capitol friend, moved to restrict the amendment to “white citizens.” Wilson’s first public speech for the amendment (30 Sept 1918) labeled it a “war measure”; the Senate still failed by two votes (1 Oct; recorded 53–31 with Jones’s tactical switch). Feb 10, 1919: another defeat (55–29) while Wilson cabled weakly from Paris — “not been a factor.”

**“Adulterating Anthony” (Feb–Mar 1919).** Senator Gay and ally Robert Ewing, with Kate Gordon’s white-only suffrage network, drafted a rewrite stripping federal enforcement so Jim Crow racial barriers would remain untouched (“pull its teeth”). On 27 Feb Wilson **immediately endorsed** the scheme. NAWSA officers and a DNC vote (28–10) backed it. Killed 4 Mar 1919 by unanimous-consent objections; re-offered and rejected 4 June when the original text finally passed.

**Final passage and ratification.** Special session only under fiscal necessity (War Risk payments — Glass). House **304–90** (21 May 1919); Senate two-thirds + two (4 June 1919). Ratification climaxed in Tennessee, 18 Aug 1920 (Harry Burn and his mother’s letter). Non-enforcement for non-white women continued under A. Mitchell Palmer; MS/GA blocked women on registration/poll-tax timing; FL Klan threats. Wilson’s 1922 claim that women were “chiefly indebted to me for the suffrage” is Cox’s epilogue exhibit of retrospective credit capture.
