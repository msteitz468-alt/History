# The Siege of Yorktown (1781)

The decisive engagement of the **[[american-revolution|Revolution]]** — the trap that
[[clinton-henry|Clinton]] had dreaded since 1778, sprung at last by the
[[franco-american-alliance-1778|Franco-American alliance]]. **[[cornwallis-charles|Cornwallis]]**,
having marched out of the Carolinas, fortified Yorktown as a naval anchorage and "gave up the
initiative" to become "a fixed target."

> **Staff-college tactical analysis:** see **[[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/siege-of-yorktown|Siege of Yorktown — Tactical Analysis]]** for the full order of battle, the conduct of the siege, and the principles-of-war assessment.

## Narrative

The allies executed "a masterpiece of coordination." [[washington-george|Washington]] and **Rochambeau**
deceived Clinton, slipped south from New York, and converged with **[[lafayette|Lafayette]]**'s
blocking force and **de Grasse**'s fleet from the West Indies. At the **[[battle-of-the-chesapeake-1781|
Battle of the Chesapeake]]** (5 September) de Grasse drove off the British fleet — the "decisive moment
of the whole campaign" — sealing Cornwallis off by sea. The allied army of **over 16,000** (a French
majority) opened a formal siege: parallels, a remorseless bombardment, and on the night of 14 October the
storming of **Redoubts 9 and 10** (one taken by **Alexander Hamilton**'s light infantry), bringing the
guns within 350 yards of the British line. A sortie and a storm-foiled river crossing failed; on **19
October 1781** Cornwallis's ~7,200 grounded their arms — the general feigning illness and sending O'Hara
to surrender — just as Clinton's relief force set sail.

## Causal Analysis

- `caused_by:` the **French naval superiority** at the Chesapeake — Conway: "without their presence and
  victory over the Royal Navy, Yorktown would not have been possible."
- `contributed_to:` the allied **combined-arms convergence** by land and sea, and Cornwallis's strange
  passivity in letting himself be sealed in.

## Consequence Analysis

- `produced:` the **surrender of a second British army** and the collapse of British will — Lord North's
  government fell, ending major operations.
- `produced:` the **[[treaty-of-paris-1783|Treaty of Paris]] (1783)** recognizing American independence.

## Historiography

The American memory centers Washington; the *West Point History* (Conway) insists the **French were the
indispensable agents** — the majority of the regulars and the whole of the decisive sea power. "The
Continental Army did not win the war in America; it was on the winning side."
