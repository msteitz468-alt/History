# Battle of Moscow (1941)

The **Battle of Moscow** was the German offensive (Operation Typhoon, launched 2 October
1941) to capture the Soviet capital, and the subsequent Soviet counteroffensive that threw
German forces back from the city's outskirts. After early breakthroughs and massive
encirclements around Vyazma and Bryansk, the German advance stalled in late
November/early December 1941 within sight of Moscow, exhausted by autumn mud, worsening
cold for which German forces were poorly equipped, and lengthening supply lines. A Soviet
counteroffensive launched 5 December 1941 under [[zhukov-georgy|Georgy Zhukov]], using
fresh Siberian reserves, pushed German forces back 100–250 km in places by January 1942.

## Consequences

The failure to take Moscow ended Hitler's hopes for a rapid, decisive campaign against the
Soviet Union and forced Germany into the prolonged, resource-draining war on the Eastern
Front that would ultimately prove fatal. [[brauchitsch-walther-von|Walther von
Brauchitsch]], German Army Commander-in-Chief, was dismissed in the aftermath, and Hitler
assumed direct personal command of the army — a consequential shift that removed a layer
of professional military judgment from German strategic decision-making for the rest of
the war.

## Historiography

Soviet-era accounts emphasized the counteroffensive as evidence of Red Army resilience and
Stalin's leadership; postwar Western and post-Soviet historiography (drawing on German
staff records and Soviet archives opened after 1991) has instead stressed German logistical
overreach, the underestimated scale of Soviet reserves, and the role of the harsh winter as
much as any single command decision. The battle remains a standard case study of the
limits of blitzkrieg against a state with sufficient depth and mobilization capacity to
absorb catastrophic early losses.
