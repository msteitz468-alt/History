# Nazi Book Burnings ("Action Against the Un-German Spirit", 10 May 1933)

## Narrative

On 10 May 1933 German students staged a coordinated "act against the
un-German spirit" in nineteen university towns, burning books seized from
libraries and bookshops in public squares. In Berlin, Goebbels joined at the
students' invitation, praising the "strong, great and symbolic act" of
"committing the evil spirit of the past to the flames." Uniform slogans
accompanied each author onto the pyre: Marx and Kautsky ("against class
struggle and materialism"); Heinrich Mann, Glaeser, and Kästner ("against
decadence and moral decay"); Freud (for "debasing exaggeration of man's animal
nature"); Emil Ludwig; Tucholsky and Ossietzky ("arrogance and presumption");
Remarque's *All Quiet on the Western Front* ("against literary betrayal of the
soldiers of the World War"). The students' "twelve theses against the
un-German spirit" declared: "Our opponent is the Jew and anyone who submits to
him."

## Causal Analysis

- Evans establishes the action as **centrally coordinated though not
  Goebbels-directed**: organized by the national students' union with uniform
  nationwide slogans and a Berlin library official's reading list — his
  recurring "apparently spontaneous but actually centrally co-ordinated"
  pattern. Students had been "combing libraries and bookshops... since the
  middle of April."
- preceded_by: the Heidelberg trade-union library burning (12 March) and the
  6 May sacking of Hirschfeld's Institute for Sexual Science (whose ~10,000
  volumes were burned on 10 May; see
  [[nazi-racial-hygiene-program-1933-1939]]).
- part_of: [[nazi-cultural-purge-1933]].

## Consequence Analysis

- Symbolically definitive of the cultural revolution's reception at home and
  of Nazism's anti-intellectual image abroad; contributed_to the émigré wave
  of writers and scholars.
- Evans invokes the 1817 Wartburg Festival book-burning as the students'
  claimed precedent — noting its actual purposes (press freedom, solidarity
  with Poland) "had little in common with its 1933 imitation" — and closes
  with Heine: "Where books are burned, in the end people will be burned too."

## Actors

- German Students' Union (organizer); [[goebbels-joseph]] (Berlin speaker);
  [[hitler-youth]] and SA (participants).

## Historiography

Evans (2003) is the ingested account; his emphasis is on the staged
spontaneity and on the continuity of the "un-German spirit" frame with the
university purges (see [[nazi-cultural-purge-1933]]).

## Shirer (1960) Addition

Shirer (1960) gives ~20,000 books burned at the Berlin rally (Unter den Linden opposite the University) and names the fuller author list on the pyre: German authors included Thomas and Heinrich Mann, Lion Feuchtwanger, Jakob Wassermann, the Zweigs, Erich Maria Remarque, Walther Rathenau, Einstein, Alfred Kerr, and Hugo Preuss (drafter of the Weimar Constitution); foreign authors included Jack London, Upton Sinclair, Helen Keller, Margaret Sanger, H.G. Wells, Havelock Ellis, Freud, Gide, Zola, and Proust. Goebbels's approving quote as given by Shirer: "These flames not only illuminate the final end of an old era; they also light up the new." Reich Chamber of Culture established 22 September 1933 under Goebbels with seven subchambers (fine arts, music, theater, literature, press, radio, film); compulsory membership, with expulsion possible for "political unreliability," effectively barred non-Nazi-approved practitioners from their professions — the institutional follow-through to the burnings. Source: [[shirer-rise-fall-third-reich-1960]].
