# Phocis

Phocis was a mountainous region of central Greece, wedged between Boeotia, Locris, and Aetolia, whose principal claim to panhellenic significance was that it contained [[delphi|Delphi]], seat of the oracle of Apollo, though the sanctuary and its immediate territory came under the jurisdiction of the pan-Hellenic Amphictyonic Council rather than remaining a purely Phocian possession after the First Sacred War (early 6th century BCE), fought to punish the nearby city of Crisa for exploiting pilgrims to the shrine.

Organized loosely as the Phocian League, a federation of small cities, Phocis played a recurring role in the fault lines of Greek interstate politics precisely because of Delphi's prestige and wealth: disputes over control of the sanctuary and the Amphictyony's rulings against Phocis triggered the Third Sacred War (356–346 BCE), in which Phocian leaders seized Delphi's treasures to fund mercenary armies, drawing in Thebes, Thessaly, and ultimately [[philip-ii-of-macedon|Philip II of Macedon]], whose intervention against Phocis won him a seat on the Amphictyonic Council and a decisive foothold in mainland Greek affairs — a key step toward Macedonian hegemony.

Phocis's small size and internal fragmentation meant it was rarely a major independent power, but its custodianship of, and repeated conflicts over, Delphi made it disproportionately significant to Archaic and Classical Greek interstate religious and political life.
