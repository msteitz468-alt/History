# Manching

## Overview

Manching is the key excavated exemplar of late Iron Age oppida development in temperate Europe. Located at the junction of the Danube and Paar rivers (major E-W route and access to Alpine passes), it was chosen for communications and resources (iron ore nearby; reasonable pasture). Occupation from ~200 BCE or earlier; grew to 200 hectares enclosed.

## Features and Development

Initially small undefended open settlement; by end of 2nd c BCE expanded and walled. "The defences were a massive operation, involving the diversion of a stream and the construction of seven kilometres of wall and four gates"; rebuilt at least once; "massive quantities of timber and nails, and stone was brought in from considerable distances" (Ch. 10 box, ~p. 313, cache ~2370s-2380s). Eastern entrance double carriageway for vehicles.

Internal: planned streets "up to ten metres wide... kept open with very little encroachment"; "regular rows of rectangular timber buildings, behind which were zones given over entirely to pits"; "large palisaded enclosures" possibly agricultural/granaries; horse stalling evidence. Zoning of activities: one area wood-workers and leather-workers, another iron-working centre. Glass rings/bracelets manufactured; pottery made; coins minted and used.

Provisioning: large faunal sample (>400,000 fragments); cattle dominant (~40%+ numbers, ~85% meat); pig/sheep/goat less; reflects non-agricultural centre function.

## Significance as Urban Prototype

"Manching clearly had many of the characteristics that would be associated with a town: the physical form of a [large] and permanently concentrated population, with a planned and well-maintained internal layout, standardized types of buildings and zoning of activities; an economic function as a centre of non-agricultural production; and a role as a focus of local and distant exchange" (Ch. 10, ~p. 313, cache ~2260s). "Manching is, moreover, unique in the extent of its excavation and in the opportunity it offers to see the growth of such a site through time" (Ch. 10, ~p. 313, cache ~2270s).

Exemplifies shift where "imports are now found predominantly in the major settlements which formed the centres of the local economy and society" (Ch. 10, ~p. 308, cache ~2160s).

**Grounded claims from Champion et al. 1984 range lines 18501-22500 (Ch. 10):** All above quotes and details from dedicated Manching box and surrounding settlement/production sections. "The site at Manching... is the key to the understanding of this phase of urban development" (Ch. 10, ~p. 310, cache ~2250s).
