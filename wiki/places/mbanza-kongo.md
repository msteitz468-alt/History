# Mbanza Kongo

**Mbanza Kongo** ("the city/capital of Kongo") was the capital of the [[kingdom-of-kongo]], located some 200 km inland from the Atlantic coast in what is today the Uíge Province of Angola. It served as the political, religious, and cultural center of the kingdom from at least the late fourteenth century until the collapse of centralized power after the [[battle-of-mbwila-1665|battle of Mbwila (1665)]]. In 2017 it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Geographic Setting

Situated on a high plateau near the Lukunga River tributary system, Mbanza Kongo commanded routes connecting the interior to both the Atlantic coast (via Soyo province and the Congo River mouth) and the eastern provinces (Nsundi, Mpangu, Mbata along the Inkisi valley). Its elevation and defensibility contributed to its selection as the royal capital.

## Political Function

As the seat of the *manikongo* (king), Mbanza Kongo was the apex of the kingdom's provincial system. The *manikongo* appointed governors (*mani*) of the six major provinces from the capital. The province of **Mpemba**, in which the capital stood, was described by Duarte Lopes (via Pigafetta, 1591) as "the heart and middle of the state of Congo and the origin of the ancient kings." Holding the province of **Nsundi**, immediately to the northeast, was traditionally the seat of the heir apparent — including Afonso I before his accession.

## Portuguese Contact and Christianization

Portuguese navigators first reached Kongo territory in **1483**. By **1491** the *manikongo* Nzinga a Nkuwu was baptized at Mbanza Kongo as João I. Under [[afonso-i-kongo|Afonso I (r. 1509–43)]] Mbanza Kongo was renamed **São Salvador** and became a Christian capital, with a cathedral, churches, and a school for the Kongolese elite. Jesuit and Capuchin missionaries operated here from the sixteenth century onward.

## Archaeology

Archaeological research before the KongoKing project (2012–16) was limited to minor excavations (Bequaert 1940; Esteves 1989; Abranches 1991). Between 2011 and 2015 an international team conducted systematic fieldwork to support UNESCO World Heritage inscription, which succeeded in 2017. The KongoKing project focused on the kingdom's northern provinces (Inkisi valley) rather than Mbanza Kongo itself.

## Historiography

Mbanza Kongo has been the focal point of most earlier Kongo historiography (missionary, colonial, and post-colonial), which tended to reconstruct the kingdom's history primarily from its capital and its Atlantic port of **Mbanza Soyo**. The KongoKing project deliberately "decentred" this capital-centric view by examining the northern provinces, yielding new perspectives on the kingdom's origins and territorial dynamics (Bostoen & Brinkman 2018).
