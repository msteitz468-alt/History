# Caere

A major Etruscan city (modern Cerveteri), one of the twelve cities of the
Etruscan League, located near the Tyrrhenian coast northwest of Rome.

## Geographic and Historical Description

Caere was among the wealthiest and most powerful Etruscan cities, with
extensive maritime trade connections across the Mediterranean and famous
monumental tumulus tombs at the Banditaccia necropolis. It maintained close,
often friendly relations with early Republican Rome, most notably receiving
Rome's sacred objects and Vestal Virgins for safekeeping during the Gallic
sack of Rome (390 BCE), an episode commemorated in the later Roman
institution of the *hospitium* between the two cities. Caere's relationship
with Rome deteriorated in the following century; after a revolt in 273 BCE,
Rome punished the city by annexing half its territory and imposing
*[[civitas-sine-suffragio|civitas sine suffragio]]* — Roman citizenship
without voting rights — a status later applied more broadly across Italy.

## Significance

Caere's role in 390 BCE illustrates the close, interdependent relations
among Etruscan and Latin city-states even as Rome's power grew; its later
downgraded status under Rome exemplifies the incremental, unequal mechanisms
through which Rome absorbed the cities of central Italy over the fourth and
third centuries BCE.
