# Arslantepe

A tell near Malatya in eastern Anatolia that [[liverani-ancient-near-east-2014|Liverani
(2014)]] uses as the exemplary **periphery node** of the fourth-millennium
[[urbanization|urban]] world — a place that adopted the Lower Mesopotamian organisational
model selectively, on its own terms.

## Significance

Already important in the Late Chalcolithic, Arslantepe became in the **Late Uruk** period
"the central commercial junction and political counterpart of Lower Mesopotamian trade."
The archaeologist Marcella Frangipane calls it a **"citadel without a city"** — an
administrative centre supervising a population dispersed across villages and pasturelands.
Its key features for Liverani's argument:

- A substantial **temple complex** and mass-produced stamped *cretulae* (clay sealings)
  contemporary with the Early Uruk phase — proto-urban institutional development in a
  marginal centre, *preceding* the rise of the southern colonies and probably driven by
  early commercial contact with Uruk.
- **Advanced metallurgy**, ahead of Mesopotamia's thanks to proximity to copper sources:
  copper swords and spearheads "of an unparalleled quality."
- It followed the Lower Mesopotamian **temple-centred political model** (no separate
  palace) yet kept local material culture and seal styles — demonstrating *selective*
  adoption of the Uruk organisational package rather than wholesale colonisation.

The best pre-writing **administrative reconstruction** comes from Arslantepe's bullae and
sealing deposits, where cross-referencing seals and impressions reveals who accessed which
warehouse and how often, despite the absence of writing.

## The collapse of the Uruk system

When the Uruk colonial/commercial system collapsed (~Eanna IV–III transition), Arslantepe's
public complex was **destroyed by fire** and replaced immediately by a village of simple
households showing Transcaucasian influences; a high-ranking tomb built on the ruins
probably memorialises the destruction. The site thus records, in one stratigraphy, both the
reach and the fragility of the first urban expansion beyond the alluvial plain.
