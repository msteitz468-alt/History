# San José Mogote

## Overview

**San José Mogote** (SJM) in the northern arm of the [[oaxaca-valley|Valley of Oaxaca]]
is the type site for early Zapotec village life, ranking, craft specialization, and the
oldest known Zapotec hieroglyphic monument. Marcus and Flannery (CHNPA II.1 Ch. 8) treat
it as the unmatched Formative center that preceded — and partially stocked — the
synoikism founding of [[monte-alban|Monte Albán]] ~500 B.C., then continued as a major
secondary center under the Zapotec state.

## Early Formative village origins

After Archaic seminomadic hunting/collecting/incipient agriculture, Oaxaca populations
became sedentary. Standard house: wattle-and-daub 3×5 to 4×6 m (nuclear family),
stamped-earth floors, thatch roofs, spaced 20–40 m with dooryard features.

**House 20** — oldest known structure, dated ~**1900–1400 B.C.** — produced the oldest
ceramics for the area: 262 plain buff-to-brown sherds of hemispherical bowls and globular
jars without paint/slip/plastic decoration (shapes moldable from prior gourd containers).
Maize, beans, and squash were already cultivated for millennia; year-round residence by
best alluvium established de facto rights via houses, storage, and near-house burial of
ancestors.

Between **1400–1150 B.C.**: Valley of Oaxaca ~18–20 hamlets on high water-table alluvium
without sophisticated irrigation. SJM largest: estimated **71–186 persons over 6.8 ha**.
Household units ~300 m². ~**1350 B.C.** first known public building (wattle-and-daub,
orientation 8° west of north, lime-stuccoed interior, platform foundation, altar-like
platform, central lime-filled pit) — analogous to ethnographic egalitarian **“Men’s
Houses”** for ritual (lime possibly for tobacco/Datura); rebuilt on the same spot. Little
rank difference yet between individuals or between Zapotec and Mixtec regions.

## Origins of ranking (1150–850 B.C.)

~40 hamlets/villages in the valley; SJM “downtown” reached **20 ha** with public
buildings and better residences; “**greater San José Mogote**” estimated
**791–1,976 persons over >70 ha** — no peer. Status continuum (not yet true classes
until Protoclassic); analogy to modest Polynesian chiefdoms. Higher-status access to deer
meat, magnetite/marine shell ornaments, mica, high-quality chert; better houses;
slab-lined graves; jade ornaments vs single bead in ordinary mouths. Tomaltepec cemetery:
flexed adult males (12.7%) held 88% of jade beads and 66% of slab coverings.

### Craft wards, Lightning/Earthquake, and long-distance exchange

Pan-Mesoamerican pottery iconography includes two supernaturals Marcus identifies as
**Sky (Lightning)** and **Earth (Earthquake)** — negatively correlated by village and by
residential ward at SJM; only male burials accompanied either type — possibly active
ancestors of male descent lines.

- **Ward A (Lightning):** magnetite mirror craft; trace elements show Oaxaca mirrors
  traded to the Valley of Morelos and **[[san-lorenzo-olmec|San Lorenzo]], Veracruz**
- **Ward B (Earthquake):** heat-treated chert bifaces
- **Ward C:** metamorphic celts

Craft concentration at SJM under “chiefly” auspices monopolizing external exchange;
house-to-house obsidian source frequencies uniform enough to suggest pooling/
redistribution; also pearl oyster, *Spondylus*, stingray spines, conch trumpets,
turtle-shell drums. Public structures now on earthen platforms with dry-laid stone
facing; household figurines for ancestor ritual (*binigulaza* later Zapotec) — never in
public buildings; figurines disappear by Protoclassic as commoners’ ancestors lose
importance.

*contributed_to:* sister-culture / multi-centric readings of Olmec interaction
([[olmec-mother-vs-sister-culture]]) — Oaxaca as peer exporter of finished goods to San
Lorenzo, not one-way recipient of “mother culture” diffusion.

## Middle Formative chiefdom peak (800–350 B.C.)

By 600 B.C. ~85 valley communities; well irrigation from ~900–700 B.C.; drainage ditches
~900 B.C.; by 350 B.C. at least two irrigation systems. Three-tier hierarchy: (1) SJM
**60–70 ha**; (2) handful of ~3 ha sites with public buildings (Huitzo, Tomaltepec);
(3) many 1–3 ha villages without civic-ceremonial functions. SJM peak **650–500 B.C.**
as focus for 20–30 northern valley communities; acropolis on central hill (Structures 28,
19B→19 multi-ton limestone from 5 km quarry; travertine from Fábrica San José —
multi-community labor). Plano-convex “bun” adobes ~850 B.C., rectangular by ~650 B.C.

## Monument 3: earliest Zapotec hieroglyphs

Between Structures 19 and 14, **Monument 3** is the oldest carved monument with Zapotec
hieroglyphs so far known: slain/sacrificed nude prisoner laid as corridor threshold so
entrants tread on the body; blood streamers from chest cavity; between feet the 260-day
calendar name/date **“1 Earthquake.”** Ritual calendar of 13 numbers × 20 day names
later supplied elite names. Evidence of high raiding among Middle Formative rank
societies; staging fortifications after 500 B.C. *produced:* early
[[mesoamerican-writing-systems|writing]] and [[mesoamerican-calendar|calendar]] evidence
in highland Mexico.

## After Monte Albán

Many valley-floor villages including SJM abandoned or shrank when Monte Albán was founded
(~500 B.C.), but Protoclassic surveys still list SJM as a **secondary center**:
**60–65 ha** with palace, ballcourt, up to ~10 temples under the four-tier Zapotec state
hierarchy headed by Monte Albán.

## Why it mattered

San José Mogote is the best single window on the **pre-state** end of the Zapotec
trajectory: sedentary village → ranking → craft monopoly and long-distance exchange →
chiefdom labor mobilization → early writing and martial sacrifice — the necessary
antecedents of Monte Albán’s primary state.
