# Nalanda

Site in present-day Bihar, India, of the great Buddhist monastic university of Nalanda Mahavihara, founded under Gupta patronage in the 5th century CE and flourishing for roughly seven centuries as one of the premier centers of learning in the ancient and medieval world. At its height, Nalanda reportedly housed thousands of monks and students studying not only Buddhist philosophy and scripture but also logic, grammar, medicine, astronomy, and mathematics, drawing scholars and pilgrims from across Asia — most famously the Chinese pilgrim-scholars Xuanzang (7th century) and Yijing, whose detailed travel accounts remain primary sources for its curriculum and daily life.

Nalanda continued to flourish under later Pala dynasty patronage in Bengal and Bihar (8th–12th centuries), remaining a major center of Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhist scholarship even as Buddhism declined elsewhere in India. The monastery and its extensive library were destroyed in the late 12th/early 13th century during the Ghurid/Delhi Sultanate campaigns into Bihar (traditionally associated with Bakhtiyar Khalji, c. 1193–1200), an episode frequently cited — though debated in its precise details — as marking the effective end of institutional Buddhist scholarship in mainland India.

## Significance

Nalanda stands as the preeminent example of institutionalized higher learning in premodern South Asia and a major node in the transmission of Buddhist learning across Asia, and its destruction is often treated as symbolic of the broader decline of Buddhism in India.
