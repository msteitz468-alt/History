# China (the Civilization and the Realm)

The civilization of the **East Asian heartland** — the world's oldest continuous political
tradition, structured for over two millennia by the **succession of imperial dynasties** and
unified, since 221 BC, around the idea of a single realm of "**All under Heaven**" (*tianxia*).
The anchor page for the wiki's China coverage; from John Keay's
[[keay-china-a-history|*China: A History*]].

## Geographic framework

Keay distinguishes a **"cradle," a "core," and a periphery**. The **cradle** is the five
northern provinces of the lower **Yellow River (Huang He)** basin (Henan, Hebei, Shaanxi,
Shanxi, Shandong) — the rich alluvial plain where, by textual tradition, China's earliest
history was enacted. The **core** adds the [[yangzi-river|Yangzi]] basin and the southern
provinces — the seventeen "productive, populous, and long-integrated" provinces of inner China.
Around them lie the eleven **peripheral** territories of sharp contrast and contested
belonging: Manchuria, Inner Mongolia, **Xinjiang** ("the New Territories," largely Turkic
Muslim Uighur), **Tibet** (Xizang/Qinghai), Yunnan, and Taiwan. Most of China's great rivers
run **west to east**, from the high dry uplands toward the moister coastal plains. Keay notes
China is roughly the size of, and at the same latitudes as, the United States.

## The dynastic dynamic

Chinese history has always been periodized by its **dynasties** — "history literally told the
time … dynasties tolled the hours." The establishment of a dynasty (whose rulers reigned by
right of birth) was the ambition of every would-be sovereign; of the ~100 self-declared
dynasties only a few won recognition in the "legitimate" succession. The **five great
dynasties** — [[han-dynasty|Han]], [[tang-dynasty|Tang]], [[song-dynasty|Song]],
[[ming-dynasty|Ming]], and [[qing-dynasty|Qing]] — each lasting three to four centuries, are
the "great plateaux." They are framed by the short but epochal [[qin-dynasty|Qin]] of the
[[qin-shi-huang|First Emperor]] (which founded the imperial system, 221 BC) and the Mongol
**Yuan** (1279–1368). The order rested ideologically on the **[[mandate-of-heaven|Mandate of
Heaven]]** (the right to rule contingent on virtue and cosmic harmony) and on
**[[confucianism|Confucian]]** and later **[[neo-confucianism|Neo-Confucian]]** statecraft.

## Arc of Chinese history

- **Bronze Age origins:** the [[shang-dynasty|Shang]] (the first historically attested dynasty,
  c. 1600–1046 BC; oracle bones, bronze) and the [[zhou-dynasty|Zhou]] (the Mandate of Heaven;
  the Hundred Schools of the Warring States, including [[confucius|Confucius]]).
- **The first empire:** [[qin-dynasty|Qin]] unification (221 BC) and the long
  [[han-dynasty|Han]] consolidation of the imperial-Confucian state.
- **Division and the second empire:** four centuries of disunion, then [[sui-dynasty|Sui]]
  reunification and the cosmopolitan [[tang-dynasty|Tang]] golden age.
- **The Song economic revolution** and its commercial, technological, and Neo-Confucian
  flowering, ended by the Mongol [[mongol-empire|conquest]] (Yuan).
- **The later empire:** the [[ming-dynasty|Ming]] restoration and the [[qing-dynasty|Qing]]
  (Manchu) empire — the largest in Chinese history — to the **1911 Revolution** and the
  twentieth-century upheavals (treated by Spence and the Cambridge volumes).

## Why this realm mattered

China was, for most of recorded history, **the largest and one of the most advanced economies
and states on earth** — the center of an East Asian world order (the tribute system) and the
source of transformative inventions (paper, printing, the compass, gunpowder). Keay's premise:
China's history is "instructive and rewarding as any other — only more so," a difference "of
scale, not character."
