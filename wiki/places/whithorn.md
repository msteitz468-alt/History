# Whithorn (Candida Casa)

A settlement in Galloway, southwest Scotland, traditionally identified as the site of *Candida Casa* ("the white house"), the church and monastic community founded around the early fifth century by [[actors/st-ninian|St Ninian]]. Bede's *Ecclesiastical History* credits it as an early center of British Christianity from which mission to the southern Picts was said to have proceeded, making Whithorn one of the earliest named Christian sites in what became Scotland.

Archaeological excavation has confirmed continuous occupation from the late Roman/sub-Roman period, including imported Mediterranean pottery attesting long-distance trade contacts, and the site developed into an important early medieval monastery and later bishopric. Its precise relationship to the historical Ninian remains debated, as the surviving *Vita* is a much later (eighth-century, Bede-derived) hagiographical composition.
