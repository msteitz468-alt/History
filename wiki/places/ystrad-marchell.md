# Ystrad Marchell (Strata Marcella)

Abbey of the Cistercian order in Powys, Wales, founded in 1170 by Owain Cyfeiliog, prince of Powys (see Gogynfeirdd), who was buried there in 1197. The abbey was colonized from Whitland (Hendy-gwyn) in south-west Wales. Building began c. 1172 on the present site on the Severn plain, 3 km north of Welshpool (Y Trallwng).

## Narrative

Almost nothing now remains above ground of the abbey church and buildings. The church was excavated in 1890, revealing an ambitious plan 62 m long (whether ever completed is open to question). Excavations revealed stone carving of great beauty dating from the early 13th century; one capital with stiff-leaf foliate carving serves as the font at Buttington church nearby.

During the 15th and early 16th centuries the community declined; by 1528 the buildings were in ruins. Abbot John ap Rhys (1527–36) obtained the right to issue an Indulgence to visitors for designated festivals in an attempt to arrest the decline. The two surviving copies are the earliest known printed documents relating to Wales; they are the work of Richard Pynson, a partner and successor of Wynkyn de Worde. The community, though supported by the princes of Powys, was in financial trouble by the 14th century and placed under the supervision of the community at Buildwas in Shropshire.

In its early days the abbey was more powerful and flourishing. The Powys court poet Cynddelw Brydydd Mawr (fl. c. 1155–c. 1195) was threatened with excommunication by one abbot. The abbey of Valle Crucis (Glyn-y-groes) was colonized from Ystrad Marchell in 1200. Ystrad Marchell also had oversight of the small Cistercian nunnery at Llanllugan (one of only two such houses in Wales), noted for its strict observance of the Rule even in the last years before dissolution in the 16th century.
