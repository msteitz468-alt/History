## Geography and Extent

Bernicia was the northern Anglian kingdom centred on the coastal strip between the Tees and the Firth of Forth. Its two principal strongholds were Bamburgh — built by Ida c. 547 and later "named after Bebba, wife of Aethelfrith (HE III, 6, 17)" — and the island of Lindisfarne, used by the Bernician chiefs as an advance base "in their wars against the northern Britons from advance bases at Bamburgh and on the island of Lindisfarne."

## Origins and British Context

The territory that became Bernicia was originally the heartland of the Votadini, a British people whose kingdom "extended originally from the Tees to the Firth of Forth." The Bernician Angles, "representing an intrusive force probably from Anglian communities further south, were establishing themselves in the north from the mid-sixth century under their leader, Ida." Oesa, Ida's grandfather in the genealogies, is said to have been the first of his line to come to Britain. Ida is credited with twelve sons in the king-lists — a figure indicating that many later princely lines traced ancestry to him rather than documenting historical reality.

Bernicia lay at the intersection of multiple competing British kingdoms: to the north and west, Strathclyde (centred at Dumbarton); to the southwest, Rheged (heartland in the Eden valley and Solway borderlands); to the south, the British territory of Elmet (still surviving in Yorkshire place-names). The early Bernician warlords fought all of these, as well as the Picts beyond the Forth and, episodically, the Dalriadic Scots from Irish Dál Riata.

## Bernician–Deiran Relationship

Bernicia was the "war-zone" in contrast to the more settled [[deira|Deira]] to the south. From the outset there seems to have been military cooperation, with Deirans "supporting and giving military aid to this northward thrust of fellow Angles beyond the Wall." The battle of Catraeth (probably Catterick), commemorated in the Welsh poem the Gododdin, may reflect a joint Bernician–Deiran effort against a Votadini-led British force.

[[æthelfrith-of-bernicia|Æthelfrith]]'s annexation of Deira c. 604/5 formalised this relationship as Bernician dominance. Even during [[edwin-of-northumbria|Eadwine]]'s Deiran-based overlordship, the underlying dynamic reasserted itself: "the history of the northern Angles in the seventh century as a whole shows clearly that the Bernicians were the dominant force for most of that time."

## Centre of Power under Oswald and Oswiu

Under [[oswald-of-northumbria|Oswald]] and [[oswiu-of-northumbria|Oswiu]], sons of Æthelfrith by Acha (sister of Eadwine), Bernicia re-emerged as the dominant element of the northern kingdom. "Within the territory of the northern Angles a Bernician leadership had reasserted itself and the political centre of gravity shifted northwards away from the plain of York." Lindisfarne became the episcopal see of the northern church under Bishop Aidan. The Bernician expansion reached Edinburgh (probably captured 638) and Stirling (Iudeu/Giudi, held by the mid-650s).

Under [[ecgfrith-of-northumbria|Ecgfrith]], Bernician power extended beyond the Forth to the kingdom of Fortriu (Picts) until the catastrophe at [[battle-of-nechtansmere-685|Nechtansmere (685)]]. After that defeat, territory south of the Forth was also contested by Strathclyde Britons.
