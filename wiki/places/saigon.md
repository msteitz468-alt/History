# Saigon

Capital of the Republic of Vietnam (1955–1975); site of final surrender 30 April 1975 ending the Vietnam War. See [[events/fall-of-saigon-1975]], [[events/vietnam-war]], [[hubs/warfare/cold-war/vietnam-war/vietnam-war]].

## Geographic Description and History of Control

Located on Saigon River near Mekong Delta approaches; major port and political/economic center. Controlled by French colonial authorities, then State of Vietnam / RVN after 1954 Geneva partition and 1955 referendum. Key US advisory and later combat support hub (MACV, DAO, embassy). Fell to PAVN 30 Apr 1975; renamed Ho Chi Minh City.

## Why It Mattered

Political heart of anti-communist South Vietnam; symbol of US commitment. Dense population, VNAF/ARVN bases (Tan Son Nhut), Presidential Palace. Strategic terminal for final PAVN Ho Chi Minh Campaign. Site of Operation FREQUENT WIND helicopter evacuation (embassy roof iconic). Capture completed reunification.

## Significance Over Time and in 1975

Pre-1975: Center of RVN government under Diem then Thieu; site of coups, protests, Tet attacks (1968). 1975: "By this point, American attention had shifted to the evacuation." Fixed-wing ops at Tan Son Nhut halted by rocket fire; final helicopter lifts from embassy 29–30 Apr amid chaos. "the embassy evacuation was 'the Vietnam War writ small.'" 30 Apr: PAVN tanks entered; Minh surrendered at Presidential Palace. "NVA tanks crashed through the gates of the Presidential Palace in Saigon."
