# Olbia (Pontic)

**Olbia** (Greek "Happy" or "Prosperous") was a Milesian foundation of the sixth century BCE at the mouth of the modern River Bug (Hypanis), about fifty miles east of Odessa. In the [[hellenistic-world|Hellenistic]] period it was a Greek *polis* under constant pressure from Scythian steppe powers — [[thonemann-hellenistic-age-2016|Thonemann's]] northern "encounter" (*The Hellenistic Age*, ch. 5).

## Economy and Scythian Neighbours

Trade was in **furs, slaves, and livestock** with Scythian partners. Relations with the Scythian **Saioi** under *skēptouchoi* and overking **Saitaphernes** took the form of ritual "gifts" (large bags of gold coins) framed as mutual respect, not mere extortion — though when rejected they could trigger royal rage. A long decree of c. **200 BCE** honours the career-benefactor **Protogenes** for cash gifts and loans amid grain shortages, debt, and liquidity crises: he gave 400, 400, and 900 gold pieces in successive crises, and funded wall repairs when a Galatian–Skiroi alliance and hostile Scythian tribes threatened to seize the city as a fort. Slaves, about **1,500 *mixellēnes*** ("half-Greeks") living "beyond the hills," metics, and many citizens fled.

## Half-Greeks and Boundaries

The *mixellēnes* were a non-citizen hinterland group of mixed origin whose loyalty was intermittent. Rural slave labour on the territory was normal (as for many Hellenistic *poleis*). Thonemann notes that on this fringe "real" Greeks were especially keen to distinguish themselves from half-Scythian *Mischlinge* — an apartheid edge-case of the fusion/colonialism debate in [[hellenistic-historiography]].

## Significance

Olbia shows the northern limit of the Greek *polis* system: gift-diplomacy with steppe kings, career euergetism under fiscal stress, and racial-boundary anxiety.
