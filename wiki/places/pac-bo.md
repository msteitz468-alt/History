## Geographic Description

Pac Bo (also Coc Bo or "the Source") is a cave site in the mountainous border region of Cao Bang province, northern Vietnam, near the Chinese frontier (close to Jingxi / Debao area in Guangxi). Limestone cliffs, dense jungle, streams; remote Nung-minority area with escape routes into China. Harsh conditions: cold, damp cave, limited food (rice + stream fish/minced meat), exposure to patrols.

## History of Control and Settlement

Used as revolutionary base 1941 onward. Ho Chi Minh (Nguyen Ai Quoc / Ho Chi Minh) and small group arrived early February 1941 after border trek from Jingxi. Established in cave behind rock; named adjacent stream "Lenin" and overlooking peak "Karl Marx." Served as headquarters for ICP external leadership and 8th Plenum (May 10, 1941). Later training site for cadres; Giap units operated from nearby Viet Bac bases. Ho returned here multiple times (e.g., after China imprisonment, 1944). Site of early armed propaganda brigade formation (Dec 1944) and symbolic first VLA actions.

## Why It Mattered

Strategic: isolated yet near Chinese border for supply/training/contact; sympathetic local Nung population; defensible with escape routes. Symbolically central to 1941 Viet Minh creation and 1945 August Revolution preparation (Tan Trao nearby as later HQ). Hosted key decisions: broad united front, timing of insurrection, OSS liaison handoff. Embodied the "base area" concept (Mao-influenced, Lenin Stream naming).

## Significance Over Time

Post-1945: iconic in DRV historiography as cradle of revolution ("Uncle Ho" cave). Remained in liberated zone. Today preserved memorial site in Vietnam. Significance shifted from clandestine HQ to national origin myth.
