# Deheubarth

A major medieval Welsh kingdom covering much of southwestern Wales, formed in the 10th century through the union of the smaller kingdoms of Seisyllwg and Dyfed, traditionally under Hywel Dda ("Hywel the Good"), who is also credited with an early codification of Welsh law. Deheubarth reached its greatest strength in the 12th century under Rhys ap Gruffudd ("The Lord Rhys"), who skillfully balanced resistance to and accommodation with Anglo-Norman marcher lords and the English crown while presiding over a notable Welsh cultural revival, including the first recorded eisteddfod (Cardigan, 1176). After Rhys's death (1197) the kingdom fragmented among rival heirs and was progressively absorbed by Anglo-Norman marcher expansion, disappearing as an independent polity well before Edward I's final conquest of Wales in 1282–83.
