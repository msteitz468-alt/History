# Kültepe (Kanesh)

The mound of **Kültepe**, near Kayseri in central Anatolia — the ancient city of
**Kanesh (Neša)** — and the hub of the [[old-assyrian-trade|Old Assyrian trade
network]]. Its lower-town merchant quarter has yielded **tens of thousands of
cuneiform tablets**, the largest and earliest dense commercial archive in the
ancient world, making Kanesh the place where **Anatolia first enters written
history**. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]] (Ch. XXIV(b)).

## The site

Kültepe has two parts: the **mound proper** (the *höyük*), seat of the native
prince's palace (*ekallum*), and the **lower town** in the surrounding plain, where
the **Assyrian merchant colony (kārum)** lived and kept its archives. This physical
separation — foreign traders *beside* but not *within* the local citadel — is the
model of the whole [[old-assyrian-trade|kārum system]]. A dagger inscribed "palace
of **Anitta**, the prince" was found on the mound, tying the site to the early
Anatolian dynasts.

## The archive

The Kültepe tablets (Old Assyrian "Cappadocian" texts) are overwhelmingly
**commercial and legal**: promissory notes, caravan manifests, partnership and loan
contracts, letters between Ashur head-offices and their Anatolian branches, and
marriage/divorce deeds. They are dated by the annual **eponym (limmu)**, allowing
the trade to be reconstructed almost year by year (see [[assyrian-king-list]] and
[[old-assyrian-trade]]). They reveal the great merchant houses (**Pushu-ken**,
**Imdi-ilum**, **Enlil-bani**), the tin-and-textile-for-silver trade, the
dissoluble local marriages of resident Assyrians, and a richly **mixed population**
of Hittites, Luwians, Hurrians, Assyrians, and Amorites.

## Native rulers and the rise of the Hittites

Kanesh was ruled by **native Anatolian princes** who swore vassal oaths to "the
City" (Ashur). It was, notably, the one Anatolian town whose prince was at one point
a **Semite**, and whose patron god (**Anna**, father of Amurrum) was West Semitic —
hints of a long-established Amorite community. Out of this milieu rose **Pitkhana of
Kushshara** and his son **Anitta**, who took the title "great prince" and destroyed
**Hattusha** — figures whom Hittite tradition placed at the head of their royal
line. Kanesh's own name, **Neša**, gave the Hittites their self-designation
("Nešili," the language of Neša).

## Significance

Kültepe is doubly pivotal: as the **best-documented commercial economy of the early
second millennium**, and as the **threshold of recorded Anatolian (and Hittite)
history**. It transforms the [[early-bronze-age-anatolia|inferred EBA metal economy]]
into a documented system and supplies the immediate background to the Hittite Old
Kingdom.

## Historiography

The archive's commercial bias means Kanesh is far better known as a *market* than as
a *polity*: its political history is pieced together from incidental references in
business documents (the eponym dates, the oaths, the divorce records mentioning
Pitkhana and Anitta). Dating rests on the Assyrian eponym canon and merchant
genealogies. See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
