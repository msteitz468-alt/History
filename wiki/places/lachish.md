# Lachish

**Lachish** (Tell ed-Duweir) was the **second city of the [[kingdom-of-judah|Kingdom of
Judah]]** — a 30-acre fortified mound in the Judean Shephelah guarding the approaches from
the Philistine coastal plain to the hill country and Jerusalem. In
[[sources/dever-beyond-the-texts-2017|Dever's *Beyond the Texts* (2017)]] it receives the
fullest treatment of any single site, because it preserves "the most thoroughly documented
prolonged siege and destruction in the entire ancient Near East" — the Assyrian capture of
701 BCE under [[sennacherib-siege-jerusalem-701bce|Sennacherib]].

## The Judahite royal fortress (Levels V–II)

- **Level V** — the first Israelite/Judahite establishment (perhaps late tenth century),
  with the red-slipped, hand-burnished pottery of the early monarchy.
- **Level IV** (ninth century) — "a 30-acre fortress, planned and constructed on a grand
  scale": a solid masonry city wall ~20 ft thick, an outer revetment and glacis, and the
  largest four-entryway gate of the period. Its dominant feature is **Podium B–C**, the
  palace-citadel platform — "the largest Iron Age structure ever found in Israel" (~120 × 250
  ft) — fronting tripartite pillared storehouses. The palace alone took up nearly a quarter
  of the site. The adjacent **Great Shaft** (80 × 80 ft, 75 ft deep) was probably the
  construction quarry.
- **Level III** (eighth century, to 701) — reused the monumental architecture; yielded
  **407 [[hezekiah|lmlk]] stamped jar handles**, the single largest concentration anywhere,
  marking it as a hub of Judah's royal provisioning system on the eve of the Assyrian
  invasion.
- **Level II** (seventh century) — a smaller, partly unwalled town rebuilt after a gap; its
  destruction in 586 BCE preserved the **Lachish ostraca** (23 Hebrew letters found near the
  outer gate), documenting the final days of Judah before the
  [[fall-of-jerusalem-587bce|Babylonian conquest]].

Dever: "Lachish IV–III is without doubt a Judahite royal fortress, as all authorities agree."

## The siege of 701 BCE

The destruction of **Level III** is the period's central archaeological event, attested from
three independent directions: the excavated remains, the **Lachish reliefs** carved for
Sennacherib's palace at Nineveh (now in the British Museum, "depicting the siege and the fall
of the city in gruesome detail"), and the Assyrian annals — while the Hebrew Bible "dismisses
the whole incident in one verse."

The Assyrian **siege ramp** at the south-west corner — built of up to **19,000 tons of stone**
and partly paved for the iron-clad wheeled battering rams shown on the reliefs — is the
oldest siege ramp known. The defenders raised a hasty **counter-ramp** inside; in this zone
excavators recovered hundreds of iron arrowheads, stone ballista balls up to **400 pounds**,
Assyrian armour scales, helmets, and horse ornaments, and 400+ lmlk handles. The gate
chambers were "found filled to the top with burnt mud-brick destruction debris," and on the
western slopes **mass graves of some 1,500 individuals** — men, women, and children — were
heaped in caves, evidently victims of the assault.

## Significance

- `part_of:` the [[kingdom-of-judah|Kingdom of Judah]] as its principal Shephelah stronghold.
- `produced:` the **Lachish reliefs** and **Lachish ostraca** — two of the richest bodies of
  evidence for Iron Age Judah, one Assyrian and visual, one Judahite and textual.
- The fall of Lachish in 701, not the sparing of Jerusalem, is the archaeologically dominant
  fact of [[sennacherib-siege-jerusalem-701bce|Sennacherib's campaign]]; Jerusalem survived
  while its second city was annihilated.
