# South Asia (the Indian Subcontinent)

**South Asia** — the Indian subcontinent, bounded by the Himalayas and the sea — is the regional
stage for one of the world's oldest continuous civilizations. This anchor page summarises the
**geographic and structural framework** that organises its history to 1526; the polities, periods,
and processes have their own pages (linked below). Documented for the wiki through Romila Thapar's
[[thapar-history-of-india-v1-1966|*A History of India*, Vol. I]] (1966).

## The defining geographic divide

Thapar's organising fact is the **contrast between north and peninsula**. The vast, fertile
**Indo-Gangetic plain** lent itself to large unitary, land-revenue-based empires (Magadha → Maurya →
Gupta → Sultanate), open to invasion through the **north-western passes**. The **peninsular south**,
cut by plateaux and rivers into smaller regions and oriented to the **sea**, produced more durable
regional polities, a maritime economy, and the distinct **Tamil/Dravidian** civilization. The
**Deccan** plateau between them served as the perennial **bridge** (and battleground) transmitting
ideas and armies north–south. This north–south interplay, and the recurring tension of **orthodoxy
and heterodoxy** (Brahmanism vs. Buddhism/Jainism/Bhakti) and of **caste and its outsiders**, run
through the whole narrative.

## Sequence of historical control (to 1526)

- **Prehistory–Bronze Age:** the [[indus-valley-civilization|Harappan]] cities (c. 2500–1500 BCE).
- **[[vedic-period|Vedic age]]** (c. 1500–600 BCE): Indo-Aryan acculturation; the origins of
  [[caste-varna|caste]] and Vedic religion.
- **Archaic–Classical:** the [[gana-sangha|republics]] and kingdoms; [[magadha|Magadha's]] rise; the
  [[mauryan-empire|Mauryan empire]] and [[ashoka|Ashoka]].
- **Post-Mauryan:** [[kushan-empire|Indo-Greeks/Shakas/Kushanas]], the [[satavahana-empire|Satavahanas]],
  the [[sangam-age-south-india|Sangam-age Tamil south]]; the mercantile and [[indian-ocean-trade|Roman-trade]]
  boom; the [[indianization-of-southeast-asia|Indianization of Southeast Asia]].
- **Classical & early-medieval:** the [[gupta-empire|Guptas]] and [[harsha|Harsha]] in the north; the
  [[pallava-chalukya-rashtrakuta|Pallavas/Chalukyas/Rashtrakutas]] and the [[chola-empire|Cholas]] in
  the south; the [[rajputs|Rajputs]] and [[indian-feudalism|"Indian feudalism"]].
- **The coming of Islam:** [[mahmud-of-ghazni|Mahmud's raids]], then the [[delhi-sultanate|Delhi
  Sultanate]] (1206–1526), the [[bhakti-movement|Bhakti–Sufi]] ferment, and [[vijayanagara-bahmani|Vijayanagara
  and the Bahmani]] in the Deccan — to the eve of the Mughals (Vol. II).

## Why this region mattered

South Asia was a **hearth of world religions** ([[hinduism|Hinduism]], [[buddhism|Buddhism]],
[[jainism|Jainism]], later Sikhism), a pillar of **Eurasian trade** (the gold-draining Roman commerce;
the [[indian-ocean-trade|Indian Ocean]] networks), the **cultural donor** to Southeast Asia, and the
home of distinctive social institutions (caste) and political-economic forms whose study (Thapar
insists) requires looking beneath dynasties to society, economy, and religion.
