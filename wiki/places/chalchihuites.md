# Chalchihuites

## Overview

**Chalchihuites** names an archaeological tradition of Zacatecas/Durango spanning the
**Classic into the Postclassic** (not to Spanish Contact) — villages, ceremonial
centers, strongholds, and mines that fed blue-green stones, turquoise processing, and
long-distance traffic between Central Mexico and the U.S. Southwest. Gorenstein (CHNPA
II.1 Ch. 7) places it at the heart of West/NW Mexico’s role as a **trade and mining
corridor** within [[greater-mesoamerica|Greater Mesoamerica]].

## Suchil and Guadiana branches

**Suchil branch** (Zacatecas Classic): Canutillo, Vesuvio, **Alta Vista**; Calichal
transitional; Retoño Postclassic. Settlement types include villages, ceremonial centers
(Alta Vista, Cerro de Moctehuma), strongholds, and mines. Sequence begins as egalitarian
agricultural; at Alta Vista, pyramids/patios, adobe, and elaborated ceramics appear.

**Weigand** roots these changes in the **mining industry**: blue-green stones
(chrysocolla/malachite), cinnabar, rhyolite, chert, ocher — km-scale tunnels and
specialized tools. Colorado–Suchil drainage survey: 8 mining areas, ~**750 mines** over
~400 years. Central Mexico was a major consumer. Alta Vista was both a local extractive
economy and a **port of entry/processing/staging ground** for chemical turquoise from
the north (Southwest/Coahuila) traded south and to Central Mexico.

**Guadiana branch** (e.g. Schroeder, Navacoyan): ballcourt, copper items, and related
Postclassic material culture after Suchil simplification.

Neighboring **Loma San Gabriel** (eastern Sierra Madre foothills, Durango; Weicker
site) — small hamlets/villages on elevated loci from ~200 B.C.; mixed farming/gathering/
hunting; mostly plainware village culture historically linked to Tepehuan; interacted
with Chalchihuites from the Middle Classic.

## La Quemada

**La Quemada** (Malpaso–Juchipila, south-central Zacatecas) is an extravagantly
fortified ceremonial center often placed within the Chalchihuites tradition. Trombold
dates major occupation ~**A.D. 600–800** — high population, defense/citadel, communal
terracing, extensive roads. Scenario: southern stratified agriculturalists migrate north
in Middle–Late Classic; “bottleneck” at the agricultural limit vs hostile sierra →
intensive terracing and citadel; long-range trade (Pacific Coast, Alta Vista, south);
intermediary Southwest–heartland role.

**Ben Nelson and Peter Jiménez Betts:** florescence is an **Epiclassic West Mexican**
phenomenon, **not** tied to the apogee of Central Mexican states. Abandonment of La
Quemada vicinity and the Suchil branch ~**A.D. 850–900** amid environmental/productivity
crisis.

## Corridor position

Inland route Classic–Middle Postclassic: **La Quemada → Chalchihuites → Schroeder →
Zape → [[casas-grandes-paquime|Casas Grandes]] → Southwest**, with La Quemada directing
much turquoise flow into Central Mexico. Teuchitlán (Jalisco) is linked in Kelley/
Weigand models to Chalchihuites/Alta Vista mining and redistribution.

## Why it mattered

Chalchihuites demonstrates that northern mesa mining, fortification, and Epiclassic
urbanism were engines of Greater Mesoamerican exchange — not passive receivers of
Teotihuacan or later Central Mexican “high culture.”
