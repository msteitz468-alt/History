# Chiapas Highlands

## Overview

The **Chiapas highlands** of southeastern Mexico form the northwestern wing of the highland Maya world treated by **W. George Lovell** (CHNPA II.2 Ch. 21) together with the [[guatemala-highlands]]. At contact the region held Zoque, Chiapanec, Tzotzil, Tzeltal and related societies; colonial Spanish settlement centered on **Ciudad Real** (modern **San Cristóbal de las Casas**). After independence, Chiapas was the **only** unit of the former Audiencia de Guatemala (since **1561**) to remain Mexican.

## Conquest and colonial geography

Luis Marín’s February **1524** expedition was reconnaissance: easy passage through Zoque country (middle Grijalva); battle with Chiapanecos at present **Chiapa de Corzo**; Zinacantán; Tzotzil engagement at **Chamula**; return toward Tabasco without pushing through Tzeltal/Coxoh country. Effective domination of the central highlands ~**1528** under Pedro Portocarrero and Diego de Mazariegos (meeting at **Comitán**). See [[conquest-of-guatemala-1524]].

Territory initially under New Spain, then (**1530–1821**) Guatemala, except self-government of “Chiapa” **1540–44**. **Dominican** missionary hegemony prevailed in Chiapas. Eighteenth-century **repartimiento** abuses (forced cotton spinning/weaving into *mantas*) were especially rampant among Tzeltal, Tzotzil, and Zoque under alcaldes mayores of Ciudad Real.

## Demography (Gerhard via Lovell)

Indian population of Chiapas: ~**275,000** at contact (**1511**) → **125,000** (**1550**) → **85,000** (**1600**) → **70,000** (**1650**) → **72,000** (**1700**) → **65,000** (**1750**) → **53,000** (**1800**) → ~**58,000** at independence (**1821**). Soconusco coastal plain (linked conquest corridor and later export zone): **80,000** (**1511**) → **7,000** (**1550**) → **4,000** (**1650/1821**). Modern highland Maya of Chiapas (Table 21.1): **160,000** (**1950**); **390,000** (**1980**); **617,250** (**1990**); Jan Rus **847,751** indigenous (**1990**, includes non-Maya). Language groups (Table 21.3): Tzeltal, Tzotzil, Tojolabal; Zoques linguistically closer to Mixe than Maya.

## Resistance and national-era transformation

**Tzeltal Revolt 1712–13** — only full-scale native colonial uprising in the regional frame: Cancuc visionary leadership; **>20** towns; armies of **3,000–6,000**; crushed by Tabasco and Guatemala militia; ringleaders executed. Nineteenth century: Liberal/Porfirian land seizures (Larraínzar/Nuevo Edén at Chamula; all 25 intact Tzotzil/Tzeltal townships suffered); **>1/4** of Chiapas Indians became permanently obligated peons within ~two decades; foreign capital in Soconusco coffee and Usumacinta logging worked by indentured highland labor. Post-Revolution Cárdenas-era contracts, unions, and partial **ejido** restitution consolidated continuous Indian control in the central highlands (Collier) — contrast with Guatemala’s lack of equivalent reparation. Late-20th-c. PRI clientelism, austerity, NAFTA, and **1992** Article 27 rollback fed the **Zapatista** uprising of **1 January 1994** (primarily a peasant rebellion with Maya participation and native-rights address — Collier & Quaratiello via Lovell).

## Why it mattered

Strategic corridor between central Mexico and Guatemala; cacao/feather/cochineal frontier of Aztec knowledge; colonial Dominican and repartimiento laboratory; modern symbol of indigenous and peasant protest in Mexico. Prehistoric southern Maya development (obsidian, Pacific links) is treated under [[maya-highland-pacific-development]]; colonial–modern survival under [[highland-maya-colonial-modern]].
