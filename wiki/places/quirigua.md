# Quiriguá

## Geographic description

**Quiriguá** is the principal Classic Maya center of the **lower Motagua Valley**, Guatemala, roughly **50 km north of [[copan|Copán]]** via the Sesesmil/Motagua corridor. It sits on the Motagua jade/obsidian exchange corridor of the Motagua Maya region (research foci: Copán Valley, middle Motagua/San Agustín Acasaguastlán, lower Motagua/Quiriguá).

## Historical significance

### Early Classic and flood interruption (Evans & Webster 2001)

Early Classic ceremonial center on the northern margin of the later core (Groups **3C-7/8**, **A.D. 400–500**). **Monument 26** (**A.D. 493**) names an early ruler and two predecessors. Political growth was interrupted **A.D. 500–650** by Motagua flood burial of constructions.

### Subordination to Copán and the 738 “axe-event”

Quiriguá’s **Altar 12 (L)** contains a reference to **Smoke Imix God K** (Copán Ruler 12), strongly suggesting subordination to its southern neighbor by **A.D. 672**. Copán Ruler 12 participated in rituals on Quiriguá Altar L. In **738**, Quiriguá’s best-known ruler **Cauac Sky** defeated, captured, and ultimately sacrificed Copán’s thirteenth ruler **18 Rabbit** (Waxaklahun Ubah K’awil) in an “axe-event” — ending Copán hegemony over the site. Copán’s sixteenth ruler **Yax Pac** later took part in Quiriguá rituals late in his career (**810**).

### Independence and Terminal Classic

Post-**737** independence under Cauac Sky brought monumental construction, with stelae surpassing some Lowland heartland heights. Domain was limited by **≥five** competing lower Motagua polities (Las Animas, Quebrada Grande, Las Quebradas, Playitas, Choco) within ~**23 km**. Last named ruler **Jade Sky** took office **A.D. 800**. Terminal Classic contacts include **Plumbate** and **Chac Mool**. The site was abandoned in the **thirteenth century**.

Lower Motagua neighbors used cobble/schist terraces and plain stelae/spheres — a different tradition avoiding Lowland Maya forms; Quiriguá may have been a ceramic/exchange nexus between Maya and non-Maya SE styles.

## Why it mattered

Quiriguá is the textbook case of a **secondary Motagua center overturning a larger neighbor**, and a key node on the [[southeast-mesoamerican-frontiers|southeastern Mesoamerican frontier]] where Classic Maya elite culture met multiethnic lower Motagua polities.
