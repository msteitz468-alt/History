# Mahdiyya

**Mahdiyya** (modern Mahdia, Tunisia) was the **first purpose-built [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] capital**, a
fortress-city on the Tunisian coast founded by [[al-mahdi-billah|al-Mahdi]] and named after him.

## A defensible maritime capital

Seeking a site that would make him and his heirs "invulnerable," al-Mahdi chose a **peninsula joined to the
mainland only by a narrow isthmus** and built the city between **916 and 919**. A massive stone wall — over
8 metres thick on the land side, with iron gates — sealed the neck; inside stood the congregational mosque
(notably **without a minaret**, a model for later Fatimid architecture) and two facing palaces separated by an
open square, the ***Bayn al-Qasrayn*** ("Between the Two Palaces"), an arrangement later reproduced at
**[[mansuriyya|Mansuriyya]]** and **[[cairo|Cairo]]**. A war-harbour cut into the rock, closed by a chain and
guarded by two towers, sheltered more than thirty ships beside a shipyard; a mint and the central treasury
(*bayt al-mal*) served the state, while the commercial populace lived in the suburb of **Zuwayla**. The
geographer **Ibn Hawqal**, visiting c. 947, praised its palaces, clean houses, bathhouses, and abundant produce.

## The great siege

In **945** the city withstood the climactic **siege by [[abu-yazid-al-nukkari|Abu Yazid]]** (20 January – 16
September): with almost all Ifriqiya overrun, al-Mahdi's walls proved unassailable and saved the dynasty. See
[[khariji-revolt-of-abu-yazid-943]].

## Significance

Mahdiyya embodied the early Fatimids' **maritime, defensive** orientation — a Carthage-like sea-city, capital
until al-Mansur built Mansuriyya. Its harbour kept it strategically important to the Zirids and later rulers
for centuries, and its plan prefigured the Fatimid Cairo.
