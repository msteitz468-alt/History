# Sherborne

Sherborne (Dorset) and its ancient public school (founded 1550, revived under headmaster Nowell Smith in the early 20th century) was the site of Alan Turing’s secondary education (1926–1931). The school embodied the English public-school ideal of “esprit de corps,” seniority, games, fagging, and character formation through hierarchy and collective ritual — values often in tension with Turing’s solitary, scientific, and non-conforming temperament.

## Significance in Turing’s Life

Turing arrived during the 1926 General Strike (cycled from Southampton). He struggled with the system (poor coordination, untidiness, aversion to organised games and languages, clashes with housemasters), but found space for mathematics and science. The friendship with Christopher Morcom developed here. Reports described him as “not a ‘normal’ boy” with “his own furrow to plough.” He eventually became a prefect, discovered running stamina, won academic distinctions, and secured a King’s College Cambridge scholarship.

The experience is emblematic of the public-school world that both shaped and marginalised him.
