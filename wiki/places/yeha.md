# Yeha

**Yeha** is the principal site of pre-Aksumite civilisation in the northern Horn, located in the Tigray highlands approximately 55 km northeast of Aksum. It is the site of the **Great Temple of Yeha**, the best-preserved pre-Aksumite monumental structure in Ethiopia, dating to approximately the fifth century BCE.

## Overview

The Great Temple of Yeha is a peristyle temple built of carefully dressed stone without mortar, exhibiting strong South Arabian architectural affinities (particularly with temples in the Sabaean kingdom of South Arabia). Associated inscriptions in a South Arabian script (Sabaean) and South Arabian-influenced material culture have generated debate about whether Yeha represents a South Arabian colony in the Horn or indigenous development with selective adoption of South Arabian cultural elements.

Phillipson's *Foundations* (2012) argues for the latter: Yeha was an indigenous elite centre that selectively adopted South Arabian script, religious iconography (the bull, ibex, and moon-god cult), and monumental architecture, while remaining fundamentally part of the local northern Horn cultural system. The site demonstrates that complex ceremonial architecture and literate elites existed in the northern Horn before the first millennium BC.
