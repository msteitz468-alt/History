# The Qinling Mountains

The **Qinling** — an east–west range of roughly 600 miles across central China — is the
**principal climatic and cultural divide** of the [[east-asian-heartland-region|East Asian
Heartland Region]], separating its northern and southern worlds. From
[[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook (2017)]].

## A Climate Barrier

The range acts as a **barrier to two opposed wind systems**: the southern summer **monsoon** is
wrung out against its southern slopes, leaving the **northern slopes dry**. The result is two
sharply different cultural zones — a **northern zone** of continental climate, loess soils, and
**millet/wheat/soybean** farming with compact, heavily walled settlements and road travel; and a
**southern zone** of monsoon rains, **rice** paddies, and boat travel. The Qinling is thus the
physical reason the [[yellow-river-valley|Yellow River]] and Yangzi worlds developed distinct
agrarian regimes.

## Significance

By dividing the Heartland into millet-north and rice-south, the Qinling shaped the diverging
Neolithic trajectories that [[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Liu (2004)]] and
[[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook]] trace, and it remained a strategic and ecological
boundary throughout Chinese history (the headwaters and passes between the Wei valley "within the
passes" — the base of [[qin-dynasty|Qin]] and Western [[zhou-dynasty|Zhou]] — and the south). The
range is also a major **biodiversity refuge** (the giant panda's heartland).
