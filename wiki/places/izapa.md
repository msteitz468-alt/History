# Izapa

## Overview

**Izapa** on the Pacific piedmont/plain of Chiapas is a major Late Preclassic center whose dense **stela-altar sculptural program** is central to debates on the origins of Maya writing, kingship imagery, and mythic narrative art. Robert Sharer (CHNPA II.1 Ch. 10) and Richard Adams (CHNPA Ch. 1) place it among the precocious Pacific centers — with Abaj Takalik and El Baúl — that prospered from **cacao** and control of trade routes transecting the coastal plain.

## Sculpture and tradition (Sharer)

Izapa has one of the **largest single assemblages of Preclassic stelae and altars without accompanying glyphic texts**, generally considered part of the **Mixe-Zoquean** tradition. Sculptural themes reinforce **ruler authority**. By contrast, the earliest monuments uniting **text and carved representations** concentrate elsewhere in the southern Maya Area: El Portón Monument 1 ~400 B.C.; [[kaminaljuyu|Kaminaljuyú]] Stela 10; Chalchuapa Monument 1; Abaj Takalik Stelae 2 and 5 with Long Count dates (Stela 5 latest = **A.D. 126**); El Baúl Stela 1 Long Count = **A.D. 36**. These early Maya-style personages and dates show that ultimate origins of Maya sculptural style lie even earlier in the Preclassic.

Adams notes NWAF work at Izapa: regional center with many religious sculptures/monuments but **practically none political** — contrasting Abaj Takalik’s religious *and* political monuments with Late Preclassic Maya-style dates and ruler depictions. Scale: Adams/Sanders place Kaminaljuyú an order of magnitude larger than Izapa and Abaj Takalik.

## Regional network

- **Jaguar Altar** style links at [[quelepa|Quelepa]] show Izapan/Kaminaljuyú connections into eastern El Salvador (Sheets).
- **Usulután** resist ware, developed in the southeastern Maya area, was manufactured and imitated across the southern Maya Area by Late Preclassic and reached El Mirador, Tikal, Chiapa de Corzo elite tombs, and as far as Costa Rica (Sharer) — a material marker of interregional unity encompassing Izapa’s world.
- Middle Preclassic monumental sculptures of elite leaders from Chiapas through southern Guatemala to western El Salvador suggest a Pacific–Isthmus–Gulf communication network (Sharer).
- After Terminal Preclassic southern decline (Ilopango shock ~A.D. 250), Pacific focus shifted; later Classic coastal power centers include Balberta and Cotzumalhuapan-style sites (Bilbao, El Baúl).
