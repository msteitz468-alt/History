# Punt

**Punt** (Egyptian *Ta-netjer*, "God's Land") was the distant southern trading partner from
which Egypt obtained its most prized exotic goods — above all **aromatic resins** (myrrh and
incense) — across more than a thousand years of expeditions. It is the classic Egyptian
"land of marvels," and one of the enduring geographical puzzles of Egyptology.

## Trade with Egypt

Egyptian missions to Punt are attested from at least the **5th Dynasty** (c.2494–2345 BCE),
seeking gold, aromatic resins, African blackwood and ebony, ivory, slaves, and wild animals
(monkeys and baboons). The Puntites are depicted with dark-reddish skin, long hair in earlier
images and close-cropped from the late 18th Dynasty. The most famous expedition is
[[hatshepsut|Hatshepsut's]], depicted in vivid detail at Deir el-Bahri — the reed huts on
poles entered by ladders, the chief Perehu and his wife, and ships loaded with myrrh trees
(some perhaps replanted at the temple). [[actors/ramesses-iii|Ramesses III]] mounted what was
"perhaps the first [expedition] since the famous venture … in the days of Hatshepsut."

## Where was it, and how reached?

The location is **debated**. Once identified with Somalia, "a strong argument has now been
made for its location in either southern Sudan or the Eritrean region of Ethiopia, where the
indigenous plants and animals equate most closely with those depicted in the Egyptian reliefs
and paintings." The **route** is likewise contested: the Deir el-Bahri sea scenes long
implied a Red Sea voyage from Quseir or Mersa Gawasis, but "it now seems likely that at least
some of the Egyptian traders sailed south along the Nile and then took an overland route to
Punt," perhaps making contact near the Fifth Cataract.
