# Tanis

**Tanis** (San el-Hagar), in the north-eastern Delta, was the royal capital of the **21st
Dynasty** and a principal seat of power through the
[[processes/third-intermediate-period|Third Intermediate Period]] — the Delta counterpart to
[[places/thebes|Thebes]].

## A recycled capital

Tanis rose as the Pelusiac branch of the Nile shifted and the older Ramesside capital
[[places/piramesse|Piramesse]] silted up. "Its chief monuments were constructed largely of
reused materials brought from Piramesse and other northern sites" — obelisks, statues, and
blocks of Ramesses II were hauled over and re-erected, long misleading early excavators into
thinking Tanis itself was Ramesside. Temples to the **Theban triad** were built there, making
it a northern mirror of Karnak, and the **royal tombs** were placed within the temple precinct.

## The royal tombs

Pierre Montet's discovery of the intact Tanite royal tombs is one of the great finds of
Egyptian archaeology, second only to Tutankhamun in its treasure. The burials held "substantial
quantities of gold and silver," including the magnificent **silver coffins** of Psusennes I and
Sheshonq II — among the finest metalwork of the age. A granite sarcophagus taken from
[[merneptah|Merneptah's]] tomb was reused for Psusennes I. The general Wendjebauendjed was even
interred inside the king's own tomb — "unthinkable in the New Kingdom, but now the king had more
the character of a feudal overlord." Actual administration, however, seems to have run from
[[memphis|Memphis]].
