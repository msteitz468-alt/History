# Luoyang

One of the **great historic capitals of China**, in the [[central-plains-china|Central Plains]]
near the confluence of the [[yellow-river-valley|Yellow]] and Luo rivers. Luoyang served as the
**eastern capital** at three decisive moments treated by
[[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook (2017)]]: the Eastern [[zhou-dynasty|Zhou]], the
Eastern [[han-dynasty|Han]], and the [[three-kingdoms-period-220-ce|Cao Wei]].

## The Eastern Zhou Capital (from 771 BCE)

The early Zhou kings built an administrative capital near the Yellow River–Luo River intersection,
**Luo Yi**, also called **Cheng Zhou** ("Created Zhou"), to balance the ancestral western capital
**Zong Zhou** in the Wei valley. When the **Quan Rong sacked the western capital in 771 BCE** and
killed King You, the surviving court relocated east to this site under King Ping — inaugurating the
**Eastern Zhou** ([[spring-and-autumn-period-771-bce|Spring and Autumn]] and
[[warring-states-period-475-bce|Warring States]] eras), during which the kings retained the title
and ritual monopolies but little real power.

## The Eastern Han Capital (from 25 CE)

After **[[wang-mang|Wang Mang's]]** Xin dynasty fell and **Chang'an** was sacked, **Emperor
Guangwu** proclaimed himself Son of Heaven in **25 CE** and fixed his capital at Luoyang — a move
Major & Cook explicitly parallel to the Zhou's own eastward shift centuries before. Luoyang remained
the seat of the **Eastern (Later) Han** until the dynasty's disintegration. Here the eunuch faction
was annihilated in the **palace massacre of 189 CE**, and here **Emperor Ming** built the **White
Horse Temple**, traditionally the **oldest continuously active [[buddhism|Buddhist]] temple in
China**, for monks returned from a Central Asian mission — an early marker of Buddhism's entry.

## Cao Wei and After

When **Cao Pi** accepted the abdication of the last Han emperor in **220 CE**, Luoyang became the
capital of **Wei**, the northern power of the [[three-kingdoms-period-220-ce|Three Kingdoms]], and
then of the **Western Jin** that briefly reunified China in 280 CE. It would be rebuilt as a great
capital again under the Northern Wei and the Tang.

## Why This Place Mattered

Luoyang is the **eastern pole** of the classic Chinese capital pairing with **[[chang-an|Chang'an]]**
in the west — the recurrent "two capitals" rhythm of the Yellow River core. Its position commanded
the Central Plains and the river crossings, making its possession a marker of legitimate rule over
"All under Heaven."
