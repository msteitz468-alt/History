# Paris

Paris, capital of France under the Ancien Régime and the Revolution, was Thomas Jefferson's primary residence 1784–1789 as Minister Plenipotentiary.

## Description and Historical Role

During Jefferson's years the city combined Enlightenment brilliance (salons, science, architecture) with deepening fiscal and political crisis. Jefferson lived first in the Hôtel de Langeac (near the Champs-Élysées), later other quarters. He moved in liberal aristocratic and intellectual circles, reported extensively on French society, agriculture, and politics, and observed the Estates-General and early Revolution from close range.

## Significance for Jefferson

The Paris years were the "full flowering of the Enlightenment" for Jefferson personally — the richest period of cultural, scientific, and aesthetic stimulation in his life. He sent home seeds, books, architectural models, and reports while serving as a "Minister of Enlightenment." He witnessed the "philosophical" early phase of the Revolution with qualified approval, counseling moderation to Lafayette and others.
