# Taosi

**Taosi**, in the Linfen basin of southern Shanxi, is the **largest Neolithic site in the
Yellow River valley** (~280–300 ha) and the most complex [[longshan-culture|Longshan]]
chiefdom on the [[central-plains-china|Central Plains]]. In
[[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's]] argument it is the crucial **negative case**: the
most hierarchically developed Longshan polity, which **collapsed without becoming a state**
— proof that statehood did not simply crown the most complex chiefdom.

## The Site (~2600–2000 BC)

Taosi runs through three phases:

- **Early phase:** a 56 ha rammed-earth enclosure in the northeast; a **palatial area (5
  ha)** and elite residential area (1.6 ha) in the south; a 3 ha cemetery with thousands of
  burials in a **pyramidal hierarchy** — large tombs (1.3%) held wooden coffins, cinnabar,
  and 100–200 grave goods (dragon-painted plates, alligator and pottery drums, jade *yu*
  axes and *cong* tubes, giant chime stones); 87% of tombs had no goods at all. The five
  largest tombs (Section III) were all **male** and filled over several generations —
  **some of the earliest evidence for hereditary status** in Neolithic China.
- **Middle phase:** a vast new enclosure (~280 ha), the **largest rammed-earth enclosure of
  the Neolithic**, plus a small elite enclosure with its own rich cemetery; construction
  required **tribute labour** drawn from across the basin (temporary worker shelters survive
  inside the wall).
- **Late phase:** political turmoil. The great enclosure was destroyed, the old palatial
  area turned over to bone-arrowhead production, 40+ violent/dismembered burials were dumped
  in a ditch, and elite tombs were looted — perhaps reflecting competition with a rival
  center, **Fengcheng-Nanshi (230 ha)**, which emerged to the south.

A cast **copper bell** — among the earliest metallurgy in the middle Yellow River — was
found in a *small* tomb with no other goods, while elite tombs held none: metal was **not yet
part of the prestige system** (that came only with [[erlitou-culture|Erlitou]] bronze).
Taosi was also a major **stone-tool center**, supplied from the Dagudui quarry 6 km south
(15 ha of lithic debris up to 4 m deep), whose chime stones and ritual knives match the
goods in Taosi's grandest burials.

## Significance

In the early phase Taosi dominated an integrated regional system (**primate** rank-size
curve, ~3,300 km² territory) — a genuine complex chiefdom. Yet by the late Longshan it
**fragmented** into competing centers and then **collapsed**, after which the Linfen region
became the **periphery** of the Erlitou state centered in the distant [[yiluo-basin|Yiluo
basin]]. Skeletal evidence of **northern migrants** (Inner Mongolia) fits climate-driven
migration into this circumscribed, attractive basin (~4,700–4,000 BP). Taosi is thus the
empirical core of Liu's claim that state formation followed **no linear ascent from the most
complex chiefdom** (see [[early-china-state-formation]]).
