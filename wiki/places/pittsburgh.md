# Pittsburgh

Major industrial city in western Pennsylvania at the confluence of the Allegheny, Monongahela, and Ohio rivers. In the nineteenth century it became the center of American iron, steel, coke, glass, and heavy manufacturing, powered by local coal, access to iron ore via the Great Lakes, and railroad connections. Allegheny City (across the river, later annexed) was the initial settlement point for the Carnegie family in 1848.

Pittsburgh's strategic location and resources made it the ideal site for the explosive growth of the steel industry after the Civil War. Andrew Carnegie's Edgar Thomson Steel Works (Braddock) and Homestead Works were located in the Pittsburgh district; Henry Clay Frick's coke operations supplied the fuel. The city was the scene of intense labor conflict, most notoriously the 1892 Homestead Strike.

Carnegie's philanthropy left a permanent mark: the Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh, Carnegie Institute (museum, music hall, library), Carnegie Mellon University (roots in the Carnegie Technical Schools), and other institutions. The city remains associated with both the industrial might and the social costs of Gilded Age capitalism.
