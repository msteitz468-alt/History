# Arles

Provençal city on the Rhône where Vincent van Gogh lived February 1888 – May 1889 and experienced his most explosive period of artistic production.

## Description and Historical Role

Arles preserved Roman remains (amphitheatre, baths) amid a vibrant market town and agricultural plain. In 1888 it offered Vincent intense sunlight, vivid color, and a "Japanese" quality of clear air and simple forms that he had dreamed of since discovering Japanese prints.

## Significance

In Arles Vincent painted more than 200 canvases in 15 months, including the Sunflowers series, the Yellow House, The Night Café, Bedroom in Arles, multiple sowers and harvests, and the first portraits of the postman Roulin and others. He rented the Yellow House intending to found a "Studio of the South" with Gauguin. The arrival and rupture with Gauguin (December 1888) led to the ear-cutting incident, hospitalization, and eventual voluntary commitment at Saint-Rémy. Arles represents the zenith of his "attack on color" and the beginning of his public recognition (Theo sold some works).
