# Sahara

The world's largest hot desert, stretching roughly 4,800 kilometers across North Africa from the Atlantic to the Red Sea and separating the Mediterranean coastal zone from sub-Saharan Africa. Once considerably wetter — the "Green Sahara" of the African Humid Period (roughly 11,000–5,000 years ago) supported lakes, savanna, and pastoralist populations before desertification set in during the mid-Holocene — it has for most of recorded history functioned as a formidable but permeable barrier between Mediterranean and West/Central African civilizations.

## History of Control and Significance

The desert's oases, wells, and seasonal routes were historically controlled by [[berbers|Berber]] pastoralist and camel-nomad groups (notably the Tuareg in its central and southern reaches), who after the spread of the domesticated camel in the early centuries CE became the essential carriers and guides of the [[trans-saharan-trade|trans-Saharan trade]] connecting Mediterranean North Africa with the gold-, salt-, and slave-trading states of the Sahel (Ghana, Mali, Songhai). Trade and pilgrimage routes across the Sahara also served as the principal vector for the spread of Islam into West Africa from the eighth century onward.

## Collection Coverage Note

The wiki's coverage of the Sahara itself as a physical and cultural zone is currently thin and derivative, developed mainly through its Berber and trans-Saharan trade coverage rather than dedicated environmental or archaeological sources on the desert's own deep history (the Green Sahara period, Saharan rock art, and Garamantian civilization remain underrepresented).
