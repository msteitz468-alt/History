# Bithynia

A region of northwestern Anatolia along the Sea of Marmara and southwestern Black Sea coast that became an independent Hellenistic kingdom under the native Nicomedes dynasty following the fragmentation of Alexander's empire, with its capital at Nicomedia. Like other non-Greek dynasts of Asia Minor (Pontus, later Pergamon), the Bithynian kings adopted Hellenistic monarchic forms and court culture while retaining an indigenous political base. Bithynia became entangled in Rome's wars with Mithridates VI of Pontus before its last king, Nicomedes IV, bequeathed the kingdom to Rome in his will (74 BCE); it was organized as the Roman province of Bithynia et Pontus. The province is best documented in the early 2nd century CE through the surviving correspondence of its governor, Pliny the Younger, with the emperor Trajan — including Pliny's well-known letter on the handling of Christians, one of the earliest external accounts of the early church.
