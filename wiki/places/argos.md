# Argos (Archaic)

**Argos** was the great rival of [[sparta|Sparta]] for mastery of the Peloponnese — the
martial city of the Argolid that reached its Archaic peak under **Pheidon**, defeated
Sparta at Hysiae, and seized control of the Olympic Games, before sinking into a long
isolation. [[boardman-cah-v3p3-1982|CAH Vol. III, Part 3]] (Ch. 42, N. G. L. Hammond)
treats it.

## The martial city

Argos "claimed to be the most martial" of the Peloponnese, and in the eighth century
the claim was just: it held the Argolid, Cynuria, Thyreatis and Cythera, fielded a
**fleet** when Sparta had none, and intervened against Sparta's expansion (helping the
"Achaeans" of Helos). Its destruction of **Asine** (whose Dryopian refugees fled to
Sparta) sowed "a bitter, ineradicable hatred" with Sparta. Unlike Corinth or Sparta,
Argos "was not involved in the colonizing movement" — Hammond infers it simply **had
enough land for its citizens**, keeping its hold on Thyreatis, Cynuria and Cythera.

## Pheidon

Argive power peaked **c. 670–660 under Pheidon**, a **Temenid** elected to the
traditional office of *basileus* but ruling "despotically" (an early near-tyrant). He
**defeated Sparta decisively at Hysiae (669)** — a classic hoplite battle on the
disputed plain of Thyreatis — and went on to **seize control of the Olympic festival of
668**, ousting the Eleans in favour of the Pisatans. Tradition credits him with the
**"Pheidonian measures"** (standard dry and liquid measures that eased the exchange of
produce) and with dedicating iron spits (*obeliskoi*) — a pre-coinage medium of
exchange — to Hera (Ephorus wrongly turned this into the invention of Aeginetan coinage).
For a time Pheidon "recovered the lot of Temenus," the mastery of the north-eastern
Peloponnese, and was entangled in the faction-politics of [[corinth|Corinth]] (where his
intervention reportedly cost him his life, c. 657).

## Decline and isolation

After Pheidon, Argos faded. Sparta's recovery in the Second Messenian War brought
defeats that led the Argives to **banish the last Temenid king, Meltas** (Pheidon's
grandson), replacing the monarchy with a college of **damiorgoi** — a shift "toward
democracy" (as Sparta inclined to oligarchy). Argos lost an unsuccessful war against
Cleisthenes of Sicyon (who insultingly renamed his Dorian tribes to spite the Argive hero
Adrastus), and in **546** was decisively beaten by Sparta at **Thyreatis** (the Battle of
the Champions), losing its eastern lands and its place as a great power — thereafter kept
"almost in isolation" by the [[peloponnesian-league|Spartan Alliance]], and notably
neutral in the Persian Wars.

## Why Argos mattered

- The **chief Peloponnesian counterweight to Sparta** — whose victory at Hysiae and
  brief Pheidonian supremacy were the high-water mark of resistance to Spartan hegemony.
- A site of early Archaic innovation — the **hoplite battle of Hysiae**, the Pheidonian
  measures, fine Argive Geometric and orientalizing pottery, and the famous statues of
  **Cleobis and Biton**.
