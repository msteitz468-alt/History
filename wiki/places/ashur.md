# Ashur (the City and the God)

The original capital and **cult center of Assyria**, on the west bank of the Tigris
in northern Mesopotamia — at once a city, a god (**Ashur**), and, in the idiom of
its own people, *the* "City" that *was* the Assyrian state. Home base of the
[[old-assyrian-trade|Old Assyrian merchant network]], it is the seed of the
[[middle-bronze-age|Middle Bronze]] Assyrian polity that [[shamshi-adad-i|Shamshi-
Adad I]] would turn into the first Assyrian territorial empire. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]] (Ch. XXV, Munn-Rankin/Landsberger).

## Pre-Semitic and Akkadian beginnings

Before the Semites, the region was inhabited by a **Hurrian/Subarian** population
(the name *Subartu* later became a derogatory term, and *Subarian* a word for
"slave," in Babylonia). The oldest stratum of Ashur's **Ishtar temple** was, in the
excavator's words, "a Hurrian broad-room." Ashur became an important administrative
center of the [[akkadian-empire|Akkadian Empire]] — Manishtusu built its Ishtar
temple, Naram-Sin left an inscription, and the oldest Assyrian royal inscription
(the *issiakkum* Ititi, dedicating booty from Gasur) dates to this Sargonic horizon.
It was later a vassal city of the [[third-dynasty-of-ur|Third Dynasty of Ur]] (its
governor Zariqum dedicating for Amar-Sin's life).

## "The City" and its rulers

A defining peculiarity: in the Old Assyrian period the title **"king" (*šarrum*)
belonged to the god Ashur alone**; the human ruler bore the modest styles
**"overseer" (*waklum*)**, **"priest-prince" (*iššiakkum*)**, or "prince"
(*rubā'um*). The state was conceived as the god's, administered on his behalf — a
mercantile city-state governed in partnership with its great merchant houses, whose
heads supplied the annual **eponym (limmu)** officials (see [[assyrian-king-list]]).

## The Assyrian devotion to Akkad

CAH stresses a striking ideological fact: where **Babylon despised** the
[[akkadian-empire|Sargonic kings]] as impious foreign conquerors, **Assyria
cherished them** as its own and as the model to emulate. The dream of a **universal
empire** ("king of the universe," "king of the four regions") — grounded in the
**moon-cult** (Sin, the one god worshipped wherever Semites went) — was an Assyrian
ideal revived again and again, with kings periodically adopting the throne-name
**Sargon** when the cycles suggested a new world-empire was due.

## Shamshi-Adad I and after

The first ruler actually to take the title **"king" / "king of the universe"** and
build a matching empire was the Amorite **[[shamshi-adad-i|Shamshi-Adad I]]**
(~1813–1781 BCE), who moved his residence north to **Shubat-Enlil** and set his
sons over Mari and Ekallatum. His name remained "one of the most beloved" in
Assyrian memory. Ashur would revive far more powerfully in the Middle and
Neo-Assyrian periods (CAH Vols. II–III).

## Significance

Ashur is the **kernel of the Assyrian civilization** — the cult-city whose god,
ideology, and mercantile institutions seeded a state that would, a millennium later,
become the first great Near Eastern empire. Its early history exemplifies the
**city-as-god-as-state** identity and the deep Assyrian attachment to the Akkadian
imperial ideal.

## Historiography

Pre-Sargonic Ashur is archaeologically thin (later kings' terracing destroyed early
levels, and the Tigris shifted its bed); its early history is reconstructed from the
**Assyrian King List**, the **eponym canon**, the Kültepe trade archive, and
synchronisms with Akkad and Ur — a far more rigorous chronological apparatus than
Babylonia's (see [[assyrian-king-list]] vs. [[sumerian-king-list]]). The
**"17 kings who lived in tents"** that open the king-list are now read as
**tribal/nomadic ancestors** of the Assyrian people, not a literal royal sequence.
See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
