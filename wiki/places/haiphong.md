# Haiphong

Major port city in northern Vietnam (Tonkin), historically the economic “lungs” of the Red River Delta. Site of the November 1946 incident and bombardment that directly precipitated the full-scale First Indochina War.

## Geography and Strategic Importance

Located on the Gulf of Tonkin at the mouth of the Red River system, Haiphong was (and remains) the primary deep-water port serving Hanoi and the northern interior. Control of Haiphong gave the French the ability to dominate maritime access and the economic life of Tonkin; loss of the port would have severely restricted their military and administrative position.

## History of Control

- Colonial era: Developed by the French as the chief port of Tonkin.
- 1945–46: Contested after the August Revolution; uneasy coexistence until the November clash.
- November 1946: French forces seized full control after the bombardment of the Vietnamese and Chinese quarters.
- 1946–1954: French base and supply hub during the First Indochina War.
- Post-1954: Major city of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam; heavily bombed during the later American war; today one of Vietnam’s largest ports and industrial centers.

## Why It Mattered (1946)

The French decision to bombard and occupy the city after a minor patrol incident demonstrated willingness to use overwhelming force against civilians to break the political impasse. The resulting thousands of deaths (exact number never firmly established) and destruction radicalized opinion and removed any remaining path to negotiated coexistence in the north.
