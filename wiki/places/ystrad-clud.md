# Ystrad Clud (Strathclyde / Alt Clut)

British (Brythonic) kingdom of the Clyde valley / Strathclyde, with centre at Dumbarton (Alt Clut); persisted as independent polity into the 11th c. Important for understanding the survival of British language and culture in the north after the Roman period.

The surname Wallace indicates a background among the northern ‘Welsh’ of Strathclyde (Ystrad Clud) or Cumbria, though the Cumbric language had probably died out a century or more before William Wallace’s period, having given ground both to Scottish Gaelic and the Anglian Scots language, the latter a close relative of English.

Legendary material associated with the region appears in later traditions: e.g. “Cumbric Strathclyde (Ystrad Clud). Three characteristics coalesce to distinguish the ‘Celtic wild man’ from similar figures...” (in context of Lailoken/Myrddin traditions linked to Rhydderch Hael and the north).

## History of Control and Settlement
Dedicated entry in Koch Vol V Contents: "Ystrad Clud 1818". Brythonic survival in north Britain. The place-name Ystrad Clud is the Welsh form for the kingdom.

## Additional Verbatim Claims from R05-Vol5-02 (Koch Vol V)

- **Mongán and Ystrad Clud conflict**: "Mongán mac Fiachna died fighting the Britons of Strathclyde (Ystrad Clud). According to the Annals of Tigernach, he was struck by a stone by Artuir (i.e. Arthur), son of Bicor the Briton." Name and lore ties to "wild man" traditions and further reading explicitly: "Ystrad Clud; ... Ulaid; Uthr Bendragon; wild man". (Koch Vol V, Mongán mac Fiachna entry; related legendary history).

- **Insular cross-links**: Ystrad Clud / Strathclyde figures in networks with Ulaid (Irish Sea interactions), Uthr (Arthurian), Teamhair (in Mongán tales), and Urien / Rheged material (north British British kingdoms). Evidence of 7th-c. military contact between Irish and Britons of Ystrad Clud.
