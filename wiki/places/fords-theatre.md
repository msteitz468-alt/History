# Ford's Theatre

Nineteenth-century theater on Tenth Street NW in [[places/washington-dc|Washington, D.C.]], less than a mile from the White House. Site of the [[events/assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-1865|assassination of Abraham Lincoln]] on 14 April 1865, when [[actors/booth-john-wilkes|John Wilkes Booth]] shot the president during a performance of *Our American Cousin*.

## Why It Mattered

Ford's was a normal wartime entertainment venue that became the fixed geographic symbol of Lincoln's death. Ellsworth's subtitle frame — "a redbrick theater located less than six blocks from the White House" — makes the place the hinge between political capital and cultural stage. The building survives as a working theater and National Historic Site; the Petersen House opposite is where Lincoln died.


## Ellsworth Detail (*Midnight on the Potomac*, 2025)

Booth's first appearance at the new Ford's (Nov 1863) packed every seat. On 2 Sept 1864, actor William J. Florence interrupted a performance to announce Atlanta's fall. 14–15 Apr 1865: *Our American Cousin* benefit for Laura Keene; assassination mid–third act; Keene recognized Booth in the wings. Afterword notes Ford's ghost lore and the building's dual life as theater and national wound-site.
