# The Andes

## Overview

The **Andes** — the continental cordillera running the length of western South America — are
the setting for the densest strand of [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|Volume
III]]: the independent rise of complex society from coastal preceramic monumentality through
[[chavin|Chavín]], the regional florescences ([[moche|Moche]], [[nasca|Nasca]]), the Middle
Horizon empires ([[wari|Wari]], [[tiwanaku|Tiwanaku]]), the Late Intermediate kingdoms
([[chimor|Chimor]], the [[aymara-kingdoms|Aymara señoríos]]), and finally
[[inca-empire|Tawantinsuyu]] and its Spanish conquest.

The defining feature of Andean life is **verticality** — the steep stacking of distinct
ecological zones from Pacific desert coast through valleys, high puna grassland, and the
eastern montaña — which Andean societies exploited through the
**[[vertical-archipelago|vertical archipelago]]** and the kin-corporate **[[ayllu]]**.

## Significance

The Andes are one of the world's small number of **primary, independent civilizational
hearths**. The volume uses the Andean sequence to test general models of how states arise (the
maritime-foundations debate, the horizon/regional oscillation) and how empires integrate
ecologically and ethnically diverse populations without markets or writing.
