# Tarentum

The Greek city of Taras (Latin Tarentum), founded by Spartan colonists around 706 BCE on Italy's "heel," was the wealthiest and most powerful of the Magna Graecia poleis of southern Italy by the fourth century BCE, prospering from agriculture, wool, purple dye, and control of the Gulf of Taranto's harbor.

A treaty limiting Roman naval movement into the Gulf, violated when Roman ships appeared off the city in 282 BCE, precipitated a crisis: Tarentine mobs attacked the Roman squadron and expelled a Roman garrison from nearby Thurii, prompting Rome to declare war. Tarentum appealed for help to [[actors/pyrrhus-of-epirus|Pyrrhus of Epirus]], whose intervention (280–275 BCE) opened the [[events/pyrrhic-war-280-275bce|Pyrrhic War]]. After Pyrrhus's withdrawal, Rome took Tarentum in 272 BCE, ending its independence and completing Roman hegemony over southern Italy on the eve of the First Punic War.
