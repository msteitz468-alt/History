# Combe Grenal

**Combe Grenal** is a key cave and rock shelter site in the Dordogne region of southern France with a long stratigraphic sequence spanning the Middle and Upper Palaeolithic. It is one of the classic sites used to illustrate changes in faunal exploitation and site use through the penultimate and last glacial periods.

## Geographic Description and Significance

The site provides evidence of repeated occupation in a region with diverse resources. Animal bone assemblages show shifting proportions of reindeer, red deer, bovids and horse, interpreted as reflecting climatic influences on prey availability rather than major changes in human hunting preferences.

"Changes in the relative importance through time of reindeer, red deer, bovids and horse bones are thought to reflect a combination of climatic factors which led to different proportions of these animals being taken from the area around the site." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~100-210)

The site is cited as an example of a densely occupied home base in site catchment models.

## Archaeological Evidence from the Range

- Long sequence containing Acheulian (lower palaeolithic) and Mousterian (middle palaeolithic) levels.
- Faunal data plotted across levels 60–1 spanning Riss III, Würm I and II.
- Interpreted within site exploitation territory frameworks (Vita-Finzi and Higgs 1970), with a ~5 km radius example referenced for comparable cave sites.

"Combe Grenal (Bordes and Prat, 1965) in the Dordogne region of southern France" (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~100-110)

The changing species proportions are shown in graphs contrasting with evidence of occupational continuity.

## Role in Settlement and Subsistence Studies

Combe Grenal exemplifies the home base settlement system where groups returned repeatedly to a central location for exploiting surrounding resources within a two-hour walking radius (~10 km critical threshold for economic returns).

It contrasts with sites used more as carnivore dens in other regions. Human activity is inferred from the faunal record combined with lithic assemblages.

## Historiography and Interpretation

The site was excavated by François Bordes. Interpretations of faunal change have shifted from simple climatic determinism to more nuanced considerations of site catchment analysis and potential storage or logistical strategies in collector settlement systems (Binford).

Grounding: "The graphs plot the changing proportions of the four main animal species identified. The archaeological levels spanned part of the penultimate and last glacial periods (Riss III, Würm I and II in the French sequence) and contained abundant artifact assemblages." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~190-210)

The site contributes to understanding that lower and middle palaeolithic subsistence involved flexible food management strategies constrained by animal protein needs for mobile northern latitude populations.
