# Saint Petersburg

**Saint Petersburg** (historically also known as **Petrograd** and **Leningrad**) is a major city in northwestern Russia, located at the mouth of the Neva River on the Gulf of Finland. 

## Description
Founded by [[peter-the-great|Peter the Great]] on May 27, 1703, Saint Petersburg was constructed on captured Swedish marshlands during the Great Northern War. Peter built the Kronstadt island fortress in 1704 to protect the new city. The construction of the city carried an immense human toll, as thousands of conscripted serfs died of disease and exhaustion during its clearing and building.

Saint Petersburg served as the capital of the Russian Empire from 1712 to 1918 (preceded_by: [[early-modern|Early Modern]] Muscovite capital in Moscow; followed_by: return to Moscow post-1917), before the capital was moved back to Moscow by the Bolsheviks. It was the physical embodiment of the shift to a European-oriented empire.

## Historical Significance
Saint Petersburg was the center of Russia's Westernisation and modernization programs. It was designed in European style, featuring classical and baroque architecture (most notably the works of Bartolomeo Rastrelli, including the Winter Palace and Tsarskoe Selo). 

It was the main stage for major political transformations in Russian history:

From Afonin (Cambridge History of Russia Vol. II, ll. 16753–16759): "Traditionally, its main theatre of operations was the Baltic Sea. ... The Royal Navy could easily block Russian access ... blockade Russian ports ... mount a realistic threat against Kronstadt and the security of St Petersburg, the imperial capital." Germany "new threat" 1880s High Seas Fleet "could pose a major threat to the security of Russia’s capital and her Baltic provinces." * **The Decembrist Revolt (1825):** Staged in Senate Square on December 26, 1825.
* **The 1905 Revolution:** Initiated on "Bloody Sunday" in Saint Petersburg, leading to mass strikes and the establishment of the Saint Petersburg Soviet.
* **The Revolutions of 1917:** Both the February and October Revolutions took place in Petrograd, culminating in the Bolshevik seizure of power.
* **The Siege of Leningrad (1941–1944):** A devastating 872-day blockade by German forces during World War II, resulting in the deaths of over one million civilians.

From Bassin (ch. 3, geographies of imperial identity): Upon conclusion of Great Northern War 1721, Senate ceremony in St Petersburg added "imperator vse-rossiiskii" to Peter's titles, formalizing Russia as European empire using Western lexicon: "the resolution to formalise this status using the foreign Latin-based terms imperiia and imperator" (lines ~5141-5142). The city exemplified the perceptual rebranding of space to distinguish metropole (European Russia) from colonial realm.

## Architecture and Culture (from Hughes ch. 4, lines 5551-5955+)
Peter summoned foreign architects (Domenico Trezzini, Jean Baptiste Le Blond, Gottfried Schädel) for regular plan contrasting Moscow's maze. Key: Peter and Paul cathedral (1712–33) with tall golden spire and basilical structure (departed from Russo-Byzantine centralised Greek cross); Peterhof (Peter's Versailles with formal gardens, fountains); Menshikov residences. "Characteristic touches in interiors were the use of blue and white Delft tiles..." (lines ~5581). "Petrine Baroque" for Western look. Painters: Louis Caravaque, Gottfried Dannhauer (portraits, Poltava scenes). Sculpture new (Rastrelli bronze bust of Peter). "stone and metal sculpture in the round was completely new to most Russians" (lines ~5649). Music/theatre: foreign masters, Kunst troupe plays in Russian from 1705. "parades... featured not only fanfares, but also choirs singing panegyric verses" (lines ~5657-5658).

Under successors (Anna/Elizabeth/Catherine): Bartolomeo Francesco Rastrelli for grand palaces (Winter Palace "solely for the glory of Russia", Catherine Palace Tsarskoe Selo, Smolnii cathedral). Neoclassicism under Catherine: Tauride palace (Ivan Starov), Cameron gallery, Quarenghi Academy of Sciences. "A planning model... for the reconstruction of Tver was adapted for other towns" (lines ~5917). Academy of the Three Fine Arts (1757) trained Russians.

## Key Institutions
* **Imperial Academy of Sciences (1725):** Founded by Peter I to sponsor scientific research.
* **Naval Academy (1715) & Noble Cadet School (1731):** Key educational institutions for the elite.
* Academy of Arts (1757, under Elizabeth; re-chartered 1764 by Catherine): centre for figurative arts.

## Historiography
The volume frames St Petersburg as the deliberate instrument of Petrine Europeanisation and the site where imperial identity was performed (Senate ceremony, mixed-sex court life). Hughes stresses continuity from 17th c. (Armoury, Academy influences) accelerated by Peter, but uneven adoption: high elite vs popular resistance (lubki, icons dominant). Bassin and Lieven underscore its role in constructing the metropole/ colony perceptual divide via Urals and Western models. Complements prior sources (Freeze) with thematic empire and culture focus. No contradiction with existing; strengthens Westernisation process and capital as symbol.
