# Merv

## Geography and Control

A major oasis city beyond the Iranian Plateau (modern Turkmenistan), a key junction on the
overland [[silk-roads|Silk Roads]] route described by **Isidore of Charax** in the
*Parthian Stations* — the eastern end of the road from Antioch, from which goods continued
into [[bactria|Bactria]] toward [[kashgar|Kashgar]]. It was a frequently contested frontier
city of the [[parthian-empire|Parthian Empire]].

## Why It Mattered

In [[benjamin-empires-ancient-eurasia-2018|Benjamin's]] account Merv is a pivot of the
**western–central** Silk Roads, the point where Mediterranean merchants handed goods to
Parthian, [[sogdians|Sogdian]], or [[kushan-empire|Kushan]] middlemen. It was a flashpoint
of the Parthian–Saka wars: **Phraates II** was killed fighting the Saka near Merv (c.
128–126 BCE) and his successor Artabanos II also died fighting them, before **Mithridates
II** recovered the city.

After the [[battle-of-carrhae-53bce|Battle of Carrhae (53 BCE)]], roughly **10,000 captured
Roman soldiers** were settled at Merv, where they married local women; some were pressed
into Parthian service and many were returned to Rome over thirty years later under a deal
with [[augustus|Augustus]].

## Significance

Merv exemplifies the [[parthian-empire|Parthian]] role as **gatekeeper-intermediary** of
the trans-Eurasian routes — a city whose control determined who profited from the silk
trade.
