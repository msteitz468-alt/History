# Southeast Asia

**Southeast Asia** — the **mainland** (Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, Vietnam) and the **island**
world (Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines) between India and China — is one of the wiki's
**critical gaps** (CLAUDE.md: "Absent"). This anchor page frames the region; its civilizations and
processes have their own pages as they are ingested. Currently grounded in Michael D. Coe's
[[coe-angkor-khmer-civilization-2018|*Angkor and the Khmer Civilization*]] (2003) — the wiki's first
dedicated Southeast Asian source — supplemented by the [[indianization-of-southeast-asia]] process page.

## The defining frame: the monsoon, rice, and the "Indian–Chinese" between

Two geographic facts organise the region's history. First, the **monsoon** — the alternation of a wet
"summer monsoon" and a dry "winter monsoon" — which governs **wet-rice agriculture**, the economic
base of every mainland state. Coe's Khmer case is the classic illustration: the **Mekong** and the
**Tonle Sap (Great Lake)**, whose feeder river **reverses its flow** each monsoon to quadruple the
lake and flood the plain, underwrote the **hydraulic civilization** of [[khmer-empire|Angkor]].
Second, the region's **position astride the maritime route between India and China** made it a
crossroads of [[indian-ocean-trade|trade]] and of cultural borrowing.

## Indianization and its successors

From the early centuries CE, mainland and island elites selectively adopted **Indian** religion
(Hinduism, then Mahayana and Theravada Buddhism), Sanskrit, script, law, and the *devaraja* kingship —
the **[[indianization-of-southeast-asia|Indianization]]** that produced [[funan|Funan]], the
[[khmer-empire|Khmer Empire]], **Srivijaya** (Sumatra), the Sailendras and **Majapahit** (Java), and
**Pagan** (Burma). From c. 1300 two further waves reshaped the region: **Theravada Buddhism** on the
mainland (Burma, Siam, Cambodia, Laos) and **Islam** in the archipelago (carried by Indian-Ocean
Muslim traders), followed by **European** intrusion (Portuguese, Dutch, Spanish, French, British) and
the colonial and Cold-War twentieth century.

## Why this region mattered

Southeast Asia was the **hinge of Afro-Eurasian maritime trade** (spices above all), a zone of
extraordinary **religious layering** (Hindu–Buddhist–Islamic–Christian), and the home of monumental
civilizations — **Angkor**, the largest pre-industrial urban complex on earth, and Borobudur — that
rival any in the world.
