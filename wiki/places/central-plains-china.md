# Central Plains (Zhongyuan)

The **Central Plains** (Chinese *Zhongyuan*) — generally central Shaanxi, southern Shanxi,
and western/central Henan — is the region **traditionally regarded as the cradle of Chinese
civilisation**, and the heartland in which complex societies on the
[[yellow-river-valley|Yellow River]] evolved into the first states. In
[[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's]] analysis it is both a real settlement landscape and
a **historiographic construct**: the "core" of the monocentric model challenged by the
multi-regional [[quxi-leixing]] paradigm (see
[[origins-chinese-civilization-monocentric-vs-multiregional]]).

## Landscape and Settlement

In antiquity the plains were dotted with lakes and marshes (40+ recorded in pre-Qin texts,
~190 in the 5th-c. *Shuijingzhu*), most since silted up. During the mid-Holocene maximum
much of the lowland was waterlogged, so [[yangshao-culture|Yangshao]] sites cluster small
and scattered on high ground while the **highlands** of southern Shanxi, western Henan, and
Shaanxi flourished. In the cooler, drier [[longshan-culture|Longshan]] period (~3000–2000
BC) the **central-Henan lowlands** opened up and saw the sharpest rise in site numbers.

Liu divides the Central Plains into **six settlement clusters** along a **circumscription**
gradient: circumscribed highland clusters — Taosi/Linfen ([[taosi]]) and Sanliqiao/western
Henan — versus semi- and less-circumscribed lowland clusters — the [[yiluo-basin|Yiluo]],
the Qin River, and northern and central Henan. The circumscribed highlands produced the
largest, most integrated (primate) polities; the open lowlands produced many medium walled
centers spaced ~40–44 km apart with similar territories (~1,250–1,520 km²), a signature of
**independent, competing chiefdoms** (the "ten thousand states" of legend).

## Significance

Crucially, the first state ([[erlitou-culture|Erlitou]]) emerged **not** from the most
complex circumscribed chiefdom ([[taosi|Taosi]], which collapsed) but from the **less
integrated, multi-centered systems** of central Henan and the Yiluo basin (see
[[early-china-state-formation]]). The Central Plains thus remains the geographic answer to
"where the first Chinese state formed," even as Liu dismantles the assumption that it was the
*sole* generative core of Chinese culture.
