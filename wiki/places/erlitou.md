# Erlitou (site)

**Erlitou**, in Yanshi (western Henan), is the type site of the
[[erlitou-culture|Erlitou culture]] and the **earliest urban center in China** — by
[[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's]] criteria, the seat of the first Chinese state. It
sits on the alluvial plains of the [[yiluo-basin|Yiluo basin]], the Yi and Luo rivers
joining nearby before reaching the [[yellow-river-valley|Yellow River]], with mountains on
three sides — advantageous for farming, defence, and transport.

## The City

Discovered in **1959** (Xu Xunsheng) and excavated continuously since, the residual site
covers **300 ha**, the largest of all its contemporaries. Urbanisation began in Phase II and
peaked in **Phase III**, when the city held an estimated **18,000–30,000** people. Its core
is a **palace/temple complex** of 30+ rammed-earth structures (6 excavated): **Palace no. 3**
(Phase II, 0.75 ha, with five elite burials in its courtyard) and the enclosed compounds
**Palace no. 1** (9,600 m²) and **no. 2** (4,200 m²), with colonnaded courtyards for ritual.
Immediately south, **bronze-casting workshops** produced piece-mold ritual vessels — found
only at Erlitou, mostly in elite graves — making control of bronze the foundation of elite
power. Erlitou is also "the first site at which residential and mortuary segregations are
intermingled," elite dead being buried **within** the palace compound.

The surrounding **Gongyi hinterland** developed its own four-tier hierarchy supplying
subsistence, stone tools, building materials, and white (elite) pottery — but **no** jades or
ritual bronzes, an asymmetrical **tributary** relationship. **Phase IV** shows clear decline
(shrinking population, scattered remains) before the site continued, much reduced, into the
early Shang.

## Significance

Erlitou is the physical anchor of the claim that a **state** existed in China by **~1800 BC
(Phase II)**: a primate-center, four-tier settlement system with a palatial administrative
core and attached craft specialisation. Whether it was the capital of the
[[xia-dynasty|Xia]] is separately disputed (see [[xia-dynasty-historicity]]); Liu's case for
its statehood does not depend on the answer.
