# Uisnech (Hill of Uisneach)

A hill in what is now County Westmeath, in central Ireland, regarded in early Irish tradition as the symbolic center-point of the island — the meeting place of Ireland's ancient provinces (cóiceda) — and, alongside [[teamhair|Tara]], one of the most important royal and ceremonial sites of pre-Christian and early medieval Ireland. Medieval Irish literary sources associate Uisnech with the great seasonal assembly (óenach) held at Bealtaine and, in mythological tradition, with the Tuatha Dé Danann and a large glacial erratic boulder known as the Ail na Míreann ("Stone of Divisions"), said to mark the point where the provinces met.

## Significance

As a ritual and assembly site distinct from, but linked to, the more explicitly kingship-focused Tara, Uisnech illustrates the layered, distributed nature of pre-Norman Irish sacral kingship geography: authority and legitimacy in early Ireland were expressed through a network of significant hills and assembly places rather than concentrated in a single fixed capital.
