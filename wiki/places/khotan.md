# Khotan

Khotan (Yutian in Chinese) was an important oasis kingdom on the southern Silk Road route around the Taklamakan Desert in modern Xinjiang. It was a key entry point for Buddhism into China and later for Islam, known for jade, textiles, and cultural synthesis between Indian, Iranian, Chinese, and local traditions.

## Role in Silk Road Exchange

Positioned on the southern Tarim route, Khotan facilitated trade in jade (highly valued in China), silk, and other goods. Royal patronage supported Buddhist monasteries and art. Documents in Khotanese (an Iranian language) reveal local administration, contracts, and religious life. It was a node for the transmission of Buddhism from India via Gandhara and a center of pre-Islamic religious diversity.

## Buddhism and Cultural Synthesis

Khotan played a major role in Buddhism's spread eastward; royal support and monastic communities made it a hub for texts and ideas reaching China. Later, it became an entry for Islam along the routes.

## Significance

Exemplifies the oasis model of small-scale trade combined with profound religious and cultural transmission via monks, traders, and migrants, as documented in excavated texts. Complements the "trickle trade" thesis with evidence of local economies tied to the routes.
