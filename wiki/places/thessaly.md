# Thessaly (Archaic)

**Thessaly** — "little other than a bigger and better Boeotia," a vast fertile plain
ringed by mountains — was the land of great horse-breeding baronial families ruling a
serf population, loosely bound into a federation that dominated the Delphic Amphictyony.
[[boardman-cah-v3p3-1982|CAH Vol. III, Part 3]] (Ch. 41, iii, W. G. G. Forrest) treats it.

## The barons and their serfs

Like Boeotia, Archaic Thessaly was a "self-sufficient, stable agricultural society" with
"little of native culture or art, no interest in the world abroad." It was ruled by
**basileis** — the great families **Aleuadae** of Larissa, **Scopadae** of Crannon,
**Echecratidae**, **Creondae** — whose notorious wealth (Theocritus's "many the calves...
thousands of sheep across the plain of Crannon") funded magnificent dedications,
banquets, the first racing-stables of Greece, and lavish patronage of poets (Simonides,
Anacreon, Pindar). Beneath them lay two distinctive dependent classes absent from
Boeotia:

- The **Penestae** — serfs, the descendants of the plain's pre-Thessalian population,
  who pastured the flocks and drove the cattle "in exchange for rations." Forrest weighs
  whether they were a conquered mass like the Spartan helots or a tied peasantry paying a
  fixed proportion (one source even has some "more prosperous than their masters"; a
  prince could field hundreds of them as cavalry — "utterly miserable yokels do not make
  cavalrymen").
- The **perioikoi** — subject/allied peoples of the surrounding hills (Perrhaebi,
  Magnesians, Phthiotic Achaeans, Dolopes) "dwelling around" the plain, owing
  light-armed troops and tribute "on a scale laid down by Scopas," and whose
  Amphictyonic votes Thessaly controlled.

## A loose federation

Thessaly comprised four regions (*tetrads*) — Hestiaeotis, Pelasgiotis, Thessaliotis,
Phthiotis — each "made up of the estates of great families," now rivals, now allies in
dynastic intrigue. How (and how far) these combined is obscure: the federal system was
traditionally ascribed to a certain **Aleuas the Red**, but the earliest firm evidence
for a federal council is the force "Thessaly by common decision" sent to aid the
Athenian tyrants (c. 511, under an alliance perhaps from the 540s). Thessaly's chief
collective ventures were external: leadership of the **First Sacred War** against Crisa
(c. 595–590) on behalf of [[delphi|Delphi]] (which Thessaly thereafter dominated through
the Amphictyony); a costly defeat by the Boeotians at Ceressus; and a failed southward
expansion checked by the **Phocian revolt** around 500. Before Xerxes' invasion the
**Aleuadae medized** while other noble houses did not — the factional split that
Thessalian "unity" always concealed.

## Why Thessaly mattered

- The Greek world's other great **serf-based agrarian society** alongside Sparta and
  Crete — the Penestae a standing comparison for the helots.
- The dominant power in the **Delphic Amphictyony**, and so a key player in the politics
  of the oracle and the First Sacred War.
- A model of **loose aristocratic federation** — ethnic consciousness only intermittently
  translated into common action, masking endemic baronial rivalry.
