# Workmen's Village (Amarna)

A small, walled, purpose-built community in a shallow desert valley ~1.1 km from the
[[places/amarna|main city of Akhetaten]] and out of sight of it, housing the artisans who
cut and decorated the royal tombs of [[actors/akhenaten|Akhenaten]]. Excavated under Barry
Kemp's Amarna Project, it is — with its companion **Stone Village** nearby — one of the
clearest windows onto how ordinary New Kingdom workers lived and worshipped.

## Layout: Planned Within, Self-Organized Without

Inside, the village was a rectangular walled enclosure subdivided into long plots by
streets, containing (after a western extension) **72 houses** of one basic design plus two
larger corner houses — a planned grid. *Outside* the wall, on the valley slopes, the
inhabitants threw up a **self-organized jumble** of brightly painted family chapels,
pig-pens and animal enclosures, gardens, small tombs and rubbish dumps — the same
contrast between imposed plan and [[concepts/state-formation|self-organization]] that runs
through Kemp's reading of the whole city. The village "especially resembles
[[places/deir-el-medina]] at western Thebes," pointing to its function and to the
likelihood that it housed the tomb-workers' community transferred from Thebes.

## Religion: The Old Gods Persist

The chapels and houses held portable cult objects invoking **non-Aten deities** — a stela
of **Shed** (protector against scorpions), the name of **Amun-Ra** invoked three times in
prayers ("the good ruler eternally, the lord of heaven"), a stone painted as a falcon
(Horus), figurines of **Taweret** and **Bes**. The chapels appear integral to the village's
original plan, not later additions. Kemp reads this as key evidence that
[[concepts/atenism|Atenism]] "expressed a preference which did not deny the validity of
others" — the formal suppression of the old gods barely touched the private devotional life
of ordinary people.
