# Ifriqiya

**Ifriqiya** — the Arabic name (from Roman *Africa*) for the region of **modern Tunisia, eastern Algeria, and
western Libya** — was the **cradle of the [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid Caliphate]]**. Its great cities were
**[[qayrawan|Qayrawan]]**, and the Fatimid foundations **[[mahdiyya|Mahdiyya]]** and
**[[mansuriyya|Mansuriyya]]**.

## The Fatimid heartland

Heir to Phoenician Carthage and Roman Africa, Ifriqiya in the 9th century was ruled by the Sunni
[[aghlabid-dynasty|Aghlabids]] under nominal Abbasid suzerainty. From the 890s the [[ismaili-movement|Ismaili]]
[[dawa|da'wa]] took root among the **[[berbers|Kutama Berbers]]** of its western mountains; in **909** the
movement overthrew the Aghlabids here and founded the Fatimid state (see
[[foundation-of-the-fatimid-caliphate-909]]). Ifriqiya remained the caliphate's centre for just over sixty
years — surviving the near-fatal **[[khariji-revolt-of-abu-yazid-943|Kharijite revolt of Abu Yazid]]** — until
[[al-muizz-li-din-allah|al-Mu'izz]] moved the capital to **[[cairo|Cairo]]** in 972 and handed the region to the
**Zirid** viceroys as a hereditary governorship.

## Significance

Ifriqiya is where a clandestine messianic movement became a territorial caliphate. Its religiously mixed,
strongly **Sunni Maliki** population also shaped the pragmatic, pluralist style the Fatimids carried to Egypt.
Its later abandonment to the Zirids — and their eventual defection to Sunni Abbasid allegiance — set up the
11th-century crises that reshaped the Maghrib.
