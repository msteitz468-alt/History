# Molodova I

**Molodova I**, level 4, is an open site in Ukraine featuring a distinctive oval arrangement of mammoth skulls and long bones enclosing an area of approximately 8 m x 6 m. It is interpreted as possible evidence of a Mousterian hut or windbreak structure.

## Description and Features

Within the oval area were fifteen hearths and a dense scatter of Mousterian flint cores and tools. The large heavy mammoth bones are thought by some to have served as weights to hold down skin coverings for a hut.

" At the open site of Molodova I, level 4, (Goretsky and Ivanova, 1982; Klein, 1969), an oval arrangement of mammoth skulls and long bones was found enclosing an area of 8m x 6m (Fig.3.8). Within this oval area were fifteen hearths and a dense scatter of mousterian flint cores and tools. These residues are interpreted as representing the remains of a hut where the large heavy mammoth bones served as weights to hold down skin coverings." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~920-940)

However, several hearths are located within the walls of the structure, suggesting the bones may have served as small windbreaks where hunters sheltered while watching for game. Repeated use under different wind directions could explain repositioning of hearths.

## Significance for Site Organization

The site contrasts with smaller structures such as the circular ~5 m² mammoth-bone enclosure at Mezhirich. It demonstrates how open-air Middle Palaeolithic groups structured space using available megafauna remains in periglacial environments.

" The size and complexity of the internal features at Molodova contrast with the late upper palaeolithic Russian site of Mezhirich (Pidoplichko, 1969)." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~950-960)
