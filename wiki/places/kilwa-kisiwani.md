# Kilwa Kisiwani

## Overview
Kilwa Kisiwani emerged as the dominant city-state on the southern Swahili coast by the twelfth century, controlling the gold trade from the Zimbabwe plateau (via Sofala) and becoming a major Islamic entrepôt in the Indian Ocean world. Its stone buildings, mosques, and wealth are documented by archaeology and travelers.

## History of Control and Settlement
Local African (Zanj) population with ruling dynasties claiming Shirazi (Persian) origins in foundation legends, later replaced by Mahdali (Yemeni) dynasty. By the 14th century (Ibn Battuta’s visit in 1331) it was a thriving Sunni Shafii Muslim town: “the majority of its inhabitants are Zanj, jet-black in colour,” ruled by a pious sultan noted for generosity. It competed with other Swahili towns (Mombasa, Mogadishu) and was tied to the gold/ivory/ambergris trade.

## Why This Place Mattered
Kilwa exemplified the integration of East African coastal societies into the Indian Ocean system through commerce and Islam. Its control of Sofala gold linked the Swahili coast to the southern African interior and the wider Islamic ecumene. Portuguese attacks in the early 16th century began its decline.
