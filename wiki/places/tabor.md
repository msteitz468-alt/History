# Tábor

Fortified town established by radical Hussites in southern Bohemia (1420 onward) as the centre of the Taborite movement. Symbol and practical base for the most extreme expressions of Hussite religious warfare, apocalyptic expectation, and social experimentation during the Hussite wars.

## Significance

Tábor represented the attempt to create a purified community of the Elect, rejecting traditional ecclesiastical and feudal structures. It served as the military and ideological heart of the radicals who, under Žižka and later Prokop, defeated multiple crusades. Early phases included chiliast (millenarian) expectations of Christ's imminent return and communal practices.

## Evolution and Violence

Housley notes that Tábor eventually cast off its most extreme apocalypticism and communism, evolving into a "radical but stable community." Its warfare was marked by a conviction of divine mandate combined with an "economy of violence" rather than total war (despite notorious early excesses).
