# Giza

The **Giza plateau**, on the west bank opposite Memphis, was the royal necropolis of
three 4th-Dynasty kings — **[[actors/khufu|Khufu]]**, **Khafra**, and **Menkaura** —
and the site of the largest stone monuments of the ancient world.

## The pyramids

- **Khufu's Great Pyramid**: ground plan 230 m per side, height 146.5 m, an estimated
  ~2.3 million blocks averaging ~2.5 tons — the largest in Egypt. A 43.4 m cedarwood
  boat was recovered from a pit on its south side; a second remains unexcavated. Three
  small queens' pyramids and the **Hetepheres** cache lie to the east.
- **Khafra's pyramid**: side 214.5 m, height 143.5 m. Its valley temple yielded the
  gneiss seated statue of Khafra protected by a Horus falcon — "a masterpiece that was
  often imitated in later periods, but never equalled."
- **Menkaura's pyramid**: side 105 m, height 65.5 m — markedly smaller, "suggesting
  that the striving for sheer size had passed its peak."

## The Great Sphinx and the cemeteries

The **Great Sphinx**, a human-headed recumbent lion carved beside Khafra's causeway, is
"some 72 m. long and 20 m. tall … the largest statue in the ancient world." It was not
worshipped in its own right until the early 18th Dynasty, as **Horemakhet**. Around the
pyramids stretch planned **mastaba fields** of royal kin and officials, laid out "by
streets intersecting at right angles" — an arrangement "unique to the 4th Dynasty," as
if the officials' tombs were extensions of the royal complex itself. Related abandoned
4th-Dynasty pyramids lie at Abu Rawash (Djedefra) and Zawiyet el-Aryan.

Jaromir Malek notes that the popular theory correlating the three pyramids' layout with
the stars of Orion "is unlikely to be correct."
