# Vix

## Overview

Vix (the famous "princess" or elite burial) is the richest known early Iron Age burial in western Europe, located near the defended site of Mont Lassois in eastern France. It exemplifies the pairing of fortified elite residence with rich tumulus burials and the peak of Mediterranean prestige exchange in the Hallstatt period (6th–5th c BCE).

## Key Finds and Context

The burial contained "a large bronze crater, or wine-mixing [vessel], and a pottery drinking cup, both imported from Greece, together with the remains of a vehicle and many other wealthy items" (Ch. 9, ~p. 283, cache ~580s). "Among the many rich graves around the Hohenasperg, two are of particular note... The recently discovered burial at Eberdingen-Hochdorf, however, was intact... a large cauldron containing the remains of a drink, drinking horns, a wheeled bronze couch of Italian or Etruscan manufacture" (parallel; Ch. 9, ~p. 283, cache ~590s). Vix itself is the standout Greek import example.

Mont Lassois defended site shows similar elite control of long-distance networks.

## Significance

Demonstrates "the effects of this trade with the classical world... in the imported goods and other wealth found in fortified sites and rich burials, such as... Vix in eastern France" (Ch. 9, ~p. 293, cache ~1420s). Part of the "climax of the whole phase of hierarchical development that had characterized Bronze Age social organization" (Ch. 9, ~p. 292, cache ~1290s). Mediterranean goods (wine service) used to enhance status; proximity to Mediterranean made control difficult as volume grew, contributing to later hierarchy shifts.

**Grounded claims from Champion et al. 1984 range lines 18501-22500 (Ch. 9):** Quotes above. "The Vix burial, for example... near the defended site of Mont Lassois, contained a large bronze crater..." (Ch. 9, ~p. 283, cache ~579s). "As the intensity of these interactions increased, there was a tendency for the affected areas to be incorporated more fully into the economic and social sphere of the classical world" (Ch. 9, ~p. 293, cache ~1430s); temperate Europe resisted full incorporation.
