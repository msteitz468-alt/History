# Oseberg

## Overview

The Oseberg burial mound in southeastern Norway contained the grave of a wealthy woman buried with a companion in A.D. 834. Among her grave furnishings were textile production tools, including a set of weaving tablets with an unfinished braid still attached. The site is one of the most significant for understanding Viking Age material culture and elite practices.

## Geographic and Historical Context

Oseberg is located in southeastern Norway and exemplifies high-status Viking Age burial practices. The mound and its contents provide a snapshot of late ninth-century (early tenth per traditional dating in context) elite material world in Scandinavia during the [[viking-age]].

## Why This Place Mattered

Oseberg yielded key evidence related to textile production. It contained grave furnishings including weaving tools that illuminate domestic industry and craft in early medieval Scandinavia. Together with finds from York and Birka, it has enabled particularly detailed reconstructions of Viking Age clothing.

## Archaeological Significance

The burial included a famous ship and rich furnishings. Textile production tools recovered provide direct evidence of weaving technology (warp-weighted looms and tablet weaving). The site demonstrates the role of women in textile production within elite households.
