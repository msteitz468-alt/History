# Khorezm (Ancient)

An ancient state in the desert oasis of the **Amu Darya (Oxus) river delta**, south of the
Aral Sea (modern Uzbekistan/Turkmenistan). From the 7th/6th centuries BCE to the 4th
century CE it saw the "sudden appearance and proliferation" of large **fortified enclosure
sites** — the material centrepiece of Michelle Negus Cleary's argument (Kim et al. 2017,
ch. 11) that these were not cities but the ceremonial-administrative enclaves of a **mobile,
non-nucleated state**.

## Geography and Significance

The oasis supported irrigation agriculture along canals branching from the Amu Darya, but
Negus Cleary's survey found that **settlement lay outside the walled enclosures**, in
low-density dispersed zones along the watercourses. The great enclosures themselves —
monumental, militarised in architecture — were largely **open space**, lacking dwellings,
streets or water systems. She reads them as "regal" or "nomad" capitals for an itinerant
royal court governing a substantially mobile population.

## Akchakhan-kala

The best-documented site (also called Kazakly-yatkan; late 3rd c. BCE – mid-2nd c. CE) is a
double enclosure — an Upper Enclosure of 12.4 ha and a Lower of 42.6 ha overall — with
double-galleried fortification walls (a central "archers' gallery," arrow loopholes,
rectangular towers, moats), preserved up to 10–12 m under sand. Its **Area 10 Ceremonial
Complex** held a hypostyle hall with fire-cult installations (ox-hide-shaped ash pits
paralleling nomadic kurgan fire altars), Achaemenid-style carved-ivory furniture, and
**polychrome murals** including a gallery of royal portraits with a painted text naming a
king, and processions of horses, camels and cattle. Mud-bricks bore tribal *tamga* marks;
portrait dress shows steppic/possibly Parthian elements. A great **siege, destruction and
burning** struck the Upper Enclosure c. mid-1st c. BCE; the site was abandoned by the
mid-2nd c. CE.

## Comparative Context

Negus Cleary sets Khorezm within a pan-Eurasian pattern of dispersed enclosure capitals:
the Chirik-rabat culture (Syr-Darya delta, linked to the [[scythians|Saka]]), Afrasiab
(Samarkand) and Kala-i Zakhoki Maron in [[sogdians|Sogdia]] (read as a Kangju "tent-city"
capital), the [[xiongnu|Xiongnu]] site of Ivolga, and the Achaemenid ceremonial centres
(Persepolis, Susa, Pasargadae) whose itinerant court "moved from capital to capital." She
adopts A. Rapoport's distinction between compact (nucleated) and **dispersed** capital
types, placing Khorezm in the second.

## Historiography

Negus Cleary's reading explicitly **overturns the earlier Soviet interpretation** (Tolstov
and others) that read the enclosures through Karl Wittfogel's *hydraulic-state* theory as
centralised urban centres controlling irrigation. Following Salzman, she treats "settled"
and "nomadic" as ends of a continuum rather than a binary (see [[pastoral-nomadism]]). The
site data supporting the revision come
largely from Negus Cleary's own fieldwork (Negus Cleary 2015a), and she frames the "mobile
capital" model as an interpretive thesis, not settled consensus.
