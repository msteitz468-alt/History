# Qayrawan

**Qayrawan** (modern Kairouan, Tunisia) was the **great inland metropolis of Ifriqiya** — the religious and
political capital of the [[aghlabid-dynasty|Aghlabids]] and the first city in which [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]]
rule was proclaimed.

## A centre of Sunni scholarship

Founded in the 7th century as the base of the Muslim conquest of North Africa, Qayrawan by the 10th century was
the foremost city of Ifriqiya and a stronghold of **Sunni** legal learning — dominated by the **Maliki**
*madhhab* (after Malik b. Anas, d. 795) and a centre of the **Hanafi** school favoured by the Aghlabids, with
only a small Shi'a community. Its scholarly climate made it the arena of the public debates (***munazara***)
between Ismaili da'is and Maliki jurists documented by **Ibn al-Haytham** in his *Kitab al-Munazarat*.

## Fatimid and Kharijite turning points

In **909** the [[berbers|Kutama]] armies of [[abu-abd-allah-al-shii|Abu Abd Allah al-Shi'i]] took Qayrawan,
driving out the last Aghlabid emir; on **4 January 910** [[al-mahdi-billah|al-Mahdi]] entered the city and the
next day the Friday khutba was pronounced for the first time in a Shi'i Ismaili Imam's name (see
[[foundation-of-the-fatimid-caliphate-909]]). In the **[[khariji-revolt-of-abu-yazid-943|revolt of Abu Yazid]]**
the city's Maliki scholars backed the Kharijite rebel and it fell to him (944) before returning to Fatimid
allegiance. After his victory [[al-mansur-fatimid|al-Mansur]] built **[[mansuriyya|Mansuriyya]]** beside it and
pointedly appointed a **Sunni Maliki chief judge** over Qayrawan — a landmark accommodation of the Sunni
majority.

## Significance

Qayrawan's persistent **Sunni Maliki identity** under Shi'i rulers made it the barometer of Fatimid relations
with their majority subjects — alternately a point of resistance and of pragmatic accommodation — until the
dynasty's centre of gravity shifted to Mansuriyya and then to Egypt.
