# New Carthage (Carthago Nova)

The great Barcid capital of Spain — modern **Cartagena** — founded by **Hasdrubal**
(c. 228) as the seat of Carthaginian power in the peninsula. Treated in
[[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Chs 2–3 (Scullard, Briscoe).

## Geography and significance

Hasdrubal moved the Barcid headquarters from Akra Leuke to **Mastia**, on a
peninsula commanding a **fine natural harbour**, with easy communications to Africa
and **rich silver mines** close by (the mine at Baebelo reputedly yielded Hannibal
300 lb of silver a day). He built a palace on a hill (Monte Molinete) commanding the
lagoon; his power there was "certainly vice-regal." The city was the **main
Carthaginian supply base and treasury** in Spain, the mustering point from which
[[hannibal]] launched his march on Italy (218), and the place where the Spanish
**hostages** guaranteeing tribal loyalty were held.

## History of control

- **Carthaginian (c. 228–209).** The Barcid capital; mint of the magnificent Barcid
  silver (Hannibal placed his father's portrait as Heracles-Melkart on the coinage).
- **Roman capture (209).** [[scipio-africanus|Scipio]] took the city in a single
  day by sending a wading party across the lagoon north of the city, which he had
  learned ebbed in the evening — folded into the "Scipionic legend" of Neptune's
  aid. The capture yielded enormous booty, eighteen ships, the Carthaginian
  armoury-workers, and the **Spanish hostages**, whose release won Scipio the
  defection of Iberian chieftains.
- **Roman Spain.** Thereafter a key Roman base; it became one of the principal
  cities of Roman Spain and a centre of the silver-mining economy.

## Foundation and name (Hoyos)

The city was founded c. 227/226 BC by **[[hasdrubal-the-fair|Hasdrubal the Fair]]** on the
site of the earlier town of Mastia — an outstanding natural harbour beside rich silver
deposits — as the capital of Barcid Spain. Dexter Hoyos
([[hoyos-hannibals-dynasty-2003|*Hannibal's Dynasty*]]) argues the name (Punic
*Qart-hadasht*, "New City" — literally another "Carthage") was **not a declaration of
independence** from the mother city, as some ancient and modern readers infer, but a
conventional Punic colonial-naming practice attested at several other sites. Its founding
and Hasdrubal's growing forces are what prompted Rome to negotiate the Ebro accord (225).
