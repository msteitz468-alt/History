# Knossos (Cnossus)

The **largest and chief palace of [[minoan-civilization|Minoan Crete]]** — the seat
of the legendary **Minos**, the labyrinth of Greek myth, and the site whose
excavation by **Sir Arthur Evans** (from 1900) created the very concept of "Minoan"
civilization. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (Chs. IV(b)
& XII, Matz; the Linear B from Chs. XIII–XII).

## The palace

Built over a Neolithic mound around **2000 BCE** and rebuilt grander after each
earthquake, Knossos reached its final form ~1700 BCE: a vast multi-storey complex
of *insulae* grouped around a great north–south **central court** (~50 m), with a
monumental west façade and forecourt, a stepped portico and viaduct for southern
visitors, and a **Domestic Quarter** on the east — the "Grand Staircase," pillared
halls opening through light-wells, and a bathroom with a **water-flushing latrine**,
fed by an ingenious clay-pipe **drainage system** unmatched in Egypt or the Near
East. The palace was both a **royal residence and a redistributive economic
centre**: its basement **magazines** of great storage jars, and its sealed archives,
served a centralized economy of grain, oil, wool and crafts. It was conspicuously
**unfortified** — the Cretan fleet was its defence.

## Art and cult

Knossos is the richest source of **Minoan fresco** (the bull-leaping "Toreador,"
the "Prince with the Feather Crown," the processions and miniature crowd-scenes) and
of the finest **gem-engraving** and the marine **Palace Style** pottery. Its
**throne-room**, with the Griffin Fresco and a gypsum throne, was created in the
final (Mycenaean) phase. The double-axe ("labrys") marks its **pillar crypts** as
sanctuaries — the labyrinth of "the house of the *labrys*" and the myth of the
Minotaur attach plausibly to this rambling, many-roomed palace.

## The Mycenaean takeover

From about **1450 BCE Knossos passed under [[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean
Greek]] control** — it is the **only Cretan site to yield [[aegean-scripts|Linear
B]] tablets** (nearly 4,000), and those tablets are written in **Greek**. The
**warrior graves** with weapons and the new throne-room belong to this régime —
a "military spirit quite new to the Minoan scene" (Evans). The Linear B archives
(sheep and the wool-clip, textiles, chariots and armouries) reveal the bureaucratic
palace economy at the moment of its fall. The palace was **finally destroyed
~1380 BCE** (the date and agent are disputed — see [[fall-of-knossos]]) and never
rebuilt; Crete thereafter was politically marginal.

## Why this place mattered

Knossos is the **type-site of Europe's first civilization** and the hinge of the
Minoan-to-Mycenaean transfer of power. As the find-spot of the Linear B tablets it
is also where **Ventris's 1952 decipherment** proved that Greeks ruled Crete in the
fifteenth century — overturning Evans's own belief that Minoans had destroyed
Mycenae, and rewriting Aegean prehistory. In myth it is forever the seat of Minos,
the labyrinth, and the tribute of Athenian youths to the Minotaur.
