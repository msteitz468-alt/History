# Staraya Ladoga

**Major 8th–10th century trade and craft production center in northwestern Russia; gateway between the Baltic Sea and Russian river routes (Volkhov–Dnieper/Volga); cited in some versions of the Russian Primary Chronicle as Rurik's original seat.**

## Geographic Description

Staraya Ladoga is situated in an ideal position to monitor access to the main communication routes through Russia, the Dnieper and Volga Rivers. Located south of Lake Ladoga at the easternmost point of the Baltic Sea, at the point where the Ladozhka tributary entered the Volkhov River, this location probably was chosen as the best spot for a harbor. (range 07 cache, Russia/Ukraine and Staraya Ladoga feature)

The site has excellent preservation due to waterlogged soil; dendrochronology provides precise dating. An estimated settlement area of 15 square kilometers; 3,600 square meters excavated. (range 07)

## History of Control and Settlement

Earliest settlement in the Earthworks Fortress quarter began about A.D. 760. Settlement expanded into the Varangian Street quarter in about A.D. 842. The town grew rapidly. During the mid-ninth century the north bank of the Ladozhka was settled, and by the tenth century the town had expanded to both sides of the Volkhov. (range 07)

Early development was haphazard, but after the mid-ninth century there is evidence for town planning and public works. The center was fortified in the second half of the ninth century (wooden in the 860s, stone in 882). In the tenth century streets were laid out on a grid and a princely residence built. Population slightly more than one thousand in the tenth century. (range 07)

More than one hundred and fifty medieval houses excavated (8th–11th centuries). Two main types: small (~5m square, corner hearth) and large (~13 by 10m, central hearth). One exceptional 894 building (17 by 10m) with walled hearth room yielded over 200 glass beads and 30 pieces of amber, suggesting trade involvement. (range 07)

Burial mounds along the Volkhov (visible from distance); more than thirty extant. Plakun cemetery notable for Scandinavian boat burials. Other cemeteries include Baltic, Finno-Ugric, and Slavic. One of the largest mounds thought for Oleg (879–912). (range 07)

## Why This Place Mattered

Staraya Ladoga was one of the most important trade and craft production centers of the eastern Baltic during the early Middle Ages. It controlled a substantial part of the route from the Baltic to the lower Volkhov; from there traders took either the Volga route to the Caspian/Islamic Caliphate or the Dnieper route to the Black Sea/Byzantium. (range 07)

Economy organized around local/regional exchange (utilitarian objects, prestige goods, raw materials for elite) and long-distance exchange (export furs and materials; import foreign prestige goods; transfer to other centers). Silver and trade scales indicate merchant activity. (range 07)

Local craft production ubiquitous: almost every house showed evidence (raw materials, tools, products at various stages, rejects, debris). Glass beads (glassworks), bronze casting (smithy from 760s with 26 tools), amber working, pottery (hand-built then fast wheel), bone/antler (knives, combs), wood (lathe-turned, carved), textiles, leather footwear. (range 07)

Agriculture, stock raising, hunting, gathering also present (plowshares; millet, berries; cows, pigs, sheep, goats, horses; beaver, fox, moose, seal, fish, birds). (range 07)

## Change Over Time and Ethnic Integration

No clear evidence that any particular ethnic group founded or administered the town. Earliest layers contain Baltic, Finno-Ugric, Scandinavian, and Slavic materials integrated throughout. Over time material culture homogenized, suggesting assimilation of a new local identity. Archaeological work in the Lake Ladoga region indicates ethnic integration outside the town as well. (range 07)

Status differentiation present: emerging elite consumed luxuries and built showy mounds; ordinary folk used utilitarian objects and simpler cremation graves. Elite influence increased over time. (range 07)

Staraya Ladoga is best understood as a trade and manufacturing town, one link in the network connecting Scandinavia, the eastern Baltic, and the Far East. It developed alongside new peoples (Scandinavians, Slavs) moving into northern Russia, stimulating trade, towns, craft specialization, and class stratification. (range 07)
