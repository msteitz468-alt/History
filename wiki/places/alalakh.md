# Alalakh (Tell Atchana)

A city of the **Amuq plain** near the mouth of the Orontes whose two great tablet
archives — **level VII** (Middle Bronze) and **level IV** (Late Bronze) — are among
the most important sources for [[hurrians|Hurrian]] Syria, the kingdom of
[[iamkhad|Yamhad]], and [[mitanni|Mitanni]]. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (Chs. I & X, Kupper & Drower).

## The two archives

- **Level VII** (~18th–17th c.): Alalakh was a vassal principality of
  **[[iamkhad|Yamhad (Aleppo)]]**, ceded by king Abba-el to a junior branch of the
  royal house. Its tablets show a society in which **[[hurrians|Hurrians]]** already
  held high office under Amorite kings and the goddess **Hepat** had entered the
  cult — the leading edge of the Hurrianization of Syria. The level was **destroyed
  by [[hittites|Hattusili I]]** (~1620s) on his way to break Aleppo.
- **Level IV** (15th c.): Alalakh (now capital of **Mukish**) is a vassal of
  **[[mitanni|Mitanni]]**, its society "Hurrianized in every respect," the
  West-Semitic element a small minority.

## Idrimi

The level-IV archive opens with **Idrimi**, whose remarkable **statue inscription**
(found smashed in the city's final destruction, but lovingly preserved and reburied)
tells his story in the first person: a son of the king of Aleppo, driven into exile,
who slept among the **Sutu bedouin**, lived seven years with the **Hapiru**, then
returned by ship to be accepted as king of Mukish — but only legitimately so once
the Mitannian Great King **Parattarna** confirmed him by treaty. His successors
(Niqmepa) remained vassals of Mitanni's **Saustatar**, who arbitrated their lawsuits
and boundary disputes. The Idrimi story is a vivid window into the **vassal-and-
overlord structure** of the Mitannian confederacy and the precarious legitimacy of a
Bronze Age king.

## Why this place mattered

Alalakh is a **stratigraphic and documentary keystone**: its level VII and level IV
archives bracket the great transformation of Syria — from the Amorite world of
[[iamkhad|Yamhad]] to the Hurrian world of [[mitanni|Mitanni]] — and quantify the
**Hurrianization** of the population across two centuries. The Idrimi inscription is
one of the few first-person royal autobiographies of the Bronze Age.
