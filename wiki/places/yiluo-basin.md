# Yiluo Basin

The **Yiluo basin** — the fertile alluvial valley where the Yi and Luo rivers join before
emptying into the [[yellow-river-valley|Yellow River]], around modern Luoyang in western
Henan — is "the heartland of early Chinese civilisation." It is the cradle of
[[erlitou-culture|Erlitou]], the earliest urban site in China, and later held the capitals
of at least ten dynasties. In [[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's]] settlement analysis it
is a **semi-circumscribed** cluster, open to the southeast toward the Huang-Huai plains and
ringed by mountains on three sides.

## Survey Evidence and Cycling

A **full-coverage survey** (Liu et al., 2002–2004) recorded **200+ sites in just 200 km²**,
far exceeding earlier traditional estimates for the whole 4,000 km² region — a methodological
demonstration that intensive survey transforms the settlement record. The data show
**population cycling**: growth in the middle/late [[yangshao-culture|Yangshao]] (the first
two-tier hierarchy), a sharp **decline** in the early Longshan, then renewed growth in the
late Longshan.

In the late [[longshan-culture|Longshan]] the basin held 171 sites (Wangwan variant) in a
**three-tier** hierarchy with a **convex** rank-size curve — a decentralised, competing
system. Its two largest centers, Poluoyao (30 ha) and Dayanghe (35 ha), sit only ~6 km
apart and so were probably **not contemporaneous**, each dominating in turn; centers averaged
~25 km apart (catchment ~491 km²). The specialised stone-tool site **Huizui** (10 ha)
produced limestone spades and lime, evidence of craft specialisation networked across
settlements.

## Significance

The Yiluo Longshan was **markedly less complex** than the [[taosi|Taosi]] cluster — fewer
tiers, smaller centers, smaller catchments — yet it was **here, not at Taosi, that the first
state arose**. Around **1800 BC** the basin underwent rapid settlement nucleation and
urbanisation at [[erlitou|Erlitou]], which grew to 300 ha and organised a four-tier
hierarchy across the basin. This inversion — state emergence from a *less* integrated
chiefdom landscape — is the linchpin of Liu's [[early-china-state-formation|trajectory
model]].
