# Anshan (Tal-i Malyan)

The **highland capital of Elam** in the Kur River basin of **Fars** (~46 km north of
Shiraz), the upland counterweight to lowland **[[susa|Susa]]**. Confirmed as ancient
**Anshan** by inscribed bricks found at Tal-i Malyan in the early 1970s (Reiner 1973) —
a discovery that reoriented Elamite studies. [[potts-archaeology-of-elam-2016|Potts]]
argues, following Vallat (1980), that **the centre of Elam originally lay at Anshan and
in the highlands around it, not at Susa in lowland Khuzistan**; the later equation of
Elam with Khuzistan is a *pars pro toto* extension of a name that first designated the
Anshan region in Fars. The royal title **"king of Anshan and Susa"** (Elamite order;
Akkadian texts reverse it to "king of Susa and Anshan") expresses the lowland–highland
union that defined the Elamite state.

## Archaeological sequence

- **Banesh phase (c. 3400–2800 BCE).** Contemporary with the [[proto-elamite|Proto-Elamite
  (Susa III)]] horizon. In the Middle Banesh phase the settlement reached **~45 ha** —
  "a small city inhabited by administrative officials and part-time craftsmen" (Sumner),
  with monumental buildings (ABC 3), a warehouse, and Proto-Elamite cylinder seals and
  tablets (32 texts, in "exact uniformity" with Susa). In the Late Banesh phase a
  **monumental mudbrick city wall ~5 km long enclosed ~200 ha** — Potts dismisses the
  idea that nomads built it as "purely speculative." Potts nonetheless holds that the
  Proto-Elamite writing system **developed at Susa, not Malyan** (the Malyan tablets are
  palaeographically later and record far smaller transactions).
- **Hiatus**, then **Kaftari phase (c. 2200–1600 BCE).** Resettled and grew explosively:
  Middle Kaftari Malyan reached **~130 ha**, "more than ten times larger than all but
  one other site in the Kur River basin." Tin-bronze became the alloy of choice; horses
  appear. A distinctive **"Anshanite" glyptic** style with Central Asian (Bactrian)
  affinities is tied by inscription to Ebarat II of the [[elam|Shimashki]] line.
- **Qaleh / Middle Elamite phase (c. 1600–1000 BCE).** A burned administrative building
  in sector EDD yielded **246 tablets**, mostly metallurgical accounts (gold, silver,
  copper, bronze), tied by royal bricks to **[[shilhak-inshushinak-i|Shilhak-Inshushinak]]**,
  Hutelutush-Inshushinak, and Kutir-Huban. Malyan served as a redistribution and
  administrative centre; Hutelutush-Inshushinak may have retreated here after
  [[nebuchadnezzar-i|Nebuchadnezzar I's]] victory (~1110 BCE).
- **Abandonment.** "The Kur River basin shows no signs of sedentary occupation between
  the late Middle Elamite and the Achaemenid periods." By the time the Persians made the
  title their own, **Anshan was an unoccupied ruin** — yet [[cyrus-the-great|Cyrus the
  Great]] and his forebears styled themselves **"king of Anshan"** (the Teispid cylinder
  seal of "Kurash the Anshanite, son of Teispes"), and [[cyrus-the-great|Cyrus]] founded
  **Pasargadae** ~40 km northeast of Malyan, reclaiming the highland Elamite legacy.

## Why it mattered

Anshan supplied the **highland resources** (metals, stone, timber, horses, manpower)
and the **dynastic legitimacy** of the Elamite state; control of "Anshan and Susa"
together was the formula of Elamite kingship for two millennia. Its afterlife as a
prestige royal title carried directly into the [[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid]] self-
presentation — a key thread in the **Elamite–Persian ethnogenesis** Potts traces.
