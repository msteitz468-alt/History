# The Hague

Seat of government and artistic center of the Netherlands where Vincent van Gogh lived and studied 1881–1883, his first sustained attempt at a professional artistic life.

## Description and Historical Role

The Hague was the residence of the Dutch royal family and home to the Hague School of painters (Mauve, Israëls, Maris brothers). It combined urban sophistication with access to Scheveningen beach and the surrounding dunes and polders.

## Significance

After his evangelical failure in the Borinage, Vincent arrived at The Hague determined to become a draftsman. His cousin Anton Mauve gave initial lessons in watercolor and drawing. Vincent began the relationship with Sien Hoornik (a pregnant former prostitute), produced dozens of figure studies of the poor and of Sien, and clashed with family over the liaison. He left The Hague in 1883 for Drenthe and then Nuenen after the relationship ended. The Hague years mark his commitment to drawing from life and the beginning of his identification with the urban and rural poor as subjects.
