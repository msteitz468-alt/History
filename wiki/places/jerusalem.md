# Jerusalem

## Overview

**Jerusalem** is the volume's example of a city **"created in stone and in
imagination"** — and *re-created* many times over — the case study of
[[yoffee-cwh-v3-2015|CWH Vol. III]] Ch. 20 (Ann Killebrew), in Part V, "Cities as
Creations." Where [[baghdad|Baghdad]] was willed into being once and
[[cahokia|Cahokia]] built in a single "big bang," Jerusalem's significance lies in
its **repeated foundation and re-foundation**, each time invested with sacred
meaning, until its power became as much imagined as material.

## A city created and re-created

Jerusalem was made and remade across millennia:
- A Bronze Age **Canaanite/Jebusite** town on a modest, defensible ridge with the
  Gihon spring.
- The **City of David** (~1000 BCE) — its arguable "creation" as an Israelite
  capital, followed by Solomon's Temple, making it a cultic center.
- Destroyed by **Babylon (586 BCE)**, rebuilt under Persian authority (Second
  Temple), monumentalized by **Herod**, then destroyed by **Rome (70 CE)** and
  refounded as the pagan colony **Aelia Capitolina**.
- Re-imagined as a **Christian holy city** under Byzantium (the Holy Sepulchre),
  then an **Islamic** sanctuary (the Dome of the Rock, 691 CE, on the Temple
  Mount/Haram al-Sharif), then a **Crusader** capital.

Each re-creation rebuilt the city in stone *and* re-inscribed it in the religious
imagination of, successively and simultaneously, **Judaism, Christianity, and
Islam**.

## Why this place mattered

Jerusalem demonstrates the volume's thesis that a city can be, above all, a
**creation of memory and meaning**: its enduring importance rests less on size,
trade, or administration than on its repeated **sanctification** and the
competing collective memories it anchors. It is the counterpart to Yoffee's
conclusion that cities "loom important... because they instantiate the images
that form historical imaginations." See [[founded-cities]].

## Historiography

Jerusalem's archaeology is exceptionally **contested** — entangled with biblical
text, religious claims, and modern politics. The scale and even existence of a
grand 10th-century BCE "City of David" is debated (minimalist vs maximalist
readings); stratigraphy is constrained by continuous occupation and sacred
access. The city is a standing example of how **memory and ideology shape**
the interpretation of an early city.
