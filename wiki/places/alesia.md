# Alesia

A hilltop oppidum of the Mandubii people in Gaul (traditionally located at Mont Auxois, near modern Alise-Sainte-Reine in Burgundy, though the identification has been debated), site of the climactic siege of Julius Caesar's Gallic War in 52 BCE. Having gathered the largest coalition army of the revolt, [[vercingetorix|Vercingetorix]] withdrew to Alesia's defensible heights after his cavalry was defeated in the field, prompting Caesar to invest the position with an extraordinary double line of fortifications: an inner circumvallation facing the besieged town and an outer contravallation facing a massive Gallic relief army summoned from across Gaul.

## Significance

Caesar's engineering feat — holding both lines simultaneously against attacks from within and without — allowed a numerically outnumbered Roman force to defeat both the besieged garrison and the relief army in the same operation, forcing Vercingetorix's surrender and effectively ending large-scale organized Gallic resistance to Roman conquest. The site's location was long disputed by antiquarians and archaeologists (with Alise-Sainte-Reine, endorsed by Napoleon III's own sponsored excavations, remaining the scholarly consensus location despite periodic revival of rival candidates).
