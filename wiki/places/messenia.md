# Messenia

Southwest Peloponnesian region west of the Taygetus; Mycenaean palace centre at Pylos; later the principal theatre of Spartan conquest and [[concepts/helotage|helotage]]; refounded as free Messene on Ithome in 369 BCE. [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] Ch.7 is the principal Hall-based treatment for Archaic Messenia.

## Geography and settlement

Lower Pamisos valley and surrounding highlands; Mount Ithome as strategic focus. Classical Messene is a fourth-century foundation, but excavations show eighth-century activity at Epaminondas’ site under Ithome (also named by Tyrtaeus). Dark Age population may have been ~10% of LHIIIB; Nikhoria shrinks then dwindles. In the twelfth–eleventh centuries Laconia and Messenia show steep site decline rather than new Dorian influx ([[controversies/dorian-migration-historicity]]).

## Spartan conquest and dependency

[[events/messenian-wars-archaic|Archaic Messenian Wars]] (chronology disputed): Tyrtaeus fr. 5 speaks of capturing **Messene**, not annexing all Messenia; progressive subjugation likely late eighth to ~600. Dependent population delivered large produce shares (Tyrtaeus frs. 6–7: half the fruits “like asses oppressed by heavy burdens”). Hall stresses a spectrum of free and unfree labor and perioikic communities (Thouriatai, Aithiaieis; coastal Asine/Methone refugees) rather than simple total enslavement.

## Unique outcome

“What was truly unique about the Messenians was not their dependency upon the Spartans but rather the fact that a very vocal Messenian expatriate community or diaspora, resident in South Italy, Sicily, North Africa, and especially Locrian Naupactus, was eventually able – with Theban help – to realize its dream of establishing a new, independent state in the Peloponnese.” Liberation 369 after Leuctra; new capital Messene on Ithome.

## Why it mattered

Messenia supplied the agrarian surplus and dependent labor that underwrote Classical Spartan military society; Messenian identity and resistance literature shaped fourth-century Peloponnesian geopolitics. Significance shifted from Mycenaean palace hinterland → Spartan helot landscape → free Hellenistic-era *polis* landscape.
