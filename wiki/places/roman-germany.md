# Roman Germany and the Rhine Frontier

The frontier zone along the **Rhine** — the militarized edge of the empire facing the Germanic
peoples, the base for Augustus' abortive attempt to conquer Germania to the Elbe, and after the
**[[battle-of-teutoburg-forest-9ce|Varian disaster]]** the enduring northern boundary of the
Roman world. Treated in [[bowman-cah-v10-1996|CAH X]] Ch. 13f (**C. Rüger**).

## The Rhine as Caesar's frontier

When Caesar left Gaul (50 BCE), its border lay on the **Rhine** — a line meant to secure the
north-west against migration. But Rüger stresses the river was a **frontier zone, not a clean
ethnic divide**: *Germani Cisrhenani* already lived on the left bank, the tribal map was a "crux"
where archaeology/linguistics (an east–west divide) and the historians' north–south Rhine line
cross. Between Caesar's departure and the arrival of Roman troops (16 BCE) — especially around
Agrippa's two periods of activity (39/38 and 19/18 BCE) — there was a deliberate policy of
**resettling right-bank Germans on the left bank** (Ubii, Batavi, and others). The
**Romanization** of the Rhineland nobility ran through the same *clientela* mechanism as in the
East: the prevalence of *Iulii, Tiberii, Claudii* among the tribal nobility (the leaders of the
revolt of AD 69–70) reflects citizenship granted in the triumviral and Augustan period in the
name of the new Caesar — sustained by the **vivid living memory of Julius Caesar** on the Rhine
(his sword kept as a relic in the temple of Mars at Cologne; bridge-building as *aemulatio
Caesaris*).

## The Augustan offensive (16 BCE – AD 17)

With pacification achieved elsewhere, Augustus turned (in "Caesarian fashion") to conquest
through a *bellum externum* aiming to make the land between the Danube bend at Vienna and the
mouth of the Weser **imperial territory**. From a reconnaissance base at **Novaesium/Neuss** (c.
16 BCE), the army built the great Rhine bases opposite the tributary mouths — **Mogontiacum/Mainz,
Vetera/Xanten, Noviomagus/Nijmegen, Fectio/Vechten** — and drove east along **four lines of
penetration** (the North Sea coast to the Weser; up the Lippe into Cheruscan land; from Mainz
through the Wetterau toward the Weser–Elbe; and a southern thrust toward the Marcomanni), with a
fifth from Basle to the Danube, secured by the annexation of **Noricum** and the Alpine passes.
The campaigns of **Drusus and Tiberius** (to 8 BCE) and **Domitius Ahenobarbus** (who reached
the Elbe) brought the area under Roman pressure — chieftains' sons like **[[arminius]]** gaining
officer rank and citizenship.

## The two altars and the planned province

The ideological centre of the project was the imperial cult: the **Ara Galliarum** at Lugdunum
(traditionally 12 BCE), followed by the **Ara Ubiorum** at Cologne — the latter created only
once "a firm basis had been laid for the occupation of the land between the Rhine and the Elbe,"
intended to anchor a **new province north of the Alps**.

## Disaster and the frontier

The project ended with the **[[battle-of-teutoburg-forest-9ce|Varian disaster]] (AD 9)** and was
definitively abandoned with the recall of Germanicus and the cessation of conquest east of the
Rhine by **AD 17**. The Rhine hardened into the permanent frontier, organized into the two
military commands that became the provinces of **Germania Superior** (upper, at Mainz) and
**Germania Inferior** (lower, at Cologne) — garrisoned by the largest concentration of legions
in the empire, and the cradle of the **Batavian Revolt** (Civilis, AD 69–70).

## The High Empire: from war-zone to province (CAH XI, Rüger)

[[bowman-cah-v11-2000|CAH XI]] Ch. 15 (also **Rüger**) carries the two Germanies through AD 70–192,
when — after the **Batavian revolt** closed the era of imperial attention — Germania "vanished from
the purview of imperial politics." Its themes:

- **Garrison reduction and the *limes*.** The Rhine legions, compromised as Vitellians, were cut
  from fourteen (under the Julio-Claudians) to four by the early second century (Xanten, Bonn,
  Mainz, Strasbourg). **Domitian's Chattan war** (AD 83) secured the right-bank **Wetterau** and
  began the **Upper German *limes*** — a patrol road and wooden towers, given a palisade under
  Hadrian, then stone towers and a rampart-and-ditch by the third century; Germania Inferior kept
  the **Rhine itself** as boundary. The legions stayed in the hinterland (Mainz, Strasbourg) as a
  strategic reserve behind ~16,000 auxiliaries on the line.
- **The making of provinces.** Around AD 85 the military districts were converted into the regular
  **provinces** of Germania Superior and Inferior — a "brilliant move of imperial propaganda"
  (Domitian's *Germania Capta* coins) that rhetorically "revised" the Varus disaster. The
  northernmost continental *colonia*, **Colonia Ulpia Traiana** (near Xanten), was founded under
  Trajan; *civitas* capitals and a few *municipia* (Nijmegen, Arae Flaviae) rose, freeing legions
  for the Dacian wars.
- **Citizenship and the villa landscape.** Veterans of legions and auxilia received **citizenship**
  on discharge and settled near their garrisons; by the mid-second century so many families held it
  that the four legions could be recruited from the Gallo-German hinterland alone, and the local
  elites were citizens. The *missiones agrariae* produced the remarkable **portico-villa landscape**
  west of Cologne (a *villa rustica* roughly every 2,000 m).
- **The army as economic motor.** The soldiers' thrice- (then four-) yearly **cash pay** drove the
  prosperity of the north-east Gallic economic zone (the *quadragesima Galliarum* tax ringing it).
  Guilds of **nautae and negotiatores** worked the river/sea network (Bordeaux–London–Cologne–Trier–
  Lyons); **stone** (Eifel millstones), **pottery** (the great *terra sigillata* works at
  **Rheinzabern**), and metals were exported, while the fertile valleys supplied the **annona
  militaris** of the two armies.
- **The third-century sequel.** Two great confederacies form across the frontier — the **Alemanni**
  (from c. 213, before the Upper German *limes*) and the **Franks** (after c. 257, east of the lower
  Rhine) — the prelude to the loss of the Agri Decumates.

## Significance

Rüger's frontier is the counterpart to Gruen's argument in
[[augustan-imperial-expansion|Ch. 4]]: the Rhine campaigns flowed from the **defence of Gaul**
and the *aemulatio* of Caesar as much as from any blueprint for an Elbe frontier; their failure
fixed the **Rhine–Danube line** that would define Roman Europe for centuries, and created the
heavily militarized Germanies whose armies would make and unmake emperors (Vitellius in
[[year-of-the-four-emperors-69ce|69]]).
