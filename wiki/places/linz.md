# Linz

City on the Danube in Upper Austria, capital of that province since the medieval period and, for a time in the sixteenth century, an occasional imperial residence under Emperor Frederick III. A regional administrative and commercial center through the Habsburg era, Linz remained a comparatively modest provincial city through the nineteenth century.

Linz holds particular significance as the childhood town of Adolf Hitler, who attended school there before failing entrance to the Vienna Academy of Fine Arts; Hitler retained a lifelong sentimental attachment to Linz, intending in his later plans to develop it (rather than Vienna, which he associated with personal humiliation and cosmopolitan decadence) into a grand cultural capital housing a vast museum for looted European art (the never-completed "Führermuseum"). The city's more recent history includes its role as an industrial center (notably steelworks) under and after Nazi rule.

## Significance

Linz's formative place in Hitler's biography — contrasted with his traumatic Vienna years — and his post-annexation plans to elevate it as a Nazi cultural capital illustrate the intersection of personal biography and ideological ambition in the Third Reich's cultural policy.
