# Beni (Llanos de Mojos)

A savanna region in the Beni department of lowland Bolivia containing one of the Americas' most extensive complexes of pre-Columbian earthworks: raised agricultural fields, canals, causeways connecting settlement mounds, and fish weirs, built by indigenous societies to manage a seasonally flooded landscape. The scale of engineering documented here — alongside comparable, though ecologically distinct, *terra preta* (Amazonian dark earth) evidence from forested Amazonia — has been central to revising Betty Meggers's mid-20th-century "counterfeit paradise" thesis that the tropical lowlands could never have sustained large, complex pre-Columbian populations. Researchers including William Denevan and Clark Erickson have used the Beni earthworks to argue for substantial indigenous populations and sophisticated landscape management prior to the demographic collapse following European contact — though, as with the broader Amazonian complexity debate, the precise population scale implied remains genuinely disputed.
