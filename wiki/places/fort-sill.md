# Fort Sill

**Fort Sill** (established 1869, Indian Territory / present-day Oklahoma) was the principal U.S. Army post and agency hub for the southern plains reservation system after the [[events/red-river-war-1874|Red River War]]. Free [[actors/comanches|Comanches]] (including [[actors/parker-quanah|Quanah Parker]]'s people) were brought in here in 1875; Quanah later built Star House nearby at Cache. The post remained a military center (artillery school) into the modern era; Geronimo and other Apache prisoners were also held in the Fort Sill orbit (Geronimo from 1894; Apache Cemetery later addressed 437 Quanah Road).

## Reservation geography

Post–[[events/medicine-lodge-treaty-1867|Medicine Lodge]], Comanche-Kiowa shared a **2.9-million-acre** reservation in southwestern Oklahoma (north of the Red River / south of the Washita region) — good land but a tiny fraction of peak Comanchería (~200 million acres) and excluding the best Texas bison plains. Early agency bungling (Leavenworth at Fort Cobb on the **Wichita** reservation; no rations; delayed annuities) is part of Medicine Lodge failure history; Fort Sill became the durable military-administrative center once permanent posts and Quaker/agent systems settled.

## Operational and administrative hub (Gwynne)

- **Agency under Lawrie Tatum then J. M. Haworth** in the Grant Peace Policy years; reservation often functioned as sanctuary — troops barred, base for Texas raids, stock driven across the Red River.
- **July 1874 (post–Adobe Walls):** Grant put agencies under military control; **Davidson** at Fort Sill ordered friendly Indians to register by 3 August with daily roll call; army free to pursue to the agency porch.
- **Mackenzie command:** 16 March 1875 [[actors/mackenzie-ranald-s|Mackenzie]] took Fort Sill command after the Palo Duro campaign; firm administrator (1876 renegade ultimatum — 20 minutes or kill all — worked). Officers' row where Mackenzie lived near Quanah's tipi after surrender.
- **Surrender wave 1875:** Lone Wolf/Kiowas (February); 825 Southern Cheyennes (March); Shaking Hand, Hears the Sunrise, Wild Horse (April) — disarmed, stock taken, internment **west of Fort Sill**. Other bands' warriors held in the **icehouse** with raw meat thrown over the walls.
- **Quahadi surrender:** **Noon 2 June 1875** — **407 Quahadis** arrived at **Signal Station a few miles west of Fort Sill** with ~1,500 horses and arms. Treated better than prior bands (no icehouse/guardhouse for Quanah's people); Mackenzie kept many of their horses: "I think better of this band than of any other on the reserve… I shall let them down as easily as I can." Pre-arrival 19 May letter about Cynthia Ann and Prairie Flower → both dead; start of Mackenzie–Quanah friendship.

## Reservation political economy (early years)

Gwynne: system as "shattering" — food rations + annuities ~$30,000/year for combined Comanche-Kiowa (~$10/person); shoddy goods often sold cheap; beef 1.5 lb/person/day issued on the hoof with 50% yield assumption failing in winter; flour/coffee/sugar/salt less than half a soldier ration when it arrived at all. Weekly issue as diversion: warriors ran down ration cows with bows/arrows/pistols. "The taibos had taken away everything that had defined Comanche existence and offered nothing but crude squalor in its place."

## Why it mattered

Fort Sill was the **hinge between free Comanchería and confined tribal life** — enrollment point, prison, agency warehouse, and later civilizational theater of Quanah's Star House politics, leasing, and peyote. Military significance persisted (artillery school; Apache detention) long after the Red River War closed free horse-tribe dominion on the southern plains.
