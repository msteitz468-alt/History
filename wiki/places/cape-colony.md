# The Cape Colony

The **Cape Colony** began as a **[[dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]] reprovisioning
station** founded by **Jan van Riebeeck at Table Bay in 1652** — the only part of sub-Saharan Africa with a
Mediterranean climate suited to European settlement — and became the seed of settler South Africa.
Documented for the wiki through **[[reader-africa-biography|Reader]]**, its history is a case study in how
permanent European settlement collided with indigenous land-use and generated a slave-and-frontier society.

## From victualling station to settler colony

Reader stresses the **clash of land-use systems**. The indigenous **Khoisan** were migratory pastoralists
(and hunter-gatherers, the "Bushmen") whose seasonal rounds the Dutch settlement disrupted by **staying
put** and monopolizing grazing. When the Company freed nine employees as land-owning "free burghers" in
1657 and imported **slaves** from 1658 (Madagascar, East and West Africa, Indonesia), the station became a
colony. A Khoisan war (1659) ended with van Riebeeck's assertion of a **"right of conquest."** The Khoisan
were then broken by **dispossession and a smallpox epidemic (1713)** that killed perhaps nine in ten;
survivors, freed slaves, and people of mixed descent became the Dutch-speaking **"Cape Coloured"**
underclass.

## The frontier and the Trekboers

As the **Trekboers** (semi-nomadic cattle farmers) pushed inland — granted huge 6,000-acre loan-farms —
the frontier advanced ~800 km east in a century, with **commandos** waging "Bushman Wars" that exterminated
Khoisan resistance and seized children as unpaid "apprentices." From c. 1770 the Boers met the
westward-expanding **Xhosa** (Bantu-speakers) in the Zuurveld, beginning a long series of **Frontier Wars**
(from 1779) over grazing.

## British conquest

Britain seized the Cape in **1795** and definitively in **1806** (confirmed 1814), valuing it as a
"stepping stone" to India. British rule combined a pro-settler frontier policy (expelling the Xhosa beyond
the Fish River; importing 6,000 settlers in 1820 as a buffer) with growing **abolitionist** pressure that
made the Cape's slave economy an embarrassment — a tension that helped drive the Boers' later Great Trek.

## Why it mattered

The Cape created the **demographic and racial template of South Africa**: a settler society built on
slavery, frontier conquest, and a chronically "short" (i.e. cheap-labour-hungry) economy — the
preconditions for the [[southern-african-mineral-revolution|mineral revolution]] and ultimately apartheid.
Its frontier turmoil also fed, and was fed by, the upheavals of the **[[mfecane|mfecane]]** to the east.
