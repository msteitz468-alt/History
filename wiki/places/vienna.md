# Vienna

## Geographic Description

Capital of the Habsburg Empire / Austria-Hungary until 1918; major European metropolis on the Danube, cultural and political center of Central Europe. Multi-ethnic, with large German, Czech, Polish, Jewish, and other populations. By 1900 a city of ~2 million; site of intense artistic, musical, and intellectual modernism (Freud, Klimt, Mahler, Wittgenstein circle) alongside political antisemitism and nationalist tensions.

## History of Control and Settlement

Long Habsburg capital. In the Long 19th Century: center of the 1848 revolutions, site of neo-absolutism under Franz Joseph, capital of the Dual Monarchy after 1867. Jewish community large and prominent in commerce, professions, and culture; also target of rising political antisemitism (Karl Lueger's Christian Socials won the mayoralty in 1897 after imperial resistance). Pan-German and völkisch movements active alongside liberal and socialist currents.

## Why This Place Mattered

For [[actors/adolf-hitler]]: the site of his decisive formation 1907–1913. After Linz school failures and mother's death, Hitler moved to Vienna, suffered double rejection by the Academy of Fine Arts, lived in men's hostels (Brigittenau), painted postcards for subsistence, and underwent the crystallization of his antisemitic, social-Darwinist, pan-German worldview. "Wherever I went, I now saw Jews..."; exposure to Lueger, Schönerer, Lanz von Liebenfels *Ostara*, extensive reading in hostels. The "indelible mark" of Vienna turned personal failure and resentment into ideological system. "The Vienna years were over. They had indelibly marked Hitler’s outlook."

Economically and culturally: hub of Central European modernism and the crisis of the multi-ethnic empire; "laboratory" for the radical right that would later destroy it.

## Historiography

Kershaw (Range 01) uses Vienna as the primary site of Hitler's "fantasy and failure" and the making of the "drop-out." Stresses ordinariness of the poverty and artistic marginality rather than romantic genius narrative. Primary traces: Academy records, surviving Hitler paintings/postcards from the period, hostel documentation, police records, Hitler's own retrospective (coloured).
