# Bohemia

Historical region and kingdom in Central Europe, core of the modern Czech Republic. A major centre of religious and political ferment in the Late Middle Ages, most notably the Hussite revolution and wars (1419–1436), which made it a "crucible of religious warfare" (Housley).

## Geographic and Political Significance

Bohemia lay at the intersection of German, Polish, Hungarian, and Austrian spheres. Rich in silver (Kutná Hora) and with a strong urban and noble class, it was unusually sensitive to church wealth, ethnic tensions (Czech–German), and reform currents from England (Wyclif) and elsewhere.

## Key Role in Religious Warfare

Site of Jan Hus's reform movement, the Tábor radical community, Žižka's military innovations, repeated defeats of papal-imperial crusades, and the evolution of "sanctified" defensive war with strong national and scriptural overtones. Also produced one of the period's most thoroughgoing rejections of religious war (Peter Chelcicky).
