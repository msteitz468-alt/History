# Auvers-sur-Oise

Village north of Paris where Vincent van Gogh spent his last 70 days (20 May – 29 July 1890) under the care of Dr. Paul Gachet and painted his final major works.

## Description and Historical Role

Auvers was a quiet artists' colony favored by Daubigny, Pissarro, and others. It offered Vincent proximity to Paris (Theo), open fields, wheat, and thatched cottages that recalled his Dutch roots while allowing rapid production.

## Significance

In Auvers Vincent painted at least 70 works, including *Wheatfield with Crows*, *Daubigny's Garden*, portraits of Gachet and his daughter, and numerous double-square canvases of village streets and fields. On 27 July 1890 he sustained a gunshot wound to the abdomen and died two days later. The precise circumstances remain disputed (suicide vs. accidental shooting by local boys). Theo arrived in time for the deathbed. Vincent is buried in the Auvers cemetery beside his brother.
