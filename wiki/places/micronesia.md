# Micronesia

**Micronesia** ("small islands") spans the small high islands and coral atolls of the
north-western Pacific: Palau, Guam and the Northern Marianas, the Federated States of Micronesia
(Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei, Kosrae), the Marshall Islands, Kiribati and Nauru. A sub-region of
[[oceania|Oceania]], it includes some of the most demanding atoll environments humans have
settled and the great navigational traditions of the Carolinian and Marshallese voyagers.

In [[denoon-cambridge-pacific-islanders-1997|the Cambridge History of the Pacific Islanders]],
Micronesia is marked above all by a uniquely heavy colonial succession — Spanish, then German,
then Japanese, then American rule — and by the [[pacific-nuclear-testing|nuclear testing]] the
United States conducted at Bikini and Enewetak in the Marshall Islands, which displaced whole
communities and left enduring contamination and health consequences. Much of post-war Micronesia
passed through the US-administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands.

## Significance

Micronesia concentrates two of the volume's central post-1941 themes — the militarisation of the
Pacific and the nuclear age — in their most acute form, and raises the sharpest questions about
sovereignty, compensation, and dependency in the freely-associated states.

## Fischer (2002): early settlement, monuments, and a hybrid social order

[[fischer-history-pacific-islands-2002|Fischer (2002)]] dates Palau's settlement to c. 4,500 BP — "centuries before Lapita emerged in the Bismarcks" — from Island Southeast Asia, with a second incursion (c. 3,300 BP) reaching the Marianas, whose "Marianas Red" pottery resembles ancient Philippine ware (L1269–1280). Kiribati was settled c. 2,200 BP, the Marshalls c. 2,000 BP, and the Outliers Nukuoro/Kapingamarangi only c. 900 BP (L1307–1320). He reads the Marianas **latte** pillars (from c. AD 900) as "an indigenous transition … from wood to stone" rather than foreign intrusion (L1323–1335), and **Nan Madol** on Pohnpei — ~100 basalt-walled islets, the Saudeleur seat begun in the ninth century — as the "Venice of the Pacific" (L1340–1350). Socially, "in emphasizing inherited rank, Micronesia followed Polynesia. But in acquiring status, Micronesia followed Melanesia" — power came through warfare and the display of wealth (L2039–2044). Micronesians "probably led the world" in canoe quality and navigation (L2064–2070); their art is that of "sober utilitarians," celebrated for geometric weaving (L2747–2756).
