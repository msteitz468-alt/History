# Pasargadae

**Pasargadae**, in the highland plain of Fars, was the first dynastic capital of the
[[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid Empire]], founded by [[cyrus-the-great|Cyrus the Great]] after the
conquest of Sardis, and the site of his tomb on its six-tiered podium. According to
[[sources/briant-from-cyrus-to-alexander-2002|Briant]] its architecture was a deliberate dynastic
program synthesizing Lydian, Babylonian, Assyrian, Egyptian, and Phoenician elements into a unified
imperial statement — not an eclectic collage — and its irrigated "paradise" gardens, structurally
integrated into the palace complex, were an Achaemenid innovation on older Assyrian models.

## Significance

- **Dynastic foundation.** Conceived as a whole by Cyrus and his counsellors, Pasargadae expressed
  the new empire's claim to gather the conquered civilizations under one Persian roof.
- **The disputed inscriptions.** The "I am Cyrus the King, an Achaemenian" inscriptions at
  Pasargadae are now widely regarded as **forgeries by [[darius-i|Darius I]]**, who used them to
  retroject his own [[achaemenid-royal-ideology|Achaemenid]] lineage onto Cyrus — making the site a
  key piece of evidence for Darius's manufacture of dynastic continuity (see [[gaumata-magush]]).
- **Ritual centre.** Pasargadae remained an ideological apex of the dynasty: the royal investiture
  required the new king to don Cyrus's robe and eat a ritual meal of figs, turpentine, and sour
  milk, in a temple of the warrior goddess (Anahita) — linking each reign to the founder. It kept
  this status, alongside [[persepolis|Persepolis]], to the end of the empire.
