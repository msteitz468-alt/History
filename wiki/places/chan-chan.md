# Chan Chan

## Overview

**Chan Chan**, in the Moche Valley near modern Trujillo, was the capital of the kingdom of
[[chimor|Chimor]] and the largest pre-Columbian city of the South American coast — an adobe
metropolis of perhaps 20,000+ people covering some 20 km². Its core comprised a series of
great walled **ciudadelas** (royal compounds), each associated with a Chimú king and, by the
split-inheritance interpretation, sealed as a mortuary palace at his death while his successor
built anew.

The city anchored an intervalley **hydraulic economy** of canals and reclaimed fields and
concentrated the metallurgical and textile workshops that made Chimú wealth famous. After the
[[inca-empire|Inka]] conquest of the north coast (c. 1470), Chan Chan's craftsmen were
resettled toward Cuzco and the city declined.

## Significance

As discussed in [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|Volume III]], Chan Chan is the
type-case of Andean **coastal urbanism**: monumental, planned, hydraulically dependent, and
organized around dynastic compounds. Its scale shows that complex urban life on the desert
coast rivaled and predated highland imperial capitals.
