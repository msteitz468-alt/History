# Zanzibar

An island archipelago off the Swahili coast of East Africa (modern Tanzania), long a node of Indian Ocean trade linking the African interior to Arabia, Persia, and India. Portugal held nominal suzerainty from the sixteenth century until Omani Arab forces, allied with local rulers, expelled them by 1698, bringing Zanzibar into the Omani sphere.

The island's importance transformed after 1840, when [[actors/seyyid-said|Seyyid Said]] moved the seat of the Omani Al Bu Said sultanate from Muscat to Zanzibar Town, developing a clove-plantation economy worked by slave labor and attracting Indian (chiefly Gujarati) merchant capital. Zanzibar became the principal entrepôt for the ivory and slave caravans reaching deep into the Great Lakes region, and its sultans' customs and naval power extended along the coast from Mozambique to Somalia. After Said's death in 1856 the dominions split into separate Zanzibar and Muscat sultanates. British anti-slave-trade pressure and commercial rivalry steadily eroded Zanzibari independence, culminating in the 1890 declaration of a British protectorate; the sultanate persisted nominally until the 1964 revolution merged Zanzibar with Tanganyika to form Tanzania.
