# Yellow River Valley

The **middle and lower Yellow River valley** — Shandong, northern Jiangsu, Henan, southern
Shanxi and Hebei, central Shaanxi, and eastern Gansu — is the heartland in which the first
Chinese states evolved, and the geographic frame of [[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's
*The Chinese Neolithic*]] (study span ~7000–1500 BC). It contains the
[[central-plains-china|Central Plains]] core and the [[yiluo-basin|Yiluo basin]] where
[[erlitou-culture|Erlitou]], the earliest urban site, arose.

## Geography and an Unstable River

The **middle** Yellow River crosses the **loess plateau**, whose fine, erodible soils made it
both fertile and dangerously sediment-laden. Soil erosion rose ~100% over the last 3,000
years (50% in the last century alone), and Neolithic farming itself may have raised the
riverbed by adding sediment. The **lower** river is notoriously mobile: since the Warring
States period it has burst its banks **1,500+ times** and changed course **26 times**, its
flood zone spanning 250,000 km² between the Hai and Huai rivers.

Two major **prehistoric course shifts** bear directly on settlement history: for most of the
Neolithic the river ran through the Hebei plains to Bohai Bay; around **4,600 BP** it shifted
to the northern Jiangsu plains (Yellow Sea); around **4,000 BP** it switched back to Hebei.
These shifts, plus marine transgression on the coast (sea level 3–5 m higher ~7,000–5,500
BP), drove population movements and reconfigured which lands were habitable — the lowlands
of central Henan opened up as lakes and marshes shrank in the drier late Neolithic.

## Significance

The valley hosts the whole developmental arc Liu reconstructs: from [[yangshao-culture|Yangshao]]
villages and lower-river [[dawenkou-culture|Dawenkou]] mortuary hierarchies, through the
dense, competitive [[longshan-culture|Longshan]] chiefdoms, to the first state at
[[erlitou|Erlitou]] in the [[yiluo-basin|Yiluo basin]] (~1800 BC). Environmental
catastrophe here — flooding, course change, transgression — is treated as a **necessary but
not sufficient** precondition of state formation (see [[early-china-state-formation]]); the
legend of **Yu the Great** taming the floods to found the [[xia-dynasty|Xia]] mythologises
exactly this dynamic.
