# Monticello

Monticello ("little mountain") was the plantation home and architectural masterpiece of Thomas Jefferson on a hilltop in Albemarle County, Virginia, overlooking the Rivanna River and the Southwest Mountains. Construction began in 1769 on land inherited from Peter Jefferson; the first version was largely complete by the time of Jefferson's marriage in 1772. Jefferson continued to redesign and expand it throughout his life, incorporating neoclassical, Palladian, and innovative elements (the dome, octagonal rooms, dumbwaiters, and mechanical devices) drawn from his reading of architecture and his observations in Europe.

It served as the center of Jefferson's domestic and intellectual life, the site of his library and experiments in agriculture and natural history, and the household that included his daughters, grandchildren, and the enslaved community whose labor sustained the plantation. British raiders under Banastre Tarleton came within minutes of capturing Jefferson there in June 1781 during the Revolutionary War invasions of Virginia.

Jefferson died at Monticello on July 4, 1826. The house and grounds remain a major historic site and museum, administered by the Thomas Jefferson Foundation. It embodies both the highest aspirations of Enlightenment republicanism and the contradictions of a slaveholding society.
