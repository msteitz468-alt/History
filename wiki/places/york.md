# York (Eoforwic / Jorvik)

The chief city of [[places/northumbria|Deiran Northumbria]], built on Roman **Eboracum**,
and the great Anglo-Scandinavian metropolis of the north. Seat of an archbishopric (a
metropolitan see restored by Wilfrid in the 660s, an archbishopric from 735) and of an
outstanding early library, "probably … destroyed by viking attack."

Captured by the [[events/great-heathen-army-865|Great Army]] in **867** during a
Northumbrian succession dispute, York became the centre of Scandinavian power north of the
Humber. Its kings sponsored a sophisticated coinage (c.895–905) blending Christian symbols
with a Carolingian *Karolus* monogram, and the **Coppergate** area was reorganised into
regular trading tenements c.900 — though the buildings are "of a type common to the trading
centers of north-west Europe, and not specific to a Scandinavian milieu." Its archbishops
acted as political **prince-bishops** (Wulfstan I receiving Óláf Guthfrithson in 939,
backing Eric Bloodaxe in 952). **Æthelstan** captured York and razed its fortifications in
927. As a flourishing emporium it was "a positive point of contact between native and
settler"; Scandinavian administrative vocabulary (**wapentakes, ridings**) penetrated
Yorkshire.
