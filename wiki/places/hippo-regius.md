# Hippo Regius

**Hippo Regius** — the "Royal Port" (*Hippo* from the Punic word for harbour), modern **Annaba**
(formerly Bône) in eastern Algeria — was the second port of Roman Africa and the see of
**[[augustine-of-hippo|Augustine]]** for nearly forty years. An ancient town predating Roman rule
by centuries, at the mouth of the river Seybouse below the Djebel Edough headland, it linked the
Numidian grain-and-tax hinterland to Rome; the Divjak letters reveal it was also a conduit for
convoys of **[[slavery-in-early-christianity|kidnapped free peasants sold into slavery]]**.

Hippo was a *civitas Romana* with a theatre seating five or six thousand, a large public bath, and
a forum with a temple on the site of an older sanctuary of Baal-Hammon. Augustine's "Christian
quarter" — church, baptistery, bishop's house, and the "Monastery in the Garden" — was newer and
modest, built partly over a former dyer's yard near the harbour. Its economy rested on grain, not
maritime trade (Augustine feared the sea and travelled overland to **[[carthage|Carthage]]**), and
its rural hinterland spoke Punic rather than Latin, forcing Augustine to seek dialect-speaking
priests. The Catholic community was a harassed minority in a region dominated by
**[[donatism|Donatism]]** until the imperial coercion after 405 tipped the town, over Augustine's
episcopate, into becoming fully Christian.

Augustine was seized here and ordained priest in 391 under Bishop Valerius, made bishop c. 395/6,
and turned his monastic household into a seminary that supplied bishops across Numidia. In the
winter of 429–430 the Arian **[[vandal-kingdom|Vandals]]** of Genseric besieged the fortified town
by land and sea; Augustine died within it on 28 August 430. A year later Hippo was evacuated and
partly burnt, though Augustine's library reportedly survived — and within a generation a Vandal's
Suebian wife was buried in Augustine's own basilica.

## From Gibbon, *Decline and Fall* (via *City of God*)

Attributed claims from [[sources/gibbon-decline-and-fall-1776]]; not asserted as modern fact.

- **Stephen miracles at Hippo.** In his notice of Augustine’s *[[city-of-god|City of God]]*, Gibbon flags the African miracle dossier of St. Stephen — **seventy or more** cures at Hippo, including three reported resurrections — as emblematic of an age that made wonders ordinary (Enlightenment irony; not modern medical history).
