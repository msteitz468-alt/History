# La Cotte de St. Brelade

**La Cotte de St. Brelade** (Jersey, Channel Islands) is a coastal cave site with a long stratigraphic sequence dating mostly to the penultimate glaciation. It contains two notable piles of mammoth and woolly rhino bones interpreted as the residues of specific Middle Palaeolithic hunting episodes.

## Hunting Episode Evidence

In layer 3 the bone pile consists of nine skulls and two lower jaws of mammoth and comparatively few limb bones. Skulls and limb bones of woolly rhinoceros also occur. No other animal species were found in these heaps, which were piled against the rock wall. Stone artifacts abundant elsewhere were absent near the bones.

"Scott has interpreted these concentrations as the end result of two different hunting episodes. The pile in layer 3 resulted from the middle palaeolithic hunters stampeding the mammoths and rhinos over the headland of which the cave of La Cotte forms a part. The ageing evidence... shows that a small herd of adult mammoth females and their young were driven in this hunting episode." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~1000-1020)

Layer 6 has rarer skulls, suggesting primary butchery occurred elsewhere and only selected portions were brought back and stacked.

## Methodological Importance

The site demonstrates the role of distance and carcass size in determining what is transported from kill site to shelter. It allows isolation of specific activities even within complex palimpsest deposits that include natural and carnivore contributions.
