# Ystrad-fflur (Strata Florida)

Cistercian abbey in Ceredigion, Wales. Founded in 1164 by Robert Fitz Stephen, lord of Pennardd; new impetus and patronage provided by the Lord Rhys (Rhys ap Gruffudd) of the independent Welsh kingdom of Deheubarth. The abbey became the mausoleum of the princely Deheubarth dynasty, with some eight representatives buried there. It served as a ‘significant custodian’ of the native Welsh cultural tradition.

## Narrative

The original site (Yr Hen Fynachlog, ‘The old monastery’) was discovered in the 19th century some 3 km from the present location; excavations revealed a stone-built church some 38.4 m long. Work on the new site (Strata Florida II) was under way by 1184 under Lord Rhys patronage; the chancel, crossing, and transepts were substantially complete by 1201 when the community occupied it. The original foundation was probably abandoned in the early 13th century. Building on church and cloister continued until at least 1250.

The completed abbey was damaged by fire in 1286 and during occupation by the forces of Edward I in the revolt of 1294–5. Rebuilding was interrupted when the monastery became a strong point for the forces of Henry IV in 1407 during the rebellion of Owain Glyndŵr. Work on the fabric, especially the church, continued until the eve of the Reformation. The abbey was suppressed in 1539 during the reign of Henry VIII.

The poet Dafydd ap Gwilym is reputed to have been buried within the precincts in the late 14th century. In the late 13th century, a now-lost Latin work forming the basis of *Brut y Tywysogyon* (‘The Chronicle of the Princes’) was composed here.
