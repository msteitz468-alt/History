# Stonehenge

**Stonehenge** (and the associated complex including Durrington Walls) is the iconic monument complex of late Neolithic / early Bronze Age Wessex, southern England. It exemplifies the construction of large-scale communal monuments, landscape organization, and the social changes that accompanied agricultural intensification and emerging differentiation in temperate Europe.

## Treatment in Champion et al. 1984

The source lists Stonehenge among principal sites for the post-3200 BCE period of settlement expansion and socio-economic change (Fig. 6.1 caption in the assigned range text). It is grouped with Durrington Walls as a key location in the British sequence.

Earlier in Ch. 5 the authors discuss the broader context of megalithic and barrow monuments in Atlantic/western Europe, of which Stonehenge forms a later, more elaborate part:

"stone monuments ('barrows' and 'megalithic tombs') to hold burials whose numbers again vary widely from area to area"

"Many barrows may reflect the same situation, with all ages and both sexes represented in them and little differentiation in goods. This is a particularly Atlantic or western European phenomenon from the fifth millennium on..."

Ard-marks preserved under barrows in southern England (e.g. South Street, Wiltshire) provide evidence for early plough use in the fourth millennium, part of the agricultural intensification that preceded and enabled the monumental landscape of Wessex:

"the existence of a variety of ploughs, light and heavy, is seen in marks scored in the subsoil and preserved under barrows dating from the earlier fourth millennium, from southern England to Poland..."

"The development of the plough may have been related to the secondary phase of agricultural colonization on to poorer, often drier soils in north and north-west Europe..."

## Significance

Stonehenge represents the culmination of trends described for the early farming period: communal labour for monument construction, the use of monuments as foci for dispersed communities, and (in later phases) increasing investment in ritual and display that signalled or produced social differentiation. The source frames these within the "gradual intensification of the agricultural economy" and the "possibilities for further development" visible by the end of the fourth millennium.

## Collection Note

Stonehenge is the pre-eminent example in the collection of a Neolithic monument complex whose scale and elaboration reflect both the achievements and the social transformations of early farming societies in temperate Europe. Later Bronze Age phases link it to the prestige economies and hierarchies treated in subsequent chapters of the source.
