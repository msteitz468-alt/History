# Begram (Kapisa)

## Geography and Control

Ancient **Kapisa**, c. 80 km north of Kabul near the junction of the Ghurband and Panjshir
rivers, one of the capitals of the [[kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]]. The site comprises the
Burj-i Abdullah enclosure and a "New Royal City" stratified above Greco-Bactrian layers; it
was destroyed by [[sasanian-empire|Sasanian]] forces by c. 262 CE.

## The Begram Treasure

Begram's fame rests on the cache French archaeologists found in the 1930s in the
thick-walled, blocked-up **Rooms 10 and 13** of the New Royal City — a spectacular
assemblage of luxury goods drawn from across Eurasia and, in
[[benjamin-empires-ancient-eurasia-2018|Benjamin's]] words, "striking evidence of the vast
size and incredibly diverse nature" of First-Silk-Roads-Era luxury trade:

- **Carved ivories and bone** from India (1,000+ pieces, women and river creatures);
- **Glassware** from Syria and Roman Egypt (vessels depicting the Pharos of Alexandria and
  an African leopard hunt);
- **Bronzes** from Egypt and the Roman Empire;
- **Lacquerware** from [[han-dynasty|Han]] China;
- molded **stucco** of apparent Greek origin.

[CONTROVERSY] Originally read as a royal "art museum" or treasure hidden late in the Kushan
period, the cache is now usually dated to the **first/early second century CE** — the era of
Kushan stability — which makes it more likely **merchants' stock collected for further
distribution** than a royal hoard (Mehendale 1996: "a point of consumption, of collection
for further distribution, of active trading, or a combination thereof"). The Roman glass
most probably arrived by sea to [[barygaza|Barygaza]] and then ~2,000 km overland up the
Indus and through the Khyber Pass.

## Significance

Begram is the densest single material snapshot of how [[kushan-empire|Kushan]] territory
functioned as the **convergence point** of the overland and [[indian-ocean-trade|maritime]]
[[silk-roads|Silk Roads]].
