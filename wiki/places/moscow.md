# Moscow

Moscow is the historic capital of Russia, emerging as the political center of the Russian lands during the Appanage period and serving as the capital of the Grand Duchy of Moscow, Tsardom of Russia, Soviet Union, and modern Russian Federation.

## Events and Significance (from Freeze (ed.) 2002, lines 600–3800)
Centre of Muscovite expansion and administration in 16th c.: Kremlin ensemble (Dormition Cathedral copied from Vladimir model, replicated elsewhere) symbolized power and conquests; "the centre-piece of the ensemble was the Dormition ( Uspenskii) Cathedral" (l2149–2151). Ivan III reconstructed churches/palaces post-1470s. Oprichnina targeted lucrative areas around Moscow (l1479). Time of Troubles: Polish occupation under Seven Boyars 1610–12; liberated Oct 1612 by Minin/Pozharskii second levy + Cossacks; "the ‘third levy’... liberated Moscow in October 1612" (l2535). 1648 uprising: "major uprising in June 1648, which together with the fires that swept through Moscow, cost approximately two thousand lives" (l2715); led to Ulozhenie. Copper uprising 1662: mobs to Kolomenskoe summer residence (l2736–2741). 1682 Streltsy massacre in Kremlin (l3155); Peter fled 1689 to Trinity-Sergius (l3265). Peter’s early play regiments at Preobrazhenskoe (near Moscow); 1698 Streltsy executions post-Grand Embassy (l3517). "Moscow remained the old capital and largest city, but after 1710 Peter visited it sparingly"; capital transferred to St Petersburg ~1713 (l3785–3787).

## Processes Here
- Rise of service landholding and enserfment policies centred on Moscow élite (pomest'e grants, forbidden years legislation).
- Church and state rituals (pilgrimages, processions) projecting unity from Kremlin.
- Westernizing experiments (theatre at Preobrazhenskoe) and backlash (Streltsy, Old Believers).

## Historiography Note
Kollmann stresses Moscow as minimalist centre tolerating regional autonomies while mobilizing resources; Torke/Alexander highlight its role in crises and Peter's pivot away from it. Collection coverage strong for Muscovite social/political history.
