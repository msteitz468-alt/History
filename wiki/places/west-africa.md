# West Africa

**West Africa** — the basins of the Senegal and Niger river systems and all the land
south of them to the Atlantic, together with the Lake Chad basin — is the regional
stage for one of the densest concentrations of indigenous state-building in
sub-Saharan Africa. This anchor page summarises the **geographic and human framework**
that organises the region's history; the great polities and processes have their own
pages (linked below). Documented for the wiki through A. L. Mabogunje's opening chapter
in [[ajayi-crowder-history-west-africa-v1-1971|*History of West Africa* Vol. I]] (1971).

## The ecological zones

The defining geographic fact is a **succession of vegetation belts running east–west**,
which structures nearly everything else:

- **Sahara** — desert, the northern barrier and (after the camel) the highway of
  trans-Saharan commerce.
- **Sahel** — the semi-arid grassland fringe, contact zone with North Africa.
- **Savannah (the Sudan)** — open grassland; the **historic centre of population and
  state-building**, where cereal agriculture (sorghum, millet), cattle, long-distance
  trade, craft production, towns, and the great empires concentrated.
- **Forest** — the tropical rain-forest belt near the coast; historically a **refuge
  zone** of smaller, more fragmented societies (no draft animals, porterage-only trade,
  limited urbanism — the Yoruba being the great exception), broken by powerful forest
  kingdoms (Benin, Oyo, the Akan, Dahomey).
- **Atlantic coast** — the lagoon-and-creek margin that became, after c. 1500, the new
  frontier of European contact, the [[atlantic-slave-trade|slave trade]], and the
  "legitimate commerce" that followed.

## Peoples and languages

Mabogunje's ethnographic survey ranges across the **Mande** (Soninke founders of
[[ghana-empire|Ghana]]; Malinke founders of [[mali-empire|Mali]]; Bambara), the
**Senegambian** Wolof, Sereer, and Tukulor; the **[[songhay-empire|Songhay]]** of the
Niger bend; the **[[mossi-states|Mossi]]** of the Volta headwaters; the **[[hausa-city-states|Hausa]]**
and the **Kanuri/Kanembu** of the [[kanem-bornu|Chad basin]] (the Sef dynasty — perhaps
the longest dynastic reign on record, c. 8th c.–1846); the forest peoples (Igbo, Edo of
[[benin-kingdom|Benin]], Yoruba, Nupe, Fon of [[dahomey|Dahomey]], Akan/Asante); and the
**[[fulani|Fulani]]** pastoralists dispersed across the whole savannah, whose sedentary,
Islamised kinsmen would launch the 19th-century [[west-african-jihads|jihads]].

## The "movement south"

Mabogunje's organising thesis is a long-run **southward movement of population** from
savannah toward forest and coast, driven first by a "push" from the north (Saharan
desiccation, Berber/Arab pressure, the spread of Islam) and later by a European "pull"
on the coast — the slave trade, then colonial export agriculture, then the growth of
coastal port-capitals. Against environmental determinism he insists these were "acts
resulting from human decisions"; geography was *permissive*, granting locational
advantage to the grassland, not deterministic.

## History of control (overview)

The region's political history runs broadly **north-to-south following the trade**: the
Sahelian gold-and-salt empires ([[ghana-empire|Ghana]] → [[mali-empire|Mali]] →
[[songhay-empire|Songhay]]) on the [[trans-saharan-trade|trans-Saharan]] termini; the
central-Sudan states ([[kanem-bornu|Kanem-Bornu]], the [[hausa-city-states|Hausa]]); the
forest and coastal kingdoms of the Atlantic era ([[benin-kingdom|Benin]],
[[oyo-empire|Oyo]], [[asante-empire|Asante]], [[dahomey|Dahomey]], the
[[niger-delta-city-states|Niger Delta city-states]]); the [[west-african-jihads|jihad
states]] and the [[sokoto-caliphate|Sokoto Caliphate]] of the 19th century; and finally
European colonial partition (see [[scramble-for-africa]]) and the modern nation-states.

## Why this place mattered

West Africa was the **southern terminus of the trans-Saharan gold trade** that financed
medieval North Africa and, via it, parts of the Mediterranean economy; the **hearth of
indigenous sub-Saharan state formation** (see [[stateless-societies]] and
[[hamitic-hypothesis-african-states]]); and, after 1500, the principal source region of
the **Atlantic slave trade** whose demographic scar Mabogunje reads in the depopulated
"Middle Belt."
