# Cyrene

**Cyrene** was the great Greek city of the North African coast — the foundation from
the island of **Thera** that became, under its **Battiad** kings, the richest and
best-documented Greek colony of the Archaic period, and the one for which "anything
approaching history can be written." It is Graham's paradigm of the colonization
process. [[boardman-cah-v3p3-1982|CAH Vol. III, Part 3]] (Ch. 37, A. J. Graham)
treats it.

## The foundation from Thera

Cyrene's foundation (c. 631 BCE) is the **best-attested of all Archaic colonizations**,
preserved both in Herodotus (IV.150–158) — a narrative bearing all the marks of the
*ktisis* genre, with its Delphic oracles and folk-tale motifs — and in the famous
**foundation decree of Thera**, inscribed (in a fourth-century copy that quotes the
original "oath of the founders") on stone. The decree records:

- the decision of overpopulated, drought-stricken **Thera** to send a colony;
- **conscription on pain of death** (one son from each family) to man it, with a
  solemn **oath and curse-ceremony** (wax images melted) binding both colonists and
  those who stayed;
- the dispatch of just **two penteconters — about 200 men** — under the oikist
  **Battus**, who consulted **[[delphi|Delphi]]** (the oracle reportedly directing him
  to Libya).

After a false start on the offshore island of Platea, the colonists settled the
fertile upland spring of Cyrene. Battus **became king** — the special hereditary
privileges of the Battiad kings (Herodotus IV.161) are the one clear case of
*inequality* in an Archaic foundation, manifestly exceptional — and at his death
became the **founder-hero**, worshipped at his tomb in the agora; his is the first
clearly attested oikist hero-cult (Pindar, *Pythian* V, praises "the splendid
sanctuaries Battus established").

## Greeks and Libyans

Cyrene is a prime case in the **intermarriage** debate: Herodotus names only men in
the expedition, there is good evidence of marriage between Greek men and **Libyan
women**, and the women of Cyrene and Barca kept Libyan-style food taboos. Growth
brought a second wave of settlers under **Battus II** and friction over land,
provoking Libyan resistance: the Libyan king Adikran appealed to Egypt, and
**[[saite-egypt|Apries]] sent an army against Cyrene (c. 570)** — which the Cyrenaeans
**crushed**, a defeat that helped unleash the Egyptian revolt that toppled Apries.
His successor **[[saite-egypt|Amasis]]** reversed course, allied with Cyrene and
**married a Cyrenaean wife, Ladice**. The lawgiver **Demonax of Mantinea** later
reorganized the citizen body into three tribes, curbing the monarchy.

## Why Cyrene mattered

- **The documentary paradigm of colonization** — the surviving Thera decree makes it
  the test case for the oath, the conscription, the oikist's authority, and the
  founder hero-cult that Graham reconstructs for the movement as a whole (see
  [[greek-colonization]]).
- **The Greek frontier in Africa** — a wealthy Hellenic foothold on the Libyan coast,
  exporting the medicinal-and-culinary plant **silphium** (its emblem on Cyrenaean
  coins), entangled in the politics of [[saite-egypt|Saite Egypt]] and then absorbed
  into the Persian and Hellenistic worlds.

## Hall (2014)

[[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]]: fourth-century Cyrene decree cites an “original” Theran colonial oath (Apollo’s command; Battos; one son per family; five-year perseverance; right of return; death/confiscation for draft refusal). Archaizing language does not prove authenticity; parallels with Herodotus’s Theran version (4.150–53) rule out pure fourth-century invention but do not prove faithful transmission from the foundation. Battos as Libyan royal title (Hdt. 4.155.2) is debated; Pindar calls the founder Aristoteles. Mid-sixth **Demonax** redistribution into three phylai (including Theraean descendants with perioikoi = indigenous hinterland) is Hall’s comparative parallel for tribal reform as territorial incorporation — analogous to [[cleisthenic-reforms-508bce|Cleisthenes]].
