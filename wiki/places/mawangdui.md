# Mawangdui

A cluster of three **early Western [[han-dynasty|Han]] tombs** at Changsha (Hunan), whose
extraordinary preservation and **buried libraries of silk manuscripts** make Mawangdui one of the
single most important archaeological finds for the study of early China. From
[[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook (2017)]], for whom Mawangdui exemplifies how
**excavated evidence** has remade the field.

> *Place-type note:* tagged `battle-site` only because the schema lacks a "tomb/necropolis"
> value; Mawangdui is a burial complex, not a battlefield.

## The Three Tombs

- **Tomb 2** — the **Lord of Dai** (chancellor of the kingdom of Changsha), d. 186 BCE; looted.
- **Tomb 1** — the **Lady of Dai** (his wife). Sealed in layers of **white clay and charcoal**
  that created an anaerobic environment, her body was preserved so completely that a **full autopsy**
  was possible: she died in her fifties of a **heart attack**, after chronic parasitic-worm
  infection and an over-rich diet — the most detailed medical record of any Han individual.
- **Tomb 3** — a younger man (a son), d. **168 BCE**; the manuscript trove.

## The Funerary Banner

Over the Lady of Dai's innermost coffin lay a **T-shaped painted silk banner**, apparently carried
in the funeral procession. Major & Cook read its subject as the **apotheosis of the Lady of Dai** —
an allegory of death and immortality in which her corporeal ***po*** soul stays with the entombed
body while her ethereal ***hun*** soul is released to roam the heavens. It is a foundational
document for Han cosmology and the **two-souls** conception of the person.

## The Silk Library

Tomb 3 yielded the **first large cache of silk manuscripts** recovered from early China — "the
first of many buried troves of texts that would increasingly shape the field of early China
studies." It included:

- early versions of the **[[zhou-dynasty|*Book of Changes*]]** and the **[[daoism|*Laozi*]]** (with
  the *De* section before the *Dao* section — the order later reversed);
- **medical manuscripts**;
- three **geographical maps**;
- a **comet/omen chart**.

These texts repeatedly **predate and diverge from** the transmitted recensions, letting scholars
see early Chinese thought before later editing — the heart of Major & Cook's
**manuscript-driven** method.

## Why This Place Mattered

Mawangdui is the type-site for the archaeological transformation of Early China Studies: a single
sealed marquisal household preserving body, banner, lacquers, and library together, it anchors
reconstruction of Western Han **elite material culture, religion, medicine, and cosmology**.
Compare the slightly earlier silk-and-bamboo finds at [[anyang|Anyang]] (oracle bones) and the
Warring States manuscript tombs (Zeng Hou Yi, Baoshan) discussed under the
[[warring-states-period-475-bce|Warring States]].
