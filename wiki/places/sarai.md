# Sarai
## Description
**Sarai** (specifically Old Sarai, *Sarai-Batu*) was the capital city of the Golden Horde, founded in the 1240s CE by Batu Khan. Located on the Akhtuba River, a distributary of the lower Volga River in southeastern European Russia, Sarai grew from a nomadic encampment into one of the largest and most prosperous cities of the medieval world, serving as the administrative, political, and financial center of Mongol rule in eastern Europe.

## Historical Significance
Following the Mongol conquest of Russia and Europe (1237–1242 CE), Batu Khan established his headquarters in the lower Volga steppe. Old Sarai served as the capital where the Horde's administrative department for Russian affairs, headed by the *daruga*, was located.

For over two centuries, Sarai held supreme authority over the Russian principalities:
* **The Investiture of Princes:** Every Russian grand prince was required to travel to Sarai to pay obeisance to the khan and receive the *iarlyk* (the patent of investiture) confirming their office.
* **Tribute Collection:** Sarai was the destination for the vast tribute collected from the Russian lands, initially by Mongol agents (*baskaks*) and later by the Rurikid princes themselves (such as Ivan I Kalita).
* **Diplomatic Center:** Sarai hosted embassies from the Byzantine Empire, the Papacy, Genoa, and Venice. It was a highly cosmopolitan city featuring separate quarters for Muslims, Christians, Jews, and pagans. In 1261 CE, an Orthodox diocese was established in Sarai to serve the Russian population living there.

**Detailed from Cambridge History of Russia Vol. I (Perrie ed. 2006, Martin ch. ll. 3709–4698):** Russian artisans conscripted "to participate in the construction of Sarai, the capital city of the Golden Horde built by Khan Baty on a tributary of the lower Volga River. They also contributed to the construction of New Sarai... Russian craftsmen were relocated to Sarai also to manufacture goods" (ll. 3783). Goods/tribute conducted down Volga to Sarai; Russian merchants appeared there with furs + imports (ll. 3854–3859). Princes travelled for patents/homage (e.g., Aleksandr, Dmitrii, Ivan Daniilovich); Mangu Temir issued privileges to Church (ll. 3929–3975, 4132). Bishopric of Sarai (1261) arranged by Metropolitan Kirill; metropolitans dealt with khans (Mangu Temir, Janibek) on privileges (ll. 4132–4134). In crisis, princes sent messengers/gifts to Sarai khans (Tokhtamysh, Edigei context); renegade Tatars fled to Muscovite service (ll. 4420–4455). See [[golden-horde]], [[moscow]], [[novgorod]], [[rus-principalities]].

The city's wealth was based on its control of the trans-Eurasian trade routes (the Northern Silk Road). However, Sarai was severely damaged during the internal civil wars of the Horde in the late fourteenth century, sacked by Tamerlane in 1395 CE, and finally destroyed around 1502 CE by the Crimean Tartars, marking the end of the Golden Horde.
