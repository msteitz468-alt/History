# Kashgar

## Geography

A fertile oasis watered by the Kashgar River and local springs at the **far western end of
the Taklamakan Desert**, where the northern and southern Tarim Basin routes reconverged
after more than 2,000 km from [[dunhuang|Dunhuang]]. In
[[benjamin-empires-ancient-eurasia-2018|Benjamin's]] account Kashgar was the practical
**western terminus of Chinese diplomatic and commercial travel**: here (or just to the
west) Chinese envoys concluded their outward journey and handed goods to
[[kushan-empire|Kushan]], [[sogdians|Sogdian]], or South Asian middlemen.

## Why It Mattered

West of Kashgar the routes fanned out around the formidable **Pamir Knot** (the meeting of
the Tien Shan, Karakorum, Kunlun, Hindu Kush, and Pamirs):
- **west** via the Pamirs to [[bactria|Bactra]] and on to [[merv|Merv]] and the
  Mediterranean;
- **northwest** via Ferghana and Samarkand;
- **south** via the Karakorum route down the Indus to the northwest Indian ports
  ([[barygaza|Barygaza]], Barbaricum) for the [[indian-ocean-trade|maritime]] leg.

The *Han Shu* preserves the envoy Tuzhin's harrowing first-person account of the "**Hanging
Pass**" west of Kashgar — a 40-cm-wide path over an unfathomable precipice where falling
pack animals "are shattered in pieces." Numismatist Joe Cribb (1984) showed **direct Kushan
political and economic control of Kashgar and Khotan** at points in the first century CE.

## Significance over Time

Kashgar's importance is structural: it sits at the single point where the desert routes
rejoin and the mountain passes begin, making it an unavoidable transshipment and customs
hub of the [[silk-roads|Silk Roads]] across both the First and Second eras.
