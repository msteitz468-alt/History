# Zurich Polytechnic

**Eidgenössische Polytechnische Schule (later ETH), Zurich.** Albert Einstein’s undergraduate institution 1896–1900 (section for specialized teachers in mathematics and physics). Less prestigious than the University of Zurich; granted diplomas but not doctorates until 1911. Key site of Einstein’s formal training, bohemian friendships, and relationship with Mileva Mari.

## Description

Solid reputation in engineering and science; new physics building with precision labs funded by Werner von Siemens (Einstein family competitor). Einstein enrolled October 1896 on full entrance exam after Aarau prep (passed math/science easily; weaker in languages). Lived on 100-franc monthly stipend from relatives; saved 20 francs/month toward Swiss citizenship.

## Einstein’s Experience

Primary physics professor Heinrich Weber: initial mutual admiration (“Weber lectured on heat with great mastery”); later antagonism over outdated curriculum (no Maxwell) and Einstein’s informal address (“Herr Weber” not “Herr Professor”). Weber: “You’re a very clever boy, Einstein... But you have one great fault: you’ll never let yourself be told anything.”

Jean Pernet (lab): lowest grade (1); official reprimand for lack of diligence; lab explosion (1899) injured Einstein’s hand and ended violin playing temporarily. Hermann Minkowski (math): “lazy dog” label for skipping advanced classes.

Self-study of contemporary masters (Kirchhoff, Helmholtz, Hertz, Boltzmann, Föppl, Poincaré) with friends. Marcel Grossmann: math-note savior; pipe-smoking philosophical discussions at Café Metropole; later secured patent-office job and GR math help. Gustav Maier (ethical culture) and Friedrich Adler (Social Democrats) reinforced political ideals.

Graduated July 1900 with 4.9 average (4th of 5); Mari lowest at 4.0 and did not graduate initially. Thesis on heat conduction (Weber specialty) received low marks (Einstein: “no interest to me”).

## Significance

Despite (or because of) frustrations with rote exams and outdated lectures, the Polytechnic years forged Einstein’s independence, visual intuition, and key relationships. “The hitch... was that one had to cram all this stuff... This coercion had such a deterring effect that, after I had passed the final examination, I found the consideration of any scientific problems distasteful to me for an entire year.” (Quickly cured.)

**Grounding (Isaacson range 1)**: Detailed accounts of professors, Grossmann notes, self-study, exam averages, “I played hooky a lot,” sailing, music soirees, political mentors, and the contrast between formal requirements and personal passion.
