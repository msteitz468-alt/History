# Chaco Canyon

## Description
Chaco Canyon, part of the San Juan Basin in northwestern New Mexico, was the center of the [[ancestral-pueblo|Ancestral Pueblo]] world from about AD 900 to 1150 (or a little later). (Older literature’s “Anasazi” label is mapped here to Ancestral Pueblo—Navajo-derived “enemy ancestors,” rejected by modern Pueblo descendants.) In 850 Chaco was much like everywhere else... Summers were short and hot, winters long and bitterly cold, edible plants and animals were few... In contrast to the even harsher environment of the San Juan basin... however, Chaco was something of an oasis. Chacoans constructed gridded gardens, canal systems, dams, and exploited a small natural lake.

In 1050 there was a “big bang” at Chaco, which became distinguished by its “great houses,” the largest of which is Pueblo Bonito, a four- or five-story structure built of sandstone masonry and holding about 650 rooms... Most of the rooms were not residential... large cooking pits in the plazas... Trash mounds near the great houses were themselves monumental constructions... formal paths (or roads) between the mounds and great houses... Great kivas, shrines, observation and signaling posts, and ramps were integrated into the landscape.

The population of the entire canyon was modest, in the neighborhood of about three thousand people... Indications of status, prestige, and wealth at Chaco are notable. Macaws, parrots and copper bells from Mexico, shells from the Pacific coast, and turquoise... stand out... Chacoans clearly knew about Mesoamerica... but these few indications of contact show the relatively minor degree of Mesoamerican influence.

Two elaborate burials in the earlier stages of Pueblo Bonito... accompanied by thousands of beads and pendants and pieces of turquoise... Residents of great houses were better nourished... The large amounts of turquoise (56,000 pieces)... were ritual items...

Pottery was imported... The connections of Chaco to its region are described as the “Chaco system” or the “Chaco phenomenon.” A system of roads connected an area of “outliers”... of about 100,000 km²... These “roads,” often only short segments... were up to 10 m wide... over-engineered... The Chaco outliers consisted of about 200 (or more) sites...

Most Chaco experts have been impressed with the arguments... that Chaco was a ritually based pilgrimage center... Robert Drennan coined the term “rituality”... By emphasizing ceremonialism and rituals at Chaco, however, I do not imply that production of food and the elaborate canals and field systems... were unimportant... There would have been no Chaco rituality without the elaborate agricultural systems...

**Chaco rituality** was short-lived. ...after 1100... half-century-long drought from 1135–80, after which the canyon was nearly abandoned. ...it was not simply the social and economic network that shattered, but also the core beliefs that made the phenomenon possible.

Lekson (1999) has argued persuasively that the site of Aztec to the north became the new center... Chaco beliefs... led to the rise of Paquime... the memory of a “White House”... refers to a past world in which katsinas taught dances and ceremonies, but which was ultimately violent and unsuccessful... its lessons became incorporated in Puebloan histories.

## Cordell & Smith (CHNPA I.1 ch. 4)

*part_of:* [[ancestral-pueblo]]; *part_of:* [[southwest-agricultural-development]]. Best-documented Anasazi regional system began in the **tenth century** in Chaco Canyon (San Juan Basin, NM); initially localized along <30 linear km of Chaco Wash, eventually ~**53,000 km²** of the basin and adjacent uplands. Famous planned “towns” include Pueblo Bonito, Chetro Ketl, Pueblo del Arroyo, Pueblo Alto.

**Pueblo Bonito / town architecture:** Pueblo Bonito is the largest Chacoan town — >600 rooms (fig. captions: >300 ground-floor rooms, 30 kivas, 2 Great Kivas, up to five stories, estimate >650 rooms); massive tapering walls of core-and-veneer ashlar sandstone masonry; formal multistory D/E/F/L plans around plazas; rooms larger and higher-ceilinged than other Anasazi sites; ~1 small kiva per 30 rooms; tower kivas; **Great Kivas** >20 m diameter with standardized features (antechambers, recessed stairs, raised firebox, paired vaults, high bench, wall niches — Chetro Ketl sealed niches held beads/pendants). Great Kiva roofs used massive timber investment (one roof >350 timbers) from Ponderosa pine forests many tens of km away. Towns yielded exotic “luxury” goods: cylindrical vessels, incense burners, copper tinkler bells, *Strombus* and *Murex* shell trumpets, painted wood, macaw skeletons, shell/wood/basketry inlaid with selenite, mica, turquoise.

**Town–village and outlier pattern:** system combined canyon/basin small unplanned villages (~16 rooms average) contemporary with towns; by ~**1150** the town–village pattern repeated in ~**150 Chacoan Outlier** communities over ≥**150,000 km²**; many outliers linked to canyon by ancient roads radiating like wheel spokes (to 9 m wide near canyon, 2–3 m distant), hard to explain without wheeled vehicles/pack animals.

**Road function debate (hedged):** older view — roads facilitated transport of surplus food/building material to towns for storage against rainfall shortfalls; challenged because eleventh–twelfth-century basin rainfall was relatively predictable, system scale exceeds central-storage expectations, and parallel double/quadruple roadways over >30 km exceed foot-traffic needs — recent discussions emphasize **symbolic/ritual** road functions. Agreement that organization of the system is **not closely mirrored** in modern Pueblo Indian villages, whose residents undoubtedly include descendants of the Chacoan Anasazi. *contributed_to:* [[north-american-states-debate]].

**Failure and aftermath:** Chacoan system failed and San Juan Basin largely abandoned by Pueblo peoples long before Europeans; after 1150 no new Chacoan buildings in the canyon; remaining occupants interacted with northern San Juan (Mesa Verde–Cortez–Yellow Jacket) Anasazi who built large thirteenth-century pueblos without Chaco-level integrated planning; check dams, terraces, reservoirs for farming; later Chaco pottery/architecture shifted toward northern styles; outliers Salmon Ruin and Aztec show Chaco then Mesa Verde-style building episodes. Eventually, by 1300, even the northern Anasazi area was abandoned by farming peoples moving south to Upper Chama and Rio Grande Valleys, Central Mountains of Arizona, Zuni Mountains, and Hopi Mesas.

Cordell & Smith: no one maintains state-level organizations occurred in the Southwest, or that hierarchically organized regional systems were at all stable — Chaco as impressive but unstable regional system, not a state. ## Additional Evidence (Snow et al. 2020)

Chaco Canyon is located in the San Juan Basin of northwestern New Mexico and is now protected as Chaco Culture National Historical Park and designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The canyon contains 11 huge stone great houses, some four stories high and containing hundreds of rooms, plus smaller stone buildings, mounds, great kivas, roads, rock-cut stairways, and water-control and garden features forming a dense, symbolically ordered landscape.

The stone available in the canyon splits easily into tabular slabs ideal for multistory masonry; masonry technique evolved from rough stone and thick mortar to finely dressed stones with minimal mortar over the period of construction. Pueblo Bonito, the largest D-shaped great house, and outlying great houses (Wijiji, Tsin Kletzin, Kin Kletso, Chetro Ketl) were rapidly completed by 1100 CE. The canyon held at least 70 outlier communities by 1115 CE at 32–40 km intervals — one hard day's journey on foot.

Residues in cylindrical vessels contain cacao, linking Chaco to the Mesoamerican chocolate ritual. Copper bells, macaws, and conch shell trumpets from Mexico have also been found. Six-toed individuals buried at Pueblo Bonito and represented in petroglyphs may have held special ritual status.
