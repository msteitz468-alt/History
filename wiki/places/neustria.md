# Neustria

**The "New West Lands" of [[francia|Francia]]** — the northwestern Frankish heartland between the
Loire and the Scheldt, centred on the Seine and on **Paris**, and the richest core of Merovingian
royal power.

## Geography and character

Neustria was "the region richest in fiscal land, in Roman cities (such as Paris, Tours, and Rouen) and
in productive population," with the Merovingian capital at Soissons and, increasingly under
[[dagobert-i|Dagobert I]], at Paris. Its wealth rested on the **royal fisc** of the Paris basin and on
the surviving Roman urban network.

## History of control

Neustria was the seat of the [[merovingian-franks|Merovingian]] kings and, in the seventh century, of
their most powerful **mayors of the palace** — Erchinoald (641–658), whose lands centred on the lower
Seine; Ebroin, the formidable mayor assassinated in 680; and Waratto and his son-in-law Bercharius.
Geary argues that the real story of late-seventh-century Neustria-Burgundy "was not really Burgundian
autonomy from Neustrian hegemony, but the individual power of Neustrian and Burgundian magnates" whose
private interests overrode regional loyalty, ecclesiastical lords turning their territories into
independent lordships.

Neustrian resistance to the Austrasians ended at [[battle-of-tertry-687|Tertry (687)]], where
[[pippinids|Pippin II]] defeated Bercharius. Pippin then absorbed the Neustrian mayoral network — his
son Drogo married Bercharius's widow Anstrudis — and bound the Neustrian church to his family through
control of monasteries such as St-Wandrille and Jumièges. Even so, the royal court at Compiègne could
still rule against the Pippinids, as when Drogo lost a property case before King Childebert III in 697.

## Why it mattered

As the fiscal and demographic heart of Francia, Neustria was the prize over which Merovingian and then
Pippinid power was decided; control of its cities, fisc, and monasteries was the index of mastery over
the whole kingdom.
