# Syracuse (Archaic)

**Syracuse** was the greatest of the western Greek cities — the **Corinthian colony**
that grew into the dominant power of [[magna-graecia|Sicily]], its expansion the
best-documented in the Greek west. [[boardman-cah-v3p3-1982|CAH Vol. III, Part 3]]
(Ch. 38, A. J. Graham) treats its Archaic phase.

## Foundation and the Gamoroi

Founded **733 BCE** by **Archias of Corinth** — a noble of the ruling **Bacchiad**
house — on the island of Ortygia, Syracuse was established by **force**: Thucydides
says Archias "first drove the Sicels from the island." The colonists won the adjacent
coastal plain to the west and south (the inland Sicel sites of Pantalica and Finocchito
end at about the foundation date). The rich plain became the property of the **first
settlers and their descendants, who lived in the city** — the ruling aristocracy
tellingly called the **Gamoroi** ("land-sharers"), a landowning oligarchy — while later
and lesser people lived in the poorer hill country. The Sicels of the territory were
reduced to **serfs (the Cyllyrii)** or tribute-paying dependants.

## The Syracusan "empire"

Syracuse extended its grip by planting **subordinate colonies** up the Anapo valley:
**Acrae (663)** and **Casmenae (643)** — high, strong, militarily-sited foundations
(Casmenae with an early orthogonal plan and a great deposit of weapons in its temple
precinct), named no oikist and "completely dependent on Syracuse," set to watch the
subject Sicel population. Its third colony, **Camarina (598)** on the south coast, was
a proper *polis* with two oikists but politically dependent — and it **revolted** c. 550
with Sicel allies, only to be crushed and its people expelled (later resettled, ceded to
Hippocrates of Gela by 500). Through colonization and subjugation Syracuse built a
territory Dunbabin reckoned at some **4,000 km²** — the base from which the fifth-century
[[deinomenid|Deinomenid tyrants]] would make it a great power.

## Why Syracuse mattered

- The **hegemon of Greek Sicily** — model of the aggressive, Dorian, native-subjugating
  pattern of [[greek-colonization|colonization]] (contrast the peaceful Chalcidian
  cities), and of the agrarian oligarchy (the Gamoroi).
- The seed of the great Classical Syracuse — the city that would defeat Carthage at
  Himera (480) and Athens in the Sicilian Expedition (413).
