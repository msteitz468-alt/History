## Geographic Description

**Etowah** is a major Mississippian mound center in north / northwestern Georgia on the Etowah River. In Bruce D. Smith’s CHNPA I.1 ch. 5 treatment it appears primarily as a **material-culture exemplar** of elite ritual costume and long-distance copper working, not as a full polity case study (unlike [[powers-phase]], [[cahokia]]/[[american-bottom]], or [[spiro]]).

## Site and Political Context

Secondary surveys describe six platform mounds on roughly 21 ha and a succession of cycling chiefdoms from ~A.D. 1000 to ~1550 — consistent with Smith’s general model of Mississippian chiefdom cycling (rapid or multi-century rise/decline; mound-center relocation within valleys). Etowah belongs to the southern Appalachian / Piedmont Mississippian world, in the projected Iroquoian-language zone of Smith’s linguistic mosaic, though local ethnic labels at the mound center remain archaeologically complex.

## Prestige Goods and SECC Link

Smith illustrates **large copper plates** recovered from Etowah depicting elite individuals wearing the costume of a **peregrine falcon** (Fig. 5.6) — products of copper exchanged from the upper Great Lakes and southern Appalachian regions and crafted into ritual garments and objects for notables. Related elite copper repertoire across Mississippian centers includes falcon headplates, cutout human/feline heads, feathers, maces, arrows, earspools, and hair ornaments. These objects sit at the core of the [[southeastern-ceremonial-complex]] prestige-goods package.

Secondary literature also emphasizes carved marble statues and embossed copper artifacts from Etowah as among the most spectacular Mississippian art finds.

## Historiography

Smith’s chapter uses Etowah for copper-plate iconography and Phillips & Brown’s shell-engraving comparative frame; fuller developmental histories of Etowah chiefdom cycling live in regional monographs outside ch. 5. Classification as complex [[chiefdom]] within [[mississippian-culture]] follows Smith’s midrange framing; state-level claims are not asserted for Etowah here (see [[north-american-states-debate]] for the general Mississippian debate, focused on Cahokia).
