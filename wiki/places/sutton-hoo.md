# Sutton Hoo

**Sutton Hoo** is the site of a major early seventh-century Anglo-Saxon cemetery in Suffolk, England, famous for its ship burials and exceptional gold and garnet metalwork that exemplify elite material culture in the early Anglo-Saxon kingdoms.

## Site and Significance

The place is best known from the 1939 excavation of Mound 1, a large ship burial containing a rich assemblage of weapons, regalia, and feasting equipment. The cemetery as a whole spans the sixth–seventh centuries and reflects the emergence of powerful East Anglian kingship.

## Evidence from Bogucki & Crabtree 2004 (range_08 caption)

The encyclopedia illustrates a key artifact with this caption:

> "OPPOSITE PAGE, TOP LEFT: Sutton Hoo Anglo-Saxon clasp from Sutton Hoo, England: cloisonné garnet, millefiori, and blue glass inlay in gold with filigree, early seventh century A.D." (range_08:173–176)

This directly dates the high-status metalwork tradition to the early seventh century A.D. and situates Sutton Hoo within the broader corpus of Migration Period / Early Medieval jewelry and regalia discussed in the volume (Visigothic tremissis, Ostrogothic fibulae, Pictish silver, Hunterston brooch).
