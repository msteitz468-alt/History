# Mitla

## Overview

**Mitla** (Nahuatl *Mictlan*-derived place name in Spanish sources; Zapotec settlement
in the eastern [[oaxaca-valley|Valley of Oaxaca]]) was the **religious capital** of the
Late Postclassic [[zapotec|Zapotec]] — counterpart to **Zaachila** as political capital
in the western valley. Marcus and Flannery (CHNPA II.1 Ch. 8) present it as the seat of
the *uija-tào* high priest and the architectural culmination of the two-room temple
tradition after the decline of [[monte-alban|Monte Albán]].

## From secondary center to religious capital

After Monte Albán’s gradual urban breakdown (A.D. 600–900), former secondary centers —
including Mitla, Zaachila, Cuilapan, Macuilxóchitl, Teotitlán, Yegüih, and others —
became regional head towns with palaces and temples lasting to Spanish contact. Nearby
**Guilá Naquitz** cave (Archaic) links the Mitla area to the deepest Oaxaca sequence, but
Mitla’s urban fame is Postclassic.

## Hall of the Columns and the high priest

While Zaachila was considered the political capital, the religious capital was Mitla. At
Mitla, the ***uija-tào*** or high priest of the Zapotec (described by the Spaniards as
“**like our pope**”) lived in the **Hall of the Columns** — an evolution of the
standardized two-room temple: a colonnaded public hall linked by private corridor to the
priest’s residence with patio and celebrated **stone-mosaic** rooms. Nearby fortified
hills (also Yagul, Corral de Piedra) served as wartime refuge in the balkanized Late
Postclassic landscape of small hostile polities.

## Why it mattered

Mitla illustrates Postclassic Zapotec **dual structure** after the fall of the single
Monte Albán primate center: political power at Zaachila, religious authority at Mitla —
continuity of state-religion architecture (two-room temple → Hall of the Columns) without
Classic-scale urban monopoly. Its stone mosaics became emblematic of Oaxaca for later
observers and tourism, but their historical significance is as elite/priestly space in a
balkanized high-culture landscape that also produced genealogical registers and faced
[[aztec-empire|Aztec]] and Spanish expansion.
