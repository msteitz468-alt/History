# Khao Sam Kaeo

**Khao Sam Kaeo** is a Preclassic port and craft complex on the **South China Sea shore of the Siamo-Malay peninsula** (modern Chumphon Province, Thailand), about **5 km inland**, covering roughly **35 hectares** on four hilltops and three valleys along the **Tha Tapao River**. It is one of the most important excavated windows onto late first-millennium BCE interaction among local Seasian communities, South Asia, and East Asia—before Funan’s Protoclassic florescence and long before [[srivijaya|Srivijaya]]. Primary synthesis: [[miksic-goh-ancient-southeast-asia-2017|Miksic & Goh 2017]] Ch. 3–4.

## Geographic Description

Valley 1 may have served as a monsoon-shelter mooring. A system of **17 ramparts** (up to ~**4.2 m** high) has been mapped: few complete enclosures; some water-retaining (pebbles/layered fills), others for social/occupational demarcation or defense. Terraces, postholes, and *pisé* floors structure occupation. The site sits on the eastern end of trans-peninsular routes that linked the Gulf of Thailand / South China Sea to the Bay of Bengal via the Kra isthmus corridor—paired in scholarly discussion with west-coast sites such as Phu Khao Thong.

## Occupation, Craft, and Demarcation

Occupation layers under and over ramparts date mainly to the **early fourth–second centuries BCE**. Iron-smithing debris is dated **390/310–200 BCE**. Villagers reported three [[dongson-culture|Dongson]] drums on Hill 1. Excavators hypothesize that **Hills 1–2** were allocated to local residents and **Hills 3–4** to foreigners from South, Southeast, and East Asia.

Craft at the feet of Hills 1–2: hard-stone and glass carving; glass possibly linked to northeast India; some Indian craftsmen hypothesized as supervisors; Fengtian (Taiwan) **nephrite**; Hills 1–2 also yield Sa Huynh–Kalanay-style shell-incised ceramics known in southern Vietnam and the Philippines.

Hills 3–4: stone working including *lingling-o*; iron working of the fourth–second centuries BCE judged “rudimentary”; looters reported high-tin bronze bowls like Ban Don Ta Phet; Indian rouletted and knobbed pottery, Northern Black Polished Ware (fourth–second centuries BCE), and **84** Western Han-style pottery pieces. Khao Sam Kaeo is the **first site in Seasia outside Vietnam** to yield Han Dynasty pottery in archaeological context. Seals in Prakrit/Brāhmī; Chinese mirror fragments, arrowhead, axe, and seals including the “Seal of Lü Yougong.”

The site was most active in the late centuries BCE but continued into the Protoclassic: two or three Brāhmī stones ~**100 BCE–100 CE** (owners’ names); a gold seal in Sanskrit palaeographically dated to the **fifth or sixth century**.

## Foreign Enclaves: A High Bar

Miksic and Goh set a **high bar** for proving foreign enclaves: foreign artifacts alone are insufficient. There are no archaeological or historical foreign enclaves in Seasia for the next ~1,000 years until the Middle Classic. Equally plausible is that Seasians trained in India made Indian-style goods. Later “South China Sea indianised” ornament repertoires **differ** from contemporary South Asian sites and “would have been produced mainly for Seasian populations.” Khao Sam Kaeo thus enters debates over [[indianization-of-southeast-asia|Indianization]] as a craft-and-exchange centre, not a colonial factory town.

## Possible Identification with Shenli

One interpretive proposal identifies Khao Sam Kaeo with **Shenli** of the *Qian Han Shu* route for the Han Wu mission: barbarian ships to the peninsula, overland to Fugandulu, then ship across the Bay of Bengal to Huangzhi (India). The identification remains a hypothesis within the [[indian-ocean-trade|Indian Ocean]] / Han diplomatic maritime itinerary.

## Why It Mattered

- Early evidence of **planned multi-community** occupation on the isthmian corridor.
- Node linking **Dongson drums**, **Sa Huynh–Kalanay** ceramics, Taiwan nephrite, Indian fine wares, and Han pottery—overlapping Preclassic prestige spheres.
- Baseline for later port hierarchies of the Protoclassic and Classic (Ko Kho Khao, Phu Khao Thong, Kedah, Straits ports).
- Cautionary case against equating Indian-style goods with Indian political colonies.

## Control History

No named indigenous kingdom is securely attached to the site in contemporary texts. Control was likely local elite management of craft zones and ramparted spaces within emerging [[southeast-asian-port-polity|port-polity]] patterns. Later Classic powers (Funan’s claimed peninsula sway in Chinese eyes, then Srivijaya) operated in the same geographic corridor centuries afterward without continuous institutional succession from Khao Sam Kaeo.

## Historiography and Source Notes

Interpretation depends on French and Thai excavation programmes of the early twenty-first century as synthesized by Miksic and Goh. Contested points: foreign residential enclaves vs local appropriation; absolute dating of iron and ramparts; Shenli identification; relationship to Ban Don Ta Phet’s bead wealth and high-tin bronze bowls. Looting has damaged hilltop contexts. The site is central to Preclassic chapters of Seasian archaeology and to critiques of colonial-enclave models (e.g. older readings of Khuan Luk Pat).
