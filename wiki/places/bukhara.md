# Bukhara

A major Silk Road city in Transoxiana (modern Uzbekistan) that reached its greatest cultural prominence as the capital of the [[samanid-dynasty|Samanid dynasty]] (9th–10th century CE), which made it one of the leading centers of Persianate Islamic scholarship, literature, and science in the medieval world. Figures associated with or educated in Bukhara's intellectual milieu include the philosopher-physician Ibn Sina (Avicenna), who as a young man had access to the Samanid royal library there, and the hadith scholar Muhammad al-Bukhari. After the Samanid collapse the city passed through successive Qarakhanid, Khwarazmian, and Mongol control (suffering severe destruction under Genghis Khan in 1220), later reviving as the seat of the Khanate and then Emirate of Bukhara, a major Central Asian Islamic polity that persisted (as a Russian protectorate from the 1868 conquest) until its abolition by the Bolsheviks in 1920.
