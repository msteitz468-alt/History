# Kingdom of Burgundy (Arelat / "Kingdom of Arles")

The Kingdom of Burgundy, also called the Kingdom of Arles (Arelat), was formed in 933 by the union of the earlier kingdoms of Upper Burgundy (around Lake Geneva and the Jura) and Lower Burgundy/Provence under the Rudolphine dynasty. It stretched along the Rhône and Saône valleys from the Jura to the Mediterranean, encompassing Provence, the Franche-Comté, and parts of western Switzerland — distinct from, though geographically adjacent to, the Duchy of Burgundy that later emerged further west within the French kingdom.

The last Rudolphine king, Rudolf III, bequeathed the kingdom to the Salian Holy Roman Emperor Conrad II, who formally acquired it in 1032/34 after suppressing rival claims, incorporating it as one of the three constituent kingdoms of the Holy Roman Empire (alongside Germany and Italy). Imperial control over Burgundy remained largely nominal and decentralized in practice, with real power devolving to regional counts, the Church, and emerging urban communes; the kingdom gradually fragmented into semi-autonomous principalities (Savoy, Provence, the Free County of Burgundy) over the following centuries, though the title "King of Burgundy/Arles" persisted among the Holy Roman Emperors into the late medieval period.

## Significance

The 1032/34 acquisition of Burgundy substantially expanded Salian imperial territory and prestige, though the kingdom's persistent institutional weakness illustrated the practical limits of imperial authority over its outlying, geographically fragmented constituent kingdoms.
