# Zundert

Small market town in North Brabant, southern Netherlands, birthplace of Vincent van Gogh (30 March 1853) and his brother Theo.

## Description and Historical Role

In the mid-19th century Zundert was a quiet rural community of farmers, weavers, and small tradespeople on the Belgian border. The parsonage of the Dutch Reformed Church where the Van Gogh family lived stood at the edge of fields and woods. The landscape of sandy paths, ditches, heaths, and pinewoods shaped Vincent's early visual memory and lifelong attachment to peasant life and nature.

## Significance

Vincent's childhood in Zundert provided the "dams and dikes," fields, and village characters that later reappeared transformed in his Dutch-period drawings and paintings. The strict Protestant parsonage culture, his father's vocation, and early family walks with Theo established patterns of intense observation, religious feeling, and fraternal devotion that defined his life and art. The town remained a touchstone; Vincent returned there in memory and occasional visits even after leaving for good.
