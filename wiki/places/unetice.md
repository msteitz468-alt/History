# Únětice (Unetice)

**Únětice (Unetice)** refers to the key Early Bronze Age culture-area and cemetery in Bohemia (Czech Republic), with important parallels and rich burials in the Elbe-Saale region of Germany. It is the classic locus for the emergence of marked social hierarchies and rich grave goods in central Europe during the early second millennium BCE, linked to proximity to metal sources.

## Key Evidence

"Around 2100-1800 BC burials with strikingly rich grave goods, including sophisticated craft items and goods obtained by long-distance exchange, appear in central Europe, particularly in the Elbe-Saale area of Germany and in Bohemia; examples include the burials of Leubingen and Helmsdorf and the cemetery at Unttice (Coles and Harding, 1979, pp. 38-42). In the former area these stand out in a particularly marked fashion because most of the other burials of the period contain very little. In Bohemia and the other regions the richest burials are not so strikingly pre-eminent and the distribution of grave wealth is a much more continuous one."

In south-west Slovakia (closely related area with early differentiation): cemeteries show internal differentiation (especially female burials with elaborate costumes), leading families, probable male-line status inheritance. Later phase (c. 2000–1800 BC): fortified sites (Vesele, Male Kosihy, Nitriansky Hradok) with advanced metalwork, moulds, craft production (bone), and new exotica (amber).

## Role in Hierarchy Development

The region demonstrates the link between metal resources and social differentiation: "if one examines the spatial distribution of those parts of central Europe which have attracted attention in terms of their evidence for social differentiation, one is inevitably drawn towards those lowland areas of good agricultural soils adjacent to local metal sources." North Bohemia/Elbe-Saale tin sources became important with true tin-bronze; earlier differentiation nearer Slovakian and Alpine ores.

"hierarchies had now arisen in central Europe which resembled their contemporaries in such regions as Wessex and Brittany in that rituals involving the possession and consumption of prestige goods were very much part of their operation."
