# Corinth

The great commercial city commanding the **Isthmus** between mainland Greece and
the Peloponnese — destroyed by Rome in 146 BCE. Treated in [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH
VIII]] Chs 8–9 (Errington, Derow).

## Geography and significance

Corinth's position astride the Isthmus, with harbours on both the Corinthian and
Saronic gulfs (and the *diolkos* portage), made it one of the richest trading cities
of the Greek world and a strategic key — its citadel, the **Acrocorinth**, was one
of Philip V's "**Fetters of Greece**." It hosted the **Isthmian Games**, a
panhellenic festival, where [[flamininus|Flamininus]] proclaimed the
[[freedom-of-the-greeks|"freedom of the Greeks"]] in 196.

## History of control

- A leading independent polis from the Archaic period; later a Macedonian strongpoint
  (the Acrocorinth garrison).
- Restored to the **[[achaean-league|Achaean League]]** in 196/194 after Flamininus'
  settlement.
- **Destroyed (146).** As the seat of the Achaean assembly that declared the
  [[achaean-war-146bce|Achaean War]], Corinth bore the brunt of Roman vengeance: after
  L. Mummius' victory the city was **sacked, its art carried to Rome, its people sold,
  and the site burned** on the Senate's decree — the same year as the destruction of
  [[third-punic-war-149-146bce|Carthage]]. It lay desolate for a century until
  refounded as a Roman colony by Caesar (44 BCE).

## Why it mattered

Corinth's fate, paired with Carthage's, marked 146 as the year Rome demonstrated its
willingness to **annihilate** great commercial cities that resisted — the symbolic
end of Greek freedom.
