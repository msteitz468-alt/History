# Corinth

The great commercial city commanding the **Isthmus** between mainland Greece and
the Peloponnese — destroyed by Rome in 146 BCE. Treated in [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH
VIII]] Chs 8–9 (Errington, Derow).

## Geography and significance

Corinth's position astride the Isthmus, with harbours on both the Corinthian and
Saronic gulfs (and the *diolkos* portage), made it one of the richest trading cities
of the Greek world and a strategic key — its citadel, the **Acrocorinth**, was one
of Philip V's "**Fetters of Greece**." It hosted the **Isthmian Games**, a
panhellenic festival, where [[flamininus|Flamininus]] proclaimed the
[[freedom-of-the-greeks|"freedom of the Greeks"]] in 196.

## History of control

- A leading independent polis from the Archaic period; later a Macedonian strongpoint
  (the Acrocorinth garrison).
- Restored to the **[[achaean-league|Achaean League]]** in 196/194 after Flamininus'
  settlement.
- **Destroyed (146).** As the seat of the Achaean assembly that declared the
  [[achaean-war-146bce|Achaean War]], Corinth bore the brunt of Roman vengeance: after
  L. Mummius' victory the city was **sacked, its art carried to Rome, its people sold,
  and the site burned** on the Senate's decree — the same year as the destruction of
  [[third-punic-war-149-146bce|Carthage]]. It lay desolate for a century until
  refounded as a Roman colony by Caesar (44 BCE).

## Why it mattered

Corinth's fate, paired with Carthage's, marked 146 as the year Rome demonstrated its
willingness to **annihilate** great commercial cities that resisted — the symbolic
end of Greek freedom.

## Hall (2014) — Archaic Bacchiadae and Cypselus

[[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] restores Archaic depth largely absent from the CAH VIII-focused treatment above. Settlement around Peirene/Sacred springs and the Lechaeum road stayed dispersed into the seventh century (unlike Athens/Argos physical coalescence). **Bacchiadae**: >200 endogamous clan members governed in common ~90 years, choosing annual *prytanis* in place of *basileus* until Cypselus (Diodorus via Ephorus; Herodotus on endogamy). Hall doubts a 747 start as early for aristocracy’s rise but keeps Cypselus ~657 (grandson archon at Athens 597/6, ML 6; Diodorus 447 years from 1104 Heraclid capture). Bacchiad rotation/exclusion of non-elites is the model “gentleman’s agreement” broken by tyranny. Cypselus ~650 as earliest mainland tyrant (with Orthagoras at Sicyon, Panaitios at Leontini later in the century); Periander as shrewd arbitrator and sage as well as vicious-tradition target; diolkos as munificence/display; Delphi greets Cypselus as *basileus*. Coinage second quarter of sixth century. Eighth-century trajectory comparable to non-colonizing Argos/Athens in prosperity even while Corinth led western foundations.

## From the Oxford History of the Classical World (1986)

**Forrest (Ch.1, [[sources/boardman-oxford-history-classical-world-1986|OHCW 1986]]).** Corinth as mother-city of Corcyra and Syracuse (733) and site of Cypselus’ 657 overthrow of the Bacchiads under *dikaiosei* justice-propaganda; later a key bilateral ally in Chilon’s Spartan network and the place of the Hellenic congress of spring 480. Corinthian refusal (with Demaratus) of Cleomenes’ c.506 attack on Athens forced League congress procedure—Corinth as **restraining voice** inside Spartan hegemony.

**Hornblower / Price.** Classical suspicions of Athenian naval imperialism help ignite 431; 146 Roman sack marks the symbolic end of Greek freedom (paired with Carthage); Ptolemaic propaganda later personified “Corinth” for League-of-Corinth freedom rhetoric.
