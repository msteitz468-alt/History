# Williamsburg

Williamsburg served as the capital of the Colony of Virginia from 1699 to 1780 and was the political, social, and intellectual center of the colony during Thomas Jefferson's formative years. Jefferson attended the College of William and Mary (1760–1762), read law in George Wythe's office, was inoculated against smallpox, attended plays, observed sessions of the House of Burgesses, and absorbed the manners and political culture of the Virginia gentry.

The town featured the Capitol (where the Burgesses met), the Governor's Palace (under Fauquier during Jefferson's student days), Bruton Parish Church, and the college. It was described by contemporaries as a "school of manners and morals" and a "school of government." Jefferson later called the years there among the most important of his life for the friendships and intellectual influences (especially William Small and George Wythe) that shaped him.

After the Revolution the capital moved to Richmond (1780). The 20th-century restoration created Colonial Williamsburg, one of the major living-history sites in the United States.
