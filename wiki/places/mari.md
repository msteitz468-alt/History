# Mari (Tell Hariri)

A great city on the **middle Euphrates** (on the Syria–Mesopotamia border) that
was the westernmost outpost of Sumerian civilization and a major power in its own
right. Though far from the south-Babylonian heartland, the
[[sumerian-king-list|Sumerian King List]] records a **Dynasty of Mari** that once
held supremacy over "the land," and excavation has confirmed it as "a principal
centre of the Early Dynastic civilization." Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]] (Chs. XIII, XVI Mallowan, XVII
Drower & Bottéro).

## Early Dynastic Mari

Mari was "one of the outposts of the old Sumerian culture, although the Sumerian
language may never have been used there" — its rulers and officials bear
**Semitic** names, and its inscriptions are in the Semitic tongue. Excavation
yielded a wealth of **votive statuary** in the full Early Dynastic style —
**Lamgi-Mari** and **Iku-Shamagan** (kings), the overseer **Ebih-il**, the miller
Idi-Narum, and the singer **Ur-Nanshe** — figures that "look 'typical'
Sumerians" yet bear un-Sumerian names, proving (per Gadd) how irrelevant dress and
appearance are to ethnic origin. An Early Dynastic III palace and rich material
attest its wealth; Mari and [[ur|Ur]] were "living in amity," exchanging gifts.

## Strategic position

Mari controlled the middle Euphrates trade artery linking southern Mesopotamia to
Syria, Anatolia, and the Mediterranean — timber, metals, and stone moving down to
the resource-poor alluvium ([[trade-network-formation]]). This made it a perennial
prize: it was absorbed by [[naram-sin|Sargon of Akkad]], fell within the orbit of
the Third Dynasty of [[ur|Ur]], and later flourished as an **Amorite** kingdom
under **Zimri-Lim** — whose vast palace and ~20,000-tablet archive (the Mari
Letters) make Middle Bronze Age Mari one of the best-documented cities of the
ancient Near East — until its destruction by **Hammurabi of Babylon** (c. 1760
BCE; see [[mesopotamian-chronology]]).

## Why this place mattered

Mari demonstrates the **reach of Sumerian-Mesopotamian civilization** far up the
Euphrates and the deep entanglement of Sumerian and Semitic populations from the
earliest historical times. As the hinge between Mesopotamia and Syria it is a key
node for every imperial system that tried to control the region, and its Bronze
Age archives are among the richest documentary sources of the age.
