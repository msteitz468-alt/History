# Delos

Small island in the Cyclades, central Aegean. Mythical birthplace of Apollo and Artemis; major sanctuary and panhellenic religious center. In the 5th century BCE became the treasury and symbolic center of the Delian League (Athenian-led alliance against Persia). Later commercial hub under Hellenistic and Roman rule.

## Geographic Description

Tiny (about 3.5 km long), rocky island between Mykonos and Rheneia. Its central position in the Cyclades made it a natural meeting point for Ionian Greeks. Sacred to Apollo; the island was periodically "purified" (e.g., removal of burials by Athenians).

## History of Control and Significance

- Archaic: Ionian religious center; Leto-Apollo birth myth.
- Classical: 478/7 BCE, site of founding of Delian League after Persian Wars. Treasury moved to Athens in 454 BCE after alleged sacrilege fears. Athenians dominated the league and used Delos symbolically.
- Hellenistic: Independent or under various controls (Ptolemies, etc.); major slave market and trade entrepôt.
- Roman: Continued commercial importance; sacked in 88 BCE during Mithridatic Wars.

Why it mattered: Religious prestige + strategic location made it the natural headquarters for the anti-Persian alliance. Its later commercial role shows continuity of Aegean connectivity.
