# Caracol

## Geographic description

**Caracol** is a large Classic Maya center on the **Vaca Plateau** of Belize, in the dissected karst foothills of the Maya Mountains. Large-scale, perhaps centrally planned **terrace systems** surround the site. At peak political power (**A.D. 650**), the city covered **>177 km²** with a population of **at least 115,000** and ~**60 km** of internal causeways.

## Historical significance

### Formative roots and Late Classic power (Evans & Webster 2001)

First occupied in the Late Formative **c. 300 B.C.**; caches and epicentral building by **A.D. 1** show participation in wider lowland ritual and long-distance trade. The dynasty is traced to **A.D. 331**. Prominence falls in the Late Classic (**A.D. 550–900**). Distinctive archaeological patterns include high dental decoration rates, intense ancestor veneration, eastern buildings with burials/caches, and fingerbone caches.

### Wars with Tikal and Naranjo

Caracol texts record **conquest of [[tikal|Tikal]] in 562** and a decade of war with **Naranjo** (**626–636**; conquest of Naranjo also noted in early-seventh-century texts at both sites). In the Martin–Grube and encyclopedia synthesis, Caracol ranks among Late Classic centers (with Tikal, [[calakmul|Calakmul]], and Dos Pilas) whose paramount kings dominated larger **alliance-based polities** through warfare and military allies. Late Classic Lowlands remained balkanized: Tikal was the principal early Late Classic center, briefly replaced by Calakmul and possibly Caracol, then reasserted dominance later in the Late Classic. **[[tikal]]**’s 125-year inscriptional hiatus has been interpreted as domination by another city, **possibly Caracol**.

### Ninth century and abandonment

Further ninth-century warfare; **Caana** rebuilt in the ninth century; **abrupt abandonment** of the center just before **A.D. 900**, with limited regional occupation possibly another ~200 years. Naj Tunich cave inscriptions (**A.D. 700–770**) commemorate elite pilgrimages from regional sites including Caracol, Ixtutz, and Sacul.

## Why it mattered

Caracol supplies the epigraphic engine for sixth–seventh-century **anti-Tikal geopolitics** and demonstrates Belizean plateau urbanism at superstate scale (population and causeway figures among the highest cited for any Classic Maya city). Essential for reading the Hiatus and the multipolar Late Classic world on [[maya-civilization]] and [[maya-classic-collapse-800ce]].
