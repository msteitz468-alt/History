# Birkbeck College

Birkbeck College is a public research university located in Bloomsbury, London, England. In the 1950s, its crystallography laboratory was led by J.D. "Sage" Bernal, one of the founders of molecular biology.

## Institutional Context

Birkbeck was founded in 1823 as the London Mechanics' Institution, intended to offer evening education to craftsmen and small employers. In the post-war period, it operated in scrappy, makeshift accommodation in Bream's Buildings near Chancery Lane, before moving to a modern block on Malet Street in 1951. Classes were held at night, and a condition of admittance was that the student be engaged in full-time day employment.

Despite its cramped, "slum-like" physical conditions, Birkbeck's crystallography group under Bernal was world-renowned. It offered a highly collaborative, non-sectarian, and egalitarian working environment that contrasted sharply with the formal, divided hierarchy of King's College.

## Research under Rosalind Franklin

Rosalind Franklin moved her Turner and Newall Fellowship to Birkbeck in March 1953. Bernal welcomed her assistance to set up a new attack on virus structures. Over the next five years, Franklin established a highly productive research group here, focusing on the structures of RNA and Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV) and starting work on the polio virus:
- **Collaborations:** She partnered with Aaron Klug, Kenneth Holmes, and John Finch, recruiting them to her virus research project.
- **Funding struggles:** The group struggled under Sir William Slater's Agricultural Research Council (ARC) funding restrictions. Slater repeatedly refused to raise Franklin's salary or grant her permanent status, telling her she ought to work under the guidance of a biochemist. This prompted Franklin to secure a large grant from the US National Institutes of Health.
- **Relocation:** In 1958, Max Perutz visited Birkbeck and invited Franklin and Klug to relocate their virus group to the new molecular biology unit being built in Cambridge, securing the long-term future of the group.
