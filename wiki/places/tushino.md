# Tushino

## Description
**Tushino** was a large village located approximately twelve miles northwest of Moscow (near the confluence of the Moscow and Skhodnya rivers) which served as the headquarters and rival capital of the Second False Dmitry (the "Felon of Tushino") between 1608 and 1610 (L2294-2296). Today, it is incorporated as a residential district within the northwestern administrative okrug of the city of Moscow.

## Historical Significance
During the social phase of the Time of Troubles, the Second False Dmitry marched on Moscow in the spring of 1608 but was checked by Prince Michael Skopin-Shuisky (L2294-2296). Unable to seize the capital, the pretender established a fortified camp at Tushino (L2296). 

From 1608 to 1610, Tushino functioned as a parallel center of power in Russia:
* **Alternative Government:** The pretender organized his own court, appointed a parallel Boyar Duma, and established a separate administration that granted land, titles, and collected taxes (L2296). 
* **Tushino "Flyers":** Many boyars and servitors switched sides, serving both Moscow and Tushino to secure double rewards, leading to a state of moral and political chaos (L2296).
* **Military Campaigns:** Tushino was the launching pad for the unsuccessful 16-month siege of the Holy Trinity-St. Sergius Monastery (L2298).
* **Wladyslaw Invitation:** In 1610, the Russian gentry in the Tushino camp bypassed the pretender and invited Prince Wladyslaw of Poland to take the Russian throne, setting the stage for Polish intervention (L2302-2303).

The camp disbanded in early 1610 after the combined forces of Prince Skopin-Shuisky and a Swedish mercenary detachment cleared northern Russia, forcing the pretender to flee to Kaluga (L2300-2302).
