# Kahun (Lahun pyramid town)

A planned settlement of the late Twelfth Dynasty (reign of Senusret II, c. 1880 BCE) built
beside the king's pyramid at Lahun, at the mouth of the Faiyum. Excavated by W. M. F.
Petrie (1888–90), it is one of the best-preserved purpose-built towns of the ancient world
and a central exhibit in Kemp's analysis of the
[[concepts/provider-state|provider state]].

## Why It Mattered

In [[sources/kemp-ancient-egypt-anatomy-2018|Kemp's *Anatomy of a Civilization*]] (Ch. 5,
"Model communities"), Kahun is read as **settlement-as-ideology**: a rigidly orthogonal
grid, sharply divided between a zone of large elite mansions and rows of small workers'
houses, walled and laid out at a stroke by the state to house the personnel of the
pyramid's construction and cult. Its geometry makes the [[concepts/divine-kingship|hierarchy]]
and provisioning logic of the Middle Kingdom state physically visible. Kemp puts it
bluntly: "Kahun was laid out by someone who saw only two social levels: top bureaucrats and
others" — in direct contrast to the more self-organized, finely graded growth of
[[places/amarna|Amarna]] (Kemp Ch. 8).

The figures carry the argument. The town was roughly square (~392 m on the north, ~9.5 ha),
its **~10 great mansions** (each ~42 × 60 m, with private granaries, garden courts and
craft areas) facing **~220 small houses** in back-to-back rows (a 1:20 ratio). A striking
discrepancy emerges between two population estimates: the small houses imply **under 2,000**
people, but the combined mansion granaries could store grain for **5,000–9,000** — meaning a
large share of the population drew **rations** from the great houses, and that the silos
were **modular distribution units** (echoing the *phyle*/crew structure) rather than one
central store. The Lahun/Kahun papyri split into a **temple/cult archive** and a **civic
archive** (debt, property, legal cases, household census lists, labour conscription); a
priest's census names 13 "serfs" that came with his office. Its ancient names were
**Hetep-Senusret** ("Senusret is at peace") and **Sekhem-Senusret** ("Senusret is strong").
