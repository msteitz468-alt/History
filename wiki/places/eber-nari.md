# Eber-Nari ("Beyond the River")

**Eber-Nari** (Akkadian *ebir nāri*, Aramaic *ʿăbar naharâ*, Hebrew *ʿēber
hannāhār* — "Beyond the River," i.e. west of the Euphrates) was the
[[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid]] satrapy comprising **Syria, Phoenicia, Palestine and
Cyprus** — Herodotus's **fifth satrapy**, assessed at 350 silver talents a year. It is
the Levantine bridge on which Persian power in **[[saite-egypt|Egypt]]** and the naval
struggle with the Greeks both depended. [[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 3b,
I. Eph'al) is the source; the detailed internal history of Judah is reserved for CAH VI.

## A region in darkness

The Persian-period Levant is, paradoxically, **harder to reconstruct than the
preceding Assyrian or following Hellenistic ages** — the royal inscriptions say almost
nothing, the Greek historians touch only the coast, and the biblical and archaeological
evidence cluster narrowly on **Judah and Phoenicia**. Most of inland Syria is "shrouded
in almost complete darkness." What survives illuminates above all the **administration**
of the empire and the **status of its ethnic groups**.

## Three kinds of subject

Eph'al divides the satrapy's political entities into three:

1. **The Phoenician city-states** — [[sidon|Sidon]], [[tyre|Tyre]], Byblos and Aradus
   ([[phoenicians|see the Phoenicians]]) — vassal kingdoms under their own dynasties,
   uniquely autonomous because Persia depended on their **war-fleets**; permitted to
   strike their own silver coinage from the mid-fifth century.
2. **The provinces** (*medinah*) — chiefly **[[yehud|Judah (Yehud)]]** and **Samaria**,
   plus Ashdod and Gaza, governed by *peḥah* drawn from the **local ethnic group** (the
   House of Sanballat in Samaria; Nehemiah and Bagohi in Judah), paying tribute, poll-tax
   and land-tax and owing corvée.
3. **The "Arabs"** — the Kedarite and proto-Nabataean tribes of the southern desert
   margin, **exempt from the *phoros* tax** and styled "friends" rather than subjects,
   because they controlled the water route to Egypt (they had supplied Cambyses' army in
   525) and the **frankincense trade**; in return they rendered 1,000 talents of incense
   a year as "gifts."

## The Persian innovation: ethnicity as administration

Eph'al's central argument is that the Persians made **ethnic-national units** — not just
territorial ones — "a significant factor in imperial policy," an innovation possible
because the Assyrians and Babylonians had already obliterated the region's old kingdoms.
Hence the **policy of repatriation** (Cyrus's edict returning the Jews and others to
their homelands for some eighty years), the **restoration of local temples** at royal
expense (the [[yehud|Jerusalem Temple]], like the temple of Apollo at Magnesia), and the
appointment of governors from within each people.

## The slow collapse of Persian control

From the late fifth century the satrapy was destabilized by **Egypt's independence
(404–342)** and its incursions into Phoenicia and Palestine, by **Persia's repeated
failed reconquests** of Egypt (basing armies at Acre), and by the **revolt of Tennes of
[[sidon|Sidon]]** (crushed savagely by Artaxerxes III). By the time
[[alexander-the-great|Alexander]] arrived in 332, Persian rule was "at most nominal":
Aradus, Byblos and Sidon surrendered or welcomed him, and the hard resistance at
**Tyre** (a seven-month siege) and **Gaza** (two months) came from **local elements**,
not Persian armies.

## Why Eber-Nari mattered

- **The strategic bridge** to Egypt and the springboard of the Phoenician fleet that
  fought the Greeks at Lade, Salamis, the Eurymedon and Cyprus.
- The setting of the **Second Temple Restoration** — the formative period of
  [[yehud|Second Temple Judaism]], known from Ezra–Nehemiah.
- A laboratory of **Achaemenid multiethnic administration**, the pattern later inherited
  by the Hellenistic kingdoms.

## Historiography

A reconstruction from scattered and lopsided evidence: the **Bible** (Ezra, Nehemiah,
Haggai) for Judah; **Phoenician royal inscriptions** (the Eshmunazar sarcophagus) and
**Greek historians** (Herodotus, Diodorus, the Alexander-historians) for the coast;
**Aramaic ostraca and papyri** (Arad, Beersheba, Wadi Daliyeh, Elephantine), **coins**
(the *yhd* and "Yohanan the priest" issues), and **destruction layers** in Palestinian
tells. Eph'al stresses how thin and coast-bound this is, and how much of inland Syria
remains unknowable.
