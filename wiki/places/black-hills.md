# Black Hills

The **Black Hills** (Paha Sapa) were a contested sacred and economic core of the northern plains horse era: early trade-fair node, Teton expansion prize, then the flashpoint of the 1874–77 war that ended free [[actors/lakota|Lakota]] life on the Powder River–Hills range.

## Geographic Description

Upland massif on the western edge of the northern plains (modern western South Dakota / northeastern Wyoming), ringed by high plains buffalo country and linked by trails to the Missouri and Powder River basins.

## History of Control and Settlement

**Early horse era:** early eighteenth-century [[actors/comanches|Comanches]] traded horses at Black Hills fairs as part of wider diffusion. Teton groups contested the Plains between the Missouri River and the Black Hills, an area occupied or frequented by Kiowas, [[actors/crow-apsalooke|Crows]], Arapahos, and [[actors/cheyenne|Cheyennes]]. By **1825** Teton groups had driven the Kiowas south of the Black Hills and Crows west toward Powder/Yellowstone.

**Great Sioux Reservation:** the **1868** treaty established the Great Sioux Reservation including the Hills and guaranteed Powder River hunting rights to the Sioux and allies. Red Cloud and Spotted Tail agencies lay on the southern boundary (North Platte/White Rivers).

**Gold and fraudulent cession:** gold discovery in the Black Hills **1874** brought miner trespass; the Yellowstone campaign was cut short when gold was found (Fowler). **1875** purchase/lease talks failed; government ordered all Sioux on reservation or face Army attack though off-reservation hunting was needed for agency food shortfalls. Winter **1876** campaign → year of Custer’s defeat at [[events/battle-of-little-bighorn-1876|Little Bighorn]]; following winter Army reinforcements, Yellowstone forts, and Congress supply cutoff pressed agency Sioux (with Arapaho and some Cheyenne) to sign a **1876 agreement** that would settle them permanently on the Sioux Reservation or Cheyenne–Arapaho [[places/indian-territory|IT]] reservation and **open the Black Hills to Americans**. Because the agreement was **obtained fraudulently** and included **only a portion of the Sioux**, fighting continued. Red Cloud and Spotted Tail agencies moved north within the reduced reservation after winter **1877** surrenders.

## Why It Mattered

Strategic buffalo and horse country; spiritual and political center for Lakota; legal core of the 1868 treaty whose violation after 1874 catalyzed the Great Sioux War. Northern Arapahos, badly weakened (under 1,000), withdrew from Cheyenne/Sioux allies when the Black Hills war erupted and enlisted as scouts 1876–77—illustrating how Hills politics realigned multi-tribal coalitions.

## Significance Over Time

From multi-tribal trade/contest zone → Teton-dominated reservation core → U.S. mining/settlement after fraudulent cession → ongoing sovereignty and land-claims politics (later reservation-era and renaissance pages).
