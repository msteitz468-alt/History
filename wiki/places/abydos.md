# Abydos

**Abydos**, in Upper Egypt, was the burial ground of Egypt's earliest kings and,
for the rest of the pharaonic period, the chief cult centre of **Osiris** and a focus
of royal piety. Its royal cemetery at **Umm el-Qaab** ("Mother of Pots") is the key
site for the emergence of the Egyptian state.

## Umm el-Qaab and the origins of kingship

The cemetery evolved from undifferentiated Naqada burials into an elite necropolis and
then into the royal cemetery of **Dynasty 0** and the **1st Dynasty**. Excavations by
the German Archaeological Institute in the 1980s–90s pushed its royal use back into the
4th millennium BCE — the evidence behind the concept of a "Dynasty 0" and the argument
that "the last one or two centuries of the 'Predynastic' were probably … politically
and socially 'Dynastic'."

The late Predynastic **Tomb U-j** (c.3150 BCE) "consisted of twelve rooms covering an
overall area of 66.4 sq. m.," held about 400 imported jars (probably Palestinian wine),
and yielded ~150 small bone/ivory labels carrying "what appear to be the earliest known
hieroglyphs." Its excavator Günter Dreyer identified it as a ruler's tomb, possibly that
of **King Scorpion**.

The 1st-Dynasty kings (Aha, Djer, Djet, Den, and others, with Queen Merneith) were
buried here, accompanied by **subsidiary burials of sacrificed retainers** — "the only
period in ancient Egypt when humans were sacrificed for royal burials," with Djer's tomb
surrounded by 338 such graves. Nearby stood great mud-brick **funerary enclosures** (the
best preserved being the *Shunet el-Zebib* of Khasekhemwy, walls still 10–11 m high), and
O'Connor's discovery of a fleet of **buried boats** 18–21 m long. See
[[actors/early-dynastic-egypt]] and [[events/unification-of-egypt-3100bce]].

## Cult centre of Osiris and the king-lists

Abydos remained a major cult centre throughout Egyptian history: in the Middle Kingdom
the 1st-Dynasty tomb of **Djer** was reidentified as the **cenotaph of Osiris**, drawing
pilgrimage for two millennia. The 19th-Dynasty temples of **Sety I and Ramesses II** here
carry the canonical royal **king-lists** that anchor
[[concepts/egyptian-dynastic-chronology|Egyptian chronology]] — selective ancestor-lists
that omit usurpers and the Amarna kings.
