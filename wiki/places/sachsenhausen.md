# Sachsenhausen

**Sachsenhausen**, built from July 1936 near Oranienburg north of Berlin, was the second-generation "model" SS concentration camp and, from August 1938, the site of the **T-Building** — the administrative nerve centre of the entire KL system (the Camp Inspectorate, later WVHA Office Group D under [[pohl-oswald|Pohl]]). Its proximity to Berlin made it the camp where several of the system's most lethal innovations were pioneered. Documented from [[wachsmann-kl-concentration-camps-2015|Wachsmann, *KL* (2015)]].

## Peacetime (1936–39)

Designed as an expandable "small city of terror," Sachsenhausen absorbed prisoners from the closing Esterwegen and Columbia House camps. Its adjacent **Oranienburg brick works** (from summer 1938) — planned as the world's largest, but a technical fiasco whose "dry-press" bricks crumbled — was the largest single KL labor detail (1,500–2,000 prisoners) and a death trap: at least 429 prisoners, mostly "asocials," died in the winter of 1938–39. Camp elder Harry Naujoks served over three years before being purged.

## The Killing Laboratory (1941)

Sachsenhausen was where the KL "moved from mass death to mass extermination." The first Action 14f13 selection of infirm prisoners took place here (4 April 1941). It then became the centre of the mass murder of Soviet POWs: [[eicke-theodor|Eicke]] briefed the local SS in August 1941, and a purpose-built **neck-shooting barrack** — a fake medical exam ending in a concealed gunshot to the neck, with a gramophone masking the sound — killed around 9,000 Soviet "commissars" in September–October 1941 alone, more than any other camp (~300–350 a day). Mobile gas vans were also first tested here on Soviet POWs. In "revenge" for Heydrich's assassination, ~250 Jews were shot in the same barrack in May 1942. The war-economy skilled-labour exceptions **Operation Bernhard** (the counterfeiting commando forging British banknotes) and the WVHA watch-repair workshop were also based here.

## Collapse

Overcrowded with evacuees in early 1945 (the boy Tommy Buergenthal among them), Sachsenhausen was largely evacuated on the death marches (the Below-forest halt) before Soviet troops found ~3,400 mostly sick prisoners on 22–23 April 1945.
