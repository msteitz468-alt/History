# Taxila

## Geography and Control

An ancient city of the [[gandhara|Gandhara]] region between the Khyber Pass and the Indus
(near modern Islamabad), at the junction of routes linking Central Asia and the Indian
subcontinent. Successively Achaemenid, Mauryan, Indo-Greek, Indo-Scythian/Parthian, and
then a major center of the [[kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]], it was excavated over decades by
British and South Asian archaeologists — who identified a **Kushan royal palace** — and
declared a **UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980**.

## Why It Mattered

In [[benjamin-empires-ancient-eurasia-2018|Benjamin's]] account Taxila is one of the major
nodes documenting Kushan material culture and a key waystation on the
[[silk-roads|Silk Roads]] corridor running from Central Asia down to the northwest Indian
ports. As a Gandharan center it lay at the heart of the **Greco-Buddhist art** world and
the [[spread-of-buddhism|spread of Buddhism]]. Its destruction by [[sasanian-empire|Sasanian]]
forces (along with [[begram|Begram]]) by **262 CE** is one marker of the Kushan collapse and
the wider [[collapse-of-first-silk-roads-era-250|end of the First Silk Roads Era]].

Source: [[benjamin-empires-ancient-eurasia-2018|Benjamin, *Empires of Ancient Eurasia* (2018)]].
