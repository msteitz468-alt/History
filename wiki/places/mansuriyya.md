# Mansuriyya

**al-Mansuriyya** ("the Victorious") was the **second Fatimid capital**, built by [[al-mansur-fatimid|al-Mansur]]
about 2 km south-east of **[[qayrawan|Qayrawan]]** on the site of his decisive **947** victory over
[[abu-yazid-al-nukkari|Abu Yazid]]. It was the seat of the [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] Imam-caliphs until the
move to Egypt, and the direct **prototype for [[cairo|Cairo]]**.

## The round city

Inspired by [[mahdiyya|Mahdiyya]] and by **Abbasid Baghdad**, Mansuriyya was laid out as a **circular city**
with a baked-clay outer wall and four gates, one per compass point, the Imam-caliph's palace at the centre, a
marketplace, and three great cisterns fed (from 959) by a 36-km aqueduct that also watered Qayrawan.
**14,000 Kutama families** were settled to populate it, with artisans and merchants in the suburbs. Its gates
(including **Bab al-Futuh**) and its **Bayn al-Qasrayn** square were reproduced when [[jawhar-al-siqilli|Jawhar]]
founded Cairo. Under [[al-muizz-li-din-allah|al-Mu'izz]] it was the setting for [[qadi-al-numan|Qadi al-Nu'man]]'s
weekly *majalis al-hikma* and the court of the poet **Ibn Hani al-Andalusi**.

## Later history

When al-Mu'izz migrated the court to Egypt in **972**, Mansuriyya passed to the **Zirid** viceroys, who ruled
Ifriqiya from it (and later from Qayrawan) until the 11th-century upheavals. Its ruins (the site of Sabra) have
yielded some of the rare surviving examples of Fatimid material art.
