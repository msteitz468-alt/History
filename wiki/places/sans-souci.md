# Sanssouci

The summer palace of [[frederick-the-great|Frederick the Great]] of
Prussia, built 1745–1747 in Potsdam, whose name ("without cares," in
French) expressed its purpose as a private retreat from the formalities of
royal ceremony.

## Geographic and Historical Description

Designed in close collaboration between Frederick and the architect Georg
Wenzeslaus von Knobelsdorff in a distinctive Prussian Rococo style, Sanssouci
was built on a terraced vineyard hillside and deliberately scaled as an
intimate single-story residence rather than a grand state palace. Frederick
used it as a personal retreat for philosophical and artistic pursuits,
hosting Voltaire as a resident guest from 1750 to 1753 during a period of
close (and eventually strained) intellectual friendship between the two men.

## Significance

Sanssouci exemplifies the Enlightenment ideal of the "philosopher king,"
embodying Frederick's self-conception as a ruler devoted to reason, the
arts, and philosophy alongside his more conventional roles as military
commander and state-builder; it remains a defining architectural symbol of
Prussian Enlightenment culture.
