# Aegean Sea

The sea and its islands lying between mainland Greece and Anatolia, the
central geographic stage of Bronze Age Aegean civilization and later of
Classical Greek naval power, colonization, and warfare.

## Geographic and Historical Description

The Aegean's dense scatter of islands (the Cyclades, the Dodecanese, and the
larger islands of Crete, Rhodes, Lesbos, and Chios) and short sailing
distances between them made it a natural zone of maritime connectivity from
the Bronze Age Minoan and Mycenaean palatial civilizations onward. During
the Classical period it became the operational theater of the Athenian
naval empire (the Delian League), the Peloponnesian War's naval campaigns,
and the later Hellenistic-era conflicts including the [[battle-of-amorgos|
Battle of Amorgos]] during the Lamian War.

## Significance

Control of the Aegean's sea lanes was the material basis of Athenian imperial
power in the fifth century BCE and remained strategically decisive well into
the Hellenistic period; the sea's islands repeatedly changed hands between
Athenian, Spartan, Persian, and Macedonian control as the balance of naval
power shifted.
