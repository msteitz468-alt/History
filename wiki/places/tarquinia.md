# Tarquinia

One of the twelve city-states of the Etruscan League (the "Dodecapolis"), Tarquinia (ancient Tarquinii) lay on a plateau near Italy's Tyrrhenian coast, with its port at Graviscae hosting a resident Greek trading community. Tradition made it the ancestral home of Rome's Tarquin kings (r. 616–509 BCE), and it reached its height of political and economic importance in the Archaic period (575–300 BCE) alongside fellow league members Cerveteri, Vulci, and Veii.

Tarquinia is best known today for its necropolis at Monterozzi, whose painted chamber tombs (including the Tomb of the Leopards, early fifth century BCE) form the single richest surviving corpus of Etruscan wall painting, depicting banqueting, dancing, and funerary games. The city was absorbed into the Roman orbit over the fourth–third centuries BCE as Rome extended control over Etruria.
