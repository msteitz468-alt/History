## Geographic Description

Fiji is an archipelago of approximately 330 islands in the south-western Pacific, located approximately 1,100 miles north of Auckland. The two largest islands, Viti Levu and Vanua Levu, contain the majority of the population. Suva (on Viti Levu) is the capital and regional hub; it hosts the University of the South Pacific and the South Pacific Commission secretariat.

Fiji sits at the boundary of Melanesia and Polynesia; its indigenous Fijian population is typically classified as Melanesian with strong Polynesian cultural influences. Rotuma, constitutionally part of Fiji, is a Polynesian outlier island.

## History of Control and Settlement

**Pre-colonial:** Organised under a system of confederacies and chieftainship (*turaga*). The Great Council of Chiefs (Bose levu Vakaturaga) represented the apex of chiefly authority across three main confederacies.

**Colonial (1874–1970):** British Crown colony. Colonial administration introduced large-scale Indian indentured labour to work the sugar industry, creating the Indo-Fijian community that by 1987 constituted approximately 43 per cent of the population. "The combined introduction of new skills, new technology and money have weakened the functional cement which binds Fijian village society" (R. G. Ward, quoted in [[denoon-cambridge-pacific-islanders-1997]]).

**Independence (1970–):** Governed by the Alliance Party under Ratu Sir Kamisese Mara for most of the independence period. The Alliance's electoral dominance depended on chiefly patronage and communal roll voting. See [[wiki/events/fiji-coups-1987|Fiji Coups 1987]].

**Post-coup (1987–):** The 1990 constitution enshrined Fijian paramountcy. The Great Council of Chiefs appoints the President (who must be of chiefly rank from one of the three confederacies) and nominates 24 of 34 Senate members. Of 70 lower-house seats, 37 are reserved for ethnic Fijians and 27 for Fijian Indians, elected from racially segregated rolls. A constitutional review commission was convened in the mid-1990s.

## Why Fiji Mattered

- **Regional hub:** Suva hosts regional institutions (USP, SPC, Forum Secretariat); the first Festival of Pacific Arts was held there (1972).
- **Ethnic complexity:** Fiji's Indo-Fijian/ethnic Fijian divide is the paradigmatic case of post-colonial ethnic tension in the Pacific, produced by British colonial labour recruitment policy.
- **Migration node:** Indo-Fijians emigrated in large numbers after the 1987 coups — "some 30,000 since 1987." Fijian Indians in the US showed "very high rates of growth" in the 1980–1990 census period.
- **Constitutional laboratory:** Fiji's 1990 constitution, its constitutional review, and the broader question of whether democracy is "a foreign flower unsuited to the Fijian soil" are treated by [[denoon-cambridge-pacific-islanders-1997]] as emblematic of the tension between majority-rule democracy and indigenous protection.
- **Banaban resettlement:** Banabans were resettled on Rabi Island in Fiji in 1945, establishing a special relationship with both the Fijian and Kiribati governments.
