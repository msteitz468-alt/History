# Berenike

A Red Sea port founded by Ptolemy II Philadelphus in the 3rd century BCE (named for his mother, Berenice I) as Ptolemaic Egypt's main gateway to Indian Ocean and Arabian trade, bypassing the difficult overland desert route by shipping goods up the Nile from a connecting road at Coptos. Under Roman rule Berenike became one of the principal termini of the lucrative Indo-Roman spice and luxury-goods trade described in the 1st-century CE *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*, alongside the nearby port of Myos Hormos. Extensive modern excavation (a long-running University of Delaware/Leiden University project) has recovered large quantities of black pepper, textiles, and other South Asian and East African imports, along with evidence the port remained active into the late Roman and early Byzantine periods before its final abandonment.

Berenike's archaeological record has substantially reshaped modern understanding of the scale and sophistication of Indian Ocean trade under Roman rule, showing volumes of long-distance exchange well beyond what literary sources alone would suggest.
