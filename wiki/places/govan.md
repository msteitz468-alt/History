# Govan

An early medieval royal and ecclesiastical center of the Brittonic kingdom of [[places/ystrad-clud|Ystrad Clud]] (Strathclyde/Alt Clut), located on the south bank of the River Clyde a short distance from the kingdom's principal stronghold at Dumbarton Rock (Alt Clut). Govan Old Parish Church preserves one of the most important collections of early medieval sculpted stones in Scotland, including some thirty-one surviving carved monuments (crosses, recumbent grave-slabs, and a distinctive group of Viking-influenced "hogback" stones) dating chiefly from the ninth through eleventh centuries, evidence of a site of considerable royal and ecclesiastical significance in a kingdom otherwise thinly documented in contemporary written sources.

Archaeological and art-historical evidence suggests Govan rose in prominence particularly after the Viking sack of Dumbarton Rock in 870, when the kingdom's political center may have shifted toward Govan and nearby Partick; the hogback stones, a monument type otherwise associated with Scandinavian-settled areas of northern Britain, point to Govan's place within networks of cultural and political contact spanning the Irish Sea and Scandinavian-influenced Britain in this period.
