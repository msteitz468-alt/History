# La Mojarra

## Geographic description

**La Mojarra** is a modest Gulf Coast site of earthen mounds covering about **1 km²** on a bend of the **Acula River** (Veracruz), in the lower **Papaloapan** drainage of the South Central Gulf Lowlands ([[gulf-lowlands]]). Richard A. Diehl (EW2001, “Mojarra, La”) places occupation from Late Formative through early Postclassic (c. **300 B.C.–A.D. 1000**).

## La Mojarra Stela 1

Diehl (EW2001): **Stela 1** (**2.34 × 1.42 m**) carries a long **Epi-Olmec / Tuxtla-script** text with Long Count dates readable as **21 May A.D. 143** and **13 July A.D. 156**—among Mesoamerica’s oldest and longest texts. Authenticity of the text is disputed by a few (forgery on a genuine monument); proposed language readings include **Pre-Proto-Zoquean** (Justeson & Kaufman), early Mayan, or nonlinguistic approaches; only tentative reading claims center on an accession narrative.

Carolyn Tate (EW2001, “Art”): post-Olmec Gulf “Bridge Culture” known from ~a dozen artifacts with writing thought to record Proto-Mixe-Zoque; the La Mojarra stela shows a ruler with stacked bird headdresses standing on an earth symbol/toponym.

South Central Gulf overview (EW2001): La Mojarra yielded a stela with elaborately garbed leader and lengthy Terminal Formative inscription; associated mound testing revealed mainly **Classic** occupation, giving the stela an **anomalous depositional context**. Script (with the **Tuxtla Statuette**) reflects pre-Proto-Zoquean language and anchors Long Count use in Terminal Formative lower Papaloapan—evidence for **Mixe-Zoquean** rather than solely Totonac presence in part of the area. Style and calendrics link to [[cerro-de-las-mesas|Cerro de las Mesas]] and earlier Izapa-related sculpture at [[tres-zapotes|Tres Zapotes]] (Diehl CHNPA; EW2001). Webster (EW2001 Warfare culture history) notes the stela (A.D. 159 in that summary) may reference military victory—a secondary reading alongside the accession narrative.

David C. Grove (EW2001 Olmec Culture): whether Formative Olmec culture “ceased” ~500 B.C. or evolved into Late Formative Tres Zapotes / La Mojarra / Tuxtla Statuette remains unresolved—La Mojarra anchors the Epi-Olmec writing horizon after monumental Olmec heartland decline.

## Why it mattered

Keystone text for **Epi-Olmec writing**, early Long Count outside the Maya area, and Mixe-Zoquean language hypotheses on the Gulf.
