# Sweden

The latest-unified and most internally diverse of the three kingdoms, formed by
the welding of the Götar of the southern lakelands (Götaland) and the Svear of
the Mälaren basin (Svealand, with the cult and royal centre at
[[uppsala|Uppsala]]), and extending its lordship across the Gulf of Bothnia into
[[finland|Finland]].

## Significance

Sweden's conversion and consolidation lagged behind Denmark and Norway; pagan
cult at Old Uppsala persisted into the late 11th century. Royal power was
contested between dynasties until the [[folkung-dynasty|Folkungs]] (Bjälbo
dynasty) under Birger Jarl and his successors built a more durable monarchy in
the 13th century, founding [[stockholm|Stockholm]] and crusading into Finland.
The Hanseatic [[gotland|Gotland]]/Visby trade tied Sweden to the Baltic economy.
Sweden's recurrent resistance to Danish dominance defined the late-medieval
[[kalmar-union-1397|Kalmar Union]] and ultimately broke it.

## Medieval Sweden in Detail (Cambridge History)

Unification was the slowest (c. 1000–1250), the Götar and Svear separated by forest;
**Olof Skötkonung** (c. 995–1020) was the first baptised king (at Husaby) and minted
the oldest Swedish coins at Sigtuna, but pagan cult at [[uppsala|Old Uppsala]]
persisted to c. 1080. Kingship was **elective**, confirmed by riding the *Eriksgata*
through the provincial things; the [[folkung-dynasty|Folkungs]] (Bjälbo dynasty) under
Birger Jarl, **Magnus Ladulås** (Alsnö/Skänninge statutes establishing noble *frälse*),
and Magnus Eriksson built a more durable monarchy, founded [[stockholm|Stockholm]]
(1252), crusaded into [[finland|Finland]], and fixed the Novgorod border at **Nöteborg
(1323)**. The dynasty ended with Albert of Mecklenburg's election (1364).

Sweden retained the **largest reserves of freehold land** (over half of farms; 90% in
Norrland and Dalarna), giving its free miners and peasants repeated capacity for
political mobilisation — the [[engelbrekt-rebellion-1434|Engelbrekt rebellion]],
[[battle-of-brunkeberg-1471|Brunkeberg]], and ultimately Gustav Vasa's revolt.
Pre-plague population was c. 500,000–650,000 on c. 75,000 farms; iron exports grew to
c. 1,100 tons by the 1490s. Sweden was the only kingdom to receive a medieval **urban
code** applicable to all towns. See [[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]].
