# Stratford-upon-Avon

Market town on the River Avon in Warwickshire, approximately 90 miles northwest of London. In the 1560s a prosperous but not large borough (population perhaps 1,500–2,000) with a grammar school, parish church (Holy Trinity), guild chapel, and regular markets and fairs. John Shakespeare (glover, wool dealer, sometime bailiff) and Mary Arden raised their family here. William Shakespeare was baptised in Holy Trinity on 26 April 1564; he almost certainly attended the King's New School; he married Anne Hathaway (from nearby Shottery) in 1582; their children were born here.

Shakespeare maintained lifelong ties to the town. He purchased New Place, the second-largest house, in 1597 and retired there around 1611–13. He was buried in Holy Trinity on 25 April 1616. The town later became the centre of the Shakespeare "industry" (birthday celebrations, Birthplace Trust, Royal Shakespeare Company, tourism).

The contrast and connection between provincial Stratford and metropolitan London is one of the structuring facts of Shakespeare's biography and imagination.
