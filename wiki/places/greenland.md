# Greenland (Kalaallit Nunaat)

World’s largest island; Arctic homeland of [[greenland-inuit|Kalaallit / Greenland Inuit]], site of Norse Eastern and Western Settlements, Danish colonial reoccupation from 1721, and Home Rule from 1979. Central geographic stage for [[arctic-native-history-norse-to-modern]].

## Geographic description

Ice-cap dominated island with habitable strips mainly on the southwest and west coasts (fjord-head meadows used by Norse; ice-edge and outer-coast zones used by Thule/Inuksuk hunters). Disko Bay region: hunting zone of early Norse–Thule contact. East coast and far north (Polar Eskimo / Thule district) entered regular European administration later. Strategic North Atlantic position controlled Allied shipping lanes and air routes in WWII; cryolite deposits acquired wartime industrial importance.

## History of control and settlement

1. **Late Dorset** remains preceded Norse on west Greenland (c. A.D. 700–900 north of Holsteinsborg); Dorset also present elsewhere during Norse times but meetings judged unlikely (`[[dorset-culture]]`).
2. **Norse** (from c. 985): Eastern Settlement (~4,000–5,000 peak) and Western Settlement (~1,000–1,600), both on southwest coast; husbandry + hunting; disappeared by end of fifteenth century (multi-causal).
3. **Thule / Inuksuk** ancestors of modern Inuit: contacts from ~thirteenth century; southward expansion with ecological adjustments; Norse materials define Inuksuk phase sites (`[[thule-culture]]`).
4. **European rediscovery** without continuous settlement until **1721** (Hans Egede); Royal Greenland Trade monopoly; 1776 coastal closure; 1782 two inspectorates.
5. **Danish colonial–then-Kingdom** administration through nineteenth–twentieth centuries; 1925 Administration Act; 1953 constitutional incorporation.
6. **Home Rule** 1 May 1979; EC membership with Denmark 1973, withdrawal 1985; continuing financial support from Denmark.

## Why it mattered

- **Medieval:** Norse North Atlantic outpost; walrus ivory and Arctic exports; earliest sustained European–Native Arctic co-occupation.
- **Colonial:** Laboratory of protectionist hunting-economy policy; mission linguistics (Greenlandic Bible/education); hybrid West Greenland population.
- **Modern:** Model of Arctic self-government short of full independence; circumpolar Inuit politics (ICC); resource and fisheries sovereignty claims; demographic and social costs of forced town centralization under G-50/G-60.

## Significance over time

From Norse dual-settlement periphery → abandoned European memory → Danish missionary-trade colony → wartime Allied outpost → integrated Kingdom province with legal equality gaps → Home Rule polity balancing Danish subsidies against Kalaallit political and cultural autonomy and ICC-oriented identity.
