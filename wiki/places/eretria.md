# Eretria

Major Euboean polis on the eastern side of the Lelantine plain; partner/rival of [[places/chalcis|Chalcis]]; West Gate elite burials; early colonial and Ionian-Revolt roles. Principal Hall discussion: [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] Chs.1, 4–5, 11.

## Geographic setting

Coastal Euboea facing the Lelantine plain and routes toward Attica and the Cyclades. Neighbor and rival of Chalcis (~24 km); relationship to [[places/lefkandi|Lefkandi]] (possible earlier focus / “Old Eretria” debates) is central to settlement history.

## Urban form and cult (Hall Ch.4–5)

Eighth-century settlement ~45 ha (population estimates ~540–2,250 under density bands). Dark Age apsidal houses replaced by rectilinear plans in the seventh century. Mid-eighth “chiefly” Building 1 continues after ca. 725 Apollo Daphnephoros *hekatompedon* — a case where a possible ruler’s dwelling coexists with monumental temple construction rather than being simply superseded. Late eighth–seventh urban elaboration ties Eretria to wider polis-formation debates.

## West Gate “hero” burials

Around 720 an anonymous Eretrian warrior received funerary honors paralleling Patroclus’ funeral in the *Iliad*: ashes in cloth with jewelry and a gold/serpentine scarab, bronze cauldron, weapons, a Late Bronze Age bronze staff, animal (including horse) sacrifice — followed by six further adult cremations and youth inhumations; after the last burial ca. 680 a triangular limestone monument hosted ritual meals into the fifth century. Often linked to [[events/lelantine-war-c700bce|Lelantine War]] dead; Hall invokes Snodgrass’s positivist fallacy — the burials might mark any of thousands of unknown Eretrian episodes. Sixteen wealthy West Gate burials over a generation suit a **powerful family**, not a full aristocratic class ([[concepts/basileus-and-big-man]]).

## Elites and alliances

Elite class traditionally **Hippeis** (“horsemen”). Herodotus (5.99) cites an earlier Eretria–Miletus vs Chalcis–Samos alliance to explain Eretrian aid to the Ionians in 499; Hall notes the undated earlier alliance may have been invented to justify fifth-century intervention. Eretria sent five ships to the Ionian Revolt; after Carystus, Eretria fell after a seven-day siege in 490 through betrayal; temples sacked, inhabitants enslaved eastward — Persian fleet then sailed to Marathon with ex-tyrant [[actors/hippias-son-of-pisistratus|Hippias]].

## Colonization and war tradition

Joint foundation traditions with Chalcis (Pithecusae per Strabo); later narratives claim Eretria fared worse after the Lelantine conflict. Outcome unrecorded; Lefkandi “destruction” is insecure evidence. See [[controversies/lelantine-war-historicity]].
