# Star Carr

**Star Carr** is a key Mesolithic site in northern England, frequently cited in syntheses of post-glacial hunter-gatherer adaptations in temperate Europe. It exemplifies early Holocene forager responses to warming climates, wetland resources, and the transition toward greater sedentism and intensification before the arrival of farming.

## Role in the Source

Champion et al. (1984) place Star Carr within the broader post-glacial (Mesolithic) developments of Ch. 4, as part of the "Hunters, Gatherers and Beyond" that preceded and framed the early farming societies of Ch. 5. The site is representative of the "gradual shift to a broad spectrum of resources in the late glacial period" and the "establishment of more sedentary settlements before the emergence of domesticated animals and cereals."

The source's discussion of forager-farmer interactions and the transition repeatedly references the preceding Mesolithic phase (e.g., "It was suggested in Chapter 4 that one motive for a shift to agricultural production in the first place may have come from social conditions of emulation or status-seeking through the provision of food").

Maps in Ch. 5 (e.g., around lines ~1674–1713 of the assigned range) explicitly label "Mesolithic site" distributions in Britain and contrast them with Neolithic settlements, underscoring the presence of established forager populations at the time of agricultural colonization.

## Geographic and Environmental Context

Located in a former lake-edge wetland (now the Vale of Pickering), Star Carr documents intensive use of lacustrine and woodland resources in the early post-glacial Atlantic period. Such sites illustrate the "post-glacial adaptation" that originated partly in the Near East but played out in parallel across Europe as climates warmed, sea levels rose, and vegetation changed.

## Historiography and Significance

Star Carr has been central to British Mesolithic studies since its excavation in the 1940s–50s. It challenges older views of mobile, marginal foragers by showing structured settlement, wooden platforms, antler frontlets (possibly for ritual or hunting), and evidence of seasonal or prolonged occupation. Within the 1984 textbook's thematic framework, it contributes to the argument that economy and society were already complex and intertwined before agriculture, providing the social and demographic context into which farming spread (via colonization with indigenous contribution or adoption).

The site anchors discussions of "early sedentism" and "intensification" in the Mesolithic section of the European prehistory narrative.

## Collection Note

Star Carr is emblematic of the strong coverage of post-glacial adaptations in the collection for Britain and temperate Europe. It supplies concrete evidence that the "Neolithic package" arrived into landscapes already occupied by sophisticated forager societies capable of sedentism and resource intensification.
