# Pincevent

**Pincevent** is a Magdalenian open site in France notable for its substantial hearths and associated spatial patterning of stone and bone debris, providing insight into Upper Palaeolithic activity areas and possible tent or open-air working locations.

## Key Features

The site has three substantial hearths placed 3 m apart. Associated with each hearth are clearly defined arcs of stone and bone debris. The two hearths with large quantities of chipped stone have a single stone seat placed on the west and east side respectively.

"The arcs of debris have been interpreted as the remains of small circular tents but equally plausible is the interpretation of two working hearths which were used in the open air. The choice of which hearth, and its seat, was used depended on the wind direction that day and the fact that smoke gets in your eyes." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~940-960)

## Interpretation

This patterning may reflect practical considerations of wind and smoke rather than necessarily roofed tents. It exemplifies how ethnoarchaeological principles and attention to formation processes help distinguish between structural remains and repeated open-air activity zones.

The site contributes to understanding Magdalenian settlement organization in western Europe during the late Upper Palaeolithic.
