# Hierakonpolis

**Hierakonpolis** (ancient *Nekhen*; modern Kom el-Ahmar, in Upper Egypt) was one of
the principal centres of [[processes/predynastic-egypt|Predynastic Egypt]] and a
crucible of [[concepts/state-formation|Egyptian state formation]]. Its temple deposit
yielded the most famous monuments of the unification era.

## A precocious elite centre

Hierakonpolis was "an important site from an early date." Already in the **Naqada I
(Amratian)** phase it had exceptionally large graves — the largest rectangular
Amratian tomb measured 2.50 × 1.80 m — and burials of evidently powerful individuals
marked by disc-shaped porphyry maceheads; it preserves the sole surviving Naqada I
built structure (a burnt rectangular house with an oven, 4.00 × 3.50 m). "From this
time onwards it was the centre of an elite group." It is also among the southernmost
sites with Badarian-related finds.

In **Naqada II**, Michael Hoffman's fieldwork showed settlement "clustered near the
river, which supported the cultivated land, where simple artificial irrigation
techniques could take advantage of the annual flood." The painted **Tomb 100** (the
"Painted Tomb"), with its scenes of boats, hunting and domination, is the outstanding
elite monument of the period.

## The unification monuments

From the temple precinct at Hierakonpolis, Quibell and Green excavated the **"Main
Deposit"** — a cache of late Predynastic and early pharaonic votive objects including
the **[[actors/narmer|Narmer Palette]]**, the **Narmer Macehead**, and the **Scorpion
Macehead**. These are the principal material sources for the
[[events/unification-of-egypt-3100bce|unification]], though (as the volume stresses)
they "commemorate rather than narrate" — symbolic statements of royal power and
[[concepts/divine-kingship|kingship]] rather than literal records of events.
