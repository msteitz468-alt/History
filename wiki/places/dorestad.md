# Dorestad

One of the most important early medieval *emporia* (trading settlements) of northwestern Europe, situated on the Rhine near the modern town of Wijk bij Duurstede in the Netherlands. Under Frankish and then Carolingian control, Dorestad flourished from roughly the 7th through 9th centuries CE as a hub of North Sea and Rhineland trade, handling goods including Rhineland wine, Rhenish pottery (e.g., Badorf ware), Mayen lava querns, and both sceatta and later Carolingian silver coinage minted on site. Its position linked river trade from the Frankish interior to maritime routes connecting Frisia, England (via emporia like Ipswich and Lundenwic/London), and Scandinavia. Dorestad's prominence declined sharply in the later 9th century amid repeated Viking raids and a shifting course of the Rhine that undermined its harbor, and the settlement was effectively abandoned by the 10th century.
