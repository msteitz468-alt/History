# The Breakers (Newport)

Seventy-room Italianate summer palace at Ochre Point, [[places/newport-rhode-island|Newport]], built for [[actors/vanderbilt-cornelius-ii|Cornelius Vanderbilt II]] and Alice Vanderbilt by architect Richard Morris Hunt after a fire destroyed an earlier house on the site (1892–1895). Named for the Atlantic breakers at the cliff base. Cooper & Howe open *Vanderbilt* with Gladys Szápáry's forced departure from the third-floor apartment on Good Friday, 30 March 2018 — the end of continuous Vanderbilt-descendant residence.

## History of control

Cornelius II spent a reported $7 million building the house (~$220 million in the authors' present-day equivalents) but died of a stroke in 1899 after only four summers. Alice used it until 1934 and left it to daughter Gladys Vanderbilt (Countess Széchényi). A 1948 arrangement leased the house to the Preservation Society of Newport County for $1 while the Countess retained responsibility for taxes and major repairs. After her death in 1965, children could not hold it: in 1972 they sold the house for $365,000 and gifted much furniture — Cooper & Howe calculate nearly $218 million equivalent "evaporated" over 77 years. Daughter Sylvia ("Sylvie") Szápáry received life tenancy of the third floor; after her death in 1998, Gladys and Paul Szápáry remained until the Society, citing fire/plumbing codes and conflict over a visitor center (2013–2015), ended residential occupancy (letter threat 2015; exit 2017–18).

## Significance

Most visited of the Newport "cottages"; material embodiment of peak Vanderbilt wealth and of the conversion of private dynastic space into heritage tourism. Half a million visitors a year in the book's account. Spatial symbol of [[processes/vanderbilt-dynasty-rise-and-fall|rise and fall]].
