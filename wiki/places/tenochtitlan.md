# Tenochtitlan

## Overview

**Tenochtitlan** was the capital of the Aztec (Mexica) Empire and, on the eve of
the Spanish Conquest, **the largest and most densely built settlement in North
America** (~200,000 people). A lacustrine city built on islands in Lake Texcoco,
it is the Mesoamerican case of the **early imperial city** in
[[yoffee-cwh-v3-2015|CWH Vol. III]] (Ch. 24, Gerardo Gutiérrez), and "the most
refined expression of Mesoamerican urbanism," heir to 2,000 years of tradition
([[teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] before it).

## From immigrants to empire

By Mexica mythology the people left **Aztlan** in 1064 CE, reaching the Basin of
Mexico — already an urban network of 40+ cities around the lakes, organized into
five warring confederations. Rejected and enslaved as newcomers, the
Mexica-Tenochca finally founded **Tenochtitlan in 1325 CE** (the eagle-on-cactus
omen) on a marginal island under Azcapotzalco's overlordship. After electing
their first *tlatoani* (Acamapichtli) and, in **1428**, defeating Azcapotzalco,
they formed the **Triple Alliance** (Tenochtitlan–Texcoco–Tacuba) — the Aztec
Empire — expanding to span central Mexico from Gulf to Pacific.

## The altepetl

Gutiérrez stresses the native political form, the **altepetl** ("the water, the
mountains") — *not* a simple "city-state": a multi-centric polity governed by
**multiple ruling lineages and a council of rulers**, embracing cities, towns,
land, and people with **no urban–rural dichotomy**. (A caution against importing
Old World [[city-state]] models — compare [[heterarchy]].)

## A tributary imperial city

Tenochtitlan's monumental form (the Templo Mayor as **axis mundi**, causeways,
chinampa agriculture, the Tlatelolco market) was financed by a vast **tributary
system**: ~652,000 mantas (cloth units) per year siphoned from ~39 provinces
within a 400 km radius — bulky staples from nearby, precious goods (gold, cacao,
feathers, jade) from afar — concentrated in the Basin and consumed in lavish
ritual, feasting, and gift-giving. The capital **depleted its hinterland**
(artificial scarcity) to subsidize the imperial center — the [[imperial-city]]
pattern, and a form of [[ruralization]].

## Conquest

Tenochtitlan was the **first American city taken by Old World siege craft**
(1521) — though its fall owed as much to **epidemic disease** and the armies of
its many resentful tributary enemies as to Spanish arms.

## The Aztec Empire in the Postclassic (CWH V)

[[kedar-cwh-v5-2015|CWH V]] (Smith, Ch 23) situates Tenochtitlan as the capital of the
[[aztec-empire|Aztec Empire (Triple Alliance)]], the great exception to a Postclassic
Mesoamerica of generally "smaller, shorter-lived, and less despotic" polities. The
Basin of Mexico — the demographic and political core of Mesoamerica since
[[teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]] — rebounded to a Late Postclassic population peak, and the
Mexica turned it into the base of a tribute empire integrated by a shared Postclassic
"international" art and symbol set and by expanding commercial networks. See
[[aztec-empire]].

## Why this place mattered

Tenochtitlan is the latest and one of the greatest of the volume's cities — an
independent American imperial metropolis whose scale, tribute economy, and
ceremonial centrality rival any Old World capital, captured at the very moment of
its violent end.

## The founding and the island city (Berdan)

Frances Berdan's [[berdan-aztecs-2021|*The Aztecs*]] details the city's improbable rise. The Mexica
founded it in **1325** on a poor lake island — the spot marked by Huitzilopochtli's omen of an **eagle on
a cactus** (the image on the *Codex Mendoza*'s first folio, and on the modern Mexican flag) — after
generations as despised mercenary vassals. They made the cramped island liveable by reclaiming farmland
from the lakebed as **chinampas** ("floating gardens," though they did not float), building an **aqueduct**
from the Chapultepec springs, and raising the **Templo Mayor** (*Huey Teocalli*) at the sacred core,
which they enlarged thirteen times. The city was divided into four quarters and many *calpolli*
neighbourhoods, knit to the shore by causeways and thronged with the canoe traffic that fed the great
**Tlatelolco market** (see [[aztec-economy]]). As the [[aztec-empire|Triple Alliance]] capital, tribute
from 38 provinces flowed into it "on the backs of lines and lines of human porters." Its ceremonial heart
— the Templo Mayor as the mythic mountain **Coatepec** — made the city the stage of Aztec
[[aztec-religion-and-human-sacrifice|cosmic ritual]]. The Spanish razed it in 1521 and built **Mexico
City** directly on top.
