# Pacific Northwest Coast

Culture-area and historical region from roughly **Yakutat Bay** (usual northern boundary of the Northwest Coast culture area) south toward **northern California**, with interior plateaus east of the coast ranges as a related but distinct zone. Principal secondary synthesis for postcontact history to the 1880s: Fisher, CHNPA I.2 ch. 11 (process page [[northwest-native-history-to-1880s]]).

## Geographic description

Common denominators: Pacific Ocean to the west, mountains to the east. Natural setting ranges from arid central Oregon through the wet temperate rainforest coast of British Columbia and Washington to cold Alaskan coasts and islands. Fertile land is comparatively scarce (e.g. BC: of ~95 million ha only ~3% crops, ~14% livestock)—so river deltas and interior lake/river valleys were both Native population centers and primary settler targets, concentrating conflict.

**Coast–interior divide:** Coastal peoples exploited seasonally abundant maritime environments with complex social structure, elaborate ceremonies, and stylized art. East of the coast mountains the environment was less prolific, population smaller, mobility greater, social organization looser, ritual and material culture less elaborate—fishing (especially salmon), hunting, southern Plateau camas/wild turnip; winter pit houses in protected valleys.

Extreme linguistic diversity: dozens of mutually exclusive languages representing most major Native North American language families—greater cultural diversity than any other North American region. Map lines overstate sharpness of dialect borders; territorial boundaries were still clearly recognized.

## Why it mattered

- **Precontact trade networks:** dentalium as standard of value (Nootka gathering, prized by southern Yuroks); Mt. Edziza and eastern Oregon obsidian widely distributed; eulachon oil, elk hides, horses, captives.
- **Fur-trade hinge of the Pacific:** sea otter to China; maritime boom after Cook; company forts (Sitka, Fort Vancouver, Fort Simpson, Fort Victoria); Native-controlled price formation and middleman systems.
- **Imperial four-way arena:** Russia, Spain, Britain, United States—later Canada/U.S. boundary at 49th parallel (1846) split the historical region and produced **different techniques of dispossession with convergent late-nineteenth-century outcomes** (Fisher).
- **Ceremonial and artistic core** of continental indigenous fame (potlatch, crest poles)—elaborated under fur-trade wealth; criminalized (Canada 1884) or undermined by poverty/allotment under settlement.

## Control and settlement change over time

1. Indigenous exclusive control and intergroup trade into mid-eighteenth century.
2. Russian coercive labor among Aleuts vs failure to control powerful [[tlingit]] after 1800 (Sitka destroyed 1802 under Kotlean; rebuilt 1804; hostility into 1855).
3. Maritime captains as transitory visitors; HBC/Russian permanent but few and enclave-limited until settlement.
4. From late 1830s–1840s, American agricultural settlement (Willamette) and post-1846 territorial regimes south; colonial BC north (Douglas then Trutch).
5. By 1880 Native dispossession through most of the region; reserves/reservations; commercial fishery competition; museum collecting under poverty.
