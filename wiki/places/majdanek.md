# Majdanek

**Majdanek** (KL Lublin), on the edge of the city of Lublin, was — with [[auschwitz|Auschwitz]] — one of the only two SS concentration camps that also operated as a **Holocaust death camp**. Ordered by [[himmler-heinrich|Himmler]] on 22 September 1941 as a huge base for Soviet-POW slave labour to build his eastern settlements, it was the first KL in the General Government. Documented from [[wachsmann-kl-concentration-camps-2015|Wachsmann, *KL* (2015)]].

## From Slave-Labour Camp to Death Camp

Himmler's original plan (up to 150,000 Soviet POWs) collapsed with the mass death of the prisoners; hardly any of the ~2,000 POWs sent in autumn 1941 survived. From spring 1942 Jews were substituted for the dead Soviets: the camp grew from ~100 prisoners (25 March 1942) to 10,660 (24 June), mostly Slovak Jews, under the transferred Buchenwald commandant Karl Otto Koch. A vast, dirty building site with no proper water supply — the survivor Dionys Lenard's account describes bottomless mud and thin weed-soup — Majdanek killed over 14,000 registered Jews in 1942 through neglect, selection, and shooting. **Gas chambers** (equipped for both Zyklon B and carbon monoxide) went into use around October 1942, and the camp became a full death camp from late 1942, running regular selections on arrival.

## Aktion Erntefest

On **3 November 1943**, under the code name Action "Harvest Festival" (*Erntefest*), some 18,000 Jews were shot in ditches at Majdanek in a single day — to Viennese waltzes piped over loudspeakers to mask the salvos — the **largest single massacre ever at an SS concentration camp**. Ordered after the Sobibór uprising, it killed ~42,000 across the Lublin-district camps and ended Majdanek as a Holocaust camp. Globocnik's Lublin office had interfered constantly in the WVHA-run camp, and it processed the plunder of Operation Reinhard. Majdanek never rivalled Auschwitz in scale; the SS abandoned it in July 1944, and its gas chambers fell largely **intact into Soviet hands** — the first major camp so exposed to the world.
