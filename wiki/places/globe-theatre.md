# Globe Theatre

Open-air polygonal amphitheatre built in 1599 on Bankside, Southwark, by the Lord Chamberlain's Men (Shakespeare among the sharers) using timbers from the dismantled Theatre. It was the company's principal London venue until the 1613 fire (rebuilt 1614). Capacity estimates range from 1,500 to over 3,000. The stage was a large thrust with a tiring-house, trap, and heavens; performances occurred in daylight with minimal scenery but rich costume, music, and sound effects.

The Globe is the space most closely associated with Shakespeare's mature work. Several plays contain explicit references to its architecture and audience relationship (the Chorus in *Henry V* calls it "this wooden O"; *Hamlet*, *Macbeth*, and others were written for its acoustics and sightlines). After 1608 the company also used the indoor Blackfriars; the two venues offered different experiences and audience mixes.

The original Globe was destroyed by fire in 1613 during a performance of *Henry VIII*. A modern reconstruction (Shakespeare's Globe) opened nearby in 1997 and has been used for research into original practices as well as contemporary performance.
