# Zhoukoudian (Dragon Bone Hill)

**Zhoukoudian** — the limestone hill of **Longgushan** ("Dragon Bone Hill"), southwest of Beijing —
is the single most important Palaeolithic locality in East Asia and the type site (with Java) for
*Homo erectus sensu stricto*, the classic "Peking Man." It holds at least **27 Palaeolithic
localities**, first identified in **1921** by the Swedish geologist Gunnar Andersson from quartzite
fragments out of place in the limestone. The "dragon bones" of the name are the fossils long sold in
Chinese medicinal shops — the same trade that, for [[anyang|Anyang]], led to the discovery of Shang
oracle bones.

## The Localities

- **Locality 1** yielded the remains of **more than 40 *Homo erectus* individuals**, dated to **more
  than 400,000 years ago**. The original fossils were **lost during World War II**; only casts of the
  skulls survive. The 40–50 m of deposits are dominated by quartz(ite) tools — **88.8%** of the
  industry — of which **75.2% are small scrapers** and only **5.4% heavy choppers**, with evidence of
  fire.
- **Locality 4**: "archaic *Homo erectus*," dated ~250 kya.
- **Locality 15**: the second-richest locality, **over 100,000 artifacts**, abundant fauna and fire
  evidence, dated between >140 and 100 kya.
- **The Upper Cave**: *Homo sapiens* remains with modern behaviour, dated **29–24 kya**, interpreted
  as a **burial area** of at least five adults, a youth and two children, accompanied by ochre and the
  **earliest "jewelry" known from East Asia** — perforated animal teeth and stones, hollow bird-bone
  segments and bone needles. Seashells here attest coastal exploitation and long-distance trade inland.
  The Upper Cave skeletons resemble Upper Palaeolithic Europeans but cannot be assigned to any modern
  population. Nearby **Tianyuan Cave** has securely dated anatomically modern remains at **42–39 cal.
  kya**.

## Significance

Zhoukoudian anchors the East Asian record of [[peopling-of-east-asia|the peopling of East Asia]]:
it fixes *H. erectus* in north China well after the earliest Mainland colonisation (~1.66 mya, the
Nihewan Basin), and it spans the full arc from *erectus* through archaic forms to behaviourally
modern *H. sapiens* in one massif. It is also the empirical heart of the **multiregionalist vs.
Out-of-Africa** debate in China: Chinese palaeoanthropologists long read the Zhoukoudian-to-modern
sequence as local continuity (*erectus* → "archaic *H. sapiens*" → modern East Asians), whereas the
consensus mtDNA model derives modern populations from Out of Africa 2 (~72 kya), allowing only brief
interbreeding at the wave front — a tension [[barnes-archaeology-east-asia-2015|Barnes]] presents as
partly reconciled but unresolved.
