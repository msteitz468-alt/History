# Plataea

**Plataea** was a small Boeotian city (fewer than 1,000 citizens) whose democratic government resisted the oligarchic Boeotian League dominated by Thebes and maintained a loyal alliance with Athens since the sixth century. Its strategic position less than eight miles from Thebes flanked the best roads from Thebes to Athens and the only route connecting Thebes to Megara and the Peloponnesus avoiding Athenian territory.

In the Peace of Nicias (421) "Thebes was to keep Plataea" as a key exception to status quo; Athens kept Nisaea. Kagan notes Pericles would have been appalled: "Plataea, Athens’ comrade in arms at Marathon and loyal ally ever since, was abandoned to Theban control." The exiles had been placed at Plataea earlier by Sparta. (Grounded from Kagan range.)

## The Theban attack (431 BCE)

On a cloudy night early in March 431, over three hundred Thebans sneaked into Plataea guided by Nauclides and oligarchic traitors who hoped to turn the town over to Thebes. The Thebans expected peaceful surrender and offered no reprisals to gain a friendly oligarchic regime. Instead the Plataeans mounted resistance; before dawn they attacked the disoriented invaders in the rain. Plataean women and slaves on rooftops hurled stones and tiles. Many Thebans were killed; survivors surrendered. The main Theban army arrived too late (Asopus River swollen by rain). Plataeans threatened to execute prisoners unless the army withdrew, then killed 180 captives anyway — "an atrocity, the first of many that only grew in horror as the years of war went by" (per Kagan). Athenians sent supplies and 80 hoplites (total garrison 480 men + 110 women for baking) to hold the city. [[sources/kagan-the-peloponnesian-war-2003|Kagan 2003]] (THE THEBAN ATTACK ON PLATAEA (431)).

The attack was a clear breach of the peace, triggering the Spartan invasion of Attica under Archidamus.

## Later significance

Plataea became a focal point of the Archidamian War. Spartans (urged by Thebans) besieged it in 429 rather than invade plague-ridden Attica. The city held with a small garrison until its fall in 427, when the Plataeans were executed or enslaved in a notorious act. Its loyalty to Athens exemplified the costs small states paid in the bipolar struggle.
