# Lake Titicaca

## Overview

**Lake Titicaca**, the great high-altitude lake of the Andean **altiplano** (c. 3,810 m), was
one of the principal hearths of Andean civilization. Its basin supported intensive **raised-
field (suka kollus) agriculture** and vast camelid herds, underwriting the rise of
[[tiwanaku|Tiwanaku]] (c. 500–1000 CE), and after Tiwanaku's collapse the competing
[[aymara-kingdoms|Aymara kingdoms]] (Lupaqa, Colla) of the Late Intermediate Period. The Inka
absorbed the basin as the core of **Collasuyu** and wove it into imperial origin myth — Inka
tradition held that the founding ancestors emerged from **Lake Titicaca / the Island of the
Sun**.

## Significance

In [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|Volume III]] the Titicaca basin is the
southern pole of highland Andean statecraft and the empirical heartland of John Murra's
**[[vertical-archipelago|verticality]]** model (drawn from the Lupaqa). Its sacred status in
Inka cosmology made control of the lake ideologically as well as economically central.
