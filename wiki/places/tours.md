# Tours

**Tours** (Roman *Caesarodunum*, then *civitas Turonum*) was a *civitas* on the **Loire**
in central Gaul whose importance in the post-Roman centuries was overwhelmingly religious:
it was the burial place and cult centre of **St Martin** (d. 397), the most popular
pilgrimage shrine and the foremost political sanctuary in Merovingian Gaul. Its bishop
**[[gregory-of-tours|Gregory]]** (r. 573–594) made the city the vantage point of the
*[[gregory-of-tours-history-of-the-franks-thorpe-1974|Ten Books of Histories]]*.

## Significance

Tours sat on the contested frontier between the Frankish kingdoms and was repeatedly
fought over. Before [[clovis]]'s victory at [[battle-of-vouille-507|Vouillé (507)]], just
outside Poitiers to the south, the region lay near the Visigothic march; Clovis received
his consular honours from the emperor Anastasius "at St Martin's church in Tours" before
moving his seat to Paris. Through the sixth-century civil wars the city changed hands among
the kings — controlled in turn by [[sigibert-i|Sigibert]], [[chilperic-i|Chilperic]], and
[[childebert-ii|Childebert II]] — and its territory was savagely ravaged: Gregory records
Chilperic's agent Roccolen threatening to "set your whole countryside alight" and armies
"setting fire to it and ravaging it," sparing "not even Saint Martin's property."

The **sanctuary of St Martin** gave Tours and its bishop real political leverage: fugitives
of the highest rank sought asylum at the tomb — Sigibert's son **Merovech**, the rebel
**Guntram Boso**, the murderer **Eberulf** — forcing kings to negotiate with the bishop
rather than simply seize them. Gregory's repeated refusals to surrender those under St
Martin's protection, even under threat of the city's burning, are among the *Histories'*
central episodes and define the Merovingian bishop's role as guardian of his city. Under
the Treaty of Andelot (587) Tours was assigned to [[childebert-ii|Childebert II]].
