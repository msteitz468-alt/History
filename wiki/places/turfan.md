# Turfan (Gaochang)

Turfan (Chinese Gaochang) was a vital oasis on the northern Silk Road route, site of the ancient city of Gaochang. It was a multilingual hub with significant Sogdian, Chinese, and local populations, known for contracts, taxes, markets, and religious diversity.

## Economic and Documentary Record

Excavated documents (Chinese, Sogdian, etc.) from the Turfan region reveal local economies: contracts for slaves, land, marriages; tax payments in silk/cloth (yongdiao); a famous 743 market register listing 350 items with high/medium/low prices, mostly local goods rather than exotics. Chinese garrisons stimulated trade through silk payments and infrastructure.

## Sogdian and Religious Role

Sogdian communities (with sabao headmen) were prominent; Zoroastrian practices documented in tombs and ossuaries. The oasis shows religious coexistence (Buddhism, Zoroastrianism, Manichaeism, Christianity) before Islam.

## Significance

Key evidence for small-scale trade, garrison economy, Sogdian networks, and the "documents as trash" revealing ordinary life on the Silk Road. Xuanzang visited; multilingual society.

**Grounded claims from Hansen**:
- 743 market with triple prices for local items.
- Sogdian sabao and Zoroastrian influence.
- Garrison-stimulated economy and post-755 contraction.
