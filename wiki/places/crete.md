# Crete (Archaic)

Archaic **Crete** — Homer's "island of a hundred cities" — was a distinctive, inward-
looking world of independent Dorian city-states that flowered in an idiosyncratic
**orientalizing** culture in the eighth and seventh centuries and became the Greek
world's great **home of law**, before fading into relative unimportance.
[[boardman-cah-v3p3-1982|CAH Vol. III, Part 3]] (Ch. 39b, John Boardman, on the
archaeology; Ch. 39c, R. F. Willetts, on laws and society) treats it.

## An idiosyncratic orientalizing culture

The prosperity of Geometric Crete continued through the seventh century. With "no other
record" than the archaeological one — the ancient authors are silent on the period,
talkative only on Crete's laws — Boardman traces a **distinctive, largely inbred
orientalizing culture**, nourished by continued contact with [[cyprus|Cyprus]], Egypt
and the Near East but "idiosyncratic and mainly inbred," expressed in painted and relief
vases, jewellery, sculpture, bronzework and especially **armour**, with great diversity
from city to city. Close to a hundred sites are known for the period; the important ones
— **Cnossus, Gortyn, Prinias, Drerus, Praesus, Lyttus, Kavousi** — span the era, and the
island shared in the very early adoption of **alphabetic writing**. After this peak,
artistic activity **declined in the sixth century**, even as Cretan artists worked
abroad — a falling-off that matches Crete's later economic and military marginality.

## The home of law

Crete's lasting contribution was in **law and the early city-state**. It belongs to the
seventh-century wave of legal codification, but with a special prestige: not a colony
needing a single code, but "a centre of pilgrimage for legal inspiration," its
authority resting on the tradition of **Minos as the Zeus-inspired lawgiver**. The
**law of Drerus** (c. 650–600, limiting re-tenure of the chief magistracy) is perhaps
the earliest surviving complete Greek law on stone, and the great **[[gortyn-code|
Gortyn Code]]** is the fullest single record of early Greek law and society anywhere.

## A conservative Dorian society

Crete preserved an archaic **Dorian agrarian order** strikingly like Sparta's — indeed
Aristotle thought the Spartan constitution derived from the Cretan via Lyttus. Its
features: magistrates (the *kosmoi*) drawn from ruling clans in place of abolished
kings; communal messes (**syssitia**) funded from public lands and **serf tributes**;
youth raised in **age-classes (*agelai*)**; and at the base a **patriarchal serfdom** —
the original populations reduced to tribute-paying *woikeis* tied to the ancestral
estate (*klēros*) — that endured for centuries alongside chattel slavery. These are
detailed on the [[gortyn-code|Gortyn Code]] page.

## Why Crete mattered

- The Greek world's **paradigm of legal codification and conservative Dorian society** —
  the claimed ancestor of the Spartan system and the source of our fullest early Greek
  law code.
- A self-contained **orientalizing channel** whose idiosyncratic art shows the eastern
  impulse working on an island that absorbed and transformed it on its own terms.
