# Mezhirich

**Mezhirich** is a late Upper Palaeolithic (Gravettian/Magdalenian) open site in Ukraine famous for its elaborate mammoth-bone structure interpreted as a hut or dwelling.

## Description

Some 385 mammoth bones were found above a shallow depression containing two small hearths and a scatter of flint and bone artifacts. The mammoth long bones and jaws had been stacked to form a wall enclosing a circular space of about 5 m². Thirty-five mammoth tusks lying on top of the bone pile were interpreted as curved supports for a roof which might then have been covered by skins. Two large hearths lay just outside the structure.

"The 'hut' at Molodova would, if it ever existed, have enclosed 48m². The excavators at Mezhirich found 35 mammoth tusks lying on top of the mammoth bone pile and these were interpreted as the curved supports for a roof which might then have been covered by skins." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~960-980)

"The decorated mammoth skull from the dwelling at Mezhirich (Pidoplichko, 1969) points to another domestic context of display in upper palaeolithic society." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~1320-1330)

## Significance

The structure was used on a number of occasions. It is smaller and more complex in bone arrangement than the Molodova I oval. The site illustrates the use of megafauna remains for shelter in eastern European periglacial zones and the integration of display (decorated skull) in domestic contexts.

Full investigation requires detailed spatial analysis of materials, as demonstrated by recent advances in reconstructing specific flint-knapping events at such sites.
