# Kiev

Kiev (Kyiv) was the historic center and capital of Kievan Rus', serving as the primary cultural and political hub of the early East Slavs.

## Geographic Description
On the Dnieper (hills/forests; water flowed below heights in Olga's time; city on heights at Gordyata/Nicephorus site; prince's palace at Vratislav/Chudin site; another palace with stone hall behind Holy Virgin Church on heights; hunting grounds outside).

## History of Control and Settlement (per Primary Chronicle, ll. 3000–6000)
Pre-852: Polyanians (Slavic) lived apart here; paid Khazar tribute (one sword per hearth). "the Russes rule over the Khazars even to this day."

862+: Askold and Dir (Varangians) settle after Rurik at Novgorod; "established their dominion over the country of the Polyanians."

882: Oleg (with Igor) captures by ruse; kills Askold/Dir on hill (later "Hungarian" site, Ol'ma castle; Dir behind St. Irene’s). "Oleg set himself up as prince in Kiev, and declared that it should be the mother of Russian cities."

Oleg imposes tribute; builds stockades. 907/911/945 treaties list Kiev first among Russian cities for trade privileges.

945: Igor killed by Derevlians on return from tribute; Olga in Kiev with Svyatoslav (palace locations described).

946+: Olga's revenge (trench/bath/feast at hall outside city); reforms.

c.955: Olga returns from Constantinople baptism.

968: Pechenegs besiege; Olga + grandsons (Yaropolk/Oleg/Vladimir) inside; youth/ Pretich rescue.

980: Vladimir enters; sets Perun (silver head, gold mustache) + other idols "on the hills outside the castle with the hall"; "the land of Rus’ and this hill were defiled with blood." (Later St. Basil church per chronicle.)

988: Vladimir returns post-baptism in Kherson (with Anna, relics); imposes Christianity (implied mass baptism, idol destruction).

## Why This Place Mattered
Strategic on Dnieper ("way from the Varangians to the Greeks"); political capital ("mother of Russian cities"); trade hub (treaties prioritize Kiev merchants); religious (pagan idols then Christian center; Olga baptism precedent; Vladimir conversion anchor). Significance grew from tribal Polyanians seat to Rurikid core, then Christian metropolis.

## Geographic and Topographic Description (from Primary Chronicle notes, Cross ed. 1953, lines 12001–16299)

Kiev lay on the west bank of the Dnieper. The Lybed’ brook "rises in the western section of modern Kiev and follows substantially the track of the modern railway from the central station to the point where the brook joins the Dnieper just south of the monastery at Vidubichi." Predslavino was near this creek (lines ~12101-12106). Dorogozhich "was apparently the flat elevation extending between the present northwestern section of Kiev known at Luk’yanovka and the locality of St. Cyril's Monastery" (lines ~12069-12072). Berestovo "now a section of the modern city of Kiev, is situated on the bluffs overlooking the Dnieper just north of the Crypt Monastery. The remains of Vladimir's residence on this site have recently been excavated" (lines ~12115-12118). The Setoml’ "was a small stream which formerly flowed into the Pochayna across the northern part of the Podol’" (lines ~12981-12983). Zolot'cha "was a small inlet from the Chertorjya creek on the east bank of the Dnieper opposite Kiev" (lines ~14393).

## Key Structures and Events

Vladimir founded the Desyatinnaya (Church of the Blessed Virgin of the Tithe) ca. 990-991 after Kherson (it "was (as far as one can judge by surviving remains) a basilica with a wooden roof"; dedicated May 12; damaged in 1017 fire and rebuilt; destroyed 1240; site excavated; lines ~12519-12540, ~12546). "The church of Our Lady of Tithe, which he built in 989 ff." included a chapel to St. Clement (lines ~12457-12458). Yaroslav's citadel "inclosed the plateau lying to the southwest of the smaller primitive citadel dating from the days of Olga and Vladimir, and extending westward as far as the modern Ul Podval'naya (formerly Yaroslavov Yar)"; Golden Gate ruins at junction of Ul Sverdlova and Korolenko (lines ~12986-12998). St. Sophia begun 1037 (cruciform, five apses, thirteen cupolas initially; Greek mosaics; lines ~12999 ff., ~13003-13038). St. George and St. Irene churches (patron saints of Yaroslav and consort) "were located within Yaroslav’s citadel south of St. Sophia" (lines ~13040-13053). Vydubichi monastery of St. Michael's "situated on the western shore of the Dnieper about 1¼ miles south of the Crypt Monastery" (church dedicated 1088; lines ~13546-13560).

Crypt (Pecherskaya Lavra) Monastery "situated on a bluff overlooking the Dnieper at the southeastern extremity of the modern city" (lines ~13242-13244). Events here include Bonyak's 1096 raid ("set fire to the shrine of the Holy Virgin"; lines ~9100s referenced in notes). Theodosius relics transferred ca. 1091 between metropolitans (lines ~14001-14027). Prior John of Crypt protested blinding of Vasil'ko violently (imprisoned temporarily in Turov; lines ~14302-14305).

Political center: assemblies, prisons, palaces (e.g., Vladimir's red palace burned 1096); veche in Podol; bridge over ravine near old citadel (lines ~13174 ff.). Svyatopolk buried Tugorkan "between the road to Berestovo and the road to the monastery" (1096; referenced). Bolestaw's 1018 and 1069 occupations noted.

From Monomakh Testament notes (lines 14500s): extensive campaigns launched from/returning to Kiev; 1113 accession of Vladimir after Svyatopolk's death (Polovcians advanced on Vyr' upon hearing of it; lines ~14597-14599).

## 11th-century events from the Primary Chronicle (Laurentian Text, ll. 9001–12000)

In 1096, during the inter-princely conflicts of the Rurikids, Polovtsian (Cuman) forces under Bonyak appeared before Kiev "on a Sunday evening, and while ravaging the environs, they burned the prince’s palace at Berestovo" (ll. 9070–9074). Later the same month, after the death of Tugorkan in battle near Pereyaslavl', Bonyak struck again: "the Polovcians almost entered the city, burned the suburbs about the town, and then attacked the monastery. After burning the monastery of Stephen and the villages of Germanus’s monastery, they came to the Crypt Monastery while we were resting in our cells after matins, and they howled about the monastery. They planted two standards before the monastery gates... The godless sons of Ishmael slew the brethren in the monastery and wandered about among the cells, breaking down the doors... Then they set fire to the shrine of the Holy Virgin... they seized the eikons, burned the doors, and blasphemed against God and our faith" (ll. 9102–9120). They also "burned the red palace which the pious Prince Vladimir had constructed upon the hill called Vydobychi" (ll. 9127–9128). The chronicler (from the Caves Monastery) frames the raids as divine chastisement for Christian sins, with the pagans as "a chastisement to the Christians" who "shall enter the Kingdom of Heaven" through suffering (ll. 9136–9126).

Kiev remained the symbolic senior seat; the 1097 Lyubech agreement explicitly assigned "Svyatopolk retaining Kiev, the heritage of Izyaslav" (ll. 9327–9328). Svyatopolk, Vladimir, and other princes repeatedly operated from or returned to Kiev; the Metropolitan and "the men of Kiev" played a key mediating role after the blinding of Vasil'ko, pleading with Vladimir Monomakh not to ruin "the land of Rus’" won by "your sires and grandsires" (ll. 9513–9526). Svyatopolk "arrived before the Crypt Monastery at matins on the feast of the Assumption" after the 1107 victory "and the brethren embraced him amid great rejoicing" (ll. 10061–10064). The city and its monasteries (esp. the Crypt/Pechersky) were central to both political assembly and monastic record-keeping.

## Later significance
Kiev's primacy eroded after Vladimir Monomakh's death, with the political center shifting northeast (see [[vladimir]]). It was sacked by Andrew Bogoliubsky in 1169 and finally by the Mongols in 1240.
