## Overview

The Caribbean Islands form a roughly semicircular archipelago stretching over 4,000 km between South, Central, and North American mainlands. They are conventionally divided into four major groups: the **Greater Antilles** (Cuba, Hispaniola, Puerto Rico, Jamaica — 88% of insular land area), the **Lesser Antilles** (the double arc from Trinidad to the Virgin Islands), the **Bahama Archipelago** (calcareous islands from the Florida coast to Haiti), and **Southern Caribbean islands** (Margarita, Aruba, Curaçao, Bonaire, Trinidad, Tobago).

Geologically, the Greater Antilles are volcanic islands forming along mountain ranges originating in northern Central America. The Lesser Antilles follow an "arcuate zone of instability" along the Atlantic edge of the Caribbean tectonic plate, with an inner arc of high volcanic cones and an outer arc of limestone islands. The Bahamas are entirely non-volcanic carbonate (limestone) formations.

The islands span tropical to subtropical climates, fall within the North Atlantic hurricane belt, and are shaped by persistent trade winds. Sea levels and climate have varied significantly over deep time—Trinidad was connected to the South American mainland until the Holocene (~7000 BCE).

## Pre-Columbian Settlement History

The Caribbean islands were never isolated landscapes. Their pre-Columbian history is a story of mobility and exchange across the Caribbean Sea, Gulf of Mexico, and Florida Straits.

**Archaic Age (~6500–400 BCE):** The first inhabitants arrived from the South American mainland and possibly Florida. Two distinct complexes are recognised: flaked-stone and ground-stone. Major sites include Barrera-Mordán (Dominican Republic, c. 4000 BCE) and Banwari Trace (Trinidad, c. 5000 BCE). See [[archaic-age-caribbean]].

**Early Ceramic Age (~400 BCE–AD 600):** Saladoid-series ceramic-using peoples migrated northward from Amazonia/Orinoco basin into the Lesser Antilles and onward to the Greater Antilles. See [[saladoid-culture]].

**Ceramic Age diversification (AD 600–1492):** Post-Saladoid cultures diversified regionally: Ostionoid and Chicoid in the Greater Antilles; Meillacoid in Hispaniola; Lucayan in the Bahamas; Kalinago-related sequences in the Lesser Antilles. The [[taino]] cultural complex emerged in the Greater Antilles by c. AD 1000–1200.

**Spanish contact (1492):** Columbus landed on Guanahaní (San Salvador) on 12 October 1492, beginning the colonial encounter. See [[columbian-exchange-1492]].

## Geographic Sub-regions

- **Greater Antilles:** Cuba, Hispaniola (Haiti + Dominican Republic), Puerto Rico, Jamaica, Cayman Islands
- **Lesser Antilles:** From Virgin Islands south through Guadeloupe, Martinique, St. Lucia, St. Vincent, Grenada to Trinidad and Tobago
- **Bahama Archipelago:** Commonwealth of the Bahamas and Turks & Caicos Islands

## Historiography

Caribbean archaeology developed largely through the framework of Irving Rouse's time-space diagram (Rouse 1992), which organised material culture into "series" (Saladoid, Ostionoid, Meillacoid, Chicoid, etc.) and migration streams. This framework has been refined and partially challenged by newer network-oriented and heterarchical approaches (Keegan and Hofman 2017; Hofman Nexus 1492 project). The colonial-era Arawak/Carib binary has been substantially dismantled archaeologically.
