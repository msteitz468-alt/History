## Overview

Hamath (modern **Hama**) is a city on the **Orontes river** in central Syria, ~146 km south
of Aleppo, dominated by a 45 m-high tell that in the first millennium formed the citadel of a
larger settlement. Occupied continuously from the Neolithic (M6 or earlier) to the present
day — excavated by a Danish team (Carlsberg Foundation, 1931–38) across thirteen phases — it
is best known as the capital of an important **Iron Age kingdom** of the same name, a polity
that began under [[neo-hittite-states|Neo-Hittite]] rulers and passed to an [[arameans|Aramaean]]
dynasty before being absorbed by [[neo-assyrian-empire|Assyria]] (Bryce 2009: 282–4).

## History

The earliest written references to Hamath (as *Amatu*) appear in the mid-M3 archive of
[[ebla|Ebla]]. Notably the name does **not** occur in second-millennium sources, prompting
J. D. Hawkins to suggest the Late Bronze Age town lay within the territory of another state
(perhaps **Tunip**) rather than being a major centre in its own right.

By the early first millennium Hamath had become the capital of a large kingdom, frequently
attested in biblical and Assyrian sources. In biblical tradition its earliest known king is
**Toi (Tou)**, who congratulated King David on a victory over Hadadezer of Zobah
(2 Samuel 8:9; if historical, c. 980). Through the first part of its Iron Age life Hamath was
ruled by a **Neo-Hittite dynasty** whose kings — **Urhilina** (= Assyrian *Irhuleni*) and his
son **Uratamis (Rudamu)** — left several [[luwians|Luwian hieroglyphic]] inscriptions recording
the building of cities, a fortress, and a temple of the goddess **Baalat**. The kingdom reached
its greatest extent under [[neo-assyrian-empire|Tiglath-pileser III]] (745–727), who lists
nineteen of its districts.

Hamath was a leading member of the **anti-Assyrian coalition** that met Shalmaneser III at the
[[battle-of-qarqar-853bce|Battle of Qarqar]] on the Orontes in 853 (the indecisive outcome
forced Shalmaneser into further campaigns in 849, 848, 845). Around 796 the Neo-Hittite line
gave way to an **Aramaean dynasty**, the first known ruler being **Zakkur**, whose early-C8
Aramaic stele (found at Tell Afis) records his rescue, by divine intervention, from a coalition
led by Bar-Hadad of Damascus. This Aramaean rule was short-lived: Hamath's last king,
**Yaubidi (Ilubidi)**, led a revolt of Syrian kingdoms against [[sargon-ii|Sargon II]] shortly
after his accession (721). Sargon crushed it, **flayed Yaubidi alive**, made Hamath an Assyrian
province, deported many of its people (some to Samaria, 2 Kings 17:24) and resettled it with
colonists. The conquest of "the whole of Hamath" by the crown prince **Nebuchadnezzar** (605),
after his victory over Egypt at [[carchemish|Carchemish]], passed it to [[neo-babylonian-empire|Babylon]].
The Iron Age city was apparently destroyed and left largely unoccupied until the Hellenistic
period, when the Seleucid king **Antiochus IV Epiphanes** refounded it as **Epiphaneia**.
