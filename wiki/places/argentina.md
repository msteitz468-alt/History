# Argentina

**Argentina** — dominated by the vast temperate grasslands of the **Pampas** in the southern-cone
of South America — is, with Uruguay, the South American member of [[crosby-ecological-imperialism-1986|
Crosby's]] [[neo-europes|"Neo-Europes"]]: temperate lands that European biota and people remade in
Europe's image.

## A grassland Neo-Europe

The Pampas exemplify the **[[portmanteau-biota|portmanteau biota]]** thesis. Horses and cattle
introduced by the Spanish in the sixteenth century escaped onto the open grassland and multiplied
into enormous feral herds — the ecological foundation of the *gaucho* economy — while European
weeds and forage grasses transformed the sward, all part of the wider
[[columbian-exchange-1492|Columbian Exchange]]. Old World diseases and, later, military campaigns
(the 1870s "Conquest of the Desert") reduced the indigenous peoples of the interior.

By the late nineteenth century the prepared grassland, filled by mass European immigration and
linked to Europe by steamship and refrigeration, made Argentina one of the world's great
exporters of temperate foodstuffs — beef and wheat — the South American counterpart to
[[new-zealand|New Zealand]], Australia, and the North American plains in Crosby's account of how a
handful of temperate regions came to supply a disproportionate share of the world's tradable
food.
