# Little Bighorn River

The Little Bighorn River (Lakota: *Greasy Grass*) is a tributary of the Bighorn River in south-central Montana. On 25–26 June 1876 its valley was the site of the largest and most famous victory of Plains Indian forces over the U.S. Army during the Indian wars, when a massive Lakota, Northern Cheyenne, and Arapaho village overwhelmed five companies of the 7th U.S. Cavalry under George Armstrong Custer.

The river valley provided water, grass, and cover for one of the largest encampments ever assembled on the northern plains (estimates range from several thousand to over ten thousand people). The battlefield terrain—river bottom, steep bluffs, and open ridges—shaped the fight: Reno's battalion was driven from the village into the timber and then to the bluffs; Custer's command was isolated and destroyed on high ground to the north.

Today the site is Little Bighorn Battlefield National Monument. Archaeological investigations (1980s onward) have refined understanding of troop and warrior movements, weapon types, and casualty patterns.
