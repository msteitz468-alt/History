# Sanxingdui

A **Bronze Age site on the Chengdu Plain** (Sichuan) whose spectacular bronzes belong to a
culture **contemporary with the late [[shang-dynasty|Shang]] but "culturally quite unlike" it**.
Sanxingdui is [[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook's]] prime exhibit against the
**[[east-asian-heartland-region|"isolated / unitary China"]]** myth: the second-millennium-BCE
[[east-asian-heartland-region|Heartland]] held **multiple, very different Bronze Age civilizations**,
not one.

## The Finds

By the late second millennium BCE the people of Sanxingdui had developed "a highly advanced Bronze
Age culture quite different from anything else known from the East Asian Heartland Region." The
defining objects, buried in **sacrificial pits** with gold, jade, and elephant ivory, include:

- large **standing human figures** with massive, mask-like heads;
- **bronze masks** with heavy features, **fearsome bulging eyes**, and thin-lipped smiling mouths —
  iconography with no parallel in Shang art;
- bronze "spirit trees" and a wealth of gold foil.

Their precise ritual function is unknown — possibly carried in processions — and the **ancient
Sichuanese script remains undeciphered**.

## Relation to the Shang

A number of **actual Shang bronzes** in the Sanxingdui caches show **(probably indirect) trade
contact** with the Yellow River world, but they are marginal beside the overwhelming
distinctiveness of the local industry. Long jade blade "scepters" produced in
[[longshan-culture|Longshan]] Shandong also reached Sanxingdui and nearby Jinsha, where they were
**reworked locally** — evidence of long-distance exchange reaching deep into Sichuan well before any
formal road. Sanxingdui is conventionally linked to the later kingdom of **Shu**, conquered by
[[qin-dynasty|Qin]] in 316 BCE.

## Why This Place Mattered

Sanxingdui is the single most dramatic refutation of a homogeneous early "China." It demonstrates an
**independent center of high bronze metallurgy and monumental religious art**, in contact with but
not derived from the Shang — exactly the plural, interacting picture the
[[east-asian-heartland-region|Heartland Region]] frame is designed to capture. Its discovery
reshaped the study of Chinese Bronze Age religion and forced the [[zhou-dynasty|Zhou]]-centric
"single uniform Shang–Zhou China" model to be abandoned.
