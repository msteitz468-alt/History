# Bergen

The principal town and effective capital of high-medieval [[norway|Norway]] and
its leading export port, founded c. 1070. Bergen was the entrepôt for the North
Atlantic **stockfish** (dried cod) trade from northern Norway and the island
communities, which drew the [[hanseatic-league|Hanseatic League]] to establish one
of its four great foreign Kontors at Bryggen, with a large resident German
merchant community. The town exemplifies [[scandinavian-urbanisation|Scandinavian
urbanisation]] and the Hanseatic penetration of the Nordic economy.

Bergen acquired town status under Olaf Kyrre (1067–93), became Norway's de facto
capital and royal seat in the 13th century, and won a legal staple monopoly c. 1300
(barring foreign ships from sailing north of the town or west to Iceland). Around 1500
it was possibly the **most populous Scandinavian town (c. 7,000)**; stockfish made up
**over 90% of its exports** (1370 Lübeck customs). The Hanseatic Kontor on Bryggen
practised internal jurisdiction and barred intermarriage with Norwegians; in 1455 its
members killed the bishop of Bergen and 60+ others at Munkeliv.
