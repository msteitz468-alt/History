# Tehuacán Region

## Geographic description

The **Tehuacán** region is a basin about **115 km long and 50 km wide** in the south-central Mexican highlands (southern Puebla, northernmost Oaxaca). The Sierra Madre Oriental shields the region from Gulf rains; annual rainfall is **<300 mm**; seven ecozones have been defined. Thirty-five years of research (1960–1995) uncovered nine cultural phases (Richard S. MacNeish, EW2001). The valley is one of the classic dry-cave sequences for [[maize-domestication|plant domestication]] and [[paleoindian-and-archaic-mesoamerica|Archaic]] lifeways in Middle America.

## Historical significance

### Preceramic sequence (MacNeish phases)

| Phase | Approx. range | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Ajuereado** | 30,000±10,000–8650 BCE | Small nomadic microbands; plant collecting and hunting/trapping; early extinct horse/antelope then modern fauna; chipped flint tools only |
| **El Riego** | c. 8650–5700 BCE | Seasonal nomadism; microbands vs macrobands; squash (*Cucurbita mixta*), chile, avocado, possibly wild maize; mortars/pestles; nets, baskets, dart shafts; elaborate multiple burials; related to Desert Culture of northern Mexico/SW US/Great Basin—later Tehuacán preceramic diverges with agriculture |
| **Coxcatlán** | c. 5700–3825 BCE (12 RC dates) | Still nomadic collectors with trapping/hunting, but accumulating domesticates—early domesticated maize, chile, avocados, gourds; later amaranth, tepary beans, zapotes, *C. moschata*; incipient agriculture with multiple origins across Nuclear America, not a single center |
| **Abejas** | 3825–2600 BCE | More sedentary; some year-round terrace pit-house settlements; canavalia, possibly *C. pepo* and common beans, hybrid maize with teosinte introgression; still **>70% wild foods**—Neolithic evolution, not revolution |

Middle Archaic highland data are richest at Tehuacán: wet-season macroband occupations as numerous as microbands; plant remains include avocado, squash (*C. mixta*, *C. moschata*), gourds, common beans, zapotes, chupandilla, chile, amaranth, and maize varieties. The **El Riego tradition** c. 7600–5000 B.C. ranges from Tecolote Cave (Hidalgo) to El Riego in the Tehuacán Valley (MacNeish Archaic overview).

### Ceramic and Formative–Postclassic sequence

| Phase | Approx. range | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Purrón** | 2600–1600 BCE | Earliest pottery yet found in Mesoamerica (crude, imitating stone vessels) |
| **Ajalpan** | 1600–900 BCE | Full-time agriculture; wattle-and-daub villages of 100–300; pottery affinities with early lowland Formative; dogs in Tehuacán caves c. 1900–1400 B.C. |
| **Santa María** | just before 900–c. 200 BCE | Villages oriented around ceremonial centers; first irrigation (**Purron Dam**); ranking rises; Olmec influence slight; Orizaba obsidian trade; Oaxaca grayware ties |
| **Palo Blanco** | 200 BCE–700 CE | Irrigation; turkeys and new crops; hilltop ceremonial centers with pyramids, plazas, ball courts; Monte Albán III–related; salt export; chiefdom possible while Oaxaca/Valley of Mexico developed city-states. **Cuayucatepec**: Terminal Formative Early Palo Blanco (150 B.C.–A.D. 250) hilltop town >30 ha, population somewhat >1,000, ball court—one of a half-dozen similar fortified/hilltop polities |
| **Venta Salada** | 700–1540 CE | Little kingdoms/city-states; Tehuacán Viejo mural in Codex Borgia style; ethnohistory that **Nonoalco from Tula** invaded and ruled Teotitlán del Camino, Coxcatlán, and Tehuacán |

### Cotton and craft

Cotton boll fragments are common by **3500 B.C.** (two controversial fragments ~2000 years earlier); loom weaving by 900–200 B.C. (perhaps 1500 B.C.) (Berdan via EW2001).

Source: Richard S. MacNeish, Robert D. Drennan, Kent V. Flannery, Frances F. Berdan in [[evans-webster-archaeology-ancient-mexico-central-america-2001|Evans & Webster (eds.) 2001]]. Later direct AMS dating of Tehuacán maize has revised some early maize ages downward relative to MacNeish’s original sequence—see historiography notes on [[maize-domestication]] and [[paleoindian-and-archaic-mesoamerica]].
