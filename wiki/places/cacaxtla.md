# Cacaxtla

## Overview

**Cacaxtla** (central Puebla-Tlaxcala hilltop) is an Epiclassic center celebrated for vivid
**Maya-influenced murals** in a highland Mexican setting — key evidence for long-distance
elite interaction and competitive statecraft after [[teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]].

## Chronology and character (Cowgill CHNPA 2000; Adams intro)

May be **coeval with Metepec Teotihuacan** or develop soon after collapse — unresolved until
better ceramics. Not exceptionally large, but extraordinary murals (including cruel slaughter
of defeated soldiers): naturalistic, individual, gruesome — **opposite Teotihuacan style**.
Eclectic with Teotihuacan elements, but **lowland Maya features dominate** (Nagao 1989);
artists almost surely Maya; rulers more likely from nearer Gulf lowlands (“Olmeca–Xicalanca”
of later traditions?) or highland natives proclaiming Maya not Teotihuacan connections.
Commerce as well as warfare is celebrated with unusual clarity (Cowgill 2000).

Adams (CHNPA intro): site also fortified, with elite-class linkages to the Gulf Coast,
perhaps Maya lowlands, and certainly [[xochicalco]] (García Cook 1986). Charlton lists
Cacaxtla among militarized hilltop centers outside the Basin in the epi-Teotihuacan
reordering.

## Evans & Webster 2001 (García Cook; Plunket & Uruñuela; Schortman & Urban)

Angel García Cook’s entry and the Puebla-Tlaxcala regional synthesis ([[evans-webster-archaeology-ancient-mexico-central-america-2001|Evans & Webster 2001]]) fix chronology, scale, and political claim:

- Fortified **Late Classic–Epiclassic** city (A.D. **600–900** by radiocarbon and cultural features), relatively small but with important murals; on a slope in the Puebla–Tlaxcala Valley with fertile surroundings and abundant water.
- Economy based on agriculture and commerce supporting ~**10,000** at peak; political control thought to be by civil and military chiefs rather than a theocratic organization.
- At least **nine moats** and a footed wall; construction of *tepetate* blocks, adobe, and stone with lime or mud plaster.
- Murals across construction phases: realistic battle scenes; agriculture/commerce (Red Temple); symbolism (Temple of Venus / Paired Scorpion); magico-religious themes (Building A). Humans denote Central Highlands groups (**jaguars**) and Maya (**birds**), perhaps linked to Tlaloc and Quetzalcoatl.
- García Cook: Cacaxtla **defeated Cholula — or contributed to its fall** — and was capital of the Puebla Valley during the Epiclassic (A.D. **650–900**); occupied by **Olmeca-Xicalanca** (described as triethnic Mixtecs, Chochopolocans, and Nahua); later destroyed by Tolteca-Chichimecs whose people migrated and helped Cholula’s resurgence; contacts with Maya area, Oaxaca, [[xochicalco|Xochicalco]], and the Basin of Mexico; contemporaneous with Xochicalco, Cantona’s second peak, and a Teotenango occupation phase.
- Plunket & Uruñuela: Olmeca-Xicalanca, an ethnic group with strong **Gulf Coast** ties, established capitals at Cacaxtla and Xochitécatl at the start of Texcalac as Teotihuacan power eroded.
- Schortman & Urban: “international” painting/architecture mixing Lowland Maya and Central Mexican elements; murals show physically distinct Central Mexican and Lowland Maya individuals.
- David Webster (Warfare entry): Central Mexican Epiclassic (A.D. **700–900**) centers exhibit strong warfare evidence — fortifications at Xochicalco and the murals of Cacaxtla.

## Why it mattered

Cacaxtla is the sharpest visual contrast to Teotihuacan’s impersonal, replicated human
figures (see [[teotihuacan-political-organization]]): individuality of pose and dress,
gruesome explicitness, and open celebration of conquest and trade. Together with Xochicalco,
its rise marks the end of Teotihuacan’s ability to inhibit rival monumental centers. EW2001
adds the fortified small-city demographics (~10k), multi-phase mural program, and Olmeca-Xicalanca
Gulf-linked political frame within [[puebla-tlaxcala]].
