## Geographic Description

Kanesh — known in Anatolian texts as **Nesa** and today as **Kültepe** (Turkish: "ash-mound") — lies in the fertile Kayseri Plain just south of the southern bend of the Kızıl Irmak (Marassantiya) river, in the region of modern Kayseri in central Anatolia. It was a Chalcolithic site in origin with a history of continuous occupation down to the Roman period. The site has two main components:

1. **Kültepe Höyük**: a 20-metre-high mound, approximately 500 metres in diameter, rising above the Kayseri Plain. This was the site of the local Anatolian settlement, dominated by the palace of its ruler.
2. **Kültepe Kārum (Kārum Kanesh)**: the Assyrian commercial centre at the foot of the mound on the northeast and southeast. It was "not inhabited exclusively by Assyrian traders; it also included many Anatolians among its inhabitants, as indicated by the names of house-owners with Anatolian names, like Peruwa, Galulu, Saktanuwa, Suppiahsu" (Bryce, *Kingdom of the Hittites*, p. 23).

## Occupation Sequence

Kanesh has four major occupation levels:

| Level | Period | Date (approx., middle chronology) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| IV | Early Bronze Age | Before c. 2300 BCE | Chalcolithic-origin settlement |
| III | Early Bronze Age | c. 2300–2100 BCE | Destroyed by fire (Early Bronze III phase) |
| II | Middle Bronze Age | c. 1975–1836 BCE | Main Assyrian colony phase; thousands of tablets; destroyed by fire |
| Ib | Middle Bronze Age | c. 1836–c. 1750 BCE | Second colony phase; only ~250 tablets; destroyed by fire |

Level II "lasted some 70 to 80 years, from relatively late in the reign of Erishum I until the end of the reign of Puzur-Ashur II, i.e. from some time in the last quarter of the twentieth century until the second half of the nineteenth century" (Bryce, p. 24). It ended in destruction by fire. After an unoccupied interval of perhaps 30 years, Level Ib followed. Level Ib extended "from the late nineteenth century to about the middle of the eighteenth century" before it too was destroyed by fire.

## Role as Hub of the Assyrian Colony Network

Kanesh/Nesa was "the focal point of the merchant operations" of the Assyrian merchant colonies (Bryce, p. 22). Its kārum was the administrative and distribution centre of the entire colony network in Anatolia. Twenty-one Assyrian settlements are attested in the merchant texts; only three have been located with certainty, of which Kanesh was by far the most important. Negotiations between local rulers and individual Assyrian colonies required referral to Kanesh as the superior administrative authority — as illustrated by the letter of kārum officials at Wahsusana: "The kārum at Kanesh is our superior. We shall write so that they may write either to you (directly) or to us" (KTP 14, 9–23).

By 1986, excavations (systematic since 1948 under Tahsin Özgüç) had recovered approximately 11,000 tablets. More recent estimates put the total at nearly **21,500 tablets** — the largest archive of Old Assyrian cuneiform documents known — though the great majority have yet to be studied in detail.

The Assyrian merchants imported tin (annukum) and woollen textiles to Anatolia and exported silver, gold, and copper. The colony period is synchronized chronologically through the Assyrian limum (eponym) date formula, which links kārum tablets to the Assyrian king list.

## Name and Language

The name **Nesa** gave rise to the term **Nesite** (nesili/nasili/nisili in the Hittite archives), the name the Hittites themselves used for the Indo-European language that became the official language of the Hittite kingdom. The Hittite archives contain texts identified by the term *nešili* — "(written) in the language of Nesa." This naming reflects the city's position as the primary centre of Indo-European settlement in central Anatolia during the colony period: "the great majority of names [in Assyrian texts found at Kanesh] are of Indo-European origin," and by this period Nesite was "probably becoming established as the Anatolian language used for written records and written communications" (Bryce, p. 15).

## Conquest by the Kussaran Dynasty

During the second colony phase (Level Ib), a dramatic political shift reshaped central Anatolia. Pithana, king of Kussara (probably in the anti-Taurus region southeast of the Kızıl Irmak), conquered Nesa "in the night by storm" and relocated his dynastic seat there. His son [[anitta|Anitta]] then used Nesa as the base for an empire-building campaign across the Kızıl Irmak basin and beyond — northward to Zalpa, and south to Purushanda.

Physical confirmation of [[anitta|Anitta's]] occupation came in 1954 with the discovery of an inscribed dagger in debris of a large building on the mound at Kültepe bearing the inscription: "the palace of Anitta, the King."

## Significance and Changing Role

Kanesh's significance evolved through three phases:
1. **Early Bronze Age**: a prosperous settlement and probable nucleus of a small kingdom; a king of Kanesh (Zipani) figures in tradition of a rebellion against Naram-Sin (c. 2254–2218 BCE).
2. **Middle Bronze Age (colony period)**: the administrative hub of the most sophisticated international commercial network in the ancient Near East at the time — "arguably one of the most enlightened [periods] in the history of the ancient Near East" (Bryce, p. 42).
3. **Post-Anitta**: with the collapse of the colony system (c. mid-18th century BCE), Kanesh ceased to be a major political centre. It was from the ruins of Anitta's short-lived empire that the [[hittites|Hittite kingdom]] eventually emerged, centred not at Nesa but at [[hattusha|Hattusa]].

## Historiography

Systematic excavation began in 1948 under Tahsin Özgüç; earlier tablets had been widely scattered in public and private collections. The site has yielded by far the richest archive of Old Assyrian cuneiform in existence. The chronology of levels II and Ib has been refined through prosopographical analysis of limum lists synchronized with the Assyrian king lists, but debates over absolute dating persist (the middle chronology used here is Bryce's choice; the low chronology would shift these dates by 60–64 years). The identification of Nesa with Kanesh/Kültepe is secure; the location of other colonies in Anitta's realm (including Kussara itself) remains unconfirmed.
