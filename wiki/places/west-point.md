# West Point

A fortified bend on the west bank of the **Hudson River**, ~50 miles north of New York City,
whose great chain and batteries commanded the river's strategic narrows during the
**[[american-revolution|American Revolution]]**. Holding the Hudson corridor was central to both
sides' strategy — the British repeatedly sought to sever New England from the other colonies along
it (the aim of [[burgoyne-john|Burgoyne]]'s 1777 [[battles-of-saratoga-1777|Saratoga]] campaign) —
and West Point was the linchpin of the American defenses.

Its most dramatic moment was a betrayal: in September 1780 its commander, **[[arnold-benedict|
Benedict Arnold]]**, conspired to surrender the fortress to the British; the plot's exposure made
his name a synonym for treason. After the war, with the army reduced to a handful of artillerists
"largely at West Point," the post became the home of the **United States Military Academy**
(founded 1802) — the institution whose modern *West Point History of Warfare* series, including
*[[west-point-history-american-revolution-2017|The West Point History of the American Revolution]]*,
supplies much of this wiki's military analysis. As the source notes, "before it was a Military
Academy, West Point was the most important fortress of the American Revolutionary War."
