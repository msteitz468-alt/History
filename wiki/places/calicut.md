# Calicut

A major port city on India's southwestern Malabar Coast, capital of the Zamorin ("Samudrin," lord of the sea) rulers, and one of the premier hubs of the medieval Indian Ocean spice trade — above all as a collection point for the pepper of the Malabar hinterland, exchanged there among Arab, Gujarati, Chinese, and later European merchants under a cosmopolitan and religiously tolerant commercial regime. The traveler Ibn Battuta described the city in the 14th century, and Ming admiral Zheng He's fleets called there during the early 15th-century Chinese maritime expeditions. Calicut's greatest historical significance came in May 1498, when Vasco da Gama's fleet made landfall nearby, becoming the first European ships to reach India by rounding the Cape of Good Hope — an event that opened the Portuguese Estado da Índia era and, with it, direct European maritime intrusion into the Indian Ocean trading world.
