# Japan (the Japanese Archipelago)

The regional anchor for the wiki's **Japan** coverage — the long-isolated island chain
off the East Asian mainland whose history is the central thread of Mason and Caiger's
[[mason-caiger-history-of-japan|*A History of Japan*]]. This page frames the geography
and the through-lines; the period pages ([[nara-period]], [[heian-period]], and the
medieval/early-modern/modern pages to follow) carry the narrative.

## Geographic description

A **string of islands** — the four largest being Honshū, Kyūshū, Shikoku, and Hokkaidō,
plus hundreds of smaller ones — arced over ~2,400 km off the coast of Korea and China.
It assumed its present island shape ~20,000 years ago when rising seas drowned the land
bridges to Asia. The terrain is overwhelmingly **mountainous**, with limited arable land
concentrated in valley bottoms and coastal plains; the summer monsoon brings the rain
that made **wet-rice agriculture** possible. Two facts shaped everything: Japan is an
**archipelago** (sea communication; regional autonomy), and it sits **just far enough
from the continent** to be secure from invasion yet close enough to borrow.

## The fundamental dynamic: isolation and deliberate borrowing

Mason and Caiger's organizing theme: **isolation gave Japan security to evolve a
distinctive civilization, and made its borrowing from abroad conscious and selective.**
Across its history Japan repeatedly absorbed continental (and later Western) models —
[[shinto|Shintō]] overlaid by [[japanese-buddhism|Buddhism]]; Yamato chieftainship
recast by Chinese imperial statecraft (the [[taika-reform-645|Taika Reform]]); Chinese
writing, the [[tang-dynasty|Tang]] capital plan, Confucian bureaucracy — and then
**domesticated** them into something Japanese. The same pattern recurs with the
nineteenth-century adoption of Western industry and institutions in the Meiji era.

## History of control and settlement (the long arc)

- **Prehistory** — [[early-agriculture-japan|Jōmon]] hunter-gatherer-potters (pottery
  before 10,000 BCE, among the world's oldest), then **Yayoi** wet-rice, metal, and
  social stratification (after ~500 BCE).
- **Archaic/Ancient** — the [[yamato-japan|Yamato]] kofun-building chieftainship
  coalesces (3rd–4th c. CE) and is transformed by Chinese models into a centralized
  imperial state ([[nara-period|Nara]], [[heian-period|Heian]]).
- **Medieval** — power passes to **warrior houses** (the Kamakura and Ashikaga
  shogunates) and fragments in the Warring States era. *(Cycle 2.)*
- **Early modern** — reunification and the **Tokugawa** peace, with the "closed country"
  (sakoku). *(Cycle 3.)*
- **Modern** — the **Meiji** transformation, imperial expansion, and the catastrophe of
  1945. *(Cycle 4.)*

A defining continuity is the **imperial institution** — claimed (uniquely) as a single
unbroken hereditary line descended from the sun goddess [[shinto|Amaterasu]] — which
survived even as actual power passed to regents, retired emperors, and shoguns: the
**sacred role of the emperor was never usurped**, only the secular government captured.

## Why it mattered / how its significance changed

From a prehistoric periphery of the [[tang-dynasty|Sinic]] world, Japan built an
independent high civilization, the only non-Western society to industrialize and become
a great power in the nineteenth century, and the Axis power whose defeat in 1945 reshaped
the Pacific. Its history is the wiki's primary case of **selective cultural borrowing**
within the [[east-asian-cultural-sphere|East Asian cultural sphere]].
