# The Roman Greek East (Greece and Asia Minor)

The Greek-speaking heart of the eastern empire — mainland **Greece**, the Aegean, **Crete** and
**Cyprus**, **western Asia Minor**, and the **Anatolian plateau** — organized by Rome through a
patchwork of provinces and client kings. Treated in [[bowman-cah-v10-1996|CAH X]] Ch. 14a
(**B. M. Levick**).

## Three regions, one Hellenism

Levick frames the East as a unity (Mediterranean geography, the Greek language, the legacy of
Alexander, the ideal of the **polis**) overlaying real diversity in **three regions**: (1)
mainland Greece and the islands — politically self-governing but in **economic decline and
depopulation**; (2) **western Asia Minor** (the provinces of Asia and Bithynia) — long
habituated to monarchy, urbanized by the Attalids, and economically rich (two-thirds of the
coining cities of Asia Minor lay in Asia); and (3) the **Anatolian plateau** — underdeveloped,
still tribal and village-based, "daunting and as yet unprofitable." Rome inherited the
**urbanization** of Alexander's successors; in Greece "there was little more to be done" but
preserve the poleis, the only viable instrument of control for an overstretched power.

## The triumviral devastation

The Greek East "suffered more in the thirteen years of intermittent civil war that followed
Caesar's death than in the swift campaigns that made him supreme." Three waves of extraction
fell on it: the **Liberators** (Brutus and Cassius robbing Lycia, Rhodes, Tarsus); the
**Parthian invasion** under Labienus (40–38, plundering Caria, Mylasa, Aphrodisias); and
**Antony**, demanding ten years' taxes and stripping the East to fight Parthia and then
Octavian. Octavian meanwhile built **clientelae** (citizenship for Seleucus of Rhosus; the
freedom and immunity won for "Caesarian" **Aphrodisias** through his freedman Zoilus).

## The Augustan restoration (27 BCE)

After Actium, Octavian made only **minor adjustments to Antony's system**, bringing Crete and
Cyprus under direct rule. The settlement of 27 distributed the provinces: **Achaea** (Greece,
detached from Macedonia, a senatorial province, seat at Corinth), **Crete-and-Cyrene**,
**Bithynia-Pontus**, and wealthy **Asia** — declared **consular**, one of the two "plum posts"
(with Africa) the Senate could offer ex-consuls. The Anatolian plateau Octavian, like Antony,
judged **not ready for direct rule** and left to a carefully chosen **mosaic of client kings**
(Amyntas of Galatia, Archelaus of Cappadocia, Polemo of Pontus, Deiotarus Philadelphus) — who
bore "the burden of defending and administering it," loyalty to Antony being forgivable if
transferred. These kingdoms were progressively **provincialized** as their rulers died (Galatia,
25 BCE; Cappadocia, AD 17) — see [[augustan-imperial-expansion]].

## The meaning of "freedom"

Levick stresses the ambiguity of the **"freedom"** granted to leagues (the Lycian federation,
admired by Strabo for its proportional representation; the Eleutherolacones), islands, and free
cities (Athens, Sparta, Delphi, Nicopolis, Mytilene). Theoretically exempt from the governor's
jurisdiction, they "still had to reckon with the emperor": Augustus intervened in Athens and
Sparta (through his partisan Eurycles), and **stripped Thessaly and Cyzicus of freedom** for
offences. The free cities learned the lesson and increasingly recognized imperial jurisdiction
(Cnidus, 6 BCE). The governors of the unarmed provinces spent much of their time on
**jurisdiction**, touring the *conventus* assize-centres.

## Significance

The Greek East was the empire's most thoroughly **urbanized and self-administering** region, run
at minimal cost through its own poleis and a shrinking ring of client dynasties whose marriages
Augustus wove into "a nexus of dynastic families." Its integration — through citizenship,
provincialization, and above all the **imperial cult** (the Asian *koina*, the temple at
Pergamum) — made it the cultural and economic counterweight to the Latin West, and the stage on
which the relationship between Rome and the Hellenic city found its lasting Imperial form.
