# Nan Madol

## Overview

**Nan Madol**, on **Pohnpei** in the eastern Caroline Islands, is the great monument of
**Micronesian** political complexity in [[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (McNiven, Ch 22): a
city of **ninety-three artificial islets** built on a fringing reef, the seat of an expanding
**chiefly architecture and centralised political control** legitimated by ancestors.

## Form and the Saudeleur

The islets were constructed between about **1,500 and 800 years ago**, with major use ceasing
~400 BP. Their **basalt-column** structures include walls up to **9 metres high**, elite
ancestral **tombs**, and the residences of chiefs and their specialist entourages — associated
with the rise of the **Saudeleur** dynasty that unified Pohnpei. Nan Madol is the most
spectacular of a family of Micronesian monumental traditions that also includes the **latte**
stone-pillar structures of the **Marianas** (~1000–400 BP) and the terracing of **Palau**.

## Micronesian Exchange: Sawei and Stone Money

Micronesia's islands were bound by **maritime exchange networks** run by master navigators.
The paramount chief of **Yap's** Gagil district controlled the **sawei** system — the largest
historically known exchange network in Micronesia — linking fifteen atoll communities across
**1,300 km** of ocean through reciprocal tribute and ceremonial goods. A separate network
carried limestone discs ("**stone money**") quarried in **Palau** over 300 km to Yap.
Intensified movement of Yapese pottery ~600 BP may mark the system's beginnings.

## Significance

Nan Madol shows that **monumental, centralised chiefly polities arose independently in
Micronesia**, as in Polynesia — built by master mariners and sustained by inter-island
exchange. With the [[polynesian-chiefdoms|Polynesian paramountcies]] it rounds out CWH IV's
picture of Oceania as a world of **seaborne connection and indigenous political complexity**.
