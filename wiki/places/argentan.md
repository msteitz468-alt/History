# Argentan

A town in Normandy, France, that together with Falaise gave its name to the "Falaise-Argentan pocket" (or Falaise Gap), the encirclement in which Allied forces trapped much of the German Seventh Army and Fifth Panzer Army in August 1944 during the Normandy campaign. American forces advancing from the south under Patton's Third Army approached Argentan while Canadian and Polish forces pushed south from Falaise, but a gap between the converging Allied spearheads — left open for several days over disputed operational reasons — allowed a substantial number of German troops to escape eastward before it was finally closed.

## Significance

The delay in closing the Argentan–Falaise gap remains a debated point in the historiography of the Normandy campaign: critics have argued it allowed thousands of German troops and considerable equipment to escape encirclement, prolonging German resistance in the subsequent retreat across France, while defenders of the Allied commanders involved point to genuine operational and boundary-coordination difficulties between American and Canadian/Polish forces converging from different directions.
