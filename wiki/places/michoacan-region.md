# Michoacán Region

## Overview

The **Michoacán region** is the west-central Mexican highland and riverine zone whose Late Postclassic political expression was the [[purepecha|Purépecha (Tarascan)]] empire — second-largest state in Mesoamerica after the [[aztec-empire|Aztec]], ruling **>75,000 km²** between the Lerma–Santiago and Balsas basins in 1522 (Pollard, EW2001 “Tarascan Culture and Religion”; “Michoacán Region”). At Spanish contact, modern Michoacán plus adjacent Guanajuato, Jalisco, and Guerrero sectors were under Tarascan control; the distinctive tradition persists as modern Tarascan/**Purépecha** culture.

Pollard critiques two marginalizing historiographies: (1) the Tarascan Empire as a delayed secondary-state replica of the Basin of Mexico; (2) Tarascans as quasi-Mesoamerican “barbarians” copying Aztecs after Chichimec conquest of Chupícuaro-like villages. Available evidence shows a **long interactive prehistory**; chronometric long-term regional sequences remain insufficient (Pollard, EW2001).

## Four geographic zones of the Tarascan domain

Pollard’s synthesis (EW2001): the domain spanned lands between Mexico’s two great river systems:

1. **Central Plateau lake basins** (e.g. Pátzcuaro, Zacapu) above ~2,000 m — political and demographic core.
2. **Semitropical plateau rims** — obsidian at Zinapécuaro–Ucareo (NE) and Zináparo (NW); thermal springs; silver/gold NE; precious metals.
3. **Balsas Depression** (<500 m) — copper, gold, silver, iron pyrites, lead; marble.
4. **Southern Sierra Madre** — copper/gold/silver deposits near Huetamo and Coalcomán.

|---|---|
| Paleoindian / Lithic (before 2500 B.C.) | Fluted points with Pleistocene mammoth/bison; best series from Lake Chapala area |
| Archaic | Nearly unknown except preceramic 2500–2200 B.C. at Los Portales Cave |
| Early Formative | **El Opeño** shaft tombs 1500–800 B.C. (Santiago–Lerma interaction with Jalisco/Nayarit); El Infiernillo Balsas villages link Capacha/Colima coast; Lake Pátzcuaro pollen cores show **domesticated maize ~1500 B.C.** |
| Late Formative | Regional cultures: **Chupícuaro** (northern/central lacustrine), Chumbícuaro (Tepalcatepec SW), Balsas/Mezcala (central Balsas fringe) |
| Classic A.D. 400–900 | Ceremonial centers (El Otero near Jiquilpan; Tres Cerritos on Lake Cuitzeo; Tingambato near Pátzcuaro; possibly Queréndaro/Zinapécuaro) — monumental architecture, elaborate tombs; open debate on local vs Teotihuacan-related development (see [[purepecha]]) |
| After A.D. 900 | Settlement nucleates at defensible sites; Red-on-Cream ceramics spread; **metallurgy** appears in Michoacán after A.D. 900 (possibly earlier in Nayarit/Jalisco/lower Balsas) — copper and copper-silver cold hammering, annealing, lost-wax of Ecuadorian styles via coastal canoe trade (Hosler via Pollard) |
| By A.D. 1200 | Regional cultures share traits later labeled Tarascan: complex metallurgy, pipes, negative polychrome, rubble-filled hillside mounds, Curicaueri petroglyphs |
| 14th–16th c. | Tariácuri unites Pátzcuaro Basin; ~1440 tributary-state institutionalization; Protohistoric kingdom A.D. 1450–1530 |

## Adjacent Bajío interaction

The fertile Bajío plain (~1,600 m; central Guanajuato + parts of Jalisco, Querétaro, Michoacán) shows Formative occupation including hierarchical Chupícuaro; A.D. 400–900 population growth, hilltop/terrace settlements, confederation with *blanco levantado* pottery and Teotihuacan-style objects indicating a Teotihuacan-related minority; agriculturalist decline ~900–1350 with defensive sites (La Gloria, Peralta); Toltec-related ceramics; late 1300s–early 1400s repopulation under Tarascan-era geopolitics (EW2001 “Bajío Region”). **Acámbaro–Cerro el Chivo** on the Lerma frontier became a Tarascan administrative/military outpost (Acambaro phase 1450–1520) defending against Aztec and Chichimec pressure (Gorenstein, EW2001).

## Sites and research focus

Most Tarascan-state research centers on capital [[tzintzuntzan|Tzintzuntzan]] and **Ihuatzio** (INAH excavations from Caso/Noguera 1930 through Cabrera Castro 1978–79 and Cárdenas 1992–94; Pollard 1970 intensive surface survey of urban Tzintzuntzan). Lake Pátzcuaro Basin Protohistoric system included **91 settlements** at Tarascan apex (Gorenstein & Pollard). Other studied large sites: Urichu, El Palacio, Milpillas, **Huandacareo** (Lake Cuitzeo corridor; elite tombs; among few sites with metal), Tres Cerritos, Zinapécuaro, Acámbaro (Pollard, EW2001).

## Why it mattered

Michoacán is the ecological and political stage on which West Mexico’s most centralized Late Postclassic state formed — controlling metal-rich Balsas and Sierra zones that the Aztecs never fully seized, and anchoring the dual-empire map of Conquest-era Mexico. Its deep Formative roots (El Opeño, early maize, Chupícuaro) undermine models that invent the Tarascan state only as a 14th-century Chichimec import.

## Historiography

Helen Perlstein Pollard’s EW2001 “Michoacán Region” and “Tarascan Culture and Religion” entries, plus her monographic work and Gorenstein collaborations, are the archaeological–ethnohistorical core. *Relación de Michoacán* (1541) remains the principal narrative source for legendary expansion. CHNPA Gorenstein and Van Young (already on [[purepecha]] / [[tzintzuntzan]]) supply comparative state typology and colonial–modern trajectory. Standing gap: insufficient long-term radiocarbon sequences across the four ecological zones.
