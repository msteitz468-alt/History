# Lavinium

An ancient Latin coastal city, traditionally regarded as the founding site
of the Latin people and, through Roman legend, an ancestral point of origin
for Rome itself.

## Geographic and Historical Description

Roman foundation legend held that the Trojan exile Aeneas founded Lavinium
after his arrival in Italy, naming it for his wife Lavinia, and that his son
founded Alba Longa, the mother city from which Romulus and Remus's Rome was
later said to descend. Archaeological finds at the site (including an
important sanctuary of thirteen altars) confirm Lavinium's genuine
importance as an early cult center within Latium, independent of the later
literary legend built around it. Roman magistrates continued to perform
sacrifices at Lavinium into the historical period, reflecting the city's
retained religious significance as a symbolic point of origin even after
its political importance had faded relative to Rome.

## Significance

Lavinium functioned less as a political or military power than as a
religious and mythic anchor for Latin, and later Roman, identity — a role
that persisted long after its practical political relevance had been
eclipsed by Rome and the wider Latin League.
