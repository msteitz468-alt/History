# Bavaria (Merovingian-era Duchy)

**The southeastern duchy of the Frankish world**, ruled by the **Agilolfing** dukes, which retained
its autonomy longer than any other peripheral territory — into the reign of Charlemagne — and whose
distinctively indigenous Christianity set it apart from the other Germanic regions.

## A frontier duchy

Bavaria lay "at the crucial intersection of the Frankish, Langobard, Slavic and Avar worlds," and its
autonomy waxed and waned with the strength of its neighbours — expanding east when Samo's Slavic kingdom
weakened (c. 660), retreating under Langobard and Avar pressure. The Agilolfings based their power partly
on surviving **late-antique fiscal lands and Roman administration**, establishing their court at
**Regensburg** in the city's former Roman *praetorium*.

## Indigenous Christianity and the papal alliance

"Unlike other Germanic regions, the conversion of Bavaria was not entirely an imported phenomenon but
rather had indigenous roots": Salzburg provided continuity with late-antique Christianity, and ancient
ties with northern Italy (especially Verona) predated the Frankish conquest. **Bishop Rupert of Worms**,
exiled from the Merovingian court c. 694, found refuge with **Duke Theodo** at Regensburg and was granted
a see at Salzburg — Bavaria serving as a refuge for Pippinid opponents. In **716** Theodo visited Rome and
sought Pope Gregory II's help to organise a regular hierarchy, preferring a **Bavarian-papal alliance** to
reliance on the Iro-Frankish/Austrasian church he feared as a Frankish fifth column — a move that
prefigured the alliances of Eudo of Aquitaine and [[charles-martel|Charles Martel]] (see
[[christianisation-of-the-frankish-aristocracy]]).

## Why it mattered

Bavaria is Geary's clearest case of a peripheral *gens* building durable autonomy on late-Roman
foundations and an independent church. Its survival into Charlemagne's reign — owed to distance from
Carolingian power, the Agilolfing alliance network, and Carolingian distraction elsewhere — makes it the
last of the semi-independent post-Roman duchies to fall.
