# Nuenen

Village in North Brabant where Vincent van Gogh lived with his parents 1883–1885 and produced his first major body of work as a dedicated artist.

## Description and Historical Role

Nuenen was a weaving village with a large weaver population living in poverty. The Van Gogh parsonage there became Vincent's base after failures in The Hague and Drenthe. He drew and painted the weavers, their cottages, the churchyard, and the surrounding landscape in a dark, earthy palette.

## Significance

In Nuenen Vincent executed the large figure painting *The Potato Eaters* (1885), his first ambitious statement of solidarity with peasant life. The period saw intense family tensions (especially with his father and sister), his relationship with the pregnant Sien earlier having ended, and his first systematic study of color theory and drawing from life. He left Nuenen after his father's death and conflicts with the Catholic priest over his liaison with a local woman. The Nuenen works mark the end of his Dutch "peasant painter" phase and the transition toward brighter color in Antwerp and Paris.
