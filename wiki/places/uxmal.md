# Uxmal

## Geographic description

**Uxmal** (“Thrice Built” / “Place of Three Harvests” in common etymologies) is the largest **Puuc** regional center of the Terminal Classic (**A.D. 800–1000**). It sits south of the Puuc Hills in a rich-soil basin with five clay-filled *aguadas*. Site extent is provisionally **≥10 km²** (no complete settlement survey). An urban-core wall encloses ~**16.5 ha** of monumental Puuc-style architecture. The layout defies natural topography. Initial occupation is Middle Formative (**600–300 B.C.**), poorly investigated.

## Historical significance

### Classic Puuc architecture (Evans & Webster 2001)

**Classic Puuc architecture** (**A.D. 800–950**; Early Puuc roots **700–800** at Edzna, Oxkintok, Xcalumkin) at Uxmal, Labna, Sayil, and Kabah combines **concrete-core veneer masonry** with standardized **mosaic façade** elements — often rated the finest of all ancient Mesoamerican architectural traditions. Hallmarks: core/veneer masonry, corbeled vaults, geometric/mosaic façades. Uxmal was walled; fortifications spread with Puuc influence after **A.D. 800** (Chacchob, Muna, Chunchucmil, Ake, Cuca). The western **Cehpech** slateware sphere and specialized *chultun* cisterns for low-rainfall storage define the regional economy.

Major monuments include the **Pyramid of the Magician** (elliptical, >**30 m**, five superimpositions; Temple I Tlaloc mask with Mexican year sign — controversial late-6th-c. ¹⁴C; Temple IV Chenes-style earth-monster doorway) and the **Nunnery Quadrangle** (four decorated range structures). The last great Uxmal ruler **Lord Chac** commissioned the **Palace of the Governor** shortly after **A.D. 900** (also named at Kabah and Chichén).

### Decline

Site failures and abandonments run from shortly after **A.D. 900** through the **twelfth century**. Competition and warfare are implicated, but underlying causes remain debated. Chronology intersects debates over contemporaneity of Puuc florescence with early [[chichen-itza|Chichén Itzá]] (see Chichén page for Hammond/Evans chronology positions).

## Why it mattered

Uxmal anchors the **northern Terminal Classic alternative** to southern lowland collapse: peak Puuc urbanism, water-management specialization (*chultunes*), and elite architecture at the moment southern dynasties failed. It is essential for reading northern succession toward Chichén and [[mayapan|Mayapan]].
