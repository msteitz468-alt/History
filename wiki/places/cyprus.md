# Cyprus (Middle Bronze Age)

The great copper island of the eastern Mediterranean — in the **Middle Cypriot (MC)
period (~1850–1550 BCE)** a still-isolated, tribally-organized society that, by the
end of the age, broke out of its insularity to become a **hinge of Levantine trade**
and, later, a Late Bronze great-power supplier of copper. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (Ch. IV(c), Catling).

## An island in transition

The transition from Early to Middle Cypriot is **gradual** — no destruction or
rupture, just the slow evolution of the **White Painted** pottery sequence. For most
of the period Cyprus is materially **disappointing**: its art and metalwork
stagnate, repeating Early Bronze types, because — in Catling's words — "the *Leitmotiv*
throughout the history of Cyprus is its dependence on foreign sources for the
reinfusion of vitality," and the island had been too long without outside contact.
**Seal usage was unknown** — a telling symptom of its undeveloped, isolated state.

## Unrest and the move east

Middle Cypriot settlement shows **tribal organization and conspicuous insecurity**:
a striking number of inland **fortresses** (Krini, Nitovikla, Ayios Sozomenos) guard
against danger **from within the island**, not from the sea, suggesting serious
internal unrest, climaxing in MC III with the **violent destruction of Nitovikla**.
Across the period the centres of population **shifted east** — away from the Kyrenia
hills toward the Mesaoria plain and, decisively, to the **east and south-east
coast**, where the future great trading ports — **Enkomi**, Hala Sultan Tekke, Pyla,
Klavdhia — were founded (Enkomi probably by the old inland capital **Kalopsidha**) to
handle the coming maritime trade.

## Cyprus and her neighbours — and "Alashiya"

Contact rose from a trickle to a flood: little foreign trade in MC I, then in MC II–III
a **two-way exchange** with **Syria, Palestine ([[alalakh|the Levant]], Megiddo, Tell
el-'Ajjul) and Egypt**, reaching Cilicia and inland to Khabur-ware country, with
some persistent **Minoan** contact (a Kamares cup at Karmi). The **Mari letters**
record copper from **"Alashiya"** — widely but not certainly identified with Cyprus;
if correct, it makes Cyprus the **copper-supplier of the Bronze Age Near East**. Its
MC III commitment to the **Asiatic** markets makes all the more striking its later
**switch of allegiance to the [[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean]] Aegean** in the
later fifteenth century, the threshold of its Late Bronze prosperity.

## Why this place mattered

Cyprus is the **copper hinge** of the eastern Mediterranean — the island whose ore
(the metal whose very name, *cuprum*, is "Cypriot") fed the Bronze Age, and whose
Middle Cypriot port-foundations set up the Late Bronze trading boom. Its possible
identity with **Alashiya** would place it squarely in the diplomatic correspondence
of the great powers.
