# Constantinople

**Constantinople** — refounded by [[actors/constantine-the-great|Constantine the Great]]
on the site of Greek Byzantium and dedicated in **330 CE** — was the capital of the
eastern Roman, then [[actors/byzantine-empire|Byzantine]], empire for more than eleven
centuries. Sited on the high road linking west and east, on a defensible promontory
commanding the Bosporus between the Mediterranean and Black Sea worlds, it became the
greatest city of late-antique and medieval Christendom.

## Geographic and Strategic Position

Constantine chose the location for its commanding access to "the lands of the march"
and its natural defenses. By the fourth century the city's population had grown
**ten-fold in under a century, from c. 30,000 to c. 300,000**, sustained by grain
fleets redirected from [[places/alexandria|Alexandria]] — in Justinian's early reign,
8,000,000 *artabai* (some 310 million liters) of Egyptian wheat arrived annually. A bread
dole established under Constantine fed 80,000 citizens, and the threat of urban riot made
the smooth arrival of grain a permanent imperative of statecraft. The city was a
**multilingual hub**: in its streets one heard Syriac and Aramaic, Coptic and Ethiopic,
Gothic and Hunnic, Persian and Arabic, alongside Latin and Greek.

## Monumental Building

[[actors/justinian|Justinian]]'s reign (527–565) saw "the grandest building spree in the
annals of Roman history," crowned by the rebuilt **Hagia Sophia** after the Nika riots —
its dome the largest of the ancient world. [[actors/procopius|Procopius]] catalogued these
works in his *Buildings*.

## Disease, Siege, and the Late-Antique Crisis

Constantinople's dense connectivity made it a magnet for catastrophe as well as wealth:

- In **447** an earthquake felled 57 towers of the land walls and left the capital
  exposed to **Attila's Huns**; according to Harper it was "the ramparts of the local
  disease pool" — endemic infection striking the besiegers — that repelled them.
- In **542** the **[[events/justinianic-plague-541|Justinianic Plague]]** reached the city
  from Pelusium via Alexandria, killing on a scale that Procopius compared to the
  emptying of the world; [[actors/justinian|Justinian]] himself contracted and survived
  bubonic plague.
- In **618**, during the Persian wars, the centuries-old state grain shipments from Egypt
  **ended forever** — "the spine of imperial connectivity was snapped" — a structural
  turning point in the contraction of the empire into a Byzantine rump state.
