# Sardis

**Sardis** was the capital of [[lydia|Lydia]] — the acropolis-city at the foot of Mt
Tmolus in the Hermus valley from which the **Mermnad kings** (Gyges to Croesus) ruled
western Anatolia, the **mint where coinage was born**, and the prize that
[[achaemenid-empire|Cyrus]] seized in 546 to make Anatolia Persian.
[[boardman-cah-v3p2-1991|CAH Vol. III, Part 2]] (Ch. 34a, Mellink) treats it.

## Geographic description

Unlike the compact prehistoric mounds of Gordium or Old Smyrna, Sardis was an **acropolis
town** — a natural citadel on the northern slope of Tmolus dominating a lower city and the
plain, commanding the **gold-bearing Pactolus** stream. Deep soundings reach Early Iron Age
and Late Bronze Age levels; from the start of the Iron Age, **Greek (Protogeometric and
Geometric) pottery is constantly mixed** with the local wares — the mark of Sardis's
exceptional accessibility to the Aeolic and Ionian coast, which set Lydia apart from inland
[[phrygians|Phrygia]].

## The royal city

Sardis was the seat of the [[lydia|Mermnad dynasty]] from **Gyges** (c. 685). Its lower
city was **captured and looted by the [[cimmerians|Cimmerians]]** under Ardys before
Alyattes finally expelled them. Under **Alyattes** the city's mint **struck the first
electrum [[coinage|coins]]**, and the kings amassed the wealth — the gold of the Pactolus
and Anatolian silver — that made **Croesus** proverbial. The great **Bintepe** ("thousand
mounds") necropolis north of the Hermus holds the royal **tumuli**, including the giant
mounds identified as those of **Gyges** (Karnıyarık Tepe, its krepis carved with monograms
read as *Gugu*) and **Alyattes** (a 60-m mound with a fine marble burial chamber) — Lydian
**built stone chambers with doors and dromoi**, a technically advanced variant of the
doorless Phrygian timber graves.

## Why Sardis mattered

- **Birthplace of coinage** — the Sardis mint's electrum issues under Alyattes are the
  start of the monetary economy that spread through the Greek world and beyond
  ([[coinage]]); Starr (CAH III.3) stresses these were struck for **state purposes**
  (later: navies, mercenaries, tolls, prestige) rather than retail trade, and that
  coinage "was not a potent economic force until well after 600."
- **The Lydian capital** — the centre from which Lydia held hegemony over western Anatolia
  and pressed the Ionian Greeks; a meeting-point of Anatolian and Greek art (Greek
  iconographic motifs appear in its architectural terracottas).
- **The hinge of 546** — its **fall to [[achaemenid-empire|Cyrus]]** ended native Anatolian
  independence and opened the Ionian Greeks to Persia, the prelude to the Greco-Persian wars.

## Sardis under Persia (CAH IV)

[[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 3e, Mellink) makes Sardis "the key site for
Western Anatolia" under Persian rule — the satrapal capital from which the
[[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenids]] governed the **second (Sardis) satrapy** and inherited
the whole Lydian apparatus of roads, garrisons and tax-collection.

- **Cyrus' conquest, archaeologically attested.** Excavation has found the **siege-mound**
  with which Cyrus breached the lower city's mud-brick rampart in **547/6**, the Lydian
  ceramics sealed beneath it, and the **stone wall** of the early Persian repair on top.
- **The capital of the satrapy.** Persia "wisely continued to rule from Sardis," keeping
  the Lydian road-network, repairing the terraced acropolis, and drawing on **Lydian
  stone-cutters and wood-carvers** (named on Darius' Susa foundation-charters, and
  detectable at Pasargadae) — so that "the art of the Persian empire owed a basic debt to
  the cultural satrapy centred on Sardis." Its satraps included **Oroetes** (who murdered
  the satrap of Dascylium in 522 and was executed by Darius) and Darius' half-brother
  **Artaphernes**.
- **The Ionian burning of 499.** In the [[ionian-revolt|Ionian Revolt]] the Ionians and
  their allies **burned the lower city of Sardis** (and the shrine of Cybebe), though
  Artaphernes held the fortified acropolis; the Persians then crushed the rebels at
  Ephesus. Burnt strata of 499 have been tentatively identified along the Pactolus.
- **Minting continues.** Darius **followed Croesus' lead**, striking *croesids* for a
  time and then **gold darics and silver sigloi at Sardis before 500** (the early types
  showing the king with the bow) — the mint of the first coins now serving the Great King.

The tombs of Sardis show **Lydian burial customs unchanged under Persia** (Attic and
Corinthian pottery, Achaemenid-style gold jewellery and cylinder-seals, locally produced
Lydian-inscribed pyramidal stamp-seals) — the material face of the Lydo-Persian
[[greco-persian-art|cultural koine]].

## Changing significance

Sardis passed from **Lydian royal capital** to **Persian satrapal seat** (the western
terminus of the Royal Road), then to Hellenistic and Roman provincial city — but its
Archaic moment as the mint of the first coins is its lasting world-historical claim.
