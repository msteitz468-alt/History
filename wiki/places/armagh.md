# Armagh

Ireland's **primatial church**, the seat of the *comarbae Pátraic* (successor of Patrick),
and the ecclesiastical anchor of the [[actors/ui-neill|Uí Néill]] ascendancy. By the tenth
century its abbot "operated different writs … In effect, he was regarded as another
provincial king," able to impose **Patrick's tribute** across Ireland; two tenth-century
abbots simultaneously headed the Columban familia, making them lords of monastery networks
in both Ireland and Scotland.

Armagh's inner oval enclosure measured 250 × 200 metres within a massive outer enclosure —
one of the proto-urban "ecclesiastical states" (with Kells and Clonmacnoise) that were
"powerhouses of economic and political power." From 965 to 1134 its office was dominated by
the **Uí Sínaich** family. In **1005** **[[actors/brian-boru|Brian Bóruma]]** visited and
recognised Armagh's rights "on behalf of future kings of Cashel," a concession recorded in
the **Book of Armagh** alongside Brian's title *Imperator Scotorum* — sealing the alliance
between Munster's new dynasty and Ireland's greatest church. Armagh's primacy was formalised
against Canterbury's claims at the councils of **Cashel (1101)** and **Ráith Bressail
(1111)**.
