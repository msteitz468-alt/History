# Saint-Rémy-de-Provence

Site of the asylum of Saint-Paul-de-Mausole where Vincent van Gogh was voluntarily committed May 1889 – May 1890 and painted many of his most famous late works.

## Description and Historical Role

The former monastery of Saint-Paul, converted to a private asylum, lay in a valley outside the Roman town of Saint-Rémy. Vincent had a small room and was allowed to paint in the walled garden and, with supervision, in the surrounding countryside (often accompanied by an attendant).

## Significance

During the year at Saint-Rémy Vincent produced the *Irises*, multiple *Starry Night* variants, *The Olive Trees*, *Cypresses*, *Wheatfield with Cypresses*, self-portraits, and copies after Rembrandt, Delacroix, and others. Despite periods of mental crisis (including eating paint and hallucinations), he worked with extraordinary discipline. The enclosed garden and swirling skies became signature motifs. He left for Auvers in May 1890 at his own request, seeking greater independence and proximity to Theo.
