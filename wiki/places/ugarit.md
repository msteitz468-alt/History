# Ugarit (Ras Shamra)

The great **cosmopolitan port of the north Syrian coast** — modern Ras Shamra — the
richest single archive-city of the Late Bronze [[canaanite-civilization|Canaanite
world]]. "More is known about Ugarit during the two centuries before her downfall...
than about any other Syrian city of the second millennium" — two-thirds of the site
excavated, and thousands of tablets recovered. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (Ch. X) and at length in
[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]] (Ch. XXI(b), Drower).

## A commercial crossroads

A natural harbour, a commanding position, and above all the **nearness of Cyprus**
gave Ugarit an exceptional commercial advantage. Its palaces, temples, and merchant
houses reveal a wealthy trading city whose population was strikingly **mixed**: a
Semitic [[canaanite-civilization|Canaanite]] majority, a strong **[[hurrians|
Hurrian]]** element (Hurrian texts in the local script, Akkadian–Hurrian bilinguals,
scribal glossaries), and resident minorities of **Cypriots, Rhodians, and Cretans**
trading from abroad. Already a place of note in the [[mari|Mari]] age (its king asked
to visit Zimri-Lim's palace), it reached the **peak of its prosperity in the 14th–13th
centuries**, the period of its great palace archives — material that belongs mostly
to the next volume (CAH II.2).

## Politics

Ugarit was a kingdom in its own right, commanding lesser towns, and it stood in the
orbit of the great powers — within reach of **[[mitanni|Mitanni]]** in the 15th
century, later a vassal of the **[[hittites|Hittites]]**. It lay just south of
**Mukish/[[alalakh|Alalakh]]** and traded with Egypt, Cyprus (Alashiya), and the
Aegean.

## The zenith (CAH II.2)

The second volume fills in the city at its height. Ugarit's wealth rested on
**middleman trade** (the natural link between the Aegean and the Levant, within
sailing distance of Cyprus and Cilicia) and on **industry** — bronze-work,
murex-dyed purple and violet textiles, olive oil, wine, salt and timber, exported in
its own shipping (some vessels of 200 tons). The **royal palace** grew to ~2.5 acres
and ninety-odd rooms, "one of the largest in western Asia," with archive-rooms whose
tablets were baked in a courtyard kiln, an ivory-carvers' workshop, and a piped water
supply; great private houses (Rapʾanu's had 34 ground-floor rooms with its own
library) and corbel-vaulted family vaults filled the residential quarters. The
population was strikingly **mixed** — a Canaanite majority, a large Hurrian minority,
and resident Cypriots, Cretans, Mycenaeans, Hittites and Egyptians.

## A Hittite vassal

Long "on the water of Egypt," Ugarit was forced by [[shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma
I's]] advance to switch sides: king **Niqmaddu II** accepted a Hittite vassal treaty
at [[alalakh|Alalakh]], paying heavy tribute but keeping his ports and even gaining
territory from Mukish. Thereafter Ugarit remained (mostly) loyal through a long line
of kings — Ar-Ḫalba, **Niqmepa** (punished with the loss of Siyannu and Ushnatu after
a revolt, reducing the kingdom by a third), Ammishtamru II, Ibiranu, down to the last
king **ʿAmmurapi** — its destiny arbitrated from the Hittite viceroyalty of
**[[unknown|Carchemish]]** (the dignified Ini-Teshub). The archives preserve a
remarkable run of **royal divorce cases** among the intermarried houses of Ugarit and
Amurru, adjudicated by the Hittite Great Kings themselves.

## Destruction

The end is documented almost to the day. The final palace **kiln was found packed
with unbaked tablets** — letters never filed. They record famine, the king's fleet
away "in the Lukka lands" and his troops "in the land of the Hittites," and a
desperate plea from [[shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma II]] for ships and grain — and
ʿAmmurapi's reply that "the enemy has plundered my coasts." This is the approach of
the **[[unknown|Sea Peoples]]**, whose path through Qode, Hatti, Carchemish, Alašiya
and Amurru Ramesses III records at Medinet Habu. The city was burned and **never
rebuilt**: "the alphabetic script of Ugarit was forgotten... Ugarit's history was
ended" (~1185 BCE). The excavator Schaeffer additionally suspected a final
earthquake; the precise mechanism is debated, but the destruction belongs squarely
to the **[[unknown|Bronze Age Collapse]]**.

## Why this place mattered

Ugarit is the **documentary heart of Canaanite civilization** and of Late Bronze
international trade. Its archives (in Akkadian, Hurrian, Hittite and the local
**Ugaritic alphabetic cuneiform**) preserve the richest body of
**[[ugaritic-religion|Canaanite myth and religion]]** known — the Baal Cycle, El and
the divine assembly, the Keret and Aqhat epics — the West-Semitic background against
which the Hebrew Bible's literature is read. Its sudden, total destruction makes it
the type-site of the **Bronze Age Collapse**.
