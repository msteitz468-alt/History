# Thebes (Waset)

The great city of Upper Egypt — Egyptian **Waset**, the Greeks' Thebes, modern
**Luxor/Karnak** — religious and royal capital of [[new-kingdom-egypt|New Kingdom
Egypt]] and home of the god **[[amun-priesthood|Amun-Re]]**. Treated across
[[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (Chs. II, VIII, IX).

## The Theban kingdoms

Thebes was the seat of the **Eleventh Dynasty** that first reunified Egypt (the
[[reunification-of-egypt-2040bce|Theban reunification]] under
[[mentuhotpe-ii|Mentuhotpe II]]) and the home town of the [[middle-kingdom-egypt|
Twelfth Dynasty]], which raised its local god **Amun** to national stature. In the
[[second-intermediate-period|Second Intermediate Period]] it became "the principal
rallying-point of the native rulers" — the impoverished but indomitable **Seventeenth
Dynasty** (Inyotef, Sobkemsaf, Seqenenre Tao II, Kamose) that launched the war
against the [[hyksos|Hyksos]], cut off from the timber, fine stone, and gold of the
wider world and "forced to make the best of local materials" (small mud-brick
pyramids, "feathered" *rishi* coffins).

## The imperial capital

With the founding of the [[new-kingdom-egypt|Eighteenth Dynasty]] by
[[ahmose-i|Ahmose I]], Thebes became the **religious capital of the empire**. On the
east bank rose the vast temple of **Amun at Karnak**, enriched by the spoils of
conquest into the wealthiest sanctuary in Egypt (see [[amun-priesthood]] and
[[temple-economy]]); the **Luxor** temple followed under [[amenhotep-iii|Amenophis
III]]. On the west bank lay the royal mortuary landscape: the **Valley of the Kings**,
the terraced temple of [[hatshepsut|Hatshepsut]] at **Deir el-Bahri**, the colossal
"Memnon" statues of Amenophis III, his palace at **Malkata**, and the artisans'
village of **Deir el-Medina**. Though the political administration often centred on
Memphis, Thebes was the ideological and ceremonial heart of imperial Egypt.

## Why this place mattered

Thebes is the **engine-room of Egyptian reunification and empire**: twice (in the
First and Second Intermediate Periods) the Theban house pulled a fragmented Egypt back
together, and in the New Kingdom its god, its priesthood, and its kings made it the
spiritual capital of the Late Bronze Age's greatest power. Its temples and tombs are
also the single richest source of New Kingdom history.
