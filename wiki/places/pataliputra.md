# Pataliputra

## Overview

**Pataliputra** (modern **Patna**, on the Ganga in Bihar) was the **first imperial city of
South Asia** — capital of the Nandas, the **[[mauryan-empire|Mauryas]]**, and later the
**[[gupta-empire|Guptas]]** and Palas. It is the **regional case study** for South Asia in
[[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (Kaul, Ch 19), chosen to show how a city's fortunes were
"inextricably linked to the political destiny of the kingdom to which it belonged."

## Rise

First referenced in the Buddhist **Tripitaka** as the village of **Patali**, it was fortified
c. fifth century BCE by the Magadhan king **Ajatashatru** as a base against the Vrijjis; his
successor **Udayin** then shifted the royal capital there from hill-girt **Rajagriha**. Its
position at the **confluence of the Ganga and Son** — commanding river trade and troop
movement, near the iron and forests of the Chhotanagpur plateau — underwrote Magadha's rise.
From it the **Nandas** built their feared empire and **[[chandragupta-maurya|Chandragupta]]**
(c. 321 BCE) launched the Mauryan state.

## The Imperial Metropolis

Pataliputra is described first-hand by **Megasthenes**, the Seleucid ambassador to
Chandragupta's court, in his **Indica** (surviving only in quotation). He reports a city laid
out as a **parallelogram** along the Ganga, girded by a **wooden wall** "crowned with 570
towers and pierced with 64 gates" — making it one of the largest cities of the ancient world.
Archaeology at Patna (**Bulandibagh**, where a wooden palisade of 24-foot uprights survives;
**Kumrahar**, with a great **pillared hall**) corroborates the timber ramparts and a Mauryan
columned audience hall.

## Significance

As the seat of South Asia's first empires, Pataliputra concentrated the political, economic,
and cultural energies of the **[[vedic-age|Second Urbanization]]**. Its history demonstrates
CWH IV's point that the great early cities were **capitals** — their fortunes rising and
falling with the states they served (it entered "a phase of exhaustion" as power diffused in
the early medieval period). Today, as Patna, it remains among the largest cities of eastern
India.

## Historiography

Strikingly little is **directly** documented about Pataliputra given its importance; its
reconstruction depends on **Buddhist texts**, the fragmentary **Megasthenes**, later
legend (the *Mudrarakshasa* drama), and limited archaeology beneath the living city of Patna.
It exemplifies CWH IV's use of a single city as a lens onto a whole civilization's
state-formation.
