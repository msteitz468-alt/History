# King's College London

King's College London (KCL) is a public research university located in London, England. During the post-World War II period, it became a major center for biological research under the leadership of John Randall, who established the Medical Research Council (MRC) Biophysics Unit there.

## Institutional Context

The KCL Biophysics Unit was funded primarily by a £22,000 Medical Research Council grant, awarded as a bounty for Randall's wartime invention (with H.A.H. Boot) of the cavity magnetron, which enabled microwave radar. This generous funding was highly resented by other King's departments struggling under post-war budgets.

Despite its scientific success, KCL possessed a highly conservative, divided academic culture:
- **Exclusion of Women:** Women researchers, including Rosalind Franklin, were excluded from the KCL Senior Common Room, where the male staff ate lunch and discussed research. This stood in sharp contrast to the cooperative atmosphere Franklin had experienced at the Laboratoire Central in Paris.
- **The division of labor conflict:** Randall's failure to clearly define the roles of Maurice Wilkins and Rosalind Franklin led to a structural division in the DNA research. To resolve this, Randall partitioned the laboratory's resources, assigning the crystalline A-form to Franklin and the B-form to Wilkins, which delayed model building and model collaboration.
- **"Rosy's Parlour" prank:** The competitive and sometimes sophomoric atmosphere was illustrated by a prank in which colleagues hung a sign reading "Rosy's Parlour" on some apparatus Franklin had covered with black-out cloth, which she rejected as "schoolboy behavior."

## Legacy

In March 2000, King's College London commemorated the contribution of both researchers by dedicating the new Franklin-Wilkins Building, opened by the Princess Royal, with James Watson in attendance to speak about their critical contribution.
