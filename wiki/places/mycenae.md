# Mycenae

The **chief citadel of [[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean Greece]]** — Homer's city
of **Agamemnon, "King of Men"** — commanding the routes north out of the Argive
plain. Excavated by **Heinrich Schliemann** (from 1876), it gave the whole Late
Bronze Age Greek civilization its name. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol.
II, Part 1]] (Ch. XIV, Stubbings).

## The Shaft Graves

Mycenae's foundational monument is its two **Grave Circles**, where the transition
from the modest [[coming-of-the-greeks-1900bce|Middle Helladic]] world to
Mycenaean splendour is caught in the act:

- **Grave Circle B** (~1650–1550, west of the citadel): begins with plain
  single Middle Helladic burials and rises to richer shaft graves.
- **Grave Circle A** (Schliemann's, ~1600–1500, later enclosed within the citadel
  wall): six large shaft graves whose dead lay full-length, dressed, under **gold
  masks**, with **inlaid bronze daggers**, gold and silver vessels, and amber from
  the Baltic.

The **profusion of gold** — startling against earlier Helladic poverty — and the
fusion of native **Minyan** forms with **[[minoan-civilization|Minoan]]** art and
**Egyptian** imports (and the first Greek **chariots**) mark the birth of Mycenaean
civilization. The graves date to the early sixteenth century BCE, contemporary with
the expulsion of the [[hyksos|Hyksos]] from Egypt (Queen Ahhotep's inlaid axe names
**Ahmose**). Circle A was reverently terraced and enclosed in later Mycenaean times,
and was still shown as the royal tombs to **Pausanias** ~1700 years later.

## The citadel

Above the graves rose the great **Cyclopean fortress** of the palace age (LH III,
later than this volume) — the **Lion Gate**, the corbelled **tholos** "treasuries"
(the "Treasury of Atreus"), and the megaron palace. In legend Mycenae was founded
(or fortified) by **Perseus** and ruled by the **Pelopids** (Atreus, Agamemnon) at
the time of the Trojan War; archaeology confirms it as **the metropolis of Bronze
Age Greece**. Linear B tablets from houses outside the walls show it as a centre of
the palace economy (the "House of the Oil Merchant," a royal perfumery).

## Why this place mattered

Mycenae is the **type-site and capital of the first Greek civilization** and the
place where Schliemann's excavations — like his work at Troy — turned the Homeric
tradition into archaeology. Its Shaft Graves are the single best evidence for the
**rise of Mycenaean civilization**, and its name marks the whole age that classical
Greece recalled, "with nostalgic pride," as the heroic past.
