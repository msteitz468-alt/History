# Mont Lassois

## Overview

Mont Lassois is the fortified hilltop settlement paired with the exceptional princely burial at Vix (the "Vix princess" tomb), representing one of the key West Hallstatt D centres of power in eastern France during the late sixth to early fifth centuries BCE. It exemplifies the concentration of elite authority at defended sites controlling river routes and long-distance exchange, particularly the influx of Mediterranean luxury goods via the Rhône corridor.

## Geographic and Settlement Description

The site occupies a commanding position on a plateau above the Seine in the Châtillon-sur-Seine region. A geophysical survey "has shown that the area within the fortifications was systematically laid out and heavily occupied. One dominant enclosure contained a large timber building with an apsidal end. The scale of the structure and the associated Greek pottery suggests that it may have been a palace." (west central europe and the mediterranean 118; range ~740s). The settlement overlooked the river bend where the Vix barrow was situated.

## Key Evidence and Mediterranean Interaction

Mont Lassois is directly associated with the Vix burial: "The rich female burial at Vix neatly exemplifies the range of imports with its huge krater for wine-mixing, jugs for pouring, basins, and Attic cups for drinking. The discovery of numerous fragments of Attic cups in the nearby hillfort of Mont Lassois is an indication of a princely residence." The imports reflect adoption of the Greek symposion complex by the local elite as an extension of indigenous feasting rites. "That the range of goods is limited to wine-drinking equipment appropriate to the Greek symposion strongly suggests that it was the idea of the symposion that was accepted and adopted by the elite." (118; range ~740–750).

The site formed part of the broader network of "princely centres" in the late Hallstatt period, with rich burials clustered nearby. It participated in the prestige goods economy in which "elite status was maintained by the manipulation of rare and valuable commodities available only to the paramount chieftains" and where "manufacture of lower-value personal ornaments such as brooches and bracelets, useful in gift exchange," occurred at such centres. (119; range ~810–830).

## Relation to Hallstatt Developments and Decline

Mont Lassois/Vix belongs to the Hallstatt D2–3 phase of power concentration west of the Rhine after c.530/520 BCE, following the foundation of Massalia and the intensification of Rhône corridor traffic. It illustrates the brief florescence of West Hallstatt chiefdoms before the mid-fifth century reorientation: "in the middle of the fifth century... rich burials ceased and the hillforts occupied by the aristocracy came to an end." The Vix burial itself dates to the end of the sixth century BCE. (124–125; range ~910s).

## Historiography and Limitations (per source)

The pairing of defended elite residence (Mont Lassois) with monumental rich burial (Vix) is a classic diagnostic of Hallstatt D social hierarchy. Excavation and survey data highlight systematic layout and "palace"-scale architecture, but full publication details and precise phasing rely on the synthesis. The Greek imports (krater, Attic pottery) are diplomatic/prestige items rather than bulk trade indicators; volume of goods through the corridor remains modest at this stage. Regional bias in preservation and research intensity affects comparisons (stronger data from German sites like Heuneburg).
