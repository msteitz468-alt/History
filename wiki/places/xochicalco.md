# Xochicalco

## Overview

**Xochicalco** is a fortified Epiclassic hilltop city in western Morelos that rose as
[[teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]]’s central Mexican dominance declined. Its Temple of the
Feathered Serpent and international iconographic mix make it a key site for the “middle”
centuries between Teotihuacan and [[tula-hidalgo|Tula]].

## Chronology and scale (Cowgill CHNPA 2000; Adams intro)

Considerably larger than [[cacaxtla]]; ceramic evidence is much better (Hirth & Cyphers
1988). Public art is **eclectic**, but Nagao (1989) argues it drew more evenly on more
sources and reinterpreted them more extensively than Cacaxtla. Together with Cacaxtla, its
rise marks the decline of Teotihuacan dominance, **probably in the 600s** — possibly coeval
with Metepec Teotihuacan while that city was still fairly prosperous (Cowgill 2000).

Adams (CHNPA intro) summarizes high-quality investigations indicating the center was
**fortified**, hybrid on the elite-class level, and that many patterns later seen among the
Aztecs were established then (Hirth 1995). Charlton places Xochicalco among new regional
centers outside the Basin — often on defensible hilltops enhanced by walls, ditches, and
earthworks — forming a militarized competitive matrix around Basin Teotihuacan-derived
city-states that underwrote expansionist forms resolved first by Tula then, after a second
fragmentation, by the Aztecs.

## Why it mattered

Xochicalco exemplifies Epiclassic **competitive multi-polity reordering** after the
inhibition of rival monumental centers under Teotihuacan hegemony ended. It is a primary
comparand for Teotihuacan’s impersonal art style versus later eclectic, individuating public
imagery.
