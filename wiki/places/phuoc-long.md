# Phuoc Long

Phuoc Long was a sparsely populated province of South Vietnam in the highlands northeast of Saigon, bordering Cambodia, of limited economic or strategic value in itself but decisive as the site of the opening battle of North Vietnam's final 1975 offensive.

In December 1974–January 1975, People's Army of Vietnam forces overran Phuoc Long province and its capital, Phuoc Binh, in a deliberate test of both South Vietnamese Army capability and, more significantly, whether the United States would intervene militarily to defend an ARVN position as it had implicitly guaranteed under the Paris Peace Accords. The absence of any American response confirmed for Hanoi's leadership that further, larger offensives could proceed without fear of renewed U.S. air power or ground intervention, directly emboldening the subsequent attack on [[events/ban-me-thuot-1975|Ban Me Thuot]] and the general offensive that led to the [[events/fall-of-saigon-1975|fall of Saigon]] in April 1975.

Phuoc Long's fall is treated by historians as the crucial confidence-building "test case" of the final Vietnam War campaign, of far greater significance to the war's outcome than the province's modest size or population would otherwise suggest.
