# Vulci

A member of the Etruscan League of Twelve Cities, Vulci controlled a stretch of the Tyrrhenian coast and the lower Fiora river valley in northern Etruria. It was one of the great manufacturing centers of the Etruscan world, particularly renowned for bronze work (chariots, tripods, cauldrons) and for importing and imitating Attic pottery on a large scale — its cemeteries have yielded roughly 1,700 Attic vases, among the richest such finds anywhere outside Greece.

Like the other Dodecapolis cities, Vulci flourished in the Archaic and Classical periods (sixth–fourth centuries BCE) before being progressively drawn into the Roman sphere, formally coming under Roman control by the early third century BCE. Its extensive necropoleis, excavated intensively since the nineteenth century, remain a principal source for Etruscan bronzework and painted pottery.
