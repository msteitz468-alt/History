# El Manatí

## Geographic description

**El Manatí** is an Early Formative ceremonial site on the swampy floodplains of the **Río Coatzacoalcos** basin in southern Veracruz, near the major Olmec center [[san-lorenzo-olmec|San Lorenzo Tenochtitlán]] (Olmec site only—not Aztec Tenochtitlan). Waterlogged spring mud created exceptional anaerobic preservation of perishable materials otherwise almost unknown for Formative Mesoamerica.

## Ritual deposits and finds (EW2001)

John E. Clark (EW2001, “Gulf Lowlands: South Region”) ranks El Manatí among the South Gulf Coast’s **four most important Formative sites** with San Lorenzo, Las Limas, and [[la-venta|La Venta]]. Finds substantiate that ceramic-using peoples were in the South Gulf by at least **1630 B.C.**, already performing rituals connected to freshwater springs.

David C. Grove (EW2001, “Olmec Culture”): Rodríguez and Ortiz excavated spring mud deposits with ritually deposited wooden heads/busts (**>2 dozen**), jade axes and jewelry, and rubber balls—confirming sacred water sources and that “the Olmec played a version of the Mesoamerican ball game” (*part_of:* [[mesoamerican-ball-game]] · *part_of:* [[olmec]] hinterland ritual).

Mary Pye (EW2001, “Wood: Tools and Products”): Early Formative wooden sculptures from the swampy Coatzacoalcos Basin at El Manatí—**>35** intact sculptures reported; **18** found in original context as ritual offerings with jade hachas, rubber balls, shark teeth, and bones. Busts of males and females with slit eyes and large downturned mouths; the corpus foreshadows later Maya figure carvings.

Diehl’s earlier CHNPA synthesis (already on [[olmec]]) reports parallel tallies (~40+ wooden busts, wooden scepters, 9 rubber balls, 100+ greenstone celts)—EW2001 numbers refine the published counts without contradicting the ritual interpretation.

## Why it mattered

Type site for **perishable Olmec art** and for early **rubber-ball** use in ballgame ritual; anchors pre-monumental ceramic presence in the San Lorenzo landscape and sacred-water cosmology later echoed across Mesoamerica. *enabled:* perishable-antecedent arguments for stone sculpture style · *part_of:* San Lorenzo regional ritual landscape.
