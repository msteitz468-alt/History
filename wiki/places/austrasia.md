# Austrasia

**The "East Land" of [[francia|Francia]]** — the northeastern Frankish sub-kingdom centred on
Metz, Trier, and the Moselle, which became the power base from which the [[pippinids|Pippinid/Arnulfing]]
family rose to displace the [[merovingian-franks|Merovingians]].

## Geography and character

Despite its name, Austrasia was not a purely "Germanic" east. In the seventh century it "included not
only the eastern regions around Metz and Trier, but such old Roman cities as Reims, Châlons, and Laon.
No linguistic boundary separated the regions" from [[neustria|Neustria]]. In the sixth century it was
"a center of Roman culture and influence." Austrasia had a **longer tradition of unity and autonomy**
than Neustria or Burgundy.

## History of control

[[brunhild|Brunhild]], the Visigothic queen of Sigibert I, pursued a Romanising fiscal policy here that
widened the rift between the Austrasian aristocracy and the monarchy; the aristocracy expelled her in
599. Their leaders **Arnulf of Metz and Pippin of Herstal** invited [[chlothar-ii|Chlothar II]] to take
the kingdom in 613, then secured Austrasian autonomy under their own management. Geary stresses that
Austrasian hostility to Neustrian control "was not based on any sort of 'ethnic' opposition between East
and West, Germanic or Roman"; the issues were "spheres of influence and local political traditions."

After the assassination of the Neustrian mayor **Ebroin (680)**, "Austrasia, under the leadership of
Pippin… would not be dominated by Neustria." [[pippinids|Pippin II]]'s victory at
[[battle-of-tertry-687|Tertry (687)]] made the Austrasian mayors masters of all Francia.

## Why it mattered

Austrasia is where the Carolingian future was incubated: the Arnulfing-Pippinid alliance, the cults of
Arnulf of Metz and Gertrudis of Nivelles, and the militarised aristocracy that would produce Charles
Martel and [[pippin-iii|Pippin III]] were all rooted here.
