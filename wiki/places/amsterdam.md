# Amsterdam

Amsterdam was the **third and culminating entrepôt** of the Low Countries in the
Bruges → Antwerp → Amsterdam sequence of
[[gelderblom-cities-of-commerce-2013|Gelderblom's *Cities of Commerce*]], and the
clearest case of his [[open-access-institutions|open-access]] institutional model.

## Rise

Founded only around 1250, Amsterdam entered international trade slowly — first via
Hamburg beer and Baltic grain and timber. Its decisive transformation came after
the Spanish recapture of [[antwerp|Antwerp]] in **1585**, when Antwerp's
merchants — Dutch, German, Portuguese, English, Italian — emigrated and most
settled in Amsterdam. **Within fifteen years** the city created well-functioning
markets for the widest variety of goods, financial services, and commercial
information, becoming the central node in merchants' international networks while
the Dutch Republic was still at war with Spain.

## The Open-Access Institutional Model

Amsterdam's response to the agency problems of a heterogeneous merchant community
differed sharply from its predecessors. Instead of granting safe-conducts,
consular jurisdictions, or special rights to separate foreign
[[hanseatic-league|nations]], the city created **inclusive institutions
protecting all merchants regardless of origin, wealth, religion, or
specialization** — the paradigm of [[open-access-institutions|open-access
institutions]]. Crucially, this happened even though the city was governed by a
**small clique of local merchants who actively excluded most traders, local and
foreign, from political power** — which Gelderblom uses to argue that the driver
of inclusive institutions was *economic* competition, not the political franchise
of merchants. Amsterdam also developed the most pronounced role as guardian of
commercial interests: after 1580 it worked with other Holland and Zeeland cities
to create a **permanent Dutch fleet**, where Antwerp had earlier resisted
princely naval protection.

## Financial and Commercial Infrastructure

Amsterdam carried the permanent-market, contracting, conflict-resolution, and
risk-spreading institutions of the earlier entrepôts to their most developed form
— supporting marine insurance, bottomry, and impersonal exchange, and underpinning
the later [[dutch-east-india-company|VOC]], the Wisselbank, and the first early
stock market. It became the hub of the [[early-modern-commerce|global early-modern
trade]] system and the [[global-silver-trade|global silver trade]].

## Significance

Amsterdam is the **mature** term in Gelderblom's sequence: the city where urban
competition produced generalized, open-access institutions most fully, and where
the divergence from the strong-states and merchant-political-control explanations
is sharpest. It eventually yielded commercial leadership to London, continuing the
Braudelian succession of commercial capitals.
