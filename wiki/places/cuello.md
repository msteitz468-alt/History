# Cuello

## Geographic description

**Cuello** is a Formative–Classic Maya site of ~**1.6 km²** in northern Belize (Orange Walk). It is notable as the **earliest known Lowland Maya village community** currently documented (comparable Middle Preclassic at Colha and K’axob). Early Classic population peak is estimated **>3,000**. **Platform 34** is the most extensively excavated locus.

## Historical significance

### Chronology and architecture (Evans & Webster 2001; Hammond entry)

Revised AMS chronology:

| Phase | Dates | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Swasey** | 1200–900 B.C. | Maize agriculture and ceramics already established; earliest southern Lowlands architecture — low platformed courtyard groups with perishable buildings |
| **Bladen** | 900–650 B.C. | Xe-sphere links; obsidian and jade (incl. possible Olmec blue jades); child burials with jade/shell imply ascribed rank |
| **Lopez Mamom** | 650–400 B.C. | Mamom-sphere participation |
| **Cocos Chicanel** | 400 B.C.–A.D. 250 | Late Formative |

**~400 B.C.:** patio group ritually destroyed with mass burial of ~**32** mostly adult males (possible captives) and construction of Platform 34. **~A.D. 100:** another mass burial of **12** males with plain **Stela 1**. **166 Formative burials** form the largest Formative Maya sample.

### Economy and long-distance goods

Maize in **>90%** of samples from early Swasey onward; root crops including **manioc** (*M. esculenta*) and tentatively **malanga** (*Xanthosoma*) from Bladen or earlier (manioc pollen suggests introduction to the lowlands ~**3000–2500 B.C.** more broadly). Deer, freshwater turtles, domestic dogs systematically killed ~**10 months** of age; *Pomacea* snails in Cocos phase. Formative maize proportion lower than Classic and lower than farther west — greater resource diversity. **Chert** from the Colha zone **26 km SE**; Maya Mountains sandstone grinding stones from Bladen (**130 km** south).

Among the **earliest recovered Maya jades** are undecorated beads from Cuello burials dating **1100–400 B.C.**; INAA shows a few Cuello (and Cerros) specimens chemically indistinguishable from a **Costa Rican** compositional group, indicating **bidirectional jade flow**.

## Why it mattered

Cuello anchors the **lowland Formative baseline**: sedentary maize-and-ceramics village life without close Gulf Coast Olmec ceramic ties, early ranked burials, early public architecture, and the start of lowland monumentality that later scales to [[el-mirador|El Mirador]] and Classic cities.
