# Ravensbrück

**Ravensbrück**, near Fürstenberg some 50 miles north of Berlin, opened in May 1939 as the **principal SS concentration camp for women** (succeeding the smaller Lichtenburg). Its history tracks the shifting place of women in the KL system — marginal before the war, central to it during the Holocaust. Documented from [[wachsmann-kl-concentration-camps-2015|Wachsmann, *KL* (2015)]].

## Structure and Staff

The first transport (~400–450 Jehovah's Witnesses) arrived from Lichtenburg in mid-May 1939. Discipline was harsher than at Lichtenburg — flogging was introduced as official punishment, and the SS relied heavily on guard dogs (Himmler believed women feared them more). Ravensbrück became the **training school for female guards** (*Aufseherinnen*), who were part of the SS "retinue" rather than full SS members and were recruited largely for economic opportunity; from 1942 they were dispatched to staff the women's compounds of the eastern camps. The female share of the KL population rose from ~3.3% (1938) to 11.7% a year later, but Ravensbrück remained far less lethal than the men's camps in the pre-war years.

## War

Ravensbrück supplied the first female staff and administrative model for the **Auschwitz women's camp** in March 1942 (under senior supervisor Johanna Langefeld, who then clashed bitterly with [[hoss-rudolf|Höss]]); within months the improvised Auschwitz compound had surpassed its parent — an early sign of the Holocaust reshaping the system. Ravensbrück was a site of the **gas-gangrene "Rabbits" experiments** (Karl Gebhardt's mutilating surgery on Polish women from 1942) and Clauberg's later sterilization trials. In its final months it acquired a **gas chamber** at the adjacent Uckermark camp, where some 3,600 women were murdered in early 1945. [[hoss-rudolf|Höss]] supervised mass shootings there. Under Himmler's endgame diplomacy, ~9,500 women were released to the Swedish and Danish Red Cross before the Red Army liberated the camp on 30 April 1945.
