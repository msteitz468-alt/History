# Deir el-Medina

The purpose-built village (ancient *Set Maat*, "the Place of Truth") that housed the
royal artisans who cut and decorated the tombs of the [[places/thebes|Theban]] Valley of
the Kings during the Eighteenth–Twentieth Dynasties. Its exceptional preservation, and
the thousands of ostraca and papyri its workers left, make it the richest single window
onto everyday life, labour and exchange in the ancient world.

## Why It Mattered

In [[sources/kemp-ancient-egypt-anatomy-2018|Kemp's *Anatomy of a Civilization*]] (Ch. 7,
"The birth of economic man"), Deir el-Medina is the key evidence that a more "modern,"
value-conscious economy of **private exchange** grew up inside the redistributive
[[concepts/provider-state|provider state]]. The community was state-provisioned (rations,
tools, a state-supplied workforce) yet its members bought, sold, lent, priced goods in
standardized units of value, struck deals, went on strike (the earliest recorded labour
action, under Ramesses III), and litigated — a "little tradition" of economic life
([[concepts/great-tradition-little-tradition]]) documented in extraordinary detail.

The concrete data are the heart of Kemp's [[concepts/birth-of-economic-man|"birth of
economic man"]]. Workers were nominally the king's employees (paid from the "Treasury" or
"Granary of Pharaoh"), yet when rations failed they **demonstrated outside the mortuary
temples** — the earliest recorded strikes, under Ramesses III. Goods were valued in *deben*
and *kite* of silver/bronze and in *khar*/*oipe*/*hin* of grain and oil, "silver" used much
as we say "money"; payment was composite barter (a coffin at 25.5 *deben* settled in goats,
a pig, logs and weighed bronze). The ostraca preserve a price series: **sandals** steady at
1–2 *deben* for ~150 years; **grain** spiking to 8–12 *deben*/khar in the late Twentieth
Dynasty "in the year of the hyenas, when there was a famine." A workman's family ration was
~**66 khar** a year. Members also made beds and coffins for sale, lent donkeys at interest,
and sold their artistic skills — private enterprise running right through the
state-provisioned village.
