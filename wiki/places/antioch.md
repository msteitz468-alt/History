# Antioch-on-the-Orontes

**Antioch** (Antioch-on-the-Orontes) was the principal capital of the
[[seleucid-empire|Seleucid Empire]] and one of the great cities of the
[[hellenistic-world|Hellenistic]] and Roman worlds. Founded by
[[seleucus-i-nicator|Seleucus I]] around 300 BCE after [[battle-of-ipsus-301|Ipsus]],
it was the chief of the **"Syrian tetrapolis"** — the four foundations that became the
heart of the kingdom (CAH VII.1 ch. 6, Musti).

## The Syrian Tetrapolis and the Westward Shift

Seleucid power began in Mesopotamia: the first capital was **Seleuceia-on-the-Tigris**
(c. 311–301). After Ipsus gave Seleucus north Syria, the political and economic centre
of gravity moved west, anchored on four new cities in the region called **Pieria /
Syria Seleucis**:

- **Antioch-on-the-Orontes** — the royal capital, a centre of metalwork and the
  goldsmiths' craft;
- **Seleuceia-in-Pieria** — its port and a chief naval base (and site of the dynastic
  cult of *Seleucus Nicator*);
- **Laodicea-on-Sea**;
- **Apamea** — the kingdom's principal **military base and barracks**, and remount depot
  for the cavalry and elephants.

Around these clustered colonies of Macedonians and Greeks with home-evoking names
(Europus, Cyrrhus, Beroea, Larissa) — the dense settlement that made north Syria the
Graeco-Macedonian core of the realm. See [[seleucid-colonization]].

## Why Antioch Mattered

- **The seat of Seleucid kingship** — the residence of the king and court, the hub of the
  *Syro-Mesopotamian nucleus* that was the kingdom's true economic and strategic heart.
- **A great cosmopolitan metropolis** — by the Roman period one of the three or four
  largest cities of the Mediterranean, a centre of trade between the Mediterranean and
  the Persian Gulf routes.
- **A long afterlife** — under Rome the capital of the province of Syria; in Late
  Antiquity one of the patriarchal sees of Christianity and a great Greek-speaking city,
  until earthquakes, Persian sack, and the Arab conquest reduced it.
