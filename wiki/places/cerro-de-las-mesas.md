# Cerro de las Mesas

## Overview

**Cerro de las Mesas** (Mixtequilla, northwest of the Tuxtlas) is, in Richard A. Diehl’s
assessment ([[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v2-1|CHNPA II.1 Ch. 4]]), the
**best-known Early Classic Gulf Coast site**: initial Late Formative occupation; growth in the
Early and Middle Classic.

## Monuments and calendrics

Fifteen stelae are reported, many with unreadable glyphs or empty cartouches that may have
contained painted glyphs. Style links to earlier Izapa-related sculpture at
[[tres-zapotes|Tres Zapotes]] and La Mojarra. Two stelae carry Long Count dates, both Middle
Classic: **Stela 6** (9.1.12.14.10 or **A.D. 468**) and **Stela 8** (9.4.18.16.8 or **A.D. 533**)
—a time when Cerro de las Mesas pottery shows **strong Teotihuacan influence**. One authority
suggests the La Mojarra stela was en route to Cerro de las Mesas when lost in the river (Diehl).

## Regional context

Cerro de las Mesas sits in the Classic Gulf continuum after Formative [[olmec|Olmec]] and
Epi-Olmec horizons: writing and monumental display continue, now under central Mexican ceramic
influence rather than sole Pacific Izapa stylistic dominance. Nearby **El Zapotal** (Late Classic)
produced life-size unfired painted clay sculpture including a skeletal figure and lines of hollow
Cihuateteotl sculptures—climax of central/southern Veracruz ceramic figurative tradition in the
same broad region (Diehl).

## Why it mattered

Primary Early–Middle Classic Gulf dataset for Long Count monumentalism outside the Maya area and
for Teotihuacan–Gulf ceramic interaction without requiring claims of direct Teotihuacan political
control (contrast [[matacapan|Matacapan]]’s stronger enclave-style material).
