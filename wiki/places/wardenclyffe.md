# Wardenclyffe

**Wardenclyffe** was [[actors/tesla-nikola|Nikola Tesla]]'s experimental wireless facility at Shoreham, on the north shore of Long Island, New York, built from 1901. Centered on a large laboratory (designed by architect Stanford White) and a ~187-foot transmitting tower topped by a great cupola electrode, it was conceived as a **"World Telegraphy Center"** that would broadcast both information and, Tesla hoped, **[[concepts/wireless-power-transmission|electrical power without wires]]** across the globe.

Financed by [[actors/morgan-john-pierpont|J. P. Morgan]] under a contract Tesla signed 1 March 1901 — $150,000 for which Morgan took, at Tesla's own insistence, 51% control of the patents ("Let the control be yours, Mr. Morgan. I insist that you take fifty-one percent and I forty-nine") — the project ran far over budget after Tesla secretly enlarged it from a 90-foot, $100,000 telegraph transmitter into a global power-and-telegraphy "World System." The land was 200 acres on Long Island Sound provided by James Warden; the laboratory was designed by **Stanford White** (McKim, Mead & White). The tower, originally planned at 600 feet, was built at **187 feet** above ground over a 120-foot shaft, topped by a 55-ton, 50-foot cupola, with sixteen iron-pipe "terrestrial grippers" driven 300 feet into the earth.

After [[actors/marconi-guglielmo|Marconi]]'s December 1901 transatlantic signal made cheaper point-to-point wireless seem the better bet, Morgan refused all further funds (repeatedly, 1903–1906: "I am not willing to advance you any more money"). Tesla told Morgan he had spent "about $250,000 in all." The tower fired up briefly in July 1903, then fell silent; Tesla signed the property over to the Waldorf-Astoria against ~$19,000 in unpaid rent (1915), and it was demolished with explosives by the Smiley Steel Company in 1917. Wardenclyffe became the central symbol of Tesla's overreach and ruin.
