# Novgorod

Novgorod (Veliky Novgorod) was one of the most important historic cities of Russia, serving as the capital of the Novgorod Republic (1136–1478) and a major center of East Slavic trade and culture.

## Geographic Description
At head of Volkhov; lake Ilmen area implied; trading posts along Msta/Luga established by Olga.

## History of Control and Settlement (per Primary Chronicle, ll. 3000–6000)
Pre-852: Slavs had "their own authority in Novgorod".

859: Varangians from beyond sea impose tribute on Chuds, Slavs, Merians, Ves’, Krivichians.

860–862: Tributaries drive Varangians out; discord ("no law... tribe rose against tribe"); invite "Varangian Russes": "Our land is great and rich, but there is no order in it. Come to rule and reign over us." Rurik locates in Novgorod; "On account of these Varangians, the district of Novgorod became known as the land of Rus’. The present inhabitants of Novgorod are descended from the Varangian race, but aforetime they were Slavs." Later: in Novgorod, first settlers Slavs; Varangian colonists.

882: Oleg leaves Novgorod (with Igor); takes south (Smolensk etc.); establishes Kiev as "mother of Russian cities." Novgorod pays 300 grivny/year tribute to Varangians "for the preservation of peace. This tribute was paid to the Varangians until the death of Yaroslav."

945+: Olga "went to Novgorod, and along the Msta she established trading-posts and collected tribute. She also collected imposts and tribute along the Luga." "Her hunting-grounds, boundary posts, towns, and trading-posts still exist throughout the whole region, while her sleighs stand in Pskov to this day."

970: Novgorodians request prince from Svyatoslav ("If you will not come to us, then we will choose a prince of our own"); Vladimir (son of Malusha) sent with uncle Dobrynya. Vladimir later returns from abroad with Varangians to challenge Yaropolk.

980: Dobrynya (Vladimir's uncle) rules Novgorod; "set up an idol beside the river Volkhov, and the people of Novgorod offered sacrifice to it as if to God himself."

## Why This Place Mattered
Origin point of Rurikid rule and "land of Rus'" name; northern anchor of Varangian-Greek route; tribute/trade hub; source of Varangian military support for southern campaigns; early Christian resistance (idol of Perun thrown later). Strategic for control of north and recruitment.

## From Primary Chronicle notes (Cross & Sherbowitz-Wetzor 1953, lines 12001–16299)

Novgorod was the key northern center; Varangians had corporate centers there ("the Swedish traders frequenting Novgorod possessed their corporate centers in the town even at this early period"; "in the original vo dvoré Poromani; this enigmatic phrase is identified as a corruption of the Old Norse farnianna garpr, 'the court of the overseas merchants'"; lines ~12663-12674). "Rokom, mod. Rakema, on the northeastern shore of Lake Il'men’ south of Novgorod" (line ~12679). Yaroslav collected Varangians there in 1015 (expecting Vladimir's attack; massacre of Varangians by Novgorodians after tribute suspension; Eymund saga parallel; lines ~12682 ff.). "The Sudomir’ (Sudoma) river, southwest of Novgorod, is a small stream rising east of Pskov and itself a tributary of the Shelon’, which in turn flows into Lake Il'men’ from the west. Yaroslav thus travelled some 500 miles in seven days to meet Bryachislav" (lines ~14623-14626).

St. Sophia of Novgorod: "Consecrated in 1052, St. Sophia of Novgorod was constructed to replace a wooden church with thirteen cupolas erected by Joachim, the first Bishop of Novgorod, which was destroyed by fire in 1045. Built of local grayish-yellow stone, St. Sophia is a modified example of the five-nave church of inscribed-cross type. ... The so-called Chersonian doors of the west front are actually Saxon work of the middle twelfth century, having been made at Magdeburg, ca. 1152" (lines ~13199-13219). Joachim died 1030; Ephrem locum tenens; Luka Zhidyata appointed 1036 by Yaroslav (disregarded Joachim's wishes); Luka accused post-Yaroslav, imprisoned 3 years in Kiev, died 1059 en route back (lines ~12964-12977).

Yaroslav assigned to distant Rostov initially; later Novgorod (after Vysheslav death; relations with father not cordial; lines ~12594-12603). Novgorodians opposed Svyatopolk's plan to exchange Vladimir-Volynsk for Novgorod (line ~14404). Mstislav (son of Vladimir Monomakh) prince there; letter from Vladimir to Oleg at his instance (1096; lines ~14617). Gytha (Harold's daughter) married Vladimir Monomakh (lines ~14553, ~14373 ref.). Novgorod contributed to Yaroslav's restoration (alliance with Swedish Olaf; Ingigerd/Irene marriage 1019; Rognvald guaranteed Ladoga; lines ~12768-12785).

"Constantine the Posadnik of Novgorod, was Dobrinya’s son, and since Dobrinya was Vladimir’s maternal uncle, Constantine was an older cousin of Yaroslav" (lines ~12779-12782). "The Chuds (Ests) attacked by Yaroslav lay to the north and west of Lake Peipus"; Yur'ev (Dorpat) founded there (line ~12935 ff.). "The Yam’ were a Finnish tribe occupying at this period the region between Lake Ladoga and the Northern Dvina" (lines ~13143-13151). "Chud’ beyond the portages" (White Sea basin Finns; lines ~15583 ff. index refs).

Political: Novgorod recognized as "the most important center" after Kiev in Vladimir's assignments (line ~12482). Yaroslav suspended tribute, recruited Varangians (lines ~12675 ff.). Contribution of "each man" to hire Varangians: "480 grivny from Novgorod" etc. (line ~13666). Later independence tendencies. 

From Monomakh Testament notes: campaigns involving Novgorod troops and Mstislav's role (multiple 1100s refs).

## 11th c. role (Primary Chronicle, Laurentian Text ll. 9001–12000)
In 1096, after David left for Smolensk, "The Novgorodians then went to Rostov in search of Mstislav, the son of Vladimir, and brought him to Novgorod as their prince, at the same time instructing David never to return" (ll. 9032–9034). Mstislav later led Novgorod forces (with Rostov/Beloozero men) against Oleg Svyatoslavich: vanguard seized tax-collectors; four-day standoff at Suzdal; victory at Kulachek (Vladimir's standard); pursuit to Ryazan; peace offers; Mstislav returned "accompanied by the prayers of the reverend Bishop Nikita" (ll. 9238–9316). In 1102, Svyatopolk tried to replace Mstislav with his own son; Novgorodians refused: "our city does not want either you or your son... Vsevolod assigned us Mstislav as our prince. We brought him up as one of us while you left us in the lurch." They took Mstislav home (ll. 9873–9889). Novgorod's veche-like autonomy and loyalty to its prince (Mstislav) are evident; Gyuryata Rogovich of Novgorod supplied northern marvel tales to the chronicler (ll. 9154–9155).
