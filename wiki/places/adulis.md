# Adulis

**Adulis** was the principal Red Sea port of the [[aksum|Kingdom of Aksum]], located on the Eritrean coast near modern Zula. It served as Aksum's window onto the Indian Ocean trade network and the gateway through which goods moved between the Ethiopian highlands and the Mediterranean–Indian Ocean world.

## Overview

Adulis is mentioned in the *Periplus Maris Erythraei* (c. AD 50) as a major emporium trading in ivory, tortoiseshell, rhinoceros horn, and slaves from the interior, in exchange for cloth, metals, wine, and olive oil. The Aksumite king Zoscales, mentioned in the *Periplus*, controlled both Adulis and much of the hinterland. The city was visited by the Byzantine merchant Cosmas Indicopleustes in the sixth century, who recorded two important inscriptions (the "Monumentum Adulitanum") attesting Aksumite imperial claims.

Adulis was destroyed by Arab forces in the seventh century CE — the most consequential single event of Aksum's commercial decline. Persian seizure of Himyar (South Arabia) in the 570s had already denied Aksumite access to the Arabian side of the Red Sea; Arab control of the littoral then terminated what remained. The fleet assembled at Gabaza (adjacent to Adulis) for Kaleb's South Arabian campaigns in the 520s–530s CE represented the apex of Aksumite maritime power; within a century that infrastructure was gone. Phillipson (*Foundations*, 2012, Ch. 15) documents the import of carved marble chancel screens from Constantinople-area quarries as among the most distant archaeological evidence of Adulis's commercial reach — Proconnesian marble confirmed by stable-isotope analysis. The destruction of Adulis severed Aksum's connection to Byzantium and the Mediterranean world and was a direct cause of the capital's relocation to eastern Tigray. See `[[aksumite-decline-700ce]]`.

## Historiography

Known primarily from the *Periplus*, Cosmas's *Christian Topography*, and archaeological excavation (Anfray). Phillipson's *Foundations* (2012) integrates Adulis into the broader Aksumite economic system.
