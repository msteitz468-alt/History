# Chogha Zanbil (Dur-Untash)

The **royal and religious city of Al Untash-Napirisha** ("town of
[[untash-napirisha|Untash-Napirisha]]"), founded **ex novo** ~1300 BCE by the great
[[elam|Elamite]] Igihalkid king ~40 km southeast of **[[susa|Susa]]**, midway toward
the highlands. Its ziggurat is the **best-preserved in the Near East** and the supreme
surviving monument of Elamite religion and architecture (a UNESCO World Heritage Site).
[[potts-archaeology-of-elam-2016|Potts]] treats it as the centrepiece of Untash-Napirisha's
unprecedented building program — but stresses it was **abandoned for monumental purposes
on the king's death**: no successor left inscriptions there.

## The ziggurat

Built of millions of bricks (sun-dried core, ~2 m baked-brick skin), it rose four
stages; the lowest measured **99.4 m on a side** on a socle of 105.2 m (~200 Elamite
cubits) — its footprint **slightly larger than the ziggurat of Babylon**. Its
construction is **unparalleled in Mesopotamia**: rather than built from the outside in,
a central solid tower was raised first and successive brick skins added around it, with
L-shaped Inshushinak temples forming the base; access ran by **internal staircases**,
not external ramps. The summit shrine — a *kukunnum* of **Napirisha** (the great
highland god) and **Inshushinak** (the city-god of Susa) — was glazed in blue, green,
gold and silver. Dedicatory bricks naming Untash-Napirisha were laid after every tenth
course of plain bricks. The joint dedication to a highland and a lowland deity makes the
monument a deliberate statement of the **union of "Anshan and Susa"** under one kingship.

## The temenos, pantheon and royal quarter

The inner *temenos* wall (~1,625 m perimeter) enclosed shrines to **twenty-five deities**
— Elamite highland gods (Napirisha, Kiririsha), Susian gods (Inshushinak), and
Mesopotamian imports (Nabu, Sin, Adad, Inanna) — a syncretic program binding the realm's
cults together. East of the temenos lay palaces and the **palais hypogée**, a structure
with five subterranean vaulted tombs containing **cremated** remains (with weapons and
jewellery) alongside one uncremated woman, possibly a Kassite bride. Cremation was
otherwise unknown in seventy years of French excavation at Susa, making the site
uniquely important for Elamite mortuary practice.

## Abandonment and afterlife

Official patronage ceased at Untash-Napirisha's death; the palaces were destroyed in the
reign of [[nebuchadnezzar-i|Nebuchadnezzar I]] (~1125–1104 BCE). Sporadic later occupation
into the 8th–7th centuries BCE is attested — consistent with the Neo-Elamite king
Ummanaldash (Huban-haltash III) fleeing to "Dur-Untash" during
[[sack-of-susa-646bce|Assurbanipal's campaign]].
