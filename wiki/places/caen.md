# Caen

Major city and road/rail hub in eastern Normandy. Primary D-Day objective for British and Canadian forces.

## Significance in 1944

Lying at the base of the Caen-Falaise Plain, Caen was seen as essential for the "master plan" shield function and for providing maneuver space for British armoured forces. It was not captured on D-Day as planned. The prolonged battle for the city and its surrounding ridges (through Operations Epsom, Charnwood, and Goodwood) defined the first six weeks of the Normandy campaign and became the focus of major historiographical controversy.

## Key Actions

- D-Day attempts by 3rd British Division and airborne forces.
- Villers-Bocage (13 June).
- Epsom (Odon, late June).
- Charnwood (early July, capture of northern Caen).
- Goodwood (18–21 July, the great armoured effort east of the city).

The city was largely destroyed by bombing and fighting. Its eventual fall and the subsequent Goodwood operation marked the transition toward breakout.
