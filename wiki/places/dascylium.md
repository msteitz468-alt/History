# Dascylium

**Dascylium** (Daskyleion; Hisartepe on Lake Manyas) was the **second satrapal capital
of Achaemenid Anatolia** — seat of the **Hellespontine satrapy (Daskylitis)** governing
the north-west — the counterpart to **[[sardis|Sardis]]** in the south-west. It is the
type-site for the **"Greco-Persian" art** of the western satrapies.
[[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 3e, M. Mellink) treats it.

## The satrapal seat

The third of Herodotus's Anatolian satrapies — the **Daskylitis** — embraced the south
shore of the Hellespont and Propontis, Bithynia and Paphlagonia, and the inland Phrygian
and Cappadocian plateau, governed from Dascylium. Its satraps run through the volume's
politics: **Mitrobates** (murdered by Oroetes of Sardis in the interregnum of 522);
**Oebares** son of Megabazus before 493; and from 479 **Artabazus**, whose
**Pharnacid** house held the satrapy as a **hereditary** possession into the fourth
century. Excavation of the mound found, below Hellenistic levels reusing blocks of the
**satrapal palace**, a **hoard of c. 300 clay bullae** with seal-impressions —
cuneiform Old Persian legends pointing to **Xerxes** (the "royal hero" grasping a
lion-griffin), Aramaic legends, and one Greek — proof that an **administrative archive**
of the satrapal residence stood here.

## Greco-Persian art

Dascylium is the **type-site of "Greco-Persian" funerary art** — the hybrid idiom of the
western satrapies. Its **tomb-stelae** (some reused in a Byzantine tomb; one with an
Aramaic epitaph for "ʾElnap son of ʾSy") carry, in flat painted relief, the **funerary
banquet, procession and hunt** of Anatolian tradition, with **some attendants in Persian
costume** — the monuments of **local noblemen and officials whose lifestyle was gradually
Persianized** while their art kept strong ties to Greece. The same idiom appears across
the satrapal world: the painted **Karaburun tomb** near Elmali (a nobleman in purple
Persian dress, spearing a Greek hoplite from horseback), looted tomb-chambers in the
Lydian–Phrygian tumuli, and reliefs from Phrygia to inner Lycia. See
[[greco-persian-art]].

## Why Dascylium mattered

- **The administrative anchor of north-west Anatolia** — the seat from which Persia held
  the Hellespont, the Propontis and the approaches to Europe and the Black Sea.
- **The clearest window onto provincial Achaemenid society** — its bullae attest the
  satrapal chancery, its stelae the Persianized-but-Hellenizing nobility of the
  satrapies, a class of collaborators rather than Persian colonists.

## Historiography

Reconstructed from **Herodotus and Thucydides** (the satraps) and from **archaeology** —
the 1954–59 excavations (the palace spolia, the bulla-hoard) and the Greco-Persian
stelae found since. The bullae are not yet matched against Persepolis impressions and
their range of dates is unsettled; the residential quarter and cemeteries await full
excavation. Mellink stresses how much of early Persian Anatolia we still under-estimate
for lack of dug satrapal centres of Dascylium's and Sardis's rank.
