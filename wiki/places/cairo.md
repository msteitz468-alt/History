# Cairo

**Cairo** (Arabic **al-Qahira**, "the Victorious") was founded in **969** as the royal city of the
[[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] Imam-caliphs beside the older Muslim town of **Fustat**, and grew
into the greatest metropolis of the medieval Islamic world. (Fustat, "the first Muslim city in Egypt," was
absorbed into the growing capital.) This page focuses on its **Fatimid foundation**;
its later Ayyubid, Mamluk, and Ottoman history is treated on the relevant actor and event pages.

## The Fatimid foundation (969)

On the night the general **[[jawhar-al-siqilli|Jawhar al-Siqilli]]** secured Egypt for
[[al-muizz-li-din-allah|al-Mu'izz]] (see [[fatimid-conquest-of-egypt-969]]), he camped a few miles north of
Fustat and laid the foundations of a new walled city — first named **al-Mansuriyya** after the Ifriqiyan
capital, then, when al-Mu'izz took up residence, renamed **al-Qahira al-Mu'izziya** ("the Victorious City of
al-Mu'izz"). Its plan deliberately reproduced [[mahdiyya|Mahdiyya]] and [[mansuriyya|Mansuriyya]]: a **square
grid**, the gates **Bab al-Futuh** (north) and **Bab Zuwayla** (south), and the ceremonial **Bayn al-Qasrayn**
square between the Imam-caliph's palace and that of his heir. Its main north–south artery is still called
**al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah Street**.

## Al-Azhar

In Cairo's south-eastern quarter Jawhar founded the congregational mosque of **al-Azhar** ("the Radiant" —
from the same root as *al-Zahra*, an epithet of **Fatima**), completed 972. Established as a centre of Ismaili
learning, it became — and remains, over a millennium later — one of the foremost centres of Islamic
scholarship, outliving the dynasty that built it.

## Significance

Cairo is the enduring monument of the Fatimid rise: a caliphal foundation that absorbed Fustat, anchored a
two-century Ismaili empire on the Nile, and became the permanent capital of Egypt under every later dynasty.
Its founding in 969–972 marks the transformation of the Fatimids from a North African into a Near Eastern and
Mediterranean power.
