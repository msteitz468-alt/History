# The Qhapaq Ñan (Inca Road System)

The **Qhapaq Ñan** ("the royal road" / "the road of the powerful") was the road network that physically
bound together the [[inca-empire|Inca empire]] — some **40,000 km** of engineered routes running the
length of the Andes and the Pacific coast, the logistical backbone of the largest state in pre-Columbian
America. Documented for the wiki through Terence D'Altroy's [[daltroy-incas-2004|*The Incas*]] (a
UNESCO World Heritage site since 2014).

## A logistical empire

D'Altroy stresses that the Incas owed their success to **strategy and logistics, not technology** — and
the road system was its instrument. Two great trunk highways ran roughly parallel — one through the
**highlands** (the central spine, from Quito past Cuzco to the Bolivian altiplano and beyond), one along
the **coast** — cross-linked by lateral routes. Because the Andes are vertical, the roads climbed in
**stone staircases**, crossed gorges on **fibre suspension bridges** (rewoven yearly by corvée labour),
and ran as **causeways** across marsh and desert. They were built and maintained by **[[mita-and-andean-reciprocity|mit'a]]**
labour and were, in principle, **reserved for state use** — armies, officials, the relay messengers
(*chasqui*) who carried [[khipu|khipu]] and verbal messages, and the caravans of llamas moving goods.

## Tampu and qollqa

The roads were studded with **~2,000 waystations (*tampu*)** at roughly a day's travel apart, and with
great clusters of **storehouses (*qollqa*)** that held food, cloth, and arms — the system that let the
Inca **feed traveling armies** far from home and sustain campaigns the empire's modular conscript forces
required. The larger nodes were **provincial centres** (Hatun Xauxa, Vilcaswaman, Huánuco Pampa,
Tomebamba) — which the archaeologist **Craig Morris** called an **"artificial urbanism"**: planted on
empty ground for interregional administration rather than local life, barely occupied most of the year,
and (tellingly) lacking cemeteries, because even the staff knew they were only temporary.

## Significance

The Qhapaq Ñan made it possible for **~100,000 Incas to govern 10–12 million people** without writing,
markets, money, or the wheel — exchange ran on **state redistribution** from the storehouses, moved on
**human and llama backs** along the roads. After the conquest the Spanish used the same roads to march on
Cuzco, but the relay-and-storehouse system that gave them meaning decayed, and the provincial centres —
"artificial" all along — fell empty. The Qhapaq Ñan stands beside the [[khipu|khipu]] and the
[[split-inheritance-and-royal-mummies|mummy-driven expansion]] as one of the distinctive engines of the
Inca state.
