# Susa

The great lowland city of southwestern Iran (modern **Shush**, Khuzistan), occupied for
**~4,500 years** — the principal urban centre of [[elam|Elamite]] civilization, a capital
of the [[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid Empire]], a Hellenistic *polis*, and a Sasanian and
early-Islamic town. Biblical **Shushan** (identified by Benjamin of Tudela in the 12th
century, confirmed by W.K. Loftus's 1852 excavations). Its mounds — **Acropole, Apadana,
Ville Royale, Ville des Artisans** — hold one of the deepest stratified records in the
Near East. ([[potts-archaeology-of-elam-2016|Potts 2016]] is the source for the
archaeological sequence below.)

## The deep sequence (per Potts)

- **Susa I (c. 4200–3800 BCE).** Founded on the Acropole; early on a *massif funéraire*
  held ~2,000 burials, famous for **tall painted ceramic beakers**. Then the **haute
  terrasse** — a stepped platform ~10 m high with a ~5,000 m² summit that "absolutely
  dwarfs" contemporary Mesopotamian temple platforms. 261 stamp seals attest early
  administration.
- **Susa II (c. 3800–3100 BCE) — the Uruk horizon.** Material culture becomes nearly
  identical to southern Mesopotamia (bevel-rimmed bowls, numerical tablets, the
  "priest-king" iconography). Potts reads this as **infiltration of Mesopotamian
  farmers/craftsmen, not political takeover** (only 3 of 13 Uruk numerical systems appear).
- **Susa III (c. 3100–2900 BCE) — [[proto-elamite|Proto-Elamite]].** A sharp rupture: new
  ceramics, abandonment of the haute terrasse, and ~1,557 undeciphered **Proto-Elamite
  tablets**. Potts argues this is **not** demonstrably ancestral to historical Elam.
- **Susa IV (c. 2600–2100 BCE).** Early Dynastic then **Akkadian** styles dominate; under
  [[naram-sin|Naram-Sin]] the site is a royal building centre, reaching ~46 ha. Indus,
  Oman, and Central Asian imports show trade running freely beneath imperial politics.
- **Sukkalmah / Old Babylonian Susa (c. 2000–1500 BCE).** A large archive of **Akkadian
  legal and commercial tablets** — heavily **Akkadized** practice (river ordeal, oaths by
  Inshushinak, the curriculum including the Code of [[hammurabi|Hammurabi]]) — even as the
  rulers bear purely Elamite names.
- **Middle Elamite Susa (c. 1500–1100 BCE).** Great temple-building by
  [[untash-napirisha|Untash-Napirisha]] and the [[shutruk-nahhunte-i|Shutrukids]], who
  filled the city with **war trophies** — the steles of [[naram-sin|Naram-Sin]] and
  [[hammurabi|Hammurabi]], the Manishtushu obelisk, the Marduk statue — which is **why
  those monuments were excavated at Susa**. Home of the bronze **statue of Napir-Asu** and
  the **Sit Shamshi**.
- **Neo-Elamite Susa, and 646 BCE.** Royal centre again until
  [[ashurbanipal|Assurbanipal's]] **[[sack-of-susa-646bce|systematic destruction in 646
  BCE]]**; a reduced government revived afterwards.
- **Achaemenid Susa (c. 539–330 BCE).** [[darius-i|Darius I]] built his **palace and
  apadana** here (the glazed-brick archer and lion friezes; the DSf materials inscription)
  on a 13-ha platform; Susa became an imperial capital, the satrapal seat of "Kissia," and
  the western terminus of the **Royal Road**. [[alexander-the-great|Alexander]] seized
  ~50,000 talents of silver here in 331 BCE.
- **Seleucia-on-the-Eulaeus and after.** Refounded as a Greek *polis* (with *boule*,
  archons, a Nanaya temple, ~50 Greek inscriptions); under the [[parthian-empire|Parthians]]
  it was contested with [[elymais|Elymais]]; destroyed by **Shapur II ~339 CE** and
  re-christened *Shush i er-kar* ("Susa made Iranian"), yet a Nestorian bishopric into the
  7th century.

## Why it mattered

Susa sat at the hinge of the **Mesopotamian lowland and the Iranian plateau** — the lowland
half of the "Anshan and Susa" formula of [[elam|Elamite]] kingship, then a Persian imperial
capital and a node of long-distance trade. Its continuous occupation makes it the single
most important archaeological key to four millennia of southwestern Iranian history.
