## Geographic Description

Vanuatu is a Y-shaped archipelago of approximately 80 islands in the south-western Pacific (Melanesia), lying between the Solomon Islands and New Caledonia. The main islands include Efate (capital Port Vila), Espiritu Santo, Malekula, Ambrym, Tanna, and Ambae. The island group has exceptional linguistic diversity — Vanuatu has the highest density of languages per capita in the world — and significant cultural diversity between islands.

Tanna is among the most prominent islands in [[denoon-cambridge-pacific-islanders-1997]]'s account, as the home of the John Frum movement and a paradigm case of kastom politics.

## History of Control and Settlement

**Pre-colonial:** Organised through complex local chieftainship, Big Man systems, and kula-like exchange networks. Kastom practices including kava drinking, pig-exchange ceremonies, and ritual music were central to social life.

**Colonial (Anglo-French Condominium, 1906–1980):** Unique "condominium" governance by Britain and France created competing political cultures: British-educated mission-affiliated groups eventually formed the Vanua'aku Pati (led by Walter Lini, Donald Kalpokas, and others), while francophone Islanders and French colons formed the "Moderates." The condominium's cultural division shaped post-independence politics.

**Independence (1980):** The Vanua'aku Pati's electoral dominance precipitated a minor revolt by John Frum supporters on Tanna and Nagriamel Movement supporters on Espiritu Santo (led by Jimmy Moli Stevens), attracted assistance from French colons and the Phoenix Foundation. Papua New Guinea troops assisted the government in suppressing the Espiritu Santo secession.

**Post-independence:** "Vanuatu's constitution declares the state to be 'founded on traditional Melanesian values'" and established a National Council of Chiefs (Malvatu Mauri), charged with advising on kastom. Bislama (a local Pidgin), string-band music, and kava became national cultural markers. President Fred Timakata (elected 1989) pledged to strengthen village leaders' powers. In 1991 the Moderates emerged as the leading coalition partner, ending Vanua'aku's dominance.

## Why Vanuatu Mattered

- **Kastom paradigm case:** Vanuatu is the site of the most extensive discussion of [[wiki/concepts/kastom|kastom]] in [[denoon-cambridge-pacific-islanders-1997]]. The Vanua'aku government "adopted a composite of customary (kastom) cultural symbols as emblematic of the nation — slit-gong drums, pigs' teeth, leaves." The tension between kastom as national adhesive and kastom as secessionist idiom (Tanna, Espiritu Santo) played out acutely here.
- **John Frum Movement:** Tanna's John Frum movement — which began circa 1940 as a millenarian resistance to missionary control, predicted American arrival, and revived kava, dance, and exchange — evolved into a durable political party fielding candidates in every election since 1975. About half of Tanna's population actively supports it. The movement institutionalised in the 1950s: "They adopted symbols from their wartime experience — American flags, red crosses, military uniforms and drill."
- **Tree and canoe identity:** Bonnemaison's analysis of Tannese identity — "man is the tree taking root, while the local group is the canoe exploring the world along established 'roads'" — provides the key metaphor for Pacific mobility-and-rootedness that structures Chapter 13 of [[denoon-cambridge-pacific-islanders-1997]].
- **Linguistic diversity:** Papua New Guinea's parliament building was criticised as "Sepik-centric"; in Vanuatu the national symbol-selection debate was fought out differently, through the Chief's Council framework.
