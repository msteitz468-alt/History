# Sijilmasa

**Sijilmasa** was a **caravan city of the Tafilalt oasis** in south-eastern Morocco, the northern terminus of
the western **[[trans-saharan-trade|trans-Saharan gold trade]]** and the place where the hidden Ismaili Imam
[[al-mahdi-billah|al-Mahdi]] sheltered — and was rescued — on the eve of the [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] rise.

## The Imam's refuge

Fleeing Abbasid pursuit from Salamiyya, al-Mahdi and his young son reached the "flourishing trade centre" of
Sijilmasa in **905**, living there in contact with the da'i [[abu-abd-allah-al-shii|Abu Abd Allah al-Shi'i]]
while the [[berbers|Kutama]] movement grew. In 909, as the Kutama were about to take [[qayrawan|Qayrawan]], the
local rulers (alerted by the [[aghlabid-dynasty|Aghlabids]]) placed the Imam under **arrest**; the Kutama
armies marched ~1,000 km west, freed him, and escorted him back to Ifriqiya, where he was proclaimed. His
public **manifestation (*zuhur*)** outside the walls of Sijilmasa on 26 August 909 is the symbolic birth of the
Fatimid state (see [[foundation-of-the-fatimid-caliphate-909]]).

## Significance

Sijilmasa's role illustrates the reach of the clandestine da'wa and the **gold-trade geography** that connected
the Fatimids' North African base to sub-Saharan Africa — the bullion route that would later underpin the famed
purity of the Fatimid dinar.
