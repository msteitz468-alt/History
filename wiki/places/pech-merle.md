# Pech Merle

**Pech Merle** is a major decorated cave near Cabrerets, France, illustrating the range of subjects, styles, and intentional layout in Upper Palaeolithic cave art according to Leroi-Gourhan's analysis. The underground passages extend for about 2 km, with about 150 m containing paintings. The original entrance is thought to have been in the back of the Le Combel chamber.

## Art and Layout (per Leroi-Gourhan)

Leroi-Gourhan recognizes two successive sanctuaries. The earlier is characterized by quadrangular signs (red dots in recesses of Le Combel) and includes a panel of horses in early style III, with side panels featuring a red hand and paired ibex and ox.

The later sanctuary (still style III) has a very large panel of black outline figures of mammoth, bison, ox, and horse, with pairings identified as ox/horse/mammoth, bison/horse/mammoth, and bison/mammoth. A scraped ceiling of some 40 m² in soft clay reveals superimposed representations including a mammoth and schematically drawn female figures.

"In the same side chamber as the ox and ibex we find the brace sign, interpreted as a female symbol, and the wounded man. This combination of signs is repeated at the cave of Cougnac..." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~1630-1650)

Footprints in the clay floor have also been found.

## Broader Context of Cave Art

The cave exemplifies how art was placed in hidden underground chambers for possible rites (e.g., initiation into full hunter status) as well as in daylight areas for common ritual and group ceremonies. Paintings provided a permanent symbol in the landscape integrating dispersed populations.

"Paintings and sculpted friezes also occur in the daylight areas of caves and rock shelters and most probably provided a backdrop to common ritual and ceremonies associated with visiting and group aggregation (Conkey, 1980). These sites provided a means of integrating widely dispersed populations by giving them a common focus, a permanent symbol in the landscape." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~1510-1520)

Leroi-Gourhan's thesis emphasizes logical and repeated patterns in subject grouping and male/female symbol pairing.
