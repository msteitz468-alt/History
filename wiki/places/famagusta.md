# Famagusta

**Famagusta** (Greek *Ammochostos*; medieval French *Famagouste*) was the chief port and
commercial capital of **[[kingdom-of-cyprus|Lusignan Cyprus]]**, on the east coast facing
the Levant.

## Lusignan emporium

After the fall of the mainland crusader ports — especially [[fall-of-acre-1291|Acre
(1291)]] — Famagusta became the primary Latin emporium of the eastern Mediterranean under
the papal ban on direct trade with Mamluk lands. Goods from Cilicia and Syria funnelled
through its harbour; Henry II fortified it (papal indulgences 1308); kings of Cyprus took
their Jerusalem crowns there (precedent for Hugh IV, Peter I, Peter II). Genoese privileges
dated from the Ibelin alliance of June 1232 (night seizure of Famagusta in the War of the
Lombards). Mid-fourteenth-century route shifts toward Alexandria and the Black Death eroded
its peak prosperity before the 1365 war and 1373 invasion.

## Genoese prize (1373–1464)

The **[[genoese-invasion-of-cyprus-1373|Genoese invasion]]** was sparked by a riot at Peter
II's Jerusalem coronation (October 1372) over the ceremonial bridle privilege. Genoa seized
the harbour castle by treachery, sacked the city, and held it first as security for indemnity,
then in sovereignty from 1383. Venetian subjects were banned from the port 1374–78; Nicolo
da Martoni in the 1390s found Famagusta in ruinous decay. Genoese occupation lasted until
1464; Venice later took the whole island (1489); the Ottomans captured Famagusta in 1571,
ending 380 years of western orbit opened by Richard I in 1191.
