# Barygaza

## Geography and Control

A major **northwest Indian port** (modern Bharuch, on the Gujarat coast at the Narmada
mouth), ringed by fertile land growing wheat, rice, sesame, and cotton. In the mid-first
century CE the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* names its ruler as **Nahapana**, an
Indo-Scythian (or Indo-Parthian) king of the Western Satraps. The *Periplus* also notes
surviving **Greek-style shrines, fort walls, and great wells** and continued use of
Indo-Greek coinage — the lingering legacy of the Greco-Bactrian world.

## Why It Mattered

Barygaza was the **western sea-terminus of a trans-Kushan land-river route**: Chinese silk
travelled overland from [[chang-an|Chang'an]] through the [[kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]] and
down the Indus, then to Barygaza for shipment to Roman Egypt. Conversely, Roman glass
landed here and went ~2,000 km north to Kushan [[begram|Begram]]. The *Periplus* records a
detailed trade profile:
- **imports**: Italian wine, copper, tin, lead, coral, topaz, flint glass, gold and silver
  coin;
- **exports**: ivory, agate, cotton and silk cloth, pepper, **lapis lazuli** (Kushan
  Badakhshan), **turquoise** ([[parthian-empire|Parthian]] Nishapur).

Ships left Barygaza in July to catch the monsoon — "more dangerous then, but... the voyage
is more direct, and sooner completed." The *Periplus* mention of "the very warlike people
of the Bactrians, under a king" near Barygaza must refer to [[kujula-kadphises|Kujula
Kadphises]].

## Significance

Barygaza shows the **seam** where the overland [[silk-roads|Silk Roads]] met the
[[indian-ocean-trade|Indian Ocean]] network, binding Han silk-weavers, Kushan
intermediaries, and Roman consumers into one chain.
