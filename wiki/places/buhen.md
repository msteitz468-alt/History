# Buhen

A major Middle Kingdom Egyptian **fortress** on the west bank of the Nile at the Second
Cataract in Lower Nubia, the type-site for the "plains-type" Nubian fortress and a key
exhibit in Kemp's account of the state extending its [[concepts/egyptian-bureaucracy|bureaucratic]],
[[concepts/provider-state|provisioning logic]] to conquered territory. (The standing
remains were lost beneath Lake Nasser after the Aswan High Dam.)

## The Fortress

In [[sources/kemp-ancient-egypt-anatomy-2018|Kemp's *Anatomy of a Civilization*]] (Ch. 5),
Buhen — in existence by year 5 of Senusret I — has a **citadel of ~150 × 138 m** behind a
mud-brick enclosure wall 5 m thick and 8–9 m high with external towers, a brick-paved
rampart pierced by triple-grouped archer loopholes, and a rock-cut dry ditch 7.3 m wide and
3.1 m deep. An outer line enclosed ~420 × 150 m, with salients, a second ditch, and a
causeway at the main western gate. The sophistication "designed to thwart a fairly
sophisticated type of siege" may have been transferred from First Intermediate Period
Egyptian civil-war fortification rather than developed against Nubians.

## A Provisioned Community, Not Just a Garrison

Kemp's central point is logistical: the Second Cataract forts were **provisioning nodes**.
The island fort of **Askut** was effectively a fortified grain store — its granary fills so
much of the interior that supply, not defence, looks primary. Granary-capacity figures far
exceed plausible garrisons (Reisner estimated 50–300 men per fort), implying support for
patrols, **Medjay** auxiliaries, trade and campaign logistics. The **Semna Despatches**
(reign of Amenemhat III) record regular written reporting to Thebes, systematic
desert-edge patrols with Medjay trackers ("We have found a track of 32 men and 3
donkeys"), and regulated cross-frontier trade. Senusret III's **Semna boundary stela**
(year 16) fixed the Second Cataract as the permanent southern limit — "as for whoever
shall abandon it... he is no son of mine."
