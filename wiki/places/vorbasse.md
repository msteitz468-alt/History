# Vorbasse

**Vorbasse** (central Jutland) is [[wickham-framing-the-early-middle-ages-2005|Chris Wickham]]’s **type-site for the northern village group**—“the best village site for Denmark in our period and one of the most significant early medieval village excavations yet conducted.”

## Settlement form and continuity

The village shows continuous identity since the first century BC, with regular **short-distance site shifts** (nine different sites) inside the same agricultural territory rather than abandonment of the territory itself. Plan form is a chessboard of fenced rectangular farm complexes with long-houses (habitation plus byre). From the third century until the eighth, one farm complex in each village phase was rather larger—interpreted as a village-leader farmstead. Structural shifts around **~200** and **~700** do **not** align with the Migration-period fifth century or the Viking ninth century—an important caution against reading village archaeology as a simple mirror of ethnic or political periodizations.

## Social interpretation

Overall the evidence points to a network of **prosperous peasant cultivators** with limited imported goods before the eighth century. Wickham stresses there is **no evidence that Danish villages like Vorbasse were under anyone’s direct domination**, either external or internal. Elite hierarchy and state-formation (Gudme, Ribe, Danevirke, Godofrid’s kingdom) sit **outside** this village level—see [[denmark]]. Analogous farmstead-village patterns appear at Nørre Snede and in Frisian/Saxon regions.

## Why it mattered

Vorbasse anchors Wickham’s argument that northern European “free” village society could be materially coherent, long-lived, and relatively autonomous even while supra-village elites and eventual kingdoms formed. It is the rural counterpart to Denmark’s archaeological richness and a foil to more aristocratically dominated landscapes (e.g. Île-de-France in [[francia]]).
