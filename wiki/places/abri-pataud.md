# Abri Pataud

**Abri Pataud** is a classic rock shelter site above the modern village of Les Eyzies in the Dordogne, France. It provides detailed evidence of Upper Palaeolithic site organization, particularly hearth arrangements and internal spatial patterning in a Perigordian level.

## Description and Excavation

Level 3 at the site contained a row of five hearths with centres spaced some 2 m apart running parallel to the shelter wall. The excavator (Movius 1966) interpreted this as the remains of a long-house with central hearths positioned between the back wall and collapsed roof blocks.

However, the spacing between hearths would have been sufficient for sleeping areas, and a more probable interpretation is the remains left by a small hunting party operating away from their base camp. The substantial size of the hearths indicates repeated re-use.

"The arrangements of hearths in palaeolithic sites have often been interpreted as evidence for huts since it is thought that only an intentional construction could produce such clear patterning as we have seen, for example, at the Abri Pataud." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~880-910)

" In level 3 at this site were found a row of five hearths, their centres spaced some 2 m apart and running parallel to the shelter wall (Fig. 3.7). The excavator, Movius (1966), interpreted this arrangement as the remains of a long-house..." (Champion et al. 1984, Ch. 3 / range lines ~830-850)

## Features and Artifacts

- Engraved stone slabs found around the hearths, including one bas-relief female carving in the 'venus figurine' tradition.
- Assemblage of some 1900 flint artifacts.
- Classified as a phase of the upper Perigordian, dated by ¹⁴C to 23,010 ± 170 BP.

The hearths contained quantities of ash and heat-cracked pebbles presumably placed in the fire to retain heat.

## Interpretation in Settlement Studies

This site illustrates the difficulty of distinguishing intentional structures from patterning produced by repeated use of space (e.g., positioning to exploit sun and fire-warmed shelter walls) without assuming roofed structures. Ethnoarchaeological comparisons (e.g., Big Elephant Cave, Erongo Mountains) show similar regular spacing of hearths and sleeping hollows determined by human body size and heat retention.
