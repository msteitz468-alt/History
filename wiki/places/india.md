# India

The Indian subcontinent: cradle of one of the world's oldest continuous civilizations, birthplace of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism, and site of successive large empires and rich cultural synthesis. Long a center of trade (spices, textiles, gems) linking the Indian Ocean world.

## Significance
- Indus Valley urbanism (Harappa/Mohenjo-daro)
- Vedic society and the composition of foundational texts
- Mauryan and Gupta imperial peaks with extraordinary artistic, scientific, and religious output
- Medieval Islamic sultanates and the Mughal Empire
- European trading companies, British colonial rule, and the independence movement

## The European Companies in Mughal India

For the early modern encounter with the trading companies, see [[surat]] (the
premier Mughal port and first company foothold), [[bombay]] (the slowly-rising EIC
headquarters), [[bengal]] (where company trade rested on provincial diplomacy), and
[[surat-merchant-brokerage]] (the Indian intermediaries and financiers the companies
depended on). [[clulow-mostert-dutch-english-east-india-companies-2018|Clulow & Mostert (2018)]]
stress that the [[english-east-india-company|EIC]] and [[dutch-east-india-company|VOC]]
were inserted into — and dependent on — a sophisticated, well-capitalised Indian
commercial world long before they became territorial powers.
