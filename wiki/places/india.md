# India

The Indian subcontinent: cradle of one of the world's oldest continuous civilizations, birthplace of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism, and site of successive large empires and rich cultural synthesis. Long a center of trade (spices, textiles, gems) linking the Indian Ocean world.

## Significance
- Indus Valley urbanism (Harappa/Mohenjo-daro)
- Vedic society and the composition of foundational texts
- Mauryan and Gupta imperial peaks with extraordinary artistic, scientific, and religious output
- Medieval Islamic sultanates and the Mughal Empire
- European trading companies, British colonial rule, and the independence movement

## Physical Geography and Its Impact on History (Avari 2007)

Geography plays a crucial part in shaping India's history. The subcontinent presents four main landscape profiles:

**1. The northern mountain chain:** Stretching from the Sulaiman, Hindu Kush and Chitral valley in the northwest to the Karakorams and the Himalayas in the north-centre. Despite appearing impenetrable, the chain contains numerous mountain passes — most famously the Bolan, Gomal and Khyber passes in the northwest. It was through these passes that the earliest humans from Africa, and later Indo-European waves including the Aryans, entered the subcontinent. The northeastern mountains, with far fewer passes, acted as a barrier between India and China. Buddhism spread from India into Central Asia via northwestern passes.

**2. The three great river systems:** The Indus, the Brahmaputra, and the Ganga–Yamuna. "It is in the vast plains and river valleys of these three great northern river systems... that the greatest concentration of the sub-continent's population lived, farmed and built cities in the ancient period." Farming first began in the fertile areas of Baluchistan and the Indus region. The first Indian civilisation, the Harappan, developed there. The Ganga–Yamuna plain was cleared for farming by the Aryans during the second millennium BCE, and it became the heartland of Vedic culture.

**3. The Deccan plateau and south:** South of the Vindhya Mountains lies the relatively inhospitable Deccan plateau, in the rain shadow of the monsoon. Its rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Tungabhadra, Penner, Kaveri) are poor in flow compared with northern rivers. The southern peoples practiced irrigation from the earliest times. Commerce — both trans-Indian and international — gave the Deccan its importance. Far in the deep south the ancient kingdoms of Kerala and Tamil peoples flourished through irrigation and international trade.

**4. The coastline:** Extensive (Makran coast in the west to the Bangladesh delta), allowing ports on the western coast to receive traders from Mesopotamia, Arabia, Persia, Egypt, Ethiopia, the Roman Empire and East Africa. East coast trade linked India with Southeast Asia. "In the ancient world India was the hub of the sea routes to everywhere."

**Etymology of "India":** The term's etymological roots lie in the Sanskrit word *sindhu* (river frontier). The earliest Vedic texts call the Punjab region *Sapta-Sindhava* (land of seven rivers). Persians in the sixth century BCE called the region *Hindhu*; the Macedonians under Alexander (fourth century BCE) used the Greek *Indos* for the river and *India* for the land. No ancient Indian would have used this term — they used *Bharat*, *Madhyadesha*, *Aryavarta*, or *Jambudvipa*. Even today India's constitution recognises the country as "India that is Bharat."

**Periodisation:** Avari defines "ancient India" as running from approximately 7000 BCE (first farming community at Mehrgarh in Baluchistan) to approximately CE 1200, when Islamic and West European civilisations began to exert intense pressure on Indian autonomy. Before CE 1200 the Indian civilisation was "a composite civilisation, but one that was deeply anchored in its native soil."

**Religious geography:** Sacred geography has deeply permeated Hindu religion. Sacred mountains include the Himalayas (abode of gods). Sacred rivers include the Ganga above all. Sacred cities include the six northern holy cities (Ayodhya, Mathura, Hardwar, Kashi/Varanasi, Prayag/Allahabad, Dwarka) and three southern ones (Madurai, Kanchipuram, Rameshwaram).
