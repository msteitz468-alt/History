# Avebury

Avebury, in Wiltshire, England, is the largest prehistoric stone circle in Europe — a massive Late Neolithic henge enclosing two smaller circles, linked by stone avenues to nearby monuments (the Sanctuary, West Kennet Avenue) within a wider ceremonial landscape that includes Silbury Hill and the West Kennet long barrow. Built and elaborated roughly in the third millennium BCE, it ranks with [[places/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] among Britain's premier megalithic sites.

For the modern history of the wiki's [[processes/druid-revival|Druid Revival]] it matters chiefly through its reception: [[actors/aubrey-john|John Aubrey]] first identified it as a major native British monument, and [[actors/stukeley-william|William Stukeley]] surveyed it intensively, publishing *Abury* (1743) and interpreting the complex — its great circle and serpentine avenues — as a Druidic "temple of the serpent." Stukeley's records are also a priceless source for stones destroyed by villagers in the eighteenth century. Avebury later became, with Stonehenge, a focus of modern Druid and countercultural gatherings.
