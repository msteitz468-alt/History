# Normandy

Historic region of northwestern France, site of the Allied invasion of 6 June 1944 (Operation Overlord) and the subsequent eighty-day campaign that liberated much of France.

## Geographic Description

Coastal plain with five main invasion beaches (Omaha, Utah, Gold, Juno, Sword). Inland: bocage (small fields enclosed by dense hedgerows and sunken lanes) extremely favorable to defenders; Caen and the Caen-Falaise Plain to the east offered better tank country once breached. Key ports and rivers (Orne, Vire) influenced operations.

## History of Control and Settlement

Occupied by Germany after 1940. Fortified as part of the Atlantic Wall. Liberated by the Allies in summer 1944. Heavy fighting devastated towns and countryside (Caen especially).

## Why This Place Mattered

The chosen site for the decisive cross-Channel invasion that opened the Western Front. Its terrain dictated the character of the campaign: initial beachhead fighting followed by brutal hedgerow attrition, then breakout and encirclement. The failure or success around Caen was central to the entire Allied strategy in Normandy.

## Significance Over Time

Symbol of the liberation of Western Europe and the largest amphibious operation ever mounted. Major battlefield tourism and commemoration sites today (cemeteries, museums, D-Day beaches).
