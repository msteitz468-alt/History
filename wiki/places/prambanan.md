# Prambanan (Loro Jonggrang)

**Prambanan** (Loro Jonggrang) is the major Early Classic **Hindu** temple complex of the central Javanese plain, consecrated in **856 CE** near the modern village of Prambanan. It is conventionally read as the Sanjaya-line monumental culmination of [[mataram-kingdom|Mataram]] architecture after the Buddhist programme associated with the [[sailendra|Sailendra]] and [[borobudur|Borobudur]]. The complex may originally have been named **Laṅkāpura**. Primary synthesis: [[miksic-goh-ancient-southeast-asia-2017|Miksic & Goh 2017]].

## Geographic Description

The complex sits on the fertile plain east of modern Yogyakarta, within a few kilometres of the Wonoboyo royal-hermitage hoard and within the Merapi volcanic risk zone that buried parts of the Early Classic landscape under sand. Layout: **224 chapels** around a central courtyard; in the courtyard, temples dedicated to **Brahmā** and **Viṣṇu**, with **Śiva** in the middle. The Śiva temple’s subsidiary shrines house Agastya, Gaṇeśa, and Durgā. A central subsidiary structure type places a Nandi flanked by Sūrya and Candra—a placement later echoed at sites such as Ngurah.

## History of Control and Consecration

By **842** the Raka of Pikatan, a Hindu of the Sanjaya line, was supreme ruler of central Java. An inscription of **856** describes consecration of an “abode of Śiva,” identified with the Prambanan group. The Hindu king who founded the complex also contributed to **Buddhist** shrines together with his Buddhist queen (e.g. Plaosan patronage)—evidence of dual sponsorship rather than total religious replacement.

Miksic and Goh treat Prambanan as the **culmination** of Early Classic Javanese architecture; thereafter inscriptional light in central Java dims, ending by **919**, with the political centre in east Java after **929**. Volcanic burial of nearby elite sites (Wonoboyo under Merapi sand ~900) supports environmental disaster as *part* of the end of the central Java temple age—not a single proven cause (see Mataram).

## Narrative Reliefs and Why It Mattered

Balustrade reliefs present the **Rāmāyaṇa** (Śiva and Brahmā temples) and **Kṛṣṇa** legends (Viṣṇu temple). This Rāmāyaṇa programme inspired reliefs at [[angkor|Angkor Wat]] and the Bayon **250–300 years later**; in Java, Rāmāyaṇa reliefs reappear in the fourteenth century. Together with Borobudur, Prambanan established the Early Classic standard of long narrative galleries; **Candi Jago** (~1280) revived that tradition in a new Late Classic low-relief, flat-perspective, distorted-figure style under [[singhasari|Singhasari]]/early Majapahit patronage.

Prambanan thus mattered as:

- a royal statement of Śaiva (and broader Hindu) cosmology after a Buddhist monumental generation;
- a workshop of narrative art that became a regional model;
- the architectural climax immediately **preceding** the political-ecological rupture of central Java Mataram.

## Religion: Distinct, Not Fused

Despite artistic similarity and occasional dual royal commemoration, Hariani Santiko argues Hinduism and Buddhism remained **officially and doctrinally distinct**. One thirteenth-century king had himself commemorated as both Śiva and Buddha in the same temple, but Late Classic inscriptions still distinguish Buddhists, Hindus, and *ṛṣi*. Prambanan’s Hindu programme should not be collapsed into a generic “Hindu-Buddhist syncretism” without qualification.

## Control Afterlife

After the eastward shift, the plain’s temples lost court maintenance; earthquakes and volcanic activity damaged structures; modern reconstruction has made Prambanan a flagship heritage site. Art-historical influence outlasted political control: east Javanese and Khmer programmes cite its relief language; subsidiary statue placement systems travel as far as later island temples.

## Historiography and Source Notes

Dating rests on the 856 consecration inscription and stylistic sequence relative to Sailendra monuments. Original name (Laṅkāpura) is conjectural. Identification of the 856 “abode of Śiva” with this specific complex is widely accepted but still an identification argument. Debates over “syncretism” vs doctrinal distinction (Santiko) affect how the dual patronage of Pikatan’s generation is read.
