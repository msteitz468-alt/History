# Cholula

## Overview

**Cholula** (Puebla) is one of Mesoamerica's longest-lived ceremonial cities, home to the
**Great Pyramid** (Tlachihualtepetl) — among the largest monuments by volume in the ancient
world — and a major pilgrimage and political center from the Formative through the
[[aztec-empire|Aztec]] period into the Spanish conquest.

## Teotihuacan connection and Epiclassic transition (Cowgill CHNPA 2000)

Cholula was an important **Teotihuacan-related** settlement, but its political status is a
“major puzzle” (Cowgill 2000). More than any other central Mexican center, it **may have
spanned the transition** between Teotihuacan-period and Epiclassic landscapes, yet remains
enigmatic (McCafferty 1996). Later traditions may preserve some Teotihuacan-tradition
persistence at Cholula after the collapse of the metropolis (possible survivor/cultural
continuities noted by Cowgill).

## Post-Tula settlement geography (Charlton CHNPA)

Charlton notes an alternative archaeological reading in which apparent northern Basin /
Mezquital depopulation after Tula’s fall marks a real shift whose **center of gravity was
Cholula** southeast of the Basin — ending a long-standing Tula–Cholula enmity narrative —
with Early Aztec I ceramic complex concentrated in the southern Basin and eastern Morelos
and strong Cholula links, versus Aztec II in the northern two-thirds of the Basin. By A.D.
1519 Cholula remained among independent eastern city-states encircled by Triple Alliance
pressure (with Huejotzingo, Tlaxcala, etc.).

## Evans & Webster 2001 (McCafferty; Plunket & Uruñuela)

Geoffrey G. McCafferty’s encyclopedia entry and the regional synthesis by Patricia Plunket Nagoda and Gabriela Uruñuela ([[evans-webster-archaeology-ancient-mexico-central-america-2001|Evans & Webster 2001]]) supply the quantitative urban and pyramid sequence:

- Continuously occupied **>3,000 years** as a major religious center in the irrigated Puebla–Tlaxcala Valley at a trade-route intersection; Postclassic *pochteca* merchant center.
- **Great Pyramid (Tlachihualtepetl)** — largest pre-Hispanic New World structure by **volume** of construction material; final form ~**16 ha**, **~66 m** high.
- Stage 1 Terminal Formative ~**120 × 17 m** with Teotihuacan affinities; Classic rebuilds include Stage 3 **180 × 30 m** (non-Teotihuacan stepped style) and Stage 10 ~**330 × 60 m** talud/tablero.
- Urban grid oriented ~**26° north of west** (summer solstice sunset) — unlike Teotihuacan.
- Classic city ~**5 km²**, **15,000–20,000** people; McCafferty stresses **no archaeological Classic collapse** — building continued into Early Postclassic. Plunket & Uruñuela instead emphasize decline from ~**A.D. 550**, building cessation ~**600**, and possible flight to Cerro Zapotecas — a tension between the site entry and the regional overview.
- By A.D. **500** (Plunket & Uruñuela) Cholula as Early Classic regional capital covered ~**10 km²** during major pyramid construction.
- Postclassic maximum ~**10 km²**, **30,000–50,000** people; Tlachihualtepetl phase (A.D. **700–1200**) as highland Olmeca-Xicallanca center with Gulf Coast/Mixteca ties and early Mixteca-Puebla style.
- Early Postclassic resurgence under Olmeca-Xicalanca (Cholulteca I–II, ~**850–1200**); Late Postclassic Tolteca-Chichimeca arrivals; Quetzalcoatl pilgrimage/market cult; Codex Borgia tradition linked to Cholula/Tlaxcala.
- Mid–late 15th c. Triple Alliance pressure and **flowery wars**; 1519 Cholula massacred as Aztec ally after Cortés’s alliance with Tlaxcalan rulers Xicotencatl and Maxixcatzin.

## Why it mattered

Cholula’s longevity makes it a critical (if still poorly resolved) hinge between Classic
Teotihuacan-era central Mexico, the Epiclassic multi-center world, and Postclassic pilgrimage
and political networks down to the conquest. EW2001 anchors the Great Pyramid’s volumetric primacy and the long Formative–Contact urban sequence within [[puebla-tlaxcala]].
