# Ulaid (Ulster)

One of the traditional provinces; in early Irish literature the territory of the Ulaid and setting for the Ulster Cycle (Táin etc.). Historic power centre in the north; later fragmented.

## History of Control and Settlement
Vol V Contents: "Ulaid 1706"; "Ulster Cycle of Tales 1708". Geographic setting in south-eastern traditional Ulster (Armagh, Down, north Leinster/Meath per related prose).

## Events and Processes Here
Setting for Ulster Cycle: central to Táin Bó Cuailnge and remscéla. "heroes of the Ulaid". Cross to Teamhair/Tara in kingship contexts.

## Historiography
Koch Vol V emphasizes literary centrality of Ulaid/Ulaid Cycle for heroic tradition and early Irish society.

## Claims from Koch (ed.), Celtic Culture Vol. V (range R05-Vol5-02)

- **Ulaid as province and in Ulster Cycle**: "Mide (Meath) was regarded in mythology as the central province (cóiced) of Ireland (Ériu), bordering the other four provinces: Connacht, Laigin, Mumu, and Ulaid." In Mesca Ulad, "this leads to a description of the later division of Ulaid, unusual in that Cú Chulainn and Conchobar are portrayed as Ulster’s rival kings". "in the tales of the Ulster Cycle, often as chief antagonists of the Ulaid and their high-hero Cú Chulainn". (Koch Vol V, Mide entry; Mesca Ulad entry).

- **Ulaid / Teamhair royal ideology**: The Ulaid's royal sites and conflicts illustrate contraction of earlier groups (e.g. Érainn); linked to high-kingship at Tara. "the homonymous royal sites Teamhair (Tara) and Temair Lúachra might also have some bearing on the contraction and displacement of the Érainn." Ulaid appears among provinces in legendary divisions between Tuath Dé and Sons of Míl. (Koch Vol V, Mesca Ulad; Mide).

- **Ulaid in later contexts**: Referenced in medieval and modern political/cultural narratives (e.g. in nationalism entries linking historic provinces to identity).
