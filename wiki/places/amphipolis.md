# Amphipolis

A strategically important Athenian colony founded in 437/6 BCE on the Strymon River in Thrace, controlling access to nearby gold and silver mines and valuable timber for shipbuilding. During the Peloponnesian War the city was seized by the Spartan general Brasidas in 424 BCE; the Athenian commander sent too late to relieve it was the historian [[thucydides|Thucydides]], whose failure to prevent the city's fall led to his exile from Athens — a personal consequence that, by his own account, gave him the access and leisure to research and write his history of the war from both sides.

## Significance

Amphipolis was recaptured by Athens only briefly and remained largely outside Athenian control for the rest of the war, a persistent strategic loss given the site's mineral and timber wealth. The city was again the site of major fighting in 422 BCE, when both Brasidas and the Athenian general Cleon were killed in a second battle there — a mutual removal of the war's two most committed proponents of continued conflict that directly enabled the negotiation of the Peace of Nicias in 421 BCE.
