# Philadelphia

Philadelphia served as the temporary capital of the United States during much of George Washington's presidency (1790–1800) and was the site of Thomas Jefferson's service as the first Secretary of State (1790–1793).

## Description and Role in Period

After the move from New York in 1790, Congress and the executive departments operated in Philadelphia. Jefferson rented a house on Market Street (later High Street). The city was the intellectual and commercial center of the new nation, home to the American Philosophical Society and a lively press.

## Significance for Jefferson and the Early Republic

The Philadelphia years were the crucible of the first party system. Here the cabinet battles between Jefferson and Hamilton over the Bank of the United States, foreign policy, commercial measures, and the meaning of the Constitution played out. The "Storm over the Rights of Man" (Paine pamphlet controversy) and the beginnings of organized Republican opposition occurred here. Jefferson found the social atmosphere of the "court" around Washington and the Federalist circle uncongenial; he returned to Monticello as often as possible.
