## Overview

Carchemish was one of the most strategically important cities of the ancient Near East, situated on the west bank of the Euphrates river at the main crossing point linking Anatolia with northern Syria and Mesopotamia. During the Late Bronze Age it served as a Mitannian stronghold; its conquest by [[shuppiluliuma-i|Suppiluliuma I]] c.1327 BCE was the culminating act of the destruction of the Mitannian empire. Thereafter it became the seat of a Hittite viceroyalty — direct royal rule by Suppiluliuma's son Piyassili (Sharri-Kushuh) and his descendants — and the administrative centre for all Hittite interests in Syria.

## Strategic Importance

Carchemish controlled the key Euphrates ford on the main land route from Hattusa to Syria and Mesopotamia — the route Bryce identifies passing "between Kummanni and the Upper Land and on the main route between Hattusa and Carchemish" via Tegarama (DS). Its position meant that any power wishing to project military force from Anatolia into Syria had to either take Carchemish or bypass it at extreme risk. Conversely, any power wishing to strike at Anatolia from Mesopotamia passed through the same chokepoint. The city was "the Mitannian stronghold" whose survival after Suppiluliuma's great Syrian war of conquest left his control of the region incomplete: "while the Mitannian king and a major centre of Mitannian power remained beyond his grasp, Suppiluliuma's conquests were incomplete" (Bryce, Ch. 7).

## History of Control

**Mitannian Period.** During the ascendancy of [[mitanni]], Carchemish served as a Mitannian stronghold west of the Euphrates, holding even when Suppiluliuma's "one-year" Syrian campaign of c.1327 BCE swept away the kingdoms of Aleppo, Mukish, Niya, Arahtu, Qatna, and Nuhashshi: "Only the Mitannian stronghold Carchemish on the Euphrates remained unsubdued" (Bryce, Ch. 7). Mitannian troops subsequently used Carchemish as a base to besiege the Hittite garrison at Murmuriga and threaten Hittite positions on both sides of the Euphrates.

**Suppiluliuma's Siege (c.1322 BCE).** During the Second Syrian War (or Hurrian War), Suppiluliuma personally led the final operation against Carchemish. His son Telipinu, viceroy at Aleppo, had already subdued "the countries of Arziya and Carchemish — though not the city of Carchemish" (DS) in the preceding campaign season. Suppiluliuma besieged the city for seven days, then took it in a single day of fighting: "He had besieged it for seven days, and on the eighth day he fought a battle against it for one day and took(?) it in a terrific battle" (DS frag. 28 a iii 28–41). Suppiluliuma displayed notable religious scruple in victory: he allowed no plundering of the upper citadel's temples. From the lower town he removed inhabitants, silver, gold, and bronze utensils and transported them to Hattusa.

**Hittite Viceroyalty.** Immediately after the conquest, Suppiluliuma installed his son Piyassili — who adopted the Hurrian throne-name Sharri-Kushuh — as viceroy in Carchemish. The kingdom of Carchemish as delimited in the Shattiwaza Treaty became a large territorial entity spanning both banks of the Euphrates: cities of the Land of Ashtata on both the western and eastern banks were assigned to "my son Piyassili" (Shattiwaza Treaty, PD no. 1, rev. 14–21). The eastern territories served as "a frontier defence zone against Assyrian encroachment across the Euphrates." To the west the kingdom reached the borders of Mukish; southward it incorporated territory along the Euphrates formerly belonging to Ashtata. Sharri-Kushuh appears to have been "generally regarded as the chief representative of Hittite interests in the region," exercising primarily military functions complementing his brother Telipinu's religious and judicial role at Aleppo (Bryce, Ch. 7).

The city of Emar, rebuilt under Hittite direction and inaugurated by Mursili II in the territory of Ashtata, was placed "under the immediate jurisdiction of the viceroy at Carchemish" for its entire history from the fourteenth to the early twelfth centuries BCE.

The Carchemish viceroyalty endured as a major Hittite institution through the rest of the New Kingdom period. After the Bronze Age Collapse (c.1200 BCE) it re-emerged as one of the most important [[neo-hittite-states]], surviving into the Iron Age.

## Significance

Carchemish was the linchpin of the Hittite empire's Syrian system. Its position as a fortified Euphrates crossing made it simultaneously the gateway between Anatolia and Syria, the buffer against Assyrian westward expansion, and the administrative centre for the network of vassal states stretching to Damascus. The appointment of a royal son as hereditary viceroy — unprecedented in Hittite practice — reflected its centrality: direct blood-royal rule rather than a treaty vassal was required to hold Syria.
