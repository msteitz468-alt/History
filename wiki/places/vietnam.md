# Vietnam

Geographic and political entity encompassing the territory of modern Vietnam, historically configured as multiple competing "Vietnams" (northern/Red River, central, southern/Mekong) rather than a single unchanging unit. The name "Vietnam" itself is relatively recent and was not consistently used by the polities that preceded the 20th-century nation-states.

## Geographic Description

Stretches ~1,650 km north–south along the eastern edge of the Indochinese peninsula. Core agricultural zones: Red River delta (north, wet-rice heartland of early Dai Viet states) and Mekong delta (south, later expansion zone). Central highlands and coastal strips add ecological and ethnic diversity. Strategic position astride sea lanes and overland routes between China and maritime Southeast Asia.

## History of Control and Settlement

- Ancient/Chinese domination (111 BCE–938 CE): Part of successive Chinese empires; resistance and cultural adaptation produced early independent identity (see Goscha Ch. 1 on Dong Son and early configurations).
- Independent Dai Viet / Le / Trinh / Nguyen eras: Northern core with southern "marcher" states; civil wars and southward expansion (Nam tiến) into Cham and Khmer territories.
- Nguyen dynasty (1802–1945): First relatively unitary state from Red River to Mekong; imperial projects in highlands and Cambodia/Laos.
- French colonial (1858–1954/55): Conquered in stages; incorporated as Cochinchina (colony), Annam and Tonkin (protectorates) within French Indochina Union (with Cambodia, Laos). See [[processes/french-indochina]].
- 1945–1975: Competing DRV (north, Ho Chi Minh) and RVN (south); partition at 17th parallel after Geneva 1954.
- 1975–present: Socialist Republic of Vietnam after reunification.

## Why It Mattered

Site of repeated contests between Chinese imperial models, indigenous state-building, French colonial "civilizing mission," and 20th-century nationalist/communist revolutions. Multiple Vietnams generated alternative political possibilities (monarchical, colonial republican, communist, anti-communist republican) whose interactions shaped modern outcomes. Post-1975 SRV has become a major regional actor with complex relations to China, ASEAN, and global economy.

## Significance Over Time

Pre-colonial: Model of adaptation of Confucian/bureaucratic forms to Southeast Asian ecology and resistance to northern domination. Colonial: Laboratory of French republican imperialism's contradictions. 20th c.: Archetypal decolonization struggle that tested and transformed Cold War containment. Contemporary: Example of authoritarian resilience combined with market reforms ("socialist-oriented market economy").
