# Liberia (Early Colony)

**Liberia** originated as an American Colonization Society (ACS) settlement on the West African coast at Cape Mesurado (Monrovia), established in the 1820s as a destination for free Black Americans and manumitted people. In the analysis of Blaakman et al., *The Early Imperial Republic* (2023), it exemplifies early U.S. imperial projection via "corridors and enclaves," entangled with the slave trade, British interests, and racial governance experiments.

## Early History and Governance

White ACS agents were nominally in charge, but mortality led to de facto settler self-rule. The 1823–24 "rebellion" over land and provisions produced a 1824 constitution expanding settler rights. The project combined anti-slavery/ benevolent empire rhetoric with colonial structures and was viewed by some Black leaders (Cuffe, Delany) as a platform for independent Black power opposing the slave trade.

## Role in U.S. Empire per Source

Part II of the volume treats Liberia alongside Hawai'i and Caribbean borderlands as sites where nongovernmental actors (ACS, missionaries, traders) extended U.S. influence and ideas of race, civilization, and sovereignty beyond the continent. It was both a supposed solution to the "problem" of free Black people in the U.S. and a node in Atlantic imperial competition.
