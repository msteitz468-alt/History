# Ithaca

**Ithaca**, the small, rocky island kingdom of Odysseus in the Ionian Sea (off the west coast of Greece). In the *Odyssey* it is the ultimate goal of the hero's nostos and the site of the poem's climactic action: the crisis caused by the suitors, the disguised return, the testing of loyalty (Eumaeus the swineherd, the loyal servants), the bow contest in the hall, the killing of the suitors, and the reunion with Penelope.

Geographically described as rugged, with multiple harbors, good for seafaring but not rich in broad plains (contrasting with the more prosperous Pylos or Sparta). It symbolizes home, rooted identity, and the value of the familiar after exotic wanderings and divine temptations (Calypso's offer of immortality).

The palace of Odysseus (with the famous immovable bed) is central. The island's society shows a small-scale heroic community with assembly, elders, and tensions between absent kingship and local power.

## Significance in the Wiki

Ithaca is the domestic and political heart of the *Odyssey*, complementing the war-focused Troy of the *Iliad*. It illustrates Greek ideas of oikos (household) as the center of meaning, xenia, and the restoration of order after chaos.
