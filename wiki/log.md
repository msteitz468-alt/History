## 2026-06-11 section | CAH Vol. VII.2 | Section: Cycle 6 — Religion and Appendices (Ch. 12: North on Republican religion; Appendix: Drummond on early Roman chronology; Consular Fasti 509–220 BCE) | Pages created: 1 | Pages updated: 2

New pages: roman-republican-religion (concept). Updated: roman-republic (Republican Religion section added), walbank source page (counts 28→29 created, 16→18 updated; Volume Synthesis Note written). Key content: North's revisionist methodological framework — rejects all developmental narratives (Warde Fowler animism → polytheism; Latte; Dumézil tripartite ideology); treats system as broadly constant across the Republic; all sources are late Republican/Principate, filtered through Christian polemic. Four priestly colleges: pontifices (widest remit; calendar; family law), augures (auspices; templum; pomerium), fetiales (just war protocol; treaties), decemviri sacris faciundis (Sibylline Books; prodigy remedia). Priests are NOT a separate caste — same nobles who dominate politics. Religious authority diffused across senate + magistrates + colleges; no monopoly possible. Gods as contractual partners (vota are binding formulae); prodigies as disrupted divine-human relationship requiring remedia; sacrifice (*litatio* required). Vestals as the most liminal figures: priests, virgins, fire-keepers, guardians of pignora imperii; pontifex maximus holds special authority over them. Transition from monarchy: rex sacrorum (title preserved, politically castrated; excluded from Senate and all office); Jupiter Capitolinus as continuity with Etruscan-regal ceremonial (triumph = general dressed as Jupiter/king — not rejected but embraced by the Republic). Innovation as normal functioning: Aesculapius 290s, Victoria temple 294 BCE, Greek rites of Ceres (third century with Greek priestesses granted citizenship), lectisternium 399 BCE. Sibylline Books as legitimation mechanism for new cults. Patrician religious monopoly via college co-optation; broken by Lex Ogulnia 300 BCE (plebeians admitted to pontificate and augurate); pontifex maximus election reform probably before Ti. Coruncanius (first plebeian holder, c. 253 BCE). Bacchanalian affair 186 BCE as first sign of new religious age (autonomous religious groups — alien to the entire system). Drummond Appendix: Varronian chronology is a mid-first-century BCE creation (probably Atticus not Varro); incorporates fictitious "dictator-years"; all dates before c. 300 BCE are reconstructed from consular lists that ancient historians already knew were defective. Consular Fasti 509–220 BCE: reference list of consuls year by year, heavily annotated with variants; purely reference material, no new analytical pages created.
