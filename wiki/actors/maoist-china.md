# The People's Republic of China under Mao (1949–1976)

**Maoist China** is the People's Republic in the era of **Mao Zedong's** dominance (1949–76) — the
transformation of China into a socialist one-party state through successive waves of mass campaign,
culminating in the two great upheavals of the **Great Leap Forward** and the **Cultural
Revolution**. From the [[cambridge-history-of-china|Cambridge History of China]], Vols 14–15 (the
PRC to 1982).

## Building the socialist state (1949–57)

After victory in the [[chinese-communist-revolution|civil war]], the CCP consolidated rule with
violent **land reform**, the suppression of opponents, and — on the **Soviet model** — the First
Five-Year Plan and the **collectivization** of agriculture and nationalization of industry. The
brief **Hundred Flowers** opening (1956–57) inviting criticism was followed by the **Anti-Rightist**
crackdown.

## The Maoist deviations (1958–76)

Impatient with the Soviet model, Mao launched two radical, catastrophic experiments:
- The **[[great-leap-forward|Great Leap Forward]] (1958–62)** — forced collectivization into giant
  **people's communes** and a utopian drive for instant industrialization — which produced history's
  deadliest **famine** (tens of millions dead).
- After a partial recovery under more pragmatic leaders, the **[[cultural-revolution|Cultural
  Revolution]] (1966–76)** — Mao's mobilization of youth (the Red Guards) against the Party
  establishment and "capitalist roaders," plunging China into a decade of turmoil, persecution, and
  paralysis.

Externally, the period saw the Korean War, the **[[sino-soviet-split|Sino-Soviet split]]** (from
c. 1960), and the surprise **rapprochement with the United States** (Nixon, 1972).

## End and significance

Mao's death in **1976** and the arrest of the **Gang of Four** ended the radical era; under **Deng
Xiaoping** China turned to "reform and opening" (from 1978), the subject of the close of CHC Vol.
15. Maoist China industrialized and unified the country and raised literacy and basic health, but at
the cost of tens of millions of deaths and vast trauma — a balance sheet still central to debate
about the PRC.

## Historiography

The CHC's PRC volumes (the last, Vol. 15, titled for the "revolutions within the Chinese
revolution") were written close to the events and stress the **internal Party struggles** and the
oscillation between Maoist mass-mobilization and bureaucratic pragmatism. The translators' preface
explicitly flags points where the Western authors' conclusions diverge from PRC orthodoxy.
