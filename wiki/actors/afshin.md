# Al-Afshin (Khaydhar ibn Kawus)

Abbasid general of Central Asian (Ushrusana, in Transoxiana) princely origin who served Caliph al-Mu'tasim as the empire's leading military commander in the 830s. His central achievement was the suppression, after a grinding multi-year campaign (835–837), of the long Khurramite rebellion led by [[babak-khurrami|Babak Khurrami]] in Azerbaijan, ending a revolt that had defied Abbasid armies for two decades.

## Role

Al-Afshin's victory made him the most powerful military figure at al-Mu'tasim's court, but his non-Arab, Central Asian background and continued adherence to elements of his ancestral pre-Islamic religious practice made him a target of suspicion. He fell from favor after the caliph's death, was accused of conspiring against the state and harboring crypto-Zoroastrian or Mazdakite sympathies, and died imprisoned in 841.

## Counterfactual Significance

Al-Afshin's career illustrates the Abbasid state's structural dependence on — and simultaneous distrust of — non-Arab military elites (a pattern that also produced the Turkic ghilman system); his downfall foreshadowed the increasing political fragility of centralized caliphal authority in the ninth century.
