# Cunedda ap Edern

Semi-legendary founder-figure of the Kingdom of Gwynedd in north Wales. According to the 9th-century *Historia Brittonum*, Cunedda migrated south with eight of his sons from Manaw Gododdin (near the Firth of Forth in the "Old North," or Hen Ogledd) some generations before the text's composition, expelling Irish (Déisi) settlers who had established themselves in north Wales and founding a dynasty whose sons' names were later attached to Welsh regional kingdoms (Ceredigion from Ceredig, Meirionnydd from Meirion, and others). Whether this represents a real migration event, a much later invention, or a garbled memory of a smaller-scale movement is disputed; the narrative's chief historical function was to provide Gwynedd's ruling dynasty with a prestigious northern British pedigree distinct from purely local origins.

## Counterfactual Significance

Regardless of its literal truth, the Cunedda foundation-legend shaped Gwynedd's dynastic self-understanding and its claimed links to the wider "Old North" for centuries, reinforcing Gwynedd's later political preeminence among Welsh kingdoms.

## Historiography

Modern scholars (e.g., David Dumville) treat the *Historia Brittonum*'s genealogical material, including the Cunedda story, with considerable skepticism as retrospective dynastic myth-making rather than straightforward historical record, while allowing that some memory of population movement from the Hen Ogledd to Wales in the sub-Roman period may underlie it.
