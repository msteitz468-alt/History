# Charles Sumner

U.S. Senator from Massachusetts, radical Republican, and the most prominent American voice demanding that the European powers treat the Confederacy as a pariah to be shunned rather than a legitimate belligerent.

## "Our Foreign Relations" (1863)

On 10 September 1863, before an enormous crowd at New York's Cooper Union Institute, Sumner delivered a 36,000-word oration titled "Our Foreign Relations." He excoriated Britain (and to a lesser extent France) for granting belligerent rights and allowing the construction of Confederate commerce raiders. He warned that a recognized slaveholding Confederacy would send "Ambassadors of Slavery" to poison every court in Europe and would force civilized nations into an "adulterous honey-moon with Slavery." He called for the maritime powers to unite to drive the Confederate flag from the oceans and to revise international law accordingly.

The speech was printed, translated, and widely circulated as a major statement of the anti-Confederate case at its peak.

## International Law and Pariahdom

Sumner argued that the unique depravity of a state founded explicitly on slavery and threatening to revive the African trade justified exceptional measures. He drew on Edmund Burke's call for isolation of revolutionary France but claimed the Confederate threat ("liberticide") was graver than the Jacobin ("regicide"). Though no formal new doctrine of recognition was adopted, Sumner's campaign contributed to the climate that made full diplomatic recognition of the Confederacy politically impossible in London and Paris.
