# St Birgitta of Sweden

Swedish noblewoman, visionary, and saint (c. 1303–1373), founder of the **Order of
Our Saviour** (Bridgettines/Birgittines) and the most internationally influential
figure of late-medieval Scandinavian religion. Per
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]], she is the
only Scandinavian saint of the charismatic "southern type" (venerated for a holy
life rather than only post-mortem miracles) in an otherwise "cold" saint-producing
region.

## Life and Legacy

Of aristocratic background and mother of eight, Birgitta experienced visions and
composed her *Revelations* after her husband's death in 1344; she moved to Rome in
1349 and addressed confrontational admonitions to popes and kings. Her order's
mother-house at **Vadstena** (buildings consecrated 1384) became the spiritual,
cultural, and at times political centre for all the Nordic kingdoms, spreading to
Denmark (Maribo, Mariager), Finland (Nådendal), and Norway (Munkeliv, Bergen). The
*Cantus sororum* liturgy and the Office of the Virgin count among Sweden's most
original theological contributions.

Birgitta was **canonised in 1391**, with the decisive support of Queen
[[margaret-i-of-denmark|Margaret I]], who combined personal piety (her tutor was
Birgitta's daughter) with political calculation regarding the recent incorporation
of Sweden. See [[christianization-of-scandinavia]].
