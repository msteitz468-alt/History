# Édouard Berth (1875–1939)

[[sorel-georges|Sorel]]'s "closest friend and most faithful disciple" —
"never... anything other than Sorel's spokesman" in the Parisian milieu — and
co-founder, with [[valois-georges|Valois]], of the
[[cercle-proudhon|Cercle Proudhon]] (1911). His January and November 1903
articles converted *Le Mouvement socialiste* to Sorelianism. Author of *Les
Nouveaux Aspects du socialisme* (1908) and *Les Méfaits des intellectuels*
(1913/14, with Sorel's famous preface), he supplied two doctrinal building
blocks Sternhell tracks into fascism: the **producer/parasite** distinction
("commercial and usurious capitalism" as enemy of industrial capitalism) and
the **Apollonian/Dionysian** formula for the Maurras–Sorel synthesis. He
proclaimed "Sorel and Maurras... the two masters of the French and...
European regeneration," argued (following his 1906 debate with
[[michels-robert|Michels]]) that there could be "a revolution without a
proletariat," championed elitism ("the government of the mass by the
capable"), the slogan "The Least State Possible!", the cult of war as "the
most profound, most sublime phenomenon of our moral life," and an explicit
anti-Semitic strain ("the Jewish speculator and financier").

After the war Berth swung toward Bolshevism (*Clarté*), left it disappointed
by 1925, returned to Sorelian syndicalism, defended Valois in 1935, and kept
insisting *Réflexions sur la violence* stood "after Justice and The Capital"
as the labor movement's greatest work. He never became a Fascist — a
complication for any straight-line reading of the
[[fascism-and-the-political-left|left-to-fascism trajectory]].
