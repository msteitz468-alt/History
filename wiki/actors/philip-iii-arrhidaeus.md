# Philip III Arrhidaeus

Philip Arrhidaeus (c. 359–317 BCE) was the half-brother of [[alexander-the-great]], son of Philip II and a Thessalian dancer, Philinna. Ancient sources report he suffered a cognitive impairment — attributed by tradition to a poisoning attempt by Olympias against a rival — that made him politically incapable of independent rule, though physically fit and popular with the army.

When Alexander died in Babylon in 323 BCE without a clear heir, the infantry assembly, wary of being ruled solely by an unborn child (the future Alexander IV, son of Roxana), acclaimed Arrhidaeus king under the regnal name Philip III. The resulting Settlement of Babylon made him joint, figurehead king alongside the infant Alexander IV, with real power held successively by the regents [[perdiccas]], [[polyperchon]], and others through the [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323|Wars of the Diadochi]].

Married to Eurydice, an ambitious granddaughter of Philip II, Arrhidaeus was drawn into the succession wars as a figurehead for factions opposing [[olympias]] (Alexander the Great's mother) and her ally Polyperchon. In 317 BCE, Olympias's forces defeated Eurydice's faction; both Philip III and Eurydice were captured and put to death on Olympias's orders, removing the last functioning Argead co-king and clearing the way for the Successor kingdoms.
