# Gilbert du Motier, Marquis de Lafayette (1757–1834)

The young French aristocrat who became [[washington-george|Washington]]'s surrogate son and a
living symbol of the **[[franco-american-alliance-1778|Franco-American alliance]]**. Lafayette
joined the **[[continental-army|Continental Army]]** as a volunteer in 1777, refusing payment,
and pressed Washington (with [[greene-nathanael|Greene]] and [[steuben-friedrich|Steuben]]) to
fight at **[[battle-of-monmouth-1778|Monmouth]]**, where he briefly held the forward command
before [[gates-horatio|Charles Lee]] pulled rank.

His decisive service came in 1781. Sent to Virginia with ~1,200 Continentals to shadow
[[cornwallis-charles|Cornwallis]] — "I am not strong enough even to get beaten," he wryly told
Washington — Lafayette skillfully evaded destruction (including escaping the trap at Green Spring),
kept Cornwallis under observation, and **pinned the British at the Yorktown peninsula** until
Washington, Rochambeau, and the French fleet could converge. At the
**[[siege-of-yorktown-1781|siege of Yorktown]]** troops under his command stormed one of the
British redoubts. He returned to France to become a central figure of the
[[french-revolution-1789|French Revolution]]'s liberal opening.
