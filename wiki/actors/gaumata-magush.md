# Gaumata the Magian (Gaumata hya Magush / Gaumrita hya Magnsh)

## Narrative
Gaumata the Magian seized power by dispossessing Cambyses of both Persia and Media. He suppressed the ayadana (places of worship) and the religious observances of the people (including sacred chanting and adoration). Darius overthrew him at the fortress of Sikayauvatiš in Media, restored what had been taken, and recorded the events in the first person on the Behistun inscription.

The inscription states it as a matter of surprise that neither a Persian, nor a Median, nor even one of the Achaemenids opposed the usurper during Cambyses' prolonged absence.

## Causal Analysis
The usurpation enabled the Magian revolution. The predominance of those Darius labels arika (heretics/Magi, followers of proscribed sacrificial worship and magical invocations) paved the way. It directly preceded Darius's counter-action and the restoration of the pure faith and observances.

## Consequence Analysis
Darius dispossessed him of the empire and restored the ayadana, abicharish (worship/adoration), gaitham (perhaps possessions or the land), and related observances that Gaumata had destroyed. The events are the foundation narrative of Darius's reign in the primary source. Greek accounts are distorted (Herodotus has two Magi brothers and gives the impostor the name Smerdis; Ctesias uses the Zend title Spendadates; Justin/Trogus has a different sequence and timing).

## Actors
Linked to [[darius-i]] (victor and recorder); Cambyses (predecessor and victim); the arika/heretics.

## Historiography
The 19th-c. vocabulary draws directly on Col. I passages for the double-accusative construction and the religious restoration narrative (e.g., "Gaumdta hya Magush ddind Kabujiyam utd Pdrsani utd Mddam"; "Ayad(a)na tytl Gaumrita hya Magnsh viyaka..."). The author connects the episode to the later Magophonia feast and Zoroastrian reverence for Darius as religious reformer. Median transcript treats related terms (arika) as proper names. The edition contrasts the inscription repeatedly with classical sources while preserving core elements.
