# John of Ephesus

The foremost **Miaphysite ("Monophysite") church historian** of the sixth century (c. 507–588), a
Syriac-writing bishop whose works are a principal source for the religious and social history of the age
of **[[justinian|Justinian]]** — preserved above all in the
**[[pseudo-dionysius-zuqnin-chronicle|Chronicle of Zuqnin]]**.

## Life

Born near **Amida** in the Roman–Persian borderland and formed in its monasteries, John was a committed
**Miaphysite** yet served the Chalcedonian emperor **Justinian** as a **missionary**, charged with
converting the remaining pagans of **Asia Minor** (he claimed tens of thousands of baptisms and the
destruction of many shrines). He became the Miaphysite **bishop of Ephesus**. Under **Justin II** the
renewed **persecution** of the Miaphysites fell on him: he was imprisoned and wrote in adversity, dying
c. 588.

## Works

- the ***Lives of the Eastern Saints*** — biographies of Miaphysite ascetics and holy men, a rich source
  for monasticism and society in the eastern provinces;
- a three-part ***Church History***, of which the first two parts are lost as independent works but the
  **second part survives, largely verbatim, inside the [[pseudo-dionysius-zuqnin-chronicle|Chronicle of
  Zuqnin]]** (Pseudo-Dionysius Part III), and the third part survives directly.

## Significance

John is the great **insider voice of the persecuted eastern Church** and an **eyewitness historian** of
the sixth century. His account of the **[[justinianic-plague-541|Justinianic plague]]** — written by a
man who travelled through the dying empire toward Constantinople in 542 — is among the most powerful
descriptions of mass mortality in all of ancient literature, and a crucial independent witness alongside
Procopius. As a historian and hagiographer he is a founder of the **Syriac historiographical tradition**.
