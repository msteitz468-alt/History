# Leonnatus

Macedonian nobleman and general (c. 356–322 BCE), a childhood companion and bodyguard (*somatophylax*) of Alexander the Great, distantly related to the Argead royal house. He distinguished himself in Alexander's Indian campaign, was wounded at the Mallian town where Alexander himself nearly died, and was among the officers granted a marriage alliance with the Persian nobility at Susa (marrying Barsine's daughter Artakama, per Arrian).

In the succession settlement after Alexander's death (323 BCE), Leonnatus received the satrapy of Hellespontine Phrygia. When the Lamian War broke out — the Greek revolt against Macedonian domination led by Athens under Leosthenes — Leonnatus crossed into Europe with reinforcements to relieve Antipater, who was besieged at Lamia. He was killed in battle against the Greek coalition shortly after crossing, before the siege of Lamia was lifted, removing one of the few remaining commanders capable of unifying the early Diadochi.

## Significance

Leonnatus's death at the opening of the Lamian War removed a potential unifying figure among Alexander's marshals at a moment when the fragmenting empire badly needed continuity of command.
