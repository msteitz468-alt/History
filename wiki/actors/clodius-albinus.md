# Clodius Albinus

**Decimus Clodius Septimius Albinus** (d. 197 CE) was Roman governor of Britannia and one
of the rival claimants to the imperial throne during the civil wars following the murder
of Pertinax (193 CE), alongside [[septimius-severus|Septimius Severus]] and
[[pescennius-niger|Pescennius Niger]].

## Role

Severus, needing to secure his rear while he dealt with Niger in the east, initially
neutralized Albinus by naming him Caesar (junior co-ruler) in 193 CE. Once Niger was
defeated, Severus turned on Albinus, who had by then been proclaimed Augustus by his
troops in Britain and had crossed into Gaul with legions drawn from the British and
Rhine garrisons. The two met in a decisive and unusually large and bloody battle near
Lugdunum (Lyon) in February 197 CE; Severus's forces prevailed, Albinus was killed
(by suicide or in battle, accounts differ), and Severus proscribed his supporters in the
Senate — cementing the Severan dynasty's hold on power.
