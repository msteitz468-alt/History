## Overview

Honda Tadakatsu was Tokugawa Ieyasu's most celebrated field commander, known throughout the Sengoku period for his personal bravery and undeviating loyalty. He is one of the "Four Heavenly Kings" (Shitennō) of the Tokugawa, alongside Ii Naomasa, Sakakibara Yasumasa, and Sakai Tadatsugu. His weapon of choice was a halberd called "Dragon-fly Cutter" (Tombo-kiri), and his recognizable helmet crest with staghorn frontlet made him visible at the forefront of every engagement.

## Key Episodes

**Iga Flight (1582).** When news of Oda Nobunaga's assassination reached Ieyasu at Sakai, it was Honda who persuaded his lord against immediate suicide and organized the dangerous overland retreat through Iga province. At the Kizugawa he "hauled out two brushwood boats" with his halberd to get the party across, then "carefully pounded them into splinters with the butt to prevent any uninvited pursuit." At the Uji River he found a ford and commandeered a small boat for Ieyasu. When bandits blocked the route, Honda scattered them. Sadler's account credits Honda's combination of force and resourcefulness as the decisive factor in the escape.

**Komaki-Nagakute (1584).** At the Battle of Nagakute, Honda commanded alongside Ii Naomasa and Sakakibara. His most celebrated action came after the battle: when Hideyoshi advanced with his main army to pursue Ieyasu, Honda rode out from Komaki with fewer than a thousand men, formed across the river at Ryusenji, and theatrically dared the entire Toyotomi host to battle, refusing to move while the great army passed by. His explicit motive was to delay Hideyoshi for every hour possible while his lord escaped. Hideyoshi recognized him immediately from the Anegawa campaign — "That's Honda Heihachiro" — and ordered his men not to cross: "He mustn't die. I need men like him and his master as my supporters in the future." Honda then pushed forward to Kobata and found Ieyasu safe inside.

**Submission to Hideyoshi (1586).** Honda Tadakatsu was one of the delegation sent to Osaka ahead of Ieyasu to prepare the way and serve as effective hostages. He also carried the betrothal presents to Hideyoshi when Asahi-hime's marriage to Ieyasu was arranged, and "was very cordially treated and handsomely rewarded."

**Odawara Campaign (1590).** Honda served in the eastern sector under Ieyasu's command. After the Kantō transfer, Hideyoshi tried to co-opt Ieyasu's chief retainers by suggesting their fiefs be larger than Ieyasu had initially proposed — intending to place them under obligation to Toyotomi. Ieyasu outmaneuvered this by understating his planned grants. Honda received approximately 100,000 koku in the Kantō assignment.

## Counterfactual Significance

Honda's physical courage and tactical reliability at critical moments — the Iga flight, the Ryusenji delaying action — gave Ieyasu options he would not otherwise have had. The Ryusenji episode is particularly significant: it is the only moment in the Komaki-Nagakute campaign where Hideyoshi had a realistic chance to destroy Ieyasu's army in detail, and Honda's single-handed deterrence (whether from genuine courage or calculated theater, or both) prevented it. Hideyoshi's admiring restraint at this moment was also decisive — he consciously chose not to destroy Honda because he already foresaw a future in which he would need both Honda and Ieyasu as supporters.

## Historiography

Honda Tadakatsu is a celebrated figure in Tokugawa historical tradition. Multiple anecdotes about him are preserved across the Mikawa Go-Fudoki, the Annals of the Honda family, and later popular tradition. His legendary status is reinforced by the claim, preserved in several sources, that he never once received a wound in battle despite fighting in dozens of engagements — an extraordinary claim that Sadler repeats without comment. Modern historians approach these accounts as idealized portraits of the loyal Sengoku retainer, while the broad outlines of Honda's career and his closeness to Ieyasu are not in doubt.
