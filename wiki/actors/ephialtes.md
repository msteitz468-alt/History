# Ephialtes

## Overview

**Ephialtes** son of Sophonides (d. 461 BCE) was the Athenian statesman who in **462/1 BCE**
carried the **reform of the Areopagus** — the constitutional revolution that, in the judgement
of [[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH Vol. V]] (Ch. 4, Rhodes), "ushered in the classical democracy."
Almost nothing else is known of him: like [[aristides-the-just|Aristides]] he was remembered
as upright and poor (incorruptible amid the temptations of the [[delian-league|empire]]), and
he once commanded a naval expedition beyond the Chelidonian Islands.

## The reform of the Areopagus

The **[[areopagus|Council of the Areopagus]]** — the body of ex-archons — had retained, on the
strength of its prestige and its title as "guardian of the laws," a cluster of political
**judicial powers** (the *epitheta*, "accretions"): the scrutiny (*dokimasia*) and audit
(*euthynai*) of magistrates and the trial of *eisangeliai* (impeachments for offences against
the state). Exploiting the absence of [[cimon|Cimon]] and his 4,000 hoplites in the
Peloponnese, Ephialtes **stripped these powers** and redistributed them to the **Council of
Five Hundred, the Assembly, and the popular lawcourts (*dikasteria*)**, leaving the Areopagus
only its ancient jurisdiction over homicide and certain religious offences. The reform is
inseparable from the **fall of Cimon** (ostracized soon after) and a reversal of foreign
policy — the abandonment of the Spartan alliance. See [[ephialtes-reform-462bce]].

## Murder and legacy

Ephialtes was **assassinated** shortly after his victory — a crime the orator Antiphon cited
as famously unsolved; the *Athenaion Politeia* names a Tanagran, Aristodicus, as the killer,
while a hostile tradition implausibly blamed [[pericles|Pericles']] envy. His associate
**Pericles** carried the democracy forward (jury pay, the citizenship law). Though the ancient
sources sometimes pair him misleadingly with the long-departed [[themistocles]], Ephialtes'
genuine partner was Pericles, and his reform was the decisive step from the Cleisthenic
[[isonomia]] to the radical **[[athenian-democracy]]** of the Periclean age.
