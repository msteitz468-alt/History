# Syrian Orthodox Church

The **Syrian Orthodox Church** (West Syrian / “Jacobite” church) is the Miaphysite communion of the Syriac-speaking world that rejected [[council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedon (451)]] and developed a separate hierarchy, liturgy, and scholarly culture under late Roman and then Muslim rule.

## Structure and identity

- **Christology:** [[miaphysitism|Miaphysite]] (Cyrilline one-united-nature teaching); polemically labeled [[monophysitism|Monophysite]] by opponents.
- **Nickname “Jacobite”:** from [[jacob-baradaeus|Jacob Baradaeus]]’s sixth-century ordinations.
- **Leading figures:** [[severus-of-antioch|Severus of Antioch]], Jacob Baradaeus, [[jacob-of-edessa|Jacob of Edessa]]; institutions such as [[qenneshre|Qenneshre]].
- **Language:** Syriac as major literary and liturgical medium — central to [[tannous-making-medieval-middle-east-2018|Tannous]]’s source base.

## Under Islam

Lived as a subject Christian community within [[umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad]] and [[abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid]] societies; participant in the long [[islamization-of-the-middle-east|Islamization]] process as both retaining members and losing them to conversion.
