# Valentinian I and Valens (the Pannonian Emperors)

The two **Pannonian soldier-brothers** who ruled the empire after the brief reign of **Jovian** —
**Valentinian I** in the West (364–375) and **Valens** in the East (364–378). Their joint rule
re-established the **permanent division of imperial labour**, and ended in the catastrophe of
**[[battle-of-adrianople-378|Adrianople]]**. Treated in [[cameron-cah-v13-1998|CAH XIII]] Ch. 3
(**John Curran**).

## The division of 364

After Julian's death (363), **Jovian** made a humiliating peace with Persia (surrendering Nisibis) and
died within months. The army chose the Pannonian officer **Valentinian I**, who at once raised his
brother **Valens** as co-Augustus and took the **West** for himself, assigning the **East** to Valens —
a division now treated as the normal structure of empire. Valentinian, an able and ferociously
energetic (and notoriously cruel) general, spent his reign on the **Rhine and Danube frontiers**,
fortifying the limes against the **Alamanni** and campaigning hard; he died of a stroke (375) in a rage
while berating Quadi envoys, leaving his sons **Gratian** and the child **Valentinian II**.

## Valens, Procopius and religion

Valens, less able and a convinced **Homoian ("Arian")**, faced early the dangerous revolt of
**Procopius** (365–6, exploiting Constantinian legitimacy) before turning to the **Gothic** problem on
the Danube and a renewed contest with **[[sassanid-empire|Shapur II]]** over **[[armenia|Armenia]]**.
His Homoian sympathies set him against the increasingly Nicene church (and against ascetics and monks).

## The Gothic crisis and Adrianople

The defining event was the **Hunnic irruption** of c. 375, which shattered the **Tervingi** and
**Greuthungi** Goths and drove them to the Danube. In 376 Valens admitted the Tervingi into Thrace —
hoping for recruits and revenue — but Roman mismanagement and the corruption of the *comes* Lupicinus
provoked a Gothic revolt. On **9 August 378**, refusing to wait for his nephew Gratian's western army,
Valens attacked the Goths outside **[[battle-of-adrianople-378|Adrianople]]** and was annihilated —
killed on the field, two-thirds of his army destroyed, in what Ammianus ranked the worst defeat since
**Cannae**. His death opened the way for **[[theodosius-i|Theodosius I]]**.

## Significance

The Pannonian emperors mark the **professional, frontier-soldier** style of late-Roman rule and the
hardening of the East–West division. Valentinian's vigorous frontier defence preserved the West for a
generation; Valens' fatal decision at Adrianople is the hinge event that let the Goths settle *inside*
the empire as autonomous *foederati* — a precedent of immense consequence for the western collapse. The
brothers' opposite religious leanings (Valentinian tolerant/Nicene, Valens Homoian) also illustrate how
the doctrinal quarrel now tracked imperial politics.
