# Anastasius I

**Anastasius I** (c. 430–518; r. 491–518) was the elderly court official chosen by Zeno’s widow Ariadne who restored eastern fiscal strength and crushed residual Isaurian power — a hinge between the fifth-century eastern recovery and the age of [[justin-i|Justin I]] / [[justinian|Justinian]].

## Reign (Mitchell)

Accession required an oath and written promise to respect Chalcedonian orthodoxy because of Anastasius’s unorthodox (Monophysite-leaning) reputation. Isaurians, removed from power, rebelled; defeated at Cotiaeum (492), they were then broken in a seven-year guerrilla campaign ending with Longinus’s capture and the razing of mountain strongholds — critical for Constantinople–Antioch routes and Cilician olive *annona*. Before final defeat they had received 5,000 lb of gold annually — more than any Balkan barbarian subsidy.

Mitchell also notes Anastasius’s Long Wall, recognition diplomacy with [[theoderic-the-great|Theoderic]], Persian frontier works including Dara, abolition of the *chrysargyron*, and a large fiscal surplus that underwrote early sixth-century eastern power.

## Counterfactual significance

Without Anastasius’s surplus and Isaurian pacification, Justinian’s later wars and building programs lack their fiscal precondition.

## Historiography

Often treated as a “quiet” reign between Zeno and Justinian; Mitchell stresses fiscal-military reconstruction. Context: [[eastern-roman-empire-425-518]]. Survey: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Mitchell 2015]].
