# Mohammad Mosaddegh

## Overview
Mohammad Mosaddegh was the nationalist prime minister of Iran, leader of the National Front and chair of the Majlis Oil Committee. On May 1, 1951 he nationalized the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company, and in June 1952 he won a case against Britain at the International Court of Justice — a diplomatic victory that earned him *Time* magazine's "Man of the Year" recognition. He was overthrown on August 19, 1953 in the CIA/MI6-organized coup [[iran-coup-1953|Operation Ajax]].

## Key Decisions and Consequences
- Nationalizing the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company (May 1951) triggered a British-led international oil boycott and set in motion the covert operation that ultimately removed him from power.
- His refusal to compromise with Britain over compensation and control, despite his ICJ victory, hardened both British and (eventually) American resolve to see him removed rather than negotiate a settlement.

## Counterfactual Significance
Had Mosaddegh survived politically, Iran's oil wealth would likely have remained under greater Iranian state control decades earlier than it in fact did, and the Shah's near-absolute post-1953 rule — with its dependence on CIA-assisted internal security (SAVAK) — might not have taken the shape it did. McCoy's broader account frames Mosaddegh's overthrow as a hinge event transferring Middle Eastern hegemony from Britain to the United States; see [[iran-coup-1953]].

## Historiography
McCoy draws on the CIA's own official history and Norman Darbyshire's interview transcripts, both declassified decades after the coup, along with the Foreign Relations of the United States (FRUS) series. Source: Alfred W. McCoy, *Cold War on Five Continents* (2024).
