# Nicholas I (Pope, r. 858–867)

> **Naming note:** Wiki file `actors/nicholas-i.md` is **Nicholas I of Russia** (1796–1855). This page is **Pope Nicholas I**. Prefer `[[pope-nicholas-i]]` for the medieval pope.

## Overview

**Nicholas I** (consecrated 24 April 858; d. 13 November 867) was, in [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]]’s judgment, a strong candidate for **greatest pope between [[gregory-i|Gregory the Great]] and Gregory VII**—the last strong pope on a large stage for 150+ years, better seen as the **last of a strong series from Gregory I** than as harbinger of later papal monarchy. Son of a senior Roman official; elected still a deacon, about age 38, with Louis II’s non-opposition.

## Major cases

### Lothar II divorce
Lothar II of Lotharingia renounced Queen Theutberga (incest/abortion allegations) for Waldrada; Theutberga appealed to Nicholas. Legates at Metz (possibly bribed) found for the king; Nicholas deposed and excommunicated the archbishops of Cologne and Trier who brought the decision. Louis II’s 864 assault on Roman clergy/relics failed to sway him. Lothar reconciled with Theutberga in 865; later annulment pleas were refused. The affair entangled uncles’ territorial ambitions and papal political calculation.

### Hincmar and Rothad of Soissons
Hincmar of Rheims (most powerful West Frankish churchman under Charles the Bald) restored an adulterous priest whom suffragan Rothad had deposed; Rothad appealed to the pope; a synod under Hincmar dismissed/imprisoned Rothad. Nicholas demanded reinstatement or Roman appearance under threat of suspension from Mass. Hincmar delayed then capitulated: “What Nicholas has decided I have not contradicted.” Logan: Rome over Rheims.

### John of Ravenna (861)
Archbishop John VIII of Ravenna (close to Louis II) faced charges of mistreating papal agents, property seizure, and abusive metropolitan practices. He refused summons, was excommunicated, fled Nicholas’s personal journey to Ravenna, was abandoned by Louis II, and submitted at Rome. Logan: metropolitans were major but secondary players—“centre stage had only room for Nicholas.”

### Photius crisis
After Michael III deposed patriarch Ignatius (857) and layman Photius was elected, Nicholas responded hostilely. Legates in 861 accepted Photius; Nicholas’s 863 Roman synod deposed him. Letter to the emperor (Dvornik: among the most important documents in papal evolution) asserted Christ-given, unchangeable Roman privileges over the universal church. Mutual excommunications of 867 did **not** produce lasting schism: Michael III was assassinated, Photius deposed, Ignatius restored; Nicholas died unaware. Photius later (877) succeeded Ignatius with papal blessing. Real East–West break mid-eleventh century; “**Photian Schism**” is an inappropriate label.

### Bulgaria and Cyril–Methodius
Boris of Bulgaria, baptized by Photius (864), turned to Rome when refused a patriarch; [[formosus]] of Porto was sent with 106 pastoral answers. Nicholas refused Formosus as Bulgarian archbishop; the mission failed; Bulgaria stayed Eastern. Yet Nicholas invited Byzantine missionaries Cyril and Methodius (Photius’s protégés) to Rome (they arrived after his death); a successor consecrated Methodius archbishop; they created a Slavic written language and liturgy. Logan: Nicholas’s vision embraced Bulgars, Slavs, and Greeks.

## Significance and aftermath

Death of Nicholas (867) removed the strongest ninth-century pope; interests had gone far beyond Italy and Carolingian remnants. After him, post-840 political atomization produced a **local, passive papacy**—Rome as plaything of factions—until the mid-eleventh century (see [[formosus]], [[john-xii]], [[benedict-ix]]).

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Nicholas’s assertive cases, the ninth-century ceiling of papal intervention in royal marriage, metropolitan discipline, and East–West claims would look lower; without the subsequent collapse, he might be remembered as start of papal monarchy rather than end of a strong series.

## Historiography

Logan’s framing (last of Gregory I’s series vs. proto-Gregorian VII) is interpretive. Photius scholarship (Dvornik) underpins the anti-“Photian Schism” point. Collection coverage moderate.

## From Logan, History of the Church in the Middle Ages (2013)

Ch.6 opens the “disarray” era after [[louis-the-pious]] by using Nicholas as the high baseline: Lothar, Hincmar/Rothad, Ravenna, Photius, Bulgaria/Cyril–Methodius—then the fall into Formosus and the tenth-century nadir. Quote-level thesis: greatest between Gregory I and Gregory VII; not the first papal monarch but the last large-stage strong pope for 150 years.
