## Role and Background

Ukita Hideie was raised close to [[actors/toyotomi-hideyoshi|Toyotomi Hideyoshi]] (his father Ukita Naoie having been an early Hideyoshi ally) and rose to become one of the Five Regents (Tairō) whom the dying Hideyoshi appointed in 1598 to govern Japan and protect his young son Hideyori's succession, alongside [[actors/tokugawa-ieyasu|Tokugawa Ieyasu]], Maeda Toshiie, Mori Terumoto, and Uesugi Kagekatsu.

## Sekigahara Campaign (1600)

When the regency collapsed into open war, Hideie sided with the Western coalition operationally led by [[actors/ishida-mitsunari|Ishida Mitsunari]]. His forces took part in the siege of [[events/siege-of-fushimi-1600|Fushimi Castle]], and at the [[events/battle-of-sekigahara-1600|Battle of Sekigahara]] he commanded the Western center alongside Konishi Yukinaga, bearing the brunt of the opening Eastern assault by Ii Naomasa and Fukushima Masanori. When [[actors/ishida-mitsunari|Ishida's]] army disintegrated after Kobayakawa Hideaki's defection, Hideie reportedly wished to fight Kobayakawa's forces hand-to-hand but was overruled by his own captains and compelled to flee.

## Aftermath

Hideie escaped after the defeat and eventually surrendered; rather than being executed, he was exiled in 1606 to the remote island of Hachijōjima, where he lived out an extraordinarily long remaining life (over 45 years) as one of history's longest-surviving political exiles, dying in 1655.
