# Ante Pavelić

Croatian nationalist politician and founder-leader of the fascist Ustaše movement (1889–1959), installed as head of state ("Poglavnik") of the Axis puppet Independent State of Croatia (NDH) after the German-Italian invasion and dismemberment of Yugoslavia in April 1941. Pavelić's regime pursued a program of extreme ethnic and religious persecution against Serbs, Jews, and Roma within its territory, including the establishment of concentration and extermination camps (notably Jasenovac) and mass killings of Serbs estimated in the tens of thousands to several hundred thousand — precise figures remain disputed and are difficult to verify given the destruction of records and the political stakes of later Yugoslav and post-Yugoslav historiography.

The Ustaše genocide against Serbs drove large numbers of Serbian refugees into the ranks of both the royalist Chetniks under Draža Mihailović and, more successfully over time, the Communist-led Partisans under Josip Broz Tito, fueling a multi-sided civil war within the broader Axis occupation. After the Axis collapse in 1945, Pavelić fled Yugoslavia, eventually settling in Argentina and later Spain under the protection of sympathetic authorities, evading capture and prosecution; he died in Spain in 1959 from wounds sustained in an earlier assassination attempt.

## Significance

Pavelić's Ustaše regime and its genocidal violence against Serbs were a central driver of the multi-sided Yugoslav civil war within the broader Second World War, and his postwar escape from justice exemplifies the broader pattern of unprosecuted Axis collaborators finding refuge through sympathetic postwar networks.
