# Zheng Zhilong

The maritime merchant-warlord who built the Fujian-based commercial and naval
empire of the [[chinese-maritime-mercantile-resurgence|Zheng family network]],
and father of [[koxinga|Zheng Chenggong (Koxinga)]].

## Overview

Rising from the polyglot world of early-seventeenth-century South China Sea trade —
where he worked as an interpreter for the [[dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East
India Company]] and apprenticed within Chinese-Japanese smuggling networks — Zheng
Zhilong consolidated the *wokou* (pirate-merchant) fleets of the Taiwan Strait into
a single dominant organisation. By the 1630s his armed trading network controlled
the sea lanes linking Fujian, Taiwan, Japan and Manila, levying protection fees and
out-competing both rival Chinese traders and the VOC.

## Significance

The late Ming state co-opted rather than crushed him, granting him official rank and
effectively privatising maritime defence to the Zheng organisation — the paradoxical
fruit of the Ming sea-ban (*haijin*) policy. When the Qing conquered the mainland he
defected (1646), but his son [[koxinga|Koxinga]] inherited the fleet and turned it
into a Ming-loyalist maritime state that expelled the Dutch from Taiwan in 1662.
Zheng Zhilong was held and ultimately executed by the Qing in 1661.
