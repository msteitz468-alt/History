# Thomas Collier Platt

**Thomas Collier Platt** (1833–1910), known as the "Easy Boss," was the dominant figure
in the New York Republican political machine and a US Senator (1897–1909). As governor of
New York, [[roosevelt-theodore|Theodore Roosevelt]] repeatedly clashed with Platt's
machine over patronage and reform, particularly Roosevelt's insistence on independent
appointments and prosecution of corrupt corporate practices. Platt, wanting Roosevelt out
of New York state politics where his reformism threatened machine interests, engineered
his nomination as William McKinley's running mate in 1900 — intending to sideline him in
the largely ceremonial vice presidency — a maneuver whose consequences were transformed
when McKinley's assassination in 1901 unexpectedly made Roosevelt president.
