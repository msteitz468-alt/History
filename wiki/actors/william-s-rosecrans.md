## Role and Background

William Starke Rosecrans commanded the Union Army of the Cumberland through much of 1862–63, winning the costly Battle of Stones River (December 1862–January 1863) and then out-maneuvering Braxton Bragg's Confederate army out of central Tennessee in the largely bloodless Tullahoma Campaign (June–July 1863) through skillful use of deception and maneuver.

## Chickamauga and Relief

Advancing into northern Georgia in September 1863, Rosecrans's army was caught by a reinforced Confederate force at the [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/american-civil-war/battle-of-chickamauga-1863|Battle of Chickamauga]]; a mistaken order opened a gap in the Union line that Longstreet's corps exploited, routing much of the army, though George H. Thomas's stand ("the Rock of Chickamauga") prevented total disaster. Rosecrans withdrew into besieged Chattanooga and was relieved of command shortly afterward, his career effectively ended by the defeat despite his earlier successes.

## Significance

Rosecrans's Tullahoma Campaign is regarded by many historians as one of the war's most skillfully executed maneuver campaigns, making his subsequent collapse at Chickamauga and abrupt relief a notable case study in how a single reversal could permanently end an otherwise capable general's career.
