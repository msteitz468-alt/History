# Alexander IV of Macedon

Posthumous son of [[alexander-the-great|Alexander the Great]] and Roxana, born shortly after his father's death in 323 BCE. He was proclaimed joint king alongside his mentally incapacitated uncle Philip III Arrhidaeus as a compromise between rival Macedonian factions at Babylon, but never exercised real power: authority rested throughout his short life with successive regents (Perdiccas, Antipater, [[polyperchon|Polyperchon]], and finally Cassander) during the [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323|Wars of the Diadochi]].

## Role

As the last legitimate Argead claimant to Alexander's empire, the boy king's existence remained a persistent threat to the ambitions of the warring successor generals, particularly Cassander, who had imprisoned Alexander IV and his mother at Amphipolis after seizing Macedon.

## Counterfactual Significance

Cassander had the twelve- or thirteen-year-old king and Roxana secretly murdered around 309 BCE once he judged the threat of an eventual majority reign too dangerous to tolerate — an act that extinguished the Argead royal line and formally cleared the way for the Diadochi to claim royal titles for themselves, marking the definitive shift from a unified Alexandrian empire to the separate Hellenistic successor kingdoms.
