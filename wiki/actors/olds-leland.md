# Leland Olds

**Summary stub created during Caro Vol. 3 (Master of the Senate) ingest.**

Leland Olds was a Federal Power Commission member and strong advocate of public power and consumer protection in utility rates. He was targeted for defeat in his 1949 renomination by Texas oil and natural gas interests.

## Key Role

As FPC chairman or commissioner, Olds had opposed private utility rate increases and favored public development. Texas oil/gas producers (and their congressional allies) viewed him as an obstacle.

In 1949, as a junior senator, Lyndon Johnson chaired a stacked Commerce subcommittee that used surprise introduction of Olds' 1920s writings (some appearing in left-wing publications during the Federated Press period) and aggressive "repudiate or reiterate" questioning to produce a unanimous subcommittee rejection. The full committee and Senate (53–15) defeated the renomination.

The episode was celebrated by Texas oil interests and the Southern bloc (including Richard Russell) and marked an early demonstration of Johnson's ruthlessness and parliamentary skill in service of key backers.

## Historiography / Notes

Caro presents the Olds fight in detail as an example of Johnson's methods (subcommittee stacking, surprise attack, use of anti-Communist climate) and his willingness to destroy a New Deal figure for Texas economic interests. It is also used to illustrate the contrast with Coke Stevenson-style integrity in prior volumes.
