# Theodora

Empress consort of [[justinian|Justinian I]] (r. 527–548), a former actress of low birth who became one of the most powerful women in Byzantine history. During the [[nika-riots-532|Nika riots]] of 532, with Justinian preparing to flee Constantinople, Theodora is reported (by Procopius) to have declared that "the imperial purple is a fine winding-sheet," stiffening his resolve to remain and crush the revolt through [[belisarius|Belisarius]] and Mundus — a decision that saved his reign.

## Role

Theodora used her position to sponsor Miaphysite ("Monophysite") Christian communities against the Chalcedonian establishment her husband favored, sheltering persecuted clergy and shaping imperial religious policy toward tolerance of the eastern churches. She also influenced legal reforms improving the legal standing of women, including restrictions on forced prostitution and expanded divorce and property rights.

## Counterfactual Significance

Procopius's own account credits her intervention as the hinge on which the Nika riots — and thus Justinian's entire subsequent reign, including the reconquests and the Corpus Juris Civilis — turned; without her, Justinian's flight would likely have ended his rule in 532.
