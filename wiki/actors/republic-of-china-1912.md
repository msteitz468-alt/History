# The Republic of China (1912–1949)

The **Republic of China** was the state that succeeded the [[qing-dynasty|Qing]] empire after the
[[fall-of-the-qing-1911|1911 Revolution]] — the first republic in Asia — but which spent most of its
mainland existence fragmented, embattled, and unable to consolidate, before its defeat by the
Communists in 1949. From the [[cambridge-history-of-china|Cambridge History of China]] (Republican
China volumes, 12–13).

## Founding and fragmentation

Proclaimed on 1 January 1912 with **Sun Yat-sen** as provisional president, the Republic was
immediately handed to the strongman **Yuan Shikai**, who suppressed the [[kuomintang|Nationalist]]
parliament, attempted to make himself emperor (1915–16), and on his death (1916) left no center
strong enough to hold the country together. There followed the **[[warlord-era|warlord era]]**
(1916–28), in which regional militarists fought for power and the Beijing government became a
fiction. The cultural ferment of the **May Fourth Movement** (1919) — a new nationalism and a
turn to science, vernacular literature, and radical ideologies (including Marxism) — was the
intellectual counterpoint to this political collapse.

## Nationalist reunification and its limits

From its Canton base the **[[kuomintang|Kuomintang]]**, reorganized by Sun with Soviet help and
allied (in the First United Front) with the new Communist Party, launched the **Northern Expedition**
(1926–28) under **Chiang Kai-shek**, which defeated the major warlords and established a Nationalist
government at **Nanjing**. The **"Nanjing decade" (1927–37)** saw real state-building — but Chiang's
violent **1927 purge of the Communists** opened a civil war, his control over much of China remained
nominal, and modernization was uneven.

## War and collapse

The Republic's mainland fate was sealed by the **War of Resistance against Japan (1937–45)** — eight
years of devastating invasion that gutted the Nationalist state and economy while allowing the
**Communists** to expand in the countryside — and the resumed **Civil War (1946–49)**, which ended
in Communist victory and the founding of the [[chinese-communist-revolution|People's Republic]]
(1949). The Nationalist government retreated to **Taiwan**, where the Republic of China continued.

## Significance and historiography

The CHC's Republican volumes (eds. with John Fairbank) frame the era around the **search for a
modern Chinese state and nation** amid imperialism, warlordism, and revolution. Long read through
the lens of the Communist victory (as Nationalist "failure"), recent scholarship gives more weight
to the Nanjing decade's genuine state-building and to the catastrophic impact of the **Japanese war**
in destroying it. The era is the hinge between imperial China and the People's Republic.
