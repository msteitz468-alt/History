# Untash-Napirisha

The greatest king of the **Igihalkid dynasty** (Middle Elamite II, ~1340–1300 BCE) and
the most fully documented Elamite ruler of the [[late-bronze-age|Late Bronze Age]]. Son
of Humban-numena and (per the "Berlin letter") a grandson of the [[kassites|Kassite]]
king Kurigalzu I, he embodies the close **Elamite–Kassite intermarriage** of the period.
His paramount achievement is the foundation of the royal cult-city
**[[chogha-zanbil|Dur-Untash (Chogha Zanbil)]]**. (His name was once misread "Untash-Gal"
or "Untash-Huban" — a relic of the imperfect grasp of Elamite.)

## Significance

At **[[susa|Susa]]** he left at least eleven different building-inscription types,
erecting or rebuilding temples for some eighteen deities — "no program of public/religious
building on this scale had previously taken place at Susa" ([[potts-archaeology-of-elam-2016|Potts]]).
But his signature act was building **[[chogha-zanbil|Al Untash-Napirisha]]** ~40 km
southeast of Susa: a ~100-ha walled city around the **best-preserved ziggurat in the Near
East**, dedicated jointly to the highland god **Napirisha** and the Susian god
**Inshushinak**, with a temenos enclosing shrines to twenty-five deities. The joint
dedication is a deliberate ideological statement of the **union of highland and lowland**
("Anshan and Susa") under a single sacral kingship.

For his queen **Napir-Asu** he commissioned the celebrated **cast-bronze statue** (1.29 m,
~1,750 kg) excavated at Susa — "rightly the most famous work of Elamite art" — whose
inscription curses anyone who damages it. (Later, [[shutruk-nahhunte-i|Shutruk-Nahhunte I]]
removed Untash-Napirisha's and Napir-Asu's statues from Chogha Zanbil to Susa "when
Inshushinak demanded it.")

## Counterfactual significance

Untash-Napirisha's reign is the Igihalkid zenith, but his great foundation proved
**personal rather than institutional**: monumental construction at Chogha Zanbil ceased
entirely on his death, no successor left inscriptions there, and the city never displaced
Susa. The episode illustrates Potts's *courte durée* reading of Elam — bursts of royal
ambition that **do not crystallize into durable structures**. Whether Dur-Untash was an
attempt to *replace* Susa as capital (Harper et al.) Potts regards as unproven.
