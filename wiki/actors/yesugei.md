# Yesügei (Yesugei)

## Overview

**Yesügei Ba’atur**, father of [[genghis-khan|Temüjin / Genghis Khan]], was a Borjigin (Borijin) leader of a small Mongol band subservient to more powerful Tayichi’ud relatives. With two brothers, after a falcon-hunt spotting, he kidnapped [[hoelun|Hö’elün]] from Chiledu of the Merkid; he already had a wife or concubine, Sochigel, and a son (Begter’s line). Weatherford (2004) stresses that he was too poor for conventional bride-price or bride-service and seized Hö’elün instead. On a Tatar campaign after the abduction he killed a Tatar warrior named Temüjin Uge and later named his newborn son after that enemy.

Years later he took nine-year-old Temüjin east for bride-service and betrothed him to [[borte|Börte]] of the Onggirat en route. Riding home alone, he joined a Tatar feast in disguise, was recognized, and was secretly poisoned; he sent for Temüjin and was dead before or when the boy returned. Weatherford: “Yesugei left behind two wives and seven children under the age of ten.” The family’s subsequent abandonment by the clan frames the *Secret History*’s early chapters and Weatherford’s opening arc of hardship.

## Role

- **Anda of Ong Khan.** Yesügei was *anda* (sworn brother) of [[togrul-ong-khan|Toghrul / Ong Khan]] of the Kereyid; Weatherford records that he helped Toghrul become khan by overthrowing his uncle the Gur-khan, fought Merkid with him, and was allied with him at Temüjin’s birth during a Tatar campaign. That anda bond later justified Temüjin’s gift of Börte’s black sable coat to Ong Khan as a claim of ceremonial sonship.
- **Borjigin leadership at small scale.** Not a paramount steppe sovereign; his death exposed how thin clan protection was for widows and minor children when levirate and patronage failed.
- **Naming and succession politics.** Naming Temüjin after a killed Tatar and leaving multiple wives/children under ten set the conditions for abandonment, foraging survival under Hö’elün, and Temüjin’s later wars of revenge against Tatars and Merkid.

## Key events (Weatherford 2004)

- Abduction of Hö’elün from Chiledu (Merkid); already household with Sochigel.
- Killing of Temüjin Uge; naming of Temüjin (birth traditionally spring 1162, Year of the Horse, Onon).
- Betrothal of Temüjin to Börte; poisoning by Tatars on the return ride (c. 1171 tradition).
- Anda service to Ong Khan (overthrow of Gur-khan; Merkid/Tatar alliances).

## Historiography

Narrative almost entirely from the *Secret History of the Mongols*, with Weatherford’s popular synthesis (*Genghis Khan and the Making of the Modern World*, 2004) elaborating poverty, kidnapping customs, and the Tatar poison story. Dates remain approximate; the poisoning is traditional, not independently documented. Later Yuan ancestral cults (under [[kublai-khan|Khubilai]]) posthumously honored Yesügei and Hö’elün in Chinese-style temple chambers (Weatherford on 1277 ancestral arrangements).
