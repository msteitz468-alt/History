# Xanthippus of Sparta

A Spartan professional soldier hired by [[carthage|Carthage]] in 255 BCE who, in a
single campaign, reversed the course of the African invasion of the
[[first-punic-war-264-241bce|First Punic War]] by destroying the army of
[[atilius-regulus|Marcus Atilius Regulus]] at the [[battle-of-the-bagradas-255bce|Bagradas]].

## Role

With Carthage reeling after Regulus' landing, Xanthippus arrived among a batch of
Greek mercenaries and openly criticized the Carthaginian commanders for fighting in
broken ground that neutralized their two strongest arms — **cavalry and elephants**.
Given command (or at least the decisive voice), he drilled the army, brought it down
onto open plain, and on the Bagradas deployed the elephants in front, the cavalry on
the wings, and the citizen phalanx in the centre. The elephants disrupted the Roman
legions while the superior Punic cavalry swept the field and enveloped them; Regulus'
army was annihilated and Regulus taken. It was, Goldsworthy stresses, a demonstration
that the Punic army's weakness had been **leadership and deployment, not material** —
the right professional could make its combined arms decisive.

## Significance and disappearance

Xanthippus illustrates the Hellenistic mercenary world on which Carthage depended:
expertise could be bought, but loyalty and continuity could not. According to the
sources he left Africa soon after his victory — one tradition has the Carthaginians,
jealous or wary, arranging his death at sea; others simply have him depart, his
contract fulfilled. His brief appearance is the clearest First-War example of how much
the outcome of an ancient battle turned on the quality of the man commanding, a theme
central to the wiki's [[hubs/warfare/strategy|strategy]] hub.
