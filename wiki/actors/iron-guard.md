# Iron Guard / Legion of the Archangel Michael (1927–1941)

Romanian fascist movement founded by [[codreanu-corneliu|Corneliu Zelea
Codreanu]] in 1927 — in [[payne-history-of-fascism-1995|Payne's]] account the
most distinctive of his "four major variants" of fascism: the only major
fascist movement with an explicitly religious (Orthodox Christian) mysticism
at its core, combined with a cult of martyrdom and death and systematic
assassination as political method.

## Role and Significance

Founded 1927 with fifteen associates and organized in local cells called
*cuiburi* ("nests" — fifty of them, ~1,000 members, by 1929), the Legion (its
militia wing named the Iron Guard) grew into proportionately the third
largest fascist movement in Europe, after the German Nazis and the Hungarian
[[arrow-cross|Arrow Cross]]. Its legal front All for the Fatherland (TPT)
officially polled 15.58 percent in December 1937; the sometime Romanian
police chief Eugen Cristescu later claimed the real figure was ~800,000
votes, over 25 percent. Its 272,000 members amounted to 1.5 percent of the
population — proportionately above the NSDAP's 1.3 percent of January 1933
(Payne 1995). Payne judges it "arguably the most unique of the entire genus,
except for the German Nazis," and possibly the only fascist movement as
vehemently antisemitic as the Nazis: Codreanu's collaborator General Zizi
Cantacuzino openly declared killing Romania's Jews the only solution to the
"Jewish problem." King Carol II's royal dictatorship suppressed it and
had Codreanu murdered in November 1938; after Carol's fall the Legion briefly
shared power in the National Legionary State (September 1940 – January 1941)
under Horia Sima alongside General Ion Antonescu, a period marked by
murderous score-settling and the Bucharest pogrom, until the Legion's full-scale revolt of 21 January 1941 —
seizing government centers and unleashing a pogrom in Bucharest's main
Jewish quarter — was crushed by Antonescu with German acquiescence (9,000
Legionnaires arrested, including 218 priests; 1,842 sentenced; 20 executed),
Hitler preferring a reliable military dictator to a chaotic fascist
movement. The Legion thus became "paradoxically the first fascist movement
to fall in a Europe where Germany reigned supreme" (Payne 1995). Antonescu's
subsequent non-fascist right-radical regime carried out the "nonautomated
holocaust" in Transnistria — 200,000–300,000 Jews killed by Romanian
soldiers and police, "by far the greatest liquidation of Jews by non-German
forces" ([[the-holocaust]]). After 1944 many thousands of ex-Legionnaires
were absorbed en masse into the Romanian Communist Party — probably the
largest such migration in eastern Europe.

## Structure, Recruitment, and Suppression (Paxton and Hessler)

The Legion won 16% of the popular vote in the December 1937 elections — the
third-largest party, behind the Liberals and the Agrarians. It organized
into cells ("nests"), whose members swore a blood oath binding them to
poverty, duty, and murder if necessary; legionaries killed eleven public
officials over the course of the 1930s. The movement's character was
strongly religious: Orthodox clergy led village missions on the Legion's
behalf, and Codreanu himself wore traditional Moldavian peasant dress.
Recruitment drew on two distinct bases: university students, angered above
all by Jewish admission quotas, and poor Moldavian family farmers.

King Carol II suspended the constitution in 1938 and jailed the Guard's
leadership; Codreanu and several others were killed in custody, officially
"while trying to escape" — a claim the source treats skeptically as a likely
extrajudicial killing rather than a genuine escape attempt. Romania's later
territorial losses (Bessarabia to the USSR, Transylvania to Hungary,
southern Dobruja to Bulgaria — one-third of Romania's territory) forced
Carol's abdication in September 1940; General Ion Antonescu then ruled as
*conducător*, with Horia Sima (Codreanu's successor) serving under him. The
Guard murdered Jews and seized property while pressing for a "second
revolution"; Antonescu crushed the Guard in three days of street fighting in
Bucharest in January 1941 — the Guard's only period of actual power lasted
about four months, and it remains the only Eastern European fascist
movement to have ruled, however briefly, without direct German occupation.

Eastern European fascism followed a general pattern, per Eugen Weber: it
drew a large distressed peasantry but only a small distressed middle class,
and it faced entrenched conservative or monarchical authoritarians — King
Alexander in Yugoslavia (1931), King Boris III in Bulgaria (1934), King
Carol in Romania (1938) — who had already "saved society" from disorder
before the fascist surge of the 1930s and who generally excluded fascist
movements from power as a result. Source:
[[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011]].

## Historiography

Central to debates over whether fascism was a secularizing "political
religion": the Legion is the limiting case where fascism and traditional
religion fused rather than competed. Griffin
([[griffin-nature-of-fascism-1991]]) certifies it as genuine fascism — its
"initiatic leader worship and death cult... unparalleled in other fascist
movements," its goal "the Resurrection of nations in the name of Jesus
Christ," its symbol the *omul nou* (new man) — and reads its fate as the
textbook [[para-fascism]] sequence: "a fascist movement had in quick
succession been crushed, wooed, coopted, and liquidated by two types of
para-fascist regime [Carol's, then Antonescu's], one of them with the full
backing of the Third Reich" — Hitler backing Antonescu over the Legion in
January 1941. See [[generic-fascism]].

## Griffin (2007): A Modernist Iron Guard

*Modernism and Fascism* challenges the standard "radically anti-modern, aberrantly religious" reading: Ioanid shows the Legion stripped Orthodoxy of its metaphysics to build "Romanianness" and the *Omul Nou* within historical time; Eugen Weber's cargo-cult comparison caught "its nature as a modern [[revitalization-movement]] based on charismatic politics." Leading Romanian eugenicists were Legion members — Fãcãoaru, Herseni, and Manuilã of the Central Institute of Statistics' Bioanthropological Section — and "had the Iron Guard come to power it may well have developed its own technocracy... including eugenics." Formative matrix (Bucur): the journal *Cuvântul* and the charismatic philosopher Nae Ionescu, atop the older völkisch culture of Eminescu, Cuza, and Iorga; the avant-garde reading of Einstein/Planck as heralding "the era of metaphysics." Modernist intellectuals drawn in: Eliade (parliamentary candidate for Totul pentru Țară, December 1937; lifelong companion of [[evola-julius|Evola]]), Cioran (cultural attaché in Paris under the Legionary regime, 1940–41), Noica — the epidemic Ionesco dramatized in *Rhinoceros*. (Source: [[griffin-modernism-and-fascism-2007]].)

## Payne (1980)

From [[payne-fascism-comparison-definition-1980]] (ch. 5): "probably the most unusual mass movement of interwar Europe" — classifiable as fascist on the typological criteria yet sui generis in its religious mysticism: the goal was "not political but transcendental — national salvation for the community of Romanian people." Codreanu offered "a new spirit" rather than a program (the *Omul nou*), with small collective enterprises rather than economic doctrine. Social base: an alliance of students and poor peasants — Romanian university enrollment rose ~400 percent in the postwar decade, "proportionately more than anywhere else in Europe," breeding an unemployed radical-nationalist intelligentsia. Legionary violence was "qualitatively different," stressing self-sacrifice and "veritable immolation reminiscent of the most moralistic and idealistic of the Russian Socialist Revolutionary assassins" — Nolte judged the "martyr complex created a degree of self-destructiveness rarely equaled." Electoral peak ~16.5 percent in the last regular elections. King Carol's 1938 conservative dictatorship blocked its access to power; "most of the Legionary leaders were massacred in cold blood," and Carol's regime then tried to fascistize itself top-down (Party of the Nation, 1940) before collapsing under German territorial amputations. Under Antonescu the "Legionary state" coalition broke down in the Bucharest fighting of early 1941, the Legion suppressed with Hitler's acquiescence — the paradigm case of Payne's law that generals-in-power liquidate their fascist partners. Payne also notes Romania produced Europe's "leading theoretician of practical, developmental authoritarian corporatism," Mihail Manoilescu.
