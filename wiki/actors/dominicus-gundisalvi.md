# Dominicus Gundisalvi

A twelfth-century archdeacon of Segovia and one of the leading figures of
the Toledo School of Translators, the collaborative effort (drawing on
Christian, Jewish, and Muslim scholars in Reconquista Iberia) that rendered
Arabic philosophical and scientific texts, and Arabic translations of Greek
works, into Latin.

## Role and Significance

Gundisalvi worked alongside Jewish and Mozarabic collaborators (notably Ibn
Daud, known in Latin as Avendauth) using an intermediary method: a
collaborator would render the Arabic text orally into the vernacular, and
Gundisalvi would then produce a polished Latin translation. He is credited
with Latin versions of major works by Avicenna (Ibn Sina) and al-Farabi, and
with his own original philosophical treatises synthesizing Avicennan
metaphysics with Latin Christian thought — making him an important conduit
for the transmission of [[avicennism|Avicennan philosophy]] into the
scholastic West.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Gundisalvi's translation and synthesizing work, Latin access to
Avicenna's metaphysics — later foundational to figures such as Aquinas and
Duns Scotus — would have depended on other, less systematic channels,
likely delaying or altering the shape of thirteenth-century scholastic
philosophy's engagement with Arabic Aristotelianism.

## Historiography

Details of Gundisalvi's biography are sparse and reconstructed mainly from
manuscript attributions and the translations themselves; the working method
and precise division of labour with his Arabic-speaking collaborators
remains a matter of scholarly reconstruction rather than direct testimony,
typical of the Toledo School's translation corpus generally.
