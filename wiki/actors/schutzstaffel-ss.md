# Schutzstaffel (SS)

Founded as Hitler's bodyguard, transformed under [[himmler-heinrich|Himmler]]
into the Third Reich's racial-ideological elite order and police empire. Its
murder of the SA leadership in the [[night-of-the-long-knives-1934|Night of the
Long Knives]] (1934) won it independence; by 1936 it controlled the entire
German police, the [[gestapo|Gestapo]], the SD intelligence service, and the
concentration-camp system, operating outside judicial review as an instrument
of "preventive" terror. In war it became the executive organ of genocide.

## Evans (2005)

The purge of 30 June 1934 — executed by SS men on kill lists Himmler and
Heydrich had drawn up around fabricated putsch evidence — freed the SS from
the SA and made it the regime's instrument of terror. Its "Death's Head" camp
guard units (from December 1934; ~6,000 men at Dachau alone by the late
1930s) ran the consolidated [[nazi-concentration-camps|camp system]] with
effective immunity from prosecution. From 1938 the SS entered the economy
(the German Earth- and Stoneworks quarry company behind Flossenbürg and
Mauthausen) and the annexations (Austrian SS trained at Dachau for the 1934
putsch; Vienna 1938 saw 21,000 arrests within a day of the Anschluss).
Himmler built it as a racial order — nearly a fifth of senior ranks titled
nobility by 1938, *Lebensborn*, pagan ritual, >25% *gottgläubig* — and its SD
intelligence arm under Heydrich incubated the young radicals (Eichmann,
Wisliceny, Dannecker) whose Vienna forced-emigration model prefigured the
machinery of genocide.

## Historiography

Höhne remains the classic apparatus study; Longerich (Himmler) and the
Wachsmann camp histories (*KL*, 2015) are now standard.

## From Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)

**The Waffen-SS.** From the ceremonial Leibstandarte under Sepp Dietrich (a former petrol-pump attendant) to ~600,000 men by 1944, absorbing Eicke's Death's Head camp guards and recruiting first "Germanic" volunteers (the Viking division, 1941), then any available Eastern Europeans as numbers beat ideology. Army professionals despised its wastefulness — Hoepner to Eicke, on being told his men's deaths counted for nothing: "That is the outlook of a butcher" — and army investigations alleged disproportionate Waffen-SS atrocity and rape cases; 34% of its 900,000 wartime personnel were killed. From 1943 parents and priests resisted enlistment as anti-Christian ("the SS was atheist and if we joined it we should go to hell").

**Persecution of homosexual men.** Himmler hunted homosexuals obsessively; Hitler (August 1941): "homosexuality is actually as infectious and as dangerous as the plague." Death penalty within the SS for "unnatural acts" (November 1941) and in the armed forces for "serious" cases (May 1943); repeat civilian offenders went from prison to the camps (pink triangle; 5,000–15,000 camp prisoners over the regime's history, up to half of whom died), subjected to "manliness tests" of brutal outdoor labor and, from Kaltenbrunner's 1943 push, threatened castration.

Its wartime economic empire ([[forced-labor-nazi-germany]]), the camps' reorientation under Pohl's WVHA, and the killing apparatus ([[einsatzgruppen]], [[operation-reinhard-1942]], [[auschwitz]]) are treated on their own pages; the "extermination through labour" agreement with Justice Minister Thierack (18 September 1942) brought 20,000+ court prisoners to Mauthausen's quarry steps, with 35% mortality by the end of 1942.
