# Galerius

**Gaius Galerius Valerius Maximianus** (d. May 311) was Caesar (293) then Augustus (305) of the eastern half of the [[roman-tetrarchy|first tetrarchy]] — Diocletian’s son-in-law (married to Valeria) and the dominant strongman of the later tetrarchic college.

## Career (Mitchell)

Mitchell’s narrative places Galerius over the Illyricum-to-Pontic theater. After Persian success he secured the Danube; in May 305, when [[diocletian|Diocletian]] and [[maximian|Maximian]] retired, Galerius and Constantius Chlorus were promoted to Augusti. Lactantius’s apologetic account (which Mitchell reports with source-critical caution) makes Galerius the decisive influence behind Diocletian’s consultation of court, officers, and the oracle of Apollo at Didyma before the edict of 23 February 303 that opened the [[great-persecution-303|Great Persecution]].

At Carnuntum (308) Galerius briefly remade the college, with himself and [[licinius|Licinius]] as official Augusti, while [[maxentius|Maxentius]] remained unacknowledged in Italy. Galerius died in May 311 after reluctantly rescinding persecution — an act Mitchell treats as a tactical admission of failure rather than conversion.

## Counterfactual significance

Without Galerius’s eastern military base and dynastic marriage, the first tetrarchy’s eastern half lacks its operational backbone; the persecution’s course and the 305–311 succession wars would look different.
