# Narmer

The Upper Egyptian king credited with completing the **unification of Egypt** and
founding the [[early-dynastic-egypt|First Dynasty]] (c. 3100 BCE). He is most
probably the historical figure behind the legendary **Menes** of the king-lists
(Herodotus's Min, Manetho's Menes). Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol.
I, Part 2]] (Ch. XI).

## Role and the evidence

Narmer is the immediate successor of **Scorpion** and the king of the famous
**Narmer Palette** from Hierakonpolis — the single most important monument of the
unification. On the obverse he wears the **White Crown of Upper Egypt** and
smites a Delta captive ("Horus brings captives of Lower Egypt"); on the reverse,
wearing the **Red Crown of Lower Egypt**, he inspects rows of decapitated
enemies. The deliberate pairing of the two crowns is read as commemorating the
**final defeat of the northern kingdom and Narmer's assumption of its crown** —
the symbolic birth of the Two-Lands monarchy. Other monuments (a mace-head, an
ivory cylinder recording a campaign against Libyan Tjehenu-land) reinforce his
role as conqueror.

The identification **Narmer = Menes** rests on an ivory label of his successor
Aha from Naqada bearing the sign *mn* (read as a funerary booth built by Aha for
the deceased Menes — i.e. his predecessor Narmer) and on a Narmer/*mn* sealing
from Abydos. Edwards regards the equation as probable but acknowledges that
"Menes" may be a composite legendary figure pooling Ka, Scorpion, and Narmer.

## Counterfactual significance

Narmer's significance is **structural rather than personal**: the Palette shows
the unification was the culmination of a process (begun under Scorpion, building
on the wealth-concentration of [[predynastic-egypt|Naqada II]]) in which the
Horus-king led a *confederation of Upper Egyptian nomes* against the north.
Whether or not one man named Narmer "founded" Egypt, the figure marks the point
at which a single divine kingship over the whole Nile Valley becomes the
organizing fact of Egyptian history — the durable form that survived three
millennia. The tradition of a single founder-unifier is itself a key piece of
[[divine-kingship|royal ideology]].
