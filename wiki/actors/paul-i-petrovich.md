# Tsar Paul I

## Biography / Description
**Tsar Paul I** (Pavel Petrovich) was the son of Catherine the Great and Peter III, who ruled as Emperor of Russia from 1796 until his assassination in March 1801 (L3224-3226, L3492, L3512-3514). Kept away from government by his mother for decades, he developed a deep hatred of her court and policies, dedicating his brief reign to reversing her decisions (L3492-3494). 

Paul I was characterized by a suspicious, irritable temperament and a style of petty tyranny (L3494). He was fascinated by Prussian military drill, introducing Gatchina-style uniforms and harsh discipline to the guards (L3494, L3512). In 1798, he accepted election as Grand Master of the Knights of Malta, leading Russia into direct geopolitical involvement in the Mediterranean (L3502).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Succession Law of 1797:** During his coronation in 1797, Paul I replaced Peter the Great's succession law (which allowed the monarch to choose any heir) with strict male primogeniture, providing Russia with a stable succession system (L3494-3498).
* **The Three-Day Law of 1797:** In the first state attempt to limit serfdom, Paul proclaimed that serfs should work three days a week for their landlords and three days for themselves, sanctifying Sunday as a day of rest (L3500).
* **Anti-Gentry Measures:** Reacting against Catherine II's Charter to the Nobility, he restored corporal punishment for the gentry and townspeople, increased taxes on the nobility, and relied on the bureaucracy in local administration (L3500).
* **War against France (Second Coalition):** Paul entered the war against revolutionary France in 1798 as a member of the Second Coalition (L3502). He sent a Russian fleet under Theodore Ushakov to capture the Ionian Islands and appointed Field Marshal Alexander Suvorov to lead Austro-Russian forces in northern Italy, where Suvorov drove out the French in 1798–1799 (L3502-3508).
* **Alliance Shift and Cossack India Expedition:** Angered by Austria and Great Britain, Paul withdrew from the coalition, allied with Napoleon in 1800, and dispatched the Don Cossacks on an ill-fated expedition to invade British India (L3510).

## Historical Significance
Paul I's reign marked a turning point in the Russian state's attitude toward the peasantry, introducing the first state regulation of serf labor (L3500). His hostility toward the gentry and unpredictable demotions of high officials provoked a conspiracy among his closest associates and guards (L3512). 

On the night of March 23, 1801, Paul was killed in a palace revolution in St. Petersburg, led by military governor Peter Pahlen and with the passive assent of his son and successor, Alexander I (L3512-3514).
