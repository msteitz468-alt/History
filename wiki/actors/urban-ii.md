# Pope Urban II (Odo of Châtillon)

**Odo of Châtillon**, who reigned as **Pope Urban II** (1088–1099), was the pope who
rebuilt the reform papacy after the disasters of [[gregory-vii|Gregory VII's]] last
years and who proclaimed the [[first-crusade-1095|First Crusade]]. A French noble and
former prior of [[cluny|Cluny]], he carried the [[gregorian-reform|Gregorian]] programme
forward with greater political skill than his fierce predecessor.

## Role and Career

Elected in 1088 while the antipope **Clement III** (Wibert of Ravenna) held Rome and
[[henry-iv-holy-roman-emperor|Henry IV]] dominated Italy, Urban patiently restored the
reform party's fortunes — building alliances (the Norman south, the Tuscan Countess
Matilda, the marriage diplomacy against Henry IV), holding reforming councils, and
gradually recovering Rome. [[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] shows him pursuing reform
on multiple fronts at once: the reconstruction of the Spanish church (the restoration
of Tarragona, 1089), the privileging of the regular canons (Rottenbuch, 1092), and the
backing of the wars against the Muslims in Spain and Sicily.

His defining act was the proclamation of the **First Crusade** at the **Council of
Clermont (27 November 1095)**, following an appeal received at **Piacenza** earlier
that year. Urban fused the pilgrimage tradition, the [[just-war|just-war]] theology of
the reform, and the indulgence into the new institution of penitential holy war — and
set in motion the expedition that took Jerusalem in 1099, two weeks before his death.

## Counterfactual Significance

Urban's importance is twofold. Politically, he salvaged and stabilised the reform
papacy at a moment when Gregory VII's intransigence had nearly wrecked it, ensuring
the survival and consolidation of the [[papal-monarchy|papal monarchy]]. Ideologically,
by inventing the crusade he opened one of the most consequential movements of the
Middle Ages — channelling the [[chivalry|knightly]] violence the Church had long sought
to control into a sanctified war that would shape Christian–Muslim and Latin–Byzantine
relations for centuries. Riley-Smith's reading (NCMH IV) makes him the indispensable
synthesiser, not the lone inventor: the materials lay ready in the reform, but it was
Urban who assembled them.

## From Logan, *A History of the Church in the Middle Ages* (2013)

[[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]] dates Clermont precisely: on **Tuesday 27 November 1095** Urban mounted a platform in a field outside the gates and announced a holy war before a throng “numbering at least in the hundreds.” Exact words unrecorded; four later accounts reconstruct the sermon. He called knights who warred on brothers to become soldiers of Christ against the heathen; Jerusalem/Holy Sepulchre as goal soon included; the crowd cried **“Deus le volt”** — the crusades’ motto. Bishop of Le Puy and others volunteered (possibly choreographed). Urban then preached across France (Limoges, Toulouse, Angers, Tours) and appointed the bishop of Le Puy legate-leader to check princely discord.

Logan’s Cluny note matches this page: before the end of the eleventh century Cluny placed on Peter’s chair two popes of exceptional abilities, **Urban II (1088–99)** and Paschal II (1099–1118). On investiture aftermath, Logan holds that Urban **lowered the rhetoric** of the contest after Gregory’s maximalism.
