# Aristophanes

Master of Athenian **Old Comedy** (active to 388 BCE): directly political theatre that named real citizens, addressed the *demos* via the chorus *parabasis*, and spanned the transition toward quieter, plot-driven Middle/New Comedy after Athens’ defeat.

## Role

Born mid-fifth century; works from the [[peloponnesian-war|Peloponnesian War]] into the early fourth (to 388). Comic theatre was **directly political** in a way tragedy was not; the chorus *parabasis* addresses the audience; real Athenians could be parodied by name. Levi (OHCW Ch. 7): devoted to democracy and comic theatre as perhaps its most characteristic institution; spans Old Comedy’s full development into a second phase after Athens’ fall, when chorus withered and plot/realism grew.

**Career highlights.** Quarrel with **Cleon** began in lost *Babylonians* (426) — Cleon who nearly achieved massacre of Mytilene; Aristophanes showed League cities as slaves at the mill. *Acharnians* (425) trumpets sex, farm life, good old days, politics, religion, town manners; *Knights* next year adds invective. Young Aristophanes attacked old **Cratinus** as a drunk; Cratinus replied 423 with Comedy deserted for Wine-bottles and Drunkenness, winning first over *Clouds* (second). Contemporaries: Eupolis (from 429; drowned in the war), comic poet Plato; competitive plagiarism charges normal. *Peace* (421) fantasy for peace-longing; *Birds* (414) Athenians fleeing endless law-court “cricket song”; *Lysistrata* (411) sex-strike for peace; *Frogs* (405) underworld contest [[aeschylus|Aeschylus]] vs [[euripides|Euripides]] (Aeschylus wins); *Wealth* (388) nearly chorless, social not political humour — transition toward Menander. Annas/Murray: *Clouds* reflects public fear of clever argument and **does not distinguish** [[socrates|Socrates]] from the [[sophists|sophists]]. Murray: *Acharnians* (425) parodied [[herodotus|Herodotus’]] war-causes account.

## Significance

Without Aristophanes, classical Athens lacks its sharpest public satire and modern audiences lose *Lysistrata* and *Clouds* as windows on war, sophists, and Socratic caricature. Levi: making comedy a state event was one of the bravest steps of the Athenian democracy.

## Historiography

OHCW (Levi Ch. 7) is essayistic; comic political “truth” vs invective is a standing interpretive problem.
