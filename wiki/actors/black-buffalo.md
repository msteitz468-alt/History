# Black Buffalo (Lakota leader, early 19th century)

Prominent Lakota (Oceti Sakowin / "Sioux") leader who commanded one of the largest and most powerful bands on the middle Missouri. Controlled commerce and river passage through a system of checkpoints and tolls. Rival of the Partisan.

In the Prologue to Fehrman (2026), Black Buffalo and the Partisan confront French traders at Cedar Island in 1803, demonstrating Lakota dominance just before the Corps of Discovery arrived. Black Buffalo took a more strategic, patient approach than his volatile rival.

Encountered the Corps in 1804 (Teton Sioux meetings). The captains' accounts and trader records show a tense but ultimately non-violent series of councils in which gifts, speeches, and displays of force were exchanged. Black Buffalo's band represented the pre-existing Native power structure that the "vast enterprise" had to navigate.

**Significance**: Embodiment of the fact that the Missouri was not an empty corridor but a contested, governed space. The expedition's success depended on working within (or around) such structures rather than simply "discovering" a wilderness.
