# Ward McAllister

Samuel Ward McAllister (1827–1895). Savannah-born New York social arbiter who, with Caroline Astor, defined "the Four Hundred" — the purported capacity of Mrs. Astor's ballroom and the outer limit of acceptable Gilded Age society. Author of *Society as I Have Found It* (1890). Cooper & Howe use him as the gatekeeper the Vanderbilts had to force — and later draw a parallel to Truman Capote as a subsequent social "king."

## Narrative

McAllister distinguished "nobs" (old birth) from "swells" (new money with style), organized the Patriarchs (from 1872), and partnered Caroline Schermerhorn Astor in policing guest lists. The Commodore himself had been excluded from this world despite his fortune. Alva's 1883 ball, which McAllister helped publicize, cracked the gate. His 1890 memoir overexposed the machinery of society and contributed to his fall; he died in February 1895 as the Astor reign itself was waning under new money and Thorstein Veblen's analytical assault on conspicuous leisure.

## Historiography

Self-mythologizing memoir; contemporary press both used and mocked him. Treat "the Four Hundred" as a constructed social technology, not a natural fact.
