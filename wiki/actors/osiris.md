# Osiris

## Overview

**Osiris** (Egyptian *Wsir*, Greek *Osiris*) is the central god of death, resurrection, the underworld (Duat), and the afterlife in ancient Egyptian religion. Originally a fertility/vegetation god associated with the Nile's annual cycle, he became the archetypal dead king who triumphs over death, model for all deceased Egyptians (especially through "Osirification" in funerary rites). His myth — murder and dismemberment by Seth, reconstitution and resurrection by Isis, judgment of the dead — underpins Egyptian beliefs in moral order (Ma'at), eternal life, and the cycle of renewal.

He is "lord of the living" in the afterlife, "foremost of the westerners" (the dead), and fused with Ra in the nightly solar journey through the Duat. Central to the *Book of the Dead* (Chapter 125 judgment, hymns, offerings).

## Myth and Role (from primary sources)

- **Myth**: Son of Geb (earth) and Nut (sky). Killed and dismembered by brother Seth (chaos, desert, foreign lands). Body parts scattered; Isis (sister-wife) and Nephthys search, reassemble, and revive him (with Anubis's embalming help). Horus (son) avenges him, defeats Seth. Osiris rules the Duat as judge and king of the dead.

- **Judgment (Book of the Dead Ch. 125)**: The deceased's heart is weighed against Ma'at's feather before 42 assessor gods. Negative confessions ("I have not killed... stolen... lied..."). Thoth records; Osiris presides (with Isis/Nephthys, Horus sons). Justified soul becomes "Osiris N" (personalized), joins the gods in eternal fields.

- **Attributes**: Green/black skin (fertility, Nile silt, resurrection); Atef crown (plumes, ram horns); crook and flail (kingship, shepherd of dead); djed pillar (stability, backbone); mummiform body.

- **Cult**: Abydos (main center, pilgrimage, mystery plays re-enacting myth); Busiris; Philae (Ptolemaic). Linked to kings (dead pharaoh = Osiris; living = Horus). Democratized in Middle/New Kingdoms for non-royals via spells.

- **Syncretism**: With Ra (as Ra-Osiris in Duat); later Serapis (Hellenistic Osiris-Apis fusion).

## Historiography and Value

Primary sources: Pyramid Texts (Old Kingdom royal spells identifying king with Osiris), Coffin Texts (Middle Kingdom), Book of the Dead papyri (New Kingdom vignettes and spells), temple reliefs (Abydos, Philae), stelae, tomb art.

This source (Budge/Romer edition) provides the full spell corpus where Osiris is invoked for protection, vindication, and union. Romer's intro notes the "democratization" of Osirian afterlife (from royal monopoly to elite access) and how spells function as magical technology.
