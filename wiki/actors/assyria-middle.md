# The Middle Assyrian Kingdom

The first **territorial Assyrian state** — the kingdom that grew out of the
city-state of **Ashur** when [[ashur-uballit-i|Ashur-uballit I]] broke free of
[[mitanni|Mitanni]] in the 14th century, and the institutional ancestor of the later
[[neo-assyrian-empire|Neo-Assyrian empire]]. This volume covers its rise and its
first imperial century; its Early-Iron-Age phase under Tiglath-pileser I closes the
coverage. Treated across [[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]] (Chs. XVIII,
XXV, XXXI).

## From vassal to great power

For three centuries Ashur had been a vassal of the Hurrian kings (see
[[ashur-uballit-i|Ashur-uballit I]]); its liberation was triggered by the
[[shuppiluliuma-i|Hittite]] destruction of Mitanni. Under Ashur-uballit's
successors — Enlil-nirari, Arik-den-ili, then **Adad-nirari I** and **Shalmaneser
I** — Assyria fought the rump Hurrian kingdom of **Khanigalbat** (the house of
Šattuara and Wasašatta) to the finish and **annexed it**, pushing the frontier to
the Euphrates and making Assyria a four-square rival of Kassite Babylonia (see the
Assyrian military chapter, Section 3). Its apex came with
**[[tukulti-ninurta-i|Tukulti-Ninurta I]]**, who defeated and captured the Kassite
king Kaštiliaš IV and **sacked Babylon itself** — celebrated in the chauvinist
**Tukulti-Ninurta Epic** — and with the Early-Iron-Age
revival under **Tiglath-pileser I** (Section 5).

## Institutions

The volume's society chapters make the Middle Assyrian kingdom one of the wiki's
best-documented Late Bronze states. Its character is harsh and bureaucratic:
- the **Middle Assyrian Laws**, with their "heavy fines and convict-labour... savage
  beatings and ghastly physical mutilations" — "a hard and primitively-minded
  society";
- the **harem edicts**, a regime of "compulsory sycophantism" in which the royal
  residence "was more of a prison than a palace";
- land-sale by public proclamation and royal survey, paralleled in
  [[kassites|Kassite]] Babylonia and at Nuzi — pointing to shared, perhaps eastern,
  legal influences across the period;
- a distinctive **"Middle Assyrian" glyptic** — the first native Assyrian seal
  style, full of combat between demons and monsters, prefiguring the later Assyrian
  taste for "protection from the assault of hostile powers."

## Significance

The Middle Assyrian kingdom is where **Assyria becomes Assyria**: a militarized,
expansionist territorial state with a documented law-code and court, the eastern
pillar of the [[interpolity-law|great-powers system]], and the entity that survives
the [[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]] (battered, pushed back by the Aramaeans) to
re-emerge as the first world empire of the Iron Age. See [[neo-assyrian-empire]] for
the continuation.

## Historiography

Built from royal annals (which here begin to take shape, from Arik-den-ili onward),
the Synchronistic History and Chronicle P, the law and edict tablets, and the
literary "epics." The Assyrian and Babylonian chronicle traditions frequently
disagree (e.g. on which reign a given war belongs to), a standing source-criticism
problem. See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
