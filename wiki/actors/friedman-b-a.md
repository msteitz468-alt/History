B. A. Friedman is a U.S. Marine Corps practitioner and theorist who authored the modern doctrinal works *On Tactics: A Theory of Victory in Battle* (2017) and its companion *On Operations*. With sixteen years as a tactician (infantry then artillery) and academic study in strategic theory, Friedman sought to rectify the lack of organizing structure for tactics comparable to Clausewitz’s *On War* for strategy.

## On Tactics
Friedman provides a timeless theory of tactics organized around nine Tactical Tenets (Maneuver, Mass, Firepower, Tempo, Deception, Surprise, Confusion, Shock, Moral Cohesion) and five Tactical Concepts (Culminating Point of Victory, the Offense/Defense/Initiative, Command and Control, Environment and Geography, and the link between tactics and strategy). 

"The intent of this tactical system is that the tactician arranges the physical means at his disposal in terms of maneuver, mass, firepower, and tempo to inflict mental effects in the mind of the opposing tactician and his units: deception, surprise, confusion, and shock. The accumulation of these mental effects will at some point overwhelm the enemy unit, at which point they will lose moral cohesion."

"Victory in the narrow scope of this work... is usually achieved when the enemy force has lost its moral cohesion and fled..."

Grounded claims and structure are detailed in the source page [[sources/friedman-on-tactics-2017]].

## On Operations

Companion volume (2021) that completes the tactics–operations–strategy bridge. Friedman distinguishes the problematic “operational level of war” (a post-Napoleonic invention that artificially severs tactics from strategy and distorts civil-military relations) from “operational art,” which he defines as the practical work performed by staffs through six core disciplines (administration, information, operations, fire support, logistics, and command & control) to enable tactics in the service of strategy.

The book surveys the historical development (Napoleonic origins → German General Staff → Soviet theory → American adoption), offers an explicit theory of operational art, details the disciplines, provides a campaign taxonomy, and illustrates with five case studies (Austerlitz, Königgrätz, Atlantic Campaign 1914–18, Battle of Britain, Operation Watchtower/Guadalcanal).

Key argument: Success attributed to the “operational level” is more accurately credited to the rise of competent general staffs. Treating operations as a distinct “level” has amputated tactics from strategy with negative consequences.
