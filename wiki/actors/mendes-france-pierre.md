# Pierre Mendès France

French Prime Minister (June 1954 – February 1955). Negotiated the Geneva Accords that ended French involvement in Indochina.

## Role

Took office in the immediate aftermath of Dien Bien Phu with a mandate to end the war. At Geneva he drove a hard but realistic bargain: French withdrawal from northern Vietnam in exchange for a ceasefire, partition at the 17th parallel, and a promise of elections in 1956 (which the U.S. and South Vietnam later refused to hold). His famous "we must go fast" approach reflected both military reality and domestic French exhaustion.

## Significance

Mendès France's government extricated France from Indochina with a settlement that, while a defeat, avoided total collapse or wider war. He simultaneously faced the Algerian crisis and attempted broader reforms. His brief tenure is remembered as a moment of clarity amid the Fourth Republic's paralysis.
