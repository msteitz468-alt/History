# Oneota

## Overview

**Oneota** is a general archaeological label for western Great Lakes societies of roughly **A.D. 1000–1500** that exhibited a considerable range of variation in structure, complexity, agricultural reliance, and habitats. Cordell & Smith (CHNPA I.1 ch. 4) treat Oneota as a northern, **non-Mississippian** maize-based trajectory after A.D. 800 — part of the northern tier spanning the Northeast and Great Lakes — not as ranked Mississippian chiefdoms (deferred to ch. 5).

## Geographic Distribution

Agricultural Oneota in southern Wisconsin/Minnesota and parts of Illinois/Iowa. Settlements clustered on lakeshores, major waterways, and wetlands combining wild resources and good soils; frost-edge microclimates and ridged fields/mounding for northern corn. Ideal locales (e.g. Center Creek/Willow Creek, Blue Earth River, MN) supported denser settlements of 50–200 people.

## Settlement and Economy

Sometimes fortified villages of perhaps **70–90 people** (larger and longer-occupied than pre-1000 antecedents). Vosburg site assemblage: bison/elk/deer plus wetland fauna, scapula hoes, storage pits, corn/beans/sunflower — mixed maize horticulture with hunting and wetland resources, enabled by northern flint / eastern eight-row maize (*enabled by:* [[maize-domestication]]).

## Interaction with Mississippian societies

Material culture changes reflect contact with Mississippian societies to the south; the **nature and extent of Mississippian influence on Oneota cultural development remain actively debated** — preserve this hedge; do not collapse Oneota into Mississippian. *concurrent_with:* [[mississippian-culture]]; *part_of:* [[north-american-agricultural-transition]].
