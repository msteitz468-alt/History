# Fidel Castro

Cuban revolutionary leader, Prime Minister (1959–1976) and President (1976–2008) of Cuba. Born 1926 or 1927 in Birán, Oriente province; trained as a lawyer. Led the 26th of July Movement against Fulgencio Batista. After the 1959 victory, consolidated power, declared the revolution socialist (1961), allied with the Soviet Union, survived the Bay of Pigs invasion and the Cuban Missile Crisis, and projected Cuban power and ideology across the Third World. Longtime patron and partner (then sometimes rival in strategy) of Che Guevara.

## Role in the Cuban Revolution

Organized the attack on the Moncada Barracks (1953), imprisoned, amnestied, exiled to Mexico. Masterminded the Granma expedition and the subsequent Sierra Maestra guerrilla war. Combined military pressure with political mobilization and urban underground work. After victory, rapidly centralized authority, carried out agrarian reform and nationalizations, and suppressed opposition. Survived U.S. attempts at regime change.

## Relationship with Che Guevara

Met Che in Mexico 1955; immediately recognized shared commitment. Che became one of his most trusted military commanders. After 1959, Che's radicalism and internationalism sometimes outpaced Fidel's state-building pragmatism. Che's 1965 departure letter was respectful but final. Fidel later invoked Che's example to demand sacrifice from Cubans during the Special Period. Anderson documents both the deep personal bond and the strategic divergences.

## International Dimension and Legacy

Under Castro, Cuba became a major actor in Cold War Third World politics: support for African liberation movements, involvement in Angola, training of guerrillas, medical internationalism. Survived the collapse of the Soviet Union; handed power to Raúl in 2008. Died 2016. His rule defined modern Cuba and made the island a symbol of resistance to U.S. hegemony for many in the global South and left.

## Historiography

Polarized: hagiographic in official Cuban accounts and some left scholarship; demonized in U.S. and exile literature. Anderson's biography of Che provides a close-up, non-Cuban perspective on Fidel as seen through Che's eyes and the inner circle. Broader Cold War sources (Leffler/Westad) treat him as a key Third World leader whose agency both served and complicated Soviet strategy.
