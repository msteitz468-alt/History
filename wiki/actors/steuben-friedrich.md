# Friedrich Wilhelm von Steuben (1730–1794)

The Prussian-trained drillmaster who professionalized the **[[continental-army|Continental
Army]]**. Born in Magdeburg, he served in the Prussian army from age 17, saw the Seven Years'
War on the staff of **Frederick the Great**, and was later forced out as the army downsized.
(The "baron" title he adopted "was spurious.") Unable to secure Austrian or French commissions,
he met **Benjamin Franklin** in Paris, who convinced him to sail to America; he reached
[[valley-forge-1777|Valley Forge]] in early 1778.

## The Valley Forge transformation

Speaking little English — his "gruff style became a source of amusement to the men" — Steuben
nonetheless impressed [[washington-george|Washington]], who entrusted him with training the army.
His method was practical: he formed a **model company of 100 men**, drilled them personally, and
issued installments of instructions translated into English, which propagated through the ranks.
The result was his **"Blue Book"** — *Regulations for the Order and Discipline of the Troops of
the United States* — and an army transformed "toward an effective military force in the European
tradition." Congress made him **major general and inspector general** on 5 May 1778. The proof
came weeks later at [[battle-of-monmouth-1778|Monmouth]], where "Steuben's drill served them
well" and the Continentals held against British regulars in open combat.
