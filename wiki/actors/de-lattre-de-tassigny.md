# Jean de Lattre de Tassigny

Charismatic French general (1889–1952), commander-in-chief in Indochina 1950–1952. Nicknamed "King Jean" or "the French Montgomery" by contemporaries. Restored French military morale and operational effectiveness after the disastrous 1950 border defeats (RC4). Combined aggressive mobile operations with political efforts to build the Associated State of Vietnam's army.

## Role and Impact

De Lattre arrived at a moment of crisis after the Chinese-backed Viet Minh victories along the Chinese border. He reorganized the Expeditionary Corps, emphasized elite units and air mobility, won a series of tactical victories in 1951 (Vinh Yen, Mao Khe, Day River), and persuaded the U.S. to increase aid dramatically. His personal style — flamboyant, demanding, inspirational — temporarily reversed the sense of inevitable decline. Illness forced his return to France; he died of cancer in 1952.

## Legacy in the War

His successes demonstrated that France could still win set-piece battles when it concentrated resources, but they did not alter the underlying political problem: the Viet Minh retained the strategic initiative in the countryside and the legitimacy of nationalist leadership. After his death the war reverted to stalemate and then deterioration.
