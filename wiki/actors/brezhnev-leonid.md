# Leonid Brezhnev
## Biography / Description
Leonid Brezhnev was born in 1906 in Kamenskoe (now Kamianske, Ukraine) to a Russian metalworker's family. He rose through the Party ranks under Stalin and Khrushchev, serving in key regional posts in Ukraine, Moldova, and Kazakhstan, and managing the Virgin Lands campaign. He became a secretary of the Central Committee and Chairman of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet. In October 1964, Brezhnev led the coalition that deposed Khrushchev. He assumed the top Party post as First Secretary (restored to General Secretary in 1966) and ruled the Soviet Union for eighteen years as part of a collective leadership alongside Prime Minister Alexei Kosygin. In his later years, he also assumed the presidency (1977) and the rank of Marshal of the Soviet Union (1976). He died on November 10, 1982, after years of failing health.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **Reversal of Khrushchev's Reforms (1964–1965):** Brezhnev repealed Khrushchev's administrative reorganizations, including the division of the Party into agricultural and industrial branches, restoring central ministries.
- **Invasion of Czechoslovakia (1968):** Brezhnev ordered the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia to crush the liberal Prague Spring. This action formulated the **Brezhnev Doctrine**, which asserted the right of the Soviet Union to intervene in any socialist country where socialist rule was threatened.
- **Detente (1970s):** Brezhnev pursued a policy of detente (peaceful relaxation of tensions) with the United States under Nixon and Ford, signing the S.A.L.T. I (1972) and S.A.L.T. II (1979) arms limitation treaties, and participating in the Helsinki Accords (1975).
- **Military Build-up:** Under Brezhnev, the Soviet Union reached strategic nuclear parity with the United States and expanded its navy into a powerful global fleet, incurring enormous economic costs.
- **Invasion of Afghanistan (1979):** In late December 1979, Brezhnev authorized the invasion of Afghanistan to support a struggling Marxist government, embroiling the USSR in a costly, deadlocked conflict.
- **Suppression of Dissent:** Brezhnev's government pursued a hard line against cultural and political dissidents, reversing much of the Khrushchev thaw, as illustrated by the trial of Sinyavsky and Daniel (1965–1966) and the exile of Solzhenitsyn and Brodsky.

## Historical Significance
Brezhnev's eighteen-year tenure was characterized by stability and the rise of the Soviet Union to its peak of global military and imperial power. However, it was also marked by a profound domestic slowdown, known as the **Brezhnev Stagnation**. Riasanovsky notes that while living standards rose in the early years and the Soviet Union closed the economic gap with the US, the late 1970s and early 1980s saw a severe deceleration in growth, energy supply issues, agricultural failures, and rising social problems like alcoholism (Lines 6629–6648). Brezhnev's refusal to implement fundamental structural reforms left a legacy of systemic rot that contributed directly to the eventual collapse of the Soviet Union.
