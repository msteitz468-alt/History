# Alfred Pleasonton

Alfred Pleasonton (1824–1897) was a Union cavalry commander who led the Army of the Potomac's Cavalry Corps for much of 1863, presiding over its transformation from a force routinely outclassed by J.E.B. Stuart's Confederate horsemen into a credible, aggressive arm. His raid-turned-battle at Brandy Station (June 1863), the largest cavalry engagement of the war, marked that turning point, and his cavalry screened and skirmished ahead of the army through the Gettysburg Campaign.

Pleasonton is best remembered as the patron who advanced the career of [[custer-george-a|George Armstrong Custer]], promoting the young officer directly to brigadier general in June 1863 on the strength of his aggressiveness and battlefield initiative — an unusually rapid elevation that placed the 23-year-old Custer in brigade command in time for Gettysburg.

Reassigned away from the eastern cavalry command in early 1864 as [[grant-ulysses-s|Grant]] and [[sheridan-philip-h|Sheridan]] restructured Union cavalry doctrine toward independent offensive operations, Pleasonton finished the war commanding in the Trans-Mississippi theater, notably helping repel Sterling Price's 1864 Missouri raid.
