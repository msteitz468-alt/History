# The House of Ibelin

The **Ibelins** were the most prominent noble house of Lusignan Cyprus and, earlier, of
the kingdom of Jerusalem (lords of Ibelin/Yavne, Ramla, Beirut, Arsur, Jaffa). From the
regency of Philip and John of Ibelin for young Henry I through the
**[[war-of-the-lombards-1228|War of the Lombards]]** against Frederick II, they shaped
Cypriot baronial politics; after the mainland losses their interests concentrated on the
island.

## Origins and Jerusalem

Pre-twelfth-century origins unknown; Chartres-viscount tradition fails scrutiny; onomastics
suggest Italian background (Pisa/Sardinia; Rudt de Collenberg proposed Norman Sicily).
Founder Barisan/Balian the Elder: castellan of Jaffa; Fulk granted Ibelin (Yavne) in the
early 1140s; marriage brought Ramla. Balian married Maria Comnena (1177); their sons by
Maria were half-brothers of Queen Isabella I, locking Jerusalem pre-eminence. The family
were **not** original Cyprus settlers and had opposed the Lusignans before Hattin.

## Rise in Cyprus

First recorded foothold: conducting niece Alice of Champagne to marry Hugh I (1210). By
September 1217 they took precedence in the Cypriot High Court. Philip of Ibelin became
Alice's lieutenant (1218); after quarrel with Alice (~1223/24) the Ibelins clung to power
despite her attempts to install Bohemond or Aimery Barlais. John of Beirut assumed control
on Philip's death (1227/28) — to enemies a usurper. Victory in the War of the Lombards
(1229–33) entrenched them: between the 1240s and 1290s all constables of Cyprus were
Ibelins; they supplied all seneschals until the 1360s; Hugh III, Hugh IV and others married
in. Five branches (from John of Beirut and Philip) concentrated in Cyprus as Syria fell.

## Character of power

Edbury rejects a "mayor of the palace" reading after 1233: the Ibelins appear as loyal
counsellors and close kinsmen, though they cloaked violent methods in legalism during the
minority (refusal of Alice's nominee, lynching of Baldwin of Belleme, war against the
suzerain's officers). Philip of Novara is their panegyrist. Inter-Ibelin conflict appeared
in the War of St Sabas (Acre, 1250s) and clearly in Cyprus in 1306 (most Ibelins backed
Amaury against Henry II; only seneschal Philip of Ibelin stood firmly royalist). James of
Ibelin claimed in 1271 that Cypriot men had "more often served the house of Ibelin outside
the kingdom than… the king."

## End

The Genoese war of 1373–74 hastened the demise: the last Ibelin lord of Arsur was beheaded;
Nicholas of Ibelin went hostage to Genoa in 1374 and disappears — "the last known male
bearer of the family name."
