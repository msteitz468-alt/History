# Alfred the Great

King of Wessex (r. 871–899), the only English monarch to bear the epithet "the Great," who halted the Viking Great Heathen Army's conquest of Anglo-Saxon England after his decisive victory at the Battle of Edington (878) forced the Danish leader Guthrum to accept baptism and a negotiated partition of England (the Danelaw boundary).

## Role

Alfred reorganized Wessex's defenses through a system of fortified burhs and a reformed military rotation (the fyrd), built a rudimentary navy, and — recognizing the collapse of Latin learning after generations of Viking raiding — sponsored translations of key Latin texts (including Gregory's *Pastoral Care*, Boethius's *Consolation of Philosophy*, and Orosius's history) into Old English, commissioning or inspiring the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle as a vernacular historical record.

## Counterfactual Significance

Had Wessex fallen alongside Mercia, East Anglia, and Northumbria to the Great Heathen Army in the 870s, no independent English kingdom would have remained to anchor the eventual unification of England under Alfred's successors; his military survival and cultural patronage are jointly credited as the foundation of both English political continuity and its vernacular literary tradition.
