# Valerian

Roman emperor (r. 253–260) and father of [[gallienus]], with whom he ruled as co-emperor during the depths of the Crisis of the Third Century. Valerian campaigned against the Sasanian Persians under Shapur I but was captured at the Battle of Edessa (260) — the only Roman emperor ever taken prisoner by a foreign enemy — and reportedly died in humiliating captivity, an event Persian and later Roman sources treat as a profound symbolic disaster for Roman prestige.

## Role

His capture left [[gallienus]] to rule alone amid simultaneous pressure from Germanic invasions in the west and the secession of the breakaway [[gallic-empire|Gallic Empire]], illustrating how a single battlefield catastrophe could destabilize an already fracturing imperial system.

## Counterfactual Significance

Valerian's capture is often treated as the nadir of third-century Roman weakness; a Roman victory or negotiated peace at Edessa might have preserved greater imperial authority in the east and altered the pace of the empire's subsequent fragmentation and recovery under [[aurelian]].
