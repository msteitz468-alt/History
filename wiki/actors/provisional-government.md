# Russian Provisional Government

The **Russian Provisional Government** was the temporary governing body of Russia from the abdication of Emperor Nicholas II during the February Revolution until its overthrow by the Bolsheviks in the October Revolution of 1917. It sought to transition Russia into a liberal, constitutional democracy but was crippled by its shared authority ("dual power") with the Petrograd Soviet and its decisions to continue World War I.

## Biography / Description

The Provisional Government was created on March 12, 1917, by a committee of the Fourth State Duma in consultation with the Petrograd Soviet. It was initially headed by Prince George Lvov, a moderate liberal and chairman of the Union of Zemstva and Towns, and was dominated by the Constitutional Democratic (Cadet) Party, with Alexander Kerensky serving as the only socialist minister. Following cabinet crises, Lvov resigned in July 1917, and Kerensky became Prime Minister, leading a government with a socialist majority.

The government suffered from a lack of legitimacy and administrative power. It considered itself "provisional," possessing authority only to govern the country until a fully democratic **Constituent Assembly** could meet to draft a permanent constitution. As a result, it postponed key decisions—such as land redistribution and minority rights—which alienated the population. Furthermore, having abolished the tsarist police, the government possessed no reliable administrative apparatus and had to rely on the Petrograd Soviet to influence the soldiers and workers. It was overthrown when Bolshevik Red Guards stormed its headquarters at the Winter Palace on November 8, 1917.

## Key Decisions and Achievements

### Liberal Reforms and Civil Liberties
During its eight months in power, the Provisional Government introduced sweeping democratic reforms:
- Granted full equality before the law for all citizens, abolishing class, religious, and ethnic restrictions.
- Established complete freedom of speech, press, assembly, association, and strikes.
- Introduced democratic local self-government, extending the zemstvo system to the *volost* (township) level.
- Declared Poland independent and granted autonomy to other national minorities.
- Enacted labor reforms, including the eight-hour day for some categories of workers, and abolished capital punishment.

### The Decision to Continue World War I
Believing that a free Russia had a moral obligation to its Western allies and that a successful war effort would unify the nation, the Provisional Government chose to continue World War I. It launched the disastrous **Kerensky Offensive** in June 1917, which failed and triggered widespread mutinies, destroying the army's remaining discipline.

### Postponement of Land Reform
The government recognized that land should belong to the peasantry but insisted that a comprehensive land transfer could only be legally enacted by the Constituent Assembly. This policy failed to satisfy the peasants, who began seizing gentry estates on their own, provoking clashes within the cabinet (notably between Lvov and Minister of Agriculture Victor Chernov) and further destabilizing the country.

## Historical Significance

The Russian Provisional Government's historical significance lies in its role as Russia's brief experiment with liberal democracy. It demonstrated the difficulty of maintaining a moderate, legalistic administration in a country undergoing a massive social upheaval, particularly under the strains of total war. By refusing to unilaterally exit the war or distribute land, and by repeatedly delaying the elections to the Constituent Assembly, the Provisional Government created a power vacuum that the Bolsheviks successfully exploited to establish a one-party dictatorship.
