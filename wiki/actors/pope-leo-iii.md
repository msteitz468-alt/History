# Pope Leo III (r. 795–816)

**Leo III** was the pope who crowned [[charlemagne|Charles the Great]] emperor on Christmas
Day 800 (see [[coronation-of-charlemagne-800]]). His pontificate's defining crisis — a violent
assault by a Roman faction — was the proximate trigger for the imperial coronation itself.

## The Assault of 799 and the Coronation

Per [[sources/nelson-king-and-emperor-2019|Janet Nelson]]:

- On **25 April 799** Leo was ambushed by insiders — "Paschal at his head, Campulus at his
  feet" (Paschal a nephew of [[pope-hadrian-i|Hadrian I]]; Campulus Leo's own treasurer). The
  attackers "tried to put out his eyes … cut off his tongue"; his recovery was reported as
  miraculous.
- Charles received the news calmly, "made for Paderborn and awaited Leo's arrival." Leo came
  bringing "no fewer than 203 of his Roman counsellors." [[alcuin|Alcuin]] supplied the legal
  doctrine that resolved the charges against Leo: "the apostolic see judges but cannot be
  judged" (from canons attributed to Pope Sylvester).
- At the **coronation of 800**, Nelson argues, "Charles and Leo between them … carefully
  planned and stage-managed the sequence of rituals. Any argument to the contrary simply
  can't be sustained." Leo had walked out to meet Charles at Mentana (c. 20 km from Rome) —
  double the customary distance — "sending an unmistakable message: the new emperor was to be
  in a different league." The *Vita Leonis* uniquely adds that Leo also anointed Charles's son
  Charles the Younger as king the same day.
