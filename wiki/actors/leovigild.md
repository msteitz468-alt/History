# Leovigild

**Leovigild** was the Visigothic King of Hispania and Septimania from 568 to 586 CE. He is widely regarded as the most successful military commander and administrative reformer of the Visigothic Kingdom, responsible for uniting the Iberian Peninsula under Visigothic rule, annexing the Sueve Kingdom, reforming the administration, and initiating Visigothic territorial law.

---

## Reign and Achievements

Leovigild ruled initially as co-emperor with his brother Liuva I (568–572) and then as sole ruler. He inherited a fragmented kingdom, with northern regions (Basques, Cantabrians) in rebellion, the Byzantine Empire occupying southern Spain (**Spania**), and the Sueves ruling the northwest.

### Military Unification
Leovigild waged continuous military campaigns to establish central authority:
*   **Northern Campaigns:** Subdued the Basques and Cantabrians, founding the city of Victoriacum (modern Vitoria) to control the region.
*   **Suevic Annexation (585):** Intervened in Suevic dynastic disputes, defeated the Sueve king Miro, and annexed the **Sueve Kingdom of Gallaecia** into the Visigothic state, ending its independence.
*   **Byzantine Containment:** Recovered Medina Sidonia and Córdoba from the Byzantines, containing the imperial province of Spania to a narrow coastal strip.

### Administrative and Legal Reforms
Leovigild sought to elevate the Visigothic crown, adopting Roman imperial style:
*   **Royal Ceremonial:** He was the first Visigothic king to wear royal robes, sit on a throne, and establish Toledo as a permanent capital.
*   **Coinage:** Minted gold coins (*tremisses*) bearing his own image and name, rather than the name of the Byzantine emperor, asserting full sovereignty.
*   **Legal Code (Codex Revisus):** Revised the *Codex Euricianus*, compiling the *Codex Revisus* (also known as the Law of Leovigild), which repealed the ban on marriages between Goths and Hispano-Romans, facilitating the social unification of the two groups.

---

## Religious Policy and Hermenegild's Rebellion

Leovigild was a committed **Arian** who recognized that religious division prevented political stability. Unlike his successors, he attempted to unify the kingdom under Arianism:
*   **Arian Synod of Toledo (580):** Leovigild convened an Arian synod that eased conversions from Catholicism, removing the requirement of rebaptism. 
*   **Hermenegild's Rebellion (579–584):** Leovigild appointed his eldest son, **Hermenegild**, to rule southern Spain from Seville. Hermenegild married the Frankish princess Ingund (a Catholic) and converted to Catholicism under the influence of Bishop Leander of Seville. Hermenegild rebelled, styling himself king, and sought alliances with the Sueves, Byzantines, and Franks.
*   Leovigild blockaded Seville, defeated the Suevic relief force, and captured Hermenegild in 584. Hermenegild was exiled and executed in Valencia in 585 for refusing to return to Arianism.

---

## Significance

Leovigild left a consolidated, unified Iberian state but a deeply unsettled religious environment. He realized at the end of his life that Arianism could not serve as the basis for national unity. His younger son and successor, **[[reccared-i|Reccared I]]**, abandoned Leovigild’s religious policy and established a Catholic monarchy at the **[[third-council-of-toledo|Third Council of Toledo (589)]]**, completing the unification Leovigild had built.
