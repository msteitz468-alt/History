# Scipio Aemilianus

The destroyer of both **Carthage** (146) and **Numantia** (133) — the dominant
Roman general and statesman of the mid-second century, and the patron of
[[polybius]]. Treated across [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Ch. 5 (Harris).

## Role and career

Born a son of **L. Aemilius Paullus** (the victor of Pydna) and adopted into the
Cornelii Scipiones — grandson by adoption of [[scipio-africanus|Scipio Africanus]].
He served as military tribune in the early years of the
[[third-punic-war-149-146bce|Third Punic War]], where Polybius' (and Appian's)
hero-worship credits him with the only Roman successes. Elected **consul for 147
illegally** (under-age, by special popular vote — an important precedent for
*provincia* assignment by the people), he instituted the blockade that took
**Carthage** in 146, razing the city and refusing a share of the booty in
ostentatious imitation of his father after Pydna.

Recalled to the unfinished Spanish war, he was again elected **consul (134)**
against the law and reduced **Numantia** by siege and starvation (133; see
[[numantine-war-153-133bce]]). On returning he aligned against the agrarian
programme of his brother-in-law **Tiberius Gracchus**, and died suddenly in 129 —
a death widely rumoured to be murder. (His political role in the Gracchan crisis
belongs to CAH IX.)

## Counterfactual significance

Aemilianus is the executor of Roman *Machtpolitik* at its height: the deliberate
destruction of two great resisting cities. His twice-illegal consulships and his
popular *provincia* grant prefigure the personalised commands that destabilised the
late Republic. His circle (the "Scipionic circle," with Polybius and Panaetius) was
also a conduit of Greek learning into Roman elite culture — the Hellenization theme
of [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Ch. 12.

## Historiography

The sources are saturated with Scipionic celebration: **Polybius** (his friend and
companion at Carthage) and **Appian**, whose *Punica* and *Iberica* are "dominated
by hero-worship." Harris cautions that this tradition exaggerated his
indispensability — Manilius did real work at Carthage before Scipio arrived. See
[[third-punic-war-149-146bce]] and [[nature-of-roman-imperialism]].
