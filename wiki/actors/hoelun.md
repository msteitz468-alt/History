# Hö’elün (Hoelun)

## Overview

**Hö’elün** (Hoelun), mother of [[genghis-khan|Temüjin / Genghis Khan]], was an Olkhunuud maiden betrothed or newly wed to Chiledu of the Merkid when [[yesugei|Yesügei]] and his brothers abducted her (Weatherford: she was about sixteen or younger). The *Secret History* has her urge Chiledu to flee—other maidens available—thrust her blouse for him to keep her scent (soul), and scream so loud she stirred the Onon and shook woods and valley. After Yesügei’s poisoning death, no levirate brother took her; widows of a previous khan excluded her from the spring ancestral ceremonial meal, cutting her “not only from the food itself but from membership in the family” (Weatherford after the *Secret History*). The clan planned to leave her and seven children; only a low-ranking old man objected and was speared.

She seized Yesügei’s horsehair Spirit Banner, mounted, and temporarily shamed the deserters; they slipped away at night with the animals. She then foraged—fruits, roots with a juniper stick—while Temüjin took rats with bone-tipped arrows and fish with sewing-needle hooks; Juvaini’s tradition of dog- and mouse-skins is cited by Weatherford. That ordeal is the emotional and moral center of Temüjin’s formation in Weatherford’s account.

## Role

- **Matriarch and scold.** After Temüjin and Khasar killed half-brother Begter (to forestall levirate takeover of Hö’elün by Sochigel’s son), she delivered the famous monologue comparing her sons to animals and cursing them with companion only their shadow: “Destroyer! Destroyer! You came from my hot womb clutching a clot of blood in your hand.”
- **Fictive-kinship policy instrument.** Temüjin had her adopt orphan boys from defeated Merkid, Tayichi’ud, Tatars, and Jurkin as younger brothers—integrating enemies into the household.
- **Late political actor.** After 1206 she lived about a day’s journey from the court with her youngest son. When shaman Teb Tengeri bound Khasar, she rode overnight, freed him, ripped open her *deel*, and shamed Genghis with her breasts and a Begter-echo tirade; he restored Khasar’s freedom and some followers. Weatherford: “Soon after the fight with her son, Hoelun, who was probably in her late fifties, died.” Her estate was then seized by Teb Tengeri and his brothers (perhaps with implied consent), pushing Temüge Otchigin aside—a grievance that fed the later break with the shaman after [[borte|Börte]]’s warning.
- **Kinship network.** Relatives through Hö’elün deserted [[togrul-ong-khan|Ong Khan]] for Temüjin after Baljuna (Weatherford).

## Historiography

Primary: *Secret History*. Weatherford (2004) amplifies foraging survival, levirate fear, Spirit Banner scene, and the late Khasar/Teb Tengeri episode. Juvaini supplies the skins-of-dogs-and-mice tradition. Yuan ancestral cult later paired Yesügei and Hö’elün in Chinese-style temple honors.
