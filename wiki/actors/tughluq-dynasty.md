## Role and Background

The Tughluq dynasty ruled the Delhi Sultanate from 1320, when Ghiyas-ud-din Tughluq overthrew the short-lived Khalji successor regime, until its effective collapse after Timur's devastating 1398 sack of Delhi, with a nominal remnant persisting to 1414.

## Reign and Overextension

The dynasty's most consequential ruler, [[actors/muhammad-bin-tughluq|Muhammad bin Tughluq]], pursued a series of ambitious and ultimately disastrous administrative experiments — including the forced transfer of the capital to Daulatabad in the Deccan and the introduction of token (non-precious-metal) currency — that strained the sultanate's finances and provoked widespread revolt, exposing the structural fragility beneath its vast territorial extent, which briefly reached further south into the Deccan than any earlier Delhi regime. His successor Firoz Shah Tughluq stabilized administration but could not prevent the empire's territorial contraction, as regional governors and successor states (including the emergence of the Bahmani Sultanate in the Deccan and the Vijayanagara Empire) broke away.

## Decline

Timur's 1398 invasion and the sack of Delhi shattered the dynasty's remaining authority, and the sultanate fragmented into competing regional powers, with the Tughluqs surviving only as a much-reduced Delhi-centered rump until their final displacement by the Sayyid dynasty in 1414.
