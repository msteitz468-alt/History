# The Khmer Empire (Angkor)

The **Khmer Empire** (802–c. 1431), centred on **[[angkor|Angkor]]**, was the dominant power of
mainland Southeast Asia for six centuries and the builder of the **largest pre-industrial urban
complex in the world**. Documented for the wiki through Michael D. Coe's
[[coe-angkor-khmer-civilization-2018|*Angkor and the Khmer Civilization*]] (2003).

## Founding: Jayavarman II and the devaraja

Out of the divided [[funan|Zhenla]] kingdoms, **Jayavarman II** unified the Khmer and, in **AD 802**
on the sacred hill of Mahendraparvata, had himself consecrated **chakravartin** ("universal monarch")
and instituted the cult of the **devaraja** (Khmer *kamraten jagat ta raja*, "the god who is king") —
the sacral kingship, linking the ruler to **Shiva** (and the royal *linga* on the temple-mountain),
that would define Angkorian sovereignty. He established his capital at **Hariharalaya** (the Roluos
group, near the Great Lake); his successors **Indravarman** and **Yasovarman** built the first great
**barays** (reservoirs) and stepped-pyramid temples (the Bakong) and moved the capital to **Angkor**
proper.

## A hydraulic civilization

The empire's foundation was **water**. The monsoon-fed **Tonle Sap (Great Lake)**, which quadruples in
the rains, and the vast **barays** and canals of Angkor (mapped in detail by recent **LiDAR** survey)
underpinned an intensive **wet-rice** economy capable of sustaining a huge population and the labour
for monumental building — the classic **agrarian-ritual state**, each great king obliged to endow his
own *baray* and temple-mountain.

## The great kings and the Cham wars

The dynastic arc ran through repeated usurpation and the chronic conflict with the **Cham** of
Champa. **Suryavarman II** (acc. 1113), a devotee of **Vishnu**, raised the empire to a height and
built **[[angkor|Angkor Wat]]**, sacking the Cham capital Vijaya (1144–45). After his death came
"disaster": in **1177** the Cham king Jaya Indravarman invaded by water up the Tonle Sap and **sacked
Angkor**, burning its timber structures. The empire was reborn under its greatest ruler,
**[[jayavarman-vii|Jayavarman VII]]** (r. from 1181), a **Mahayana Buddhist** who crushed the Chams,
rebuilt the capital as **Angkor Thom** with the **Bayon** at its heart, and launched an unprecedented
building and welfare programme — temporarily **displacing the Hindu devaraja** with a Buddhist
kingship.

## Significance

The Khmer Empire is the supreme achievement of the [[indianization-of-southeast-asia|Indianized]]
mainland — a civilization that took Indian kingship, religion, and architecture and built something
wholly its own, on a scale (Greater Angkor sprawled over ~1,000 km²) unmatched in the pre-industrial
world. It is the wiki's anchor for **mainland Southeast Asian** statehood, and the parent of modern
**Cambodia**.

## Decline

After Jayavarman VII the empire slowly contracted (see [[coe-angkor-khmer-civilization-2018|Cycle 3]]):
the turn to **Theravada Buddhism** (which dispensed with the temple-building royal cult), mounting
pressure from the rising **Thai** of Ayutthaya, and the strains on the hydraulic system led to the
**abandonment of Angkor** (the Thai sack of c. 1431 is the conventional marker) and the shift of the
Khmer centre toward Phnom Penh.
