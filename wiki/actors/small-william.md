# William Small

William Small (1734–1775) was a Scottish-born professor of natural philosophy at the College of William and Mary who profoundly influenced the young Thomas Jefferson. Small introduced Jefferson to the empirical sciences, mathematics, and the broader currents of the European Enlightenment. Jefferson credited Small with "fix[ing] the destinies of my life" by opening to him the world of ideas beyond classical and legal studies.

Small arrived in Williamsburg in 1758. He was the only non-Anglican member of the faculty at the time and brought a liberal, scientific spirit that contrasted with the clerical dominance of the college. He befriended Governor Fauquier and the lawyer George Wythe; the three formed an intellectual circle that included the student Jefferson. Small arranged for Jefferson to read law with Wythe and left Virginia in 1764 (returning to Britain for health and career reasons). He died young in 1775.

Jefferson maintained contact and later recalled Small as the man who first directed his mind toward "the expansion of science, and... the system of things in which we are placed."
