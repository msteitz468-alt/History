# Liu An (King of Huainan)

A prince of the early **[[han-dynasty|Han]]** imperial house (c. 179–122 BCE), patron and editor of the
**[[huainanzi|Huainanzi]]** — the great syncretic compendium of Western Han thought — and an emblem of
the losing side in the Han struggle between regional kingdoms and the centralizing throne.

## Life

Grandson of the Han founder Liu Bang, Liu An became **king of Huainan**, a sizeable semi-autonomous
kingdom within the Han empire. A cultivated patron of learning, he gathered scholars and *fangshi*
("masters of methods") at his court and, in **139 BCE**, presented to the young **Emperor Wu** the
encyclopedic work that bears his kingdom's name. As Emperor Wu's reign drove relentlessly toward
**centralization** at the expense of the kingdoms, Liu An fell under suspicion; accused of plotting
rebellion, he was driven to **suicide in 122 BCE** and his kingdom abolished — part of the wider
extinction of the regional kingdoms that secured Han autocracy.

## Significance

Liu An matters as the **sponsor of the *Huainanzi***, one of the supreme monuments of early Chinese
**intellectual synthesis**, and as a marker of the political moment when the **centralized empire**
finally subordinated the great regional princes. In later legend he became a Daoist immortal who
ascended to heaven with his household (even the dogs and chickens).
