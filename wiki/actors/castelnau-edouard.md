# Édouard de Castelnau

French general; worked on Plan XVI; served as deputy chief of the General Staff under Joseph Joffre after the 1911–12 High Command reorganization. Later commanded army groups.

## Role in Pre-War Reforms and Planning

Following the July 28, 1911 decree reorganizing the High Command under Joffre, Messimy appointed General Yvon Dubail chief of staff of the army (subordinate to Joffre on most matters). Joffre's preferred first deputy (Ferdinand Foch) was blocked by Messimy; Joffre instead chose Castelnau "who had worked on Plan XVI and was thoroughly familiar with the administrative procedures of the War Ministry."

Castelnau contributed to the refinement of war plans leading to Plan XVII. As deputy chief, he assured Joffre (during analysis of German capabilities) that if the Germans attempted a deep advance beyond the Liège-Namur line across central Belgium, they would "dangerously overextend their front and have an insufficient density for a vigorous action." This assessment (underestimating German ability to integrate reserves effectively for a deep envelopment) informed Joffre's acceptance of the likelihood of a Belgian violation while doubting the scale west of the Meuse-Sambre.

Castelnau later served Joffre as an army-group commander.

## Significance

Castelnau provided critical continuity in planning staff work during the transition to Joffre's unified command. His assessments on German reserve employment and front density shaped the assumptions embedded in Plan XVII.
