# Salmon P. Chase

**Summary page.** Full network and Lincoln context on the Lincoln pages.

Ohio governor, leading anti-slavery politician, Treasury Secretary under Lincoln, and later Chief Justice. One of the three major 1860 rivals incorporated into the cabinet. Ambitious, principled, often prickly; repeatedly considered for the presidency even while serving Lincoln; pushed hard for emancipation and black rights but frequently clashed with Lincoln over timing and patronage.

## Overview

Chase combined moral intensity on slavery with relentless personal ambition. As Treasury Secretary he financed the war (greenbacks, national banks) while maneuvering politically. His daughter Kate Chase Sprague was a central figure in Washington social and political life and an active manager of her father’s presidential hopes. Lincoln valued Chase’s abilities and antislavery commitment but managed his ego and repeated resignation threats with patience and occasional firmness. Chase resigned in 1864; Lincoln promptly nominated him Chief Justice after Taney’s death.

## Key Developments

- Pre-presidency: Dartmouth, law, Cincinnati, anti-slavery activism, Free Soil Party, governor of Ohio, U.S. Senator.
- 1860: Strong contender at Chicago; lost to Lincoln.
- 1861–1864: Treasury; war finance; emancipation advocate in cabinet; political tensions with Blair and others; repeated offers to resign.
- 1864–1873: Chief Justice; presided over Johnson impeachment; continued presidential aspirations (1868 Democratic, 1872 Liberal Republican); death 1873.

## Significance and Character

Chase supplied the moral and financial backbone of the hard-line Republican wing. Goodwin’s use of Chase’s diaries and Kate’s papers reveals both his idealism and his consuming ambition. Lincoln’s ability to keep Chase inside the administration for three critical years while advancing emancipation on his own timetable is a central example of the “team of rivals” management.
