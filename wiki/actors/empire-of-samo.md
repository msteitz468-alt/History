# Samo's Empire

**Samo's Empire** (c. 623–658 CE) was the first documented Slavic political union in Central Europe. It was a tribal federation founded by the Frankish merchant **Samo**, who led a successful Slavic rebellion against their nomadic overlords, the Avars. The federation successfully defended its territory against both the Avar Khaganate and the Merovingian Kingdom, but dissolved following Samo's death.

## Foundation and Rise

The primary source for Samo's Empire is the contemporary seventh-century *Chronicle of Fredegar*. According to Fredegar, Samo was a Frankish merchant from the district of Sens who travelled to the Slavic regions of Bohemia and Moravia to trade. 

At the time, the local Slavs (referred to by Fredegar as the *Wenedi*) were subject to the **[[avars|Avar Khaganate]]**. The Avars used the Slavs as agricultural tributaries and military shock troops. Following the Avar defeat at the [[siege-of-constantinople-626|Siege of Constantinople]] in 626, Avar prestige collapsed, and the Slavs rose in revolt.

Samo joined the Slavic rebels and demonstrated outstanding military leadership. In gratitude for his help, the Slavs elected him as their king (*rex*) c. 623 CE. He ruled for thirty-five years, marrying twelve Slavic wives and fathering twenty-two sons and fifteen daughters.

## The War with the Franks and Wogastisburg (631 CE)

Samo's Empire expanded to include parts of modern Bohemia, Moravia, Silesia, Lusatia, and Carantania. This expansion brought him into conflict with the Merovingian king **Dagobert I** (r. 629–639).

In 631, Frankish merchants were murdered in Samo's territory. When Dagobert sent an envoy to demand compensation, Samo refused, agreeing only to pay if the Franks recognized his state's sovereignty. Dagobert launched a three-pronged invasion, supported by the Alemanni and the Lombards.

The main Frankish army, led by Dagobert, clashed with Samo’s forces at the **Battle of Wogastisburg** (631 CE), an unidentified stronghold. The Slavs routed the Frankish army, plundered their camp, and launched counter-raids into Thuringia. Following this victory, Prince Dervan of the Sorbs (a Slavic tribe on the Saale River) renounced Merovingian suzerainty and joined Samo's state.

## Dissolution

Samo's Empire was a loose tribal federation that depended on the personal prestige and military leadership of Samo. It lacked permanent administrative institutions or a hereditary succession system. Following Samo’s death c. 658 CE, the federation collapsed, and the Slavic tribes returned to independent regional chiefdoms, while the Avars reasserted partial suzerainty over the southern Danube.
