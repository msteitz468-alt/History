# Chilperic I

**Chilperic I** (c. 539–584) was a son of **[[chlothar-i|Chlothar I]]** (by Aregund, sister
of his wife Ingund) who took the kingdom of **[[neustria|Neustria]]**, ruled from Soissons,
in the partition of 561. He is the great villain of
**[[gregory-of-tours-history-of-the-franks-thorpe-1974|Gregory's *Histories*]]**, who
labelled him "the **Nero and Herod** of our time" and, in his obituary, wrote that "he
cared for no one… and in return he was loved by none." Gregory's portrait must be read as
the verdict of a hostile churchman, but it is the fullest contemporary account of any
Merovingian king. He was the husband of **[[fredegund|Fredegund]]** and father of
**[[chlothar-ii|Chlothar II]]**.

## Career

On Chlothar's death Chilperic seized the royal treasury at Berny and tried to grab Paris,
but "his brothers leagued against him and drove him out." His marriage to the Visigothic
princess **Galswintha** — sister of [[brunhild|Brunhild]] — ended when, loving Fredegund,
"he had her garrotted by one of his servants… [and] within a few days he had asked
Fredegund to sleep with him again." The murder set off the feud with his brother
[[sigibert-i|Sigibert I]]; Chilperic was besieged in Tournai and saved only by Sigibert's
assassination in 575.

Dominant after Sigibert's death, Chilperic seized Brunhild, exiled her to Rouen, and
imprisoned and destroyed two of his own sons (Merovech, who had married Brunhild, and
Clovis). Gregory dwells on his vices and pretensions: crushing **new taxes** ("extremely
heavy," driving people into exile); building **amphitheatres** at Soissons and Paris;
adding **four letters to the Latin alphabet** and writing "feeble" verse modelled on
Sedulius; **forcing the baptism of Jews**, "help[ing] some of them out of the baptismal
pool himself"; and meddling in theology by decreeing "no distinction of Persons in the Holy
Trinity," which Gregory debated to his face. He persecuted bishops — engineering the
deposition of **Praetextatus of Rouen** with a forged canon — and ordered mutilation as
routine punishment: "he must be punished by having his eyes torn out."

He was **assassinated at his manor of Chelles in 584**, returning from the hunt, when "a
man stepped forward, struck him with a knife under the armpit and then stabbed him a second
time in the stomach." His four-month-old son was recognised as **Chlothar II**, whose cause
his magnates rallied around. Gregory and King [[guntram|Guntram]] each reported visions of
Chilperic's posthumous damnation — boiled away in a cauldron until "no trace at all of him
remained."

## Counterfactual Significance

Chilperic was the most aggressive and innovative of Chlothar's sons — his tax drives,
building, and assertion of royal authority over Church and coinage prefigure later state-
building — but Gregory's framing turned him into the moral antitype of the good king. His
assassination left an infant heir under Fredegund's regency, prolonging the civil wars and
making possible the [[gundovald-affair-585|Gundovald pretender's bid]] for his vacated
south.
