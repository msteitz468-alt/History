# Chester Nimitz

U.S. Navy admiral; Commander-in-Chief, U.S. Pacific Fleet (from late 1941) and Pacific Ocean Areas. Directed the carrier victory at Midway (1942) that turned the tide; oversaw Guadalcanal, central Pacific drive (Tarawa, Saipan, Iwo Jima, Okinawa), and Leyte Gulf. Master of carrier and amphibious operations in the vast Pacific theater. Signed the Japanese surrender aboard USS Missouri.

See: [[world-war-ii-1939-1945]], [[battle-of-midway-1942]].

**From Murray & Millett *A War to Be Won* (range 3 extraction):**
- "Nimitz urged Spruance to keep the campaign going... He made a bold decision to bypass most of the Marshalls and take only one of the atolls, Kwajalein... Nimitz showed that his grasp of the importance of isolating Japanese islands was as good, if not better, than MacArthur’s." (p. 346)
- "Since the Fifth Fleet had proven its effectiveness... Nimitz, with King’s enthusiastic support, advanced his timetable for taking all of the eastern Mandated Islands. Only one real objective, Eniwetok atoll..." (p. 347)
- "With the capture of the Marshalls all but certain by March 1944, Admiral Nimitz... decided that he did not want to capture Saipan, Guam, and Tinian... Spruance canceled the invasion of Guam... ordered the action broken off with Ozawa’s force still in range... This decision... does seem too cautious." (p. 353-354, 357)
- Post-Tarawa: "the performance of the 27th Infantry Division in taking Makin did not make the army’s case" and interservice tensions; improvements in amphibious techniques (more amtracs, longer/practiced naval gunfire, frogmen/UDT, armored amtracs). (p. 345-346)
- "Spruance could no longer justify his cautiousness by claiming naval weakness. For any forthcoming battle, he would have Task Force 58, commanded by the tough and experienced carrier commander Vice Admiral Marc Mitscher..." (p. 355-356)

**From Murray & Millett *A War to Be Won* (range 4 extraction - final campaigns/assessment):**
- Nimitz/Halsey/Spruance theater command (Iwo/Okinawa): "Pressed by MacArthur and Nimitz, Halsey’s carrier battle groups of TF 38 returned to the war on 10 December for more raids on Japanese air bases, but on 19 December the task forces ran into a typhoon whose course Halsey had misjudged. Task Force 38 lost 3 destroyers and 18 other warships damaged." Halsey’s airmen claimed >150 kills but lost 86 aircraft (mostly accidents); kamikazes penetrated. "Admiral Nimitz, as commander of the Pacific Ocean Areas Theater..." Nimitz (with subordinates) questioned Formosa; unanimously favored Iwo Jima/Okinawa for siege bases. "Recovering the Third Fleet from Halsey, Spruance—trusted by all except the carrier admirals—would command the joint task forces built from the Fifth Fleet in both operations." MacArthur approved Nimitz plan (to sink Formosa option). On Okinawa: "Only Spruance and Nimitz had the authority to order Buckner to change his plan, and once again the admirals shrank from conflict with the army." Spruance lost flagship; Mitscher shifted flags multiple times under kamikazes. Fifth Fleet heavy losses (64 ships sunk/heavily damaged; carriers Bunker Hill, Franklin, etc.) but maintained sea control. Nimitz signed surrender for US on Missouri (Halsey’s flagship); MacArthur presided. **From West Point WWII Vol 1 (range 4 extraction - Pacific Pearl to Midway context):**
- **Nimitz (Coral Sea positioning and pre-Midway decisions, ~3815–3821):** “Admiral Chester W. Nimitz, commander in chief of the Pacific Fleet since December 31, 1941, quickly positioned two carriers, Lexington and Yorktown, in the Coral Sea. He was looking for a showdown with the Japanese fleet.” (Context: “After Pearl Harbor, the U.S. Pacific Fleet had been reorganized into four task forces, each with a carrier as its center and screened by cruisers and destroyers.”) “In mid-March U.S. cryptanalysts broke the main Japanese naval cipher, JN-25. Decrypted communications (code-named Ultra intelligence) then made clear to the Allies that a major enemy amphibious thrust against Port Moresby was imminent, to be spearheaded by two fleet carriers.”
- **Outcome of Coral Sea (tactical draw but strategic effect reducing Midway force, ~4723–4737):** “American and Japanese fleets had both had enough, and each withdrew, ending the first naval engagement where opposing ships never sighted each other and aircraft did all the fighting. The battle was a tactical draw: each fleet had one carrier sunk and another damaged. But in the broader picture, the outcome favored the Americans: the Japanese had to postpone their amphibious assault on Port Moresby and, more important, had to weaken the size of their carrier task force at the subsequent battle of Midway by one-third.”
- Broader: “The twin Allied victories of Coral Sea and Midway in May and June 1942 reversed a long run of Japanese successes in the Pacific War.” “What Midway did accomplish, according to a Naval War College study, was to ‘put an end to Japanese offensive action’ and restore the naval balance in the Pacific. ...
