# William Clark (1770–1838)

Co-captain (with Meriwether Lewis) of the Corps of Discovery. Younger brother of Revolutionary War hero George Rogers Clark. Experienced frontier soldier and riverman.

**Role in the Expedition**: Clark handled much of the day-to-day command, river navigation, and mapping. His journals are often more consistent and practical than Lewis's. He was popular with the men and maintained discipline. Famously promised Lewis he would join "with great pleasure" in "this vast enterprise."

**York**: Clark brought his enslaved body servant York on the expedition. York proved an exceptional hunter, swimmer, and cultural figure among Native groups (who had never seen a Black man). Clark's later life included manumitting York (debated timing and terms).

**Later career**: Brigadier general of militia, Superintendent of Indian Affairs at St. Louis (long tenure shaping U.S. policy toward western tribes), and territorial governor roles. Lived until 1838.

**Historiography**: Often overshadowed by Lewis in early accounts; modern treatments (Fehrman 2026 and others) restore his centrality to the expedition's operational success and his complex later relationship with Native nations and with York.
