## Overview

Ine was king of the West Saxons from c. 689 to 726, the founder-consolidator of the historic kingdom of Wessex and one of the earliest English lawmakers. A son of Cenred with whom he probably co-ruled initially, he abdicated in 726 to go to Rome — following the precedent Cædwalla had set — leaving a disputed succession.

## Accession

Kirby argues Ine did not acquire royal power until 689, not 688: the West Saxon Genealogical Regnal List gives him a thirty-seven-year reign, and working back from his abdication in 726 places accession in 689. [[cædwalla-of-wessex|Cædwalla's]] abdication in 688 created unsettled conditions among the West Saxons, and Ine's father Cenred appears as a co-ruling king in Ine's early years. Ine's laws "date to the period before 693/4 (when Eorcenwald, bishop of London, whose help he also acknowledges, died)" and Ine acknowledges both Cenred's advice and Eorcenwald's assistance in the laws' preface.

## Lawgiving

Ine's laws are the first from any West Saxon king and cover a wider range than the Kentish codes of Wihtred — religious matters (baptism, Sunday observance, sanctuary, church taxes) alongside "a range of civil as well as criminal matters, including aspects of agrarian economy." Stenton described them as "the work of a responsible statesman"; Richardson and Sayles thought them "more mature, less experimental in some ways, than the surviving body of Kentish law." Kirby suggests the laws of Ine and Wihtred were produced in a period of reconstruction immediately following the payment of compensation for Mul's burning, reflecting the need "to enhance their standing with their subjects by prestigious acts of law-making."

## Military Expansion

- **South Saxons**: Subjected them to "severe oppression"; their bishopric was absorbed within Winchester diocese.
- **West**: In 710 fought alongside his kinsman Nothhelm (Nunna) of the South Saxons against Geraint, king of Dumnonia, bringing the West Saxon frontier to the Tamar.
- **East Saxons**: Applied pressure over sheltered exiles; a letter from Bishop Wealdhere of London (704–5) reveals estrangement and a forthcoming council at Brentford to resolve the crisis.
- **Surrey**: Retained Surrey; the 694 compensation payment from Kent (30,000 pence for the burning of Mul) seems to have involved border territory.

## Church Patronage and Ecclesiastical Politics

Ine patronised Malmesbury, Muchelney, and Glastonbury. He resisted Canterbury's demand for partition of the Winchester diocese until Bishop Hæddi's death in 705 forced the issue, resulting in the creation of Sherborne (under Aldhelm) and Winchester (under Daniel). Boniface entered public life c. 710–15 as Ine's envoy to the archbishop of Canterbury.

## Later Reign and Abdication

The reign did not end peacefully. In 721 Ine slew the ætheling Cynewulf; in 722 his queen Æthelburh demolished Taunton; by 722–25 the ætheling Ealdberht, an exile in Surrey and Sussex, led a rebellion that required a campaign against the South Saxons in 725 before Ealdberht was slain. Surrey appears to have been slipping from West Saxon control, possibly reflecting either renewed Kentish influence or the rising power of [[æthelbald-of-mercia|Æthelbald]]. In 726 Ine abdicated to Rome, leaving a disputed succession between his kinsman Æthelheard and the ætheling Oswald — conditions that Æthelbald exploited in the south.

## Counterfactual Significance

Ine "set the seal on the creation of the historic kingdom of Wessex." Without his long reign consolidating the gains of [[cædwalla-of-wessex|Cædwalla]], Wessex could plausibly have fragmented between rival æthellings and been absorbed into Mercian hegemony before the ninth century. His lawcode established a model of Christian royal governance that influenced Æthelberht's Kentish tradition onward.

## Historiography

The primary sources are the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, Bede (HE IV, 15; V, 18), and Ine's own laws. Kirby's main chronological point is the 689 accession date (not 688), derived from the thirty-seven-year reign figure in the Regnal List. He also stresses the close connection between Wihtred's and Ine's laws as products of a shared post-Cædwalla reconstruction phase.
