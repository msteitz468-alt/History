# Timoleon

## Overview

**Timoleon** (c. 411–c. 337 BCE), the Corinthian sent to Sicily in **344**, was the
**liberator and refounder of Greek Sicily** — the man who ended the tyrannies, broke the
Carthaginian advance at the **Crimisus**, and restored republican government and prosperity
to a land ruined by a generation of war. [[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 13,
Westlake) treats him through the laudatory traditions of Plutarch and Diodorus, warning
that the **"darling of the gods"** portrait must be read critically.

## The unlikely liberator

Timoleon came to the task by an extraordinary route. Years earlier, when his brother
**Timophanes** made himself tyrant of Corinth, Timoleon — after vainly remonstrating —
**stood by while associates killed him**, an act of patriotic **tyrannicide** that some
Corinthians praised and others condemned as impious; he withdrew into twenty years of
retirement. When the Syracusan exiles appealed to **Corinth** (their mother-city) for help
against tyranny, the lukewarm Corinthians sent only **700 mercenaries and ten ships** under
this elderly, untried figure — whose "**only palpable qualification was that he had been
instrumental in expelling a tyrant**."

## The reconquest of Sicily

Against the odds, Timoleon succeeded brilliantly. He landed despite Carthaginian and
tyrant opposition, **expelled [[dionysius-ii|Dionysius II]]** (who surrendered Ortygia and
was shipped off to Corinth, 344/3), and defeated the would-be tyrant Hicetas. In **341** he
won his great victory over a large Carthaginian army at the **river Crimisus** in western
Sicily — stabilizing the frontier roughly at the Halycus and securing the Greek east. He
then **overthrew the tyrants** across the island, **refounded Syracuse and other cities**
with waves of new settlers from Greece and Italy (reversing the depopulation), and
**restored republican/democratic constitutions** before retiring, by then blind, to honoured
private life. He died around 337, mourned as the second founder of Syracuse.

## Significance

Timoleon is the **antithesis of [[dionysius-i|Dionysius I]] and of [[dion-of-syracuse|
Dion]]**: where the elder Dionysius built a territorial monarchy and Dion's philosophic
project collapsed into autocracy, Timoleon **abolished** tyranny and rebuilt Sicily on
**civic, republican** foundations — a brief golden recovery before the rise of Agathocles.
His career closes the wiki's fourth-century Sicilian arc and stands as antiquity's model of
the **disinterested liberator** who took no power for himself.
