# Conn Cétchathach ("Conn of the Hundred Battles")

A legendary High King of Ireland in medieval Irish pseudohistorical tradition, traditionally dated to the 2nd century CE, and the eponymous ancestor after whom the province of Connacht ("the fifth of Conn") is named. He features in the *dindshenchas* (place-lore) tradition and in tales such as *Baile Chuinn Chétchathaig* ("The Ecstasy of Conn of the Hundred Battles"), a prophetic vision-text listing his royal descendants. No independent historical evidence supports his existence; he functions in the sources primarily as a genealogical charter figure legitimizing the later Connachta and, through claimed descent, the Uí Néill dynasties that dominated early medieval Ireland.

## Counterfactual Significance

As a genealogical anchor point, Conn's legendary status mattered less for any historical deeds than for the political work his supposed lineage did for medieval Irish dynasties claiming descent from him — a pattern common to many early medieval "founder" figures across Ireland and Britain.

## Historiography

Modern scholarship (T. F. O'Rahilly and successors) treats Conn Cétchathach as a legendary/mythological figure whose "history" was constructed retrospectively by medieval genealogists rather than transmitted from any 2nd-century reality; comparable in function to figures like [[cunedda]] in early Welsh tradition.
