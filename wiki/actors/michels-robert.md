# Robert Michels (1876–1936)

German-Italian sociologist, an "Italianized German" who joined the SPD and
the PSI simultaneously (1902), wrote for *Avanguardia socialista* and *Il
divenire sociale*, and produced in *Political Parties* (1911) — the "iron law
of oligarchy" — a classic of political science that was also, in Sternhell's
reading, part of the [[revolutionary-syndicalism|revolutionary syndicalist]]
critique of socialist parties. He proclaimed the "collapse of German
socialism" after Mannheim (1906), argued from the Krupp workers that "class
egoism alone is not sufficient to attain a revolutionary objective," debated
[[berth-edouard|Berth]] in 1906 on whether socialism could exist without a
proletariat, and called for a "grandiose union" of the revolutionary idea
with a great revolutionary force — hoping first for the proletariat, falling
back on the nation (he held a theory of proletarian imperialism and wrote in
*La lupa*).

He became "one of the foremost theoreticians of fascism until his death in
1936": *Sozialismus und Fascismus in Italien* (1925) used Sorel's and
Pareto's categories to argue a continuity from Pisacane and Garibaldi to
fascism; he taught at Perugia from 1929 and joined the PNF in 1928. With
Pareto and Mosca he supplied the "new social sciences" legitimation of
elitist antidemocracy — the sociological wing of the intellectual quartet
(Sorel, Michels, Pareto, Le Bon) whose ideas the war was said to have
vindicated. A key exhibit in [[fascism-and-the-political-left]].
