# James F. Byrnes

American politician who held an unusually broad range of top federal offices — U.S. Representative and Senator from South Carolina, Supreme Court Associate Justice (1941–42), and wartime Director of the Office of War Mobilization under Franklin Roosevelt, a role that earned him the nickname "Assistant President" for his sweeping control of the domestic war economy. He accompanied Roosevelt to Yalta and, after Roosevelt's death, served as [[truman-harry-s|Harry Truman]]'s first Secretary of State (1945–1947), representing the United States at the Potsdam Conference and in the early postwar foreign-ministers' negotiations that hardened into Cold War confrontation with the Soviet Union; his 1946 "Speech of Hope" in Stuttgart signaled the shift toward rebuilding rather than punishing occupied Germany. He later served as Governor of South Carolina (1951–1955), where he became a prominent segregationist opponent of federal desegregation efforts.

## Counterfactual Significance

As the man widely expected to succeed Roosevelt as vice president in 1944 before Truman's selection, Byrnes represents one of the war's clearer contingent turning points; as Secretary of State he shaped the tone of the earliest postwar diplomacy with Moscow before Truman moved toward a harder containment line.

## Historiography

Standard postwar-diplomacy and origins-of-the-Cold-War literature treats Byrnes's early conciliatory approach as a contrast to the firmer line associated with his successor, Dean Acheson.
