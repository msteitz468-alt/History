# Fastrada, Queen of the Franks (c. 765–794)

**Fastrada**, of East Frankish noble family, became [[charlemagne|Charles the Great]]'s
queen after the death of [[hildegard]] (married 783). She died at Frankfurt on
**10 August 794** and was buried at St Alban's, Mainz.

## Rehabilitation Against the "Wicked Stepmother" Libel

[[sources/nelson-king-and-emperor-2019|Janet Nelson]] mounts a pointed rehabilitation of
Fastrada, whom [[einhard|Einhard]] and the *Annales regni Francorum* Reviser blamed — long
after her death — for the cruelty that provoked the revolts of the late 780s and 792:

> "Since both the ArF reviser and Einhard were writing considerably after Fastrada's death,
> and no other source alleges her cruelty, it looks as if she was retrospectively made a
> scapegoat for the actions of others — especially those of Charles himself."

Nelson adds the general principle: "Casting the blame on a woman (often in order to whitewash
a man) is a common enough political tactic."

The contemporary evidence shows a genuine political partner:

- Charles entrusted her with **sole responsibility for the victory litanies** during the
  791 Avar campaign, coordinating with the bishops and the community of St Emmeram's. Her
  surviving 791 letter exchange reveals "a personal relationship, and a strong emotional
  bond," with anxious inquiries about her health.
- At the **Council of Frankfurt (794)** she held court independently before Charles arrived,
  presiding over a legal judgment: "the participants came to Frankfurt not only because
  Charles was there but because Fastrada was too."

Theodulf's brief epitaph, Nelson argues (following Staab's repunctuation), is respectful
rather than dismissive — its brevity practical, destined for a stone inscription.
