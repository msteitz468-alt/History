# Datis

Persian commander (with Artaphernes) of the expeditionary force sent by Darius against Eretria and Athens in 490 BCE. Landed at Marathon.

## Role in the Campaign

Datis shared command of the fleet-borne army that first destroyed Eretria on Euboea and then crossed to Attica. The landing site at Marathon was selected under the guidance of the exiled Athenian Hippias. The force included mounted and dismounted archers drawn primarily from the Persian/Median/Parthian professional core.

At Marathon the Persians were met by an Athenian force positioned in the Vrana valley. Unable to bring their cavalry advantage to bear effectively because of the constricted and protected terrain, the Persian force was defeated in the resulting battle. Datis oversaw the attempted re-embarkation under pressure.

## Army Character

The force under Datis exemplified the Persian army type described by Delbrück: professional warriors rather than mass levy. The bow was the principal weapon; even the mounted men were archers. Light armor predominated. The army did not fight as a closed tactical body like the Greek hoplite phalanx.
