# Kingdom of Pontus

The Kingdom of Pontus was a Hellenistic-era state on the southern shore of the Black Sea in northeastern Anatolia, founded c. 281 BCE by a Persian-descended local dynasty (claiming Achaemenid ancestry) that survived the [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323|Wars of the Diadochi]] on the fringes of the major Successor kingdoms. Its capital was Amasea (later Sinope), and it blended Persian royal traditions, Anatolian populations, and a thin veneer of Greek civic culture typical of minor Hellenistic monarchies alongside neighbors such as Bithynia and [[pergamon|Pergamum]].

Pontus rose to major-power status under [[mithridates-vi]] ("the Great," r. 120–63 BCE), who expanded the kingdom around the Black Sea (Crimea, Colchis, parts of Armenia) and fought three prolonged wars against Rome (the Mithridatic Wars, 89–63 BCE) — including the massacre of tens of thousands of Roman and Italian settlers in Anatolia in 88 BCE — before being decisively defeated by Pompey and dying in 63 BCE.

After Mithridates VI's fall, Pontus was absorbed piecemeal into the Roman provincial system (organized by Pompey as part of Bithynia et Pontus), though a remnant client kingdom persisted intermittently until final annexation under Nero in 62 CE.
