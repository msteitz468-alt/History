## Overview

Wulfhere was the first king of an independent Mercia after the battle of the Winwaed. Penda's young son — probably in his mid-teens at accession — he was installed by a Mercian revolution that expelled the agents of [[kingdom-of-northumbria|Oswiu]] probably in 659 (not 658 as earlier scholarship assumed). Kirby argues his seventeen-year reign should be read as dying *in* his seventeenth year, giving a reign of 659–675.

## Rise to Power

The Mercians rebelled against Northumbrian overlordship and "set up Penda's young son, Wulfhere, as their king" (Kirby, ch. 6). The precise year depends on the date of the Winwaed: if fought in 656, the revolution occurred in 659. Wulfhere's queen was Eormenhild, daughter of Eorcenberht of Kent, a marriage designed to draw Mercia into the Anglo-Frankish cultural orbit during Christianisation.

## Reign and Hegemony

Wulfhere's reign is "primarily distinguished in the surviving record by his re-establishment of the Mercians as a significant political and military force in southern England." His key moves:

- **Lindsey**: Exercised episcopal authority there through bishops Chad and Wynfrith, working to detach the territory from Northumbrian dependency.
- **East Saxons**: Organised the reconversion of Sigehere's people after the plague of 664; with London's bishopric, he sold the see to Wine, the expelled bishop of Winchester.
- **Surrey**: Between 673 and 675 Wulfhere confirmed sub-king Frithuwald's grant at Thame, placing Surrey firmly in a Mercian orbit following the death of Kent's Ecgberht.
- **Isle of Wight and Meonware**: Gave both to Aethelwealh of the South Saxons on his baptism in Mercia, building a Mercian–South Saxon axis that "created a new hierarchy of power in southern England."

## Defeat and Death

Defeat by [[kingdom-of-northumbria|Ecgfrith]] c. 674 cost Wulfhere Lindsey temporarily. In 675 Aescwine of Wessex fought him at Biedanheafde. Wulfhere died that same year "in his mid-thirties," his position already weakened; his brother and successor Æthelred could not maintain the southern hegemony.

## Counterfactual Significance

Kirby notes that in Wulfhere's reign a southern England dominated jointly by Mercians and South Saxons was "perhaps the most immediate prospect." His premature death foreclosed that path and allowed the West Saxons to survive without further dismemberment. Had he lived a decade longer, the kingdom of Wessex "could have been stillborn or at least confined to political insignificance beyond Selwood."

## Historiography

Kirby's principal chronological correction: Wulfhere's reign is 659–675, not 658–675. The seventeen-year figure in Bede (HE III, 24) is reconciled with Æthelred's reign start by the council of Hatfield evidence: Æthelred's sixth year on 17 September 680 places his accession to a point after 17 September 674 and before the anniversary of that date in 675, making Wulfhere's death in 675 and his reign seventeen years counted inclusively. Charter evidence (CS 34: S 1165) supplies the Surrey material; the Isle of Wight gift is placed later in the reign than the Chronicle's s.a. 661 on the strength of Bede's phrase "not long before" the Wilfridian mission to the South Saxons in the mid-680s (HE IV, 13).
