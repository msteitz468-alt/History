# Aymara Kingdoms (Altiplano Señoríos)

## Overview

After the collapse of [[tiwanaku|Tiwanaku]] (c. 1000 CE), the high plains around
**Lake Titicaca** fragmented into a set of competing **Aymara-speaking kingdoms** or
*señoríos* — most prominently the **Lupaqa, Colla, Pacajes, and Carangas**. These were
wealthy pastoral-agrarian polities controlling vast herds of llamas and alpacas and exploiting
resources across altitude zones through the **[[vertical-archipelago|vertical archipelago]]**
strategy famously documented for the Lupaqa by John Murra (from the 1567 Garci Diez de San
Miguel *visita*).

In [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|Volume III]] these kingdoms appear in
Lumbreras's account of the post-Wari reorganization and again in the chapters on the Inka and
the conquest. The [[inca-empire|Inka]] incorporated them as the core of **Collasuyu**, the
southeastern quarter of Tawantinsuyu, after hard-fought campaigns against the Colla and Lupaqa.

## Significance

The Aymara kingdoms are the best-documented Andean **señoríos** of the Late Intermediate
Period and the empirical basis for Murra's model of Andean **verticality** and the
**[[ayllu]]**-based dual organization. Their herds underwrote a distinctive altiplano wealth
that the Inka prized and taxed.
