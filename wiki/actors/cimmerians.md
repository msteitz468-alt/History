# The Cimmerians

The **Cimmerians** (Assyrian *Gimirrai*) were "one of the earliest peoples of Eastern
Europe whose name has come down to us" — the mounted steppe raiders whose flight before
the [[scythians|Scythians]] carried them into Anatolia, where for two generations they
**shattered Phrygia, captured [[lydia|Lydia's]] capital Sardis, and threatened Assyria**.
[[boardman-cah-v3p2-1991|CAH Vol. III, Part 2]] (Ch. 33a, Sulimirski & Taylor) treats
them. **Note:** as with the Scythians, the chapter's **culture-historical** equations
(Cimmerians = the "Catacomb" culture) are Sulimirski's and now qualified.

## Origins and the flight before the Scythians

The earliest reference is in Homer (*Odyssey* XI). Sulimirski regards the Cimmerians as
**Indo-European** (a Thracian branch, or a Thracian people with an Iranian ruling class),
divided into a "royal race" and a "Cimmerian people" — a split he links to an early
Iranian (Median) elite imposed on the Pontic population. Under pressure from the advancing
**[[scythians|Scythians]]**, the Cimmerians (per Herodotus IV.11–12) **fled into Asia
Minor**, some settling around Sinope, others mingling with the Caucasian Koban culture;
Eusebius's date (three centuries before the first Olympiad) puts an early invasion of
Western Asia in the eleventh century.

## The conquests in Anatolia

The Cimmerians are best documented in the **Assyrian and Urartian records** of their
seventh-century career:

- First clashes with **Urartu** (Argishti II / Rusa I) in the late eighth century; the
  Assyrian crown prince Sennacherib reported a Urartian defeat in **Gamir** (Cimmerian
  territory).
- **705**: they defeated and **killed [[sargon-ii|Sargon II]]** on the Assyrian frontier.
- **679**: their king **Teushpa** was defeated by [[esarhaddon|Esarhaddon's]] governor.
- **c. 696/680**: allied with Rusa II, they **invaded and shattered the Phrygian kingdom
  of Midas**.
- Defeated c. 663 by **Gyges of [[lydia|Lydia]]**, but they returned and **captured
  Sardis (c. 640)** — the summit of their power.
- **c. 626/625**: routed by **Alyattes of Lydia**, who killed their king **Dugdamme
  (Lygdamis)**; the remnant under his son **Sandakshatru** was defeated by
  [[ashurbanipal|Ashurbanipal]] (with help from the Scythians under Madyes), after which
  "they disappeared from history."

## Causal Analysis

caused_by: the westward pressure of the [[scythians|Scythians]] on the Pontic steppe.
produced: the **destruction of Phrygia** and a generation of upheaval in Anatolia and
the Assyrian north; their capture of Sardis was a blow [[lydia|Lydia]] survived to
become the regional power.
contributed_to: their own end — caught between Lydia, Assyria, and the pursuing Scythians.

## Historiography

Known from **Homer**, **Herodotus**, and crucially the **Assyrian and Urartian
inscriptions** (which name kings — Teushpa, Dugdamme — and fix the seventh-century
campaigns), plus the disputed archaeological identification with the Pontic "Catacomb"
and Caucasian Koban cultures. The Armenian name for Cappadocia, *Gamirkʿ*, preserves
their memory. Sulimirski's culture-equations and the "Thraco-Cimmerian" assemblages of
Central Europe are, as Taylor notes, contested. They are interwoven throughout with the
history of the [[scythians|Scythians]] who displaced them.
