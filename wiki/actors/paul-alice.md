# Alice Paul (1885–1977)

American suffrage strategist; founder of the Congressional Union and leader of the National Woman's Party (NWP) tradition of militant, federal-amendment pressure politics. Architect of the 1913 [[woman-suffrage-procession-1913|Woman Suffrage Procession]] on the eve of Wilson's inauguration and of the [[silent-sentinels-1917|Silent Sentinels]] White House pickets. With [[burns-lucy|Lucy Burns]], she forced the Anthony Amendment onto the national agenda against [[wilson-woodrow|Woodrow Wilson]]'s prolonged resistance.

## Role and significance

Paul imported British militant methods (she had worked with the Pankhursts) into a U.S. campaign that targeted the party in power — especially a Democratic president whose New Freedom rhetoric of self-government excluded women. Cox's *Light Withdrawn* treats her as central antagonist-hero to Wilson on the suffrage axis: procession, pickets, Occoquan imprisonment, hunger strikes, and relentless publicity that made Wilson's "democracy" rhetoric internationally awkward during [[first-world-war-1914|World War I]].

## Historiography

Long cast as the "militant" foil to Catt's "moderate"; recent work (and Cox 2024) stresses that Paul's federal strategy was strategically rational once the Anthony Amendment was the only route past southern Democratic and Wilsonian obstruction. Primary base: NWP papers; contemporary press; Wilson administration records of arrests.
