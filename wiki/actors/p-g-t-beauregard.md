# P. G. T. Beauregard

Confederate general who ordered the first shots of the Civil War at Fort
Sumter (April 1861) and commanded Confederate forces at the First Battle of
Bull Run (July 1861), the war's first major engagement.

## Role and Major Decisions

Beauregard directed the bombardment that forced the surrender of the Union
garrison at Fort Sumter, precipitating the war's outbreak, then shared
command (with Joseph E. Johnston) of the Confederate victory at First Bull
Run. He later commanded at Shiloh and directed the defense of Charleston
and Petersburg's approaches, including a crucial early stand that helped
delay Grant's initial assault on Petersburg in June 1864 until Lee's main
army arrived. His relations with Confederate President Jefferson Davis were
persistently strained, limiting his influence over broader Confederate
strategy despite his early prominence.

## Counterfactual Significance

Beauregard's early defensive stand at Petersburg in June 1864, before Lee's
army reinforced the city, is credited by some historians with preventing an
early Union capture of the city that might have shortened the war by
months.

## Historiography

Beauregard promoted his own reputation vigorously in postwar writing,
often exaggerating his strategic contributions relative to the assessments
of contemporaries and later historians; his personal rivalry with Davis is
a persistent theme in accounts of Confederate command dysfunction.
