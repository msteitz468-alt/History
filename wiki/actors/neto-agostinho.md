# Agostinho Neto

Marxist physician-poet, founding leader of the **MPLA** (People's Liberation Movement of Angola) and first president of independent Angola (1975–1979).

## Overview and Role

Per [[mccoy-cold-war-five-continents-2024|McCoy (2024)]], Neto earned a medical degree in Portugal in 1958 — one of very few non-white doctors in the colony — and was flogged and imprisoned in Luanda and Lisbon after his return before escaping to the Congo and rebuilding the MPLA (founded 1956 among the Mbundu of the Luanda region) with roughly 250 guerrillas. CIA briefings dismissed him as "a drunken psychotic poet," a characterization McCoy notes was false. With Soviet arms and a decisive Cuban military intervention, Neto's MPLA defeated the FNLA and Jonas Savimbi's UNITA in the opening battles of the [[angolan-civil-war-1975|Angolan civil war]] (Quifangondo and Ebo, November 1975), securing Luanda and the Cabinda oil enclave at independence on 11 November 1975.

## Significance

Neto established the MPLA state that would hold power in Angola for decades, aligned with Moscow and Havana. He died in 1979 (in the Soviet Union) and was succeeded by José Eduardo dos Santos. His government's survival depended on the Cuban expeditionary force that also checked apartheid South Africa's regional projection — a link in the chain McCoy traces from Angola to the fall of white-minority rule in southern Africa.
