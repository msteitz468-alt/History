# Zoroaster (Zarathustra)

The prophet and reformer credited with founding [[zoroastrianism|
Zoroastrianism]], one of the oldest continuously practiced religions and a
major influence on later Abrahamic theology (concepts of a single supreme
creator god, cosmic dualism between good and evil, final judgment, and
resurrection).

## Role and Significance

Zoroaster is known almost entirely through the *Gathas*, a set of hymns in
an archaic form of Avestan attributed to him and preserved within the
Zoroastrian *Avesta*. He proclaimed Ahura Mazda ("Wise Lord") as the supreme
uncreated god locked in cosmic struggle against the destructive spirit Angra
Mainyu, reframing existing Iranian polytheistic religion around ethical
dualism, individual moral choice, and eschatological judgment. Zoroastrianism
became the dominant religion of the Achaemenid, Parthian, and Sasanian
Persian empires.

## Counterfactual Significance

Zoroastrian theological concepts — a single creator deity, cosmic dualism,
individual judgment, heaven and hell, and a coming savior figure — are
widely argued by historians of religion to have influenced post-Exilic
Judaism (and through it Christianity and Islam) during the period of
Achaemenid Persian rule over Judea, making Zoroaster's reforms a
plausible distant influence on the theological architecture of all three
Abrahamic religions.

## Historiography

Zoroaster's dates, birthplace, and even historicity are genuinely disputed:
traditional Zoroastrian chronology places him many centuries before the
Achaemenid period, while most modern linguistic and archaeological
scholarship favors a date somewhere in the second millennium BCE based on
the archaic language of the Gathas, though a range of centuries remains
defensible depending on methodology. The `dating_method` for this figure is
overwhelmingly linguistic (archaic Avestan vocabulary and grammar) rather
than documentary or archaeological.
