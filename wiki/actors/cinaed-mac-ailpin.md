# Cináed mac Ailpín (Kenneth MacAlpin)

King of Dál Riata who died in 858 and who later medieval Scottish tradition credited with founding the unified Kingdom of Alba by conquering (or, in some later legends, treacherously massacring) the Pictish nobility and merging Scots and Picts under a single crown — the origin of his epithet as founder of the Scottish royal line, from which all subsequent Scottish monarchs claimed descent. Contemporary evidence for his reign is extremely thin, and the dramatic "conquest of the Picts" narrative is a later (10th–12th century) retrospective construction serving the political needs of his successors.

## Counterfactual Significance

Whether or not Cináed personally "created" a unified kingdom, his dynasty's later success in retrospectively claiming him as founder became the ideological foundation of Scottish royal legitimacy for centuries, making the mac Alpín succession myth arguably more historically consequential than Cináed's actual reign.

## Historiography

Alex Woolf's revisionist account (*From Pictland to Alba*, 2007) argues the "unification" was a gradual process of dynastic and cultural convergence over generations rather than a single conquest by Cináed, and that much of the traditional narrative reflects later 10th-century political myth-making rather than 9th-century events.
