# John A. Rawlins

John Aaron Rawlins (1831–1869) was an Illinois lawyer who became [[grant-ulysses-s|Ulysses S. Grant]]'s closest wartime confidant, serving as his adjutant and eventually chief of staff from the war's early months through Appomattox and beyond. A civilian volunteer with no prior military training, Rawlins earned Grant's trust through blunt personal loyalty rather than battlefield command.

His most consequential role was as Grant's self-appointed guardian against alcohol: Rawlins policed Grant's drinking with sometimes confrontational directness, a service later credited by biographers with helping preserve Grant's career and reputation at critical moments (notably around Vicksburg). Beyond this, he functioned as Grant's chief administrator and sounding board, managing correspondence, staff discipline, and the practical machinery of headquarters through the Overland Campaign, the Siege of Petersburg, and the Appomattox Campaign of 1865.

Rawlins followed Grant into the postwar army and was appointed Secretary of War in Grant's presidential cabinet in March 1869, but died of tuberculosis that September after only a few months in office, having contracted the illness partly from wartime exposure.
