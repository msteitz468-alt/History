# Hákon Hákonarson (Hákon IV)

King of [[norway|Norway]] (1217–1263), under whom the kingdom reached its medieval
zenith — the *Norgesveldet*, embracing the North Atlantic tributaries. Grandson of
[[sverre-sigurdsson|Sverre]], he ended the Civil Wars definitively by defeating Earl
Skule (1240) and obtained a **papal coronation in 1247**, securing the Church's
recognition of his legitimacy.

## Achievements

Per [[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]]:
- his **law of succession (1260)** prescribed a virtually automatic hereditary right
  beginning with the eldest legitimate son — a distinctively Norwegian step toward a
  genuinely hereditary monarchy;
- he peacefully incorporated the [[icelandic-free-state|Icelandic Free State]]
  (submission completed 1262–64) and Greenland into the Norwegian crown;
- he concluded a peace and trade treaty with **Lübeck (1250)**, securing vital Baltic
  grain imports — and inadvertently opening [[bergen|Bergen]] to the German merchants
  who would form the later [[hanseatic-league|Hanseatic]] Kontor;
- his Bergen court commissioned Norse translations of French chivalric romance
  (Tristan, Chrétien de Troyes), a key conduit of [[saga-literature|courtly
  culture]] into Scandinavia.

His dynastic policy (betrothing his line into Sweden) also fed the
[[unification-of-the-scandinavian-kingdoms|inter-Scandinavian entanglement]] leading
to the 1319 union. His son **Magnus Lagabøte** ("the Lawmender") issued Norway's
national **Landlaw (1274)** and **Townlaw (1276)**. See
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]].
