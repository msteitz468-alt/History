# The Kingdom of Sicily (Norman, 1130–1194)

The **Kingdom of Sicily** was the wealthy, centralised, multicultural state created by
the Normans in southern Italy and Sicily. [[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] (G. A. Loud,
Ch. 15b, "Norman Sicily in the twelfth century") treats its creation as the abrupt
reversal of the fragmentation that had followed the
[[norman-conquest-of-southern-italy|eleventh-century Norman conquest]]: by 1100 the
Norman principalities were dissolving, but **Roger II** reunited the entire region in
1127–30 and was **crowned king at Palermo in 1130**, uniting southern Italy and Sicily
"for the first time since the age of Justinian." The kingdom he founded endured (with
many changes of dynasty) until 1860.

## A Strong, Multicultural State

Loud's central theme is the **imposition of strong central government** and the
challenges it faced. The *regno* was, by the standards of twelfth-century Europe,
exceptionally:

- **Centralised and wealthy** — a salaried bureaucracy, a literate administration
  (drawing on Greek, Arabic, and Latin clerks), royal control of justice and revenue,
  and the rich resources of Sicily and Apulia.
- **Multicultural** — an "intersection for Arab, Greek, Latin and Frankish cultures"
  (NCMH IV, editors' Introduction): the Norman kings employed Muslim and Greek officials
  and soldiers, patronised Arabic and Greek learning (the geographer al-Idrisi served
  Roger II), and built monuments — the Cappella Palatina, Monreale, Cefalù — whose
  Byzantine-style mosaics "proclaim the ambitions of the Norman rulers to emulate the
  empire of Byzantium."
- **Contested** — Roger II's coronation ushered in nearly a decade of baronial civil war,
  and royal authority over the turbulent mainland barons remained a recurring struggle
  under his successors William I ("the Bad") and William II ("the Good").

## A Mediterranean Power

The Sicilian kings were aggressive Mediterranean players: they attacked
[[byzantine-empire|Byzantium]] and the [[komnenian-restoration|Komnenian]] Balkans,
seized footholds in North Africa, allied with and against the [[papacy|papacy]] (their
nominal overlord since the [[norman-conquest-of-southern-italy|Norman conquest]]), and
played a part in the politics of the [[crusades|Crusades]].

## End of the Norman Kingdom

The marriage of **Constance** (Roger II's daughter and eventual heiress) to
**[[frederick-i-barbarossa|Barbarossa's]]** son **Henry VI** brought the kingdom to the
**Hohenstaufen** in 1194 — uniting the *regno* with the empire and creating the
papal-encircling power whose conflict with the papacy dominates the thirteenth century
([[abulafia-ncmh-v5-2000|NCMH V]]). The Norman kingdom thus ended dynastically even as
the kingdom of Sicily itself continued.
