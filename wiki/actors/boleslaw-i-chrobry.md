# Bolesław I Chrobry and the Piast State of Poland

**Bolesław I "Chrobry" (the Brave)** (967–1025; ruler from 992, king in 1025) was the architect of the first powerful Polish state and, with the [[premyslid-bohemia|Přemyslids]], one of [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]]'s (Strzelczyk) "two examples of successful western Slavonic state-formation."

## The Piast Foundation

The **Piast** dynasty arose among the **Polanie** of Great Poland (around Gniezno and Poznań), supplanting the older Goplanie/Popiel tradition. Bolesław's father **Mieszko I** (d. 992) founded the durable state: he **accepted baptism in 966** (sealed by marriage to the Bohemian princess **Dobrava**), founded the bishopric of **Poznań** (968), concluded a tributary *amicitia* with [[otto-i|Otto I]] (Margrave Gero, 963), and — in the document *Dagome iudex* — placed his realm under the protection of **St Peter** (the papacy). The Piast state was built on a network of strongholds (*grody*) and a ducal retinue (*drużyna*).

## Bolesław's Reign

Bolesław hugely expanded Piast power: he seized **Cracow and Little Poland**, intervened in **Bohemia** (occupying Prague 1003, see [[premyslid-bohemia]]), campaigned toward Kiev (intervening in the Rus′ succession, 1018), and pressed against the Elbe-Slav and Saxon marches. His relationship with the Reich swung from partnership to war:

- At the **[[synod-of-gniezno-1000|Act/Synod of Gniezno (1000)]]**, [[otto-iii|Otto III]] — on pilgrimage to the tomb of the martyred **St Adalbert of Prague** (whom Bolesław had ransomed and enshrined) — founded a **Polish archbishopric at Gniezno** (with suffragans at Cracow, Wrocław, and Kołobrzeg), concluded a friendship-pact, placed his own diadem on Bolesław's head, and (in Thietmar's hostile phrase) made him "a lord instead of a tributary." Otto's *Renovatio* envisaged Poland as a member-kingdom of a renewed Christian empire.
- Under [[henry-ii-holy-roman-emperor|Henry II]] this collapsed into **three wars (1004–1018)**: Henry, allied even with the pagan **Liutizi**, failed to dislodge Bolesław from the Lausitz and Milzener lands, conceded as fiefs at the **Peace of Bautzen (1018)**.

In **1025**, shortly before his death, Bolesław was finally **crowned king** — the first Polish royal coronation, the culmination of the Gniezno programme of Christian kingship.

## Significance

Bolesław Chrobry converted the Piast duchy into a Christian kingdom with its own ecclesiastical province, fixing Poland's place in **Latin Christendom** and its long rivalry with Bohemia (Prague vs. Gniezno) for primacy among the western Slavs. The Gniezno archbishopric gave Poland ecclesiastical independence from the German church — a foundation of its lasting identity. The crisis after his son **Mieszko II** (the Bohemian sack of Gniezno in 1038, removing St Adalbert's relics) showed the fragility of the new state, but the Piast kingdom endured.
