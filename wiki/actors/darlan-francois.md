# François Darlan (1881–1942)

Admiral of the Fleet; head of the Vichy government February 1941–April 1942 —
the period in which, per Paxton, France moved "closest to outright military
cooperation with Germany." From a republican family, in practice "permanent
Minister of the Navy" 1926–39, he built the strongest French navy in history
by 1939 and committed it to the armistice while secretly ordering it scuttled
rather than surrendered. His animus was anti-British (Mers-el-Kébir above
all) more than ideological.

## The Grand Design, 1941

Sent to Beauvais on 24 December 1940 to assure Hitler that Laval's dismissal
changed nothing, Darlan then accumulated more offices than anyone else at
Vichy (vice-premier, foreign affairs, interior, information, later defense).
His "Grand Design": French naval-imperial power inside a German-dominated
continental system, France as Europe's "Atlantic bridgehead" — the
alternative being, if Britain won, "a second-class Dominion, a continental
Ireland" (radio, 2 May 1941). The sequence: **Berchtesgaden** (11 May 1941),
where Hitler offered only "donnant-donnant"; the **Paris Protocols** (28 May
1941) trading Syrian airfields, Bizerte as a Rommel supply route, and an
eventual Dakar U-boat base for POW releases and minor relief; **Syria**
lost to the British and Free French by 14 July 1941 — "France had reached the
high point of military aid to Germany, only to lose Syria for her pains"; and
the **note verbale of 14 July 1941**, the boldest French proposal of the whole
period — replace the armistice with a treaty of sovereignty and cooperation —
which Ribbentrop dismissed as "a naive French blackmail attempt." Paxton
revises the Liberation-era account that Weygand blocked the Protocols: German
disinterest after Barbarossa, not internal opposition, killed the Grand
Design. Darlan purged the doubters anyway (Doyen, then Weygand in November
1941). Under his government the technocrats had their "high noon" — and the
regime created its anti-Jewish Commissariat (Vallat, March 1941) partly to
wrest "Aryanization" from German hands (see [[vichy-anti-jewish-laws-1940]]).

## Fall, defection, assassination, 1942

Pétain replaced him with Laval on 26 April 1942 — "Darlan's fall grew out of
a Vichy decision, not a German demand" — though he kept command of the armed
forces. Caught in Algiers by [[operation-torch-1942]] (visiting his
polio-stricken son), he commanded the Vichy defense, accepted a cease-fire on
11 November 1942 only after the Germans crossed into the unoccupied zone, and
formed an "Imperial Council" — an "inverse Vichy" nominally with the Allies
while keeping Vichy legislation (including the Jewish disabilities) in force
in North Africa. He was assassinated in Algiers on **24 December 1942**.
