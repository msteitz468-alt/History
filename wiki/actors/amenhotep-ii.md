# Amenhotep II

**Amenhotep II** (r. ~1427–1400 BCE), son of [[thutmose-iii|Thutmose III]] (with whom he
shared a brief co-regency), consolidated his father's [[processes/egyptian-imperialism|Syrian
empire]] and presided over the **Mitanni peace** that opened the great-power diplomacy of the
later [[new-kingdom-egypt|18th Dynasty]].

## The athlete-king

Amenhotep II cultivated a public image of unmatched physical prowess — driving a chariot with
the reins tied around his waist while shooting arrows clean through copper targets,
commemorated on the **Giza Sphinx Stele** and miniaturized on scarabs across the Levant. (Sara
Morris has argued this Egyptian motif lies behind Achilles' target-shooting in the *Iliad*.)
Breaking with tradition, he acknowledged no royal wife — only his mother Merytra as "great
royal wife" — part of the deliberate move away from the powerful princess-queens of the early
dynasty.

## Empire and the Mitanni peace

His **Levantine campaigns** in years 7 and 9 targeted "unaligned chiefs and rebellions among
recently acquired vassals," with enormous plunder (year 7: "6,800 deben of gold and 500,000
deben of copper … 550 *mariannu* captives, 210 horses, and 300 chariots"). He brought seven
defeated chiefs of Takhsy back hung head-down, six displayed on a temple wall and one carried
south to Napata — calculated terror. Yet the stelae, composed after the fighting, no longer
treat **Mitanni** as an enemy: a Memphis stele reports that "the chiefs of Nahrin, Hatti, and
Sangar (Babylon) arrived before the king bearing gifts" — "certainly the first official
announcement of the creation of a Mitanni peace." He also developed the cult of the Giza
Sphinx as **Horemakhet**.
