# Blackfoot Confederacy

The **Blackfoot Confederacy** (Fowler: Blackfeet groups—**Siksika, Blood, Piegan**; Algonquian) occupied western Saskatchewan and eastern Alberta and, with Gros Ventres and Sarsis, shaped northwestern Plains warfare and fur-trade politics from the mid-eighteenth century through Treaty Seven (1877).

## Overview

Assiniboines and Plains Crees expanded into Saskatchewan, displaced Gros Ventres and some Blackfeet, and dominated eighteenth-century fur trade as middlemen; guns traded at York Factory for 12 Made Beaver resold to Blackfeet for 36. With guns from Assiniboines and Crees, confederated **Blackfeet, Gros Ventre, and Sarsi** warriors began to drive the Shoshones south in the **1740s**. **1781 smallpox** so weakened Shoshones that by the 1790s they and Flathead/Kutenai allies were pushed west of the Rockies out of Montana.

North West Company then Hudson’s Bay posts up the Saskatchewan (e.g. Hudson House 1778) let Blackfeet groups, Gros Ventres, and Sarsis sell provisions directly; fur-depleted Assiniboines/Crees moved southwest into Blackfeet/Gros Ventre territory; by early nineteenth century better-armed Assiniboines/Crees drove Gros Ventres and some Blackfeet into Montana, displacing Shoshones.

After the Louisiana Purchase and Lewis and Clark, Americans pushed Upper Missouri beaver in Blackfeet and Crow country; American Fur Company monopoly on Upper Missouri by 1827; policy change **1831** let Native hunters exchange at forts. [[actors/crow-apsalooke|Crows]] eventually accepted Americans who helped against Tetons, but **Blackfeet and Gros Ventres**, angry at being bypassed by trappers and hunters, **repelled Americans wherever they found them** and continued to trade with the Hudson’s Bay Company. Fort Mackenzie served Blackfeet robe trade after accommodation.

## Role and Structure

- **Military:** equestrian warfare vs Shoshones then later vs Sioux pressure; horse-wealth stratification and medicine bundles (Plains-wide pattern).
- **Political:** multi-band confederacy; Treaty Seven (1877) named Siksika (Northern Blackfeet), Blood, Northern Piegan, Sarsi, and some Assiniboine in southern Alberta among numbered-treaty parties.
- **Economic:** provision trade to Saskatchewan posts; robe trade; resistance to American trapper bypass.

## Key Events (full fidelity)

### Marias River Piegan massacre (1869–70 context)

Montana Gold Rush **1862** and Territory **1864** brought demands to remove Natives north; Siksikas, Bloods, Northern Piegans withdrew into Canada. **1869** troops sent after squatters’ pressure; a **friendly Piegan camp sick with smallpox was attacked without warning and massacred on the Marias River**, far from the miners’ settlements. Piegans could not risk war with both the U.S. and Tetons; **1873** Grant reduced reservation without consultation/compensation; **1874** Congress moved southern boundary to the Marias; Piegans withdrew north of the river.

### Gros Ventre related pattern (Fowler)

Gros Ventres east of Piegans had about one-third of Blackfeet confederacy population and absorbed much Sioux attack; hunting far north of settlements, they viewed Americans as allies vs Tetons, Yanktons, Yanktonais, and Santees flooding Montana 1860s–70s; allied with Crows and Upper Assiniboines; did not settle near Fort Belknap agency until **1878**.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Blackfeet–Gros Ventre–Sarsi military expansion after guns and horses, Shoshone/Plateau geography of the northern Rockies would differ; without HBC orientation after 1831, American Fur Company geography of the Upper Missouri would face less resistance. Gold-rush pressure and Marias massacre still illustrate the U.S. pattern of unprovoked winter/village destruction concurrent with southern plains wars.
