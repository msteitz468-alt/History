# Pertinax

Publius Helvius Pertinax (126–193 CE) was a Roman general and senator of humble origin (son of a freedman) who rose through a long equestrian and senatorial career — provincial governorships, consulships, and command of frontier legions — before being proclaimed emperor by the Senate on 1 January 193, immediately after the assassination of [[commodus]] the previous night.

His three-month reign attempted rapid fiscal and disciplinary reform: he cut Commodus's extravagances, tried to restore senatorial dignity, and moved to tighten discipline over the Praetorian Guard, whose donative he could not fully pay. The Guard, resentful of the reforms and the reduced payout, mutinied and murdered him on 28 March 193.

His death triggered the notorious auction of the empire, in which the Guard sold the throne to Didius Julianus, discrediting the succession and prompting provincial commanders — including [[septimius-severus]], [[pescennius-niger]], and Clodius Albinus — to march on Rome, opening the Year of the Five Emperors (193).
