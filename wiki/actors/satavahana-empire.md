# The Satavahana (Andhra) Dynasty

The **Satavahanas** (also the **Andhra** dynasty) were the dominant power of the **Deccan** in the
post-Mauryan centuries (c. 1st c. BCE – 3rd c. CE) — the polity that made the Deccan the
**connecting link between northern and peninsular India**, in politics, trade, and the exchange of
ideas. Documented for the wiki through Romila Thapar's
[[thapar-history-of-india-v1-1966|*A History of India*, Vol. I]] (1966).

## Rise and apogee

Probably former Mauryan administrators (Ashoka names the Andhras among his subject peoples), the
Satavahanas rose during the post-Mauryan confusion, centred on **Pratishthana (Paithan)** in the
north-western Deccan. **Satakarni** ("Lord of the South," *Dakshinapathapati*) expanded in all
directions and performed the **horse-sacrifice** to assert imperial claims. After a period of defeat
at the hands of the western **[[kushan-empire|Shaka satraps]]** (Nahapana's coins circulate in the
Nasik area), **Gautamiputra Satakarni** (early 2nd c. CE) **drove the Shakas out** — his name
overstruck on Nahapana's coins — and his son **Vasishthiputra Shri Pulumavi** (Ptolemy's "Siro
Polemaios") raised the dynasty to its height, controlling the Deccan from coast to coast. A
matrimonial alliance with the Shaka **Rudradaman** failed to end the rivalry; after Rudradaman the
Satavahanas pressed back, but by the 3rd century local governors were asserting independence and the
dynasty dissolved.

## Government and culture

Thapar reads Satavahana rule as **decentralised**: unlike the Indo-Greeks and Kushanas, they took
**no grandiose imperial titles**, distributing power among civil (*amatya*) and military
(*mahasenapati*) governors — the latter allowed to marry into the royal house and even mint coins,
so that when the centre collapsed they became independent rulers. The village remained the
administrative unit. The dynasty patronised **brahmanical orthodoxy** (Vedic sacrifice, the use of
Sanskrit/Prakrit over the local dialect) and so was a key agent of the **Aryanisation of the
Deccan** — even as Gautamiputra boasted of "stopping the contamination of the four *varnas*" while
marrying into a Shaka family, one of Thapar's standing examples of the **gap between caste theory
and practice** (see [[caste-varna]]). Their Deccan, criss-crossed by trade routes, drew lavish guild
donations to Buddhist monuments (the Nasik caves, the Sanchi gateways).

## Significance

The Satavahanas integrated the **Deccan** into the subcontinental and [[indian-ocean-trade|Indian
Ocean]] economy, ending the isolation of the peninsular south and channelling the **Roman trade** and
the spread of Aryan/Sanskritic culture southward — the bridge that made later north–south interaction
(and the [[chola-empire|Chola]] and Deccan empires) possible.
