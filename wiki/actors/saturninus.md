# Lucius Appuleius Saturninus

## Overview
Lucius Appuleius Saturninus (d. 100 BCE) was the radical *popularis* tribune of
**103 and 100 BCE** whose alliance with [[marius|Gaius Marius]] and escalating use
of organized political violence ended in his death under a *[[senatus-consultum-ultimum|
senatus consultum ultimum]]* — the second such episode after [[gaius-gracchus|Gaius
Gracchus]], and a further step in normalizing lethal factional conflict.

## Role and Significance
Humiliated as quaestor when the Senate stripped him of the Ostia grain supply and
handed it to Scaurus, Saturninus turned to the tribunate. As tribune he:
- established a permanent *maiestas* (treason) court and drove the disgraced
  Arausio commanders Caepio and Mallius into exile through mob pressure;
- passed **land grants for Marius's veterans** — a retirement-bonus model
  (freely disposable allotments, extended to Italian allies as well as citizens)
  that set an enduring precedent binding soldiers' loyalty to their general;
- attached to that law a **mandatory senatorial oath** never to repeal it, which
  all senators swore except **Metellus Numidicus**, who chose exile rather than
  submit;
- partnered with the praetor **Gaius Servilius Glaucia** and exploited the memory
  of the Gracchi (even producing an impostor claiming to be Tiberius Gracchus's
  son).

His faction's violence culminated in the murder of the consular candidate **Gaius
Memmius** during the elections for 99. The Senate then passed the *SCU* and
charged Marius — Saturninus's own former ally — with restoring order. Marius
besieged Saturninus and his followers on the Capitol by cutting the water pipes;
after their surrender a mob broke into the Senate house and stoned them to death
with roof tiles.

## Counterfactual Significance
Saturninus is a hinge between [[gaius-gracchus|Gaius Gracchus]] and the warlord
tribunes (Sulpicius, [[cornelius-cinna|Cinna]]): he showed how a tribune allied
with a great military patron could drive a legislative programme by force — and
how readily that patron would abandon and destroy him when the violence turned
inconvenient. His veteran land law fused the professional army created by the
[[marian-army-reforms|Marian reforms]] to the politics of land, a combination
central to the [[mos-maiorum|collapse of Republican norms]].

## Historiography
Saturninus is known through Appian, Plutarch's *Marius*, and a hostile Cicero
(who nonetheless rated him the ablest demagogue since the Gracchi). Because Marius
turned on him, the tradition — much of it filtered through anti-Marian sources — is
unsympathetic; his precise legislative record and the degree of Marius's complicity
in the final violence are debated.
