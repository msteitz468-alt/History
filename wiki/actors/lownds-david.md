# David Lownds

U.S. Marine Corps colonel (1920–2011), commander of the 26th Marine Regiment during the 77-day siege of Khe Sanh Combat Base (January–April 1968). Lownds directed the base's defense against a sustained North Vietnamese Army siege that coincided with the opening of the Tet Offensive, holding roughly 6,000 Marines and attached forces against an estimated two NVA divisions while relying heavily on American air power (Operation Niagara) for resupply and fire support once ground routes were cut.

The siege drew intense attention from Washington, with President Johnson reportedly demanding regular briefings and comparisons drawn (by the press and some officers, though contested by historians) to the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu. The base held throughout the siege and was relieved by Operation Pegasus in April 1968, after which it was controversially abandoned by U.S. forces later that year, fueling debate over whether the immense effort to hold it had been strategically necessary.

## Significance

Khe Sanh under Lownds's command became one of the most closely watched engagements of the Vietnam War, later debated as either a vindication of American firepower and defensive resolve or as a costly diversion exploited by North Vietnamese planners as a feint ahead of Tet.
