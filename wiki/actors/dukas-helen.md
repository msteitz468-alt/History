# Helen Dukas

**1896–1982.** German-Jewish secretary, archivist, and longtime household manager for Albert Einstein. Hired 1928 in Berlin; accompanied the family into exile; served as gatekeeper, protector, and companion in Princeton until Einstein's death and beyond as archivist of his papers.

## Role and Affiliations

Employed initially as secretary ("Cerberus" per observers) to manage correspondence and shield Einstein from intrusions. After Elsa Einstein's death (1936), became "even more of a loyal guardian." Lived in the Mercer Street household in Princeton. Accompanied Einstein on his longest postwar trip (Florida recuperation 1948/9). During final illness (April 1955): set up bed beside his, applied ice to dehydrated lips; managed visitors and medical decisions.

## Key Actions and Character

- Zealous protector: screened unannounced visitors (e.g., turned away Albert Shadowitz initially before admitting on story); handled mail and intrusions.
- Loyalty in crisis: during aneurysm episode and collapse, provided round-the-clock care; "Dukas found him the next morning in agony"; over-wrought but calmed by Einstein's humor ("You’re really hysterical...").
- Posthumous: helped preserve papers; collaborated on archives (with Otto Nathan etc.).

Suspicions (e.g., by Hans Albert Einstein of possible affair with Einstein) circulated in family circles but were viewed as implausible by others who knew her.

## In Exile Context

Embodied the refugee support network that enabled Einstein's American phase. Part of the tight Princeton household (with stepdaughter Margot) that sustained him after European losses. Present at cremation and ash scattering (Delaware River, April 1955).

`affiliated_with:` [[actors/einstein-albert]] (primary); `enabled:` Einstein's focus on equations and public statements by handling domestic and external pressures. `part_of:` the exile household dynamics documented in Isaacson.

## Historiography

Isaacson (drawing on family recollections, Fantova journal, Bucky family) portrays Dukas as indispensable "guardian" without romanticizing. Limited independent sources; her role is seen through Einstein-centric accounts. No major controversies beyond family rumors.
