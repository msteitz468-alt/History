# Sasanian Empire

## Overview

The **Sasanian Empire** (224–651 CE) was the last great pre-Islamic Iranian empire
and, with [[byzantine-empire|Byzantium]], one of the **"two eyes of the earth"**
(Khusro II's phrase) that dominated [[late-antiquity|Late Antique]] Southwest Asia.
Founded by **Ardashir I**, who overthrew the [[parthian-empire|Parthians]] in 224 CE,
it recast the Iranian state on a more **centralized and religiously unified** basis —
binding the former Parthian nobles and client kingdoms into **Iranshahr** ("Empire
of the Iranians") and making **[[zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism]]** an imperial state
religion. It claimed the land "from the Oxus to the Euphrates and beyond."

## Religion and State

Where Parthia was religiously tolerant, the Sasanians forged a **Zoroastrian
"orthodoxy"** tied to the throne — kings shown on coins and reliefs receiving the
diadem from Ohrmazd or Anahita; the **Avesta** codified; **fire-temples** founded
across the empire (the holiest, Adur Gushnasp). After early flirtation with **Mani's**
universalist [[manichaeism|Manichaeism]], the priestly class (the Magi) persecuted
Manichaeans, and intermittently Jews, Christians, and Buddhists — though by the 5th
century the empire also hosted an organized **Church of Persia** (a *catholicos* at
[[ctesiphon|Ctesiphon]]). See [[universal-religions]] and [[zoroastrianism]].

## Rome/Byzantium and the Silk Roads

Roman–Sasanian rivalry structured Near Eastern politics for four centuries.
**Shapur I** (3rd c.) defeated, killed, or captured three Roman emperors (famously
taking **Valerian**); the wars ruined the caravan cities (Dura-Europos, **Palmyra**).
The Euphrates became the fortified border. The Sasanians aggressively managed the
**[[silk-roads|silk]] trade** — their merchants buying up Indian Ocean silk to
control prices, denying the **Sogdians** direct access to Roman markets, and seizing
**South Arabia (568 CE)** to dominate the maritime route. **Khusro I** (6th c.)
reformed coinage, taxation, administration, and the military, and built the **Arch of
Khusro** at Ctesiphon — a cosmopolitan capital rivaling Constantinople.

## The Late-Antique World System and Collapse

Sasanian Iran, [[byzantine-empire|Byzantium]], and [[sui-dynasty|Sui]]/[[tang-dynasty|Tang]]
China — with their courts at **Ctesiphon, Constantinople, and Chang'an** — and the
in-between Arabs, Armenians, Sogdians, and [[gokturk|Türk khaganates]] constituted
the **Eurasian world system of Late Antiquity**. The system was shattered by Khusro
II's all-out invasion of Byzantium (602–628; seizing Palestine, Egypt, and the True
Cross, besieging Constantinople), reversed by Heraclius's counter-invasion (a
"proto-Crusade"). The mutual exhaustion of both empires opened the way for the
**Arab-Muslim conquest**: defeats at **Qadisiyya (637 CE)** and elsewhere, and the
death of the last king **Yazdgerd III (651 CE)**, ended the empire — though Sasanian
administrative, fiscal, and courtly forms were inherited wholesale by the
[[umayyad-caliphate|Umayyad]] and [[abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid]] caliphates.

## Historiography

Comparatively **source-poor** and long known mainly through hostile Roman, Armenian,
and later Islamic accounts, Sasanian history has been substantially rebuilt from
coins, seals, rock reliefs, and archaeology (Daryaee's own work; the Idea of Iran
project). James Howard-Johnston's thesis that Khusro II's overreach "destroyed the
ancient world order" frames the empire's fall as the hinge between antiquity and the
Islamic Near East. Compare [[fall-of-rome-causes]] and [[empire]].

A contemporary witness to the Roman–Persian frontier is now ingested as the
**[[watt-pseudo-joshua-stylite|Chronicle of Pseudo-Joshua the Stylite]]**, the best source for the
**[[anastasian-war-502-506|Anastasian War (502–506)]]** between Kavadh I and Anastasius — the opening
round of the sixth-century wars.

## The Sasanians and the End of the First Silk Roads Era (Benjamin 2018)

In [[benjamin-empires-ancient-eurasia-2018|Benjamin's]] account the Sasanians were *born*
from the unwinding of the [[silk-roads|First Silk Roads Era]] and then helped finish it.
Ardashir I defeated and killed the Parthian Artabanus V at **Hormozdgan (28 April 224 CE)**
and was crowned **Shahanshah at [[ctesiphon|Ctesiphon]]**. His son **Shapur I** (r. 241–272)
pressed Rome hard and took Bactria from the [[kushan-empire|Kushans]]; by **262 CE**
Sasanian forces had destroyed the Kushan royal cities at [[begram|Begram]] and
[[taxila|Taxila]], incorporating the northwest as the province of **Kushanshahr** — a key
strand of the [[collapse-of-first-silk-roads-era-250|collapse of the network]]. The
Sasanians promoted **[[zoroastrianism]]** as a unifying state cult (fire temples in
conquered territory), but Benjamin's larger point is that — like the Kushans and Achaemenids
before them — they functioned as a **spatial bridge between China and the West and a
chronological bridge** from late antiquity to Islam; Sasanian coins and silk (Simurgh
motifs) circulated across the Indian Ocean and into Tang China, and Persian models of rule
persisted across a millennium.
