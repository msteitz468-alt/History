# Alfred Gwynne Vanderbilt

Alfred Gwynne Vanderbilt (1877–1915). Great-grandson of the Commodore via [[actors/vanderbilt-cornelius-ii|Cornelius II]]; sportsman, coach-driver, Vanderbilt Hotel investor. Died in the sinking of the [[events/sinking-of-lusitania-1915|RMS *Lusitania*]] on 7 May 1915.

## Narrative

Cooper & Howe stress that Alfred was not the expected head of his branch: William Henry Vanderbilt II died of typhoid, and brother Neily was effectively frozen out after a disapproved marriage, leaving Alfred with a large share (on the order of $35 million in the book's figures) of Cornelius II's estate. He married Ellen French (1901), conducted a long affair with former actress Agnes Ruiz, divorced Ellen in 1908, and saw Agnes die by suicide in 1909; he later married Margaret Emerson. He booked passage on *Lusitania*'s May 1915 crossing; when the ship was torpedoed on 7 May he was among those lost. Contemporary press celebrated (and mythologized) reports that he gave his lifebelt to others and helped women and children — accounts from passengers such as Slidell and crew such as steward/Ratcliffe.

## Historiography

Separate press hagiography from documented sequence. Cooper & Howe also place Alfred's private life (Ruiz) beside the hero narrative. Lusitania munitions/auxiliary-cruiser debates belong to military-diplomatic historiography, not to the family chapter alone.
