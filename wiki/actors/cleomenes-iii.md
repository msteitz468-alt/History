## Role

Cleomenes III (c. 260–219 BCE), Agiad king of [[sparta|Sparta]], was the second and far
more formidable of Sparta's **revolutionary kings** — the man who revived and radicalised
the failed reforms of **[[agis-iv|Agis IV]]**, briefly made Sparta the dominant power of
the Peloponnese, and drove [[aratus-of-sicyon|Aratus]] and the [[achaean-league|Achaean
League]] to call Macedon back into Greece. He is a central figure of CAH VII.1 ch. 12
(Walbank).

## The Spartan Revolution (227)

After early successes in the [[cleomenean-war-229|war with Achaea]] (Mt Lycaeum, the
seizure of Arcadian cities), Cleomenes carried out a long-planned **coup** (autumn 227):
he killed four of the five **ephors**, abolished the ephorate (condemning it as an
"excrescence on the Lycurgan constitution"), and enacted the radical programme Agis had
attempted:
- **all land pooled and redistributed** into ~4,000 lots;
- **cancellation of debts**;
- the citizen body refilled (to ~5,000) from *perioikoi* and resident foreigners;
- the **revival of the *agoge*** and common messes.

Crucially he also **modernised the army**, introducing the Macedonian-style **phalanx
with the long *sarissa*** — making Sparta a serious military power again. Walbank stresses
his aims were **practical, not ideological** (despite the Stoic Sphaerus' alleged role):
to restore the old Spartan hegemony, not to enact a Stoic utopia.

## Triumph and Fall

The revolution made Cleomenes formidable. He routed the Achaeans at the **Hecatombaeum**
(226), and as the [[achaean-league|League disintegrated]] he swept up Argos, Corinth, and
much of the Peloponnese — his social slogans (land-redistribution, debt-cancellation)
drawing support from the poor across the cities. This was exactly what alarmed the
propertied [[achaean-league|Achaean]] class: rather than submit, **[[aratus-of-sicyon|Aratus]]
called in [[antigonus-iii-doson|Antigonus III Doson]]** (surrendering the Acrocorinth,
224). With Egyptian subsidies cut off by [[ptolemaic-egypt|Ptolemy III]], Cleomenes was
crushed at **[[battle-of-sellasia-222|Sellasia]]** (222); his reforms were cancelled and
the ephorate restored. He fled to Egypt and died (219) leading a futile revolt in
Alexandria.

## Counterfactual Significance

Cleomenes is the fullest realisation of the **Hellenistic social revolution from above**
that Agis had begun — a king turning land-redistribution and debt-cancellation into an
engine of state revival and a Peloponnesian power-bid. His threat reveals the **fragility
and class-anxiety** of the federal Achaean order, and his defeat — bought by inviting
Macedon back into the Peloponnese — marked the end of independent Spartan greatness and a
decisive reassertion of Antigonid hegemony. He became a Stoic and revolutionary legend
(via Phylarchus and Plutarch). See [[sparta]], [[agis-iv]], [[cleomenean-war-229]],
[[battle-of-sellasia-222]].
