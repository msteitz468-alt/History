# Girolamo Savonarola

## Overview

**Girolamo Savonarola** (1452–1498), Dominican friar, became the dominant voice and **de facto ruler of Florence** for six years after the death of **Lorenzo de’ Medici**. [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]] places his rise among the emblematic events of **1492**.

## Rise (Logan’s 1492 vignette)

Lightning struck Florence’s duomo cupola on the night of **5 April 1492**—Lorenzo took it as a death omen. Three days later he received Savonarola’s blessing and died. Without Lorenzo’s restraint, Savonarola’s denunciations of church vice, social corruption, and luxury made him Florence’s de facto ruler for six years. Carnival **1497** saw the **bonfire of the vanities**. Logan likens the fervour to Black Death flagellants and notes parallels some have drawn to Calvin’s Geneva—then states that the religious fervour **ended with no obvious lasting effects**.

## Counterfactual Significance

Logan’s own judgment is that Savonarola’s theocratic moment left little institutional residue in Florence—an episode of intense piety rather than a durable reformation. Without Lorenzo’s death, the friar’s political ascent might never have occurred.

## Historiography

Logan’s treatment is epilogue-brief and deflationary on lasting impact. Fuller biographies and Florentine political histories (not his brief) debate Savonarola’s relationship to republicanism, prophecy, and the Borgia papacy of Alexander VI; those dimensions exceed Logan’s 1492 sketch.
