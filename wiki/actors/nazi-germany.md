# Nazi Germany (the Third Reich, 1933–1945)

The totalitarian one-party state established when **[[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]]** and the NSDAP
dismantled the [[weimar-republic|Weimar Republic]] in 1933. Through *Gleichschaltung* (spring/summer 1933) the regime
absorbed civil society into a racial "people's community" ([[volksgemeinschaft]]); the process was 'for the most part undertaken voluntarily and with alacrity' with remarkably little direct initiative required from Hitler himself (Reich Governors law 7 April smashed remaining Länder sovereignty). The Enabling Act (23 March) allowed the Reichstag to vote itself out of existence. The 'Night of the Long Knives' (30 June 1934) eliminated the SA threat and secured army loyalty. Persecuted Jews and
other targeted groups (the Nuremberg Laws, [[kristallnacht-1938|Kristallnacht]]), rearmed, and
pursued territorial expansion.

In 1939 it launched [[second-world-war-1939|the Second World War]], conquering most of Europe before
the tide turned at Stalingrad. Its war of annihilation in the East and the industrialized murder of
six million Jews — [[the-holocaust|the Holocaust]] — make the regime the moral and analytical centre
of modern German history and the reference point against which the whole [[german-sonderweg|Sonderweg
debate]] is conducted. It ended in total defeat, the suicide of Hitler, and unconditional surrender in
May 1945, followed by Allied [[cold-war|occupation]] and division.

**War economy before the war (Flynn 1944, attributed)**: [[flynn-as-we-go-marching-1944|Flynn]]
reads the regime's economics as continuation, not innovation — the same
borrow-and-spend device of every predecessor government, applied first to
Autobahns, housing, and Kraft durch Freude and after 1935 to rearmament
(unemployment 6,000,000 in 1932 → 164,000 in 1938; debt estimates so
politically managed they ranged from Stolper's 40bn marks to a 1943 Berne
report's 203bn). Schacht's "wizardry" he reduces to ordinary devices possible
only under dictatorship: compulsory bond purchases by cash-rich firms,
auto-renewing promissory notes to contractors, plus outright confiscation of
Jewish property, trade-union funds, and the republic's gold reserve. Militarism
was "Germany's greatest single industry" — the one deficit-financed
public-works program conservatives would fund — and, on Flynn's contested
reading, it was Hitler's policy that produced the war rather than popular
war-desire that produced Hitler.

**Payne's structural account (1995)**: [[payne-history-of-fascism-1995|Payne]]
describes the Third Reich as a "dual state" (Fraenkel) or polycracy — the
regular bureaucracy paralleled by some sixty party boards and agencies in
"administrative chaos" Hitler may have preferred because it maximized his
arbitration power (Manoilescu's contemporary formula: the Soviet system a
state run by a party, Italy a state over the party, Germany a party-state
dyarchy). Rearmament spending ran 1bn RM (1933) → 9.3bn (1936, Four Year
Plan) → 30bn (1939); unemployment fell from 6 million to effective
elimination by 1938 — but Payne stresses German unemployment was no worse
than democratic Norway's, so the Depression alone cannot explain the
triumph. Against both the "agent of capitalism" theory (refuted via Turner:
big business advised against Hitler; the party was member-financed) and the
"revolution of nihilism" reading, Payne presents Nazism as "a unique and
radical kind of modern revolutionism" (Bracher's seven revolutionary
qualities: artist-genius leader cult, Social Darwinist restructuring, racial
revolution, state-regulated national socialism, Volksgemeinschaft status
revolution, world-scale racial imperialism, media-technological
mobilization) and a pagan political religion (Bormann: "The National
Socialist and Christian conceptions are incompatible"). The racial state
escalated from ~200,000 sterilizations by 1937 through the euthanasia
program (~5,000 children, ~100,000 "incurably sick" adults, gas chambers
staffed by later Final Solution personnel — "a sort of trial run") to the
wartime New Order: near eight million forced foreign laborers (a fifth of
the labor force), and total killings Payne tallies at ~10 million direct
liquidations plus 3.3 million Soviet POWs starved and nearly four million
slave laborers worked to death — "exceed[ing] that of any equivalent phase
even of Stalinism." Full totalitarian structural control, he notes (with
Arendt), arrived only in the last wartime phase under Goebbels'
total-war plenipotentiary powers. His five-point Nazism/Italian-Fascism
comparison and hedged ten-point Nazism/Communism parallel are recorded at
[[generic-fascism]] and [[communism-and-fascism]]; intentionalist vs.
structuralist historiography at [[the-holocaust]].

**Evans's peacetime anatomy (2005)**: [[evans-third-reich-in-power-2005|Evans]]
covers the consolidated dictatorship 1933–39 domain by domain, and his
synthesis is the wiki's fullest account of the pre-war state:

- **Terror**: a police state resting less on the (small, denunciation-fed)
  [[gestapo|Gestapo]] than on the ordinary justice system — People's Court
  and Special Courts (~3,400 tried 1934–39; executions rising 64 (1933) →
  117 (1938)), "security confinement" of 14,000+ petty "habitual criminals,"
  Gürtner's Justice Ministry legalizing it all — with the SS
  [[nazi-concentration-camps|camp system]] (only ~3,000 inmates in mid-1934,
  21,000 by 1939) reoriented from the crushed left onto "community aliens."
  Evans defends a qualified top-down-terror reading against the strong
  "self-policing society" thesis, and qualifies Fraenkel's dual state: state
  organs (tax offices, Reichsbank) were "if anything, even more unscrupulous"
  than party ones in despoiling Jews.
- **Opinion**: Goebbels's total media machine (radio in 70%+ of households;
  the press reduced from 4,700 to under 1,000 titles) had by 1937–38
  abolished "public opinion" as such — while Sopade/SD reports showed
  boredom, grumbling, and war-anxiety persisting beneath compulsory
  acclamation; Klemperer's *LTI* documents the corruption of the language
  itself.
- **Society**: [[volksgemeinschaft]] as psychological achievement atop
  unchanged class structure; wages at 1928 levels only in 1939 on longer
  hours; the [[german-labour-front|Labour Front/KdF]] apparatus; corruption
  as a system property, rooted (Evans argues) in the Leader's own position
  above the law — Hitler's Mein Kampf royalties, the written-off 400,000-RM
  tax bill, the 50-million-RM stamp-royalty cheque, down through Göring's
  Carinhall and Ley's villas to camp commandants' embezzlement.
- **Trajectory**: "a vast experiment in human engineering" pointed at war —
  "If ever a state merited being called totalitarian, then it was the Third
  Reich" — modern (motorways, rocketry, eugenics-as-science), driven from
  above, and never completely coordinated (churches, private life, the
  generations).

**The Nazi empire (Mazower, via Evans 2015, attributed)**: Mazower's
*Hitler's Empire* (2008) gives the first systematic Europe-wide account of
the Reich as empire-builder. Lebensraum ideology emerged in late
19th-century Germany/Austria explicitly on the model of New-World settler
expansion; after the failure of overseas empire, the target became Eastern
Europe. The empire in practice was administratively incoherent — a spectrum
from collaborationist regimes (Slovakia, Vichy) through military government
to Reich Commissariats and outright annexation, with **no central
direction** (no equivalent of Japan's Greater East Asia Ministry): Hitler
bypassed the civil service for Party "Old Fighters" and Gauleiters, while
Himmler's SS bypassed everyone in redrawing the racial map. Hitler's
imperial model was British India ("The Russian space is our India"), and
racial dogma foreclosed co-opting anti-Soviet nationalism — Ukrainians who
greeted the Wehrmacht with bread and salt were, in a Rosenberg aide's words
(Feb 1944), "within a year, chased into the woods and swamps, as
partisans." The 1940–41 "New Order in Europe" economic-integration
planning gave way to simple exploitation (occupation costs, rigged
exchange rates, looted rolling stock), and Western Europe contributed far
less to the war effort than Berlin hoped. Mazower's larger claim (Evans
endorses it as his most original): the empire's very ruthlessness
discredited the idea of racial world-empire generally — 1945's victors
were anti-imperialist powers, and the postwar European project was a
de-fanged descendant of the "New Order" debates (Erhard had taken part).
Evans's caveats: colonial-administration continuity is overstated (see
[[colonialism-nazism-continuity]]), and the older European empires,
however racist and sometimes exterminatory, rested on collaboration and
compromise foreign to the Nazi empire's narrow exploitative nationalism.

**The Foreign Office and its afterlife (*Das Amt*, via Evans 2015, ch. 17,
attributed)**: the 2010 independent commission history (Conze, Frei,
Hayes, Zimmermann), triggered by the Nüsslein/Krapf obituary rows, exploded
the ministry's founding postwar myth of having been "a hotbed of
opposition." The old aristocratic diplomatic corps self-coordinated
voluntarily in 1933; wartime diplomats fed the machinery of deportation
(Benzler/Rademacher in Serbia: "Liquidation of Jews in Belgrade") while a
few (Altenburg in Greece) showed the choices available. Denazification was
token; by 1955 the rebuilt Foreign Office had proportionally *more* ex-NSDAP
members than the wartime ministry, and men implicated in gas-van planning
(Bräutigam) returned to senior roles. Evans's assessment: scholarly flaws
("a whiff of the witch-hunt," unfootnoted overclaims such as the FO "taking
the initiative" in the Final Solution) but the core finding — the FO "was an
essential part of the machinery of government in the Third Reich" — proven
beyond reasonable doubt; the case of diplomat Curt Prüfer, who doctored his
own diaries in 1946 to manufacture an anti-Nazi past, stands as the emblem
of the memory politics involved.

**Why it fought to total destruction (Kershaw's *The End*, via Evans 2015,
ch. 22, attributed)**: no negotiated exit was structurally possible —
totalizing ideology ("either Germany would achieve global hegemony or it
would go under"); "charismatic rule without charisma" persisting until the
bunker; no institutional counterweight (no Fascist Grand Council to depose
the leader, as Italy deposed Mussolini); terror turned inward in the final
months (the Volkssturm's 175,000 dead; public executions of "defeatists";
Himmler's 3 April 1945 order to shoot all males in houses showing white
flags; ~20,000 soldiers executed for desertion vs. 8 in WWI; the death
marches); and fear of Soviet retribution sharpened by guilty knowledge
("Have we not slaughtered Jews in their thousands?"). The generals'
postwar blaming of the Casablanca unconditional-surrender demand is
rejected — the High Command took "hardly any notice" of it at the time.
Evans's caveat: Kershaw underweights sincere German-nationalist conviction
as a motive for fighting on.

**Consumer promises and the People's Car (Rieger via Evans 2015, ch. 12)**:
the KdF-Wagen scheme took 990-RM savings stamps from over a quarter-million
Germans and delivered not one car — funds went to armaments; only 630
pre-war Beetles were built, mostly for officials; the factory switched to
Kübelwagen production with forced labor. The 1934 racial-community Highway
Code abolished speed limits and produced Europe's worst accident rate
(~8,000 deaths/year). Evans reads Rieger as "another nail in the coffin" of
the backward-looking-atavism interpretation of Nazism; the postwar Beetle's
rise under Nordhoff (himself a forced-labor-tainted manager) traded on
Nazi-era *Leistung* rhetoric while suppressing the origins — and Wolfsburg's
embittered expellees gave a neo-Nazi party two-thirds of the local vote in
1948.

**Plunder and the built environment (Evans 2015, chs. 27–28)**: the regime
was "a byword for corruption" whose Social-Darwinist property doctrine
licensed both state spoliation and mass individual theft — from the 1933
confiscation of union and socialist assets, through the staged
expropriation of the Jews (Vienna 1938 as the model; emigration-asset
seizure; deportees' property falling to the Reich), to Posse's
8,000-object collection for Hitler's planned Linz museum,
[[goring-hermann|Göring's]] Carinhall hoard, compulsory registration and
confiscation of all pre-1850 Polish artworks (Dec 1939), and the looting
of Soviet collections (the Amber Room). Nazi town planning fused the same
racial utopianism with anti-urbanism: Stosberg's 1941 model "new German
town of Auschwitz" — people's dwellings, garden cells, IG Farben
employment, New Year's cards announcing its "birth" — was built beside
the extermination camp after the deportation of the town's Jews and
Poles, and stands (Evans) as the model of what Nazi planners intended for
the postwar East. Restitution of looted art, dormant from the 1950s,
revived only after 1989–90 (Washington Conference 1998).

On the regime's propaganda theory see [[big-lie-propaganda]]; on the popular dispute over fascism's place on the left–right spectrum see [[fascism-and-the-political-left]].

## Founding institutions of 1933 — Evans, *The Coming of the Third Reich* (2003)

The Reich Ministry for Popular Enlightenment and Propaganda (decree of 13
March 1933, Goebbels with a cabinet seat) was Hitler's "most original
institutional creation" — ~350 officials averaging barely thirty, nearly a
hundred wearing the golden party badge. The professional purges of 1933:
1,145 of 7,758 established university teachers (15%) dismissed — nearly a
third in Berlin/Frankfurt, twenty past or future Nobel laureates lost
(Einstein, Schrödinger, Born, Haber among them). For the full seizure-of-power
machinery — auxiliary police, camps, the destruction of parties and unions,
coordination of every association down to the village singing club — see
[[nazi-seizure-of-power-1933]], [[gleichschaltung]],
[[nazi-cultural-purge-1933]], [[destruction-of-german-trade-unions-1933]],
[[dissolution-of-german-parties-1933]]. Source:
[[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003]]

## The New Order economy — Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)

Occupied Europe was run as a plunder economy under a "hodge-podge" of regimes — annexation, Gau attachment, protectorates, Reich commissariats, military administrations, client states — with no coherent imperial design beyond extraction and race. France alone yielded 2,170 tanks, 4,260 locomotives, and by end-1943 some 25 billion RM in "occupation costs"; over 30% of Western Europe's net wartime production was extracted, and ~25% of Reich revenues ultimately came from conquest. Yet with 75% of GDP devoted to war by 1944, Germany still faced an Allied economic preponderance above 3:1. See [[german-war-economy-1914-1945]], [[forced-labor-nazi-germany]], and [[speer-albert]]. Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## The New Order and occupation economics — Paxton and Hessler (2011)

Paxton and Hessler describe Hitler's continental empire as an entity "without parallel since Imperial Rome": a German-dominated Großwirtschaftsraum (greater economic space) ringed by allied states — Horthy's Hungary, Antonescu's Romania, Tiso's Slovakia, Pavelić's Croatia, Mannerheim's Finland, and Vichy France — with Franco's Spain friendly and Sweden supplying some 10 million tons of iron ore a year; by late 1942 only Portugal and Switzerland remained effectively neutral. Hitler's own table talk of 17 October 1941 sketched the intended endpoint: Germanize Russia's conquered space and "look upon the natives as Redskins."

Occupation costs reached 58% of French tax receipts. Poland was subjected to a compulsory-labor decree from 26 October 1939; more than a million Poles were working in Germany by 1942, forced to wear the violet "P" badge, with the death penalty applied for relations with German women. Of Soviet prisoners of war, four in five worked or starved to death in German captivity. French workers became the largest single foreign-labor contingent in Germany late in the war. In Ukraine, Reich Commissar Erich Koch treated the feeding of the civilian population as "a matter of the utmost indifference" — an instance of racial ideology repeatedly overriding economic rationality: Ukrainian farm restoration was foregone, and offers to relax the "mongrelized" status of occupied France were refused on similar grounds. Source: [[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011]].

## Wartime radicalization of state and justice — Evans (2008), Parts 5–6

As Hitler withdrew (see [[adolf-hitler]]), the regime fragmented into competing empires — Bormann's Party Chancellery, Himmler's police-state expansion into the Interior Ministry (August 1943), 42 Gauleiter Reich Defence Commissioners, Goebbels's bombing-crisis apparatus — while radicalizing downward: Ley's Block Wardens surveilled households monthly in an explicit return to "time of struggle" psychology. Justice became terror: the "National Pests" decree (5 September 1939), 40+ capital offences by 1940, death sentences rising 329 (1939) → 5,336 (1943), execution by hanging for speed — including the Plötzensee night of 7–8 September 1943, when 186 prisoners were hanged in batches to clear bomb-damaged cells and six men executed by file-error were "corrected" by hanging six more. The Thierack–Himmler agreement (18 September 1942) transferred "asocials" to SS extermination through labor; the draft 1944 "community aliens" law — never enacted — would have let police eliminate anyone deemed work-shy or disorderly. Military justice matched it: ~30,000 wartime death sentences (48 in all of WWI), ~21,000 carried out, 15,000 of them for desertion. Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## Griffin (2007): The Third Reich's Biopolitical Modernism

*Modernism and Fascism* (chs. 9–11) analyses the Third Reich as a totalitarian [[modernist-state]] created by a [[revitalization-movement]]. Against Henry Turner's "utopian anti-modernism," Griffin's corrective: "Nazi modernity was not a two-headed Janus, but a three-headed Chimaera" — mythicized past, intense modernization, and the systematic purge of the "dysgenic." Documentation: Zuse's Versuchsmodell 4 (1941, Reich-funded, "the first fully operational modern computer"); SS use of custom IBM/Dehomag punch-card machines with IBM New York's full collaboration (1.5 billion cards in 1943; Auschwitz Hollerith tattoo numbers); televised 1936 Olympics and the Fernseh-Volksempfänger; the "intensely modern, technologically advanced, supremely stylized" face Irene Guenther documents (German Fashion Institute — with a "sewing room" in Auschwitz tailoring for SS wives); Robert Ley's DAF/KdF Taylorist welfare utopia for "Aryans" while "slaves went to their excruciating, anonymous deaths"; Generalplan Ost technocrats ("Autobahnen would anchor German settlements from Leningrad to the Caucasus, with rural electrification to power their milking machines"); a slave workforce of 7.6 million by 1945 serving Krupp, IG Farben, Daimler Benz, Opel, BMW, AEG; Prinz's "staggeringly modern" postwar planning (pedestrian zones, cycle lanes). The collusion mechanism: 1929–33 hollowed out German identity (Broch's sleepwalkers); Nazism filled the nomic vacuum, securing "not just 'dominion' but extensive 'cultural hegemony' till deep into the Second World War" — Heidegger's conversion (Nazism as "the encounter of a globally determined technology with modern man") and Ley's millenarian 1924 conversion as case studies; "never collectively 'Hitler's willing executioners' in the way Goldhagen so simplistically maintains," but millions of accomplices. The biopolitical core — Nuremberg Laws fusing völkisch (Lagarde) and eugenic (Ploetz) racisms, the euthanasia vanguard, the "gardening state" — issues in the "ecology of genocide" documented at [[the-holocaust]]. "The resulting crimes against humanity were thus not incidental, but integral to Nazism's version of modernity." (Source: [[griffin-modernism-and-fascism-2007]].)

## Paxton (2011): Terror, Racial Revolution, and Economic Recovery

[[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011|Paxton and Hessler]] add figures on the pace
of consolidation and recovery. On *Gleichschaltung*: the March 1933 election gave
the NSDAP only 44% of the vote despite full state resources and SA violence in the
streets — "Germans did not vote again in contested elections until after Hitler's
defeat." Source: [[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011]].

**Terror and racial revolution**: the Gestapo grew increasingly arbitrary in its
reach; roughly 300,000 compulsory sterilizations had been carried out by 1939
under the July 1933 hereditary-health law; the April 1933 law purged Jews and
political opponents from the civil service; the Nuremberg Decrees followed in
September 1935. Kristallnacht (9–10 November 1938) — 7,500 storefronts wrecked,
over 200 synagogues burned, 91 killed, a 1-billion-mark fine levied on the
victims themselves, and 20,000 sent to concentration camps — Paxton frames
explicitly as "not yet genocide," a still-improvised pogrom rather than the
systematic extermination program that followed. Source:
[[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011]].

**Economic recovery**: unemployment fell from six million (1932) to 164,000
(1936), with labor imports needed after 1936. Hjalmar [[schacht-hjalmar|Schacht]]'s
managed economy ran deficit-financed public works, then price and wage controls,
then insulation of the mark from foreign markets; the Autobahnen program served as
cover for pre-rearmament construction capacity; military spending reached 60% of
the budget and 21% of GNP by 1938. The Four-Year Plan (1936) saw Göring displace
Schacht, pursuing autarky at prices divorced from world markets — synthetic Buna
rubber cost seven times the natural product — as deliberate war preparation, aimed
at freeing Germany from dependence on Eastern European wheat and oil imports it
instead intended to conquer. Distributionally, real wages recovered only to
1913/1929 levels and the wage share of national income fell even as output rose;
Krupp and IG Farben were the principal beneficiaries. The Volkswagen savings
scheme collected 285 million marks from ordinary Germans buying stamps toward a
"people's car" — production was diverted entirely to the army once war began in
1939. Source: [[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011]].

## Payne (1980): Zwangswirtschaft, dual state, and the revolutionary question

*Fascism: Comparison and Definition* ([[payne-fascism-comparison-definition-1980]]) reads the Nazi economy against the "capitalist domination" thesis. Hitler had no precise political economy ("Gemeinnutz geht vor Eigennutz"; he boasted "there was no need to nationalize the economy since he had nationalized the entire population"); the Wagener corporative-Ständesozialismus faction lost to Funk's big-business-friendly authoritarian Planwirtschaft by 1934 (Schweitzer's periodization: "partial fascism" 1933–36, "full fascism" with the rearmament drive). After 1936 came *Zwangswirtschaft* — subordination by controls, allocation, and cartelization under state guidance rather than formal nationalization; Payne: "the label 'capitalist domination' ought more accurately to be inverted," and war intensified this into what "might as well be termed 'military socialism.'" He cites Milward against Kühnl/Czichon: the Nazis "changed the rules of the game so that a new system was emerging." His own barb: German capitalism had more autonomy before and after Hitler — "what ultimately saved German capitalism was the defeat of National Socialism... by the Anglo-American capitalist powers." The 1938–39 resource crunch was the *effect* of subordinating the economy to war preparation, not the capitalist *cause* of war; Payne frames Nazi strategy as "Hitler's reversal of Stalin's revolutionary priorities — revolutionary conquest abroad must precede the completion of revolution at home."

Structure: a "highly peculiar and sui generis" state that "never reached maturity" — Fraenkel's dual state; in Manoilescu's comparative typology the USSR was a state run by a party, Italy a party subordinate to the state, Germany "a dual system of divided powers between party and state." Locally the party absorbed the state (gauleiters became district officials — the opposite of the Italian pattern), and by the final war years "party leadership had indeed largely taken over the home front," with the SS's parapolitical role steadily expanding; from 1943 National Socialist Guidance Officers in the army were the equivalent of Soviet commissars. On Payne's strict definition Germany "failed to achieve full totalitarianism, though in its final phase it drew nearer and nearer" — with Arendt, full Nazi totalitarianism "could have developed only after victory in the war."

Against "revolution of nihilism" and anti-modernism readings: "Hitler and his crew, however repellent, were not nihilists but held tenaciously to firm and evil values," rooted in late-19th-century biological scientism — "Nazi racism was conceivable only in the twentieth century." The Final Solution began with "not inconsiderable moderation" in the compromise phase and "accelerated ultimately as a final end in itself, an attempt to achieve a racial revolutionary goal while all else was being lost"; the "unique Nazi tactic was to modernize the process," accomplishing mass murder "more efficiently and surgically" than the century's other genocides. Payne endorses Bracher's seven revolutionary qualities of Nazism: the Führer cult of the "artist genius"; a Social Darwinist restructuring of government and society; racial revolution replacing traditional nationalism; the first state-regulated national-socialist economics; an organic status revolution toward the [[volksgemeinschaft|Volksgemeinschaft]]; a completely new racial imperialism on a world scale; and the stress on advanced technology and mass mobilization. He quotes Eugen Weber ("wars constitute the primary revolutionary processes of this century"; Hitler had "a more profound impact on the world than any other revolutionary of the twentieth century") and, at length, Jacques Ellul's verdict that interwar observers saw Nazism as "an important and authentic revolution... against the bureaucracy, against senility, in behalf of youth; against the entrenched hierarchies, against capitalism, against the petit-bourgeois mentality... for the liberation of instinct... the will to power." Payne's own social-revolution formula: "essentially one of status transformation — the identity and values of a racial Volksgemeinschaft"; trade unions were replaced by the DAF rather than syndicalism precisely because corporatism recognized the class distinctions Nazism rejected. For the five regime differences from Fascist Italy and the ten similarities to Soviet communism, see [[mussolini-benito]] and [[communism-and-fascism]].
