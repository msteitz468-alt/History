## Role

Philip V (238–179 BCE), last great king of the [[antigonid-dynasty|Antigonid line]],
acceded to Macedon in 221 at seventeen, on the death of his guardian
[[antigonus-iii-doson|Antigonus III Doson]]. CAH VII.1 closes (ch. 12, Walbank) at the
threshold of his reign — with the **[[social-war-220|Social War]]** and the **Peace of
Naupactus (217)**, the moment Macedonia and Greece were about to be drawn into the orbit of
Rome. (His later wars with Rome belong to CAH VIII.)

## The Social War and Naupactus (the close of CAH VII.1)

His accession coincided (220 BCE) with three other new rulers —
[[antiochus-iii-the-great|Antiochus III]] in Syria, Ptolemy IV in Egypt, Hannibal in Spain
— the conjunction [[polybius|Polybius]] chose to begin his *Histories* as the moment when
"the separate historical threads were about to become inextricably combined" (his
***symploke***). As *hegemon* of Doson's **Hellenic Symmachy**, Philip led the
**[[social-war-220|Social War]]** (220–217) against the [[aetolian-league|Aetolian League]],
guided at first by [[aratus-of-sicyon|Aratus]]. He proved an able, fast-moving general (the
raid on Aetolian Thermum; the descent on Sparta), purged the guardian-Friends Doson had
left him, and increasingly fell under the influence of **Demetrius of Pharos** — whose
counsel turned his ambitions westward toward Illyria and Italy.

The war was ended at the conference of **Naupactus (217)**, where the Aetolian **Agelaus**
made his famous speech warning the Greeks to compose their quarrels before the "**cloud in
the west**" — the war between Rome and Carthage — settled over Greece. The Peace restored the
*status quo*; Philip emerged with the best gains (Triphylia, Phthiotic Achaea, Zacynthos)
and stood "the darling of the Greeks" — but, just then, news of **Hannibal's victory at
Lake Trasimene** reached him, and he turned decisively toward the west.

## Counterfactual Significance

Philip is the hinge between the self-contained Hellenistic balance of power and the age of
Roman intervention. His turn toward Italy after Naupactus — encouraged by Demetrius of
Pharos — set Macedon on the road to the Macedonian Wars with Rome, "disastrous for Greece
and ultimately for his own kingdom and dynasty." At the close of CAH VII.1 that future is
only a "cloud in the west"; the volume ends on the brink of *symploke*. See
[[antigonid-dynasty]], [[social-war-220]], [[polybius]].
