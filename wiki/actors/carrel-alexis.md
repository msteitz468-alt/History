# Alexis Carrel

## Overview
Alexis Carrel was a French surgeon, biologist, and Nobel laureate who was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1912 for his work on suturing blood vessels and organ transplantation. Operating at the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research, Carrel sought to keep human organs alive outside the body for surgical repair, which led to a close scientific collaboration with Charles Lindbergh in the 1930s. He remains a highly controversial figure due to his eugenicist views and his association with the Vichy regime during World War II.

## Actions and Decisions
- **Perfusion Pump Development (1930–1935):** Partnered with Charles Lindbergh to design the perfusion pump (the "glass heart"), proving that human and animal organs could be kept viable outside the body.
- **Co-authoring *The Culture of Organs* (1938):** Published their findings detailing how thyroids, hearts, and kidneys could be perfused and kept alive for weeks.
- **Eugenicist Philosophy (*Man, the Unknown*, 1935):** Advocated for the rule of an intellectual aristocracy, selective breeding, and euthanasia for criminals, heavily influencing Lindbergh's ideas on child-rearing and civilizational decline.
