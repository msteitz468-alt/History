# David Halberstam

**David Halberstam** (1934–2007) was an American journalist and author. He served as a New York Times correspondent in Vietnam from 1962 to 1964, where his reporting on the deteriorating situation and the gap between official claims and ground reality earned him the Pulitzer Prize for International Reporting in 1964 (shared). After leaving daily journalism he wrote numerous books; his 1972 work *The Best and the Brightest* became the defining critical account of the Kennedy and Johnson administrations' path into the Vietnam War.

## Major Contribution

Halberstam's *The Best and the Brightest* provides an extended character study of the men (McNamara, McGeorge Bundy, Dean Rusk, Walt Rostow, Maxwell Taylor, and others) whose intelligence, confidence, and faith in systems analysis and American power led to incremental but fateful escalation. Drawing on his own reporting experience and extensive interviews, he shows how personal ambition, bureaucratic momentum, fear of domestic political weakness, and profound misreadings of Vietnam, China, and the limits of military power produced a war that the "best and the brightest" could not manage or win.
