# George Wythe

George Wythe (1726–1806) was a Virginia lawyer, judge, law professor, signer of the Declaration of Independence, and mentor to Thomas Jefferson. One of the most respected legal minds in colonial Virginia and a leading figure in the movement for independence and legal reform.

Wythe trained Jefferson in the law after introducing him (via William Small) to the General Court practice. Jefferson later called him "my second father" and credited him with instilling classical learning, precise reasoning, and republican principles. Wythe served in the House of Burgesses, the Continental Congress (signed the Declaration), and the Virginia House of Delegates. He was a principal revisor of Virginia's laws alongside Jefferson, Madison, and Pendleton (1776–1779), producing reforms that abolished primogeniture and entail and advanced the cause of religious freedom.

Later appointed the first professor of law at the College of William and Mary (1779), where he taught John Marshall among others. Served as Chancellor of Virginia. Died in 1806, widely believed to have been poisoned by a grandnephew; the case became a landmark in forensic testimony.
