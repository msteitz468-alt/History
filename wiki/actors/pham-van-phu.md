# Pham Van Phu

Major General Pham Van Phu (1928–1975) was commander of the Army of the Republic of Vietnam's (ARVN) II Corps, responsible for South Vietnam's Central Highlands, during the final North Vietnamese offensive of early 1975. A veteran of the French-era Vietnamese airborne forces and the earlier Indochina War (including captivity at Dien Bien Phu), he took over II Corps in late 1974.

When North Vietnamese forces struck Ban Me Thuot in March 1975 ([[events/ban-me-thuot-1975]]), catching II Corps by surprise, President [[nguyen-van-thieu]] ordered Phu to abandon the Highlands and withdraw his forces to the coast along Route 7B, a deteriorated secondary road, in an attempt to consolidate defenses in more defensible lowland territory. The retreat collapsed into a disorganized rout under North Vietnamese pressure and amid huge columns of fleeing civilians, destroying II Corps as a fighting force and precipitating the wider collapse of South Vietnamese defenses that led directly to the [[events/fall-of-saigon-1975|fall of Saigon]] weeks later.

Widely blamed for the disaster — whether for the withdrawal decision itself, its chaotic execution, or simply for carrying out Thieu's order — Phu was among the South Vietnamese commanders who took their own lives around the fall of Saigon in April 1975 rather than surrender or flee.
