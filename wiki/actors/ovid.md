# Ovid (Publius Ovidius Naso)

The last of the great **Augustan poets** (43 BCE–17/18 CE), author of the
*[[ovid-metamorphoses|Metamorphoses]]* — the 15-book epic of mythological transformations that became,
after the Bible and Virgil, perhaps the single most influential classical text in later European art and
literature.

## Life

Born at **Sulmo** in 43 BCE (the year of Cicero's death) to an equestrian family, Ovid abandoned a
public career for poetry. He became the dominant poet of Rome after the deaths of **Virgil** and
**Horace**, producing the witty erotic *Amores*, the *Heroides*, the notorious **Ars Amatoria** (The Art
of Love), the calendar-poem *Fasti*, and the *Metamorphoses*. In **8 CE** he was abruptly **exiled by
Augustus personally** to **Tomis** (modern Constanța) on the Black Sea — for, in his own cryptic phrase,
*"carmen et error,"* "a poem and a blunder" (the poem the *Ars Amatoria*; the *error* an unspecified
indiscretion touching the imperial house, never explained). Neither his eloquent exile poetry (the
*Tristia* and *Epistulae ex Ponto*) nor the accession of Tiberius won him recall; he **died at Tomis**
c. 17–18 CE.

## Significance

Ovid is the supreme poet of **myth, metamorphosis and erotic psychology**, ironic and dazzlingly
inventive where Virgil is grave. The *Metamorphoses* served as the **mythological handbook of the
West** — the channel through which Greco-Roman myth reached Dante, **Chaucer**, **Shakespeare**, and
the painters of the Renaissance and Baroque. His exile by an autocrat for his art made him a perennial
emblem of the poet at odds with power.
