# Hippias son of Pisistratus

Last Pisistratid tyrant of Athens; expelled by Spartan intervention in 510; later accompanied the Persian expedition that sacked [[places/eretria|Eretria]] and landed at Marathon (490). [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] uses Hippias in discussions of tyranny’s end, intentionality of sources, and Medism/ostracism politics.

## Expulsion, 510 BCE

After Ankhimolios’s abortive sea expedition, Spartan king Cleomenes invaded Attica by land, routed Thessalian cavalry aiding Hippias, and besieged the acropolis. Capture of Hippias’s family as hostages forced Hippias to leave within five days for Sigeum (Herodotus 5.63–65).

Athenian story that Alcmaeonidae bribed the Pythia via Delphic temple contract (marble facade after 548 fire) is weak: archon list likely shows Cleisthenes as eponymous archon 525/4, so Alcmaeonidae were not in continuous exile; bribery charge may have been turned from Cylonian curse slur into claim of liberating Athens.

Fifth-century Spartan claim of ideological *eunomia* against tyranny is best judged by later interventions: expanding into the Saronic Gulf; wanting a docile ally north of the isthmus after Pisistratid strengthening of Athens; if Hippias refused Spartan orders, Cleomenes had reason to replace him. “It is highly likely that Cleomenes wanted to ensure Sparta had a docile ally north of the isthmus.”

Herodotus and Thucydides insist tyranny ended with Spartan expulsion of Hippias in 510, not with Harmodius and Aristogiton’s 514 killing of Hipparchus — insistence that reveals widespread contrary popular belief (intentionality case in Hall Ch.2).

## Marathon and aftermath

After Eretria’s fall in 490, the Persian fleet sailed to Marathon “accompanied by the former tyrant, Hippias, who no doubt entertained hopes of being reinstalled in the city as a Persian vassal” (Hdt. 6.102). Post-Marathon ostracisms of Pisistratid/Alcmaeonid associates and Medism accusations reflect treason fear after Hippias’s presence and demotic critique of elite eastern luxury ([[concepts/invention-of-the-barbarian]]).

## Counterfactual significance

Without Hippias’s expulsion and later Medism, Athenian narratives of liberation and of Persian threat would lack their principal personal villains; Spartan intervention still fits hegemonic interest north of the isthmus regardless of later democratic ideology.
