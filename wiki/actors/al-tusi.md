# Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī

Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī (1201–1274), the foremost mathematician-astronomer of the medieval Islamic world and
a polymath of philosophy, theology, and ethics. Surviving the Mongol conquests, he secured the patronage
of the Ilkhan Hülegü and founded the great **[[maragha-school|Maragha observatory]]** (1259) — the
institutional center of the most advanced astronomy of its age.

His signature contribution to [[islamic-astronomy]] was the **[[tusi-couple|Ṭūsī couple]]**, a geometric
device generating linear motion from two circles that allowed astronomers to reform Ptolemy's models
without violating the principle of uniform circular motion. The Ṭūsī couple, transmitted westward,
reappears in the work of [[ibn-al-shatir|Ibn al-Shāṭir]] and later of
[[islamic-science-and-copernicus|Copernicus]] — a key link in Saliba's argument for the transmission of
Islamic astronomy to Renaissance Europe.
