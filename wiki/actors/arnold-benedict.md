# Benedict Arnold (1741–1801)

The most aggressive American combat commander of the early war — and its most infamous traitor.
Arnold co-led the capture of Fort Ticonderoga (May 1775), whose guns later broke the
[[siege-of-boston-1775|siege of Boston]], and led a heroic, doomed winter expedition through the
Maine wilderness against Quebec (1775). His finest hour came at **[[battles-of-saratoga-1777|
Saratoga]]**: though stripped of command after a furious feud with [[gates-horatio|Gates]], he
hurled himself into the fighting at Bemis Heights — taking a shattered leg when his horse fell on
him — and his "aggressive participation … certainly contributed to British defeat."

Embittered by perceived slights and debt, Arnold opened treasonous correspondence with the
British and, as commander of **[[west-point|West Point]]** in September 1780, plotted to betray
the fortress. The plot was exposed; Arnold fled to the British, who made him a brigadier general.
He then led the destructive 1781 raids into Virginia and the burning of New London, Connecticut.
He spent the rest of his life in obscurity in England, his name a byword for treachery.
