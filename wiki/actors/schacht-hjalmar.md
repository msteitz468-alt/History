# Hjalmar Schacht

Reichsbank President (1933–39) and Economics Minister (1934–37); the financial
engineer of early Nazi rearmament through the disguised deficit financing of
the Mefo-bill system and the "New Plan" of trade controls. Sidelined when
[[goring-hermann|Göring]]'s [[four-year-plan-1936|Four-Year Plan]] (1936)
subordinated economic orthodoxy to autarky and accelerated war preparation.
Later loosely connected to conservative resistance circles; acquitted at
Nuremberg.

## Evans (2005)

Born Horace Greeley Hjalmar Schacht (1877), a Weimar *Democrat* who stabilized
the 1923 currency, drifted right over reparations, and met Hitler at Göring's
dinner table in 1931. As Reichsbank President (from March 1933) he invented
the **Mefo-bill** system (May 1933): state-guaranteed bills from a shell
"Metallurgical Research Institute," financing ~50% of arms purchases 1934–36
(~12 billion RM by March 1938) while hiding rearmament from the budget. As
Economics Minister (1934–37) his "New Plan" (19 September 1934) built the
bilateral-barter Mitteleuropa trade bloc (South-eastern Europe's share of
German imports: 7.5% in 1928 → 22% in 1938). Judging the economy at its limits
by 1936, he lost to Göring's [[four-year-plan-1936|Four-Year Plan]], resigned
the ministry (26 November 1937), and — after the Reichsbank board's joint
warning letter of 7 January 1939 that state spending was bringing "the
finances of the state to the edge of ruin" — was sacked with his whole board
(20 January 1939), Hitler telling him he "no longer fitted into the general
National Socialist scheme of things."

Evans is unsparing on his antisemitism: Schacht "shared in full measure the
antisemitic prejudices of many bourgeois Germans," publicly defended the 1933
and 1935 antisemitic laws, banned Jewish stockbrokers (26 November 1935), and
built the Reichsbank's foreign-currency investigation apparatus that fed
Aryanization — his celebrated Königsberg criticism of street violence ("Lord,
preserve me from my friends") opposed illegality, not persecution. From 1938
loosely tied to the Goerdeler–Oster–Canaris conspiracy circle.

## The "Miracle of the Rentenmark" (Paxton & Hessler 2011)

Schacht was appointed currency commissioner by [[gustav-stresemann|Stresemann]]
on 12 November 1923, at the height of the hyperinflation crisis. His
"miracle of the Rentenmark" replaced the worthless mark with a new currency
(1 Rentenmark = 1 trillion old marks), backed not by scarce gold or
foreign-exchange reserves but by a notional mortgage on all German land,
industry, and commerce, combined with strict limits on government spending
and firm borrowing. Once [[dawes-plan-1924|Dawes Plan]] loans began flowing
in 1924, Schacht shifted Germany to a gold-based Reichsmark, stable until
the Great Depression. His deflationary discipline forced weaker firms out
of business while large, rationalized firms — leading to United Steel
(1926) and I.G. Farben (1925) — profited from the late-1920s boom. Source:
[[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011]].

## Shirer (1960) Additions

- Resigned the Reichsbank presidency in 1930 over opposition to the Young Plan; met Göring in 1930, Hitler in 1931; by 1932 was actively lobbying industrialist and banker circles to back Hitler for Chancellor. He wrote to Hitler in 1932: "I have no doubt that the present development of things can only lead to your becoming Chancellor... No matter where my work may take me in the near future, even if someday you should see me imprisoned in a fortress, you can always count on me as your loyal supporter," signed "With a vigorous 'Heil.'" Shirer notes the grim irony: Schacht was indeed later imprisoned by the regime he helped install — in a concentration camp — but as an opponent, not a "loyal supporter" (see [[german-resistance-to-nazism]]).
- 20 February 1933 secret meeting at Göring's Reichstag President's Palace: Schacht hosted roughly two dozen industrialists (Krupp von Bohlen, Bosch/Schnitzler of I.G. Farben, Vögler of United Steel) to solicit campaign funds for Hitler and Göring; Schacht testified at Nuremberg, "I collected three million marks."
- Replaced Hans Luther as Reichsbank president on 17 March 1933; later also became Minister of Economics and Plenipotentiary-General for War Economy. Shirer credits him as the single most important figure in financing Nazi rearmament, though he later broke with Hitler, lost or resigned his offices, and joined the anti-Hitler conspiracy.
- 30 September 1934 Schacht report to Hitler, "Report on the State of Work for War-Economic Mobilization," proudly noted his ministry "has been charged with the economic preparation for war." A 3 May 1935 memo states: "the accomplishment of the armament program with speed and in quantity is the [Schacht's italics] problem of German politics; everything else therefore should be subordinate to this purpose." Rearmament had to be "camouflaged completely until March 16, 1935" (the conscription announcement); financed partly via "the printing press" and confiscated Jewish/enemy-of-state assets — Schacht's own words: "our armaments are partially financed with the credits of our political enemies."
- The Mefo-bill system, additional operational detail from Shirer: totaled 12 billion marks 1935–38, kept off published budget/bank statements to preserve rearmament secrecy; Finance Minister Schwerin von Krosigk privately called them just "printing money." An Army publication, *Militaer-Wochenblatt* (22 January 1937, Schacht's 60th birthday), credited him as "the man who made the reconstruction of the Wehrmacht economically possible," growing the army from 100,000 men.
- March 1938: Schacht personally traveled to Vienna to take over the Austrian National Bank even before the Anschluss plebiscite, and on 21 March 1938 addressed its staff defending the Anschluss's brutal methods, closing with "Thank God... Adolf Hitler has created a communion of German will and German thought," administering a loyalty oath and leading a triple "Sieg Heil" — a data point cutting against Schacht's later self-presentation as an opposition-adjacent figure, and worth weighing against the "loosely tied to the Goerdeler–Oster–Canaris conspiracy circle" line already on this page from Evans.
- Winter 1939–40 resistance activity, per Shirer: Schacht's sole concrete resistance gesture in the August 1939 crisis was an aborted plan to confront Brauchitsch at Zossen and argue that war without Reichstag approval was "unconstitutional" — a plan Shirer calls "ridiculous" given the Reichstag was a rubber stamp. Schacht never went, first because Canaris warned he'd likely be arrested, then because it was "too late"; he made this episode central to his Nuremberg defense. [SHIRER-THESIS]: Shirer treats Schacht's actual resistance contribution as minimal relative to his own postwar self-presentation.
- Held in Gestapo custody in the final months of the war and liberated by U.S. troops on 4 May 1945 at Niederdorf, South Tyrol, along with Halder, Schuschnigg, Léon Blum, Schlabrendorff, and General von Falkenhausen, just as Gestapo guards were about to execute the whole group.

Source: [[shirer-rise-fall-third-reich-1960]].

## Historiography

Kopper's biography and Tooze, *The Wages of Destruction* (2006) frame the
debate over how far Schacht's system was "war financing" from the start.
