# Penelope

**Penelope**, daughter of Icarius, wife of Odysseus, mother of Telemachus. The archetype of the faithful, intelligent wife who preserves the household during her husband's long absence.

In the *Odyssey* she is besieged by 108 suitors from Ithaca and neighboring islands who consume Odysseus' wealth while she delays them for three years by weaving (and secretly unweaving) a shroud for Laertes. She is clever, cautious, and tests the returned beggar (Odysseus in disguise) with questions and the famous bed-token (the immovable bedpost carved from an olive tree).

Her mêtis parallels her husband's. The poem contrasts her fidelity with Clytemnestra (Agamemnon's wife) and explores the limits of a woman's power in a male heroic world. She is not merely passive; her choices drive much of the plot's tension and resolution.

## Significance

Penelope embodies ideals of Greek womanhood (fidelity, household management, intelligence within constraints) while subverting simple stereotypes. Later traditions and modern receptions (e.g., Atwood's *Penelopiad*) have reinterpreted her voice and agency.
