# Crazy Horse (Tȟašúŋke Witkó)

Oglala Lakota war leader renowned as one of the most skilled tacticians of the northern Plains wars against the United States. He fought at the Battle of the Rosebud (17 June 1876) against General George Crook and, days later, played a leading role in the allied Lakota-Cheyenne-Arapaho victory over Lieutenant Colonel [[custer-george-a|George Armstrong Custer]]'s 7th Cavalry at the Battle of the Little Bighorn (25 June 1876), the war's most significant Native American victory. Unlike some contemporaries he refused to be photographed and rarely signed treaties, and continued resisting after Little Bighorn until mounting military pressure and the destruction of the northern buffalo herds forced his surrender at Camp Robinson, Nebraska in May 1877. He was killed there in September 1877 in a struggle during an attempted confinement, under disputed circumstances that remain debated.

## Counterfactual Significance

Crazy Horse's tactical leadership at the Little Bighorn produced the U.S. Army's most humiliating single defeat of the Indian Wars, but the victory triggered an intensified military response that broke Lakota resistance within a year — illustrating how tactical success could accelerate, rather than avert, strategic defeat.

## Historiography

Much of what is known comes from oral Lakota testimony collected decades later (notably by Mari Sandoz and, more critically, in the collection edited by Richard G. Hardorff) rather than contemporary written records, since Crazy Horse left no personal account and avoided non-Native documentation; historians caution against over-reading later hagiographic retellings back onto the 1876–77 events.
