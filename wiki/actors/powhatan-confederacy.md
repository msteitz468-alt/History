# Powhatan Confederacy

Tsenacommacah / Powhatan “confederacy”: ~15,000–20,000 Algonquian-speakers under Powhatan, werowance of the coastal plain from Potomac south shore to the James (Salisbury, CHNPA I.1 ch. 7). Political entity destroyed by 1646; descendant communities endure in Virginia.

## Overview

### Early Jamestown (1607–18)

Despite suspicion of 1607 James River settlers, Powhatans saw friendship possibilities from English food need and copper. Capture of John Smith and “rescue” by Pocahontas was **probably an adoption ceremony** expressing alliance (Salisbury). After 1608 ceremonial “coronation,” colonists sought superiority; Natives refused corn → war. Winter 1609–10 siege nearly starved Jamestown; reinforcements enabled English to drive Natives from the James by 1611. 1614: after Pocahontas captured and converted, bitter Powhatan submitted to humiliating peace; she married John Rolfe and moved to England. People already demoralized when commercial tobacco (1618) and immigration surge brought new epidemics and land pressure.

Sixteenth-century context (Trigger & Swagerty): coastal Virginia still had few iron tools into the 1580s–1607; European goods reached the Chesapeake more via St. Lawrence–interior circuits (Susquehannock/Iroquois hatchets) than direct Atlantic trade.

### Uprisings and destruction (1622–46)

Influx of tobacco planters/laborers eroded Native autonomy. Winter 1621 new werowance **Opechancanough** endorsed coexistence policy and opened land; others rallied to shaman **Nemattanew** (“Jack of the Feathers”) as countermagic to English armor. March 1622 Englishman killed Nemattanew; Opechancanough-led surprise attack killed 350 (~one-third of colony). English war of extermination; by 1634 drove surviving Indians from lower James and York, taking ~300,000 acres. English population to 8,000 next decade; nonagenarian Opechancanough coordinated second attack (~500 English deaths); war 1644–46; captured Opechancanough murdered by a guard against Berkeley’s orders. Treaty deprived Indians of land south to Blackwater, restricted movement; **Powhatan confederacy destroyed as a political entity**.

### Later Chesapeake context

Bacon’s Rebellion (1675–76) further devastated coastal tributary Indians regardless of loyalty. Piedmont: Erie/Westo refugees from Beaver Wars repelled Virginia expulsion at the James fall line 1656, later becoming heavily armed Virginia/Carolina slave-raid clients.

## Historiography

Smith narratives and English colonial records dominate; ethnohistory re-reads Smith’s “rescue” as adoption, stresses Opechancanough’s strategy, and demography. Wood et al. *Powhatan’s Mantle* maps southeastern contact. Confederate “destruction” is political, not total biological extinction.
