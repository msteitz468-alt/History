# COICA (Coordinator of Indigenous Organizations of the Amazon Basin)

The transnational coordinating body of Amazonian indigenous organizations, founded in 1984
in Lima, linking the national federations of the nine Amazon-basin countries (including
[[cidob|CIDOB]] in Bolivia, AIDESEP in Peru, CONFENIAE in Ecuador, and Brazilian, Colombian,
and Venezuelan organizations). COICA represents Amazonian indigenous peoples in
international forums on territory, environment, and rights.

This is the concise summary; COICA recurs in the twentieth-century chapters of
[[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-2|CHNPA III Part 2]].

## Role and Significance

- **Transnational advocacy** — COICA brought Amazonian indigenous voices into global
  environmental and human-rights politics, linking territorial defense to rainforest
  conservation.
- **Self-representation** — it embodied the shift, central to the late-twentieth-century
  [[south-american-indigenous-rights-movement|indigenous-rights movement]], from being
  spoken *for* (by [[indigenismo]]) to speaking for themselves.
