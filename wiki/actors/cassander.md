## Role

Cassander (c. 355–297 BCE), son of the regent [[antipater]], made himself king of
**Macedon** and was the [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323|Successor]] most directly responsible
for **extinguishing the Argead dynasty** of [[philip-ii-of-macedon|Philip]] and
[[alexander-the-great|Alexander]]. Passed over for the regency by his own father in
favour of [[polyperchon]], he broke with Polyperchon, allied with
[[antigonus-i-monophthalmus|Antigonus]], [[ptolemy-i-soter|Ptolemy]], and
[[lysimachus]], and seized Macedon and most of Greece (319–316).

## Decisions and Significance

- **Control of Greece**: he installed the philosopher-statesman
  [[demetrius-of-phalerum|Demetrius of Phalerum]] as his governor of Athens (317–307)
  and held garrisons across Greece — until [[demetrius-i-poliorcetes|Demetrius
  Poliorcetes]] expelled him from Athens (307).
- **The end of the Argeads**: he captured and executed [[olympias]] (316) after she had
  murdered Philip III Arrhidaeus and Eurydice; held the boy-king Alexander IV as a
  hostage; married Alexander's half-sister Thessalonike (founding the city of that name);
  and, c. 310, **had Alexander IV and Roxane murdered** — and later the bastard Heracles
  too (309). By the terms of the peace of 311 the boy was to rule at his majority;
  Cassander ensured that day never came.
- **King and coalition partner**: he took the title *basileus Makedonon* (the only
  Successor to style himself specifically "king of the Macedonians"), and was a constant
  member of the coalitions against Antigonus, sharing in the victory at
  [[battle-of-ipsus-301|Ipsus]] (301), after which he held Greece and Macedon
  unchallenged until his death.

## Counterfactual Significance

Cassander's murders closed off the last possibility of a legitimist restoration and
made the spear-won kingship of the [[hellenistic-monarchy|Hellenistic monarchies]]
inevitable: with no Argead alive, "no argument could be used to challenge the rights of
the strongest." His own dynasty proved short-lived — his sons fell to civil strife and
to Demetrius — and Macedon passed, after years of chaos, to the
[[antigonid-dynasty|Antigonid line]]. See [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323]].
