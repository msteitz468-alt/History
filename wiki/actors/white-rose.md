# The White Rose (1942–1943)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: the Munich student resistance circle — Hans and Sophie Scholl, Alexander Schmorell, Christoph Probst, Willi Graf, with Professor Kurt Huber as mentor — formed from the autonomous youth-movement milieu and radicalized above all by Eastern Front atrocities several members had witnessed as army medics. Their six leaflets (hundreds to several thousand copies, with branches reaching Freiburg, Stuttgart, and Hamburg) condemned the mass murder of the Jews and the Polish elites and German apathy; after [[battle-of-stalingrad-1942|Stalingrad]] they added graffiti — "Hitler Mass Murderer," "Freedom." Hans and Sophie Scholl were caught scattering leaflets at the university on 18 February 1943 (betrayed by a porter), tried before Freisler's People's Court on 22 February with Probst, and beheaded the same day; Huber, Schmorell, and Graf followed. The Hamburg branch survived to June 1944. The sixth leaflet reached Britain via Sweden, and the RAF dropped hundreds of thousands of copies over Germany in spring 1943.

## Historiography

Postwar Germany's most honored resistance symbol precisely because the group was untainted by prior complicity — contrast the [[july-plot-1944|July Plot]] milieu. Evans stresses how explicitly and early the leaflets named the genocide.
