# Catawba

Siouan piedmont people of the Carolina interior; Salisbury stresses they **only finally consolidated as a distinct people in the first two decades of the eighteenth century**, drawn from communities shattered by disease, enslavement, and war with English and Iroquois.

## Overview

Virginia traders dealt with Siouan piedmont peoples “soon called Catawbas” alongside Westos and [[tuscarora]] in deerskin/slave circuits. After Savannah [[shawnee]] moved north (from 1707), SC armed piedmont Indians against them; Shawnees enlisted Iroquois → Catawbas became new mourning-war object after Detroit peace—**seventy years intermittent Iroquois–Catawba warfare**. Yamasee War 1715: Catawbas assassinated English traders in villages; uprising failed when Cherokees joined English; renewed English-encouraged Iroquois attacks further weakened Catawbas. Thereafter increasingly dependent on VA/SC traders but avoided captive alliance to either; used trade guns to strengthen identity, retaliating vs Iroquois and raiding southern Iroquois allies (Upper Tuscaroras, Nottoways, Meherrins). Piedmont crowded by settlers mid-century. Revolution: fully subjugated eastern groups including Catawbas supported Americans.
