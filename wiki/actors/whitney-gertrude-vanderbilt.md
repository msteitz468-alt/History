# Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney

Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney (1875–1942). Daughter of [[actors/vanderbilt-cornelius-ii|Cornelius Vanderbilt II]] and Alice; sculptor, art patron, founder of the Whitney Museum of American Art; aunt who won custody of [[actors/vanderbilt-gloria|Gloria Vanderbilt]] in the 1934 trial.

## Narrative

Cooper & Howe treat Gertrude as both cultural builder and courtroom antagonist. As an artist and patron she created an institutional legacy (the Whitney) independent of railroad operations. In 1934, after Gloria Morgan Vanderbilt's long absences in Europe and a crisis around child Gloria's health and household, Gertrude took the child in, cut the mother's allowance from the child's trust, and fought a tabloid-saturated custody trial (October–November 1934). On 21 November 1934 the court granted her sole custody, with limited maternal visitation. Gloria Morgan publicly contrasted her "very little" with Gertrude's reported $78 million.

## Historiography

Art-historical literature on the Whitney Museum is separate from tabloid custody historiography and from Gloria's later memoirs. Cooper & Howe write with family access and emotional stake; keep those layers distinct. Do not let the custody spectacle erase Gertrude's artistic career.
