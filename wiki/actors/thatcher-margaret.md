# Margaret Thatcher

British Conservative politician and Prime Minister of the United Kingdom (1979–1990), the first woman to hold the office, whose government pursued an aggressive program of privatization, trade union reform, deregulation, and monetarist anti-inflation policy — "Thatcherism" — that reshaped the postwar British economic settlement. She formed a close ideological and personal partnership with US President [[reagan-ronald|Ronald Reagan]] in confronting the Soviet Union, and led Britain through the 1982 Falklands War against Argentina.

## Role

Thatcher's domestic program dismantled much of the nationalized industrial base built up since 1945, most dramatically in the confrontation with the National Union of Mineworkers during the 1984–85 miners' strike, while her foreign policy combined firm Cold War alliance with the United States (she was an early and influential voice urging the West to engage with Mikhail Gorbachev as a genuine reformer) with resistance to further European political integration.

## Counterfactual Significance

Thatcher's willingness to back Reagan's hardline early-1980s posture toward the USSR, combined with her comparatively early recognition of Gorbachev's reformist sincerity ("a man we can do business with," 1984), is frequently cited by historians as a significant factor easing the diplomatic transition from Cold War confrontation to the negotiated end of the Cold War later in the decade.
