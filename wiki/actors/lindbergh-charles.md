# Charles A. Lindbergh

## Overview
Charles Augustus Lindbergh (nicknamed "Lucky Lindy" and "The Lone Eagle") was a pioneering American aviator, inventor, author, and activist whose solo transatlantic flight in May 1927 transformed him into a global cultural icon. Beyond this epochal flight, Lindbergh played a critical role in early commercial aviation development, scientific innovation, and military aviation operations during the Second World War. However, his leadership in the America First Committee's pre-WWII anti-interventionist campaign and his controversial comments regarding race and religion permanently divided his legacy.

## Actions and Decisions
- **Transatlantic Flight (1927):** Flew the custom-built *Spirit of St. Louis* solo nonstop from New York to Paris, winning the Orteig Prize and establishing the viability of long-distance air travel.
- **Support for Rocketry (1929):** Visited and advocated for Robert H. Goddard, securing crucial funding from the Guggenheim Foundation that enabled Goddard's liquid-fuel rocket tests in Roswell, New Mexico.
- **Scientific Innovation (1930–1935):** Collaborated with Dr. Alexis Carrel to invent the perfusion pump, a sterile glass heart-lung apparatus that kept organs alive outside the body.
- **America First Campaigning (1939–1941):** Led the isolationist movement to keep the United States out of WWII, culminating in his controversial Des Moines speech.
- **Wartime Combat Services (1944):** Flew 50 combat missions as a civilian advisor in the South Pacific, doubling the F4U Corsair bombload and optimizing P-38 Lightning fuel consumption to extend its range by 500-600 miles.
