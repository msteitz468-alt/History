# Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar

**Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar** (1891–1956), "Babasaheb", was the pre-eminent leader of India's Dalit (formerly "untouchable") communities, chief architect of the Indian Constitution, and Gandhi's most formidable critic on caste and representation.

Documented initially through broader India histories (Spear) and substantially deepened by Ramachandra Guha's [[sources/guha-gandhi-the-years-that-changed-the-world-2018|*Gandhi: The Years That Changed the World*]] (2018), especially the dedicated treatment of the Gandhi-Ambedkar relationship 1931–1937 and beyond.

## Relation to Gandhi and the freedom movement

Ambedkar insisted that political independence without the annihilation of caste would simply transfer power to a new set of oppressors. He clashed publicly with Gandhi over separate electorates for the Depressed Classes (the "Communal Award" crisis of 1932), the Poona Pact compromise, temple-entry campaigns, and the pace and sincerity of Congress efforts on untouchability. Guha reconstructs the arguments from contemporary letters, speeches, and press with unusual precision, showing both men's sincerity and the structural constraints each faced.

Ambedkar later served as Law Minister in Nehru's cabinet and chaired the Drafting Committee of the Constitution, embedding strong anti-discrimination provisions and reservations. In 1956 he converted to Buddhism with hundreds of thousands of followers, rejecting Hinduism's caste order entirely.
