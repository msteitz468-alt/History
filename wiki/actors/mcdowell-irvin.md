# Irvin McDowell

Union Army brigadier general who commanded federal forces at the First Battle of Bull Run (Manassas) on 21 July 1861, the first major land battle of the American Civil War. Under political pressure from Washington to move against the Confederate army before its enlistments expired, McDowell advanced a large but green, poorly-drilled force against Confederate positions along Bull Run; an initially promising flanking attack stalled and then collapsed into a disorganized retreat after Confederate reinforcements under Joseph E. Johnston arrived by rail and a defensive stand (earning Thomas J. Jackson his "Stonewall" nickname) held the Confederate line. The rout, watched in part by civilian spectators who had come from Washington expecting an easy Union victory, shocked Northern public opinion into recognizing the war would be long and costly.

McDowell was relieved of army command shortly afterward (replaced by George B. McClellan) but continued to serve, including a subsequent defeat at Second Bull Run in 1862 that further damaged his reputation; he spent much of the rest of his career on the West Coast, away from major field command.
