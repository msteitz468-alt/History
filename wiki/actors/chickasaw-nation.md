# Chickasaw Nation

Smaller but strategically powerful Muskogean nation of the upper Tombigbee / northern Mississippi region; English-aligned in the colonial lower Mississippi; removed west under allotment treaties and later re-separated from the [[choctaw-nation]]. Green, CHNPA I.1 ch. 8; colonial Salisbury ch. 7.

## Overview

Colonial: solidly English with Creek ties; alternately attacked and offered peace to Choctaws, Natchez, Illinois, and other French allies—principal barrier to French Louisiana expansion. English slave raids among Choctaws and weakened Illinois. Revolution: warriors participated; towns not invaded. Hopewell 1785–86: U.S. recognized prewar borders. Early national period: refused land sales until Louisiana context shifted.

## Civilization

Plow agriculture, livestock, Natchez Trace road commerce, mission schools; written law code and national police; bicultural trader-descended elites led schools, slaveholding, infrastructure.

## Removal path

Only southern nation to attend Jackson/Eaton’s **Franklin, TN, Aug 1830** talks. Demanded survey into fee-simple allotments so each family control sale of improvements and have capital for the West; right to select homeland; void if no acceptable place. Jackson/Eaton agreed; land rangers found nothing liked. **1832 Pontotoc** treaty repeated allotment on more generous terms still contingent on locating a region. Increasingly harsh Mississippi law. **January 1837:** Chickasaw leaders **purchased the right to settle among the Choctaws**. Many sold allotments at good prices, invested in slaves and livestock, moved summer/fall. Unhappy being swallowed in Choctaw Nation; **1855** amicable separation re-created independent Chickasaw Nation.

## Civil War

Virtually unanimous southern (Confederate) support (with Choctaws); 1866 reconstruction treaty gave up western lands.

## Historiography

Often paired with Choctaws in Removal literature; Green stresses allotment strategy and later separation as distinct Chickasaw path versus Choctaw mass trek mortality.
