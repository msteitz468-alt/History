# Phocas

**Phocas** (r. 602–610) was the centurion raised by a Danube army mutiny who overthrew Maurice and opened the catastrophic final Roman–Persian war under [[khosrow-ii|Khusro II]] — until [[heraclius|Heraclius]] toppled him in 610.

## Usurpation and regime (Mitchell)

Mitchell places the fall of Maurice in a context of fiscal stringency and winter campaign orders that detonated army loyalty. Phocas’s regime is remembered for urban and court terror (including the 605 purge); [[khosrow-ii|Khusro II]] used Maurice’s murder as *casus belli*, reversing the 591 settlement. Heraclius’s African-based revolt ended Phocas’s rule in 610.

## Counterfactual significance

The 602 coup is the hinge between the late sixth-century eastern residual state and the seventh-century dual crisis (Persia then Islam). Without Phocas, Maurice–Khusro II diplomacy might have deferred the 603–628 war.

## Historiography

Universally blackened by Heraclian and later sources; Mitchell retains the structural reading (army mutiny + fiscal pressure) without rehabilitating the regime. Survey: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Mitchell 2015]]; related: [[successors-of-justinian]], [[heraclius]].
