# United Nations

International organization founded in 1945 at the San Francisco Conference, replacing the [[league-of-nations|League of Nations]] as the principal forum for collective security and international cooperation after the [[world-war-ii-1939-1945|Second World War]]. Its Charter established a Security Council with five permanent, veto-wielding members (the United States, Soviet Union/Russia, United Kingdom, France, and China) alongside a General Assembly, Secretariat, and affiliated agencies (UNESCO, WHO, UNICEF, and others).

## Role

Unlike the League, the UN was designed with enforcement mechanisms and great-power buy-in intended to avoid the earlier body's fatal weaknesses, though Cold War rivalry between the US and USSR frequently paralyzed the Security Council through mutual vetoes, confining much of the organization's practical Cold War-era action to peacekeeping, decolonization oversight, and humanitarian and development work rather than collective security enforcement.

## Counterfactual Significance

Despite chronic criticism of its effectiveness, the UN's survival across the Cold War and into the post-1991 order — unlike the League's collapse before 1939 — is attributed largely to its built-in great-power veto, which sacrificed decisive collective action for institutional durability.
