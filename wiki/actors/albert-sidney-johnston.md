# Albert Sidney Johnston

Confederate general regarded at the outset of the American Civil War as the Confederacy's most capable field commander, given overall command of the western theater by Jefferson Davis. He assembled a scattered Confederate defense in Kentucky and Tennessee before Union advances under [[grant-ulysses-s|Grant]], following the fall of Forts Henry and Donelson, forced a general withdrawal into Mississippi.

## Role

Johnston organized a surprise concentration against Grant's army at Pittsburg Landing, launching the assault that opened the Battle of Shiloh (6–7 April 1862) and drove Union forces back toward the Tennessee River on the battle's first day. He was struck by a bullet behind the knee during the fighting and bled to death before the wound was recognized as mortal, becoming the highest-ranking officer killed in action on either side during the war.

## Counterfactual Significance

Confederate command passed to P. G. T. Beauregard, who halted the first day's advance short of a decisive breakthrough; many contemporaries and later historians have argued Johnston's death removed the aggressive leadership needed to press the advantage before Union reinforcements (Buell's army) arrived overnight, turning a near-victory into a Confederate defeat.
