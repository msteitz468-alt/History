# Menshevik Party
## Biography / Description
The **Menshevik Party** (originally a faction of the Russian Social Democratic Labor Party [RSDLP]) was a moderate Marxist political organization in Russia. The faction emerged in 1903 during the RSDLP split, when Julius Martov and George Plekhanov opposed Vladimir Lenin's proposal for a highly exclusive, conspiratorial party structure, advocating instead for a larger, looser mass party model. Mensheviks believed that Russia had to undergo a bourgeois-democratic development phase before it would be mature enough for a socialist revolution. The party was suppressed and banned by the Bolsheviks in 1921.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **Participation in the Provisional Government (1917):** In the wake of the February Revolution, the Mensheviks participated in the Petrograd Soviet and later joined the liberal-led Provisional Government, advocating for continued participation in World War I alongside the Allies.
- **Menshevik Republic of Georgia (1918–1921):** Following the October Revolution, Georgian Mensheviks successfully established an independent Democratic Republic of Georgia, creating a relatively stable and democratic social democratic state until it was invaded and annexed by the Red Army in 1921 (Line 5799).

## Historical Significance
The Mensheviks represented the moderate, democratic Marxist alternative to Bolshevik radicalism. Unlike Lenin, they sought to maintain humanistic standards and legal procedures, a division that Plekhanov saw as a fundamental parting of ways in modern Russian history (Line 5717). Their defeat in 1917 and final suppression in 1921 marked the end of democratic socialist options in Russia and the onset of the Bolshevik single-party monopoly.
