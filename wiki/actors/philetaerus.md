# Philetaerus

Philetaerus (343–263 BCE) was a Greek eunuch officer, originally from Paphlagonia, who served as treasurer of the citadel of Pergamon under [[lysimachus]], custodian of a vast war chest of some 9,000 talents. When Lysimachus fell at the [[battle-of-corupedium-281]] against Seleucus I, Philetaerus judged the political winds and defected, handing Pergamon's treasury and garrison to the Seleucids while retaining effective personal control of the citadel and its funds as a nominally subordinate but functionally autonomous dynast.

Over the following two decades he used the treasury to buy security — subsidizing Greek cities, ransoming prisoners from Galatian raiders, and cultivating civic goodwill — while never taking a royal title himself. He had no children (a consequence of his condition) and adopted his nephew Eumenes as heir, founding the Attalid dynasty that would rule [[pergamon|Pergamon]] as an independent Hellenistic kingdom from the 260s BCE.

His pragmatic, low-profile consolidation is a textbook case of a Successor-era treasury official converting custodianship of Diadochi wealth into durable dynastic power, without the military risk-taking that destroyed rivals like Lysimachus and Ptolemy Ceraunus.
