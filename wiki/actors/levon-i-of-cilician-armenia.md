# Levon I, King of Cilician Armenia (r. 1198/99–1219)

**Levon I** — "Levon the Magnificent," the baron **Levon II** of the Roupenid house before
his coronation — transformed the Armenian baronies of [[cilicia|Cilicia]] into a
**kingdom** recognized by the Latin West, and is the founding monarch of the
**[[cilician-armenian-kingdom|Cilician Armenian Kingdom]]** as a crowned state. He governed
first for his captive brother Roupen II, then in his own right from 1187.

## Path to the Crown

Levon understood that a royal crown required a Western patron. He won the promise of one
from Emperor **Frederick I Barbarossa** in return for supporting the [[crusades|Third
Crusade]] (1189); when Frederick drowned en route (1190), the obligation passed to his son
**Henry VI**, and the diplomacy was carried forward with Popes **Clement III** and
**Celestine III**. The crown came at an ecclesiastical price: Rome required three liturgical
conformities (universal feast-dates, public Mass, observance of the Easter-eve fast) and a
gesture of **union with the Roman Church**, sealed in the **Act of Union of 1198** (signed
by Nerses of Lampron and Archbishop John VI of Sis). On these terms Levon was
**[[coronation-of-levon-i-1199|crowned King Levon I in 1199]]** at Tarsus, jointly by the
Armenian patriarch **Grigor VI Pahlavuni** and **Conrad of Wittelsbach**, archbishop of
Mainz, severing the kingdom's Byzantine connection. In 1199 **Pope Innocent III** sent him
the banner of St Peter in recognition.

## Reign and Methods

Levon built a **unified Christian state**, styling himself (1210) "Leo, by the grace of God
and of the Roman Empire, king of Armenia." He grounded its wealth in **transit trade**,
granting charters to **Genoa, Venice, and Pisa** and developing the port of **Ayas** as the
Mediterranean terminus of the routes to Persia, India, and China. He instituted the
kingdom's **multilingual chancery**. His abiding ambition was **Antioch**: he seized Baghras
(1194), captured Bohemond III by deceit, and after years of war installed his grandnephew
**Raymond-Roupen** in Antioch in **1216** (with Hospitaller help), against the rival
Bohemond IV of Tripoli — a hold that collapsed at Levon's death.

The **union with Rome** he had promised never materialized in substance — a recurring
pattern [[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian]] reads as Armenian church
policy being **political rather than doctrinal** — and Levon was excommunicated by Innocent
III in 1211 over the Antioch dispute. He died in **May 1219** leaving no son; his daughter
**Zabel (Isabel)** succeeded as queen, and her marriage to **[[hetum-i-of-cilician-armenia|Hetum
I]]** united the Roupenid and Hetumid lines.

## Counterfactual Significance

Levon converted a survival strategy — playing the Latins against Byzantium — into permanent
statehood. Without his crown, Cilician Armenia might have remained a cluster of contested
baronies absorbed by Byzantium or the Seljuks; with it, the Armenians gained a recognized
monarchy and a Mediterranean commercial base, but also locked themselves into the **union
question** with Rome that would divide Armenian society for the next two centuries (see
[[armenian-church-union-question|the union controversy]]).

## Historiography

Reported by the Armenian chroniclers (Vahram, Kirakos of Gandzak) and the Latin
chroniclers of Outremer; synthesized in [[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian
(2000)]], whose account is sympathetic and emphasizes the kingdom's role in the crusading
world.
