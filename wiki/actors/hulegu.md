# Hülegü

**Hülegü** (Hulagu; Weatherford: Hulegu), son of Tolui and [[sorghaghtani-beki|Sorghaghtani Beki]]
and grandson of **[[genghis-khan|Chinggis Khan]]**, founded the **[[ilkhanate|Ilkhanate]]** through
Möngke's Middle Eastern campaign: destruction of the Ismaili fortresses, the
**[[hubs/warfare/high-middle-ages/mongol-conquests/siege-of-baghdad-1258|sack of Baghdad (1258)]]**,
and the brief Mongol occupation of Syria before his withdrawal on Möngke's death left
**[[kitbuqa|Kitbuqa]]** overextended at Ain Jalut. [[may-mongol-art-of-war-2016|May]] stresses his
leisurely advance with pasture management and the engineering-heavy siege of Baghdad.

## Weatherford (2004) — Army of the Right and campaign style

At the spring 1253 *khuriltai* [[mongke-khan|Möngke]] assigned Hülegü (Weatherford: "the brother
with the best military training") the **Army of the Right** against Baghdad, Damascus, and Cairo,
while [[kublai-khan|Khubilai]] took the Army of the Left against the Song; both headed out May
1253. [[weatherford-genghis-khan-making-modern-world-2004|Weatherford]] contrasts Hülegü with
Genghis's lightning columns: "deliberately advanced more slowly and ostentatiously," with a larger
Chinese engineering corps expanded by European craftsmen, medical corps, scribes, and supply carts
of wheat, rice, and wine; winter campaigning after summer fattening of horses; vanguard clearing
herds and negotiating.

**Assassins.** Pretext: Grand Master's Karakorum delegation suspected of designs on Möngke; Imam
surrendered Alamut 19 November 1256 (first anniversary of his power), was paraded to order other
fortresses to yield, then stomped to death near Karakorum after Möngke refused an audience.

**Baghdad.** Legal grievance: Caliph had failed to assist against the Ismailis despite earlier
allegiance to Genghis. November 1257 march with Armenian, Georgian, and Turkic vassals; by late
January 1258 encirclement. Secret ties to Baghdad Christians (mother and two wives Christian; many
of his men Christian) fed hopes of freedom from Muslim domination; Caliph's late offer of
submission and Friday prayer in the Great Khan's name was refused. After the sack, Hülegü's realm
from Afghanistan to Turkey became the **Ilkhanate** ("vassal empire"); Weatherford claims he
controlled richer lands and cities than the rest of the empire combined and took some Azerbaijan
pastures of the Russian cousins — seed of the later Berke–Hülegü feud. Neither he nor the Golden
Horde returned for election after Möngke's death. Mamluk victory at Ain Jalut (3 September 1260)
fixed the western border of the empire.
