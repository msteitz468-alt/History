# Babak al-Khurrami (Babek)

Babak (Arabic form of Iranian Pāpak), chief of the Khurrami sect. Said to be the son of an itinerant oil-merchant. Rose to prominence after the death of Djawidhan b. Sahl, chief of the Khurramis, whom he claimed had his spirit enter him. Began stirring up the population of the district of al-Badhdh in Arran (201 AH = 816-817 CE).

In 204 AH (819-820) Yahya b. Mu'adh attacked him without success. In the reign of al-Mu'tasim, after the advance guard under Bogha the Elder was defeated at Heshtad-Ser in the mountains of Maragha, Afshin prepared the expedition (221 AH = 836 CE). Afshin used mountain troops with signals and chevaux-de-frise (hasak) to advance cautiously. al-Badhdh was captured and plundered on Friday 18 Ramadan 222 AH (= 26 April 837). The town was demolished by engineers. Babak fled but was seized by Sahl b. Sonbat (Armenian Patriarch) while hunting and handed to Afshin. Sent to Samarra (Thursday 2 Safar 223 AH = 3 January 838).

**Bibliography notes from source**: Detailed in Arab chronicles (Tabari etc.).

## Historiography note (EI1)
EI1 presents the standard Arabic narrative of the Khurrami revolt as a politico-religious uprising against Abbasid authority in the Caspian/Arrani borderlands, with Afshin's campaign as a model of methodical mountain warfare.
