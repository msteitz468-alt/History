# Yaghi Siyan (Governor of Antioch)

Seljuq Turkish governor (amir) of Antioch, appointed by Sultan Malik Shah in 1086–7 as part of the post-Philaretus settlement in northern Syria. He held the city during the nine-month siege by the First Crusade (October 1097 – June 1098).

## Role in the Siege of Antioch

Yaghi Siyan governed a major fortified city on the strategic route from Anatolia into Syria and the Levant. His position was complicated by the broader Seljuq succession crisis after Malik Shah's death (1092) and the rivalry between Ridwan of Aleppo and Duqaq of Damascus. He initially sought to hold and wait, relying on the city's immense walls and the expectation that a relief army would arrive. The crusader blockade was porous (the circuit too long to seal completely, especially the mountain approaches), allowing some messengers and limited supply to reach the city.

Yaghi Siyan was killed during the crusader capture of the city by treachery on the night of 2/3 June 1098. The fall reversed the strategic situation: the besiegers became the besieged inside the walls when Kerbogha of Mosul's relief army arrived shortly afterward.

## Historiography / Sources

Portrayed in Latin chronicles (Gesta Francorum, Raymond of Aguilers, Albert of Aachen) primarily as the defender whose city fell to a combination of starvation pressure on the besiegers and internal betrayal. Islamic sources (e.g., Ibn al-Athir) place him in the context of the fragmented post-Malik Shah politics of Syria. France (*Victory in the East*) stresses that despite political disunity, the defence of Antioch was serious and prolonged (nine months, multiple relief attempts).
