# Tassilo III, Duke of Bavaria (c. 741 – after 794)

**Tassilo III** was the last autonomous Agilolfing duke of Bavaria, deposed by
[[charlemagne|Charles the Great]] in 788 in the annexation that absorbed the last
semi-independent Frankish duchy. Married to Liutperga, a daughter of the Lombard king
[[desiderius]], he was — like Charles himself before 771 — a son-in-law of Desiderius.

## The Engineered Fall (787–8)

[[sources/nelson-king-and-emperor-2019|Janet Nelson]] reads Tassilo's overthrow as a
**systematically planned and ruthless operation**, not the just punishment of a serial
oath-breaker that the official record claims:

- Tassilo submitted at the **Lechfeld on 5 October 787** (a date preserved only in the
  Chesne Fragment), returning Bavaria "with a staff … carved [with] the likeness of a man"
  and becoming Charles's *vassus*. The three converging Frankish armies massed at
  symbolically chosen sites — the Lechfeld (where Pippin had beaten Tassilo's father Odilo
  in 743), Pförring, and Bolzano.
- A letter discovered by Bernhard Bischoff shows Charles's agents **securing the duke's
  family and treasure *before* the formal arrest** in 788 ("*mando, non peto*" — "an order,
  not a request"). The *Annales Nazariani* describe Liutperga, the children, and the treasury
  seized first; Tassilo then disarmed and begging not to be tonsured publicly "because of the
  shame and disgrace." He was tonsured privately at St-Goar and exiled to Jumièges.
- The **788 Ingelheim trial** rested on a historic *herisliz* charge (army desertion in 763)
  and a topical charge of colluding with the [[avars|Avars]] — though "no Frankish or
  Bavarian source mentions an Avar threat before 788." Nelson calls the *Annales regni
  Francorum* version a "retrospective construction," a "shadow-biography of Tassilo as
  'Charlemagne's significant other'."
- The **794 Frankfurt** re-staging of the case Nelson reads not as a "show trial" but as a
  quasi-penitential synodal ceremony; the contemporary Irish court poem is "the only source
  that leaves open the possibility that Tassilo, still, saw alternative options" in 787.
