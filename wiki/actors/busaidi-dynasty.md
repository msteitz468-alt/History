# Busaidi Dynasty

## Overview
The Busaidi dynasty rose to power in Oman in 1749 and built an Indian Ocean empire centered on maritime trade. Under Seyyid Said it shifted focus to East Africa, developing clove plantations and relying on Indian (Gujarati/Khoja) merchant capital while serving as a proxy for British interests.

## Major Developments
- 1798 treaty granted Britain rights at Masqat and excluded French/Dutch.
- Seyyid Said (r. 1804–1856) visited East Africa from 1826; defeated Mazrui at Mombasa (1837); moved capital permanently to Zanzibar (1840).
- Clove trees introduced ~1819; plantations on Zanzibar and Pemba used slave labor, making Zanzibar a major colonial primary crop producer.
- Customs farming awarded to Indian merchants (Shivji family dominant at Zanzibar into 1880s).

## Geographic Scope and Interaction
Core in Oman (Masqat) and Zanzibar/Pemba; extended influence along East African coast north of Portuguese territories, Gulf, and western Indian Ocean. Interacted with British antislavery treaties (1822, 1873), Indian capital networks, and local African societies via caravan trade.
