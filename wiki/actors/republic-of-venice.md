# The Republic of Venice (Origins)

## Overview

**Venice** emerged in the eighth and ninth centuries from the lagoon settlements of the old
Byzantine province of **Venetiae**, exploiting its amphibious position on the frontier between the
eastern and western empires to become an autonomous, commercially oriented maritime state. In
[[mckitterick-ncmh-v2-1995|NCMH II]] (Brown, Ch. 13) Venice's early history exemplifies the
transformation of [[exarchate-of-ravenna|Byzantine Italy]] into new political forms.

## Between Two Empires

The lagoon communities, refuges from earlier Lombard and other incursions, were governed by a local
**doge** (*dux*) — traditionally from c. 697 or c. 715 — under loose Byzantine suzerainty. The decisive
test came with Frankish expansion: **Charlemagne** sought to bring Venice into his orbit (recognising it
briefly as a Frankish fief under his son Pippin), but the **Frankish-Byzantine peace of 812** confirmed
Venice within the **Byzantine** sphere while leaving it effectively self-governing. The ducal seat was
consolidated at **Rialto** under the doge **Agnello Particiaco** (Partecipazio), and the wills of the
period (e.g. that of the doge) already attest the wealth and sophistication of Venice's commercial role.
The Venetians also pursued **ecclesiastical independence** to match their political autonomy — symbolised
by the later translation of the relics of St Mark.

## Significance

Venice's early choice — Byzantine allegiance in name, practical autonomy in fact, and commerce as the
basis of the state — set the pattern for its extraordinary later career as the dominant maritime power of
the medieval and early modern Mediterranean.

## Historiography

Early Venetian history is obscured by later, highly mythologising Venetian tradition (the foundational
narratives of flight from the barbarians, the antiquity of the dogeship). NCMH II (Brown) reconstructs the
ninth-century reality from Frankish and Byzantine diplomatic records, the chronicle of John the Deacon, and
charters, stressing Venice's emergence *out of* the Byzantine provincial system rather than as an
independent foundation.
