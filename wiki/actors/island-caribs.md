# Island Caribs (Kalinago)

## Overview

The **Island Caribs** (Kalinago) were the indigenous peoples whom Europeans encountered in the
**Lesser Antilles** at contact, distinguished by the Spanish from the [[taino|Taíno]] of the
Greater Antilles and notoriously labeled **"Caribs" / cannibals** — the word from which both
"cannibal" and "Caribbean" derive. **Neil Whitehead's** chapter on the invaded Caribbean and
**Louis Allaire's** archaeology chapter in
[[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-1|Volume III]] treat the Carib–Taíno
distinction critically: the "Carib" label was as much a **colonial construction** — a legal
designation marking peoples who could be enslaved as resisters — as a clear ethnographic
reality.

Archaeologically the Lesser Antillean sequence (Saladoid → Troumassoid → island Carib) does not
map neatly onto a simple "Carib invasion of peaceful Taíno," and the volume stresses the
**fluidity and colonial manufacture** of Caribbean ethnic categories.

## Significance

The Island Caribs are a prime example of the volume's argument that **European categories
distorted indigenous realities**: the cannibal/Carib label justified enslavement and conquest
and has shaped the historiography ever since. They also illustrate the catastrophic post-1492
collapse of Caribbean native societies.
