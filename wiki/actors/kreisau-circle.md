# The Kreisau Circle

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: the loose planning network (100+ members; the Gestapo coined the name) around Moltke's Silesian estate, meeting in three larger conferences 1942–43. Its agreed postwar principles (9 August 1943) mixed Christian moral regeneration, restored basic freedoms, and internationalism with radical-conservative anti-liberalism: a weak-centre federal state of self-governing districts, voting age 27, suspicion of both Weimar parliamentarism and capitalism (common ownership of vital industries), even guaranteed family gardens against urbanism — Stein-reform nostalgia more than democracy. Moltke opposed assassination on religious grounds, preferring to await collapse — a standing friction with the Goerdeler/military conspirators (the 8 January 1943 joint meeting failed: Moltke thought Goerdeler "reactionary"; Hassell thought the Kreisauers unrealistic "youngsters"). Moltke had written to his wife as early as August 1941 that the eastern murders were "burdening the German people with a blood-guilt that can never be expunged in our lifetime." Arrested January 1944, before the plot; tried and executed January 1945.

**Complicity caveat** (recorded per the contradiction flag, without adjudication): some members and allies of the wider resistance milieu — Schulenburg (forced-labor and deportation policy in Silesia), Stülpnagel (reprisals and deportations in Paris), Helldorf (persecution of Berlin's Jews), Nebe (Einsatzgruppe B commander) — were themselves implementers or perpetrators of persecution before turning against Hitler; Goerdeler's 1942 postwar memorandum still proposed racial classification of surviving Jews, though by 1944 he condemned the persecution as "the most inhuman, merciless and deeply shaming."
