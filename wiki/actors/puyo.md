# Puyo (Buyeo)

**Puyo** was the **earliest state in the northeast**, in the central Manchurian Basin, emerging during
the Early [[han-dynasty|Han]] from the earlier Xituanshan bronze culture from the 4th century BC. In
[[barnes-archaeology-east-asia-2015|Barnes's]] account it is the northern node of the
[[yellow-sea-interaction-sphere|Yellow Sea sphere]] and the **claimed ancestor of both
[[koguryo|Koguryo]] and [[paekche|Paekche]]**, whose ruling lineages traced descent from Puyo as a
charter of legitimacy.

## A Sedentary, Horse-Wealthy State

Puyo was **not nomadic**: millet and soybean farming continued, but the pig of the earlier Xituanshan
culture was displaced in importance by the **horse**, alongside cattle, sheep and goats. Its growth was
enabled by proximity to Yan's port of trade — the Erlonghu walled site, 160 km north of Yan's long wall.
Iron tools from Yan and Han (hoes, adzes, chisels, sickles, awls), "buried primarily with females,"
generated the surplus that supported an elite, while horse, chariot and horse-riding armour transformed
war and transport.

## Capital and Cemeteries

The ostensible capital was the **Dongtuanshan** complex at Jilin City — three ring walls plus two outer
walls following the terrain in a roughly **circular** plan, unlike the square/rectangular Mainland
cities. The two major cemeteries, **Maoershan** (earlier) and **Laoheshen** (later), held rich burials:
iron double-edged straight swords, horse gear, armour, jade/gold/silver ornaments, Han coins and
mirrors, belt hooks, and nomadic gilt-bronze plaques and cauldrons. Laoheshen shows extreme
stratification — two tombs with >200 artifacts, 25 with 50–200, and 101 with fewer than 50.

## Decline

During Wang Mang's Xin Dynasty (9–23 AD), both Puyo and Koguryo received Han titles and objects while
being recruited for border conflicts — and Han set Puyo against the rebellious Koguryo. Puyo was
**attacked and destroyed in 286 by the Xianbei Murong**, its people taking refuge in the Korean
Peninsula; a rump survived until 494. Its founder-myth lived on as the legitimating ancestry of Koguryo
and Paekche.
