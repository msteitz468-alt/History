# Tigranes II "the Great," King of Kings

The Armenian monarch who built — and lost — the **largest empire of the late
Hellenistic Near East**, stretching from the Caucasus to Syria, before it was
dismembered by **Lucullus** and **[[pompey|Pompey]]**. Treated in
[[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]] Ch. 8a (Sherwin-White).

## Role and career

Tigranes came to the Armenian throne c. 96 as a **Parthian vassal**, but exploited
Parthian dynastic weakness to annex **Sophene, Gordyene, Mesopotamia, Adiabene, and
Media Atropatene**, and c. 82 expelled the last Seleucids from **northern Syria and
lowland Cilicia**, taking the title **"King of Kings."** He married into the house of
**[[mithridates-vi|Mithridates]]** and founded a new southern capital,
**Tigranocerta**, peopled by deportees from a dozen Greek cities.

When the defeated Mithridates fled to him (71), Tigranes' refusal to surrender his
father-in-law gave **Lucullus** his pretext: the Roman crossed the Euphrates and
**routed Tigranes' great army at Tigranocerta (69)**, where the Armenian had mocked
the small Roman force as "too large for an embassy, too small for an army." His empire
began to dissolve. In **66** he submitted to **Pompey**, laying his diadem at the
imperator's feet; Pompey, in a calculated act of clemency, **restored him his core
kingdom as a "friend and ally of the Roman people"** but stripped away every
conquest, even handing Sophene to his disloyal son.

## Counterfactual / structural significance

Tigranes' empire is the measure of the **vacuum left by Seleucid collapse** — and of
how readily Rome stepped into it. By preserving him as a dependent buffer-kingdom
rather than annexing Armenia, Pompey established the pattern of **client monarchy**
on the eastern frontier that framed Roman–Parthian relations for a century, with
Armenia the perennial bone of contention.

## Historiography

Plutarch (*Lucullus*, *Pompey*), Appian, Dio, and **Strabo**. The chronology of his
reign and the **site of Tigranocerta** remain debated (Sherwin-White).
