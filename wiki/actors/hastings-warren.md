# Warren Hastings

**Warren Hastings** (1732–1818) was the first **Governor-General** of British India
(Governor of Bengal 1772, Governor-General 1774–85). Where [[clive-robert|Clive]]
won the [[company-state|Company-state]] its territory, Hastings gave it
**administrative shape** — turning a plundering trading body into a governing one —
and then became the defendant in the great impeachment that defined the moral terms
of empire.

## Role and Key Decisions

- **Creating "British India."** [[roy-east-india-company-2016|Roy]] credits Hastings
  with "a new model that could be called British India, one dominated by bureaucrats
  largely recruited from among the elites of India rather than merchants." His
  guiding tenet was that Bengal "was a British province" needing officers who learned
  "Persian, Sanskrit and Bengali, and [ruled] by means of Indian traditional law" —
  the root of [[colonial-knowledge|colonial knowledge production]]. In Spear's
  phrase, he deposed the Indian revenue deputies for a Board of Revenue and English
  district collectors, "the real beginning of British administration in India."
- **The Regulating Act (1773).** Passed amid the Company's near-bankruptcy and the
  Clive scandals, it raised administrators' salaries while **prohibiting their private
  trade** and created a Supreme Court at Calcutta — the first systematic attempt to
  separate the Company's commercial and sovereign functions (the very confusion Adam
  Smith condemned).
- **Patronage and its abuses.** Hastings tolerated loyal zamindars: his protégé
  Kantobabu acquired estates "using fraudulent means," and the terror of the loyal
  zamindar Devi Singh sparked the **Rangpur peasant rebellion**.

## The Nandakumar Affair and Impeachment

When the official **Nandakumar** accused Hastings of bribery (1775), he was
counter-charged with forgery, tried before Chief Justice **Elijah Impey** —
Hastings's old schoolfellow — and hanged; to ordinary Bengalis the case symbolized
"European despotism." On his return to Britain, **Edmund Burke** led his
**impeachment** (trial 1788; acquittal c. 1795). Though acquitted, the trial
established, in Roy's reading, that "governance in India could not be left to the
discretion of a group of opportunistic men driven by the lure of making quick money"
— "with this episode… the British empire in India began."

## Counterfactual Significance

Hastings is the hinge between the **trader-plunderer** and the **administered
colonial state**. Without his insistence on professional, law-bound administration —
and without the public reckoning of his impeachment — the transition from
[[english-east-india-company|Company]] plunder to a regularized colonial bureaucracy
might have come later or taken a more nakedly extractive form. His career thus bears
directly on the [[drain-of-wealth-colonial-india|drain debate]]: he both presided
over Bengal's fiscal exploitation and built the apparatus that later contemporaries
used to constrain it.
