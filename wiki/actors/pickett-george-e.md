# George E. Pickett

Confederate major general whose name is permanently attached to "Pickett's Charge," the massed infantry assault against the Union center on the third day of the Battle of Gettysburg (3 July 1863), ordered by [[actors/robert-e-lee|Robert E. Lee]] as the climax of the Confederate invasion of Pennsylvania. Pickett's division formed roughly a third of the approximately 12,500 attacking troops (alongside divisions under Pettigrew and Trimble) that crossed nearly three-quarters of a mile of open ground under artillery and rifle fire to strike the Union line at the "copse of trees" and "angle" — briefly breaching it before being repulsed with catastrophic losses, over half the attacking force killed, wounded, or captured. The charge's failure effectively ended Lee's Gettysburg campaign and, combined with the fall of Vicksburg the same week, marked a decisive turning point against the Confederacy.

Pickett himself survived the charge (having remained largely behind the advancing lines per normal division-command practice) but never fully recovered his prewar reputation; his relationship with Lee cooled afterward, and his later war record, including a further defeat at Five Forks in 1865, did little to restore it. He is remembered almost entirely through the lens of the charge that bears his name.
