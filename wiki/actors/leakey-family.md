# The Leakey Family

The **Leakeys** were a family of paleoanthropologists whose fieldwork in East
Africa, spanning the mid-20th century, did more than any other single program to
establish the **African origin and antiquity of humankind**. Their discoveries at
Olduvai Gorge and Laetoli supplied the fossil and footprint evidence for early
bipedalism and toolmaking that anchors the wiki's [[periods/deep-prehistory|Deep
Prehistory]] and [[periods/early-prehistory|Early Prehistory]] accounts.

## Louis Leakey (1903–1972)

Born in a Kikuyu village near Nairobi to British missionary parents, Louis grew
up speaking Kikuyu (he said he dreamed in it), wrote its definitive grammar, and
was initiated into Kikuyu manhood. Educated at Cambridge (Firsts in Languages and
in Archaeology & Anthropology, 1926), he became convinced early that humans had
originated in Africa — against the then-prevailing focus on Asia and Europe — and
led East African expeditions from 1926 onward. A charismatic showman and
publicist, he was also the mentor who launched the primate field studies of Jane
Goodall, Dian Fossey, and Biruté Galdikas. His flair for publicity contrasted
with, and was often underwritten by, Mary's rigor.

## Mary Leakey (1913–1996)

Mary Douglas Nicol, an expelled-from-school but gifted artist and archaeologist,
married Louis and became the program's scientific anchor. The *Times* obituary
judged that "for every vivid claim made by Louis ... the supporting evidence
tended to come from Mary." Her discoveries include the ~18-million-year-old
Miocene ape *Proconsul africanus*; the 1.8-million-year-old *Zinjanthropus*
(*Australopithecus*/*Paranthropus*) *boisei*; the Oldowan tool assemblages of the
"Zinj floor"; and — her most celebrated find — the **Laetoli footprints**.

## Olduvai Gorge and Laetoli

**Olduvai Gorge**, a 300-foot-deep ravine in the Serengeti (discovered in 1911 by
the butterfly-chasing entomologist Wilhelm Kattwinkel), was the Leakeys' principal
site for three decades, yielding thousands of stone tools and fossils under
hard field conditions. At nearby **Laetoli** in 1978, Mary's team excavated an
89-foot trail of hominin footprints preserved in volcanic ash ~3.6 million years
ago — made by upright walkers (associated with *Australopithecus afarensis*,
"Lucy's" species) whose feet, with aligned big toes, were nearly modern. "Now
this is really something to put on the mantelpiece," Mary is said to have
remarked. The prints are among the clearest evidence of early **bipedalism**,
predating large brains and confirming that upright walking came first.

## The Wider Legacy

Louis named and championed *Homo habilis* ("handy man") as the first toolmaker
and an ancestor of later humans. The couple separated in his last years; their son
**Richard Leakey** continued the work, discovering skulls of *Homo habilis*,
*Homo erectus*, and *Australopithecus aethiopicus* (and later became a prominent
conservationist). The family's cumulative achievement was to shift the search for
human origins decisively to Africa and to extend the documented hominin record
into the Pliocene.

## Counterfactual Significance

The African-origins thesis would likely have prevailed without the Leakeys — the
South African Taung Child (Raymond Dart, 1924) already pointed there — but their
sustained Rift Valley program produced the dated, abundant evidence that made it
unanswerable decades sooner, and trained the field (and the public) to look east
and south in Africa. Their privileging of fieldwork and dramatic single finds also
shaped the discipline's competitive, discovery-driven culture, for good and ill
(named "species" on fragmentary remains; the Dmanisi finds later suggested some
such splits were over-fine).
