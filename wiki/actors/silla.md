# Silla

**Silla** was the southeastern kingdom of the [[korea-three-kingdoms|Korean Three Kingdoms]] that
ultimately **unified the Peninsula** (660–668) in alliance with Tang and ruled as United Silla until
935. It is famous archaeologically for the **gold** of its un-lootable royal tombs.

## Origins

Silla grew, by tradition, from a small **Chinhan** polity called **Saro** (the name changing to Silla in
the late 4th/early 5th century), in the iron-rich Yongnam region it shared with the [[kaya|Kaya]]
chiefdoms (see [[samhan|Samhan]]). Archaeologically Yongnam was a common culture; **Silla and Kaya
ceramics diverged at the end of the 3rd century**, perhaps amid warfare. The sudden appearance of large
**wood-chamber tombs and gold** in Silla territory may reflect new ruling clans — possibly Lelang
refugees after [[koguryo|Koguryo]] destroyed [[lelang-commandery|Lelang]] in 313. The **Kim** clan name
and the capital **Kumsong** both mean "gold."

## Tombs and Gold

Silla's distinctive tomb — a wooden chamber and coffin mounded **first with cobbles, then earth** —
made looting nearly impossible, preserving its treasures; the form fuses a Han wood-chamber with the
Koguryo cobble-mound. Silla gold-working manipulates **sheet gold and gold wire**: openwork, repoussé,
twisted-wire chains hung with spangles. **Five tree-and-antler gold crowns** have been excavated in the
royal cemetery (earlier crowns silver, later bronze); Kaya and Yamato elite imitated them in gilt
bronze and silvered iron. Earrings could incorporate **curved jadeite beads whose only source was the
Japanese Islands** — a marker of the [[yellow-sea-interaction-sphere|sphere's]] reach.

## Unification and After

Silla took coastal **Pon-Kaya in 532** and conquered **Tae-Kaya in 562**, bringing it against Paekche.
Allying with **Tang**, it conquered **Paekche (660)** and **Koguryo (668)**. As **United Silla** it
replaced the native title *maripkan* with Chinese *wang*, organised nine provinces with provincial and
regional capitals, established a **Confucian Academy (682)**, and built **Sokkuram (751)** — a Buddhist
sanctuary constructed like a corridor-chamber mounded tomb on the eastern border, facing the sea
"as protection from marauding pirates from the Japanese Islands." Its distinctly Korean **bone-rank
(kolpum)** system ranked descent groups while accommodating Chinese, Paekche and Koguryo immigrants.
