# Church of the East

The **Church of the East** (often polemically “Nestorian”) was the principal Christian church of the Sasanian and then early Islamic East — Mesopotamia and beyond — dyophysite in christology and institutionally independent of the Roman imperial church. It forms the third major pole, with Chalcedonians and [[miaphysitism|Miaphysites]], of the post-Chalcedonian Christian map of the Middle East.

## Structure and identity

- Dyophysite tradition linked historically to Theodore of Mopsuestia and the school of Nisibis/Edessa; rejected by Ephesus (431) in the imperial church’s telling.
- Self-governing catholicosate (Seleucia-Ctesiphon and successors).
- Missionary reach into Central Asia, India, and China (beyond this page’s core focus).

## In Tannous’s frame

One of the competing Christian “mansions” ordinary people might belong to without deep doctrinal grasp; part of the plural Christian world that early Muslims encountered and that [[islamization-of-the-middle-east|Islamization]] gradually transformed.
