# Albert Kesselring (1885–1960)

German field marshal. Commander-in-Chief South (Oberbefehlshaber Süd). Theater commander responsible for the defense of Sicily and southern Italy.

Kesselring recognized early that Sicily could not be held indefinitely with the forces available. He supported Hube's decision to conduct a fighting withdrawal and the subsequent evacuation across the Strait of Messina. Post-war he argued that an immediate Allied follow-up after Tunisia could have succeeded easily, and that the Allies' failure to cut the evacuation routes was a major error.

## Sicily (D'Este)

- Overall Axis theater commander.
- Reinforced Sicily with German mobile units (including the Hermann Göring Division and 15th Panzer Grenadier) when Italian collapse became likely.
- Approved the plan for deliberate evacuation ("Lehrgang") that saved the German corps as a fighting force.
- Later criticized the Allies for failing to follow up the Tunisian victory immediately or to block the Strait effectively.

Kesselring remained a formidable opponent in Italy, conducting a series of delaying actions that made the Allied advance extremely costly.
