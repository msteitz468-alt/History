# Catullus (Gaius Valerius Catullus)

Late Republican lyric and short-poem master from **Verona** (Cisalpine Gaul), centre of the so-called **Neoteric** movement that prized Callimachean precision over long annalistic epic.

## Role

Son of a leading municipal family with an estate at Sirmione; wrote highly personal short poems (hendecasyllables and other metres) on elite social life — a Roman individuality beyond Hellenistic epigram. He lampooned [[julius-caesar]] and his chief-of-staff, then, after apology, dined with Caesar (Caesar “was not amused but knew the rules of the genre”). The **Lesbia** poems address **Clodia** (sister of Clodius; probably wife of Metellus Celer, cos. 60): a real-relationship cycle from passion (“I hate and yet love”) to disillusionment that Nisbet (OHCW) calls unprecedented in Greek literature. The **Neoteric** movement (Callimachean roots; stimulated by Parthenius, brought to Rome c.65) prized short, learned, precious mythological poems against long annalistic *Annals*; Cinna’s *Zmyrna* took nine years — Catullus 95 praises it vs Volusius’ disposable *Annals*. Poem **64** (*Peleus and Thetis*, ~400 hexameters) is his ambitious epyllion; Ariadne’s abandonment foreshadows [[virgil]]’s Dido. Nisbet’s synthesis: Lucretius and Catullus share Republican candour and elegant directness; Catullus echoes Lucretius, but Neoteric art-for-art’s-sake risked “forgetting the nature of things.”

## Significance

Catullus made private emotion and learned short form central to Latin poetry’s self-image, clearing ground for Augustan lyric and elegy ([[horace]], Propertius, Tibullus, [[ovid]]). Griffin notes his sketch-book of incidents and people can be paralleled in antiquity only in [[cicero]]’s letters.

## Historiography

OHCW (Nisbet) is a literary-historical essay, not a full commentary; Clodia identification is traditional and generally accepted with caveats.
