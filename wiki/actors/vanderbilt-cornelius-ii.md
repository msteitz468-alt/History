# Cornelius Vanderbilt II

Grandson of [[actors/vanderbilt-cornelius|Commodore Cornelius Vanderbilt]] and eldest son of [[actors/vanderbilt-william-h|William H. Vanderbilt]] (1843–1899). President and chairman of the New York Central Railroad; builder, with wife Alice Claypoole Gwynne, of [[places/the-breakers-newport|The Breakers]] in Newport and of the colossal Fifth Avenue house that held the record as New York's largest private residence.

## Narrative

After William H.'s death in 1885 the railroad empire and social leadership passed to Cornelius II's generation. When the first Newport house burned in 1892, he and Alice hired Richard Morris Hunt to rebuild on a palatial Italianate scale; The Breakers was completed in 1895 at a reported cost of $7 million. Cooper & Howe note he was deeply involved in construction details but died of a stroke in 1899 at fifty-five, only four years after completion. Alice summered there until 1934; the house passed to daughter Gladys (Countess Széchényi) and eventually out of family hands (sale to the Preservation Society, 1972). Among his children was [[actors/whitney-gertrude-vanderbilt|Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney]]. His estate division and the typhoid death of an intended heir (William Henry Vanderbilt II) unexpectedly elevated [[actors/vanderbilt-alfred-gwynne|Alfred Gwynne Vanderbilt]] in the male succession of that branch.

## Role

Peak-display generation: railroad steward and palace builder. Material symbol of the dynasty's conversion of transport profits into limestone and marble.

## Historiography

Cooper & Howe treat him through houses and transmission of fortune rather than as operator; business histories and Stiles cover the corporate line. Distinguish carefully from his grandfather the Commodore and from Cornelius Jeremiah ("Cornie").
