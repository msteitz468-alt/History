# Coke Stevenson

**Summary page.** Full analysis of the 1948 campaign lives in the detailed biography and source pages.

Coke R. Stevenson (1888–1975) was a Texas governor and the leading conservative Democrat in the state in the 1940s. He embodied the "Old" politics — personal, conservative, low-key, rural — against which Lyndon Johnson ran in the pivotal 1948 Democratic primary for the U.S. Senate.

## Overview

A self-made man from West Texas, Stevenson served as Governor during World War II years. He was widely respected for personal integrity, fiscal conservatism, and a plain-spoken style. In 1948 he was the heavy favorite to win the Senate seat until Johnson's all-out, modern campaign (helicopter, money, organization) closed the gap and then overtook him through the disputed votes in Box 13.

## Key Developments

- Long career in Texas politics culminating in governorship.
- 1948 Senate race: positioned as the conservative, anti-New Deal alternative; refused to adopt modern campaign techniques.
- The primary results (official 87-vote Johnson margin after late-counted Box 13) led to court challenges that ultimately failed to overturn the result in time for the general election.
- Symbol in Caro's narrative of the passing of an older political era.

## Significance and Counterfactuals

Stevenson represented a vanishing style of Texas politics. His defeat marked the triumph of the new methods (money, media, organization, flexible ethics) that Johnson perfected. Had the Box 13 votes not been altered, Stevenson would likely have gone to the Senate; Johnson's path to national power would have been blocked or significantly delayed.

## Historiography

Caro's account in *Means of Ascent* is the most detailed reconstruction of the 1948 campaign and Stevenson's role. Other accounts exist, but Caro's use of interviews and documents makes this volume the essential source.
