# The Thirty Tyrants

## Overview

The **Thirty** (*hoi triakonta*, later "the Thirty Tyrants") were the oligarchic board
installed at [[athens]] in **404 BCE**, after its surrender ended the
[[peloponnesian-war|Peloponnesian War]], to "draft new laws" for the city. Imposed under
the pressure of **[[lysander]]** — who "sailed in with a hundred ships and enforced a
solution" — they ruled for some eight months of escalating violence before being
overthrown and the democracy restored in 403. [[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 2,
Lewis) treats them as "a larger decarchy for a larger state," Athens' instance of the
oligarchic settlements Lysander imposed across the [[spartan-hegemony|new Spartan empire]].

## The regime

The Thirty were appointed as a drafting commission on the proposal of **Theramenes**, but
quickly assumed full **executive power**, naming magistrates and a council for 404/3. Lewis
stresses that they were **not simply men of birth and wealth**: "the largest single
identifiable element is that of men who had been in trouble with the democracy." Some of
their early measures were "Athenian responses to Athenian problems" — deleting parts of
the post-410 legal codification, repealing the laws of [[ephialtes]] on the Areopagus,
simplifying the Solonian laws that had given the popular courts too much power, and
purging the **sycophants** (for whom "none of our sources has any sympathy"). Lewis is
sceptical of the view that they were "trying to remake Attica in the Spartan image."

## The split and the terror

The regime fractured between two returned associates of Alcibiades:
- **Critias** — of an old and widespread family (which in the same generation produced
  **Plato**), schooled in the sophistic movement, who "showed a clear-minded instinct for
  personal power" and drove the regime toward extremism.
- **Theramenes** — son of the Periclean general Hagnon; whether he wanted a "tight
  oligarchy" or the restoration of the **hoplite franchise** he had briefly engineered in
  411 remains debated (it is the "deepest contradiction" in the sources).

To pay for a **Spartan garrison** on the Acropolis, the Thirty began executing wealthy
**metics** and prominent Athenians for their property; they also sought (from the satrap
Pharnabazus) the death of **[[alcibiades]]**. When the majority moved to reduce the
citizen body to **3,000**, Theramenes protested, was struck off the roll, condemned, and
forced to drink hemlock — his dying defiance becoming famous through Xenophon.

## Fall and the Amnesty

The terror drove a stream of exiles abroad. **Thrasybulus** led the democratic resistance
back from Phyle to seize the Piraeus; Critias fell in the fighting at Munychia, and with
Spartan acquiescence (Pausanias the king moderating Lysander's hard line) the **democracy
was restored in 403**. The settlement was sealed by a celebrated **Amnesty** — the
agreement "not to recall past wrongs" — whose documentary detail survives in the
*Athenaion Politeia*. The restored democracy proved durable, governing Athens for the rest
of the period covered by [[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH VI]] (see [[athenian-democracy]]).

## Significance

The Thirty are the Athenian face of the [[spartan-hegemony]]: the demonstration that
Sparta's victory "for the freedom of Greece" meant, in practice, the imposition of narrow,
garrison-backed oligarchies. Their brief, bloody rule and the moderate restoration that
followed shaped fourth-century Athenian politics — including, indirectly, the milieu of
**Socrates**, executed in 399 partly for his association with men like Critias and
Alcibiades.
