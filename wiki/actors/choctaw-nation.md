# Choctaw Nation

Large Muskogean nation of the lower Mississippi / central Mississippi region; first of the southern nations subjected to mass Removal under the [[indian-removal-act-1830]]. Green, CHNPA I.1 ch. 8; colonial alignments Salisbury ch. 7.

## Overview

Colonial: principal French Louisiana allies vs English-aligned [[chickasaw-nation|Chickasaws]] and [[creek-muscogee|Creeks]]; factionalism reinforced by gifts and scalp bounties; Red Shoes (Shulush Homa) civil strife mid-18th c.; Natchez suppression role 1730. Revolution: warriors participated; towns not leveled. Hopewell 1785–86: U.S. recognized prewar borders, opened trade, demanded recognition of U.S. sovereignty in place of England’s. Early U.S. period: largely isolated until French Louisiana transfer; refused early sale negotiations. Nabokov (CHNPA intro): dual Nani Waiya ethnogenesis sites near Philadelphia, Mississippi—mother mound and emergence cave—protect territorial claims as both incoming and aboriginal people; possible 17th-c. multi-group ethnogenesis.

## Civilization and Doak’s Stand

Plow agriculture, livestock, Natchez Trace commerce, mission schools; constitution **1826** (not full U.S. republican model). **1820 Doak’s Stand:** voluntary western land between Arkansas and Red Rivers; small number moved. Jefferson had suggested western migration to Chickasaw/Choctaw/Cherokee as early as Louisiana Purchase era.

## Removal 1831–34 (atrocity-dense)

Fall 1830 Sec. War Eaton sought sale of remaining eastern holdings ~**10 million acres**. Leaders overwhelmingly opposed; after tirade **threatening declare war and invade**, many left; **Greenwood LeFlore** and **Moshulatubbee** hammered treaty: total cession; annuities; school/church funds; pay all removal expenses + one year subsistence; substantial allotments for families wishing to remain Mississippi citizens—but Agent **William Ward** made registration virtually impossible; only **69** received allotments.

**1831–34:** three groups trekked **550 miles**. First of the mass removals; marked by government **mismanagement, confusion, and fraud** imposing serious unnecessary suffering. Journey cost lives of some **2,500 people**. News of hardships led several thousand to refuse; landless squatters; late 1840s–early 1850s many finally left; about **2,000 never left**—descendants remain in Mississippi on tribally owned land in/near **Neshoba County**.

## Indian Territory

[[chickasaw-nation|Chickasaws]] purchased right to settle among Choctaws 1837; amicable separation **1855** re-created independent Chickasaw Nation. Civil War: Choctaw virtually unanimous southern support; 1866 treaty gave up western lands.

## Historiography

Foreman removal standards; Green synthesis. Choctaw death toll (~2,500) is a load-bearing Green figure for first mass removal mismanagement—retain at full fidelity.
