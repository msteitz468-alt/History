# Friedrich Paulus

**actor_type:** person (German field commander, General of Panzer Troops / Field Marshal).

Born 1890, son of minor civil servant; product of imperial Germany aspiring to upper-class military milieu. Married Romanian noblewoman 1912 (quasi-aristocratic status). WWI captain, staff officer at bn/regt level. Interwar: standard General Staff specialist career; brief troop assignments (inf company alongside Rommel; 3rd Motor Transport Bn 1934–35, prototypical armored recon for panzer arm). Reverted to staff: chief of staff of panzer forces 1935; chief of staff XVI Army Corps (Mot) 1938; chief of staff Heeresgruppe 4 1939.

**1939–1941:** Chief of staff Tenth Army (under Walter Reichenau) for Poland invasion. Reichenau (early Nazi convert, meteoric) patron; Paulus's "methodical staff work was the perfect complement to Reichenau’s personal, assertive style." After 1940 Western campaign, Oberquartiermeister I (Deputy Chief of General Staff) under Halder; "drafted the original plan for Operation Barbarossa, although he later claimed to have doubts about the outcome." Refined, reserved; "never comfortable arguing with coarser, more assertive men such as Hitler and Reichenau."

**1942 Stalingrad campaign:** "On 5 January 1942, the man who had never commanded anything larger than a battalion was promoted to full general of panzer troops and appointed to head the German Sixth Army, one of the spearheads of Plan Blau." Reichenau (envisioned leading from front with protégé) died days later of cold; Bock replaced him. "Thereafter, Paulus was on his own."

As Sixth Army commander during Fall Blau advance to Volga (see [[events/battle-of-stalingrad-1942]], [[hubs/warfare/world-wars-era/second-world-war/battle-of-stalingrad-1942]]), later Field Marshal. Central figure in encirclement and surrender Feb 1943. Controversial postwar for role and capitulation.

**From Glantz/House *To the Gates of Stalingrad* (Ch. 1, Range 01):** Detailed bio establishes Paulus as staff expert rather than field commander; appointment reflected Hitler's preference for "obedient minions who would faithfully execute their Führer’s orders." Links to broader Wehrmacht command realities (Hitler CinC Army, FHQ interventions limiting auftragstaktik). `part_of:` [[concepts/plan-fall-blau]] execution. `key_events:` the drive to Stalingrad and its prelude.
