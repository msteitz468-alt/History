# Elam (Awan, Simashki, and Susa)

The great **civilization of southwestern Iran** — the chief power of the Iranian
world for two millennia before the Medes and Persians, and Mesopotamia's perennial
eastern rival and trading partner. "The history of Persia before ~1000 BCE is
almost entirely the history of Elam," because Elam alone left written records.
Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]] (Ch. XXIII, Hinz).

## Highland and lowland: the federal state

Elam's defining structure was the **union of the irrigated lowland plain of
Susiana** (around **[[susa|Susa]]**) **with the mountains and plateaux** to its
north and east (Anshan, Simashki) — fertile farmland joined to highlands rich in
**timber, metal ores (copper, tin, lead, silver), stone, and horses.** This
lowland–highland link, broken only by the Persian conquest ~600 BCE, made Elam both
wealthy and politically distinctive: a **federation** of provinces under
"governors" (*ḫal-menik* / *ensi*), a "viceroy" (*šakkanakkum*), and a king
(*sunkir*), held together by **consanguinity and brother–sister marriage** — a
fraternal succession system unusual in the ancient world.

## The leitmotiv: enmity and exchange with Mesopotamia

Elam–Mesopotamia relations run on a single recurring theme: **hereditary enmity
mitigated by constant economic exchange.** Mesopotamia needed Elam's highland
resources; Elam coveted the riches of the lowland cities. The earliest notice has
**Enmebaragisi of Kish** carrying off "the arms of Elam"; the
[[sumerian-king-list|king-list]] records the kingship passing back and forth between
**Awan** and Mesopotamian cities. Gilgamesh, Sargon, and the kings of Ur all
campaigned east; Elam repeatedly raided west.

## The Awan dynasty and the Akkadian wars

The first historical Elamite line is the **dynasty of Awan**. [[sargon-of-akkad|
Sargon]] subdued it (the first firm synchronism in Elamite history, ~2325 BCE,
under king Lukh-ishshan); Rimush crushed a revolt of Elam and Warakhshe. Most
remarkable, [[naram-sin|Naram-Sin]] concluded a **treaty with king Khita of Awan**
— the **oldest known Elamite-language document and one of the earliest diplomatic
treaties on record** ("Naram-Sin's enemy is my enemy; Naram-Sin's friend is my
friend"), invoking some 35 Elamite gods. The dynasty culminated in **Kutik-In-
Shushinak** (Puzur-Inshushinak), who rose from governor of Susa to "**mighty king
of Awan**," claimed "the four quarters of the earth," built extensively on the Susa
acropolis, and wrote in both Akkadian and the indigenous **"proto-Elamite" linear
script** — a gesture of national pride. His kingdom fell, like Akkad, into the
Gutian anarchy.

## Simashki and the revenge on Ur

The succeeding **dynasty of Simashki** (highland Luristan) lived mostly under
Mesopotamian shadow — subordinated by [[shulgi|Shulgi]] (who married daughters to
the governors of Anshan and Warakhshe, then campaigned against Anshan anyway). But
Elam took its **revenge** at the collapse of the [[third-dynasty-of-ur|Third
Dynasty of Ur]]: a king of Simashki led the [[fall-of-ur-2004bce|Elamite sack of
Ur]] (~2004 BCE), carrying **Ibbi-Sin captive to Anshan** and the statue of the
Moon-god away — "the desolation of Ur" lamented in Sumerian poetry. Elam thereafter
entered its powerful **sukkalmah** ("grand regent") period (CAH Vol. II).

## Religion and language

Elam had a distinctive pantheon headed by the mother-goddess **Pinikir**, the great
Susa city-god **In-Shushinak**, the sun-god **Nahhunte**, and **Humban**, **Napir**,
and **Narundi** — many female, reflecting Elam's relatively prominent place for
goddesses and royal women. The **Elamite language is an isolate**, related to no
known tongue (perhaps ancestral to the Lurs), written first in proto-Elamite
linear script and then in borrowed cuneiform — and still imperfectly understood,
one of the chief obstacles to Elamite history.

## Significance

Elam is the wiki's first major **non-Mesopotamian, non-Egyptian literate
civilization** — the eastern counterweight whose highland resources fed the Bronze
Age economy and whose armies repeatedly checked and sometimes toppled Mesopotamian
empires. It establishes the enduring **Iran–Mesopotamia frontier** that runs
through all later Near Eastern history, down to the Achaemenids who would make
[[susa|Susa]] an imperial capital.

## Historiography

Elamite history is "pitifully" thin: few documents, overwhelmingly from **Susa**
(French excavations since 1897), in a poorly-understood **isolate language**, with
chronology pegged to Mesopotamian synchronisms. The king-lists' schematic "twelve
kings" of Awan and Simashki "must be regarded with caution." Reconstruction leans
heavily on the [[akkadian-empire|Akkadian]] and [[third-dynasty-of-ur|Ur III]]
records of their wars and treaties. See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
