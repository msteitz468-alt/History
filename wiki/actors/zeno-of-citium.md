# Zeno of Citium

Founder of [[stoicism|Stoicism]] (c.333–262 BCE). From **Citium in Cyprus**, he came to [[athens|Athens]] c.**310 BCE** and established a school in the **Stoa Poikile** (“Painted Porch”), giving the movement its name. The city of Athens later voted him **public honours** — Barnes’s index of philosophy’s popularity in the Hellenistic city.

## Role

**Jonathan Barnes** (OHCW, 1986): Zeno set the Old Stoic programme — reject hedonism, counsel a life of virtue; materialism without atomism; empiricism with a major role for reason. His mantle passed to **Cleanthes of Assus** (d. c.232) and then **Chrysippus** (d. c.206) from Asia Minor, who so systemized the school that the saying ran: “if Chrysippus had not existed, the Stoa would not have existed either.” The school was not uniform even in the Old phase: Ariston of Chios ethics-only; Chrysippus differed from Zeno/Cleanthes on many points. Cleanthes’ hostility to Aristarchus’ heliocentrism (wanting Greeks to indict him for impiety) illustrates the Stoa’s distance from Alexandrian mathematical science.

## Significance

Zeno’s Porch supplied the ethical language of duty, nature, and cosmopolitan citizenship that Roman elites from [[cicero]]’s age through [[marcus-aurelius]] would appropriate. Counterfactual without Zeno: Hellenistic practical philosophy reduces largely to the Garden and the sceptical Academy. With [[epicurus|Epicurus]], he anchors the sectarian “Art of Living” that Barnes takes as the Hellenistic schools’ lasting inheritance — narrower and more introspective than Aristotle’s unity of science and philosophy.

## Historiography

Almost no continuous works survive; doctrine is reconstructed from later reports (Diogenes Laertius, Stobaeus, Cicero, Plutarch). OHCW (Barnes) is a reliable mid-1980s outline, not a replacement for technical Stoic studies.
