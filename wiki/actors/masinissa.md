# Masinissa

King of **Numidia**, the Roman-allied Numidian ruler whose cavalry decided
[[battle-of-zama-202bce|Zama]] and whose long pressure on [[carthage]] helped
provoke the Third Punic War. Treated in [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Chs 3 & 5
(Briscoe, Harris).

## Role and career

Masinissa, prince of the **Massyli**, fought first **for Carthage** in Spain, but
in 206 — after the Roman victory at Ilipa and a meeting with
[[scipio-africanus|Scipio]] — changed sides. His rival **Syphax** (the Masaesyli),
who married Hasdrubal Gisgo's daughter and went over to Carthage, drove Masinissa
into flight. In the African campaign Masinissa's defection gave Scipio **cavalry
superiority**: he helped destroy the camps, capture Syphax, and win
[[battle-of-zama-202bce|Zama]] (202). The peace of 201 restored and **enlarged his
kingdom**, recognising "all lands possessed by his ancestors."

For half a century thereafter Masinissa expanded Numidia at Carthage's expense,
exploiting the treaty clause that forbade Carthage to make war without Roman
permission. His encroachments, and Carthage's eventual armed resistance (which
technically breached the treaty), gave Rome the pretext for the **Third Punic War**
and the destruction of Carthage (149–146) — though Masinissa himself died in 148,
before the end.

## Counterfactual significance

Masinissa is the pivot of the African theatre: his change of sides converted
Scipio's invasion from a gamble into a victory, and his lifelong pressure on a
disarmed Carthage manufactured the conditions for Carthage's final destruction.
He also created a durable, partly Hellenised North African kingdom — a model of
the Roman client-king.

## Historiography

The Numidian succession-struggle (Syphax vs. Masinissa, the shifting alliances of
214–204) is tangled in [[polybius]] and [[livy]]; Briscoe stresses that Masinissa's
overtures to Rome **preceded** Syphax's open defection. His later reign is known
mainly from Livy's epitomes and Appian, and from the archaeology of Numidian
royal monuments.
