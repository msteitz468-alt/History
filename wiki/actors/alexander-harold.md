# Harold Alexander (1891–1969)

British field marshal. Deputy Allied C-in-C Mediterranean and ground force commander (15th Army Group) for Operation Husky.

D'Este's central critical figure: "Alexander: The Great Enigma." Alexander is portrayed as passive, lacking strategic imagination, distrustful of American troops (a view formed at Kasserine and never fully revised even after American performance improved), and unable or unwilling to impose a coherent operational plan on his two army commanders. His plan for Sicily is described by Montgomery (and D'Este) as "idiotic."

Alexander failed to ensure aggressive pursuit or to prioritize blocking the Strait of Messina, allowing the Germans to evacuate the bulk of their forces.

## Sicily Role (D'Este)

- Nominally commanded all ground forces.
- Issued orders that kept Seventh Army in a subordinate role.
- Failed to resolve the boundary dispute between Patton and Montgomery in a way that enabled decisive results.
- Did not press hard enough for interdiction of the German evacuation.
- Later memoirs devoted only four pages to the campaign.

## Historiography

Alexander enjoys a generally positive reputation in British accounts as a "gentleman" commander who maintained coalition harmony. D'Este's archival work presents a much harsher verdict on his operational leadership in Sicily and the consequences for the Italian campaign. Modern assessments often agree that Alexander was out of his depth at Army Group level in a coalition context.
