# Frederick William I of Prussia

**Frederick William I** ("the Soldier King," r. 1713–1740) was King in Prussia and father
of [[frederick-the-great|Frederick the Great]]. He built the disciplined, well-drilled
Prussian army (famous for its "Potsdam Giants" grenadier regiment) into one of Europe's
most effective military instruments relative to the kingdom's small population and
resources, financed by austere personal frugality and a rigorously centralized fiscal-
military bureaucracy — the administrative and military foundation his son would use to
seize Silesia and make Prussia a great power.

## Character and Relationship with Frederick

Frederick William was famous for a harsh, often brutal parenting style toward his heir,
contemptuous of the young Frederick's interests in music, French culture, and literature,
which he regarded as effeminate distractions from soldierly duty. Their relationship
reached a crisis in 1730 when Frederick attempted to flee to England with his friend
Hans Hermann von Katte; Frederick William had Katte executed before the prince's eyes and
imprisoned Frederick, a formative trauma often cited by historians (e.g., Duffy) as
shaping the future king's later emotional guardedness and pragmatic statecraft.
