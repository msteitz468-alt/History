# George H. Thomas

Union general known as "the Rock of Chickamauga" for holding the Union left
after the rest of the army broke at Chickamauga (1863), and later as
commander of the Army of the Cumberland that destroyed John Bell Hood's Army
of Tennessee at the Battle of Nashville (December 1864).

## Role and Major Decisions

A Virginian who remained loyal to the Union, Thomas commanded with a
reputation for deliberate, methodical planning rather than dash. His
defensive stand at Chickamauga preserved the Army of the Cumberland from
destruction; his subsequent command in the Franklin-Nashville Campaign
(1864) first absorbed Hood's costly frontal assault at
[[battle-of-franklin-1864|Franklin]] and then, at Nashville, executed one of
the most complete tactical victories of the war, effectively destroying the
Confederate Army of Tennessee as a fighting force.

## Counterfactual Significance

Had Thomas broken at Chickamauga, the Union's Western armies would have
faced a far more difficult 1864 campaign season; his careful generalship at
Nashville is often cited as the war's most decisive single battlefield
destruction of an enemy army, removing Hood's force from the strategic
picture entirely.

## Historiography

Thomas is comparatively under-celebrated relative to Grant, Sherman, and
Sheridan in popular Civil War memory, a discrepancy historians attribute to
his Southern origin, his relatively low profile in postwar political life,
and his death soon after the war (1870), which left him without the
memoir-writing career that shaped the reputations of his peers.
