# Postumus

Marcus Cassianius Latinius Postumus (c. 220–269 CE) was a Roman commander on the Rhine frontier under [[gallienus]] who, in 260, exploited the chaos following the capture of Emperor Valerian by Persia and Gallienus's preoccupation elsewhere to have himself proclaimed emperor by the Rhine legions after killing Gallienus's son and Praetorian prefect. He founded the breakaway [[gallic-empire]], comprising Gaul, Britain, and (for a time) Spain.

Rather than march on Rome, Postumus consolidated a defensible, Romanized separatist state: he maintained Roman law, minted his own coinage (styling himself *Restitutor Galliarum*), fielded a Senate-like council, and focused his energy on defending the Rhine against Germanic incursions rather than contesting the central throne. Gallienus's attempts to reconquer the Gallic Empire (265–266) failed. Postumus was killed by his own troops in 269 after refusing to let them sack Mainz, a city that had briefly supported a rival usurper.

His nine-year reign demonstrated that the empire could fracture into durable regional successor states and still function — a template later echoed by the Palmyrene Empire in the east — and set the stage for [[aurelian]]'s eventual reunification of Gaul under Postumus's successors in 274.
