## Role and Background

Hosokawa Tadaoki was a senior daimyo who fought for [[tokugawa-ieyasu|Tokugawa Ieyasu's]] Eastern coalition at the [[battle-of-sekigahara-1600|Battle of Sekigahara]], attacking [[ishida-mitsunari|Ishida Mitsunari's]] wing. He was the son of [[hosokawa-yusai|Hosokawa Yusai]] and the husband of Hosokawa Gracia, the Christian convert whose death before Sekigahara disrupted Ishida's hostage strategy.

Sadler characterizes him as "eminent as soldier, diplomat, and aesthete" — fighting by his father's side at age eleven at the battle of Makinohama, studying Japanese verse under his father, and learning tea ceremony under the great master Sen Rikyu — while also describing him as "by nature overbearing and violent," with a domestic life that was "hardly ideal." He quarreled with his father and brother, drove away his elder son, and killed his second.

## Pre-Sekigahara Diplomacy

Tadaoki played a key mediating role in preventing a clash between Ieyasu and Maeda Toshiie in 1598–99. When Ishida was attempting to organize the anti-Ieyasu party around Maeda, Tadaoki — who was a friend of Maeda and also under obligation to Ieyasu — worked with Kuroda Nagamasa to persuade Maeda Toshinaga not to support Ishida. The argument they offered: "Ieyasu is the finest leader of the day, and is supported by all the wisest and strongest in the land, so that to try and overcome him is like the worm resisting the wheel." This mediation was central to preventing the anti-Tokugawa coalition from forming with Maeda at its head.

## Battle of Sekigahara

Tadaoki commanded a force at Sekigahara that attacked Ishida's wing alongside Kuroda Nagamasa, Kato, Kanamori, and Takenaka. He had been part of the vanguard force that secured Kiyosu castle and the Tokaido corridor before the main battle.

## Marriage to Gracia and Its Consequences

Tadaoki's marriage had been arranged by Nobunaga. His wife was the daughter of Akechi Mitsuhide (Nobunaga's assassin); when her father rebelled, Tadaoki sent her away and refused to join the rebellion. After Hideyoshi defeated Akechi, Tadaoki took her back through Hideyoshi's mediation.

His relationship with her was violently controlling: he did not allow her to go out or mix with others (which the Jesuit missionaries noted), cut off the hair of her ladies and expelled them, and cut off the nose and ears of her foster-mother "for some trifling matter." When she converted to Christianity in his absence (during the Korean campaign), he threatened her life on his return. Nevertheless, she remained firm in her faith and became one of the most spiritually accomplished women of the era, learning Portuguese and Latin and reading the *Imitation of Christ*.

When Ishida's men came to arrest her as a hostage before Sekigahara, she was killed by the retainer Ogasawara Shosai on standing orders, almost certainly Tadaoki's own instructions. The manner — Ogasawara driving a halberd through her heart through the doorway of her room, preserving the etiquette of not entering — and his subsequent self-immolation with the household reflect both the loyalty of the retainer and the violent domestic world Gracia had inhabited. The episode disrupted Ishida's hostage strategy and gave other wives of Eastern generals the opportunity to escape.

After the battle Tadaoki received the domain of Buzen at 369,000 koku.
