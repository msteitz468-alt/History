# William the Conqueror (William I of England)

**William the Conqueror** (c. 1028–1087), Duke of Normandy from 1035 and King of
England from 1066, was the architect of the **[[norman-conquest-of-england-1066|Norman
Conquest of England]]** and the founder of the Anglo-Norman realm.
[[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] (Marjorie Chibnall, Ch. 7) traces how he first
**restored ducal authority and military power in Normandy** — surviving a turbulent
minority as the illegitimate son ("the Bastard") of Duke Robert I — before uniting the
duchy with the English kingdom.

## Career

As duke, William mastered the fractious Norman aristocracy and built the military and
ducal institutions that made the conquest possible. Claiming the English throne on
Edward the Confessor's death (1066) by an alleged designation and Harold Godwinson's
broken oath, and armed with **papal approval**, he invaded, defeated and killed Harold
at **Hastings (14 October 1066)**, and was crowned at Westminster. He suppressed English
revolts with severity (the **Harrying of the North**, 1069–70), **replaced the
Anglo-Saxon landed aristocracy** with his Norman and French followers, reorganised the
English Church under Archbishop Lanfranc, and commissioned the great survey of
**Domesday Book (1086)**. He spent his last years defending Normandy's frontiers and
died at Rouen in 1087, leaving Normandy to his eldest son Robert Curthose and England to
his second surviving son, William Rufus.

## Counterfactual Significance

William's conquest is one of the most consequential single events in English and
European history: it severed England from the Scandinavian world and bound it to
continental France, transplanted a continental aristocracy and tenurial system,
transformed the language and the Church, and created the cross-Channel polity whose
later expansion (the [[angevin-empire|Angevin empire]]) and contraction would drive
English and French history for centuries. His achievement also illustrates the wider
**Norman expansion** of the age, parallel to his cousins'
[[norman-conquest-of-southern-italy|conquest of southern Italy and Sicily]].
