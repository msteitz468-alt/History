# The Dorians (and the Heracleidae)

The **Dorians** were the last of the Greek-speaking peoples to enter the Aegean
heartland — a tribal group, speaking a distinct **West-Greek (Doric)** dialect, who
descended from the mountainous northwest at the end of the Bronze Age, overran the
Mycenaean kingdoms of the [[dorian-invasion-1120bce|Peloponnese]], and became the
ancestors of historic **Sparta, Argos, Corinth, Crete, Rhodes, and Cos**. Their
memory survived as the "**Return of the Heracleidae**." [[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH
Vol. II.2]] (Ch. XXXVI, Hammond) reconstructs them from the literary tradition,
the dialect map, and the thin northwestern archaeology.

## Who the Dorians were

In Greek tradition (Herodotus I.56) the Dorian tribal group wandered through a fixed
sequence of homes — Phthiotis, then below Ossa and Olympus (Histiaeotis), then
**Pindus** (where they were called *Makednon*, "highlanders," akin to the
Macedonians), then **Doris** (whence the name *Dorieis*) — before "coming to the
Peloponnese." Hammond fills the gap in the sequence by routing them through the
interior of **Epirus**, and reads them as a **pastoral, transhumant sheep-rearing
people** who, like the later Vlachs, "leave little for the archaeologist." Their
three constituent tribes — **Hylleis, Pamphyli, Dymanes** — kept their names through
all the migrations and reappear, intermixed, in every later Dorian community.

## The Heracleidae

The Dorians were led not by tribal chiefs alone but by the **Heracleidae**, the clan
of the "sons and grandsons of Heracles" — Achaeans by descent (claiming the line of
**Perseus**, king of Mycenae), not themselves Dorians. The foundational legend has
the Dorian king **Aegimius** adopt **Hyllus**, son of Heracles, promising him "a
third of the land and a third of the kingship" — explaining the historic Dorian
institution of **three kings** (the *phylobasileis*, tribal kings of the three
tribes). On the eve of the conquest all three tribal kings were Heraclids and
brothers: **Temenus, Cresphontes, and Aristodemus** (whose twin sons, Eurysthenes
and Procles, founded the dual Spartan kingship). The clan provided the leadership for
the whole movement, including the parallel **Thessalian** invasion (led by Heraclids
descended from Thessalus).

## The first attack and the hundred years

Before the Trojan War, **Hyllus** had already led the Dorians against the Mycenaean
Peloponnese and been killed in single combat by **Echemus of Arcadia** at the
Isthmus of Corinth (~1220 BCE) — after which the Heracleidae undertook **not to
return for a hundred years**. The Mycenaeans' wall across the Isthmus (built late in
LH IIIB) was raised against exactly this northern pressure. The tide turned a century
later in the [[dorian-invasion-1120bce|invasion of c. 1120 BCE]].

## The dialect evidence

Hammond stresses that the Dorian invasion would have to be inferred from the
**dialect map alone** even without the legends: the **West-Greek** group (Doric +
Northwest-Greek) is sharply distinct from the **East-Greek** of the peoples it
displaced, yet closely enough related to it to require that the two groups were "in
close proximity... before they invaded." The non-Dorian invaders of the same wave
(Thessalians, Boeotians, the followers of Oxylus in Elis) spoke a common
**Northwest-Greek** dialect, marking them as a single body out of Epirus and western
Macedonia. The survival of pre-Dorian **East-Greek** forms in Laconian (the name of
Poseidon) and Cretan confirms that the conquest meant **fusion, not replacement** —
a numerically weak conqueror absorbing or enserfing the older population. The matter
is set out fully in [[coming-of-the-greeks-1900bce|the prehistory of the Greek
language]].

## Institutions and religion

The Dorian form of kingship — the king as owner of the land and of "any land won by
the spear" — anticipates the **Macedonian** kingship and survived at Sparta. The
Dorians introduced **serfdom** (the Helots of Laconia, the *penestai* of Thessaly)
where they conquered. They worshipped **Zeus** (brought, Hammond suggests, from
Dodona in Epirus) and **Apollo of Pytho** (Delphi), whose cult they had joined as
fringe members of the Delphic Amphictyony before the invasion; **Apollo Carneius**,
the ram-god, preserved their pastoral past.

## Significance

The Dorians are the agents who finished the [[mycenaean-civilization|Mycenaean]]
world on the mainland and reset the linguistic and political map of historic Greece.
Their arrival drove the [[ionian-migration-1050bce|Ionian and Aeolian migrations]]
that carried Greek civilization across the Aegean; their West-Greek dialect, their
three-tribe organization, their kingship, and their conservative, militarized social
order shaped **Sparta** above all. The "Return of the Heracleidae" became the
charter-myth by which Sparta and the other Dorian states justified their possession
of the Peloponnese.

## Historiography

The Dorians are the great test of the migration tradition. They have **no agreed
archaeological signature** — no distinctive Dorian pottery, weapon, or burial that
marks their advance — so their reality rests on (1) the consistency of the literary
genealogies across independent centres (Argos, Sparta, Aeolis, Achaea, Athens), (2)
the dialect distribution, and (3) thin northwestern finds (cist-graves in tumuli,
"Lausitz" types, Naue II swords) that Hammond reads as the material trace of the
invaders' homeland. CAH II.2 is broadly confident in a real Dorian invasion;
much later scholarship is far more sceptical of a single mass "invasion," preferring
internal collapse plus gradual infiltration — but the West-Greek/East-Greek dialect
divide remains the hard fact any account must explain. The "Great Man vs. structural
causation" tension runs through the whole reconstruction.
