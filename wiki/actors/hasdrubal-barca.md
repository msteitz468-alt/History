# Hasdrubal Barca

Second son of [[hamilcar-barca|Hamilcar Barca]] and younger brother of
[[hannibal|Hannibal]], Hasdrubal commanded the Carthaginian war effort in **Spain**
for most of the [[second-punic-war-218-201bce|Second Punic War]] and died trying to
bring its army to his brother in Italy.

## Role

When Hannibal marched on Italy in 218, Hasdrubal was left to hold the Spanish base
that supplied Carthaginian manpower and silver. For nearly a decade he fought a
see-saw war against the elder Scipios (whom he and Mago destroyed in 211) and then
against the young [[scipio-africanus|Publius Cornelius Scipio]]. After Scipio stormed
[[new-carthage|New Carthage]] in 209 and forced an inconclusive but strategically
decisive action at Baecula (208), Hasdrubal slipped away north and repeated his
brother's feat — crossing the Pyrenees and Alps into Italy in 207 with a fresh army
to join Hannibal.

## Counterfactual significance

Hasdrubal's march was Carthage's best chance to combine its two field armies in Italy
and overwhelm Rome. Its failure was decisive. His message to Hannibal was intercepted;
the consuls Livius Salinator and Claudius Nero concentrated against him, and his army
was destroyed at the [[battle-of-the-metaurus-207bce|Metaurus]] in 207. Hasdrubal,
seeing the day lost, rode into the Roman ranks to die. His severed head was thrown
into Hannibal's camp — the moment, Goldsworthy notes, that Hannibal is said to have
recognized "the doom of Carthage." After the Metaurus the strategic initiative passed
permanently to Rome.

> Not to be confused with **Hasdrubal the Fair** (Hamilcar's son-in-law, who built up
> Punic Spain and was assassinated in 221) or with the **Hasdrubal** who defended
> Carthage in the [[third-punic-war-149-146bce|Third Punic War]].
