# Oskar Lange (1904–1965)

Polish economist; principal architect of the "market socialist" or
**competitive solution** answer to [[mises-ludwig|Mises]] in the
[[socialist-calculation-debate]]. In *On the Economic Theory of Socialism*
(with Fred M. Taylor, ed. B. E. Lippincott, Minneapolis 1938) Lange proposed
a **Central Planning Board** that issues parametric prices for producer
goods and adjusts them by trial and error against observed shortages and
surpluses, while consumer-goods and labor markets remain real — plant
managers are instructed to equate marginal cost and price. H. D. Dickinson's
*Economics of Socialism* (1939) offered a parallel scheme (a "Supreme
Economic Council"). Lange famously quipped that a socialist commonwealth
should erect Mises "a statue in the marble halls of the future Central
Planning Board" for forcing socialists to confront the problem.

[[hayek-friedrich|Hayek]]'s 1940 *Economica* reply (reprinted in
[[hayek-individualism-economic-order-1948]]) attacked the scheme's
adjustment speed, its inapplicability to non-standardized goods, its
centralized investment allocation, and its managerial unaccountability; he
also disputed Lange's history of the debate (the claim that critics had
"retreated to a second line of defense"). The mid-century textbook verdict
nonetheless credited Lange with answering Mises — a verdict re-litigated
after 1989.

Professor at Chicago in the late 1930s–40s; after 1945 renounced US
citizenship and returned to Poland, serving the communist government as
ambassador to the United States, UN Security Council delegate, and senior
economic planner — a career arc from Chicago market socialist to official of
an actually planned economy that historians of economics find emblematic of
the century. *(Summary actor page; his economics belongs to the sibling
Economics wiki.)*
