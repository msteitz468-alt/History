## Overview

Pakistan was created by the 1947 [[partition-of-india-1947]] as a homeland for South Asian Muslims, the political culmination of the [[muslim-league]]'s two-nation theory under Muhammad Ali Jinnah. Jinnah's own vision — a state for Muslims rather than an explicitly Islamic state, "concerned only with minority representation" — was contested almost immediately after independence and has remained the central fault line of Pakistani politics: an ongoing negotiation between an Islamic-constitutional identity and a secular-nationalist one.

## Role and Key Developments

The name "Pakistan" originally denoted five Muslim-majority regions (Punjab, the Afghan/NWFP region, Kashmir, Sindh, Baluchistan), excluding Bengal, which pursued a separate track before joining as East Pakistan. Partition itself caused roughly 10 million migrations and up to a million deaths.

Pakistan's 1949 Objectives Resolution, promoted by [[mawdudi-abu-ala]] after Jinnah's death, declared that "Sovereignty belongs to Allah alone; but He has delegated it to the State of Pakistan" while affirming democracy, freedom, and equality — a formula intended to reconcile Islamic sovereignty with constitutional governance. It became the preamble to the 1956 constitution, which declared that Pakistan's laws could not contravene Islam, though implementation remained vague. General Ayub Khan's 1958 military regime pushed toward a more secular order, dropping "Islamic" from the country's official name and restricting the [[jamaat-i-islami]].

The 1970 elections, won by the Awami League in the east and the PPP (Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto) in the west, precipitated civil war and the 1971 separation of Bangladesh. Bhutto's 1973 constitution reversed course, making Islam the state religion, requiring civil servants to affirm Islamic ideology, and in 1974 declaring the Ahmadis non-Muslim. General Zia ul-Haq's 1977 coup, backed by a Jamaat-i-Islami-aligned alliance, launched systematic top-down Islamization, including the 1979 Hudud Ordinances criminalizing adultery, false accusation of adultery, theft, and alcohol consumption under Islamic law. Zia-era Pakistan also became the organizational and logistical base for the anti-Soviet jihad in Afghanistan.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Jinnah's insistence on a minority-representation framing over an explicitly Islamic one, the state's founding compromise (and its subsequent unraveling under Mawdudi's Objectives Resolution and Zia's Islamization) would likely have taken a different shape entirely — Pakistan's history since 1947 can be read as a repeated renegotiation of a question its founder deliberately left open.

## Historiography

Formichi (2020) frames Pakistan's trajectory as a case study in how a nation-state founded on communal/minority logic became progressively reinterpreted in explicitly Islamic terms, driven less by popular demand than by ideologues (Mawdudi) and military rulers (Zia) exploiting the ambiguity Jinnah left in the founding settlement.
