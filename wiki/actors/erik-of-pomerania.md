# Erik of Pomerania

First crowned king of the [[kalmar-union-1397|Kalmar Union]] (king of all three
realms, 1397–1439), the great-nephew and chosen heir of [[margaret-i-of-denmark|Margaret
I]], who dominated government until her death in 1412. Per
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]] he was probably
crowned king of Norway by hereditary right as early as 1392, before his elections in
Denmark and Sweden in 1396.

## Reign

Erik's reign combined fiscal innovation with overreach:
- around 1429 he began levying the **Sound Dues** (a toll on every ship passing the
  Øresund) — an innovation of enormous future consequence for the Danish crown;
- in 1417 he acquired **København** from the bishop of Roskilde and began developing
  it as the capital;
- he waged a long, costly **war over Schleswig** with the Holstein counts and pursued
  an aggressive **anti-Hanseatic policy** (favouring English and Dutch merchants,
  blockading Hanseatic ships), provoking a Lübeck-led naval war from 1426;
- he appointed **non-native bailiffs** to castles, violating the union charters'
  demand for rule by native men.

The resulting tax and constitutional grievances triggered the
[[engelbrekt-rebellion-1434|Engelbrekt rebellion]] (1434); the Swedish council
withdrew allegiance, and Erik was eventually **deposed by all three kingdoms**
(1439). He withdrew to **[[gotland|Gotland]]**, from which he turned to piracy, and
died in Pomerania in 1459. See [[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]].
