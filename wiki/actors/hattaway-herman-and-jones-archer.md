# Herman Hattaway and Archer Jones

Herman Hattaway and Archer Jones (co-authors) produced the standard operational and logistical history of the American Civil War, *How the North Won* (University of Illinois Press, 1983; paperback edition with new introduction 1991).

## How the North Won (1983/1991)

This work emphasizes high command, strategy, and logistics rather than detailed battle tactics. It argues that strategy, management, and execution outweighed superior numbers and resources in determining the outcome. Key themes include:
- The intrinsic power of the tactical defense in the rifle-musket era (analogous to World War I).
- The decisive role of Union logistics and the development of superior managerial and staff systems (civilian businessmen, coordination).
- Simultaneous advances across theaters as the path to victory.
- Reinterpretation of leaders: Lincoln's and Grant's command roles; critiques and revisions of McClellan, Halleck, Lee, etc.
- Importance of raids, guerrillas, and "hard war" elements.

The authors provide an appendix primer on the study of military operations and quantitative analysis of mobilization and combat effectiveness. The book is the operational/logistical counterpart to tactical studies (e.g., West Point History series) and is the primary reference for "why and how the North won" at the campaign level.
