# Sverre Sigurdsson

King of [[norway|Norway]] (1177–1202) and the decisive figure of the
**Norwegian Civil Wars**. Claiming to be a royal son, Sverre took over the leadership
of the **Birkibeinar** ("birchlegs") — originally a proletarian faction from the
peripheral districts of Østlandet — and turned it into the dominant party, defeating
and killing the crowned king Magnus Erlingsson (Fimreite, 1184).

## Significance

Per [[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]], Sverre
made lasting contributions to Norwegian state-building: he extended local
administration through royal **sýslumenn**, made the original move of appointing
royal **lawmen** (*lögmenn*), and built the first stone strongholds at Trondheim and
Bergen. Against the Church he asserted an ideology of **kingship by divine grace
without ecclesiastical mediation**, articulated in the polemical *En tale mot
biskopene* ("A Speech against the Bishops"); he was excommunicated by Innocent III
but prevailed. His own saga, *Sverris saga*, partly composed in his lifetime, is a
remarkable near-contemporary source. The Sverre dynasty's sole rule was secured when
Earl Skule, who had himself proclaimed king in 1239, was defeated and killed the
next year — ending the Civil Wars. See [[battle-of-stiklestad-1030]] (an earlier
phase of the same dynastic problem).
