# Sheshonq I

**Sheshonq I** (r. ~945–924 BCE), "Chief of the Meshwesh," founded the **22nd Dynasty** and
briefly reasserted strong central rule during the
[[processes/third-intermediate-period|Third Intermediate Period]]. He is almost certainly the
biblical **"Shishak, king of Egypt."**

## Reunifier of the north

From a Libyan family settled at Bubastis that had risen "through judicious marriages with the
royal family and links with the high priests at Memphis," Sheshonq took power smoothly from the
last Tanite king Psusennes II (his son's father-in-law). He "sought to re-establish the
political authority of the king," reviving New Kingdom models and curbing Theban independence by
installing his own son **Iuput** as high priest of Amun and army commander — though this very
practice of staffing offices with royal sons would later drive the dynasty's fragmentation (see
[[actors/libyan-dynasties-egypt]]).

## The Palestine campaign

Around 925 BCE Sheshonq launched "a major military expedition … against Israel and Judah and the
principal towns of southern Palestine, including Gaza and Megiddo." The Old Testament confirms
it — "Shishak, king of Egypt" stripped the treasures of Jerusalem (1 Kings 14:25–26), with "an
army that included Libyans and Nubians" (2 Chronicles). He recorded the victory on the
**Bubastite Portal** at Karnak, "one of the most valuable historical sources for the entire
period." But the campaign "remained only a flash in the pan": Sheshonq died soon after his
return, and Egypt did not recover its Asiatic empire.
