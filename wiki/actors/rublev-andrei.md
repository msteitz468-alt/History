# Andrei Rublev
## Biography / Description
**Andrei Rublev** (c. 1370 – 1430 CE) was the most celebrated icon painter and fresco artist of medieval Russia. Operating during the dark and turbulent decades of the appanage period and the Mongol yoke, Rublev was a monk associated with the Holy Trinity-St. Sergius Monastery and the Andronikov Monastery in Moscow. 

His works represent the highest peak of Russian Orthodox spirituality and artistic achievement, setting the standard for icon painting across the Eastern Christian world.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **The Old Testament Trinity (early 15th century):** Rublev painted his masterpiece, the *Old Testament Trinity*, representing the three angels who visited Abraham at Mamre. The icon is famous for its exquisite drawing, composition, rhythm, harmony, and lyricism (l. 1911).
* **Fresco Painting:** Rublev, often working with his associate Daniil Chernyi, painted major frescoes in key cathedrals of northeastern Russia, including the Cathedral of the Assumption in Vladimir (1408 CE) and the Trinity Cathedral of the St. Sergius Monastery.
* **Leadership of the Muscovite School:** Rublev founded a distinct Muscovite school of icon painting that merged the elegance of the older Suzdal school with a deep, contemplative spirituality.

## Historical Significance
Andrei Rublev’s art represents a profound theological and cultural response to the crises of the appanage period. In an era marked by violence, fragmentation, and foreign domination, Rublev’s icons emphasized harmony, peace, light, and spiritual reconciliation. 

Scholars emphasize that his art directly reflected the spiritual legacy of St. Sergius of Radonezh. As art historian Muratov observed, the *Trinity* is "imbued with a suave and mystical spirituality... a revelation of the spirit of St. Sergius" (quoted in Riasanovsky, l. 1913). Rublev was canonized as a saint by the Russian Orthodox Church in 1988.
