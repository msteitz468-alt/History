# Napoleon Bonaparte

French military and political leader who rose during the Revolution, became First Consul, then Emperor (1804-1814/1815). Master of operational art, rapid maneuver, and combined arms. Conquered much of Europe but overreached with the Russian invasion (1812) and final defeat at Waterloo (1815). Legacy includes Napoleonic Code, nationalism, and modern warfare concepts.

**Whitman's reading** ([[whitman-verdict-of-battle-2012|The Verdict of Battle]],
2012): Napoleon deliberately *re-monarchized* war — reviving monarchy from 1801
(Kingdom of Etruria), replacing sister republics with family kingdoms, marrying
a Habsburg for dynastic legitimacy — which is why classic
[[verdict-of-battle|pitched-battle warfare]] survived him. Despite the
Clausewitz/Jomini myth of relentless pursuit-to-annihilation, he "never
systematically abandoned the retreat rule," claiming traditional victories at
Marengo, Wagram, and Borodino without pursuit. Weigley's paradox, which Whitman
adopts: even Napoleon's genuine annihilations (Austerlitz, Jena-Auerstädt) were
"decisive only in the short run" — his map of Europe barely outlasted a decade,
whereas legal verdicts under the [[jus-victoriae]] endured.
