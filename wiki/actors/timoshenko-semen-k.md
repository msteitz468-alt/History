# Semyon Timoshenko

Soviet Marshal, People's Commissar for Defense (1940–1941), and front
commander during the early years of the Second World War, notably
responsible for the disastrous Soviet offensive that led to the
[[second-battle-of-kharkov-1942|Second Battle of Kharkov]] (May 1942).

## Role and Major Decisions

Timoshenko had earlier overseen Red Army reforms following the poor Soviet
performance in the Winter War against Finland, and directed the successful
defense that halted the German drive on Moscow's southern flank in late
1941. In May 1942 he pressed for and led a major Soviet offensive to retake
Kharkov, which German forces encircled and destroyed, inflicting catastrophic
Soviet losses and opening the path for the German summer offensive that led
to Stalingrad. He was subsequently sidelined to less prominent commands for
the remainder of the war.

## Counterfactual Significance

The Kharkov disaster is frequently cited as removing Soviet forces and
reserves that might otherwise have blunted the German 1942 summer offensive
earlier, contributing directly to the crisis that culminated in the
Battle of Stalingrad.

## Historiography

Soviet-era accounts downplayed Timoshenko's personal responsibility for the
Kharkov defeat, attributing blame more heavily to political interference
(notably from Stalin and Nikita Khrushchev, then a political commissar on
the front); post-Soviet historians using declassified archives have offered
more balanced assessments distributing responsibility among Timoshenko,
Stalin's Stavka, and front-level intelligence failures regarding German
intentions.
