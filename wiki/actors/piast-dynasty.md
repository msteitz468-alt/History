# Piast Dynasty

The Piast dynasty was the first historical ruling house of Poland, traditionally traced to a semi-legendary peasant-wheelwright ancestor (Piast the Wheelwright) and emerging into the historical record with Mieszko I (r. c. 960–992), who unified the Polanie tribal lands around Gniezno and Poznań and accepted Christianity in 966, aligning the emerging Polish state with Latin Christendom rather than the Byzantine or pagan spheres.

Under [[boleslaw-i-chrobry|Bolesław I Chrobry]] (the Brave, r. 992–1025) the dynasty achieved its first royal coronation (1025) and greatest early territorial reach, contesting the Holy Roman Empire and briefly holding Kiev. The Piast realm subsequently fragmented into competing duchies during a prolonged period of feudal division (1138–1320), before Władysław I Łokietek and Casimir III the Great reunified the Polish crown in the 14th century, with Casimir's death without a male heir in 1370 ending the direct male Piast line and passing the crown to the Angevins.

The Piasts established the enduring institutional and territorial core of the Polish state — the bishopric of Gniezno, the royal capital tradition later centered on Kraków, and the dynastic legitimacy later Polish rulers (Jagiellonians and successors) invoked. Cadet Piast branches (Silesian, Masovian) continued to rule regional duchies, some surviving as vassals into the early modern period.
