# Boyar Duma

## Biography / Description
The **Boyar Duma** was the supreme advisory council of the Grand Princes and Tsars of Muscovite Russia (L2548-2550). Composed of the highest aristocracy (boyars, service princes, and select gentry), it sat in near-continuous daily sessions, advising the monarch on judicial, financial, administrative, and foreign policy matters (L2550-L2554). As a body, it represented the shared authority of the sovereign and the leading aristocratic clans of the realm, summarized in the traditional Muscovite legislative formula: *"the sovereign directed and the boyars assented"* (L2554).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Appanage Integration:** The Duma grew from around 30 members under Boris Godunov to 59 under Alexis and 167 under Theodore, increasingly incorporating bureaucratic servitors (*dumnie diaki*) alongside hereditary princes (L2550-2552). 
* **The Seven Boyars Interregnum (1610–1613):** Following the deposition of Tsar Basil Shuisky in July 1610, a council of seven boyars led by Prince Theodore Mstislavsky assumed executive control of the state (L2306). They negotiated the invitation of the Polish prince Wladyslaw to the Russian throne in August 1610 (L2312-2314).
* **The Ulozhenie of 1649:** The Boyar Duma worked with the Zemsky Sobor to draft and approve the landmark legal code that codified serfdom (L2428-2430).
* **Abolition of Mestnichestvo (1682):** Under Tsar Theodore, the Duma approved the abolition of the *mestnichestvo* (pedigree-based appointment system) and the burning of genealogical registers to modernize the state administration (L2466, L2546).

## Historical Significance
While nineteenth-century historians like Vasily Kliuchevsky demonstrated that the Boyar Duma acted primarily as an advisory body rather than a constitutional limit on autocracy, it remained an indispensable element of Muscovite statecraft (L2554). The Duma acted as the executive power in the absence of the Tsar or during interregnums (L2554). 

However, its reliance on the rigid *mestnichestvo* system hindered efficiency and talent-based promotion (L2538-2540). To bypass boyar resistance, Ivan IV established the *Oprichnina* (1565), executing and expropriating many of the service princes (L2120-2122, L2148-2150). The Duma's influence steadily declined in the late 17th century as tsars relied on specialized bureaus (*prikazy*) and personal favorites (L2421, L2574-2576). The Boyar Duma was officially abolished by Peter the Great in 1711, replaced by the newly created Governing Senate (L2956-2958).
