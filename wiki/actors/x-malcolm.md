# Malcolm X (Malcolm Little / El-Hajj Malik El-Shabazz)

**Malcolm X** (May 19, 1925 – February 21, 1965), born Malcolm Little and later known as El-Hajj Malik El-Shabazz, was an African-American Muslim minister, human rights activist, and prominent Black nationalist leader. He rose to national prominence as the chief spokesman for the **Nation of Islam** (NOI) in the 1950s and early 1960s before breaking with the organization, undertaking the Hajj, and moving toward a broader pan-Africanist and orthodox Islamic position. His evolving ideology, oratory, and assassination made him one of the most influential and controversial figures of the Cold War-era Black freedom struggle.

## Summary

Malcolm's life was marked by repeated reinvention. The son of a Garveyite father (Earl Little) and a Grenadian mother (Louise Langdon Little), he experienced early family tragedy, moved through street life in Boston and Harlem as "Detroit Red," served time in prison, converted to the Nation of Islam, became its most effective minister, split with Elijah Muhammad after the 1963 Kennedy assassination remark, traveled to Mecca in 1964, and founded the Organization of Afro-American Unity (OAAU) before his murder at the Audubon Ballroom in Harlem.

He is remembered both for his searing critique of white supremacy and American liberalism ("by any means necessary") and for his later turn toward internationalism and more inclusive politics. His story is inseparable from the history of the NOI, the civil rights / Black Power transition, and global decolonization.

## Career

- **Early life and criminal years (1925–1946)**: Born in Omaha, Nebraska; family moved to Michigan. Father killed (officially accident, widely suspected lynching or murder). Mother institutionalized. Malcolm moved to Boston, then Harlem; became a hustler, zoot-suiter, and burglar ("Detroit Red"). Arrested 1946; sentenced to 8–10 years. In prison encountered the NOI teachings of Elijah Muhammad.
- **Nation of Islam ascent (1948–1963)**: Converted in prison; upon release became a minister. Built Temple No. 7 in Harlem into a major center. Famous for "The Hate That Hate Produced" (Mike Wallace documentary, 1959) which brought the NOI to national attention. By early 1960s, Malcolm was the most visible and eloquent spokesman for Black separatism and self-defense.
- **Break and transformation (1963–1964)**: Publicly suspended after stating the Kennedy assassination was "chickens coming home to roost." Discovered Elijah Muhammad's personal scandals. Left the NOI. Made the Hajj (April 1964); adopted Sunni Islam and the name El-Hajj Malik El-Shabazz. Founded Muslim Mosque, Inc. and the OAAU.
- **Final phase (1964–1965)**: Traveled to Africa and the Middle East; met with heads of state and revolutionary leaders. Sought broader alliances including with some civil rights figures while maintaining radical critique. Increasingly targeted; assassinated February 21, 1965 while speaking in Harlem. Three NOI members were convicted; questions about wider conspiracy persist.

## Historiography Note

Primary popular image long shaped by *The Autobiography of Malcolm X* (1965, with Alex Haley). Manning Marable's 2011 biography (*Malcolm X: A Life of Reinvention*) uses new archival sources (including extensive FBI files) to present a more complex, multi-layered figure whose political and religious positions were in constant evolution. Debates continue over the precise circumstances of his murder, the extent of government surveillance and provocation, and the degree of continuity vs. rupture between his NOI and post-NOI phases.
