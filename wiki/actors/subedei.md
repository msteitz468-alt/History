# Sübedei

**Sübedei** (also Subutai, Subedei, Weatherford: Subodei) was among the greatest operational
commanders of the [[mongol-conquests-13th-century|Mongol conquests]] — one of Chinggis Khan's
**dörben noqas** ("four hounds") and the architect, with **[[jebe|Jebe]]**, of the 1220–23
pursuit of Sultan Muhammad II and the reconnaissance campaign that ended in the
**[[battle-of-the-kalka-river-1223|Battle of the Kalka River]]**. [[may-mongol-art-of-war-2016|May]]
treats him as emblematic of Mongol meritocratic command: battlefield promotion, keshik training,
and independent execution of timetabled strategic objectives.

## Career (Weatherford 2004 narrative + May framework)

[[weatherford-genghis-khan-making-modern-world-2004|Weatherford]] narrates Sübedei's "discovery"
of Europe as accidental reconnaissance later sold at Ögödei's *khuriltai*:

1. **Caspian circuit (1221–23).** With Jebe, he pursued the Khwarazmshah around the Caspian;
   after the sultan's death they received permission to probe north, found Christian Georgia
   under Giorgi III, and used the **Dog Fight** feigned retreat — Jebe drawing Georgians into
   Sübedei's waiting regiment — destroying the Georgian army and aristocracy and making Georgia
   the first European vassal.
2. **Steppe reconnaissance to Kalka.** Systematic probes of eastern European plains (numbers,
   cities, rivalries); some Kipchak allies; Dnieper by late April 1223; nearly two-week feigned
   withdrawal ending at the Kalka (Sea of Azov drainage); after victory, Crimean rest where
   Genoese trading posts were noted, and the felt-rug crushing of Prince Mstislav and
   sons-in-law for killing ambassadors.
3. **Advocate of the European war.** At Ögödei's target *khuriltai*, the ~sixty-year-old
   Sübedei (Weatherford: possibly one-eyed, so fat he needed an iron chariot) argued for Europe
   over India, Arab lands, or the Song, claiming prior test of European armies. Ögödei allegedly
   disliked/mistrusted him; Jochi's line (Batu) strongly backed the plan. Campaign under Batu
   with Sübedei as operational guide; grandsons from all four branches.
4. **1236–42 zenith.** 1236 (Year of the Monkey): Sübedei north up the Volga against Bulgars,
   Möngke south against Kipchak; Volga base then three-year campaign across Rus′/Ukraine.
   February 1241: scouts on frozen rivers; three-pronged ~50,000 toward Hungary and diversionary
   ~20,000 across Poland toward Germany. Weatherford calls the five-year European campaign the
   **zenith of Mongol military ability** but an economic disappointment, and the dual-theater
   (Song + Europe) decision the worst in imperial history.
