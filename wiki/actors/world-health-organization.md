# The World Health Organization (WHO)

## Overview

The **World Health Organization**, whose constitution was ratified in **1948**, is the
United Nations' specialized health agency and, in Erez Manela's account in
[[mcneill-cwh-v7-2015|CWH VII]], the institutional embodiment of the twentieth-century
**rise of "global health"** — the idea that disease control is a *global*, not merely
national or international, task. Designed to serve "not nations, but humanity itself,"
it emerged from the institutional lineage of the nineteenth-century international
sanitary conferences, the colonial and Rockefeller Foundation campaigns, and the
**League of Nations Health Organization** and its "epistemic community" of experts.

## Founding Conditions and Structure

The WHO inherited and globalized a long effort to coordinate disease control across
borders (see [[disease-in-modern-history]]). Its governing body is the **World Health
Assembly**. Its early years were shaped by the [[cold-war|Cold War]]: the Soviet bloc
withdrew in 1949, so the WHO's first major campaign — the **malaria eradication
programme (from 1955)** — was a US-backed, Soviet-absent effort reflecting US strategic
priorities, and it ultimately failed.

## How It Shaped Events

The WHO's defining achievement is the **[[smallpox-eradication|eradication of
smallpox]]** — the only deliberate extinction of a human disease (last natural case
1977, certified 1980). Launched in 1959 after Soviet deputy health minister **Viktor
Zhdanov** (under Khrushchev's "peaceful coexistence") called for a global campaign,
and intensified from 1967, it became a rare arena of **superpower collaboration across
the ideological divide** — Soviet vaccine production and US funding and personnel — and
a landmark of internationalist public health. It became the model for later campaigns
(polio, Guinea worm).

## Significance

The WHO demonstrates the volume's argument that the post-1945 world was *connective*
as well as destructive: even at the height of the Cold War, a genuinely global,
cooperative institution could eradicate one of humankind's oldest scourges. It is also
a case study in the entanglement of [[international-law|international institutions]],
the Cold War, and decolonization.
