# Romanov Dynasty

The **House of Romanov** was the second reigning dynasty of Russia, ruling from the election of Michael Romanov in 1613 until the abdication of Nicholas II in 1917.

## Biography / Description
The Romanovs rose to power following the Time of Troubles, succeeding the extinct Rurikid dynasty. They presided over the consolidation and dramatic expansion of the Russian state, transforming it from a landlocked tsardom into the vast trans-continental Russian Empire.

The dynasty officially ended in March 1917 during the February Revolution, when Nicholas II abdicated, and the imperial family was subsequently executed by the Bolsheviks in July 1918.

## Succession of Monarchs
* **Consolidation (1613–1682):** Initiated by Michael I (r. 1613–1645) and his father Patriarch Philaret, followed by Alexis (r. 1645–1676), who codified serfdom in the *Ulozhenie* of 1649 and faced the church schism ([[raskol-schism|Raskol]]), and Theodore III (r. 1676–1682), who abolished the lineage service system ([[mestnichestvo|Mestnichestvo]]). "In 1619, on his return to Moscow, Filaret ascended the vacant patriarchal throne and, in practice, also acted as effective head of his son’s government." (lines 17452–17455). Under Alexis, "Chapter 19 of the Law Code (Sobornoe Ulozhenie) of 1649 made the merchantry into a privileged estate or caste." (lines ~15099–15102); "The Ulozhenie gave the government power over nearly all of society, thus consolidating its almost total control over two of the major economic factors (land and labour)." (lines 15363–15364). "The vast majority of the population in the years 1613–89 were serfs, perhaps 85 5 15 per cent... roughly five million inhabitants in 1613, perhaps nine or ten million in 1689." (lines 15186–15188). Popular revolts included 1648 Moscow uprising leading to Ulozhenie, 1650 Pskov/Novgorod, 1662 copper riot, Razin 1670-71. "All of these revolts, to a greater or lesser extent, assumed the form of ‘rebellions in the name of the tsar’: that is, they were directed primarily against the ‘traitor-boyars’ rather than against the reigning tsar." (lines 17177–17180).
* **Petrine Era & Successions (1682–1762):** Led by [[peter-the-great|Peter the Great]] (r. 1682–1725), who established the Russian Empire, built Saint Petersburg, and initiated radical Westernisation. After his death, succession was volatile, passing through Catherine I (r. 1725–1727), Peter II (r. 1727–1730; extinguishing the direct Romanov male line), Anne (r. 1730–1740; the *Bironovshchina*), Elizabeth (r. 1741–1762), and Peter III (r. 1762; establishing the Holstein-Gottorp-Romanov branch).

Peter + successors achieved military-fiscal state making Russia European great power. Empire burden on Russians (Lieven ll. 4449).

From range 8001–12000 (ch.8–10): Empire under Romanovs acquired "half a million Jews" via Polish partitions + "largest population of Poles"; "Russia also became the country with the largest population of Jews" (l8001–7803). Nicholas I Jewish draft/cantonists, kahal abolition; Alexander II selective integration; 1881 pogroms + quotas under Alexander III; revolutionary involvement (Sverdlov execution of Nicholas II family 1918 emblematic) (l8179–8395). Islam: Orenburg Assembly 1788, multiple muftiates, 'Muslim clergy' estate; Shamil resistance, 1916 revolts; "Muslims were involved in the political opposition that finally crushed the imperial regime in 1917" (l8481–8881). Poles/Ukrainians: constitutive builders of empire infrastructure/culture; "no Russian empire could survive without Ukraine" (l8021–8031). `produced:` 1917 collapse of dynasty/empire amid nationalities ferment.

* **The Golden Age & 19th Century (1762–1881):** Dominated by [[catherine-the-great|Catherine the Great]] (r. 1762–1796) and succeeded by her son Paul I (r. 1796–1801; assassinated). The 19th century rulers include [[alexander-i|Alexander I]] (r. 1801–1825; victor over Napoleon), [[nicholas-i|Nicholas I]] (r. 1825–1855; creator of the Third Section and Official Nationality), and [[alexander-ii|Alexander II]] (r. 1855–1881; "Tsar-Liberator" who emancipated the serfs and executed the Great Reforms before his assassination).
* **Late Empire (1881–1917):** Governed by [[alexander-iii|Alexander III]] (r. 1881–1894; counter-reforms and industrialization), and [[nicholas-ii|Nicholas II]] (r. 1894–1917; the last Tsar, overthrown in the February Revolution).

## Historical Significance
The Romanov dynasty built one of the largest empires in human history, bridging Europe and Asia. Their rule congealed the modern Russian identity, but their persistent refusal to yield autocratic privileges or reform the institution of serfdom in a timely manner led to immense social polarization, culminating in the revolutions of 1917.
