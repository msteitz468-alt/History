# Romanov Dynasty

The **House of Romanov** was the second reigning dynasty of Russia, ruling from the election of Michael Romanov in 1613 until the abdication of Nicholas II in 1917.

## Biography / Description
The Romanovs rose to power following the Time of Troubles, succeeding the extinct Rurikid dynasty. They presided over the consolidation and dramatic expansion of the Russian state, transforming it from a landlocked tsardom into the vast trans-continental Russian Empire.

The dynasty officially ended in March 1917 during the February Revolution, when Nicholas II abdicated, and the imperial family was subsequently executed by the Bolsheviks in July 1918.

## Succession of Monarchs
* **Consolidation (1613–1682):** Initiated by Michael I (r. 1613–1645) and his father Patriarch Philaret, followed by Alexis (r. 1645–1676), who codified serfdom in the *Ulozhenie* of 1649 and faced the church schism ([[raskol-schism|Raskol]]), and Theodore III (r. 1676–1682), who abolished the lineage service system ([[mestnichestvo|Mestnichestvo]]).
* **Petrine Era & Successions (1682–1762):** Led by [[peter-the-great|Peter the Great]] (r. 1682–1725), who established the Russian Empire, built Saint Petersburg, and initiated radical Westernisation. After his death, succession was volatile, passing through Catherine I (r. 1725–1727), Peter II (r. 1727–1730; extinguishing the direct Romanov male line), Anne (r. 1730–1740; the *Bironovshchina*), Elizabeth (r. 1741–1762), and Peter III (r. 1762; establishing the Holstein-Gottorp-Romanov branch).

* **The Golden Age & 19th Century (1762–1881):** Dominated by [[catherine-the-great|Catherine the Great]] (r. 1762–1796) and succeeded by her son Paul I (r. 1796–1801; assassinated). The 19th century rulers include [[alexander-i|Alexander I]] (r. 1801–1825; victor over Napoleon), [[nicholas-i|Nicholas I]] (r. 1825–1855; creator of the Third Section and Official Nationality), and [[alexander-ii|Alexander II]] (r. 1855–1881; "Tsar-Liberator" who emancipated the serfs and executed the Great Reforms before his assassination).
* **Late Empire (1881–1917):** Governed by [[alexander-iii|Alexander III]] (r. 1881–1894; counter-reforms and industrialization), and [[nicholas-ii|Nicholas II]] (r. 1894–1917; the last Tsar, overthrown in the February Revolution).

## Historical Significance
The Romanov dynasty built one of the largest empires in human history, bridging Europe and Asia. Their rule congealed the modern Russian identity, but their persistent refusal to yield autocratic privileges or reform the institution of serfdom in a timely manner led to immense social polarization, culminating in the revolutions of 1917.
