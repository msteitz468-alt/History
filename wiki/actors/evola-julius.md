# Julius Evola

Italian Traditionalist philosopher, racial theorist, and — before all that — one of Italy's foremost Dadaist painters. Evola's trajectory from avant-garde nihilism to the ideology of a "more radical Fascism" makes him the opening case study of [[griffin-modernism-and-fascism-2007|Griffin, *Modernism and Fascism* (2007)]], which treats him as living proof that the "revolt against the modern world" is itself a form of [[modernism]].

## From Dada to Tradition

A member of the pre-war Florentine avant-garde around *Leonardo* and *Lacerba*, briefly associated with the Futurist Giacomo Balla (though aloof from interventionism, "drawn more to the Prussian cult of discipline than the decadence of Britain and France"), Evola re-emerged after WWI as a leading Italian Dadaist (his 1920 painting *Abstraction*). In his autobiography *The Cinnabar Path* he explained Dada's appeal as "a general vision of life" of absolute emancipation — the "thrill of reawakening" in "a great negative work of destruction" needed to "clean up the filth" of modernity: Griffin's "active nihilism" exactly. Jeffrey Schnapp argues the Dada phase sowed "the seeds of that subsequent wholesale revolt against modernity, founded on the advocacy of elitism, spiritual racism, and pagan imperialism that propelled Evola – alone among the fascist theorists – beyond the catastrophe of Mussolini and Hitler into postwar prominence." The connective tissue is gnosis, "knowledge as vision" — which also explains his later essay extolling Ernst Jünger's *Der Arbeiter* and its *homo technologicus* (an "archeofuturism," in Guillaume Faye's later coinage).

## "Revolt Against the Modern World" as Modernism

*Rivolta contro il mondo moderno* (1934; German ed. 1935) taught that the West had reached the nadir of the Hindu **Kali Yuga** but stood on the threshold of a new **Krita Yuga** — *if* Fascist and Nazi leaders grasped the metaphysical dimension of their mission. Evola called his vision "a more radical Fascism, more fearless, a really absolute Fascism, made of pure force, impervious to any compromise" — and it was, Griffin notes, "deeply racist." Gottfried Benn's rapturous 1935 review ("Thus we were and so we shall be") shows its resonance in German modernist circles. On 2 February 1938 Karl Wiligut ("Himmler's Rasputin") reported approvingly to Himmler on Evola's SS lecture "The Restoration of the West on the Basis of the Original Aryan Spirit." His two 1941 racial treatises argued the "Aryan" New Man must unite body, mind, and soul, rejecting Nazi biological racism as itself a betrayal to "modern materialism and the materialistic science of Darwinism"; his Fascist doctrine of race was submitted personally to [[mussolini-benito|Mussolini]]. Griffin stresses that Evola "had no perceptible influence on mainstream Nazism or Fascism"; he remained a lifelong intellectual companion of Mircea Eliade, whose Iron Guard entanglement parallels his own case (see [[iron-guard]]).

## Counterfactual Significance

Negligible for the regimes themselves; enormous for the post-war extreme right. His attacks on "soulless democratic and communist Europe" made him "the guru of a new generation of post-war extreme right-wing intellectuals" — Giorgio Almirante of the MSI called him "our Marcuse only better." He is thus the chief conduit from interwar Traditionalism to the European New Right and later "archeofuturist" currents.

## Historiography

Schnapp on the Dada–Traditionalism continuity; Griffin (2007) on Evola as paradigm of the epiphanic-to-programmatic slide within modernism (contrasted with the left-modernist Ananda Coomaraswamy: with Evola "Traditionalism allied itself overtly to totalitarianism, misogyny, anti-Semitism, racism, imperialism, and biopolitics, and hence became the accomplice to the most elitist, uncompromising, and terroristic forms of Fascism and Nazism"). Guénon's original Traditionalism remains a distinct, politically more ambiguous lineage.
