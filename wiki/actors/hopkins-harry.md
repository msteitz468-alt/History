# Harry Hopkins (1890–1946)

Social worker, relief administrator, and the closest adviser of
[[roosevelt-franklin-d|Franklin D. Roosevelt]] — arguably the most powerful
unelected official in American history to that point. Hopkins ran the New
Deal's relief apparatus (FERA and CWA from 1933; the WPA from 1935, spending
billions and employing millions), served as Commerce Secretary 1938–40, and
then, living in the White House itself from May 1940, became Roosevelt's
personal envoy and de facto national-security aide: first emissary to Churchill
(January 1941) and to Stalin (July 1941), architect-administrator of
[[lend-lease|Lend-Lease]], and a principal at the wartime conferences from
Placentia Bay through [[yalta-conference-1945|Yalta]].

**Role in the network.** Hopkins is the personal hinge between the domestic
[[new-deal|New Deal]] and wartime coalition diplomacy — the same man who ran
work relief negotiated with Stalin. His WPA also made him a central figure in
the era's political controversies: charges that relief was used to build a
political machine ("tax and tax, spend and spend, elect and elect," a remark
attributed to him that he denied) were a staple of contemporary opposition.

**Counterfactual significance.** Without Hopkins, Lend-Lease administration
and the early Anglo-American-Soviet linkage would have lacked their key
personal channel; Churchill judged his contribution to Allied cohesion
war-winning ("Lord Root of the Matter"). Domestically, federal work relief
would likely have taken a more decentralized, state-administered form (the
Hopkins–Ickes rivalry over relief vs. public works shaped its actual mix).

**Biographical spine** (per Flynn 1948, corroborated by the mainstream
literature): born Sioux City, Iowa, a harness-maker's son; Grinnell College;
New York settlement-house and public-health social work; ran Straus's New York
TERA relief administration under Governor Roosevelt from 1931; FERA
administrator 1933 (at Eleanor Roosevelt's urging), CWA that winter, WPA from
1935 — Hopkins estimated 25 million people drew their living from the WPA;
after Commerce (1938–40), lived in the White House sending directives to
cabinet officers signed "H.H."

**The patronage charge.** The documentary core of the case against Hopkins's
WPA is a Senate committee report on the 1938 primaries (Kentucky, Pennsylvania,
Tennessee, Cook County) documenting relief jobs conditioned on party
registration and campaign "contributions"; his deputy Aubrey Williams told a
WPA conference, "We've got to stick together; we've got to keep our friends in
power." Flynn builds his chapter on this report plus Marquis Childs's
*Saturday Evening Post* profiles; the mainstream literature accepts the abuses
were real (the 1939 Hatch Act was the congressional response) while rejecting
the reduction of the whole relief program to machine politics. See
[[roosevelt-and-new-deal-revisionism]].

## Historiography

Mainstream treatment is anchored by Robert Sherwood's Pulitzer-winning
*Roosevelt and Hopkins* (1948) — sympathetic, built on Hopkins's own papers —
and by his prominence in Smith, *[[smith-fdr-2007|FDR]]*. Revisionist accounts
(Flynn, *[[flynn-roosevelt-myth-1948|The Roosevelt Myth]]*, where he figures as
"Harry the Hop," chief of the "Happy Hot Dogs" patronage network) cast him as
the emblem of spending-as-politics and of amateur personal diplomacy; Cold
War-era writers added (never-substantiated) charges of excessive
accommodation of Moscow. See [[roosevelt-and-new-deal-revisionism]].
