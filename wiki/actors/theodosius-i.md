# Theodosius I ("the Great")

The Spanish-born general raised to the purple after **[[battle-of-adrianople-378|Adrianople]]** —
**Augustus of the East from 379**, briefly sole ruler, and the emperor who made **Nicene Christianity the
official religion of the Roman state**, settled the Goths inside the empire, and at his death (395)
**divided the empire permanently** between his sons. The last to rule both halves. Treated in
[[cameron-cah-v13-1998|CAH XIII]] Ch. 3 (**John Curran**).

## Accession and the Gothic settlement

Recalled from retirement on his family's Spanish estates after the death of his father, Theodosius was
crowned by **Gratian at Sirmium (January 379)** to retrieve the disaster of Adrianople. Unable to destroy
the Goths militarily, he rebuilt the shattered army with **harsh conscription** and unprecedented
**barbarian recruitment**, then concluded the epochal **foedus of 3 October 382**: the Goths were settled
on Roman soil (Lower Moesia, Thrace) as **autonomous allies under their own chiefs** — fighting for Rome
as *foederati* but retaining their native structure. Heather calls this perhaps "the most momentous
*foedus* in Roman history"; it set the template for barbarian settlement within the empire.

## The Nicene state church

Theodosius was a committed **Nicene** Christian. His edict ***Cunctos populos*** (380) defined orthodoxy
as the faith of Rome and Alexandria; he deposed the Homoian bishop of Constantinople and summoned the
**Council of Constantinople (381)** — which reaffirmed Nicaea, **sealed the defeat of Arianism**,
anathematized dissenters, and declared Constantinople second in honour after Rome ("the New Rome"). After
his absolution by **Ambrose of Milan** for the **massacre of Thessalonica (390)** — a landmark assertion
of ecclesiastical authority over the emperor — Theodosius issued the sweeping **anti-pagan laws of
391–392**, closing all temples and banning all sacrifice (public and private). Paganism was now illegal.

## The usurpers and the Frigidus

Theodosius twice intervened in the West against usurpers who had toppled the house of Valentinian:
**Magnus Maximus** (killer of Gratian, defeated 388) and the puppet **Eugenius**, behind whom stood the
Frankish *magister militum* **Arbogast**. The decisive **battle of the Frigidus (6 September 394)** —
where Eugenius marched under images of Jupiter and Hercules, and a providential storm broke his line —
was cast as the triumph of Christianity over a last pagan rally. Victorious and sole ruler, Theodosius
died at Milan months later (**17 January 395**).

## The division of 395

At his death the empire passed to his two young sons under guardians — **Arcadius** in the East and the
child **Honorius** in the West (under the half-Vandal generalissimo **[[stilicho|Stilicho]]**). This
**division of 395 proved permanent**: the two halves never again shared a single ruler, and their
divergent fates — the East's survival, the West's fragmentation — flow from this moment.

## Significance

Theodosius is the emperor who **fixed the religious and political shape of late antiquity**: Nicene
Christianity as the state faith, paganism outlawed, the church (in Ambrose) able to discipline the
emperor, and the empire permanently bipartite. His Gothic *foedus* — a pragmatic response to catastrophe —
embedded autonomous barbarians within the frontiers, while the division of 395 launched the divergent
histories of Byzantium and the post-Roman West. "The Great" is earned, but its legacy is double-edged.
