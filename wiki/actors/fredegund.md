# Fredegund

**Queen of [[neustria|Neustria]]**, wife of Chilperic I, and the instigator of the dynastic feud
that consumed the Merovingian house for three generations — the Neustrian counterpart and arch-rival
of [[brunhild|Brunhild]].

## Overview

Fredegund rose from low birth to become Chilperic I's queen. To secure her position she instigated the
murder of **Galswintha**, Chilperic's Visigothic wife and [[brunhild|Brunhild]]'s sister: "egged on by
Fredegund and fearful that Galswintha might try to return home with her dowry, he had her killed." This
killing opened the long Neustrian–Austrasian feud — "a bitter feud initiated by Chilperic's wife
Fredegund and her arch rival Brunechildis."

## Method and reputation

Fredegund's reputation for ruthlessness is fixed in the sources by episodes such as the fate of
**Leudast, count of Tours** — the great enemy of [[gregory-of-tours|Gregory of Tours]] — who "was
tortured to death on the orders of Queen Fredegund." She defended the Neustrian throne for her son
Chlothar II, who would ultimately win the whole feud in 613.

## Counterfactual significance

Where [[brunhild|Brunhild]] stood for a centralising, Romanising monarchy, Fredegund represents the
politics of faction and family violence that the aristocracy could exploit. Her son's eventual victory —
sealed by Brunhild's execution and the **Edict of Paris** — passed the initiative decisively to the
magnates whose rise the [[pippinids|Pippinids]] would crown.

## Historiography

Almost everything known of Fredegund comes through [[gregory-of-tours|Gregory of Tours]], who had
personal reason to loathe her circle; her portrait is correspondingly dark and must be read as
partisan. See [[geary-before-france-germany-1988]].
