# Sami (Sámi) People

The indigenous Finno-Ugric-speaking hunting, fishing, and (later) reindeer-herding
people of the Scandinavian and Fenno-Scandian north. Per
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]], most
researchers now hold that Sami ethnic and linguistic identity developed **gradually
in situ** among northern hunting-fishing groups as a consequence of a distinctive
way of life, rather than through a single migration; the proto-Sami / proto-Finnic
linguistic split is argued to have occurred in Finland during the late Stone and
Bronze Ages, and proto-Sami / proto-Scandinavian differentiation in northern Norway
and Sweden by the pre-Roman Iron Age.

## Significance and Relations

The huge Sami burial ground at **Mortensnes** (Varangerfjord) spans an unbroken
period from before the birth of Christ to the 17th–18th centuries, giving rare
archaeological continuity. From the Viking Age and early Middle Ages north Norwegian
chieftains collected the **"Finn tax"** (*finneskatt*) from the Sami — possibly
rooted in a Merovingian-period redistributive economy — and Sami furs fed the
long-distance trade through emporia such as [[birka|Birka]]. The Sami remained
largely outside Christianity through the high Middle Ages; missions begun under
[[hakon-hakonarson|Hákon Hákonarson]] (1217–63) and continued into the early modern
period had little success. See [[scandinavian-prehistory]],
[[christianization-of-scandinavia]].
