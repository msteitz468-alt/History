## Role

Seleucus I "Nicator" ("Victor," c. 358–281 BCE) was a Macedonian officer of
[[alexander-the-great|Alexander]] who founded the **[[seleucid-empire|Seleucid
kingdom]]**, the largest of the successor states, spanning at its height from the
Aegean to the borders of India. Notably, he was the **only Diadoch to keep his Iranian
wife** (Apama, mother of Antiochus I) from Alexander's mass marriages at Susa — a fact
of dynastic and symbolic weight for a kingdom that ruled vast Iranian populations.

## Decisions and Significance

- **Rise from Babylon**: a commander of the elite hypaspists and one of
  [[perdiccas|Perdiccas']] assassins, he was granted **Babylonia** at Triparadisus
  (321). Driven out by [[antigonus-i-monophthalmus|Antigonus]] (315), he sheltered with
  [[ptolemy-i-soter|Ptolemy]], helped win the battle of Gaza (312), and **recovered
  Babylon** the same year — the epoch from which the **[[seleucid-era]]** (the first
  continuous year-count in history) was reckoned.
- **The upper satrapies and India**: from Babylon he reconquered the Iranian "upper
  satrapies" that Antigonus had held, then campaigned east to the Indus, where he came
  to terms with the Mauryan **[[mauryan-empire|Chandragupta]]** (c. 303): ceding the
  easternmost provinces (Paropamisadae, Arachosia, Gedrosia) in exchange for **c. 500
  war-elephants** — the force that proved decisive at Ipsus.
- **[[battle-of-ipsus-301|Ipsus]] (301)** and after: with [[lysimachus]] he destroyed
  Antigonus, taking Syria — though Ptolemy seized Coele-Syria, the seed of the
  [[syrian-wars|Syrian Wars]]. Master of the north Syrian *Seleucis*, he founded the
  **Syrian tetrapolis** (Antioch-on-the-Orontes, Seleuceia-in-Pieria, Laodicea, Apamea)
  and **Seleuceia-on-the-Tigris**, the twin hearts of his realm.
- **[[battle-of-corupedium-281|Corupedium]] (281)**: late in life he defeated and
  killed [[lysimachus]] at Corupedium, briefly reuniting most of Alexander's Asian and
  European inheritance — only to be assassinated months later by
  [[ptolemy-ceraunus|Ptolemy Ceraunus]] as he crossed into Europe to claim Macedon. His
  son [[antiochus-i-soter|Antiochus I]] (whom he had wisely made co-regent) inherited
  the kingdom.

## Counterfactual Significance

Seleucus built the most ethnically and geographically diverse of the Hellenistic states
and faced its defining structural problem: governing an immense, multi-ethnic Asian
expanse through Greek cities and military colonies (see [[seleucid-colonization]]). His
foundation of [[antioch|Antioch]] and Seleuceia, his Iranian marriage, and the era that
bears his name make him the true architect of the [[seleucid-empire|Hellenistic Near
East]]. Detailed treatment of the kingdom belongs to CAH VII.1 ch. 6 — see
[[seleucid-empire]].
