# Saxo Grammaticus

Danish cleric and historian (c. 1150–c. 1220), author of the **Gesta Danorum**, the
great Latin history of the Danes in sixteen books, composed under the patronage of
Archbishop **Absalon** of Lund and completed in the first decades of the 13th
century. Per [[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003|the Cambridge History]],
Saxo wrote in an ambitious classical rhetorical style (modelled on Virgil and Horace)
and drew on Eddic poetry and Icelandic saga tradition for the legendary early books.

## Significance

The *Gesta Danorum* served as the definitive history of [[denmark|Denmark]] for
centuries and supplied the **Amleth** story that became Shakespeare's *Hamlet*. Its
ideological orientation is debated — whether Saxo primarily championed the
centralising [[valdemar-dynasty|Valdemarian monarchy]] and its new service
aristocracy (Kværndrup) or the Church and Archbishop Absalon (B. Sawyer). Together
with [[snorri-sturluson|Snorri's]] *Heimskringla*, the *Gesta Danorum* is one of the
two monumental national histories produced in 13th-century Scandinavia. See
[[saga-literature]].
