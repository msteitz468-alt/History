# Dmitry Donskoy
## Biography / Description
**Dmitry Ivanovich**, known as **Dmitry Donskoy** ("of the Don"), was the Grand Prince of Moscow (r. 1359–1389 CE). Succeeding his father Ivan II at the age of nine, Dmitry's reign witnessed the transition of Moscow from a cautious tributary appanage into the military champion of all Russian lands. 

He is legendary for leading the first unified Russian coalition to defeat a major Mongol army at the Battle of Kulikovo in 1380, breaking the myth of Mongol invincibility.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Unification and Defenses of Moscow:** Under the guidance of Metropolitan Alexis, Dmitry consolidated Muscovite authority in the northeast, successfully defending Moscow from Tver and Lithuanian campaigns. In 1367 CE, he replaced Moscow’s wooden Kremlin walls with stone, making the citadel virtually impregnable.
* **Battle of the Vozha River (1378 CE):** Dmitry achieved the first significant Russian victory over a Mongol force on the Vozha River, signaling Moscow's growing defiance of the Horde.
* **Battle of Kulikovo (1380 CE):** In response to a massive invasion force mobilized by the Mongol strongman Mamai (allied with Lithuania), Dmitry seized the initiative, crossed the Don, and routed the Mongol army on September 8, 1380. Dmitry himself fought on foot in the thick of the battle and was found unconscious among the dead (l. 1519).
* **Survival of Tokhtamysh's Raid (1382 CE):** Only two years after Kulikovo, Khan Tokhtamysh of the Horde sacked and burned Moscow by ruse. Dmitry, caught unprepared, was forced to resubmit to the khan and pay tribute in exchange for confirmation as Grand Prince.
* **Princely Consolidation:** Dmitry utilized his final years to rebuild Moscow and reinforce his authority over Tver, Riazan, and other Russian principalities.

## Historical Significance
Dmitry Donskoy's victory at Kulikovo was a watershed moment in Russian history, establishing Moscow as the political and moral leader of the Russian people in the struggle against foreign oppression. Although the Mongol yoke was not fully overthrown for another century, the Battle of Kulikovo proved that Russian unification could break the power of the steppe. 

Dmitry also strengthened the principle of direct succession from father to son in Moscow, bypassing the chaotic Kievan rota system. He was canonized by the Russian Orthodox Church.
