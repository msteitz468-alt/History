# Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain

Union colonel (1828–1914), a former Bowdoin College professor who commanded the 20th Maine Infantry at Gettysburg. On 2 July 1863, positioned at the extreme left of the Union line on Little Round Top, Chamberlain's regiment repelled repeated Confederate assaults by Alabama troops attempting to turn the Union flank; when his men ran low on ammunition, Chamberlain ordered a downhill bayonet charge that broke the attacking force and secured the position.

The defense of Little Round Top became one of the most celebrated small-unit actions of the Civil War, credited with preventing a Confederate flanking success that could have unraveled the Union position on the battle's second day. Chamberlain received the Medal of Honor for the action (awarded 1893) and went on to a long postwar career as Governor of Maine and president of Bowdoin College. He was also present at Appomattox, where he was designated to receive the formal surrender of Confederate infantry and famously ordered his men to salute the surrendering Confederates.

## Significance

Chamberlain's defense of Little Round Top exemplifies how tactical improvisation and small-unit determination at a single flank could shape the outcome of a battle of this scale.
