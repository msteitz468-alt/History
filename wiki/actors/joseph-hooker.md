# Joseph Hooker ("Fighting Joe")

Union major general (1814–1879), commander of the Army of the Potomac from January to June 1863. A West Point graduate and Mexican-American War veteran, Hooker earned his nickname for aggressive combat leadership at division and corps level earlier in the war, and reorganized the Army of the Potomac's administration, supply, and cavalry corps to considerable effect after taking command.

At Chancellorsville (April–May 1863) Hooker executed a promising opening maneuver, crossing the Rappahannock and Rapidan to outflank Lee's army, but lost his nerve after initial contact, ordering a defensive consolidation around Chancellorsville that surrendered the initiative. Robert E. Lee and Stonewall Jackson exploited the pause, and Jackson's flank attack on the exposed Union XI Corps routed Hooker's right and forced a Union retreat despite Union numerical superiority. Hooker resigned command shortly before Gettysburg amid friction with Washington over strategy and reinforcements.

## Significance

Chancellorsville is often cited as Lee's tactical masterpiece and Hooker's greatest failure of nerve — a case study in how numerical and positional advantage can be squandered by loss of confidence at the decisive moment.
