# Benedict IX (Pope; intermittent 1032–1048)

## Overview

**Benedict IX** (r. 1032–45; briefly 1047–48), third successive Tusculum-family pope after uncles Benedict VIII and John XIX, **closed the papacy’s worst days** in [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]]’s judgment. His chaotic exits, sale/resignation of the office, and the **Synod of Sutri** (1046) under Henry III opened the path to the reform papacy from 1049.

## Career and charges

Alleged accession under age 12 is exaggerated. Charged with theft, murder, and “unspeakable deeds.” Expelled and briefly replaced by Sylvester III (January 1045), restored by March. Resigned 1 May 1045 to godfather John Gratian (**Gregory VI**) for an enormous sum (also alleged conscience or marriage motives; a cousin then refused him).

## Synod of Sutri (1046) and aftermath

Three claimants prompted Henry III’s synod: Sylvester degraded; Benedict treated as resigned; Gregory VI resigned. After Clement II’s death Benedict reoccupied the throne for about eight months (from 8 November 1047) until imperial troops installed Damasus II. He may have died penitent at Grottaferrata (d. 1055/56).

## Significance

“With the close of the pontificate of Benedict IX the worst days in the long history of the papacy came to an end.” The decade after Sutri leads into the reforming line (including, later, the world of Gregory VII). Logan also notes that the tenth century had already seen northern reforming movements treated in his next chapter.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Sutri’s clearing of three claimants, Henry III’s reform appointments and the 1049+ reform papacy would have faced a still-crowded Roman field; Tusculan family capture might have prolonged the nadir.

## Historiography

Reform-era sources are hostile; Logan trims childhood-accession legend while accepting the sale-of-office core and Sutri’s structural importance. Prism pope for the end of Ch.6 disarray narrative, after [[formosus]] and [[john-xii]].

## From Logan, History of the Church in the Middle Ages (2013)

Tusculan third pope → scandals and double expulsion/restoration → sale to Gratian/Gregory VI → three-way schism → Henry III at Sutri → brief reoccupation → end of worst papal days; reform path open.
