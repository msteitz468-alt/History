# Přemyslid Bohemia

The **Přemyslid** dynasty unified Bohemia and made it, with [[boleslaw-i-chrobry|Piast Poland]], one of [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]]'s (Strzelczyk) "two examples of successful western Slavonic state-formation" — though, unlike Poland, Bohemia developed as a **duchy within the orbit of the German Reich**.

## Formation

Bohemia's consolidation was speeded by pressure from **Great Moravia** (whose collapse c. 906 under Magyar pressure freed Bohemia but removed its counterweight to east Francia) and from the **east Frankish kingdom**. The first historical Přemyslid, **Bořivoj** (d. c. 889), centred on **Prague**; tradition (the Legend of Christian) has him baptised by **Methodius**. His successors steered between Moravia, the Polabian Slavs, and the rising **[[ottonian-dynasty|Liudolfings]]**:

- **Wenceslas (Václav) I** (c. 921–935) fostered ties with Bavaria to counter Henry I, who defeated him and imposed tribute; he was murdered by his brother **Boleslav I** and venerated almost at once as a saint — becoming **Bohemia's national patron**.
- **Boleslav I** (935–c. 972) subdued the whole Bohemian plain, kept his distance from the Germans (letting Magyars pass through his lands), but **submitted to [[otto-i|Otto I]] in 950** and fought beside him against the Magyars in 955 — fixing a long-lasting **dependence on Germany**.
- **Boleslav II** (c. 972–999) obtained the **bishopric of Prague** (under the archbishop of Mainz, c. 973) and, in **995, violently eliminated the rival Slavnikid clan at Libice** — completing political unification but driving the Slavnikid bishop of Prague, **Adalbert (Vojtěch)**, into the exile and martyrdom (997) that made him a cult-figure of [[synod-of-gniezno-1000|Otto III's Gniezno]] programme.

## Crisis and Reconstruction

Under **Boleslav III** the state nearly collapsed (c. 1000–1003) amid dynastic murder and noble revolt; **[[boleslaw-i-chrobry|Bolesław Chrobry]] of Poland** intervened, seized Prague, and ruled it directly (1003) until **[[henry-ii-holy-roman-emperor|Henry II]]** — refused homage by Bolesław for Bohemia — drove the Polish garrison out and restored the Přemyslids **Jaromír** and **Oldřich** as imperial vassals. Under **Břetislav I** (1035–1055) the Bohemian monarchy was completed: he received **Bohemia and (for the first time) Moravia as imperial fiefs at Bamberg**, raided Poland in its post-1038 weakness (sacking Gniezno and carrying off St Adalbert's relics, 1039), codified the **seniorate succession** (linking it to the indivisibility of the realm), reformed the coinage (the Prague *grivna*), and patronised new monasteries (Sázava, Rajhrad).

## Significance

The Přemyslids built a durable, **Latin-Christian Bohemian state** that — unlike Poland — accepted **integration into the Reich** as an imperial fief while retaining internal autonomy, a status that defined Bohemia's medieval position. The Prague–Gniezno rivalry, the Slavnikid massacre, and the cult of the martyrs **Wenceslas** and **Adalbert** all proved foundational for Czech identity.
