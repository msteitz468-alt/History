# John T. Flynn (1882–1964)

American journalist and economic writer whose career traces the arc of the Old
Right: a muckraking liberal economist and *New Republic* financial columnist
(from 1933) who broke with [[roosevelt-franklin-d|Franklin Roosevelt]] over the
NRA, deficit finance, and rearmament, chaired the New York chapter of the
America First Committee, and ended as a McCarthy-era anticommunist pamphleteer.

## Role and Significance

Flynn's importance to historiography rests on two books. *As We Go Marching*
(1944) — see [[flynn-as-we-go-marching-1944]] — is the most systematic
contemporary statement of the [[as-we-go-marching-thesis|thesis]] that fascism
is an economic-policy syndrome (deficit spending, autarky, corporatism,
militarism, imperialism) which the [[new-deal|New Deal]] was replicating in
America. *The Roosevelt Myth* (1948) — see [[flynn-roosevelt-myth-1948]] —
founded the popular revisionist attack on FDR's reputation
([[roosevelt-and-new-deal-revisionism]]).

His anti-militarist, anti-imperialist strain made him legible to both the
1970s New Left (Ronald Radosh's 1973 recovery) and the later libertarian
movement, while his 1950s work (*The Road Ahead*, *The Lattimore Story*)
tied him to McCarthyism — a double reception that itself figures in
[[fascism-and-the-political-left]].

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Flynn, New Deal revisionism loses its most energetic popularizer, but
the structural critique existed independently ([[hayek-road-to-serfdom-1944|Hayek]],
the Beardian isolationists). His distinctive contribution was fusing fiscal
analysis of Italy and Germany with American self-criticism — an argument
otherwise largely absent from 1940s mainstream debate.

## Historiography

Radosh (1973) reads Flynn as a serious anti-imperialist wrongly reduced to a
reactionary; mainstream FDR scholarship treats his fascism analogy as category
error (see [[roosevelt-and-new-deal-revisionism]] and
[[fascism-and-the-political-left]] for the positions). Both sides agree the
1944 book is his most analytical work and the 1950s output his weakest.
