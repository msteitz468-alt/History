# The Khazar Khaganate
## Biography / Description
The **Khazar Khaganate** (or Khazar state) was a powerful, cosmopolitan Turkic-speaking state that dominated the lower Volga, the northern Caucasus, and the southeastern Russian steppe from the seventh century to the late tenth century. Originating as semi-nomads from Asia, the Khazars developed a highly commercialized empire that served as the crossroads between Europe, Asia, the Byzantine Empire, and the Islamic Caliphate.

Uniquely among medieval empires, the Khazar ruler (the *khakan*) and the ruling class converted to Judaism in the eighth and ninth centuries, adding an exceptional chapter to their history.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Bulwark Against Islam:** The Khazars fought long and bitter wars against the Arab Caliphate in the Caucasus, successfully stopping the northward expansion of Islam into eastern Europe.
* **Promotion of Urbanization and Trade:** Although originally semi-nomads, the Khazars promoted the building of major trading towns along key water routes. These included their capital **Itil** (near the mouth of the Volga), **Samandar** (in the northern Caucasus), and **Sarkil** (a fortress on the lower Don).
* **Cosmopolitanism and Religious Tolerance:** Khazaria was renowned for its legal tolerance. Pagans, Muslims, Christians, and Jews mingled freely and enjoyed autonomy, living under their own respective laws. 
* **Standing Army:** The Khazars were among the first steppe peoples to institute a permanent, paid armed force, which was financed by transit duties and tributes levied on subject peoples (including several East Slavic tribes).

## Historical Significance
The Khazar Khaganate represents a distinct model of steppe development characterized by commercial sophistication and political crystallization. Riasanovsky emphasizes that their prosperity "dependend less on the resources of the country than on its favorable position across important trade-routes" (l. 631-635). 

The Khazars established a period of relative stability (often termed the *Pax Khazarica*) that allowed the East Slavs to develop agriculture and trade in the forest-steppe zone. Several East Slavic tribes paid tribute to the Khazars at the dawn of Kievan history.

However, the Khazar state was decisively weakened when Prince **[[sviatoslav-i|Sviatoslav I]]** of Kiev launched a devastating eastern campaign in 965 CE. The Russians smashed the Khazar army, sacked Itil and Samandar, and captured Sarkil. Though the Khazar state lingered for another half-century, it never recovered and was soon replaced by the more hostile and primitive Pechenegs.
