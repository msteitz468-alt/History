# Wang Mang

**Wang Mang** (45 BCE – 23 CE) was the Han official and consort-clan magnate who **usurped the
throne** and founded the short-lived **Xin (新, "New") dynasty** (9–23 CE), interrupting the Han and
dividing it into the **Former (Western)** and **Later (Eastern)** Han. From the
[[cambridge-history-of-china|Cambridge History of China]], Vol. 1 (the Qin-Han volume; Bielenstein's
chapter on Wang Mang and the Han restoration).

## Rise and usurpation

Wang Mang rose through the **Wang family**, the dominant consort-clan at the late Former Han court
(his aunt was the empress dowager). As the CHC stresses, late-Former-Han politics turned on the
fortunes of **imperial consort-families**, and Wang Mang mastered that system: serving as regent
for a succession of child emperors, cultivating a reputation for Confucian rectitude and modesty,
and accumulating titles until, in **9 CE**, he set the puppet aside and declared himself emperor of
a new dynasty — a rare usurpation justified not by conquest but by the claim that the Han had lost
the **[[mandate-of-heaven|Mandate of Heaven]]**.

## The reformist programme

Wang Mang is the classic example of the **"reformist" (改造派)** tendency the CHC identifies in Han
statecraft — politicians who looked to the idealized **Zhou** past rather than to Qin precedents. He
launched sweeping, archaizing measures: an attempt to **nationalize land** ("the king's fields,"
ending great estates and limiting holdings), restrictions on **slavery**, state monopolies, and
repeated, destabilizing **currency reforms**. Intended to restore a Confucian golden age and curb
the magnates, the measures were poorly administered, alienated the powerful, and were largely
rescinded.

## Fall

Catastrophe sealed his fate: a massive **Yellow River** change of course and flooding displaced
huge populations and triggered famine, feeding the **Red Eyebrows** peasant uprising, while Han
loyalist gentry rose against him. Wang Mang was killed when rebels stormed Chang'an in **23 CE**;
after further fighting the Han was restored under **Emperor Guangwu** (the Later Han, from 25 CE).

## Significance and historiography

In the traditional Chinese historiography that the CHC both transmits and re-examines, Wang Mang
is the archetypal **usurper** — vilified by the *Han shu* (whose hostile portrait, from a restored-
Han viewpoint, is almost our only source). Modern historians debate whether he was a cynical
power-seeker, a sincere Confucian utopian whose reforms were ahead of their time, or a figure
trapped by the contradictions of consort-clan politics and natural disaster. Either way, his reign
is the hinge between the Former and Later Han and a textbook case of the **[[mandate-of-heaven|
dynastic-cycle]]** ideology in action.
