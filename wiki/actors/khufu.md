# Khufu

**Khufu** (full name *Khnum-khufu*, Horus Medjedu; r. ~2589–2566 BCE), the second king
of the 4th Dynasty and son of [[actors/sneferu|Sneferu]], built the **Great Pyramid at
[[places/giza|Giza]]** — the largest pyramid ever raised and the supreme monument of the
[[actors/old-kingdom-egypt|Old Kingdom]] state.

## The Great Pyramid

The Great Pyramid has "a ground plan of 230 sq. m. and a height of 146.5 m." Its burial
chamber lies high in the core of the structure rather than underground. "The usually
quoted figure of some 2,300,000 building blocks averaging about 2.5 tons … may be
approximate, but probably not far off the mark." The project was overseen by the vizier
**Hemiunu** (whose father, Prince Nefermaat, had been Sneferu's vizier) — a member of
the royal family, illustrating the 4th-Dynasty pattern of staffing the highest offices
with princes (see [[concepts/egyptian-bureaucracy]]).

Around the monument were a dismantled **cedarwood boat** 43.4 m long (recovered and
restored from a pit on the south side), three subsidiary pyramids of his queens, and the
cache of his mother **Hetepheres** — intact furniture but no body. Objects bearing
Khufu's name reach as far as **Byblos** and gneiss quarries north-west of Abu Simbel,
marking the geographic reach of the Old Kingdom state.

## Reputation

The colossal scale of the pyramid may itself have shaped Khufu's later reputation as a
**heartless despot**, "hinted at in Egyptian literature and reported by Herodotus" — the
inverse of his father's benevolent memory. The contrast is a caution against reading the
monuments as a guide to a king's actual character.
