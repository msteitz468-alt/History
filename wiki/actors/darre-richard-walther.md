# Richard Walther Darré

Nazi agrarian ideologue, coiner of "blood and soil," Reich Minister of Food
and Agriculture and "Reich Farmers' Leader" (1933–42), and first head of the
SS Race and Settlement Head Office (RuSHA) — the channel through which his
racial-peasant mysticism shaped [[himmler-heinrich|Himmler]]'s vision of an SS
racial aristocracy. His books (*The Peasantry as the Life-Source of the Nordic
Race*, 1928; *New Aristocracy from Blood and Soil*, 1929) located German
racial quality in the medieval peasantry. As minister he ran the corporatist
**Reich Food Estate** (est. 13 September 1933, ~9 million farmers and workers)
and authored the **Reich Entailed Farm Law** (29 September 1933): farms of
7.5–125 ha (700,000 farms, 22% of the total) made unsellable, unmortgageable,
and indivisible, passing to a single heir. Evans documents its effects as the
opposite of intent — disinherited children fled to the cities, credit dried up
(entailed farms could not serve as collateral), engagements were broken for
lack of dowries — while price controls bred black markets and open peasant
hostility even in pro-Nazi regions. Autarky in food was never approached
(fats/butter ~50% short; >90% of vegetable oils imported). Sidelined by his
deputy Herbert Backe and by [[goring-hermann|Göring]]'s Four-Year Plan, and
outflanked ideologically by the technocratic wing that wanted industry plus a
reordered countryside, Darré was effectively powerless well before his formal
removal in 1942. Convicted at the Nuremberg Ministries trial; died 1953.
