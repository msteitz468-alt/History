# Friedrich Ebert

Social Democratic party leader, chairman of the Council of People's Deputies
after the [[november-revolution-1918|November Revolution]], and first President
of the [[weimar-republic|Weimar Republic]] (1919–1925). A saddler's son and
trade-union functionary, Ebert steered the SPD through war and revolution
toward parliamentary democracy rather than a soviet-style state.

## Role and Decisions

Per Evans ([[evans-coming-of-third-reich-2003]]): born 1871, a tailor's son
who became a saddler and rose through the unions and the Bremen SPD (pub as
organizing hub, 1893; national party secretary 1905; Reichstag 1912; joint
party leader after Bebel's death in 1913). A pragmatic administrator rather
than an orator, he drove the SPD's organizational efficiency and its 1912
electoral triumph, and pushed the expulsion of the war's opponents that
produced the 1917 Independent (USPD) split. He accepted the Kaiser's
abdication only to forestall social revolution — "I don't want that, indeed I
hate it like sin" — then led the Council of People's Deputies and forced
through the 1919 Constituent Assembly election against the council-based
alternative.

As President he invoked Article 48 on 136 occasions, deposed the elected
left governments of Saxony and Thuringia (1923), and retroactively legitimized
summary executions of Ruhr "Red Army" members in 1920. Evans's judgment
(attributed): his "excessive use, and occasional misuse, of the Article
widened its application to a point where it became a potential threat to
democratic institutions" — a precedent of asymmetric enforcement (against the
left, "virtually unused" against the right) inherited by
[[hindenburg-paul-von|Hindenburg]] and the presidential cabinets after 1930.

## Death by Vilification

Ebert was subjected to sustained right-wing defamation — a bathing-trunks
photo scandal, financial smears — against which he filed 173 unsuccessful
libel suits. In 1924 a court fined a man who had called him a "traitor" a
token 10 marks, effectively endorsing the charge because Ebert had maintained
contact with striking munitions workers in 1918 (in fact to end the strike).
Consumed by the fight to clear his name, he neglected a ruptured appendix and
died at 54 on 28 February 1925 — opening the presidency to Hindenburg.

## Counterfactual Significance

Evans's material supports two counterfactual hinges: (1) had Ebert lived past
1925, the presidency need not have passed to a monarchist field marshal with
no faith in democratic institutions; (2) his Article 48 practice normalized
the instrument [[bruning-heinrich|Brüning]], [[papen-franz-von|Papen]], and
[[schleicher-kurt-von|Schleicher]] later used to de-parliamentarize the
Republic.

## Historiography

Evans treats Ebert sympathetically but critically — the Free Corps bargain and
the Article 48 precedents are presented as tragic self-undermining choices of
a genuine democrat (see [[november-revolution-1918]], [[freikorps]]).
