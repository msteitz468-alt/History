# Sir Henry Clinton (1730–1795)

British commander in chief in North America from May 1778 to 1782, inheriting the war just as
French intervention transformed it. Clinton had urged a bolder envelopment at
[[battle-of-bunker-hill-1775|Bunker Hill]] in 1775 and was "capable of showing great boldness,"
but as supreme commander he was "perennially fearful for his headquarters" at New York.

His one great offensive success was the **[[siege-of-charleston-1780|siege of Charleston]]**
(1780), capturing an entire American army — "one of the worst American disasters of the war." But
he then handed the southern command to [[cornwallis-charles|Cornwallis]] and returned north,
and his **3 June 1780 proclamation** revoking South Carolina paroles — forcing the population to
take up arms for the king or be treated as enemies — was, the *West Point History* judges, "a
major blunder" that drove the backcountry into rebellion.

From 1778 Clinton "dreaded the prospect of the American army cooperating with the French navy to
capture an exposed British outpost" — precisely the nightmare that materialized at
**[[siege-of-yorktown-1781|Yorktown]]**. His relief fleet sailed on 19 October 1781, the very
day Cornwallis surrendered. The two generals spent the rest of their lives blaming each other for
the catastrophe.
