# Abd Allah al-Mahdi bi'llah

**Abd Allah al-Mahdi bi'llah** ("the One Rightly Guided by God," r. 909–934) was the **first
[[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] Imam-caliph** and the founder of the earliest sustained Shi'i caliphate of
the medieval Muslim world. He transformed the clandestine [[ismaili-movement|Ismaili]] [[dawa|da'wa]] into a
territorial state, was proclaimed **Commander of the Faithful** at [[qayrawan|Qayrawan]] in 910, and built
the fortress-capital of [[mahdiyya|Mahdiyya]] on the Tunisian coast.

## From concealment to caliphate

Al-Mahdi was born **Abd Allah** (c. 874) into the line of hidden Ismaili Imams who, since the death of
[[imamate|Imam]] Ja'far al-Sadiq (765), had directed the da'wa from concealment (*dawr al-satr*). His secret
headquarters was a merchant's mansion at **Salamiyya** in Syria, publicly the home of a wealthy Hashimi
trader. Pronounced Imam by his predecessor around **899** at the age of about 25, within a decade the da'wa
proclaimed him the awaited **[[mahdi]]** — a claim rejected by the Iraqi da'is **Hamdan Qarmat** and
**Abdan**, whose followers (the **Qarmatians**) split off as lasting Fatimid enemies.

When a zealous da'i's indiscretion revealed his whereabouts, Abbasid agents moved to arrest him. Al-Mahdi
fled Salamiyya by night (c. 902) with his young son **Abu'l-Qasim** (the future [[al-qaim-bi-amr-allah|al-Qa'im]]),
crossing Palestine and Egypt on a seven-year journey — during which his servant **Ja'far al-Hajib** was
tortured but never betrayed him — and reached the flourishing Saharan trade city of **[[sijilmasa|Sijilmasa]]**
in Morocco in **905**. There he waited in contact with his da'i, [[abu-abd-allah-al-shii|Abu Abd Allah
al-Shi'i]], who was meanwhile winning the [[berbers|Kutama Berbers]] of Ifriqiya to the cause.

## Founding the state

In 909 the Kutama armies overthrew the Sunni [[aghlabid-dynasty|Aghlabids]], took Qayrawan, then marched
1,000 km west to free al-Mahdi from Aghlabid arrest at Sijilmasa. On **26 August 909** he emerged publicly
before the Kutama (his *zuhur* or manifestation); on **4 January 910** he entered Qayrawan, and the next day
the Friday **khutba** was pronounced for the first time in the name of a Shi'i Ismaili Imam rather than the
Abbasid caliph. He took the regnal title **al-Mahdi bi'llah** and the caliphal style **Amir al-Mu'minin**.
See [[foundation-of-the-fatimid-caliphate-909]].

## Reign (909–934)

Al-Mahdi built the new administration from a religiously and ethnically mixed populace (Sunnis, Shi'a,
Kharijis, Christians, Jews; Berbers and Arabs), **retaining former Aghlabid Sunni officials** in taxation
and the mint while placing an Ismaili over the markets, and personally presiding over the ***mazalim*** court
of grievances. In **911** he faced the first crisis from *within* the movement: charged with conspiracy,
the very da'i who had built the state — Abu Abd Allah al-Shi'i — and his brother Abu'l-Abbas were **executed**
on al-Mahdi's order, though he led the funeral prayer and Ismaili tradition preserved Abu Abd Allah's honour.
He put down Kutama revolts that raised a rival *mahdi*, confirmed **al-Qa'im** as heir, and launched the first
(unsuccessful) **expeditions against Abbasid Egypt** (from 914, briefly taking Alexandria).

From 916 he sought a defensible seat and built **[[mahdiyya|al-Mahdiyya]]** (completed 919), a walled
peninsula-fortress with a chained war-harbour — the model for the later Fatimid capitals. He extended Fatimid
authority over **Sicily** (governors from 910; Palermo secured 916), stabilising rule from western Algeria to
the Egyptian border. He died on **4 March 934** after a 25-year reign, having secured the succession.

## Counterfactual significance

Al-Mahdi is the hinge between the underground da'wa and two-and-a-half centuries of Fatimid rule: without his
survival of the flight to Sijilmasa and the Kutama rescue, the Ismaili imamate might have remained a stateless
messianic movement like the Qarmatians. His decision to **execute his own founding da'i** established the
primacy of the Imam-caliph over the missionary organisation that made him — the defining structural fact of
the dynasty.
