# Free Corps (Freikorps)

Paramilitary units of demobilized veterans and younger volunteers formed after
the [[november-revolution-1918|November Revolution]], recruited and armed with
the sanction of the mainstream Social Democratic government — which feared a
Russian-style "red terror" — to suppress the far left. They became the seedbed
of Weimar's right-wing paramilitary violence.

## Role and Actions

Per Evans (2003): the Free Corps suppressed the Spartacist rising in Berlin
(January 1919) with "unprecedented violence and brutality," murdering Karl
Liebknecht and Rosa Luxemburg; revolutionaries were mown down or summarily
executed in multiple cities, including the bloody suppression of the
[[munich-revolution-1918-1919|Munich Soviet Republic]]. In spring 1920, after
defeating the [[kapp-putsch-1920|Kapp Putsch]] by general strike, the workers'
"Red Army" in the Ruhr was crushed by Free Corps units backed by the regular
army and the mainstream SPD: "well over a thousand members of the Red Army
were slaughtered, most of them prisoners 'shot while trying to escape.'"

Their ideology fused the [[stab-in-the-back-myth|stab-in-the-back myth]] with
eliminationist hatred of the revolution. Evans records their self-description:
"The 'reds', they believed, were an inhuman mass, like a pack of rats, a
poisonous flood pouring over Germany, requiring measures of extreme violence
if it was to be held in check."

## Turn Against Their Sponsors

Having been legitimized and armed by the SPD against the left, the Free Corps
turned on their own moderate-socialist sponsors as "November criminals."
Secret assassination squads (Organization Consul) murdered
[[walther-rathenau|Walther Rathenau]], Matthias Erzberger, and Hugo Haase.
Evans's causal point (attributed): the SPD's own recruitment of the Free Corps
contributed_to the right-wing paramilitary violence that later helped destroy
the [[weimar-republic|Republic]] — a self-undermining dynamic.

## Legacy

The Free Corps provided the organizational template, personnel, and violent
political culture from which Nazi paramilitarism drew: many later
[[sturmabteilung-sa|SA]] leaders (and camp commandants such as the head of
the Dürrgoy camp, a convicted Free Corps assassin) came out of the milieu.

## Historiography

Evans (2003) is the ingested treatment; he stresses the double delegitimation
mechanism — the Free Corps poisoned SPD–Communist relations permanently while
arming the anti-republican right. Fuller monographic treatment (Waite, Gerwarth
on paramilitary violence after 1918) is not yet ingested.
