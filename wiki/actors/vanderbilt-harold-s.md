# Harold Stirling Vanderbilt

Harold Stirling Vanderbilt (1884–1970). Son of [[actors/vanderbilt-william-kissam|William K. Vanderbilt]]; great-grandson of the Commodore. Yachtsman, New York Central director, naval officer in WWI, Harvard graduate, and influential figure in standardizing contract bridge. Cooper & Howe open their 1934 chapters on his America's Cup defense with *Rainbow* against T.O.M. Sopwith's *Endeavour*.

## Narrative

Harold had already defended the Cup with *Enterprise* (1930). In September 1934 *Rainbow* was selected over *Yankee*; after a no-contest opening day and an early *Endeavour* lead (including a race finished with C. Sherman Hoyt at the helm), Harold held course in a near-collision that forced Sopwith to give way. Sopwith's Rule 30 protest was dismissed by the New York Yacht Club race committee on a timing technicality — a decision that embittered the British camp. Harold retained the Cup. Cooper & Howe contrast Newport's elite spectacle with Depression-era textile strikes and FDR's interrupted holiday viewing. *Rainbow* was later scuttled (by about 1940 in the book's account). Gertrude Conaway sailed with Harold's campaigns and later married him.

## Historiography

Better documented in yachting and bridge histories than in general dynastic narrative; Cooper & Howe use him as a window on 1930s elite leisure amid economic crisis.
