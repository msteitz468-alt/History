# Kievan Rus′

**Kievan Rus′** was the Slavic-Scandinavian polity centred on **Kiev** that emerged along the river routes of European Russia in the late ninth and tenth centuries and, with its conversion to Orthodox Christianity in 988, became the founding state of the Russian, Ukrainian, and Belarusian historical traditions. [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]] (Thomas Noonan) deliberately sets the Rus′ state within the wider world of **European Russia** — the Byzantine Crimea (Cherson), the **Khazar khaganate**, the **Volga Bulgar amirate**, the steppe nomads (Khazars, Pechenegs), and the east Slav agriculturalists of the forest — rather than treating Kiev in isolation.

## Sources and the Problem of the Primary Chronicle

Noonan stresses the **severe source problem**: the peoples of European Russia produced almost no written sources before c. 1000. The **Rus′ Primary Chronicle** (the *Tale of Bygone Years*) was compiled only after the mid-eleventh century, and "there is a lively debate about the veracity of its information for the period before c. 1000"; the Khazar *Correspondence*, the Scandinavian sagas (thirteenth-century, Icelandic), and the Byzantine and Islamic accounts (only Ibn Faḍlān wrote from personal experience) all present serious difficulties. Reconstruction therefore leans heavily on **archaeology, numismatics, and linguistics** — which cannot, for instance, resolve "whether feudalism existed in the early Rus′ state."

From the Primary Chronicle (Laurentian, Cross & Sherbowitz-Wetzor 1953 trans., range 12001–16299): the Laurentian is the basis for the 1377 copy; redacted 1116 by Sylvester, Prior of St. Michael's (Vidubichi). "the colophon of Sylvester was on a separate leaf" (lines ~14506-14508). The famous conversion "test of the faiths" "contains no truth and ... is a later invention, and, at that, in all probability, not even a Russian, but a Greek invention" (Golubinsky, lines ~12229-12232); it fuses missionary icon tradition with the Kherson 989 baptism/marriage to Anna (lines ~12257 ff.). The text uniquely interpolates after 1096 Vladimir Monomakh's Testament (Pouchenie), letter to Oleg, and prayer. The Pouchenie "opens with excerpts from the Psalter and from patristic works (particularly the Asketika and Discourses of St. Basil the Great), followed ... by extensive admonitions to Vladimir’s sons, and finally, by a somewhat incomplete summary of Vladimir’s campaigns and adventures in approximate diary form, beginning in 1073 and ending in 1117"; "represents a fusion of at least two intrinsically unrelated documents"; "military activity against the nomads was more strenuous and constant than the annals themselves would indicate" (lines ~14519-14530). Composed shortly before death 19 May 1125 (lines ~14531-). Letter to Oleg written "at the instance of Vladimir's son, Mstislav, Prince of Novgorod" after Murom battle 6 Sept 1096 (lines ~14617-14620). Prayer derives from Byzantine Shrovetide/Lent services (lines ~14633-14635). Notes supply precise identifications and correct chronology (e.g., Svyatoslav-Tzimiskes 971, lines ~12003 ff.). Pro-Rurikid/Kievan bias; use critically with archaeology/foreign sources.

## The Rise of the State

The Rus′ — Scandinavian (Varangian) traders and warriors operating the **Volga and Dnieper river routes** that linked the Baltic to the Islamic Near East and to Byzantium — established control over the east Slav and Finnic tributary peoples. 

Per Primary Chronicle (Laurentian Text, ll. 3000–6000): pre-852 Polyanians etc. pay Khazar tribute (one sword per hearth; elders warn "These men shall impose tribute upon us and upon other lands"; "the Russes rule over the Khazars even to this day"). 859 Varangians impose on Chuds/Slavs/Merians/Ves’/Krivichians; Khazars on Polyanians/Severians/Vyatichians (white squirrel per hearth). 860–862: after driving Varangians out, tribes invite "Rurik... with their kinsfolk" due to "no order"; Rurik at Novgorod ("the district of Novgorod became known as the land of Rus’. The present inhabitants of Novgorod are descended from the Varangian race, but aforetime they were Slavs"), Sineus Beloozero, Truvor Izborsk; later Askold/Dir seize Kiev from Khazar tributaries. 

Oleg (Rurik kin, regent for young Igor) 880–882 unifies: takes Smolensk/Lyubech, tricks/kills Askold and Dir at Kiev ("You are not princes nor even of princely stock, but I am of princely birth"; Igor shown as son of Rurik); "Oleg set himself up as prince in Kiev, and declared that it should be the mother of Russian cities." Imposes tribute on Slavs/Krivichians/Merians; Novgorod pays 300 grivny/year "to the Varangians until the death of Yaroslav." Subjugates Derevlians (black marten), Severians (light tribute, ends Khazar), Radimichians (shilling). 907: 2000-ship raid on Tsar’grad (wheels on ships under sail); detailed 911/912 treaty (trade rights, 12 grivny/man, Perun/Volos oath, full legal stipulations on murder/theft/ransom/inheritance; Russes hang shields on gates). Oleg dies 912 from serpent per horse prophecy.

Igor succeeds; 941 raid repulsed by Greek fire; 944/945 major treaty with Romanus/Constantine/Stephen (detailed envoys list including for Olga; certificates, allowances by city priority starting Kiev, Kherson protections, mutual military aid, sworn at Perun statue and St. Elias church "a parish church, since many of the Varangians were Christians"). 945 Igor killed by Derevlians collecting extra tribute near Iskorosten ("If a wolf come among the sheep...").

**Olga** (regent for Svyatoslav) exacts revenge (ll. 4174–4261): first envoys dropped/buried alive in trench ("worse than the death of Igor’"); second burned in bathhouse; third massacred (5,000) at funeral feast at Igor's tomb. 946 takes Iskorosten with fire-bearing birds (3 pigeons/sparrows per house); imposes fixed tribute (2/3 Kiev, 1/3 Vishgorod); establishes laws/trading posts/hunting preserves in Dereva and Novgorod region (Msta/Luga). c. 955 travels to Tsar’grad; baptized by Emperor Constantine (son of Leo) and Patriarch as Helena ("Blessed art thou among the women of Rus’"); refuses emperor's marriage proposal ("How can you marry me, after yourself baptizing me and calling me your daughter?"). Urges Svyatoslav to convert; he refuses ("How shall I alone accept another faith? My followers will laugh at that").

**Svyatoslav** (sole rule 962): nomadic-style campaigns ("neither wagons nor kettles... cut off small strips... roasted... horse-blanket under him"). 965 defeats Khazars, takes Bela Vezha; conquers Yasians/Kasogians. 966 Vyatichians tributary. 967 conquers Bulgars (80 Danube towns); bases at Pereyaslavets ("the centre of my realm, where all riches are concentrated"). 968: first Pecheneg siege of Kiev (Olga + grandsons Yaropolk/Oleg/Vladimir inside); youth swims Dnieper warning; Pretich rescues with boats/trumpet deception; Svyatoslav returns, drives Pechenegs. Olga dies 969 ("the precursor of the Christian land"). 970 sets sons (Yaropolk Kiev, Oleg Dereva, Vladimir Novgorod). 971 war vs Greeks (victories but outnumbered; accepts arms not gold gifts); detailed treaty with Tzimiskes (peace, no attack on Kherson/Bulgaria; Perun/Volos oath "may we be accursed of the god in whom we believe"). 972 ambushed/killed by Pechenegs (Kurya) at Dnieper cataracts; "took his head, and made a cup out of his skull, overlaying it with gold, and they drank from it." 28-year reign.

The **Rurikid** dynasty (traditionally from Rurik at Novgorod) moved its centre to **Kiev** in the late ninth century (Oleg, traditionally c. 882), exacting tribute (one pelt per hearth) and channelling the **fur, slave, wax, and honey** trade southward. The dynasty repeatedly raided and treated with **Byzantium** (the 907/911 and 944 treaties), and under **Svyatoslav** (mid-tenth c.) destroyed the **Khazar khaganate** (c. 965) and campaigned in Bulgaria. **Olga**, regent for her son, was baptised at Constantinople (c. 957) — an early, personal conversion that did not yet make Rus′ a Christian state. (See dedicated actor pages oleg-of-kiev.md, olga-of-kiev.md, sviatoslav-i.md and rurikid-dynasty.md for full grounded extracts.)

## Conversion and Apogee

The decisive turn was the **[[conversion-of-vladimir-988|conversion of Vladimir I (the Great) in 988]]**, which made Orthodox Christianity the state religion and bound Rus′ to the Byzantine cultural and ecclesiastical world. Under Vladimir and his son **Yaroslav the Wise** (d. 1054) Kiev reached its height: the building of the cathedral of **Holy Wisdom** at Kiev, the first written law (the ***Pravda Russkaia***, from which private landholding can be confirmed only from the later eleventh century), a network of dynastic marriages across Latin and Orthodox Europe, and the elaboration of a Church-Slavonic literate culture.

From Primary Chronicle (Cross trans., exclusive lines 6001–6478): Vladimir returned from Cherson, overthrew idols in Kiev (Perun dragged by horse-tail, beaten by 12 men, cast in Dnieper to "Perun’s sandbank"); mass baptism in Dnieper ("countless multitude... priests stood by and offered prayers"); devil's lament; founded St. Basil church on Perun's hill; "began to found churches and to assign priests throughout the cities"; sent elite children for book-learning. 989: imported Greek artisans for Church of Holy Virgin (Desyatinnaya); "bestowed upon this church a tithe of my property and of my cities"; gave Kherson loot/images to Anastasius of Kherson. Pecheneg wars: 992 champion combat by Trubezh leads to founding of Pereyaslavl'; forts built vs nomads. Vladimir's charity, silver spoons remark ("with a retinue he was in a position to win these treasures"), peace with neighboring princes. 997: Belgorod's clever siege defense (porridge/mead pits convince Pechenegs). Vladimir dies 15 July 1015 at Berestovo; eulogized as "the new Constantine of mighty Rome" who "baptized himself and his subjects"; "marvelous what benefits Vladimir conferred upon the land of Rus’ by its conversion." (See conversion-of-vladimir-988.md for full quotes.)

## Fragmentation in the 11th–12th Centuries (NCMH IV; Primary Chronicle testimony)

[[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] (Martin Dimnik, Ch. 9, "Kievan Rus′, the Bulgars and
the southern Slavs, c. 1020–c. 1200") carries the story past the apogee. After Yaroslav
the Wise (d. 1054), whose succession arrangement (the "rota" rotation of princes among
the towns by seniority) sought to keep the realm united, Rus′ entered a long process of
**political fragmentation** into competing principalities (Kiev, Novgorod, Chernigov,
Galicia-Volhynia, and the rising Vladimir-Suzdal′ in the north-east). Despite a brief
restoration under Vladimir Monomakh (1113–25), the primacy of Kiev decayed; the realm
became a loose family confederation of increasingly autonomous principalities — the
"appanage" pattern that would leave the Rus′ lands divided on the eve of the Mongol
conquest of the thirteenth century.

The **Russian Primary Chronicle (Laurentian Text, Cross & Sherbowitz-Wetzor 1953, ll. 9001–12000)** provides the primary contemporary voice for the 1090s–1110s phase of this fragmentation and the Polovtsian (Cuman) steppe wars. Key testimony:

- **Inter-princely strife and Polovtsian raids (1096):** Svyatopolk and Vladimir Monomakh summoned Oleg (Svyatoslavich) to join a foray; Oleg "did not follow the same route." After they seized Polovcian herds and slaves, they demanded he kill or surrender Itlar's son ("an enemy of Rus’"). Oleg refused; "there was enmity between them" (ll. 9011–9022). Polovcians besieged Yur'ev all summer; Svyatopolk pacified them but "the citizens of Yur'ev abandoned their town and fled to Kiev"; Svyatopolk founded Vitichev for the refugees with Bishop Marinus; "The Polovcians then burned the deserted town of Yur'ev" (ll. 9023–9030). David (Svyatoslavich) left Novgorod for Smolensk; Novgorodians installed Mstislav (Vladimirovich). Izyaslav (Vladimirovich) took Murom (ll. 9031–9041).

- **Oleg vs. the coalition; siege of Starodub (1096):** Svyatopolk and Vladimir summoned Oleg to Kiev "before the bishops and the priors and in the presence of the vassals of their fathers and of the citizens" for a convention on defense. Oleg arrogantly refused: "it was not proper for him to submit to the judgment of a bishop, or priors, or of the common people." They marched on Chernigov; Oleg fled to Starodub and "barricaded himself in the city." After 33 days' siege with heavy casualties on both sides and starvation, Oleg "came forth from the city asking for peace," kissed the cross, and promised to join David and report to Kiev (ll. 9042–9069). Bonyak raided Kiev environs, "burned the prince’s palace at Berestovo." Tugorkan (Svyatopolk's father-in-law) besieged Pereyaslavl'; princes crossed undetected ("God protected them"); July 19 victory: "the Lord performed a great deliverance... Their prince Tugorkan was killed" (ll. 9070–9096). Svyatopolk buried his foe "between the road to Berestovo and the road to the monastery" (ll. 9097–9100). Days later Bonyak struck Kiev again, attacked the Crypt Monastery post-matins: slew brethren, "set fire to the shrine of the Holy Virgin... blasphemed against God and our faith" ("Where is their God?"), burned Vladimir's red palace at Vydobychi (ll. 9101–9138). Chronicle derives Polovcians from Ishmael (4 races: Torkmens, Pechenegs, Torks, Polovcians) and links to Alexander's enclosed unclean peoples (Methodius of Patara; ll. 9139–9153). Gyuryata Rogovich tale of northern mountain peoples cutting out (Gog/Magog motif; ll. 9154–9189).

- **Oleg kills Izyaslav at Murom; Mstislav's campaign (1096):** Oleg broke oath, marched on Murom ("this is my father’s territory here"); Izyaslav refused to yield; "a fierce battle ensued. Izyaslav... was thus slain on September 6." Oleg took Murom, Suzdal (only Crypt Monastery + St. Demetrius survived his burning of the city), Rostov; "began to exact tribute" (ll. 9190–9223). Mstislav Vladimirich (from Novgorod) pursued; after maneuvering and a four-day standoff, victory at Kulachek when Vladimir's standard terrified Oleg's forces; Oleg fled to Murom/Ryazan; Mstislav retook territories and offered peace (ll. 9224–9317).

- **Congress of Lyubech (1097) and blinding of Vasil'ko:** Svyatopolk, Vladimir, David Igorovich, Vasil'ko Rostislavich, David Svyatoslavich and Oleg "met at Lyubech to make peace" and declared: "Why do we ruin the land of Rus’ by our continued strife against one another? The Polovcians harass our country in divers fashions... Let us rather hereafter be united in spirit and watch over the land of Rus’, and let each of us guard his own domain; with Svyatopolk retaining Kiev, the heritage of Izyaslav, while Vladimir holds the domain of Vsevolod, and David, Oleg, and Yaroslav between them possess that of Svyatoslav. Let the domains apportioned by Vsevolod stand..." Oath on the Holy Cross: if any attacked another, "all the rest, with the aid of the Holy Cross, would be against the aggressor." "May the Holy Cross and the entire land of Rus’ be against him" (ll. 9320–9341). Immediately, David Igorovich (crediting false reports that Vasil'ko plotted with Vladimir) persuaded Svyatopolk; Vasil'ko was seized Nov 5 at the palace, fettered, taken to Belgorod and blinded by a Tork ("struck him in one eye, and took out the pupil, and then in the other eye"; "This scar Vasil'ko bears to this day"; ll. 9342–9454). Vladimir Monomakh reacted with horror: "Such a crime as this has never been perpetrated in Rus’ in the time of either our grandfathers or our fathers." He rallied David and Oleg; envoys confronted Svyatopolk ("Why have you blinded your cousin? If there was any charge against him, you should have accused him before us... Now make plain this fault"); Svyatopolk blamed David. Kievans (Vsevolod's widow and Metropolitan Nicholas) pleaded not to ruin the land won by "your sires and grandsires"; Vladimir yielded and peace was patched (ll. 9481–9548). Vasil'ko later told the chronicler (Vasiliy) he had no plots against kin and ascribed his fate to pride in ambitions against Poles, Polovcians, and Danube Bulgars (ll. 9550–9594). Volodar and Vasil'ko then campaigned against David; revenge killings followed (Vsevolozh stormed, innocents slain; Vladimir [Volynsk] siege forced surrender and hanging of Turyak, Lazar, Vasiliy) (ll. 9595–9655).

- **1100 Uvetichi judgment and 1103 victory:** At Uvetichi (1100) the cousins deprived David Igorich of Vladimir Volynsk ("you cast a sword among them in a fashion hitherto unknown in the land of Rus’"); assigned lesser domains and compensation (ll. 9800–9846). At Dolobsk (1103) Svyatopolk's boyars opposed a spring campaign vs Polovcians (ruin to peasants/fields); Vladimir countered: "Are you concerned for the horse and not for the peasant himself?" Coalition marched; April 4 victory at Suten': Polovcians "fled before the Russian troops without even waiting to meet them"; "twenty Polovcian princes were slain" (Urusoba et al.); Beldyuz captured and executed by Vladimir ("Your blood be upon your own head") (ll. 9905–9997). Later raids continued (e.g., 1107 Lubny victory over Bonyak and Sharukan "through the prayers of the Holy Virgin and of our holy father Theodosius"; ll. ~10048–10071).

**From notes in Primary Chronicle (lines 12001–16299):** The Lyubech settlement "momentarily consecrated the status quo" with precise appanage list (Svyatopolk Kiev/Turov/Gorjn’; Vladimir Monomakh Pereyaslavl’/Rostov-Suzdal’/Smolensk/Novgorod; David Chernigov, Oleg Novgorod-Seversk, Yaroslav Murom-Ryazan’; Volodar’ Przemysl, Vasil'ko Terebovl’; lines ~14363-14375). "It would seem from this arrangement that the principle of succession in one family was to apply to Kiev as well as to the other principalities" (line ~14376). Monomakh Testament (interpolated post-1096) provides his own diary-like campaign summary (1073–1117) and admonitions drawn from Psalter/Basil; composed shortly before death 19 May 1125 ("na dalechs puts" / "na sanékh sédya"); reveals heavier nomad warfare than main annals show (lines ~14519-14535). Letter to Oleg (at Mstislav's urging) after 6 Sept 1096 Murom battle where Izyaslav fell (lines ~14617 ff.). Prayer from Byzantine Lenten rite (lines ~14633 ff.). Vsevolod "understood five languages" per son's Testament (line ~14065). Polovtsian chiefs Tugorkan/Bonyak active in Byzantine politics too (lines ~14111 ff.). Notes correct chronology/identify sites throughout (e.g., Torchesk as "town of the Torks", line ~14074).

- **1110 portent and 1116 redaction:** Fiery pillar at Crypt Monastery "halted over the tomb of Theodosius" interpreted as angelic sign (ll. 10106–10130). "I Sylvester, Prior of St. Michaels, wrote this Chronicle in the year 6624 (1116)" (ll. 10131–10135). Appendices preserve Vladimir Monomakh's Testament (83 campaigns, 19 Polovtsian treaties, detailed autobiography of wars/hunts/piety; explicit reference to Starodub, Murom/Izyaslav context, and counsel against strife) and his Letter to Oleg (lament over Izyaslav's death at Oleg's hands, plea for reconciliation, "I did not desire to see your blood shed at Starodub"; ll. 10339–10703).

This Primary Chronicle material (composite, pro-Rurikid/Kievan, with monastic voice from the Caves) supplies the granular detail of the rota system's collapse into hereditary appanages, oath-breaking, and the persistent Polovtsian pressure that defined the era. See also the source page for full grounded extracts. Monomakh's later restoration (1113–1125) briefly checked the decay.

## The Mongol Conquest (NCMH V)

[[abulafia-ncmh-v5-2000|NCMH V]] (Simon Franklin, Ch. 18d) carries the story to its
catastrophe: the **[[mongol-empire|Mongol]]** conquest of the eastern Slav lands, "the symbolic
divide" ending old Kievan Rus′ and beginning the "Tartar yoke." After the Mongols destroyed a
Rus′–Polovtsian army at the **Kalka (1223)**, Batu's onslaught took **Riazan** (1237) and
**Vladimir** (1238), killed the grand prince Iurii at the river Sit′, and sacked **Kiev (6
December 1240)**. The fragmented Riurikid principalities — now treated under
[[rus-principalities|the Rus′ under the Mongols]] — became tributaries of the **Golden Horde**,
their centre of gravity shifting north and east toward Vladimir, Novgorod and the rising Moscow.
Franklin frames the lasting historiographical debate as a **pendulum between catastrophe and
continuity**.

## Significance

Kievan Rus′ created the first durable east Slav state, fixed Orthodox Christianity (and the Cyrillo-Methodian Slavonic liturgy) as the foundation of Russian and Ukrainian civilisation, and tied the forest zone of eastern Europe into the long-distance commerce of the Viking and Byzantine worlds. Its choice of **Constantinople over Rome** in 988 set eastern Europe on a confessional and cultural trajectory distinct from the Latin west of [[capetian-dynasty|France]], the [[ottonian-dynasty|Reich]], and Latin-Christian [[boleslaw-i-chrobry|Poland]] and [[premyslid-bohemia|Bohemia]].

## Additional Detail from Freeze (ed.) 2002 (Martin ch., lines 697–1399)
The formative geography: "The lands that made up Kievan Rus were located in the forest zone of Eastern Europe along a group of rivers, the Dnieper, the western Dvina, the Lovat-Volkhov, and the Volga, the headwaters of which all emanate from the Valdai hills. They were populated mainly by Slavic and Finnic tribes" who supported themselves "by fishing, hunting, and gathering" but "primarily agriculturalists" using slash-and-burn (l713–714). Varangians entered for silver trade, establishing tribute/protection relationships: "each band of Varangians protected its own group of Slavs from competing Scandinavian pirates in exchange for regular tribute payments" (l719–721). Legend of Riurik invited 862 after tribes "ejected previous Scandinavian protectors" (l725). Vladimir consolidated after 980 fratricidal wars, converted 988: married Byzantine Emperor's sister Anna; "the patriarch of Constantinople appointed a metropolitan to organize the see of Kiev and all Rus, and in 988 Byzantine clergy baptized the population of Kiev in the Dnieper river" (l755–757). Resistance e.g. Novgorod idol of Perun thrown in river provoked uprising (l757). Political: "the political structure they devised for Kievan Rus was based on the concept that its lands were the possession of the dynasty"; princes assigned portions for tax, defence, trade routes (l745–746). Law: Russkaia pravda issued by Iaroslav, enforced by princes (l747).

Mongol impact: 1237–40 campaigns "devastated the lands of Kievan Rus"; Riazan, Vladimir, Suzdal, Pereiaslavl, Chernigov, Kiev severely damaged; villages ravaged; "farming, trade, and handicrafts ceased" where they passed (l977–979). Yet "did not disturb all the norms": some NE towns (Rostov, Tver, Iaroslavl) untouched; Novgorod continued Baltic trade; Riurikid lines continued; "the metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus was still the leader" (l981–983). Golden Horde: khans confirmed princes via iarlyk (patent), demanded tribute (initially men/livestock/furs, later silver); princes collaborated; "when Riurikid princes competed for seniority, they appealed to the khan for arbitration" (l997–1001). Alexander Nevsky served khan loyally (l1001).

**Heritage persistence in north-eastern Russia post-1240 (Cambridge History of Russia Vol. I, Perrie ed. 2006, Martin ch. ll. 3709–4182):** "The Mongol invasion did not immediately destroy the heritage left by Kievan Rus’. The two institutions, the Riurikid dynasty and the Orthodox Church" continued to define the realm; NE principalities recognised common centre despite centrifugal branches (ll. 3754–3733). Vladimir (Suzdalia) retained bishopric ties to Kiev metropolitan; 1214 split preserved subordination (ll. 3750–3752). Princes required to recognise Golden Horde suzerainty; tribute/commercial networks via Volga/Sarai integrated region; metropolitans (Kirill, Maksim to Vladimir 1299) preserved "Kiev and all Rus’" see against Galicia/Lithuania (ll. 3785–4182). Political reorientation: "The bonds linking north-eastern and south-western Russia had noticeably loosened" as focus shifted to NE + Novgorod + Horde (ll. 4006–4110). See [[golden-horde]], [[rurikid-dynasty]], [[vladimir]], [[novgorod]], [[late-middle-ages]].

Rise of Moscow: After Ivan Kalita (Iurii's brother) secured throne ~1325 post-Tver elimination, "with the exception of a few brief interludes later in the fourteenth century, the princes of Moscow retained the position of grand prince until their dynastic line expired at the end of the sixteenth century" (l1097–1099). Territorial: controlled Moscow river, Oka segment, Volga portions; acquired via Ivan I (Beloozero, Uglich, Galich), Dmitrii Donskoi (Galich, Starodub, Rostov influence) (l1129–1135). Kulikovo 8 Sept 1380 vs Mamai: "Dmitrii’s armies were victorious over Mamai, whose Lithuanian allies failed to arrive" (l1280); reduced khan dependence though Tokhtamysh 1382 reasserted (l1294). Internal cohesion: few apanages; "most of their lands eventually reverted to the grand prince" (l1141–1143). Church bolstered: "provided a measure of domestically based legitimacy" via unity/continuity ideology; "Ivan Kalita... the initiator of the process of ‘gathering the Rus lands’" (l1331–1371). By 1425 "Muscovy had strengthened both its material and ideological foundations" for centralized state (l1387).
