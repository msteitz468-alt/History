# Murray Gell-Mann

American theoretical physicist, awarded the 1969 Nobel Prize in Physics for his classification of elementary particles and their interactions, including introducing the quark model (independently of George Zweig) as the fundamental constituents of hadrons. A longtime professor at Caltech and later a founder of the Santa Fe Institute for the study of complex systems, he was a contemporary and occasional intellectual rival of [[actors/feynman-richard|Richard Feynman]], with whom he also collaborated (notably on the V−A theory of the weak interaction). Known for a formidably broad range of interests beyond physics, including linguistics and ornithology, and for a famously demanding, competitive personality often contrasted with Feynman's more informal public style.
