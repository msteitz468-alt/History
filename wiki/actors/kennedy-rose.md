# Rose Kennedy

**Summary page.** Rose Elizabeth Fitzgerald Kennedy (1890–1995) was the matriarch of the Kennedy family, wife of Joseph P. Kennedy, mother of President John F. Kennedy.

## Overview

Daughter of Boston mayor John F. "Honey Fitz" Fitzgerald. Devout Catholic, structured, emotionally reserved in child-rearing. Accepted a long-term arrangement allowing her husband's infidelities in exchange for freedom to travel and pursue her own interests (including a private "cottage"/shed for prayer and solitude at Hyannis). Later became public symbol of family resilience and faith after multiple tragedies.

## Key Developments

- Mothering: Notecards per child (ailments, shoe sizes, vaccinations); weekly weigh-ins; dinner quizzes (history, Bible, prayers); "a good talking-to was good parenting"; tenderness lacking ("She was never there when we really needed her. She never really held me and hugged me. Never. Never!" — Jack re: illnesses/scarlet fever where Joe vigil, Rose absent).
- Marriage accommodation: After Joe's infidelities (Gloria Swanson etc.) left while pregnant, returned under pressure ("divorce not an option"); "Freedom" for Joe; "Always be a supplicant, dear"; "secret place" shed for rosary (no plumbing/electricity; Jackie: "about humility"); "I don’t think Jack’s mother is too bright... she would rather say a rosary than read a book" (Jackie).
- Hyannis 1952: Locked shed routine observed by Jack/Jackie/Janet DesRosiers; "like two sentinels" flashlight vigil with Jack by sea.
- Later: Interviews (Coughlan 1972 etc.); "caring makes you weak" (post Joe stroke); central to family public image and private dynamics.

## Significance

Core to "Ties That Bind" and early formation sections. Her choices around marriage and mothering are analyzed as templates for Jack's later relationships with women and his wife. Oral histories, Robert Coughlan interviews (1972), and family papers provide primary grounding.
