# John Lindsay

Mayor of New York City (1966–1973), a liberal Republican (later Democrat) known for his reformist ambitions, engagement with the city's Black and Puerto Rican communities amid urban unrest, and running conflict with the city's powerful public authorities and unions. Lindsay's mayoralty coincided with mounting fiscal strain, labor unrest (notably the 1968 sanitation and teachers' strikes), and the broader unwinding of the mid-century model of large-scale public works exemplified by Robert Moses.

Lindsay's administration marked a generational and stylistic break from the Moses-era model of city-building by unaccountable authorities, favoring greater community input and social programs, though critics credited his tenure with accelerating the city's fiscal crisis of the mid-1970s. He ran unsuccessfully for the Democratic presidential nomination in 1972.

## Significance

Lindsay's mayoralty is frequently contrasted with Robert Moses's decades of authority-driven infrastructure building, symbolizing New York's shift toward more contested, socially engaged, and fiscally strained urban governance in the late 1960s and 1970s.
