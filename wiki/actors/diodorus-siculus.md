# Diodorus Siculus

A Greek historian from Sicily (fl. mid-first century BCE), author of the
*Bibliotheca Historica* ("Library of History"), a universal history in
forty books spanning mythical origins through Julius Caesar's Gallic Wars.
Although not regarded as a first-rank original historian, his work is
critically valuable as one of the main surviving vessels for otherwise lost
earlier historians.

## Role and Significance

Diodorus compiled his history largely by excerpting and summarizing earlier
authors — including Hieronymus of Cardia for the Wars of the Diadochi and
Ephorus for much of Classical Greek history — often preserving substantial
material from sources that do not otherwise survive. His account of the
Hellenistic period following Alexander's death, in particular, is a
principal surviving narrative for events such as the [[battle-of-amorgos|
Battle of Amorgos]] and the Wars of the Diadochi.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Diodorus's compilation, large stretches of Hellenistic narrative
history — dependent on lost historians such as Hieronymus of Cardia — would
be known only through scattered fragments rather than a connected account.

## Historiography

Diodorus is valued by modern historians less for his own analytical
judgment (his narrative is often uneven and derivative) than as a
transmission channel for earlier, lost historiography; source-critical
scholarship focuses heavily on identifying which underlying source Diodorus
is following in any given book, since his own editorial voice is
comparatively thin.
