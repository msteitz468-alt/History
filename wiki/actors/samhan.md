# Samhan (Three Han)

The **Samhan** ("Three Han") were the iron-producing polity-clusters of the southern Korean Peninsula
during the first centuries BC–AD — the **Late Iron Age** that grew out of the [[mumun-culture|Mumun]]
and out of which the southern [[korea-three-kingdoms|Three Kingdoms]] emerged.
[[barnes-archaeology-east-asia-2015|Barnes]] prefers "Samhan / Late Iron Age" to the teleological
"Proto-Three Kingdoms," and treats the Samhan as the engine room of the
[[yellow-sea-interaction-sphere|Yellow Sea sphere's]] iron trade.

## The Three Han

The three were **Mahan** (west; 54 polities, >100,000 households, ~500,000 people), **Pyonhan** (south;
12 polities) and **Chinhan** (east; 12 polities). Total Samhan population in the mid-3rd century AD
approached **one million**. Their emergence is marked archaeologically from the 3rd century BC by
**wooden coffins in earthen pits** and a new grey hard-fired **Wajil** earthenware replacing Mumun
pottery. After the Han conquest of Ko-Choson and the founding of [[lelang-commandery|Lelang]] (108 BC),
"population increased, full-time specialists emerged, and surpluses were managed by elites in exchange
networks." Each cluster later crystallised into a state: **Saro** (Chinhan) → [[silla|Silla]], **Boji**
(Mahan) → [[paekche|Paekche]], **Kuya** (Pyonhan) → the [[kaya|Pon-Kaya]] chiefdom.

## Iron — "all surrounding peoples obtained their iron"

The Wei chronicles single out **Pyonhan and Chinhan (Yongnam)** as the great iron producers from whom
all neighbours — Lelang and the Japanese [[yayoi-culture|Yayoi]] — obtained iron. Production centred on
**Kimhae** (south coast) and **Ulsan** (southeast); **Yonjae-ri** gives the earliest clear bloomery
smelting (2nd–3rd c. AD, an estimated 1–2 tons produced). Flat **axe-shaped iron ingots** served both
as forging stock and as **currency**; from the 2nd century AD many Yongnam burials had floors paved with
them. Iron was exported to Lelang by the overland "Yongnam highway" and by sea up the west coast to the
Taedong mouth. Bronze production, meanwhile, shifted from the southwestern to the southeastern Peninsula
around the turn of the millennium, generating a series of small bronzes (animal images, bosses).

## Absorbing the World

The **Taho-ri** burial #1 (late 1st c. BC, near the south coast) captures the Samhan elite at work:
writing brushes and slip-shaving knives, cast- and bloomery-iron axes, a lacquered sword sheath copying
Early Iron Age bronze forms, a Han "star-and-cloud" mirror and *wuzhu* coins. Barnes's reading is not
of a Han personage imposed on the coast but of "the local elite absorbing cultural elements wherever
encountered." After Wei fell (265), Chinhan and Mahan polities sent missions directly to the Western Jin
court — 18 recorded between 276 and 291 — on the eve of their consolidation into states.
