# Thutmose IV

**Thutmose IV** (r. ~1400–1390 BCE), son of [[actors/amenhotep-ii|Amenhotep II]] and father
of [[amenhotep-iii|Amenhotep III]], reigned briefly but actively. His succession had not been
publicly signalled, and defaced stelae of his brothers at the Giza Sphinx temple hint at a
possible **dynastic struggle** — though "we cannot confirm or deny that Thutmose IV was the
usurper."

## The Sphinx Stele and the solar turn

His most famous monument is the **Dream (Sphinx) Stele**, set between the paws of the Great
[[places/giza|Giza]] Sphinx, recounting a vision in which the Sphinx — addressed as
**Horemakhet-Khepri-Ra-Atum** — promised him the kingship if he cleared the monument of sand.
Tellingly, the stele **omits Amun entirely**: "Thutmose's omission of Amun from his stele must
surely have been deliberate, perhaps reflecting both the increasing importance of the
Heliopolitan gods and the political influence of the north." This solar emphasis prefigures
the religious developments of his son and grandson — the run-up to [[concepts/atenism|Amarna]]
(see [[concepts/divine-kingship]]).

## Foreign relations and administration

He sealed the **Mitanni peace** by marrying a daughter of king Artatama I, and conducted a
single recorded Levantine campaign (a laconic dedication "from the plunder of his Majesty …
from his first campaign of victory") and desert policing of the Nubian gold-mine routes
(Konosso Stele). Under him the **militarization of the elite receded**: military titles grow
scarce while "royal scribe" proliferates, offices filled by "bureaucrats, often selected from
long-established elite families" — a shift toward the civilian court culture of the later
dynasty.
