# Henry A. Wallace (1888–1965)

Iowa agricultural scientist and editor (a pioneer of hybrid corn and founder of
Pioneer Hi-Bred), Secretary of Agriculture 1933–40, Vice President of the
United States 1941–45, Commerce Secretary 1945–46, and Progressive Party
presidential candidate in 1948. Wallace was the [[new-deal|New Deal]]'s chief
agricultural architect — the AAA's production-control regime, including the
notorious 1933 crop and livestock destruction, was his department's — and the
administration's most visible ideological visionary ("the century of the
common man," 1942).

**Role in the network.** Wallace marks the left edge of the Roosevelt
coalition and its breaking point: dumped from the ticket at the 1944 Democratic
convention in favor of [[truman-harry-s|Harry Truman]] — a bosses-brokered
substitution that, given [[roosevelt-franklin-d|Roosevelt's]] health, chose the
next president — then fired by Truman in 1946 for publicly opposing the
hardening line toward Moscow, and finally the 1948 standard-bearer of
Popular-Front progressivism, whose campaign's communist-adjacent support
effectively read that tendency out of mainstream politics.

**Counterfactual significance.** The 1944 vice-presidential switch is among
the highest-leverage personnel decisions in American history: a Wallace
succession in April 1945 would have put a Soviet-conciliationist in charge of
the atomic bomb, the occupation settlements, and the onset of the Cold War.

**Personality and controversy.** Wallace's mysticism (the "guru letters" to
Nicholas Roerich, a running press scandal) and administrative feuds (the
1943 Wallace–Jesse Jones brawl over the Board of Economic Warfare) made him a
lightning rod; contemporary critics treated him as the emblem of New Deal
utopianism.

**Documented specifics** (per Flynn 1948, attributed): Wallace's 1940
vice-presidential nomination passed 627 7/10 to 327 4/15 over Bankhead amid
open delegate hostility, forced through on Roosevelt's insistence; in 1944 he
led the first ballot (429½ to Truman's 319½) before the bosses' second-ballot
switch made it Truman 1,100 to Wallace 66. As wartime Board of Economic
Warfare chairman he spent some $1.2 billion of RFC money on "preclusive
buying" until his public 1943 brawl with Jesse Jones led Roosevelt to dissolve
the agency. The "guru letters" to Roerich rest on Westbrook Pegler's reporting
(Pegler claimed authentication by three handwriting experts; Wallace never
confirmed or denied) — an attribution chain to keep visible, since Flynn
relies on Pegler rather than direct evidence. Wallace's own words supply the
flavor his critics exploited: society might move toward "something like the
theocracies of old" (Federal Council of Churches, December 1933); Roerich was
"a great versatile genius" (1935).

## Historiography

Mainstream lives: Culver & Hyde, *American Dreamer* (2000), the standard
biography. Revisionist treatment: Flynn, *[[flynn-roosevelt-myth-1948|The
Roosevelt Myth]]* devotes a chapter to Wallace as the type-case of the
administration's "crackpot" visionary wing. New Left and post-revisionist
writers (most recently Oliver Stone's circle) have inverted the picture,
casting the 1944 switch as the moment an avoidable Cold War became inevitable
— itself a contested counterfactual. See [[roosevelt-and-new-deal-revisionism]].
