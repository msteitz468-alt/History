# Alexander II of Russia

**Alexander II** (1818–1881; r. 1855–1881), known as the **"Tsar-Liberator,"** was Emperor of Russia. In response to the systemic crises exposed by the Crimean War, Alexander II enacted the **Great Reforms**, most notably the **Emancipation of the Serfs** in 1861, which dismantled the foundations of old Russian society and initiated the transition to modern capitalism. He was assassinated by populist revolutionaries in 1881.

## Biography / Description

Alexander was the eldest son of Emperor Nicholas I. He received a thorough education under the guidance of tutor Vasily Zhukovsky, a romantic poet who instilled humane sentiments in the grand duke. Alexander also received practical training in administration. Unlike his father, Alexander did not possess a strong-willed or highly talented character, and he held essentially conservative views throughout his life. 

Upon succeeding his father in 1855, however, Alexander found himself confronting the military, administrative, and technological collapse of the empire during the Crimean War. Convinced that the preservation of Russia's great-power status demanded a fundamental departure from the policies of Nicholas I, he resolved to reform the empire's key institutions.

His reform program, while successful in transforming the country's legal, military, and administrative structures, failed to satisfy either the conservative gentry (who sought to preserve their privileges) or the radical intelligentsia (who demanded a constitution and full peasant land ownership). As radical movements turned to terrorism in the 1870s, Alexander survived several attempts on his life. On March 13, 1881 (March 1, Old Style), he was assassinated in St. Petersburg by a bomb thrown by members of the revolutionary organization **"The Will of the People"** (*Narodnaya Volya*).

## Key Decisions and Achievements

### The Emancipation of the Serfs (1861)
- Proclaiming in 1856 that "it would be better to begin to abolish serfdom from above than to wait until it would begin to abolish itself from below," Alexander initiated the complex process that culminated in the **Emancipation Manifesto of March 3, 1861** (February 19, Old Style).
- The reform abolished the legal ownership of 22 million private serfs and was later extended to state and imperial family peasants. It provided peasants with personal freedom and land allotments, which they were required to purchase through long-term **redemption payments** to the state. The land, however, was generally transferred to the collective **peasant commune** (*obshchina* or *mir*), rather than to individual peasants.

### The Great Reforms
Following the emancipation, Alexander implemented a series of institutional modernizations:
- **The Zemstvo Reform (1864):** Created district and provincial assemblies (*zemstva*) elected on a class basis, giving rural Russia its first measure of local self-government. The zemstva became highly effective in establishing schools, medical clinics, roads, and agricultural improvements.
- **The Judicial Reform (1864):** Reorganized the legal system based on Western models, establishing the independence of the judiciary, public and oral trials, equality before the law, trial by jury for criminal cases, and a professional bar of lawyers.
- **The Municipal Reform (1870):** Applied self-government principles to cities, creating town councils (*dumy*) elected on a tax-paying class basis.
- **The Military Reform (1874):** Reorganized the military under Minister of War Dmitry Miliutin, establishing universal conscription (extending military service obligation to all classes), reducing active service from 25 to 6 years, organizing a reserve force, and establishing schools and literacy programs for draftees.
- **Financial Reforms:** Led by Valery Tatarinov, the government created a single state treasury, published the state budget for the first time, and founded the State Bank (1866).

### Foreign Policy and Reaction
- Alexander crushed the Polish January Uprising of 1863, ending the relative autonomy Poland had enjoyed in the early years of his reign and subjecting the territory to intense Russification.
- He oversaw the expansion of the empire in Central Asia and the Far East, and fought the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, which liberated Bulgaria but strained Russian finances.

## Historical Significance

Alexander II is one of the most consequential figures in Russian history. By ending serfdom, he swept away the legal and social framework that had bound the majority of the Russian population for centuries. His reforms stimulated the rapid growth of capitalism, urbanization, and industrialization in the late 19th century, while creating the civic spaces (the courts and the zemstva) that nurtured a modern civil society.

However, the incomplete nature of his reforms—specifically the retention of autocracy, the financial burdens placed on the peasantry, and the restriction of peasant mobility through the commune—created deep-seated social instability. His assassination in 1881 cut short the plans for a consultative national assembly and ushered in a period of intense counter-reform under his son, Alexander III.
