# Erwin Rommel

German field marshal. Famous for command of Afrika Korps in North Africa 1941–43 ("Desert Fox"); later involved in Atlantic Wall defenses. Associated with chivalrous reputation among adversaries; implicated (or suspected) in 20 July plot; forced to suicide. Symbol of the "clean Wehrmacht" myth in post-war memory.

See: [[world-war-ii-1939-1945]], [[adolf-hitler]], [[heinz-guderian]].

**From Murray & Millett (ranges 1-2 targeted extraction):**
- Rommel (France Meuse): "across with the first battalion"; "almost a miracle" (Guderian quote); drove off French armor personally; "inspired piece of generalship". (Ch. 4)
- Rommel (France Arras/Gazala): British isolated battles vs German combined teams; "cauldron" 88mm screen; Gazala plan south around line, overwhelmed brigades.
- Rommel (North Africa dilemma/Tobruk): "horns of a dilemma" on port; "premier battlefield commander"; energy/inspiration despite difficulties; "sixth sense for battlefield".
- Rommel (El Alamein stand-fast): Received Hitler "stand fast... victory or death"; shaken; retreat despite order. (range2)
- Rommel + Kesselring + Hitler (supply/Tunisia): Kesselring Luftflotte helped supply; Hitler divided command (Arnim vs Rommel); "Smiling Albert" optimistic reports; final 275k POW collapse. (range2)

## From Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)

Evans's verdict on North Africa is Rommel's own (1944): "the war in North Africa was decided by the weight of Anglo-American material." Aware of the July Plot but not a participant or approver, Rommel nonetheless told Hitler to his face the war should be ended; implicated under interrogation, he was offered staged suicide with a state funeral or a public trial, and took the poison (14 October 1944) as the SS surrounded his convalescent village.
