# Muʾayyad al-Dīn al-ʿUrḍī

Muʾayyad al-Dīn al-ʿUrḍī (d. 1266) was a Syrian astronomer, mathematician, and instrument-maker from Damascus who became one of the two pivotal figures — alongside [[nasir-al-din-tusi|Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī]] — of the [[maragha-school|Maragha school]]. He is best known for introducing [[urdi-lemma|ʿUrḍī's Lemma]], a geometric theorem that enabled a physically consistent solution to the equant problem in Ptolemy's model for the upper planets. He also oversaw the construction of the precision instruments at the [[maragha-observatory|Maragha Observatory]].

## Career

Al-ʿUrḍī was from Damascus and had established his reputation there before joining the Maragha Observatory shortly after its founding in 1259. He was the astronomer explicitly identified by Saliba as the one who enunciated the mission of the new Islamic astronomy: "to create an astronomy that did not suffer from the cosmological shortcomings of Ptolemaic astronomy, that could account for the observations just as well as Ptolemaic astronomy could do if not better, and that did not limit itself to criticizing Ptolemy only, despite all the benefits that one derived from the detailed critique of Ptolemy's mistakes" ([[saliba-islamic-science-renaissance-2007]]).

Saliba rates his role at Maragha as "pivotal" and "comparable only to that of Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī" ([[saliba-islamic-science-renaissance-2007]]).

## Astronomical Contributions

### The Equant Problem and the Lunar and Mercury Models

When addressing the lunar model and Mercury, al-ʿUrḍī found that adjustments to directions and magnitudes of motions — without introducing new mathematics — were sufficient to make all spheres move uniformly around their own centers. He "decided to take advantage of the similarities between the two models, and tried to reconfigure them by adopting three new steps. First he decided to shift the directions of the motions of the various spheres. Then he adjusted the magnitudes of those motions. And finally he tried, in a global way, to avoid the Ptolemaic handicaps that plagued both models by making all spheres move uniformly on axis that passed through their centers" ([[saliba-islamic-science-renaissance-2007]]). In doing so he remained within the existing mathematics of Euclid's *Elements*.

He was prepared to criticize Ptolemy's theoretical reasoning (*ḥads*) while retaining full admiration for Ptolemy's observational and mathematical control of data — a nuanced evaluation characteristic of the Maragha tradition.

### ʿUrḍī's Lemma and the Upper Planet Model

For the upper planets — Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Venus — al-ʿUrḍī concluded that the Ptolemaic model was "no longer redeemable" and required a fundamentally new mathematical approach. He introduced [[urdi-lemma|ʿUrḍī's Lemma]]:

> Given any two equal lines that form equal angles with a base line, either internally or externally, the line joining the extremities of those two lines will be parallel to the base line.

Applied to the upper planets, he shifted the deferent center to point K (halfway between the Ptolemaic deferent center T and the equant D), added a small epicycle of radius TK = KD moving at the same speed and direction as the new deferent, and showed by the lemma that the center of the Ptolemaic epicycle appeared to move uniformly around the equant — while in fact every sphere rotated uniformly around its own center. The lemma was, structurally, a generalization of Apollonius's theorem to interior angles.

Saliba writes: "From that perspective, this relatively simple lemma proved to be an epoch maker, just like the Ṭūsī Couple. And like the Ṭūsī Couple too, once it was discovered, it allowed several generations of astronomers to think differently about Ptolemaic astronomy" ([[saliba-islamic-science-renaissance-2007]]).

## Instrument Construction

Al-ʿUrḍī's work on the Maragha instruments is noted by Saliba as an example of the Islamic astronomical tradition's attention to observational precision — "those of ʿUrḍī regarding the construction of the Maragha observatory instruments" — alongside Khujandī's work on larger, more precise instruments ([[saliba-islamic-science-renaissance-2007]]). This practical dimension of his work at Maragha reflects the dual character of the school: simultaneously advancing observational technique and theoretical reform.

## Major Work

- ***Kitāb al-Hayʾa*** (Book on Astronomy): his theoretical astronomical treatise, containing the statement and proof of ʿUrḍī's Lemma and his reformed models for the planets.

## Legacy

The lemma was adopted by virtually every subsequent Islamic astronomer engaged in the reform of Ptolemaic models:
- Quṭb al-Dīn al-Shīrāzī (d. 1311): lunar model and upper planets
- [[ibn-al-shatir|Ibn al-Shāṭir]] (d. 1375): multiple models — he called ʿUrḍī's Lemma one of the two founding *uṣūl* (principles) of his system
- ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn al-Qushjī (d. 1474): Mercury model
- Shams al-Dīn al-Khafrī (d. 1550): upper planets (double use)
- **Nicolaus Copernicus** (d. 1543): upper planets, in the same mathematical structure

The use of the lemma by Copernicus for the same model (upper planets) to which al-ʿUrḍī had first applied it is a key element of Saliba's transmission argument — that the mathematical tools of the Maragha school were not independently reinvented in Europe but transmitted through channels not yet fully documented.
