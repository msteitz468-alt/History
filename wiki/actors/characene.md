## Overview

Characene (also **Mesene**; capital **Spasinou Charax**) was a small but wealthy kingdom
at the head of the Persian Gulf, at the confluence of the Tigris and Euphrates. For most
of its existence it was a client/vassal of the [[parthian-empire]], but it retained "an
uncanny degree of independence" (D. T. Potts) thanks to its command of the only harbour
facilities linking the Mesopotamian interior with the Gulf, South Arabia, Egypt, and
India — including access to the Gulf pearl market. Fisher treats it, with [[hatra]] and
the [[kingdom-of-edessa|kingdom of Edessa]] (Osroëne), as one of the "Parthian Near West"
client states amid whose populations Graeco-Roman writers detected "Arabs."

## Foundation and Rulers

The principal city began as **Alexandria-on-the-Tigris**, founded by [[alexander-the-great]]
in 324 BCE. After a devastating flood, **Antiochus IV** refounded it c. 166 BCE as Antioch,
appointing a satrap, **Hyspaosines** (apparently of Indo-Bactrian origin). When Seleucid
power collapsed after 139 BCE (the capture of Demetrius II by the Parthians), Hyspaosines
campaigned in Babylonia (138–132 BCE) and then declared himself king — an event dated by
the cuneiform **Astronomical Diaries** to 24 June 127 BCE. He refounded the city as
**Spasinou Charax**, "the fortress of Spaosines." Characene profited from — and may have
hastened — the economic decline of the rival Gulf trading city of Gerrha.

Hyspaosines' heyday was short (last coin issue c. 121 BCE); after his death the Parthian
king **Mithridates II** reasserted dominance, over-striking Characenian coins. Pliny the
Elder records that "Spaosines" ruled "the neighbouring Arabs." In the 2nd century CE a
Palmyrene, **Yarhai**, served as satrap on Bahrain (Tylos) on behalf of "Meredat
[Mithridates], king of Spasinou Charax" — one of many attestations of Characene's reach in
Palmyrene inscriptions.

## Rome and Decline

During [[trajans-parthian-war-114-117|Trajan's eastern campaign]] Characene briefly "went
over" to Rome (114 CE) before reverting to tightened Parthian control; the
Parthian-appointed governor Meredat held Spasinou Charax until driven out by **Vologases
IV** in 151 CE. Like the other Parthian clients, Characene did not survive the collapse of
the Arsacid dynasty (224 CE) as an independent entity; it was absorbed into the Sasanian
system.

## Archaeology

In 2016 excavation began at **Jabal Khayaber** near Basra, identified as Spasinou Charax —
exactly where Pliny located it in the *Natural History*. Work has revealed residential
areas and a large Arsacid-era palace.

## Historiography

Characene is poorly documented in narrative sources; its history is reconstructed from
coins, the Astronomical Diaries, Palmyrene trade inscriptions, Pliny, and now archaeology.
D. T. Potts is the standard modern authority. The kingdom's chief significance for Fisher's
argument is as evidence for the Gulf's integration into long-distance trade and for the
loose Graeco-Roman application of "Arab" to Gulf-shore populations.
