# Abu Yazid al-Nukkari

**Abu Yazid Makhlad b. Kaydad al-Nukkari** (c. 883–947), remembered as **"the Man on the Donkey"
(*Sahib al-Himar*)**, was the **Kharijite Berber preacher** whose revolt of **[[khariji-revolt-of-abu-yazid-943|943–947]]**
nearly destroyed the [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] state, overrunning Ifriqiya and besieging
**[[mahdiyya|Mahdiyya]]** before he was defeated and killed by [[al-mansur-fatimid|al-Mansur]].

## Background

Educated at **Tahert** (western Ifriqiya) and belonging to a Berber clan rivalling the [[berbers|Kutama]], Abu
Yazid was an **Ibadi/Nukkari Kharijite**. Kharijism — which held that piety alone, not lineage or ethnicity,
qualifies a man to be Imam — had spread among North African Berbers resentful of Arab overlordship since the
8th century; its imamate at Tahert had been ended by [[abu-abd-allah-al-shii|Abu Abd Allah al-Shi'i]] in 909.
Abu Yazid spent some fifteen years building support against the Fatimids, backed by the **Umayyads of Córdoba**.

## The revolt

From **943** he launched surprise attacks; in October 944 he took **[[qayrawan|Qayrawan]]**, where the city's
Sunni **Maliki** scholars — whose standing had waned under Fatimid rule — allied with him despite the
ideological gulf. His armies pushed Fatimid authority back to the coast, leaving only Mahdiyya and a few
strongholds. He personally led the **siege of Mahdiyya** (20 January – 16 September 945), but al-Mahdi's massive
walls held and the siege broke. Under the new caliph al-Mansur the tide turned: Qayrawan's people shut their
gates against him, and in a series of battles in 947 al-Mansur routed the Kharijites. Cornered in the **Hodna
mountains**, Abu Yazid **died of his wounds in August 947**.

## Significance

Abu Yazid's revolt was the gravest threat the Fatimids faced in their Ifriqiyan phase — a coalition of
Kharijite doctrine, Berber tribal rivalry, Sunni Maliki grievance, and Umayyad sponsorship. Its defeat both
confirmed the durability of al-Mahdi's fortress strategy and prompted al-Mansur's conciliatory post-war
settlement (tax remission, a Sunni Maliki chief judge), shaping how the dynasty governed its Sunni majority.
