# Cuthbert of Lindisfarne and his Community

**St Cuthbert** (d. 687), monk, prior, and bishop of Lindisfarne, was the pre-eminent saint
of [[places/northumbria|Northumbria]], combining ascetic authority with episcopal office —
his cult promoted from the late seventh century with hagiography (Bede's *Life*) and the
**[[concepts/insular-art|Lindisfarne Gospels]]** (written by Bishop Eadfrith in his honour).
This page covers the saint and, above all, the **community** that bore his relics and became
a major political and territorial power.

## The Wandering Community

Leaving Lindisfarne in **875** before the Viking threat, the community wandered for seven
years (also visiting sites over which it asserted proprietary claims), settling at
**Chester-le-Street** and finally **Durham** (995). King Guthred granted it the land between
Tyne and Wear — the core of the **liberty of St Cuthbert** — and the community even
appointed the Viking Guthred as a candidate for the Northumbrian kingship, illustrating how
quickly ecclesiastical and Scandinavian politics intertwined.

## Power and Endowment

By 1000 the Church of St Cuthbert claimed estates "from Lothian … to Yorkshire, and from
Carlisle … to Durham," so that "anyone wishing to extend his lordship into the region had to
reach an accommodation" with it. Æthelstan lavished gifts on the shrine (927); **Cnut** made
a barefoot pilgrimage to it. In **1083** Bishop William of Saint-Calais introduced the
**Benedictine rule** to Durham, breaking "at a stroke" the controlling interest of
Northumbrian families in the cult and integrating it into post-Conquest ecclesiastical
structures.
