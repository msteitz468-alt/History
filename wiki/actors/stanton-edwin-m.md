# Edwin M. Stanton

**Summary page.** Cabinet dynamics and Lincoln’s leadership on the Lincoln pages.

Called “Mars” by Lincoln, Stanton was the intense, hard-driving Secretary of War from 1862 onward. Initially treated Lincoln with open contempt when they met earlier in their careers; after appointment he became one of the president’s most loyal and emotionally attached lieutenants. His organizational energy transformed the War Department. After Lincoln’s death he wept for weeks and later clashed with Andrew Johnson, leading to the impeachment crisis.

## Overview

Stanton brought administrative drive and fierce Unionism. His relationship with Lincoln evolved from disdain to profound respect and personal affection. He was present at key moments (City Point, Richmond visit planning, assassination night). His emotional reaction to Lincoln’s death is one of the most moving testimonies in the record. After the war his Radical sympathies put him at the center of the Tenure of Office battle.

## Key Developments

- Pre-war: Ohio lawyer, Attorney General under Buchanan, brief collaboration with Lincoln (reaper case).
- 1861–1862: Initial service under Cameron; appointed War Secretary January 1862.
- 1862–1865: Overhaul of department; close (sometimes stormy) partnership with Lincoln and Grant; 1864 election support; final campaigns.
- 1865–1869: Assassination night; service under Johnson; barricade in office; impeachment role; Supreme Court nomination (died before taking seat).

## Significance and Character

Stanton exemplifies the conversion of a former critic into a devoted subordinate through Lincoln’s leadership. Goodwin’s family letters and sister’s memoir supply the human side of the “hard” war secretary.
