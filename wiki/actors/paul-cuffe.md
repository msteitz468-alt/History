# Paul Cuffe

**Paul Cuffe** (1759–1817) was a free Black (of African and Wampanoag descent) Quaker
merchant and ship captain from Massachusetts who became one of the wealthiest men of color
in the early United States through his shipping and whaling ventures. A committed
abolitionist and early advocate of Black emigration to Africa as a route to autonomy and
economic opportunity beyond the reach of American slavery and racial discrimination, Cuffe
personally funded and led a voyage carrying free Black settlers to the British colony of
Sierra Leone in 1815–1816, using his own ship and capital. His example and correspondence
influenced the American Colonization Society (founded 1816–1817), though Cuffe's own
vision — Black-led, self-financed emigration — differed in emphasis from the white-led
ACS, whose motives mixed anti-slavery sentiment with a desire to remove free Black people
from American society; Cuffe died before the ACS's Liberia project began in earnest.
