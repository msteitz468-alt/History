# Alan Turing

**Summary & network:** [[hubs/biographies/world-wars-era/turing-alan|Detailed analytic biography]] · **Biography hub:** [[hubs/biographies/biographies-hub|Biographies Hub]]

Alan Mathison Turing (23 June 1912 – 7 June 1954) was an English mathematician, logician, cryptanalyst, and computer scientist. His 1936 paper “On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem” defined the universal Turing machine and thereby the theoretical foundation for general-purpose digital computation. During the Second World War he was the leading scientific figure at Bletchley Park in the breaking of the German Enigma cipher and related systems, work that made a material contribution to Allied victory. After the war he pursued the practical construction of computers (ACE project at the National Physical Laboratory; Manchester Mark I) while also exploring morphogenesis and the philosophical question of machine intelligence (the “Turing test”).

In 1952 Turing was convicted of gross indecency for a consensual homosexual relationship. He accepted chemical castration (stilboestrol) as an alternative to prison. He died by cyanide poisoning on 7 June 1954; the inquest recorded suicide. His wartime contributions remained secret for decades; public recognition of his role in the origins of computing and in the war effort rose dramatically only in the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

## Role and Significance

Turing stands at the intersection of three 20th-century transformations: the formalisation of computation, the application of abstract mathematics to total war, and the collision between individual sexual autonomy and the mid-century security state. His life is a case study in how a single mind can reconfigure what machines can do while remaining subject to the social and legal constraints of his time.

## Key Events and Processes

- Formation at Sherborne School and King’s College, Cambridge; friendship with Christopher Morcom (d. 1930).
- 1936 paper on computable numbers and the universal machine.
- Bletchley Park service 1939–1945 (bombe design, naval Enigma, organisational and technical leadership).
- Post-war computer projects (ACE, Manchester).
- 1952 arrest, trial, and “treatment”; death in 1954.
