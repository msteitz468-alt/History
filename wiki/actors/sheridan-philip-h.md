# Philip H. Sheridan

Philip Henry Sheridan (1831–1888) was one of the Union's most aggressive field commanders, rising from infantry brigade command (Perryville, Stones River, Chickamauga, and a decisive charge at Missionary Ridge, 1862–1863) to cavalry corps command under [[grant-ulysses-s|Grant]] in 1864, and finally to independent army command in the Shenandoah Valley.

Grant gave Sheridan command of the Army of the Shenandoah in August 1864 with orders to destroy the valley's agricultural capacity to supply Confederate armies (the "Burning") and to eliminate the Confederate threat to Washington posed by [[early-jubal-a|Jubal Early]]. Sheridan defeated Early decisively at Winchester and Fisher's Hill, then, after a surprise Confederate counterattack nearly routed his army at Cedar Creek in October 1864, personally rallied his troops in a celebrated ride from Winchester to reverse the battle — cementing his reputation and effectively ending organized Confederate resistance in the Valley.

Recalled east in early 1865, Sheridan's cavalry played the decisive role in the Appomattox Campaign, cutting off Lee's retreat at Five Forks and Sailor's Creek and helping force the surrender at Appomattox Court House. After the war he commanded frontier campaigns against the Plains peoples and succeeded [[william-t-sherman|Sherman]] as commanding general of the U.S. Army (1883–1888).
