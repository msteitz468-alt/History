# Horemheb

The **general who became pharaoh** and completed the dismantling of the Amarna
order — the last king of the Eighteenth Dynasty and, by his choice of successor, the
bridge to the Nineteenth. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]]
(Ch. XIX, Aldred). Reign ~27 years (volume chronology, anchored on the Mes
inscription).

## From staff officer to king

A career soldier — "**Great Commander of the Army**" and King's Deputy under the boy
[[tutankhamun|Tutankhamun]] — Horemheb was a "staff officer rather than a field
commander" whose organizing ability brought him to power. He acted as the king's
mouthpiece to foreign envoys and provincial governors, continued in office under
[[unknown|Ay]], and succeeded him (probably as designated heir) without direct royal
blood, his queen **Mutnodjme** apparently providing the dynastic link. He presented
himself **not as a usurper but as the legitimate restorer**, even usurping
Tutankhamun's Restoration Stela.

## Restorer and reformer

Two great projects define the reign:
- **The erasure of Amarna.** He restored the temples "from one end of the land to
  the other," and at Karnak built the Second, Ninth and Tenth Pylons and began the
  Great Hypostyle Hall — **filling their cores with thousands of dismantled blocks
  from [[akhenaten|Akhenaten's]] Aten temples**, physically demolishing the heretic's
  monuments to build his own. (These reused *talatat* blocks have let modern scholars
  reconstruct the lost Aten temples.)
- **The Edict of Horemheb.** His great Karnak decree attacks the **corruption,
  extortion and "arbitrary exactions" of an undisciplined army and fraudulent
  tax-collectors** — the legacy of Akhenaten's over-centralized neglect. It restores
  supreme judicial power to **viziers in Memphis and Thebes**, reorganizes the local
  courts under salaried, respected judges, and threatens "savage punishments" for
  bribery. It is a major document of New Kingdom administration and of the
  **over-mighty army** that the next dynasty would be founded on.

## Significance

Horemheb closes the Amarna episode and **stabilizes the post-revolution state**, but
his deeper significance is dynastic: childless, he passed the throne to his
vizier-general **Ramesses I**, "regarded as founding a new dynasty" — the
[[unknown|Nineteenth Dynasty]] of Sethos I and Ramesses II (Section 3). The
later king-lists treated him, not Tutankhamun or Ay, as the legitimate successor of
[[amenhotep-iii|Amenophis III]], writing the Amarna kings out of history.

## Historiography

Documented by his **Memphite private tomb** (built before his kingship, its reliefs
now scattered across museums), his **Theban royal tomb** (KV57, the first to carry
the *Book of Gates*), the **Coronation Inscription** and the **Edict**. The length of
his reign and his identity with the official **Paatenemheb** at Amarna are debated.
