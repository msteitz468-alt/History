# Khosrow II

**Khosrow II** (Khusro II / Khusrau / Chosroes; r. 590–628) was the last great [[sasanian-empire|Sasanian]] king of kings — restored with Maurice’s help in 591, then the author of the 603–628 war that briefly took Syria, Palestine, and Egypt before [[heraclius|Heraclius]]’s counter-offensive and Khosrow’s overthrow.

## Career (Mitchell)

Mitchell’s mid-sixth to early-seventh Rome–Persia frame is dominated by **three major acts of aggression**: [[khosrow-i|Khusro I]] in 540, Justin II in 573, and **Khusro II in 603**. After Bahram’s usurpation, Maurice restored Khusro (591); Phocas’s murder of Maurice gave Khusro a pretext to tear up the settlement. Persian armies under generals including Shahrvaraz overran the Near East; the coordinated Avar–Persian threat peaked at the [[siege-of-constantinople-626|siege of Constantinople (626)]]. Heraclius’s eastern campaigns and the 627 Nineveh victory unseated Khosro; he was killed in 628. Within a generation the Sasanian state fell to the Arabs (Qadisiyya, Nihavand; Yazdgird III killed 651).

## Counterfactual significance

Without the 603–628 war’s mutual exhaustion, the speed of [[islamic-conquests-seventh-eighth-centuries|early Islamic conquests]] in both Roman and Sasanian space is harder to explain — though Mitchell’s Ch 13 also stresses plague-driven demographic recession as a deeper eastern cause.
