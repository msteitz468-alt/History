# Creek (Muscogee) Confederacy

Muskogean multi-town confederacy of the Southeast; central player in colonial play-off diplomacy, the Creek War of 1813–14, and forced Removal to [[indian-territory]]. Green, CHNPA I.1 ch. 8; colonial synthesis Salisbury ch. 7. Prefer this slug over “Muskogee”/“Five Tribes” generic frames.

## Overview

Post-Mississippian ethnogenesis of confederated polities (Smith via Green’s volume context): refugee-survivor regrouping after disease and contact. Colonial era: English “Creek” trade label; Yamasee War; **Brims** of Coweta’s sophisticated multipolar play-off (French Fort Toulouse, Spanish Mexico City, English Carolina). Queen Anne’s War alignments; by 1712 Creeks among most powerful Native forces in the Southeast. Revolution: warriors participated but no invading armies leveled Creek towns (unlike Cherokees).

## McGillivray and early U.S. treaties

**Alexander McGillivray** (Scottish trader father; Creek Wind-clan mother; bicultural leader) held the Creek Nation a sovereign independent nation whose rights Peace of Paris 1783 could not impair (letter 10 July 1785 to Spanish West Florida). Treaty of Augusta 1783 (Georgia, handful of headmen) demanded three million acres; Pensacola 1784 with Spain; Creek confederacy limits—no central government to coerce towns. 1786 Creek/Spanish war on Georgia; Constitution empowered federal support of Georgia. **Treaty of New York 1790:** peace; U.S. protection; Georgia right-of-conquest treaties treated invalid; partial cession; federal monopoly; civilization implements. Benjamin Hawkins agent 1796–1816.

## Creek War, Fort Mims, Horseshoe Bend (atrocity-dense)

Lower (eastern) towns more responsive to Hawkins; Upper (western) less. 1805 large cession + road; 1811 U.S. built road over objections. [[tecumseh]] 1811 success mainly Upper Creeks; civil war after National Council (William McIntosh) executed settlers’ killers. **Aug 1813 Fort Mims:** Nativist warriors defeated and **slaughtered** a compound of “civilized” Creeks and families → general war. [[jackson-andrew]] with Choctaw, Cherokee, and Creek accommodationist allies. **March 1814 Horseshoe Bend:** ~**800** Nativist warriors with prophets killed; Aug 1814 Jackson dictated **twenty million acres** cession; Nativists dead, hiding, or exile among [[seminole|Seminoles]] in Florida. Creek military power shattered; Black Belt cotton boom on the cession.

## Political adaptation and Indian Springs

Less full embrace of civilization than Cherokees: some Lower Town plow agriculture/slaves/cotton; only two mission schools; written laws 1818, national police, forbid clan vengeance—**no constitution**. **Jan 1825 Indian Springs:** McIntosh private deal ceded all Georgia-claimed Creek lands + ~two-thirds west of Chattahoochee in Alabama—violated National Council law; **McIntosh and cronies executed 1 May 1825** by national police; near federal–Georgia war.

## Removal (atrocity-dense)

Jackson furious Creeks refused Franklin 1830. Alabama law made lives miserable; winter 1831–2 Council caved under harassment and Jackson’s refusal to enforce protective treaties. **March 1832** allotment treaty under **Opothle Yoholo**. Alabama wrecked the scheme: land grabbers evicted allotments; fake court charges; **grave robbers stole jewelry from the dead and buried**; hunting by Indians illegal; women could not plant/harvest in peace; **people starved**. 1836 after minor incidents Jackson sent troops (election year; claimed possible Seminole common cause); Army rounded up and marched them off; abandoned allotments/improvements/property; reached new homes in one of the **coldest, iciest, snowiest winters on record**.

## Indian Territory and Civil War

Corn surpluses (100,000 bushels for Irish famine 1846); deep Civil War factional bloodshed; 1866 treaty sold western half of country.
