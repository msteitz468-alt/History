# David M. Glantz and Jonathan M. House

Co-authors of *When Titans Clashed: How the Red Army Stopped Hitler* (1995; revised and expanded 2015), the standard operational history of the Eastern Front in World War II.

**David M. Glantz**: Retired U.S. Army colonel, former director of the Combat Studies Institute at Fort Leavenworth, and founder/editor of *The Journal of Slavic Military Studies*. Leading Western expert on the Red Army and Soviet operational art. Author of dozens of books drawing on Russian archives, including studies of specific operations (Stalingrad, Kursk, Bagration, Manchuria 1945), Soviet military deception (*maskirovka*), and the development of deep battle theory. His work shifted the historiography from predominantly German-memoir sources toward a balanced, archive-grounded Soviet perspective.

**Jonathan M. House**: Retired U.S. Army officer and professor of military history at the U.S. Army Command and General Staff College. Specialist in combined-arms warfare and 20th-century military operations. Co-author with Glantz on multiple works that synthesize archival evidence with operational analysis.

Together they provide the clearest English-language account of how the Red Army recovered from the 1941 disasters, mastered large-scale mechanized operations, and destroyed the Wehrmacht in the East through successive periods of defense, counteroffensive, and strategic offensive. Their emphasis on force ratios, logistics, command adaptation, and deception makes the book the primary reference for the wiki's coverage of the Soviet-German War.

## Key contributions (from the source)
- Three-period framework of the war (First Period 1941–Nov 1942; Second Nov 1942–Dec 1943; Third Jan 1944–May 1945).
- Detailed critique of German explanations for defeat (Hitler alone, hordes, weather/terrain as decisive).
- Documentation of Red Army evolution in operational art, maskirovka, mobile groups, and Rear Services.
- Extensive statistical tables on orders of battle, strengths, losses, and production.
