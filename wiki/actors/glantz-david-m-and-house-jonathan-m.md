# David M. Glantz and Jonathan M. House

Co-authors of *When Titans Clashed: How the Red Army Stopped Hitler* (1995; revised and expanded 2015), the standard operational history of the Eastern Front in World War II.

**David M. Glantz**: Retired U.S. Army colonel, former director of the Combat Studies Institute at Fort Leavenworth, and founder/editor of *The Journal of Slavic Military Studies*. Leading Western expert on the Red Army and Soviet operational art. Author of dozens of books drawing on Russian archives, including studies of specific operations (Stalingrad, Kursk, Bagration, Manchuria 1945), Soviet military deception (*maskirovka*), and the development of deep battle theory. His work shifted the historiography from predominantly German-memoir sources toward a balanced, archive-grounded Soviet perspective.

**Jonathan M. House**: Retired U.S. Army officer and professor of military history at the U.S. Army Command and General Staff College. Specialist in combined-arms warfare and 20th-century military operations. Co-author with Glantz on multiple works that synthesize archival evidence with operational analysis.

Together they provide the clearest English-language account of how the Red Army recovered from the 1941 disasters, mastered large-scale mechanized operations, and destroyed the Wehrmacht in the East through successive periods of defense, counteroffensive, and strategic offensive. Their emphasis on force ratios, logistics, command adaptation, and deception makes the book the primary reference for the wiki's coverage of the Soviet-German War.

See also: [[sources/glantz-house-when-titans-clashed-2015]], [[sources/glantz-house-to-the-gates-of-stalingrad-2009]] (detailed operational history of Fall Blau / drive to Stalingrad, April–early Sep 1942). Vol. I supplies granular Ch. 2 Soviet rebuild (new tank corps/armies), strategic debate, 1 May 1942 OOB, Khar’kov plan details, commander experience assessments, and soldier quality analysis that underpin the wiki's operational analysis of the 1942 southern campaign.

## Key contributions (from the source)
- Three-period framework of the war (First Period 1941–Nov 1942; Second Nov 1942–Dec 1943; Third Jan 1944–May 1945).
- Detailed critique of German explanations for defeat (Hitler alone, hordes, weather/terrain as decisive).
- Documentation of Red Army evolution in operational art, maskirovka, mobile groups, and Rear Services.
- Extensive statistical tables on orders of battle, strengths, losses, and production.

## Contributions from *To the Gates of Stalingrad* (2009, Vol. I)
- Full operational history of the 1942 German southern campaign (Apr–Aug 1942 to Volga "gates") and Soviet response, emphasizing the "path to the city" and flanks that exhausted Sixth Army before urban fighting. "Contrary to popular belief, the city of Stalingrad was not the original objective... only a passing reference in the original German operations order."
- New sources: daily German/Soviet records (incl. first use of NKVD reports for independent view), 62nd Army/subunit records, German divisional histories, post-1980s German official history, new Russian historians. Corrects Chuikov memoir errors on unit locations/compositions/actions and earlier Western/German accounts.
- Range 01 (Preface, Prologue, Ch. 1): German 1941–42 rebuilding (917,985 casualties by 31 Jan; ~2.5M men/1,700 tanks East by Mar; only 8/162 divs capable; reduced infantry/panzer strength); satellite armies (52 divs: 27 Rom etc., deficiencies for mech war); Plan Fall Blau (Directive 41 verbatim + phased design, Voronezh/Millerovo/Stalingrad/Caucasus axes, AG split, logistics challenges 760+ km); April 1942 Axis OOB (AG South details, Paulus/Bock/commanders); Prologue 5th Tank Army Voronezh counterstroke (23 Jul 1942) as microcosm of Soviet coordination problems yet delaying effect ("repeated Soviet counterstrokes of this kind delayed and weakened the Germans long before they reached Stalingrad").
- Updates [[hubs/warfare/world-wars-era/second-world-war/battle-of-stalingrad-1942]] (Strategic Context, OOB table, Critical Decisions, Principles of War, Historiography) and [[events/battle-of-stalingrad-1942]] (pre-city planning context, causes). Supports [[concepts/plan-fall-blau]], [[actors/paulus-friedrich]], [[actors/bock-fedor-von]]. See source page Range 01 block for verbatim grounding.
