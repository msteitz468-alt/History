# Abenaki

Algonquian peoples of Maine and adjacent regions (Eastern Abenaki including Kennebec, Penobscot; Western groups). Sought autonomy on the New England–New France frontier (Salisbury).

## Overview

### Early contact and Micmac pressure

Penobscot sagamore Bashaba failed to trade with both Port Royal and English Kennebec (1605); Abenakis no match for Micmac metal weapons (Chouacoet 1607; again 1615, Bashaba killed). English contacts often violent, especially with alcohol. Plymouth traders from 1625 traded wampum and maize for pelts among Kennebec Abenakis.

### Druillettes and anti-Iroquois diplomacy

1630s Kennebecs’ New France wampum introduction initially rejected (fear Canadian furs to English). Jesuit **Gabriel Druillettes** (1646) during deadly epidemic planted syncretic “conversion” by selfless care and preaching compatible with tradition; 1650 political return sought anti-Iroquois alliance after Huron dispersal—failed to enlist Puritan colonies but gained Sokokis, Pawtuckets at Pennacook, Mahicans.

### King Philip’s era and Dummer’s War

Eastern Abenakis initially neutral in 1675; English ammunition restrictions and drowning of an Indian child brought all to arms by summer 1676; fought to standoff; peace Aug 1677 / treaty 1678—contrast with southern New England catastrophe. After Utrecht, English return and unregulated Maine private trade alienated Abenakis; Norridgewock attacked settlement 1721; war 1722–25 with open New France/missionary support (**Sébastien Rale** killed when English destroyed Norridgewock). Peace 1725–27 acknowledged British sovereignty with subsistence/religion protections; tensions resumed. Revolution: initial rebel lean conditional on goods/victories.
