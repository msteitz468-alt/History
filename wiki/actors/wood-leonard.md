# Leonard Wood

US Army physician-turned-general, commander of the Rough Riders regiment
(with [[roosevelt-theodore|Theodore Roosevelt]] as his second-in-command)
during the Spanish-American War, later military governor of occupied Cuba
and Governor-General of the Philippines.

## Role and Major Decisions

Wood commanded the 1st US Volunteer Cavalry ("Rough Riders") in the 1898
Cuban campaign, then served as military governor of Cuba (1899–1902), where
he oversaw yellow-fever eradication measures (informed by Walter Reed's
research) and administrative reconstruction ahead of Cuban independence. He
later commanded the Philippines Department and served as Governor-General of
the Philippines (1921–1927), and was a leading contender for the 1920
Republican presidential nomination.

## Counterfactual Significance

Wood's early sponsorship and later close political partnership with
Theodore Roosevelt were central to Roosevelt's rise to national prominence;
Wood's own presidential ambitions, cut short by his narrow loss of the 1920
nomination to Warren Harding, represent a significant might-have-been in
early twentieth-century American politics.

## Historiography

Wood is documented extensively through his own military and colonial
administrative records and through his close association with Roosevelt's
much better-chronicled career; his colonial governorships in Cuba and the
Philippines are increasingly examined by historians of American empire as
case studies in US colonial administration and public-health policy.
