# Nikita Khrushchev
## Biography / Description
Nikita Khrushchev was born in 1894 to a peasant family in Kalinovka (near the Ukrainian border) and worked as a metalworker before joining the Bolsheviks in 1918. He rose through the Party ranks under Stalin's patronage, serving as Party boss of Moscow and later of Ukraine, and participating in the Politburo. Following Stalin's death in March 1953, Khrushchev became First Secretary of the Communist Party. He successfully navigated the succession struggle, eliminating Beria in 1953 and defeating the "Anti-Party Group" of Malenkov, Molotov, and Kaganovich in 1957. In 1958, he also assumed the premiership. He ruled until October 15, 1964, when he was abruptly deposed by a coalition of Party leaders led by Leonid Brezhnev. Khrushchev lived in quiet retirement until his death in 1971.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **Secret Speech and De-Stalinization (1956):** At the Twentieth Party Congress, Khrushchev delivered a closed-session speech denouncing Stalin as a cruel, irrational tyrant, initiating the "thaw" and the rehabilitation of purge victims. At the Twenty-second Congress (1961), he intensified this policy, removing Stalin's body from the Red Square mausoleum.
- **Virgin Lands Project (1954):** To resolve the chronic agricultural crisis, Khrushchev opened up seventy million acres of arid lands in Siberia and Kazakhstan for cultivation.
- **Sino-Soviet Split (1956–1964):** Khrushchev's policies of de-Stalinization and "peaceful coexistence" with the West alienated Mao Zedong's China, leading to a bitter ideological and geopolitical split that fractured the communist bloc.
- **Crushing of the Hungarian Revolution (1956):** Despite his rhetoric of liberalization, Khrushchev ordered Soviet tanks to invade Budapest in November 1956 to crush Nagy's coalition government and keep Hungary within the Warsaw Pact.
- **Sputnik and Space Age (1957):** Khrushchev heavily sponsored rocket and space programs, leading to the launch of Sputnik I (October 1957) and Yuri Gagarin's historic space flight (1961), which boosted Soviet prestige.
- **Cuban Missile Crisis (1962):** Khrushchev ordered the installation of nuclear missiles in Cuba, leading to a direct thermonuclear standoff with US President John F. Kennedy. His subsequent decision to withdraw the missiles in exchange for US concessions was viewed as a capitulation by his domestic critics, contributing to his fall.

## Historical Significance
Khrushchev was a transitional leader whose rule marked a shift away from Stalinist mass terror toward a milder, more consumer-oriented version of authoritarianism. Riasanovsky characterizes him as an enthusiastic, ebullient, and sometimes erratic reformer of peasant background (Line 6515, 6523). His foreign policy was marked by a tense search for "peaceful coexistence" alongside highly aggressive maneuvers. His erratic reorganizations (such as splitting the Party into industrial and agricultural branches in 1962) and economic failures ultimately led to his peaceful removal from office in 1964, a sign that the Soviet system had become more institutionalized and less dependent on a single despot.
