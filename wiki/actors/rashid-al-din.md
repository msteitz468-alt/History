# Rashid al-Din Hamadani

A Persian physician of Jewish origin (a convert to Islam) who rose to become
chief vizier of the [[ilkhanate|Ilkhanate]] under the Mongol rulers Ghazan
and Öljeitü, and the principal author of the *Jami' al-Tawarikh*
("Compendium of Chronicles"), the first attempt at a genuinely world
history, covering the Mongols, Turks, Chinese, Franks, and Indians within a
single universal framework.

## Role and Major Decisions

As vizier, Rashid al-Din oversaw major fiscal and administrative reforms
intended to stabilize the Ilkhanate's finances and reconcile Mongol rule
with the settled Perso-Islamic administrative tradition. His *Jami'
al-Tawarikh*, compiled with a scholarly team using Mongol oral tradition,
Chinese sources, and eyewitness testimony, remains a foundational primary
source for Mongol history. He was executed in 1318 on charges (widely
regarded by modern historians as politically motivated) of having poisoned
Öljeitü.

## Counterfactual Significance

Rashid al-Din's history preserved Mongol imperial tradition and genealogy in
a form otherwise largely inaccessible outside Mongolia and China; its
absence would leave a substantially thinner documentary record for Ilkhanid
and broader Mongol imperial history.

## Historiography

The *Jami' al-Tawarikh*'s scope and method (deliberate cross-cultural
synthesis, drawing on informants from multiple traditions) make it a
frequently cited early exemplar of "world history" as a genre; modern
scholars treat it as an invaluable but not neutral source, shaped by its
author's position at court and his need to legitimize Ilkhanid rule within
both Mongol and Islamic frameworks.
