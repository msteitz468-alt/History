# The Order of the Golden Fleece

The premier chivalric order of the [[valois-burgundian-state|Valois Burgundian state]], founded by
**Philip the Good** at Bruges in January 1430 on his marriage to Isabel of Portugal. Discussed by
Bertrand Schnerb in [[allmand-ncmh-v7-1998|NCMH VII]] (Ch. 20) and by Philippe Contamine on the
European nobility (Ch. 4), where it appears as the model instance of the late-medieval prince's use
of a chivalric **order** to gather the nobility into his service.

## Founding Conditions and Function

Philip had three aims: to **bind the aristocracy** of all his scattered lands to himself personally;
to create a **single institution uniting nobles from every Burgundian territory** (it was, as
Contamine notes, "the only centralised and centralising institution of a rather amorphous
Burgundy"); and to **enhance the prestige** of the founder-prince as the order's sovereign. Placed
under a pagan and a biblical patron (Jason and Gideon), it also carried the resonance of the
**crusading ideal** and the lure of the east.

## How It Shaped Events

Above all the order was "a political instrument in the hands of the duke." Initially restricted to
ducal subjects and vassals, its growing prestige soon gave it **diplomatic leverage**: the collar
was bestowed on princes wholly unconnected with Burgundy — John V of Brittany, Charles of Orléans,
Alfonso V of Aragon — much as the English **Order of the Garter** drew in foreign members. After the
fall of Charles the Bold (1477) the order passed with the Burgundian inheritance to the **house of
Habsburg**, becoming a great dynastic order of the Habsburg (and later Spanish and Austrian)
monarchies.
