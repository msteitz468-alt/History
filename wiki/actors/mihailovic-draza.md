# Draža Mihailović

Yugoslav Royal Army colonel (later general, 1893–1946) who organized the Chetnik movement, a Serbian nationalist and royalist resistance force, after the Axis invasion and partition of Yugoslavia in April 1941. Initially recognized by the Yugoslav government-in-exile and the Western Allies as the legitimate resistance leader, Mihailović's Chetniks fought the Axis occupation intermittently but increasingly prioritized combating the rival Communist-led Partisans under Tito and, in various regions, engaged in ethnically-targeted violence against Croats and Muslims, while some Chetnik commanders entered into local accommodations or collaboration with Axis and collaborationist forces to avoid German reprisals and to focus on the Partisan threat.

As the war progressed, British and Allied support shifted decisively from Mihailović to Tito's Partisans (1943), who proved more consistently effective against the Axis and better organized as a genuinely multi-ethnic movement. After the war, the new Communist Yugoslav government captured, tried, and executed Mihailović in 1946 on charges of treason and wartime collaboration, cementing Tito's Partisans as the officially sanctioned resistance narrative — though the Chetniks' historical reputation has remained a subject of intense controversy and revision, especially after the collapse of Yugoslavia in the 1990s.

## Significance

Mihailović's trajectory — from recognized Allied resistance leader to sidelined rival and finally trial for collaboration — illustrates both the fractured, multi-sided nature of the Yugoslav wartime civil conflict and the way postwar political victors shaped the historical memory of resistance and collaboration.
