## Overview

The Luwians were one of several **Indo-European-speaking peoples** whose presence in Anatolia
is attested in second-millennium written sources, alongside the **Hittite- (strictly Nesite-)
speaking** group of north-central Anatolia and the **Palaic-speaking** group south of the Black
Sea. Most scholars date the arrival of these peoples to the Early Bronze Age (some propose a
much earlier entry); their early dispersal produced the marked linguistic differentiation we
see in M2 records. The Luwian-speaking group had, by the Late Bronze Age, **dispersed widely
through Anatolia** (Bryce 2009: 427–9).

Crucially, "**Luwiya**" — a name attested in the earliest versions of the [[hittites|Hittite]]
laws (and later replaced there by "Arzawa") — was **never a single political entity**. Its
connotation was ethno-geographical: a general region with many Luwian speakers but no fixed
territorial limits, perhaps a term of convenience used only by outsiders. There is, therefore,
no "Luwian empire"; the Luwians are a linguistic-cultural people, not a state.

## Language, script, and distribution

The Luwian language is attested in **two scripts**: cuneiform (religious passages embedded in
Hittite festival, ritual, and incantation texts at [[hattusha|Hattusa]], marked *luwili*,
"in the language of Luwiya") and a distinctive **hieroglyphic** script of pictographic symbols.
The earliest hieroglyphic example is a C16 seal of Isputahsu, king of Kizzuwadna, but most
hieroglyphic texts date to C13 and — overwhelmingly — to **c. 1100–700, the period of the
[[neo-hittite-states|Neo-Hittite kingdoms]]**. Decipherment was greatly aided by the C8
**Luwian–Phoenician bilingual** from **Karatepe** in eastern Cilicia (discovered 1946).

By the middle of M2, Luwian-speaking groups occupied much of southern Anatolia — from Classical
Lycia through Pamphylia, Pisidia, Lycaonia, and Isauria to Cilicia — and gave rise to Hittite-
period states such as **Kizzuwadna** (Hurrian–Luwian) and **Tarhuntassa** (created by
[[hittites|Muwattalli II]] in early C13). Substantial numbers also lived in central Anatolia,
including the Hittite homeland, partly through the deportation of conquered populations.

## A live debate

How far Luwians actually *settled* in Iron Age Syria is contested. The continued use of the
hieroglyphic script by the surviving branches of the Hittite royal family in Syria and southern
Anatolia is **not** proof of a Luwian-speaking population there: Bryce stresses the script was a
carry-over from Late Bronze Age Hittite royalty and, like the title "Great King," a trapping of
kingship adopted by later, lesser rulers. Apart from the inscriptions, there is little
independent evidence (e.g., a concentration of Luwian personal names) for a significant Luwian
presence in Iron Age Syria — whereas in **southern Anatolia** the persistence of Luwian names
through M1 (especially in Lycia and Rough Cilicia) does indicate the survival of Luwian
population groups, in some areas down to the Hellenistic and Roman periods. I. Yakubovich has
further argued the Luwian core lay in central Anatolia (the Konya plain), challenging the older
assumption that western Anatolia was Luwian-dominated for much of M2.
