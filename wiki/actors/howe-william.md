# Sir William Howe (1729–1814)

British commander in chief in North America from October 1775 to May 1778. A Seven Years' War
veteran (he led the forlorn hope up the Heights of Abraham at Quebec in 1759) and developer of a
new light-infantry drill, Howe was, in the *West Point History*'s judgment, repeatedly
"superior to [[washington-george|Washington]] … as a battlefield tactician and an operational
commander" — yet he never won the war.

He commanded the assault at **[[battle-of-bunker-hill-1775|Bunker Hill]]** (June 1775), whose
appalling cost left him with a lasting "reluctance to storm fixed defenses." He out-generaled
Washington at **[[battle-of-long-island-1776|Long Island]]**, captured New York, and took
Philadelphia after **[[battle-of-brandywine-1777|Brandywine]]** and **[[battle-of-germantown-1777|
Germantown]]**. But as a **liberal Whig** who "sympathized with American grievances," Howe
"sought not to destroy the American army but to inflict defeats that would bring the rebels to
the peace table" — and so "missed multiple opportunities to break Washington's army," most
glaringly by declining to storm Brooklyn Heights in 1776. He also failed to march up the Hudson
to support [[burgoyne-john|Burgoyne]], helping doom the Saratoga campaign. He lost political
favor and departed for Britain in May 1778, succeeded by [[clinton-henry|Clinton]].
