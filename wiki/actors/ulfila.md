# Ulfila (Wulfila)

The "**apostle of the Goths**" (c. 311–383), the Gothic bishop who **converted the Goths to (Arian)
Christianity**, **invented the Gothic alphabet**, and **translated the Bible into Gothic** — producing
the earliest substantial text in any Germanic language and shaping the religious identity of the whole
Germanic world for centuries.

## Life and Mission

Of mixed Gothic and Cappadocian-Christian descent (his forebears were Roman captives taken in the
third-century raids), Ulfila grew up among the **Tervingi** north of the Danube. Sent to
Constantinople, he was **consecrated bishop c. 341** (by Eusebius of Nicomedia and the Homoian party)
and returned to evangelize his people. Persecution by the Gothic judge **Athanaric** drove Ulfila and
his converts across the Danube into Roman **Moesia** (c. 348), where the emperor **Constantius II**
settled them — Ulfila living out his life as bishop of the "Gothi minores." He died in **Constantinople
in 383**, professing to the end the **Homoian ("Arian")** faith.

## The Gothic Bible

Ulfila's monumental achievement was to render Scripture into **Gothic**, for which he **devised a new
alphabet** (drawing on Greek, Latin and runic letters). The translation — of which the most famous
surviving witness is the sixth-century **Codex Argenteus** ("Silver Bible") — is the **oldest extensive
document of a Germanic language** and a unique linguistic treasure. (Tradition says he omitted the books
of Kings, lest their wars inflame an already warlike people.)

## Significance

Because Ulfila and his successors preached a **non-Nicene, Homoian Christianity**, the Goths — and,
through them, the **Vandals, Ostrogoths, Burgundians, Sueves and Lombards** — entered the post-Roman
world as **Arians**, divided in faith from their Catholic Roman subjects. This religious gulf is one of
the master-themes of Late Antiquity: it underlies the **Vandal persecution** of Nicene Africa, the long
estrangement of Visigothic Spain until **Reccared's conversion** at the
**[[third-council-of-toledo-589|Third Council of Toledo (589)]]**, and the distinctiveness of the
**[[germanic-peoples|Germanic]]** successor kingdoms generally.

## From Mitchell, Later Roman Empire (2015)

Attributed claims from [[sources/mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Stephen Mitchell, *A History of the Later Roman Empire* (2015)]].

- **Gothic Christianity and Arian ethnic identity.** Ulfila was consecrated by Eusebius of Nicomedia (336 or 341); Christian Goths expelled in the late 340s to Nicopolis (Lower Moesia); he devised the Gothic alphabet and Bible translation. The 376 Tervingi crossing required Christian profession, with Ulfila as spokesman and Gothic priests as go-betweens before [[battle-of-adrianople-378|Adrianople]]. Socrates/Theodoret link Gothic Arianism to the Valens/Eudoxius moment; Sozomen stresses Ulfila’s pre-Nicene gospel primitivism hostile to non-biblical *homoousion* (mentor Eusebius of Nicomedia). Constantinople 381 can. 2 excepted barbarian churches to “usage of the Fathers”; the 382 *foedus* left Goths Arian like Valens while Romans were neo-Nicene under [[theodosius-i|Theodosius]]. After 381 Arianism became mainly an ethnic marker of federal Germans rather than an internal Roman christological issue — Ambrose vs Justina’s Arian Easter liturgy for Gothic troops; Chrysostom banned Gothic Arian liturgy inside Constantinople; Vandals used Arianism for nation-building and African Catholic persecution. Mitchell’s summary formula: “Orthodoxy was thus not merely equated with being Christian, but with being Roman. Conversely, the barbarians were fully aware of the importance of their Arian identity in constructing their own ethnic cohesion.” Related: [[arianism|Arianism]]; [[late-roman-church-institution|late Roman church]].
