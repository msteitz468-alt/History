# Gürtner, Franz (1881–1941)

Conservative German jurist and Reich Minister of Justice (1932–1941) under Papen, Schleicher, and Hitler. Career civil servant in the Bavarian and then Reich justice systems; not a Nazi Party member until late. Chaired the critical June 5, 1934 Justice Ministry meeting at which American race law was presented and discussed as a model for the future Nuremberg Laws. Represented the "outdated type of jurist" (in radical Nazi eyes) committed to legal science, conceptual coherence, and limits on arbitrary legislation.

## Role and Significance

Gürtner chaired the planning session for what became the Reich Citizenship Law and Blood Law. He presented the Ministry's prepared memo on US race legislation and allowed extensive discussion, including radical interventions by Roland Freisler. While he did not block the radical direction, he and his staff (notably Bernhard Lösener) pushed for modifications on grounds of legal "science" and workability. He remained in office through the escalation of the 1930s, lending an appearance of continuity and legality to the regime's justice apparatus even as it was hollowed out.

## Key Events / Processes

- June 5, 1934 meeting: chaired the session; presented the Ministry's prepared memo on American race law. Allowed detailed discussion (radicals championed US models; moderates including himself pushed back on grounds of legal science and the fact that US law was not directed at Jews and classified them as Caucasian). Represented the "outdated type of jurist" committed to conceptual coherence and limits on arbitrary power.
- Retained conservative instincts on some procedural questions but yielded to the "will of the Führer" on core racial policy.
- Later involved in the Blomberg-Fritsch affair (1938) and other episodes of regime consolidation of legal power.

## Counterfactual Note

Had Gürtner and the traditional jurists prevailed more fully in 1933–34, the legal architecture of persecution might have been slower, more hedged with formal requirements, and less immediately usable for mass operations. The radicals' victory in sidelining "legal science" accelerated the shift toward discretionary, goal-oriented law ("working toward the Führer").

## Historiography

Traditional accounts often present Gürtner as a figure of limited resistance or tragic accommodation. Whitman uses the 1934 transcript to show him as still able, in mid-1934, to invoke juristic tradition against the most extreme proposals, even while the political tide had turned. Detailed treatment in the legal-history literature on the drafting of the Nuremberg Laws.
