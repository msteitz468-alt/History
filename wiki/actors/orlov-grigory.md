# Grigory Orlov

Count Grigory Grigorievich Orlov (1734–1783) was a Russian artillery officer and one of the principal organizers of the 1762 palace coup that placed Catherine II on the throne. He became Catherine's early favorite and lover; the Orlov brothers (Grigory, Alexei, and others) formed the core military support for the coup against Peter III.

## Role in the 1762 Coup

Orlov, serving in the Izmailovsky Guards, mobilized key regiments. His brothers played supporting roles (Alexei notably in the confrontation with Peter III at Ropsha). After the coup, Grigory received titles, estates, and influence, though his political weight waned as Catherine consolidated and later favored Potemkin.

## Later Life

Orlov was sent on diplomatic missions (e.g., to the peace negotiations ending the first Turkish war). He and Catherine had a daughter (later Countess Bobrinskaya). His influence declined after 1772.

## Historiography

Massie and narrative sources emphasize the Orlovs' decisive role in the swift, relatively bloodless overthrow. The brothers represented the guards' praetorian power in 18th-century Russian succession politics. Links restricted to established names.
