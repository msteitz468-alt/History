# Sviatoslav I
## Biography / Description
**Sviatoslav I Igorevich** was the Prince of Kiev from 962 to 972 CE. Sviatoslav stands out in history as a classic warrior-prince, simple, severe, indefatigable, and brave, who spent his entire reign on campaign, sharing all hardships with his men (l. 785). He was the first Rurikid ruler to bear a Slavic name, though his appearance, dress, and military tactics closely resembled the nomadic peoples of the steppe.

Under Sviatoslav, early Rus' underwent an astonishing period of expansion—known as "the great adventure"—extending Rurikid influence from the Danube to the Volga and Caspian Sea.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Eastern Campaign (964–967 CE):** Sviatoslav launched a sweeping military offensive. He subjugated the East Slavic tribe of the Viatichi, raided the Volga Bulgars (sacking their capital, Great Bulgar), and launched a crushing campaign against the [[khazar-khaganate|Khazar Khaganate]]. The Russians captured the Khazar capital of **Itil**, Samandar, and the fortress of Sarkil on the Don.
* **Balkan Invasion (968–971 CE):** At the invitation of Byzantine Emperor Nicephorus Phocas, Sviatoslav invaded Bulgaria, capturing the capital and taking Tsar Boris prisoner. He liked the Balkan lands so much that he attempted to move his capital to **Pereiaslavets on the Danube**, declaring it the true center of his wealth and realm (l. 797).
* **War with Byzantium:** Fearing the new Rus' empire on the Danube, the new Byzantine Emperor John Tzimisces launched a counteroffensive. The Russians were defeated and besieged at the fortress of **Dorostolon** on the Danube.
* **Treaty of 971 CE:** After desperate fighting, Sviatoslav signed a peace treaty with John Tzimisces, agreeing to abandon the Balkans and the Crimea, and promising not to attack Constantinople again.

## Historical Significance
Sviatoslav’s eastern campaign completed the unification of the East Slavs around Kiev and secured control over the entire Volga trade route. 

However, his total destruction of the Khazar Khaganate removed the vital buffer that had protected eastern Europe from other Asiatic nomads. This allowed the hostile and primitive Pechenegs to sweep into the Pontic steppe. On his return journey to Kiev in 972 CE, Sviatoslav was ambushed and killed by the Pechenegs at the Dnieper cataracts; the Pecheneg khan reportedly had a drinking cup made out of Sviatoslav’s skull (l. 800).
