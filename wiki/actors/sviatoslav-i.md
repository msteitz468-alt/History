# Sviatoslav I
## Biography / Description
**Sviatoslav I Igorevich** was the Prince of Kiev from 962 to 972 CE. Sviatoslav stands out in history as a classic warrior-prince, simple, severe, indefatigable, and brave, who spent his entire reign on campaign, sharing all hardships with his men (l. 785). He was the first Rurikid ruler to bear a Slavic name, though his appearance, dress, and military tactics closely resembled the nomadic peoples of the steppe. "Stepping light as a leopard... neither wagons nor kettles... cut off small strips... roasted... horse-blanket under him."

Under Sviatoslav, early Rus' underwent an astonishing period of expansion—known as "the great adventure"—extending Rurikid influence from the Danube to the Volga and Caspian Sea. Key from Primary Chronicle (ll. 4454–4800): 965 Khazars (Bela Vezha); 967 Danube (Pereyaslavets); 968 Kiev siege rescue; 971 Greek treaty (Perun/Volos); 972 killed by Pechenegs (skull cup).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Style of rule and early campaigns (956+):** "When Prince Svyatoslav had grown up and matured, he began to collect a numerous and valiant army. Stepping light as a leopard, he undertook many campaigns. Upon his expeditions he carried with him neither wagons nor kettles, and boiled no meat, but cut off small strips of horseflesh, game, or beef, and ate it after roasting it on the coals. Nor did he have a tent, but he spread out a horse-blanket under him, and set his saddle under his head; and all his retinue did likewise. He sent messengers to the other lands announcing his intention to attack them." 966: conquers Vyatichians, imposes plough-tribute "just as his father had done." (ll. 4454–4475).
* **Eastern Campaign (965):** "Svyatoslav sallied forth against the Khazars. When they heard of his approach, they went out to meet him with their Prince, the Kagan... Svyatoslav defeated the Khazars and took their city of Bela Vezha. He also conquered the Yasians and the Kasogians" (ll. 4466–4472).
* **Balkan campaigns and Pereyaslavets (967–969):** "Svyatoslav marched to the Danube to attack the Bulgarians... Svyatoslav overcame the Bulgarians, and captured eighty towns along the Danube. He took up his residence there, and ruled in Pereyaslavets, receiving tribute from the Greeks." To mother/boyars: "I do not care to remain in Kiev, but should prefer to live in Pereyaslavets on the Danube, since that is the centre of my realm, where all riches are concentrated; gold, silks, wine, and various fruits from Greece, silver and horses from Hungary and Bohemia, and from Rus’ furs, wax, honey, and slaves." Olga (ill): "You behold me in my weakness. Why do you desire to depart from me?" (ll. 4476–4484, 4561–4580).
* **Pecheneg siege of Kiev and rescue (968):** While at Pereyaslavets, Pechenegs invade "for the first time." Olga + grandsons (Yaropolk, Oleg, Vladimir) shut in Kiev; "surrounded it with an innumerable multitude... weak from hunger and thirst." Youth volunteers: swims Dnieper (bridle, feigns local, speaks language); warns "if we are not relieved on the morrow, we must perforce surrender." Pretich: "Tomorrow we shall approach by boat... If we do not bring this to pass, Svyatoslav will put us to death." Dawn boats + trumpets; Pechenegs think prince returned, flee. Olga/grandsons to boats. Pecheneg prince meets Pretich alone; gifts exchanged (spear/sabre/arrows for breastplate/shield/sword) to feign vanguard. "The Pechenegs raised the siege". Svyatoslav returns, kisses, "regretted what they had suffered at the hands of the Pechenegs... collected an army, and drove the Pechenegs out into the steppes" (ll. 4485–4560).
* **War with Byzantium and treaty (971):** Returns to Pereyaslavets; Bulgars sally, "great carnage"; Svyatoslav: "Here is where we fall! Let us fight bravely, brothers and companions!" Victorious, takes city. Greeks pay tribute after deception (inflated numbers). Envoys gifts (gold/silks ignored; arms accepted/praised). Advances. Treaty with Johannes Tzimiskes (Silistria, July 6479/971): "I desire to preserve peace and perfect amity with each of the great Emperors... I will therefore contemplate no attack upon your territory, nor will I collect an army... particularly the district of Kherson, or the cities adjacent, or against Bulgaria." "may we be accursed of the god in whom we believe, namely, of Perun and Volos, the god of flocks, and we become yellow as gold, and be slain with our own weapons." Accepts gifts; returns (ll. 4637–4766).
* **Death (972):** On return (Sveinald advises horseback due to Pechenegs at cataracts; ignored): "Svyatoslav approached the cataracts, where Kurya, Prince of the Pechenegs, attacked him; and Svyatoslav was killed. The nomads took his head, and made a cup out of his skull, overlaying it with gold, and they drank from it." "Now all the years of Svyatoslav’s reign were twenty-eight." Sveinald to Yaropolk in Kiev (ll. 4777–4800).

## Historical Significance
Sviatoslav’s eastern campaign completed the unification of the East Slavs around Kiev and secured control over the entire Volga trade route. 

However, his total destruction of the Khazar Khaganate removed the vital buffer that had protected eastern Europe from other Asiatic nomads. This allowed the hostile and primitive Pechenegs to sweep into the Pontic steppe. On his return journey to Kiev in 972 CE, Sviatoslav was ambushed and killed by the Pechenegs at the Dnieper cataracts; the Pecheneg khan reportedly had a drinking cup made out of Sviatoslav’s skull (l. 800).
