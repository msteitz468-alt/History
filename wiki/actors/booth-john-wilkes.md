# John Wilkes Booth

American actor and Confederate sympathizer who assassinated President [[actors/lincoln-abraham|Abraham Lincoln]] at [[places/fords-theatre|Ford's Theatre]] on 14 April 1865. Ellsworth (*Midnight on the Potomac*, 2025) presents Booth not as a solitary madman but as the most visible node in a networked kidnap-then-kill conspiracy involving Confederate agents and a small circle of recruits ([[events/assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-1865]]).

## Overview

Born into a celebrated theatrical family, Booth was a successful matinee idol with strong Southern sympathies. By mid–late 1864 he was recruiting for a plan to seize Lincoln and deliver him to Richmond; after military collapse made that plan obsolete, the plot shifted toward multi-target assassination (Lincoln, Seward, and others). Booth shot Lincoln at Ford's Theatre, fled, and was killed by pursuing Union forces at the Garrett farm on 26 April 1865.

## Role and Significance

- **caused_by / contributed_to:** Booth's act is the immediate agency of [[events/assassination-of-abraham-lincoln-1865]]; enabling context includes Confederate defeat, radicalized proslavery politics, and wartime secret-service networks.
- **Counterfactual:** Without Booth (or a successful plot), Lincoln would have entered Reconstruction alive — altering the Johnson succession and the Radical–presidential balance. Causal weight of the individual assassin vs. structural forces remains a standing historiographical question.

## Historiography

Popular and scholarly literature ranges from lone-gunman psychological portraits to arguments for substantial Confederate Secret Service direction. Ellsworth stresses recruitment, Harbin/Surratt links, and evolution from kidnap to murder while remaining a narrative synthesis rather than a definitive conspiracy monograph. Cross-check specialist Booth/assassination studies against this popular frame.


## Career and Conspiracy (Ellsworth, *Midnight on the Potomac*, 2025)

- **Stage career:** Matinee superstar; first Ford's appearance Nov 1863 packed; Ford paid $700/week; specialty Shakespeare/*Richard III*. Met Tad Lincoln backstage spring 1863 and gave him roses.
- **Ideology:** 1864 private letter framed the country as for the white man, African slavery as a "blessing," abolitionists as traitors. Public Union façade vs private confession to sister Asia that "my soul, life, and possessions are for the South"; quinine smuggling and warnings to Rebel partisans.
- **Parker House (26 Jul 1864):** Register listed Booth with Toronto/Montreal aliases; Cordial Crane sent list to Stanton; no follow-up. Ellsworth (citing *Come Retribution*) reads a Canada-apparatus conference.
- **Recruitment:** Aug 1864 Arnold/O'Laughlen for Soldiers' Home kidnap; Montreal Oct 1864 with Sanders/Martin; Dec 1864 quasi-residency in Washington; weapons cache early 1865; Harbin and John Surratt links.
- **14 Apr 1865:** Shot Lincoln at Ford's; "Sic semper tyrannis!"; 12-day flight; killed in Virginia barn aged 26.
- **Memory:** Ellsworth argues Lost Cause culture later isolated Booth as mad lone actor to protect Confederate leadership reputations—**interpretation**.
