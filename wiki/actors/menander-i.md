# Menander I

The most famous of the **Indo-Greek kings** — the deepest penetration of Greek power
into India, and a ruler remembered in Buddhist tradition as "Milinda." Treated in
[[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Ch. 11 (Narain).

## Role and career

Menander is the best-attested of the Greek kings who ruled in
**[[greco-bactrian-kingdom|Bactria and north-western India]]** in the second century
BCE. Strabo (following Apollodorus) records that "more tribes were subdued by them
than by Alexander — mostly by Menander, *at least if he actually crossed the Hypanis
towards the east and advanced as far as the Imaus*." He ruled a large Indo-Greek
realm centred on the Punjab, the high point of Greek expansion into India. His
**coinage** — abundant, in Greek and Indian Prakrit (Kharosthi) bilingual legends —
is the principal evidence for his reach.

He survives in Indian memory as **Milinda**, the Greek king of the Buddhist dialogue
the ***Milindapañha*** ("Questions of Milinda"), in which he debates and is converted
by the monk Nagasena — a rare literary trace of a people who "left no history of
their own."

## Counterfactual / structural significance

Menander embodies the paradox of the Indo-Greeks: politically the farthest reach of
Hellenism, yet **culturally absorbed** into the Indian world (the Buddhist Milinda,
the bilingual coins). Narain's framing inverts the older "Greek civilising mission"
view: the Greeks of Bactria and India "came, they saw, but India conquered" — their
story is one of synthesis (Greco-Buddhist art, the later Gandhara school) rather than
domination. They are recoverable today almost entirely through **numismatics**.

## Historiography

The Indo-Greeks were "ignored by ancient historians in both areas, squeezed out
between the two." Western sources (Strabo via Apollodorus, Trogus/Justin) and Indian
ones (the Puranas, the *Milindapañha*) give only fragments; the dynastic sequence —
30+ kings on the coins versus 8–9 in the texts — is reconstructed mainly from coins
and is highly uncertain. Tarn's grand reconstructions are heavily qualified by Narain.
