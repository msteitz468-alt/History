# Thoth

## Overview

**Thoth** (Egyptian *Dḥwty* or *Thoth*, Greek *Thoth* or *Hermes Trismegistus*) is the ibis- or baboon-headed god of wisdom, writing, hieroglyphs, magic (heka), the moon, and scribes. As "Lord of the Hieroglyphs," "Reckoner of Time," and divine scribe, he records the judgment of the dead (heart vs. Ma'at), mediates between gods, and embodies the power of words and knowledge. In the *Book of the Dead*, he is essential in spells for vindication, protection, and transformation.

Often paired with Ma'at (as recorder of truth) and Anubis (in judgment scenes). In myth, he heals Horus's eye, aids Osiris's resurrection, and is self-created or born from Seth's forehead in some tales. Later syncretized with Greek Hermes as founder of Hermeticism.

## Role and Myth (from primary sources)

- **Myth and functions**:
  - Scribe of the gods: Records decrees, measures time (lunar calendar), writes the "Book of the Dead" spells.
  - Judgment: In Ch. 125, stands by scales with Anubis; records the deceased's negative confessions and verdict ("justified" or "true of voice").
  - Magic and knowledge: Inventor of writing, medicine, astronomy; "Lord of Ma'at" in some aspects; heals (e.g., Horus's eye = Wadjet).
  - Moon and mediation: Lunar god (opposed to solar Ra); messenger, reconciler (e.g., between Horus and Seth).

- **Attributes**: Ibis head (or full ibis); baboon (sacred animal, "wise"); palette, reed pen, scroll; moon disk/crescent; ankh, was-scepter.

- **Cult**: Hermopolis Magna (main center, "City of the Eight"); involved in royal rituals, scribal training. Later, Trismegistus ("thrice-great") in Greco-Roman magic/philosophy.

## Historiography and Value

Primary: Pyramid Texts (Old Kingdom spells naming Thoth), Coffin Texts, Book of the Dead papyri/vignettes (e.g., Ani papyrus: Thoth at scales, recording), temple reliefs (Edfu, Philae), scribal palettes, statues.

This source (Budge/Romer) provides the full spell corpus invoking Thoth for "not letting the heart be taken," "coming forth by day," and wisdom. Romer's intro notes his role in New Kingdom theology: embodiment of heka (magic as ordered knowledge), link between writing, cosmos, and afterlife. Budge's prefaces highlight Thoth as "divine intelligence" and scribe in judgment/Osiris myth.
