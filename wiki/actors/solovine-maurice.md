## Role and Background

Maurice Solovine was a Romanian-born student of philosophy in Bern who, around 1902, answered a young [[actors/einstein-albert|Albert Einstein's]] newspaper advertisement offering private tutoring in physics. The tutoring sessions evolved into a small informal discussion circle — with Conrad Habicht — that the three humorously styled the "[[concepts/olympia-academy|Olympia Academy]]," reading and debating works of philosophy and science (Mach, Poincaré, Hume, Spinoza) during the years Einstein was developing the ideas behind special relativity.

## Later Career

Solovine went on to become Einstein's principal French translator, rendering several of Einstein's major works into French, and later edited and published collections of their correspondence, which remain an important primary source for Einstein's intellectual formation and personal reflections on science and philosophy.
