# George W. Bush

**Summary & detailed analysis:** [[hubs/biographies/contemporary/bush-george-w|Detailed Biography]] · **Source:** [[sources/smith-bush-2016|Jean Edward Smith, Bush (2016)]]

George Walker Bush (b. 1946), 43rd President of the United States (2001–2009). Son of President George H. W. Bush and Barbara Bush. Governor of Texas (1995–2000). His presidency was defined by the September 11, 2001 attacks and the subsequent "War on Terror," including the invasion and long occupation of Iraq, the Afghanistan campaign, controversial counterterrorism policies, the response to Hurricane Katrina, and the 2008 global financial crisis.

## Overview

Bush was born in New Haven, Connecticut, raised primarily in Midland, Texas. Education at Andover, Yale (B.A.), and Harvard Business School. Early career in oil (Arbusto, Spectrum 7), ownership stake in Texas Rangers baseball team. Married Laura Welch 1977; born-again Christian experience 1985. Narrowly lost 1978 congressional race. As governor emphasized education reform, tort reform, and bipartisan style in Austin; executed record number of death-row inmates. Won disputed 2000 presidential election after Florida recount and Supreme Court decision in *Bush v. Gore*.

Transition: Cheney-led with unprecedented authority ("staffed largely by Dick Cheney"); personal Rumsfeld Austin vet Dec 22 ("Rumsfeld impressed me... They clicked"); GHWB strain noted but overruled. Early signals: flat Texas team (Hughes in every meeting); "I am THE DECIDER"; rigid religiosity-tinged schedule.

<!-- Range 01 (Smith formation details) -->
Key formation dynamics per Jean Edward Smith: stark East Coast establishment vs. Texas regional identity ("he went to Greenwich Country Day and I went to San Jacinto Junior High"); Andover/Yale as sites of academic shortfall compensated by charisma, head cheerleader role, DKE/Bones membership, and deliberate anti-intellectual "people smart" posture ("I didn’t learn a damn thing at Yale"); preferential Texas Air National Guard service 1968–73 via family connections (direct commission despite minimum aptitude scores; "champagne unit"; no records for Alabama transfer duty); post-Yale "nomadic years" of heavy drinking, personal crises, PULL community service, and Harvard Business School with no job offers. Turnaround anchored by 1977 marriage to Laura Welch (stabilizing listener to his talker) and 1985–86 religious conversion + sobriety decision (Billy Graham at Kennebunkport; "I could not have stopped drinking without faith"). Oil ventures (Arbusto to Harken) relied on family networks for capital but produced personal profits amid investor losses; Texas Rangers managing partnership (1989) provided statewide visibility and wealth (~$14.9M on 1998 sale). 1978 congressional loss taught resilience; 1994 gubernatorial win demonstrated disciplined "compassionate conservative" retail politics and consensus skill with Democrats Bullock and Laney in a weak-governor system. Author emphasizes how early reliance on connections, relational gifts, and bold decisiveness prefigured presidential style.
<!-- /Range 01 -->

As president, initially focused on domestic "compassionate conservatism" and education. After 9/11, launched invasion of Afghanistan (rapid Taliban overthrow) then pivoted to Iraq (2003 invasion justified on WMD and terrorism links that proved unfounded). Adopted doctrine of preventive war. Authorized enhanced interrogation techniques (waterboarding etc.) via Office of Legal Counsel memos. Response to Hurricane Katrina (2005) widely criticized for slow federal action. Second term saw "surge" in Iraq (2007), financial meltdown leading to TARP bailout, and historically low approval ratings. Left office with two ongoing wars, sharply increased national debt, and damaged international standing.

## Role and High-Level Events / Processes

- **2000 election and transition**: Florida controversy; installed with narrow legitimacy.
- **Early administration & Hegelian turn (Range 03)**: Post-inauguration staff dominated by Cheney and neoconservative subcabinet (Wolfowitz, Libby, Feith, Abrams influence via PNAC continuity). 9/11 response: immediate framing as "war on terror," "no distinction... those who harbor them" (Bush's own addition), rejection of delegation ("I will run the meetings... commander in chief"), NSC meetings where Wolfowitz/Cheney broadened target to states/Iraq within 24 hours. Religious/messianic language ("rid the world of evil," "crusade," "I’m here for a reason"). Early CIA broad authority, "dead or alive," Afghanistan launch, immediate Iraq options requested (Nov 27).
- **Post-9/11 decisions**: Afghanistan war; Iraq decision (2002–2003); expansion of executive power (unitary executive, FISA bypass, military commissions, Geneva denial, enhanced interrogation).
- **Occupation and insurgency**: "Mission Accomplished"; failure of planning for postwar Iraq; Abu Ghraib, sectarian violence.
- **Domestic signature items**: No Child Left Behind; Medicare prescription drug benefit; failed immigration reform.
- **Crises**: Katrina; 2008 financial crisis (housing bubble, Lehman collapse, auto bailouts).
- **Legacy processes**: War on Terror framework; unitary executive theory tested in courts; shift in Republican Party attitudes toward intervention and Bush record.

**Counterfactual significance (brief):** Without Bush's choice to invade Iraq and the manner of its execution, the United States likely avoids the multi-trillion-dollar commitment, thousands of US casualties, rise of ISIS, and severe alliance erosion of the 2000s. Post-9/11 unity and moral authority might have been sustained longer for focused counterterrorism and coalition-building. Domestic regulatory and fiscal trajectory might have differed on financial oversight. The "unipolar moment" ended under different terms.

## Historiography (concise)

Smith's 2016 biography is sharply critical, emphasizing unpreparedness, ideological capture by neoconservatives, and decisiveness without sufficient deliberation. Range 03 material highlights the "Hegelian" subcabinet (Wolfowitz et al.) pushing Iraq from the first NSC meeting, Cheney's staff short-circuiting process on commissions, Bush's religious certitude and "commander in chief" personalization, pre-9/11 intelligence posture ("failure to grasp the warnings," PDB no Iraq link), and causal chain from early framing ("kick some ass," "crusade") to two wars. Contrasts with Bush's own *Decision Points* (2010) which defends the choices as necessary and takes ownership. Woodward's contemporaneous books (*Bush at War*, *Plan of Attack*, *State of Denial*, *The War Within*) provide granular decision-making texture from inside access. Later works debate intelligence failures vs. deliberate shaping, the role of 9/11 as catalyst vs. pretext, and Katrina as symptom of executive style. Collection coverage of the Bush years remains thin outside this source and related modern volumes; flag for future primary and revisionist work.
