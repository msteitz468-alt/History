# Bobby Baker

**Summary page.** Bobby Gene Baker (b. 1928) served as Secretary to the Senate Majority Leader (under Lyndon Johnson) and became one of the most powerful unelected figures in mid-century Washington. Known as "Little Lyndon," he was Johnson's eyes, ears, and fixer in the cloakroom and beyond.

## Overview and LBJ Connection

Baker rose from page to key aide. He was instrumental in Johnson's information monopoly, vote counting, and deal-making. After Johnson left the Senate for the vice presidency, Baker continued operations and expanded into private business (vending, hotels, influence). By late 1963 a major scandal was breaking: allegations of kickbacks, call girls, and influence for sale. A *Life* magazine investigation titled in drafts "Lyndon Johnson's Money" was poised to publish when the assassination intervened. Baker later stated that if he had talked fully, Johnson "might have incurred a mortal wound."

## Key Developments (per Caro Vol. 4 context)

- Longtime LBJ protégé and operational right hand in Senate.
- 1963: scandal reaches Senate Rules Committee and national press; Baker resigns post.
- Transition impact: one of two major scandals (with the Life probe) hanging over LBJ on November 22; contributed to atmosphere of vulnerability at the moment of ascension.
- Johnson moved quickly to distance and contain.

## Significance

Illustrates the machine-style politics LBJ built in the Senate and the risks carried into the presidency. Part of the "difficulties" of the transition story.
