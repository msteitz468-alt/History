# The Bosporan Kingdom (the Spartocids)

## Overview

The **Bosporan Kingdom** was the Greco-barbarian state that controlled the **Cimmerian
Bosporus** — the Kerch Strait between the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov (Lake Maeotis), in
the eastern Crimea and the Taman peninsula — and became, in the fourth century, **the chief
grain supplier of Athens**. [[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 9f, John Hind) gives the
wiki its fullest treatment of this northern Black Sea power, a rare window onto the
[[scythians|steppe]] world and the Greek colonial frontier.

## The Spartocid dynasty

From **438** the strait was ruled by the dynasty of **Spartocus I** (a name suggesting
Thracian or native origin) from the city of **Panticapaeum**. Its great rulers were
**Satyrus I**, **Leucon**, and **Paerisades I**, who through the late fifth and fourth
centuries unified the Greek cities of both shores and extended their rule over the native
**Sindi** and **Maeotian** tribes — annexing **Theodosia** and **Sindica** (c. 390–380).
Their **double titulature** captures the hybrid nature of the state: toward the Greek
cities they were **"archon of Bosporus and Theodosia"**; toward the natives they were
**"king (*basileus*) of the Sindi, Toreti, Dandarii"** and other tribes. To Athenian
writers they were simply "tyrants" or "dynasts," and the family governed in the manner of a
*tyrannis* — sole rule for life with sons and kinsmen (*paradynastae*) serving as regional
governors. Notably, they never claimed to rule the independent **Scythians** of the steppe.

## The grain trade with Athens

The kingdom's importance to the Greek world was **grain**. The fertile Crimean and
Kuban hinterland made the Bosporus the granary of the Aegean, and **Athens** depended on
it: Demosthenes calls Leucon ***kyrios tou sitou***, "**in control of the corn supply**."
The Spartocids granted Athenian merchants special concessions and tax exemptions; Athens
reciprocated with **citizenship and honours** (the decree of 347/6 honouring Spartocus II
and Paerisades). This dependence is the northern counterpart to the strategic battles over
the **Hellespont** corn-route that shaped the [[corinthian-war-395bce|Corinthian War]] and
the [[kings-peace-386bce|King's Peace]] — when Sparta or Persia cut Athens' Black Sea
grain, the city was brought to terms.

## Significance

The Bosporan Kingdom is a model of the **fusion of Greek polis and native dynasty** on the
steppe frontier — Greek in culture and commerce, monarchical and "barbarian" in its rule
over the tribes — and a reminder, in CAH VI's words, that "**not all matters of importance
were happening in the eastern Mediterranean**." Its grain underwrote the population of
fourth-century Athens; its later kings (down to Mithradates of Pontus) kept the strait a
power into Roman times.
