# J. E. Lendon

Modern historian and classicist, author of *Soldiers and Ghosts: A History of Battle in Classical Antiquity* (2005).

## Role and Contribution

Lendon is the leading modern exponent of a cultural/psychological interpretation of Greek and Roman tactics and command. His work emphasizes that ancient soldiers and generals often fought to emulate or compete with "ghosts" — the heroes of epic (Homer above all for Greeks), ancestral exemplars, and previous famous battles — rather than (or in addition to) optimizing for victory through the most efficient available means.

Key thesis: the cultural authority of the past frequently overrode or channeled "rational" military adaptation. This explains both striking continuities (hoplite ideals persisting, Roman preference for aggressive decisive battle) and the specific forms that innovation took (new methods had to offer status within the existing honor/competition system).
