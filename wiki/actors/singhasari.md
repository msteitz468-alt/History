# Singhasari

**Singhasari** (Singosari) was the thirteenth-century east Javanese kingdom founded by **Ken Angrok** after he assassinated Kediri’s ruler **Kertajaya** in **1221**. It fell in **1292** when the last ruler, **Kertanagara**, was killed during a Tantric ritual and Jayakatwang of Kediri seized power—opening the sequence that produced [[majapahit|Majapahit]]. Original name **Tumapel** (palace Kutaraja); renamed Singhasari in **1254**. Despite violent successions, the kingdom revived stone architecture after a ~400-year lapse and projected power to [[malayu|Malayu]] and Bali. Primary synthesis: [[miksic-goh-ancient-southeast-asia-2017|Miksic & Goh 2017]] Ch. 7.

## Overview and Succession

Succession was bloody: Angrok murdered by Anusapati; Tohjaya; Viṣṇuvardhana; Kertanagara killed in **1292**. Zhao Rugua (1225) described an east Javanese kingdom **Sujidan** controlling southwest Borneo, Bali, and possibly Maluku (Wunugu)—a Chinese snapshot near the kingdom’s founding horizon. Angrok appears in the sixteenth-century *Pararaton* as Sri Girinātha, with Brahma-peasant origin myth, cursed *kris*, and Ken Dedes; he was an esoteric Buddhist with Śaiva ascetic symbolism and was murdered in **1227**.

**Viṣṇuvardhana** reigned (jointly with Mahisa Champaka/Narasiṃhamūrti **1248–1254**) until death in **1268**—“the only ruler of Singhasari who seems to have died naturally.” The Mulamanurung of **1255** appointed family over Madura, Kediri, east Java, and possibly Bali. He was memorialized as Śiva (Blitar, statue lost) and as Amoghapāśa at **Candi Jago**.

**Kertanagara** ruled **1254–1292** (fourteen-year overlap with his father). The **Charter of Sawwardharma (1269)** exempted religious establishments from secular oversight—*ṛṣi* communities gained autonomy but lost government funding; thereafter temples were numerous but smaller, with perspective effects.

## External Projection: Malayu, Bali, Champa, Yuan

In **1275** the **Pamalayu** expedition against Malayu is recorded in the *Pararaton* and *Desawarnana* (*Nāgarakṛtāgama*) cantos 41–42. In **1284** Bali was subjugated (queen captured and exiled). An Amoghapāśa statue inscription of **1286** at Rambahan presents a Javanese donation as “delight for people of *bhūmi Malayu*”—such religious “donations” were signs of **political dominance**. Alternative historiography (Hall summary) reads alliance against the Yuan; Miksic and Goh prefer Krom’s military-compulsion reading given Java–Sumatra hostility.

Between **1285 and 1306** the Javanese princess Tapasi married Champa’s Jaya Siṅhavarman III—possible mandala marriage policy concurrent with [[champa|Champa]] diplomacy.

Yuan pressure: **1279** Kublai ordered a Javanese royal relative to bow; **1280–81** missions unanswered; **1289** envoy’s face mutilated; attack ordered. The fleet arrived in **1292** **after** Kertanagara had already been assassinated by Jayakatwang. The *Pararaton* gives a dim portrait; the *Desawarnana* a solemn Buddhist one; Kertanagara’s body was never found; he was commemorated as a Jina Buddha and as Ardhanaresvari. The Mongol intervention then entangled with Raden Wijaya’s founding of Majapahit—**enabled** by the Singhasari collapse sequence rather than caused by a planned succession.

Kertanagara revived **Sanskrit** inscriptions after a **248-year** gap (Wurare **1289**; Akṣobhya lotus cushion); Nāgarī script appears on Amoghapāśa bronzes and Candi Jago statues.

## Architecture

- **Candi Kidal** (~1260, for Anusapati’s *śrāddha* ~12 years after 1248 death): oldest of its type; resumed permanent temple architecture after four centuries; Garudeya depictions unique; continuity with central Javanese base/shrine/superstructure + *kāla*.
- **Candi Jago**: Amoghapāśa Lokeśvara for Viṣṇuvardhana’s soul (assumed *śrāddha* **1280**); esoteric Buddhist Bengal-related deities; Nāgarī labels; unique three-terrace rectangular plan; revived narrative reliefs like [[borobudur|Borobudur]]/[[prambanan|Prambanan]] but in new low-relief flat style; remodeled **1343/44** with Mañjuśrī by Adityavarman (see [[malayu]]).
- **Candi Jawi** (Kertanagara, Mt Welirang): *Desawarnana* records Hayam Wuruk’s 1361 pilgrimage—lower room Śiva, pinnacle Buddha Akṣobhya (lightning 1331 likely destroyed the Buddha); first Sūrya ceiling-relief practice later common in Majapahit; clockwise reliefs depict perishable buildings like Balinese *meru*.

Ngurah temple mouldings elsewhere resemble the central Java system while statues more closely match thirteenth-century Singhasari—showing style mobility across islands.

## Key Links

- **preceded_by** Kediri and the long east Java post-Mataram sequence.
- **produced** claim of suzerainty over Malayu (Pamalayu) and Bali.
- **followed_by** Majapahit after the 1292 crisis and Mongol war.
- **analogous_to** Early Classic central Java in stone architecture and narrative relief revival after centuries of sparse permanent building.

## Historiography

Core narratives come from later Old Javanese texts (*Pararaton*, *Desawarnana*) written under Majapahit agendas, plus Chinese notices (Zhao Rugua, Yuan history) and temple archaeology. Contradictions between dim and solemn portraits of Kertanagara, and between alliance vs compulsion readings of Pamalayu, remain open. Angrok’s *Pararaton* biography is literary myth-history. Dating of *śrāddha* temples uses the twelve-year ritual cycle as a heuristic. Source base: Miksic & Goh 2017 Late Classic chapter.
