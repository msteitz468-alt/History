## Role and Background

Robert F. Wagner Jr., son of the New Deal-era US Senator of the same name, served three terms as Mayor of New York City (1954–1965), presiding over the city during a period of postwar growth, urban renewal, and rising racial and fiscal tension. Initially a Tammany Hall-aligned Democratic machine politician, he gradually distanced himself from machine politics over his tenure.

## Relationship with Robert Moses

Wagner's mayoralty overlapped with the later, most contested phase of [[actors/moses-robert|Robert Moses's]] decades-long control of the city's public authorities and construction agencies; where earlier mayors had largely deferred to Moses, Wagner presided over the gradual erosion of Moses's independent power, contributing to the process that eventually curtailed Moses's authority under Wagner's successor John Lindsay.

## Significance

Wagner's mayoralty bridges the high-growth, machine-influenced postwar city of the 1950s and the more fiscally and racially strained city of the mid-1960s, and his shifting relationship with Moses illustrates the broader postwar transition away from unaccountable public-authority power in American urban governance.
