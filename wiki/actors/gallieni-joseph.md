# Joseph Gallieni

French general renowned for colonial service (Tonkin, Madagascar, Sudan operations). Briefly considered for supreme command in 1911; commanded Fifth Army until retirement April 1914; later played a prominent role in the Battle of the Marne (Paris military governor). Expressed pre-war doubts about Plan XVII's deployment and Fifth Army's mission.

## 1911 High Command Succession

During the Agadir crisis, Minister of War Adolphe Messimy sought to replace the discredited General Victor Michel. Gallieni was Messimy's first choice for vice president of the Superior Council of War / effective command. Gallieni refused:
- Only two and a half years remained before mandatory retirement.
- He considered it inappropriate to accept the post after playing a key role in Michel's ouster.

Messimy then turned to Paul Pau (who demanded authority over all general officer appointments) before selecting Joseph Joffre.

Gallieni had previously praised Joffre's performance in Madagascar (organizing the port of Diégo Suarez and building fortifications) and affirmed Joffre should rise to the highest levels.

## Command of Fifth Army and Plan XVII Critique

Gallieni commanded Fifth Army (positioned on the extreme left under Plan XVII) until his retirement on April 24, 1914. Shortly after Plan XVII's publication, using map exercises, he expressed strong doubts about the strength and mission of his army for operations into Belgium. He:
- Demanded more troops.
- Insisted that fortifications at Maubeuge be improved.

His successor, General Charles Lanrezac, submitted a similar critical report in late July 1914.

## Significance

Gallieni represented experienced colonial leadership and caution regarding the feasibility of Plan XVII's left-flank offensive into Belgium without adequate forces or preparations. His refusal in 1911 helped place Joffre in command; his later Marne role (with Joffre) is noted in campaign analyses. His pre-war assessment of Fifth Army's weaknesses highlighted force maldistribution in Plan XVII (three-quarters of the army south of Verdun despite anticipated German Belgian maneuver).
