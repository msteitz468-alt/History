# Bolshevik Party
## Biography / Description
The **Bolshevik Party** (originally a faction of the Russian Social Democratic Labor Party, later renamed the Russian Communist Party [Bolsheviks] and eventually the Communist Party of the Soviet Union) was the ruling political party of the Soviet Union. Founded in 1903 during the RSDLP split in Brussels/London, it was led by Vladimir Lenin, who advocated for a highly disciplined vanguard party of professional revolutionaries. The party seized power in the October Revolution of 1917 and established a single-party monopoly on political power that lasted until its activities were suspended and banned by Boris Yeltsin in the aftermath of the failed August Coup of 1991.

The party, too, needed to be made dependable. Expulsions started in May 1929, resulting in a loss of eleven per cent of the membership. A recruitment campaign began at the same time, and the party expanded its number of members from 1.3 million in 1928 to 2.2 million in 1931 (line 1494). Party secretaries at the various local levels were the Politburo’s local chief executives... In the RSFSR Stalin constructed a regional tier... (line 1494). Yet still the central leadership could not regard the party with equanimity... Deceit was deeply embedded in the mode of industrial and agricultural management (line 1496). Stalin had tried to root out every possible challenge... His victory was not yet totally secure (line 1510).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **Vanguard Structure (1903):** Lenin insisted on a tight, disciplined structure under **[[democratic-centralism|democratic centralism]]**, establishing the party as a conspiratorial elite rather than a loose mass organization.
- **Seizure of Power (1917):** Under Lenin and Trotsky, the party launched the October Revolution, overthrowing the Provisional Government and establishing the Soviet state.
- **State Monopoly and Internal Discipline:** Following the suppression of other socialist parties (Mensheviks and Socialist Revolutionaries) during the Civil War, the party established a total monopoly... Stalin used the party as a weapon to terrify all opposition to his economic policies. He needed to operate through an institution that could be trusted to maintain political fidelity, organizational solidity and ideological rectitude (line 1492). In January 1935, as Kamenev and Zinoviev received their prison sentences, a general exchange of party cards was announced... by May 1935, 281,872 persons had ceased being Bolsheviks (line 1672).
- **Seventeenth Congress and Aftermath (1934):** The press had indicated that it would be a Congress of Victors... Stalin in his speech to the Congress, however, indicated that he was not going to be gracious in victory: ‘Consequently it is necessary not to sing lullabies to the party but to develop its vigilance...’ (lines 1658, 1660). Only one in thirty delegates to the Seventeenth Party Congress in 1934 returned to the Eighteenth Congress in 1939. The loss from the Central Committee was also drastic: just sixteen out of seventy-one members survived (line 1722).
- **Ideological Campaigns and Short Course:** The Short Course traced the rise of the Bolsheviks... Stalin... was the textbook’s general editor... It divided everything between black and white... Violence, intolerance, pitilessness, command, discipline, correctness and science were the central themes (lines 1804, 1808). In December 1938 the NKVD was ordered to seek permission from the party apparatus before taking any official of the party into custody... Stalin confirmed the fresh attitude by asserting the necessity to ‘value cadres like the gold reserves of the party and state, esteem them, have respect for them’ (line 1772). The party was pushed back into the role proposed for it by Kaganovich in the mid-1930s: it was meant to supervise the implementation of policy, not to initiate it (line 2246).
- **Name Change and Late Structure (1952):** At the Nineteenth Party Congress... The biggest event was the change of name from the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) to the Communist Party of the Soviet Union (line 2380).

## Historical Significance
The Bolshevik Party was the central driving force and structural spine of the Soviet Union. At its peak in the 1980s, it numbered nearly 20 million members. Its organization structured every level of Soviet administration, and its top decision-making body (the Politburo) was the supreme authority in the land. The party was dissolved and banned in late 1991, which immediately precipitated the final collapse of the Soviet Union.

He had broken the party as an independent, supreme political agency... The Politburo was ceasing to meet on a regular, formal basis... The NKVD’s star had risen while the party’s had fallen (line 1768). The party provided the ideological cement... Its cohesive capacity was equally important organizationally: holders of governmental, administrative and military office were virtually obliged to be party members (line 1774). Yet the uppermost élite lived in greater safety than in 1937–8... Stalin could not afford to reduce his associates to the condition of robots (line 1828). The party remained rather special. It incarnated continuity with the October Revolution, with Lenin, with Marxism-Leninism (line 1774).
