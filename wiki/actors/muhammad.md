## Gibbon, *Decline and Fall* (1776–1788) — Chs. L–LI (historiography only)

[[sources/gibbon-decline-and-fall-1776|Gibbon]]’s “Mahomet” chapters are **eighteenth-century orientalizing historiography**, not modern Islamic studies. Do not treat the following as wiki-voice fact; full life and movement belong on the canonical page.

- **Causal frame:** While state and church were exhausted by Persian war and Nestorian/Monophysite discord, “Mahomet, with the sword in one hand and the Koran in the other, erected his throne on the ruins of Christianity and of Rome.”
- **Geopolitical spectator:** “Placed on the verge of the two great empires of the East, Mahomet observed with secret joy the progress of their mutual destruction.”
- **Enthusiast or impostor:** Famous balance — “From enthusiasm to imposture, the step is perilous and slippery.”
- **Enlightenment creed-portrait:** “A philosophic theist might subscribe the popular creed of the Mahometans”; Unitarian simplicity; each Muslim his own priest; “the sword of Mahomet was not less potent than the rod of Moses.”
- **Unification and diffusion:** Hostile tribes united; valor once spent in domestic quarrels directed abroad; extent of conquest later dissolves Arabian sovereignty back toward Bedouin independence.
- **Closing character-judgment (Gibbon’s own):** Even bitter Christian/Jewish foes “must allow” he assumed a “false commission to inculcate a salutary doctrine” and checked idolatry, revenge, and oppression of widows/orphans.

## From Mitchell, Later Roman Empire (2015)

Brief late-antique frame (full life on [[muhammad-and-the-rise-of-islam]]): the last Rome–Persia war brought both empires to the verge of collapse as Muhammad kindled Arab ambitions. In **622** Muhammad (Hashemite Qurayshi of the Mecca area) was exiled to Medina — *hijra*, epoch of the Islamic calendar. To his death (**632**) he built power on (1) warrior enrichment from caravans/tribes and (2) new monotheism drawing pagan (Black Stone), Jewish, and biblical Christian strands into exclusive Allah worship. Sebeos presents him teaching an Abrahamic land promise to the “sons of Ishmael” as divine conquest mandate. Before death he controlled Medina; followers were dominant in western Arabia/Hijaz to Aqaba/Wadi Rumm. Leadership passed to caliphs beginning with Abu Bakr; raiding became holy war. “From 622 until his death a decade later Muhammad led a rapidly growing coalition of Arab tribes on a campaign to overthrow the world order.”

Source: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Mitchell, *A History of the Later Roman Empire* (2015)]].
