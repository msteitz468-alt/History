# Nabopolassar

**Nabopolassar** (Nabu-apla-usur, reigned 626–605 BCE) was the **Chaldaean founder
of the [[neo-babylonian-empire|Neo-Babylonian Empire]]** — the man who, allied with
the **[[medes|Medes]]**, destroyed [[neo-assyrian-empire|Assyria]] and made
[[babylon|Babylon]] "the uncontested successor of Nineveh and the new capital of
the world." He was the father of **[[nebuchadnezzar-ii|Nebuchadnezzar II]]**.
[[boardman-cah-v3p2-1991|CAH Vol. III, Part 2]] (Ch. 25, J. Oates) is the
authority.

## Revolt and accession (626)

A **[[chaldeans|Chaldaean]]** who first appears as an **Assyrian-appointed official
in the Sealand**, Nabopolassar revolted against his sponsors amid the succession
chaos that followed the death of [[ashurbanipal|Ashurbanipal]] (627). After battles
in and around Babylon — and a year in which "no king was recognized" — he was
acclaimed **king of Babylon on 26 Ululu 626**, founding the dynasty. The Assyrian
king Sin-sharra-ishkun contested Babylonia for a decade; Assyrian-garrisoned
**Nippur** held out until 617, and **Uruk** changed hands repeatedly, the cities
suffering famine so severe that parents sold their children for food.

## The destruction of Assyria

From **616** Nabopolassar carried the war onto Assyrian ground, campaigning up the
Euphrates and Tigris. The decisive turn came from the **[[medes|Medes]]**: in
**614** Cyaxares destroyed **Ashur**, and on the battlefield Nabopolassar made a
**formal alliance** with him. In **612** their combined armies (with the
Ummanmanda/[[scythians|Scythians]]) besieged and **sacked
[[fall-of-nineveh-612bce|Nineveh]]**. The Assyrian remnant under **Ashur-uballit
II** fled to **Harran**, which the allies took in **610**; an Egyptian attempt
under **Necho II** to restore Assyria failed, and after 609 Assyria ceased to
exist.

## The final years and the succession

The contest with Egypt for Syria continued. In **605**, with Nabopolassar aged and
ill in Babylon, his son and crown prince **[[nebuchadnezzar-ii|Nebuchadrezzar]]**
shattered the Egyptians at **[[battle-of-carchemish-605bce|Carchemish]]** — but on
hearing of his father's **death** he raced back to Babylon to secure the throne.
Nabopolassar had reigned 21 years and handed his son an empire reaching to the
Egyptian frontier.

## Significance

- `produced:` the **[[neo-babylonian-empire|Neo-Babylonian Empire]]** and (with
  the [[medes|Medes]]) the **end of Assyria** — the transfer of Near Eastern power
  "from Nineveh to Babylon."
- `enabled:` the reign of [[nebuchadnezzar-ii|Nebuchadnezzar II]], the empire's
  great builder and conqueror.

## Historiography

His reign is reconstructed almost entirely from the **Babylonian Chronicle series**
(especially the "Fall of Nineveh" chronicle), since his own royal inscriptions are
mostly building-texts; the chronology of the Assyrian collapse depends on Oates's
careful reading of these tablets (CAH III.2 Ch. 25). The tribal-official origins
of the founder of so grand an empire are themselves a notable historical irony.
