# Sergio Panunzio (1886–1944)

Legal theorist of Italian [[revolutionary-syndicalism]] — a "pure Sorelian"
of myth and violence rather than economics — who became the chief juridical
philosopher of the Fascist corporate state: for
[[sternhell-thesis|Sternhell]], "only Giovanni Gentile outshone him" among
Fascist theoreticians, and he is the clearest personal embodiment of the
syndicalism→fascism continuity. Champion of Sorel's theory of proletarian
violence in *Avanguardia socialista*, student of Mosca's elite theory, he
argued as early as August 1904 that war "can rapidly advance the socialist
cause quicker than any reformism," and in 1914 demanded the destruction of
"the German feudal and military hegemony" as socialism's precondition.
Writing in Mussolini's *Utopia* he foresaw "a Jacobin revolution led by
Mussolini" (May 1914); in 1918 he pronounced Mussolini's national
syndicalism "the only social movement to have integrated the immense
historical experience of the war."

His **"Program of Action"** (March 1919) was a full corporatist constitution:
the state grants producers the *utenza* (use) of land and factories,
parasites are excluded from production and property, the population is
organized into "organic classes" and corporations with an "aristocratic"
restricted parliament — see [[national-syndicalism]]. Against
[[de-ambris-alceste|De Ambris]] he argued a "proletarian nation" precluded
internal class struggle. Under the regime: Fascist deputy (1924), PNF
leadership, National Council of Corporations, principal corporatism theorist
alongside Rocco and Gentile, professor at the Fascist Faculty of Political
Science in Perugia. His 1921 formula distinguished revolutionary from
national syndicalism.
