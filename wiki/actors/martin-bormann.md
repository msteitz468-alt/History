# Martin Bormann

Head of the Nazi Party Chancellery and, from the last years of the war, Adolf Hitler's private secretary (1900–1945). Bormann built enormous behind-the-scenes power through control of access to Hitler, management of Hitler's personal finances and estates, and administrative authority over party appointments — a position that made him one of the most powerful, if publicly obscure, figures in the regime's final years, eclipsing rivals such as Göring and Himmler in day-to-day influence over Hitler.

Bormann was present in the Führerbunker during Hitler's final days and was named a executor of Hitler's will and a minister in the short-lived government Hitler designated before his suicide on 30 April 1945. Bormann attempted to escape the bunker complex during the Soviet capture of Berlin on the night of 1–2 May 1945; he was tried and sentenced to death in absentia at Nuremberg, his fate unknown for decades until skeletal remains found in Berlin in 1972 were confirmed by DNA testing in 1998 to be his, showing he had died attempting to break out of the bunker area, likely by suicide after being trapped.

## Significance

Bormann's control over access to Hitler and the machinery of party administration made him arguably the second most powerful man in the Nazi state by 1945, despite his low public profile — a case study in the power available to a regime's chief administrator of access and information.
