# The Papacy in the Eighth and Ninth Centuries

## Overview

In the eighth and ninth centuries the **papacy** transformed itself from a beleaguered Byzantine
bishopric into the head of an independent territorial state and the spiritual centre of a new Latin
Christendom allied to the Franks. In [[mckitterick-ncmh-v2-1995|NCMH II]] (Noble, Ch. 21) this is the
era in which the **"Republic of St Peter"** — the papal state — came into being.

## Breaking with Byzantium

The popes were driven out of the Byzantine orbit by two pressures: **[[byzantine-iconoclasm|iconoclasm]]**,
which they rejected, and the **inability of Byzantium to defend Italy** against the
[[lombards|Lombards]]. Popes Gregory III and Zacharias pursued an intense, many-sided diplomacy — with the
Lombard court, the dukes of Spoleto and Benevento, the Bavarians, and the Franks — in which a Franco-papal
alliance was at first "only one somewhat remote possibility among several."

## The Franco-Papal Alliance and the Papal State

The Lombard capture of [[exarchate-of-ravenna|Ravenna]] (751) was the turning point. **Pope Stephen II**
crossed the Alps and concluded with [[pippin-iii|Pippin III]] a personal alliance of *amicitia, pax et
caritas* (754); Pippin campaigned in Italy (755, 756), defeated King Aistulf, and returned the seized lands
to Rome — the **Donation of Pippin**, which brought "fully into being" the papal state that had been
emerging since the 730s. When the Lombards renewed the threat, **[[charlemagne|Charlemagne]]** intervened
for **Pope Hadrian I**, deposed Desiderius, and made himself king of the Lombards (774;
[[carolingian-conquest-of-lombard-italy-774]]); his territorial settlements of 774, 781, and 787 left the
popes in secure possession of a broad swathe of central Italy from Ravenna to Rome (confirmed by
[[louis-the-pious|Louis the Pious]] and Paschal I in 817).

## Popes, Nobility, and Emperors

As the papal administration took over central Italy, the old Byzantine-era military aristocracy was
absorbed into papal government — and the quarrels of the Roman nobility were "domesticated in the Lateran,"
producing recurrent factional violence over papal elections. The relationship with the Frankish ruler was
sealed when **Pope Leo III** crowned Charlemagne emperor in 800 ([[coronation-of-charlemagne-800]]).
Later in the period **Pope Nicholas I** (858–867) asserted papal authority forcefully — in the
[[conversion-of-boris-i-864|Bulgarian church question]] and the **Photian schism** with Constantinople, and
against over-mighty archbishops and kings in the West.

## Forged Authority

The era's ambitions were buttressed by audacious **forgeries**: the **[[donation-of-constantine]]** (a
fabricated grant of western dominion to the papacy) and the **Pseudo-Isidorian Decretals** (false canon law
enhancing the independence of bishops and the supremacy of Rome), both products of this period and both of
enormous long-term consequence.

## Significance

The eighth- and ninth-century papacy created the **temporal papal state** that would last until 1870 and
established the papacy as an autonomous power at the head of Latin Christendom, in partnership with — and
eventual rivalry with — the Frankish/imperial monarchy.

## Historiography

NCMH II (Noble) draws on the *Liber Pontificalis*, papal letters (the *Codex Carolinus*), and Frankish
sources. Noble's own *The Republic of St Peter* (1984) is the standard account of the papal state's
formation. Debates concern the authenticity and intent of the territorial "donations," the degree of papal
versus Frankish initiative, and the dating and purpose of the great forgeries.
