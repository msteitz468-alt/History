# Ngo Dinh Diem

First president of the Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam), 1955–1963. Catholic mandarin from central Vietnam who became the U.S.-backed leader after Geneva. Overthrown in a U.S.-tacitly approved coup in 1963.

## Role in the "Making of America's Vietnam"

After Geneva, Diem (with U.S. support, especially from Lansdale and the Eisenhower administration) consolidated power in the south against the odds: defeated the sects (Binh Xuyen, Cao Dai, Hoa Hao), refused the 1956 elections provided for at Geneva, and built a centralized anti-communist state. Logevall and others portray him as a complex figure — sincere nationalist, authoritarian, nepotistic (the "Ngo family" rule), ultimately unable to build broad legitimacy.

## Key Traits and Outcome

Anti-communist, Confucian-influenced, suspicious of both French and American tutelage while dependent on the latter. His regime's repression, failure on land reform, and alienation of Buddhists contributed to the growth of the insurgency that became the Second Indochina War.
