# Alceste De Ambris (1874–1934)

Italian syndicalist labor leader — of the post-1904 organizing generation
alongside Corridoni and Michele Bianchi — and the central practical figure of
[[national-syndicalism]]: leader of the Parma Labor Chamber, UIL leader,
author of the Carta del Carnaro, and ultimately an anti-Fascist exile.

His trajectory tracks every turn of the movement. He **opposed the Libyan
War** (1911), accusing Labriola, Olivetti, and Orano of being "false
revolutionaries" serving "parasites" and "the bourgeois state" — then led the
**interventionist Left in 1914**: his 18 August 1914 speech attacked
neutralism, equated the war with the French Revolution, and split the USI
(the anarchist majority under Borghi stayed neutralist); the dissidents
founded the Fascio rivoluzionario d'azione internazionalista (October 1914),
which [[mussolini-benito|Mussolini]] joined. In 1917 he coined "The Land to
the Peasants" ("In order to bind the peasants to the nation, one has to give
them the land"); in 1919 he designed the **partial expropriation** program —
confiscating speculators' war profits for veterans while sparing productive
capital — which he presented to Fascist cadres in Milan on 9 June 1919 and
which the June 1919 Fascist program copied "almost [as] an exact replica."
At Fiume he became D'Annunzio's secretary for civil affairs (13 January
1920) and drafted the **Carta del Carnaro** ("Materially, it was I who
prepared it, but under the inspiration and direction of the Commandante") —
see [[fiume-expedition-1919]].

Unlike the "ethical revolutionaries" (Panunzio, Olivetti, Orano, Lanzillo),
De Ambris held socioeconomic transformation to be the end and nationalism a
means; when fascism moved right after 1920 he diagnosed its "two faces"
(agrarian-conservative vs. urban-revolutionary), judged it "an
antirevolutionary instrument manipulated by the bourgeoisie," went into
opposition in 1922, and died in French exile. His case is load-bearing for
both sides of the [[fascism-and-the-political-left]] controversy: fascism's
program was demonstrably his, yet he refused fascism itself.
