# Ludwig von Mises (1881–1973)

Austrian economist, senior figure of the Austrian School between the wars
and teacher of [[hayek-friedrich|Hayek]]. His April 1920 article "Die
Wirtschaftsrechnung im sozialistischen Gemeinwesen" (*Archiv für
Sozialwissenschaften*) opened the [[socialist-calculation-debate]]: rational
economic calculation requires money prices applied to *all* factors of
production, so abolishing markets in the means of production makes rational
allocation impossible. Hayek's verdict (1935): Mises "first formulated the
central problem of socialist economics in such a form as to make it
impossible that it should ever again disappear from the discussion." Max
Weber and Boris Brutzkus reached parallel conclusions independently in
1920–21. Expanded as *Die Gemeinwirtschaft* (1922; English *Socialism*,
1936), defended in replies of 1924 and 1928; Hayek recalled the 1920 thesis
"was greeted with a howl of derision," while [[lange-oskar|Lange]] later
mock-promised Mises "a statue in the marble halls of the future Central
Planning Board." Mises's "geistige Arbeitsteilung" (intellectual division of
labor) is credited by Hayek as a source of the [[knowledge-problem]].

Economic adviser at the Vienna Chamber of Commerce; convener of the private
seminar that formed a generation of Austrian economists (including Hayek);
emigrated via Geneva (1934) to New York (1940), where he taught at NYU and
anchored the postwar Austrian revival. *(Summary actor page; his economics
belongs to the sibling Economics wiki.)*

## Counterfactual significance

Without Mises's 1920 intervention the calculation question might have
remained scattered anticipations (Gossen, Pierson, Barone) rather than the
organizing controversy of interwar economics; Hayek's entire line of work —
from [[hayek-individualism-economic-order-1948|the calculation essays]] to
[[hayek-road-to-serfdom-1944|*The Road to Serfdom*]] — descends from it.

## Historiography

This page derives from Hayek's own partisan history of the debate; Mises's
stature is contested between Austrian-school hagiography and mainstream
accounts that long treated Lange as the debate's victor.
