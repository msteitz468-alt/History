# Lucius Verus (Lucius Aurelius Verus)

The **co-emperor with [[marcus-aurelius|Marcus Aurelius]]** (161–169) — the first time two
*Augusti* ruled jointly with formally equal powers. The pleasure-loving junior partner who
nominally commanded the eastern war. Treated in [[bowman-cah-v11-2000|CAH XI]] Ch. 3.

## Co-emperor

Adopted (with Marcus) into the Antonine house by Hadrian's design, Lucius had been kept "a private
citizen for twenty-three years" by **[[antoninus-pius|Antoninus Pius]]**, who favoured Marcus. On
Pius' death (161), **Marcus insisted that Lucius receive equal powers and the name Augustus** —
establishing the precedent of **collegial rule**. Marcus held the greater *auctoritas* (he was the
elder, the *pontifex maximus*, and long the designated heir); Lucius was the junior partner,
married to Marcus' daughter **Lucilla**.

## The Parthian War

When the Parthians overran Armenia and Syria, **Lucius was sent to the East (162)** as the
emperor present at the front, "physically robust and younger." His leisurely "royal progress" and
his comfortable residence at **Antioch and Daphne** (with his mistress Panthea) drew comment — the
hostile tradition makes him a self-indulgent figure, and the real victories of the
**[[trajans-parthian-war-114-117|Parthian War]]** (recovering Armenia, taking Ctesiphon) were won
by his able generals (Statius Priscus, Avidius Cassius, Martius Verus). He returned in 166 to a
joint triumph, bringing back also the **[[antonine-plague-165|Antonine Plague]]**.

## Death and significance

Lucius accompanied Marcus to the **Danube** for the opening of the [[marcomannic-wars-166-180|Marcomannic
Wars]], but died suddenly (probably of the plague) in **169**, leaving Marcus sole ruler. His
brief co-reign demonstrated both the **workability of joint rule** (one emperor at the front, one
at Rome) — a precedent that would be revived under the Severans and institutionalized in the late
empire — and the danger of elevating a junior partner without aptitude. Marcus deified him.
