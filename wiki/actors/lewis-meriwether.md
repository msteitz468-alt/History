# Meriwether Lewis (1774–1809)

Virginia planter's son (Locust Hill) and Jefferson family acquaintance. Army officer who rose to captain; participated in the suppression of the Whiskey Rebellion. Selected by Jefferson as co-leader (with William Clark) of the Corps of Discovery.

**Personality and style**: Intense, technically curious (air rifle enthusiast), emotionally volatile ("hypochondriac affections," family history of depression), martially minded, and a gifted observer of nature. Capable of rapid action (as in the accidental shooting incident near Pittsburgh) but struggled with structure, delay, and post-expedition responsibilities.

**Role in the Expedition**: Primary scientific and diplomatic leader on the outbound journey; kept detailed natural-history notes; bore much of the command burden. The expedition was the central event of his life.

**Aftermath**: Governor of Louisiana Territory (with difficulties); struggled to prepare the journals for publication. Died 11 October 1809 at Grinder's Stand on the Natchez Trace; the death is generally accepted as suicide, though theories persist. His death delayed the journals' publication and complicated his legacy.

**Counterfactual significance**: Without Lewis's combination of Jefferson's trust, army experience, and scientific bent, the expedition's character and the quality of its observations would have differed substantially. His personal arc illustrates the psychological costs of command on the early American frontier.
