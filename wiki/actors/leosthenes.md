# Leosthenes

Athenian mercenary commander (*condottiere*, d. 322 BCE) who, in the years before Alexander the Great's death, had gathered and stationed a large body of experienced Greek mercenaries at Cape Taenarum in the Peloponnese. When news of Alexander's death reached Greece in 323 BCE, Leosthenes led Athens into open revolt against Macedonian rule — the Lamian War — combining his mercenary force with Athenian citizen troops and rallying much of the Aetolian League and other Greek states to the coalition.

Leosthenes won early successes, defeating Macedonian regent Antipater's forces and besieging him at Lamia in Thessaly. However, he was killed by a stray missile during the siege — a loss the sources treat as a serious blow to the coalition's cohesion, since no subsequent commander matched his ability to hold the diverse Greek alliance together. Macedonian reinforcements under Leonnatus and later Craterus eventually broke the siege and the coalition, ending the war in Macedonian victory (322 BCE) and the effective end of independent Athenian democracy.

## Significance

Leosthenes's death at Lamia is presented by ancient sources as the turning point of the war — the moment the last major Greek attempt to overturn Macedonian hegemony lost its most capable unifying commander.
