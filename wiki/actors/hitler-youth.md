# Hitler Youth (Hitlerjugend)

The Nazi Party's youth organization under Baldur von Schirach, with its
female branch the League of German Girls (BDM). From a minority movement in
1932 it grew — by absorption of rival youth leagues, pressure, and finally
legal compulsion (1936 law, 1939 compulsory service decrees) — to encompass
nearly all German adolescents, combining paramilitary training, ideological
schooling, and the deliberate erosion of parental, school, and church
authority over the young.

## Evans (2005)

From a marginal 18,000 members in 1930 to 2.3 million by end-1933 (absorbing
suppressed rivals), ~4 million (1935), and 8.7 million by early 1939 — nearly
the entire 10–18 cohort — with membership legally compulsory from 25 March
1939. From December 1936 an autonomous educational institution under Schirach
reporting directly to Hitler. Openly premilitary by 1938: 45,000 in the
Naval-HY, 74,000 in Flying Units, 1.2 million receiving small-calibre shooting
instruction. Evans stresses the mixed reality: superficial indoctrination by
anti-intellectual leaders, absenteeism and dues-only membership, brutal camp
punishments (including a hushed-up 1935 case in which a boy was sexually
assaulted and killed at camp and his complaining mother arrested), and an
institutional arrogance that eroded school, parental, and church authority —
which was, Evans argues, much of the point. Its socialization showed in the
young perpetrator crowds of [[kristallnacht-1938|Kristallnacht]]. See
[[nazification-of-german-education-1933-1939]].

## Historiography

Kater, *Hitler Youth* (2004) is standard; memoir literature (Maschmann,
*Account Rendered*) documents the generational appeal Evans stresses.
