# Hitler Youth (Hitlerjugend)

The Nazi Party's youth organization under Baldur von Schirach, with its
female branch the League of German Girls (BDM). From a minority movement in
1932 it grew — by absorption of rival youth leagues, pressure, and finally
legal compulsion (1936 law, 1939 compulsory service decrees) — to encompass
nearly all German adolescents, combining paramilitary training, ideological
schooling, and the deliberate erosion of parental, school, and church
authority over the young.

## Evans (2005)

From a marginal 18,000 members in 1930 to 2.3 million by end-1933 (absorbing
suppressed rivals), ~4 million (1935), and 8.7 million by early 1939 — nearly
the entire 10–18 cohort — with membership legally compulsory from 25 March
1939. From December 1936 an autonomous educational institution under Schirach
reporting directly to Hitler. Openly premilitary by 1938: 45,000 in the
Naval-HY, 74,000 in Flying Units, 1.2 million receiving small-calibre shooting
instruction. Evans stresses the mixed reality: superficial indoctrination by
anti-intellectual leaders, absenteeism and dues-only membership, brutal camp
punishments (including a hushed-up 1935 case in which a boy was sexually
assaulted and killed at camp and his complaining mother arrested), and an
institutional arrogance that eroded school, parental, and church authority —
which was, Evans argues, much of the point. Its socialization showed in the
young perpetrator crowds of [[kristallnacht-1938|Kristallnacht]]. See
[[nazification-of-german-education-1933-1939]].

## Historiography

Kater, *Hitler Youth* (2004) is standard; memoir literature (Maschmann,
*Account Rendered*) documents the generational appeal Evans stresses.

## Shirer (1960) Additions

- Baldur von Schirach (descended from two Declaration of Independence signers on his American mother's side; joined NSDAP 1925 age 18) was named Youth Leader of the NSDAP in 1931, "Youth Leader of the German Reich" in June 1933; he used an armed 50-man Hitler Youth detachment to seize the offices of the Reich Committee of German Youth Associations from General Vogt and Admiral von Trotha, dissolving the pre-Nazi youth movement (which had claimed 10 million members pre-1933, versus the Hitler Youth's mere 107,956 in 1932).
- Age-graded structure, verbatim oath: Pimpf (ages 6–10) → Jungvolk (10–14, oath: "I am willing and ready to give up my life for him, so help me God") → Hitler Youth proper (14–18) → Labor Service/Army. Girls: Jungmädel (10–14) → Bund Deutscher Mädel/BDM (14–21), with a "Land Jahr" farm-service year at 18 emphasizing "healthy mothers of healthy children."
- [EYEWITNESS] Shirer reports "angry complaints from parents about their daughters' having been made pregnant" during the Land Jahr and Household Year for Girls, and personally heard BDM leaders "lecture their young charges on the moral and patriotic duty of bearing children for Hitler's Reich — within wedlock if possible, but without it if necessary." He quotes a contemporary satirical couplet: "In the fields and on the heath / I lose Strength through Joy."
- Elite training pipeline, additional to Evans's account above: Adolf Hitler Schools (from age 12, ten founded after 1937, principal one at Brunswick); National Political Institutes of Education (SS-run, restoring a Prussian-military-academy ethos, 31 schools by war's outbreak, 3 for women); Order Castles (Ordensburgen) — four sequential castles over six years, the final year at Marienburg (the former Teutonic Knights stronghold, East Prussia) focused explicitly on Lebensraum expansion into Slavic lands — an explicit ideological preparation for eastern conquest.
- [EYEWITNESS] Shirer's own comparative observation, May 1940 between Aachen and Brussels: he contrasted "bronzed and clean-cut" German soldiers, the product of the Hitler Youth outdoor/nutrition regime, against the "hollow chests, round shoulders, pasty complexions and bad teeth" of early British POWs, attributed to interwar British neglect of youth welfare.
