# Anatoly Fomenko

Mathematician and author of the "New Chronology" (Novaia khronologiia) project.

## Overview

Anatoly Timofeevich Fomenko (born 1945) is a Russian mathematician, Full Member (Academician) of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Full Member of the Russian Academy of Natural Sciences, and Professor, Head of the Section of Mathematics in the Department of Mathematics and Mechanics at Moscow State University. He is known in mathematics for work on the Plateau problem (minimal spectral surfaces), invariants and topological classification of integrable Hamiltonian dynamical systems, symplectic geometry, and related fields. He is a laureate of the 1996 National Premium of the Russian Federation in Mathematics.

Fomenko is the principal author of a large body of work applying empirico-statistical and astronomical methods to the analysis of historical chronicles and the reconstruction of chronology. This project, presented in multiple volumes and consolidated in the 2003 English edition *History: Fiction or Science?*, argues for a radical revision of conventional ancient and medieval chronology.

## Role and Significance

Fomenko's chronology work is a prominent example of 20th/21st-century quantitative revisionism. It has generated significant popular discussion (especially in Russia) while remaining outside mainstream academic consensus in history, classics, archaeology, and related fields. In the wiki it is treated as a historiographical artifact rather than a source of factual historical claims.

## Historiography

Fomenko's mathematical credentials are undisputed. His chronology publications have been criticized by historians and scientists for methodological issues, selective data handling, and contradictions with multiple independent dating techniques and contemporary records. The project is frequently classified as pseudohistory or fringe science in academic literature. It is documented here for its influence on popular and alternative views of chronology and as an example of statistical approaches applied to historical texts.
