## Role and Background

Major General Melvin Zais commanded the US 101st Airborne Division during the May 1969 [[events/battle-of-hamburger-hill-1969|Battle of Hamburger Hill]] (Dong Ap Bia, Ap Bia Mountain) in the A Shau Valley, ordering repeated frontal assaults over ten days against heavily fortified NVA positions.

## Hamburger Hill and Controversy

The hill was taken at a cost of over 70 American dead and several hundred wounded, only for US forces to abandon the position within weeks as inconsistent with the broader policy of avoiding fixed positions in the A Shau Valley. The apparent disconnect between the assault's cost and the hill's subsequent abandonment made Hamburger Hill one of the most publicly controversial actions of the war, fueling domestic criticism of attritional tactics and contributing to political pressure that accelerated US troop withdrawal policy; Zais publicly defended the operation's necessity and the performance of his troops against the ensuing criticism.
