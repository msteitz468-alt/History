# Pierre Curie

**Summary page.** Full narrative and network links live here. See the detailed analytic biography for [[actors/curie-marie]].

Pierre Curie (1859–1906) was a French physicist known for work on piezoelectricity, magnetism, and crystallography. He formed a scientific and personal partnership with Marie Skłodowska; together they discovered polonium and radium in 1898 and shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics with Henri Becquerel. His death in a 1906 carriage accident left Marie to continue and institutionalize the work. Their collaboration exemplified both romantic partnership and rigorous science under difficult conditions.

## Narrative

Born into a family of doctors and teachers, Pierre showed early talent in mathematics and physics. With his brother Jacques he discovered piezoelectricity. At the École de physique et chimie he worked on magnetism and symmetry in crystals. He met Marie in 1894; they married in 1895 and began the research that led to radioactivity. He supported Marie's career in an era when few did. After the 1903 Nobel, he was appointed professor at the Sorbonne. He died suddenly on 19 April 1906 after slipping in front of a horse-drawn wagon on rue Dauphine. His death devastated Marie but also freed her to take his chair and lead the field.
