## Overview

The **Aetolian League** (*koinon* of the Aetolians) was the great federal state of
**central Greece**, the chief rival of [[antigonid-dynasty|Macedon]] and of the
[[achaean-league|Achaean League]] in the third century. Rooted in a poor, mountainous,
tribal land north of the Corinthian Gulf, it rose through the prestige of having
**defended Delphi against the Gauls (279)** to dominate the shrine and much of central
Greece. CAH VII.1 ch. 7 (Walbank) describes its structure and expansion.

## Federal Structure

Developed from a tribal *ethnos* into a true federal state (by c. 367), the League
combined cities-with-territory and intermediate districts (*tele*). Its organs:

- a **primary assembly** of all adult male citizens, meeting twice yearly (the spring
  *Panaetolica*, the autumn elections at **Thermum**) with wide democratic powers;
- a large representative **council (*synedrion*)**, over 1,000 strong — too unwieldy for
  quick action;
- consequently a small elected committee, the **apokletoi**, under the single annual
  **general (*strategos*)** — which made the administration progressively **oligarchic**
  in practice.

Aetolia remained socially backward and lightly urbanised; its "capital," Thermum, was
little more than a temple and an annual fair.

## Piracy, Asylia, and Expansion

The Aetolians supplemented a scanty agriculture with **piracy, brigandage, and mercenary
service** — and had a correspondingly bad reputation (relayed by the hostile
[[polybius|Polybius]], whose Achaea was their enemy). Their access to the Malian Gulf
exposed the Aegean to raids. States therefore sought from them grants of **asylia**
(immunity from reprisals) and **isopoliteia** (potential citizenship / legal access) —
which the Aetolians also used as **instruments of political expansion**, attaching distant
states (Ceos, Chios, Cephallenia, Messene, Phigalea) without annexation. Walbank notes the
spread of *asylia* and arbitration as signs of a "growing humanity" in the Hellenistic
period — even if the Aetolians exploited it for their own ends.

Their territorial growth is tracked through their rising number of votes on the **Delphic
Amphictyonic Council** (2 in 277, rising to 9 by c. 250s) as they absorbed Locris,
Phocian districts, and more. In c. 246/5 they reorganised the **Soteria** (commemorating
the Gaulish repulse) as a quadrennial panhellenic festival — a bid to have the Greek world
ratify their control of Delphi.

## Significance

The Aetolian League is the other great Hellenistic federal experiment alongside the
[[achaean-league|Achaean League]] — and proof that [[greek-federalism|federalism]] could
make a backward, semi-tribal people into a major power. It was the perpetual third force
in mainland Greece between Macedon and Achaea, leaning toward [[ptolemaic-egypt|Ptolemy]]
against Macedon. Its long collision with Macedon culminates in the [[social-war-220|Social
War]] (220–217; ch. 12).

## Rome's first Greek ally (CAH VIII, Ch. 4, Errington)

The League was **Rome's first formal ally in the Greek world**. Seeking a proxy to
keep [[philip-v|Philip V]] occupied during the [[first-macedonian-war-214-205bce|
First Macedonian War]], Rome concluded a treaty (212/211) by which **Aetolia took
the conquered cities and Rome the moveable booty** — turning the war over to Aetolian
ambitions in central Greece. Worn down by a resurgent Philip and feeling
under-supported, the Aetolians made a **separate peace (206)** in breach of the
treaty — the act that left Rome to settle at Phoenice (205) and that Rome long
remembered. The League's later turn *against* Rome (siding with
[[antiochus-iii-the-great|Antiochus III]]) brought its subjugation (167). See
[[first-macedonian-war-214-205bce]], [[achaean-league]], [[greek-federalism]],
[[celtic-invasion-of-greece-279]].
