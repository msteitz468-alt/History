# Empress Anne

## Biography / Description
**Empress Anne** (Anna Ivanovna) was the daughter of Tsar Ivan V (Peter the Great's half-brother) and the Duchess of Courland from 1711 until her accession to the Russian throne in 1730 (L3122, L3132-L3134). Following the death of Peter II in 1730, she was invited by the Supreme Secret Council to rule Russia under conditions that limited autocracy, which she initially accepted but subsequently tore up to restore absolute rule (L3132-L3136). She ruled as Empress of Russia from 1730 until her death in 1740 (L3132, L3198). Her reign is historically associated with the dominant influence of her Baltic German favorite Ernst-Johann Biren and the police terror known as *Bironovshchina* (L3196-3198).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Tearing up the "Conditions" (1730):** The Supreme Secret Council offered Anne the throne on the condition that she yield control over war, peace, taxation, military appointments, and succession to the council (L3134). Exploiting the dissatisfaction of the guard regiments and gentry who opposed this oligarchic clique, Anne publicly tore up the conditions and abolished the Supreme Secret Council to restore autocracy (L3136-3138).
* **Bironovshchina:** Anne relied heavily on Courland favorites, particularly her lover Ernst-Johann Biren, who dominated court affairs (L3196). Her government conducted a campaign of political police surveillance and exile to Siberia (L3198).
* **Gentry Privileges:** Anne's government systematically enhanced the rights of the gentry: it repealed Peter I's inheritance restriction in 1731, established the Noble Cadet Corps to allow nobles to become officers without serving in the ranks, and limited compulsory state service to twenty-five years in 1736 (L3232-3236).
* **Foreign Policy:** Under the diplomatic guidance of Ostermann and the military leadership of Munnich, Russia formed a formal alliance with the Hapsburgs in 1726, fought the War of the Polish Succession (1733–1735), and waged a war against Turkey (1736–1739) that resulted in the Treaty of Belgrade (L3256-L3258, L3266).
* **Succession Law:** Before her death in 1740, she designated her infant grand-nephew Ivan VI as emperor, appointing Biren as regent (L3198).

## Historical Significance
Anne's accession and rejection of the "Conditions" marked the defeat of the only formal attempt in 18th-century Russia to limit autocracy through a written constitution (L3136-3138). Her heavy reliance on German favorites fueled a nationalistic backlash in Russian historiography, which castigated her era as one of foreign exploitation, though modern research highlights the continuity of Petrine policies during her reign (L3196). Under her administration, the gentry began its successful escape from compulsory state service, while the peasantry suffered under expanded serfdom (L3228-3230, L3244).
