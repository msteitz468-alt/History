# Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus ("Pompey the Great")

The soldier whose **wholly irregular, wholly military career** — private armies,
extraordinary commands, a triumph and a consulship before he had held any office —
broke open the [[sullan-constitution|Sullan order]] and prefigured the dynasts.
This page covers his career **through the eastern command (to 62 BCE)**; his
collision with Caesar belongs to later cycles. Treated in [[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]]
Chs. 6–7 (Seager) and Ch. 8a (Sherwin-White).

## The rise to 70 BCE

**A private army (83).** Son of the unloved **Pompeius Strabo**, the young Pompey
raised **three legions on his own initiative** in Picenum and brought them to
[[sulla|Sulla]] in the **[[sullan-civil-war-83-82bce|civil war]]** — a private citizen
levying a private army, the defining act of the new age.

**Sicily and Africa, and the first triumph (82–81).** Sulla sent him to recover the
grain provinces; he executed Carbo and Domitius (earning the tag *adulescentulus
carnifex*, "the teenage butcher"), and then, refusing to disband, **bullied Sulla
into granting a triumph at the age of 24, while still an *eques*** — warning that
"more men worship the rising than the setting sun." Sulla hailed him **Magnus**.

**Lepidus and Sertorius (77–71).** After Sulla's death Pompey helped Catulus crush
the **revolt of Lepidus** (77), then again refused to disband, angling for the
Spanish command against **[[sertorian-war-80-72bce|Sertorius]]** — which the Senate,
having no one else, gave him "*non pro consule sed pro consulibus*" (L. Philippus).
After years of hard, often unsuccessful fighting (the disaster at Lauro, the drawn
battles of the Sucro and Segontia), the war was won when Sertorius was murdered by
his own officers; Pompey settled Spain with his trademark "blend of humanity and
self-interest," building vast *clientelae*.

**Spartacus and the consulship (71–70).** Returning to Italy, Pompey mopped up 5,000
fugitives from the **[[spartacus-revolt-73-71bce|Spartacus revolt]]** and claimed —
to Crassus' fury — to have ended the war. Both men kept their armies near Rome and
stood for the **consulship of 70**: Crassus duly qualified, Pompey **exempted by
special decree** from the *lex annalis* despite being too young and never having
sat in the Senate (he asked Varro for a handbook on senatorial procedure). As
consuls they **restored the legislative power of the tribunate** — their one act of
cooperation, and a decisive breach in the [[sullan-constitution|Sullan settlement]].

### The Eastern command (67–62)

Out of office after 70, Pompey was raised by two tribunician laws to
**Mediterranean-wide commands** (see [[pompey-pirate-command-67bce]]): the **Lex
Gabinia** (67) against the pirates — cleared in three months — and the **Lex
Manilia** (66), transferring the war against **[[mithridates-vi|Mithridates]]** and
Tigranes from [[lucullus|Lucullus]]. In the
**[[third-mithridatic-war-73-63bce|Third Mithridatic War]]** he broke Mithridates,
received the submission of **[[tigranes-ii|Tigranes]]**, campaigned through the
Caucasus, and then carried out the **[[pompeian-eastern-settlement-63bce|settlement of
the East]]** — annexing **Syria**, reorganizing Pontus, subjecting **Judaea** (the
siege of Jerusalem, 63), and ringing the new provinces with client kings. He came
home in **62** the richest and most powerful man in the Roman world — and, fatefully,
**disbanded his army**. The Senate's refusal to ratify his eastern *acta* or settle
his veterans drove him into the compact with Caesar and Crassus (Cycle 5).

### The triumvirate and the drift to war (60–50)

Blocked by the Senate over his eastern *acta* and his veterans' land, Pompey joined
**[[julius-caesar|Caesar]]** and **[[crassus|Crassus]]** in the
**[[first-triumvirate|First Triumvirate]]** (60), sealing it by marrying Caesar's
daughter **Julia**. He got his settlement ratified and his veterans settled in
Caesar's consulship (59); the alliance was renewed at **Luca** (56), giving him a
**second consulship (55)** and a five-year **Spanish command** he held *in absentia*
from near Rome. He held the **corn supply** (a great extraordinary command, 57).

The bonds then broke: **Julia died (54)**, and **Crassus fell at
[[battle-of-carrhae-53bce|Carrhae]] (53)**. Amid the anarchy after **Clodius'
murder**, Pompey accepted an unprecedented **sole consulship (52)**, restored order,
married into the Metelli (**Cornelia**), and drifted to the **optimates** — the very
*factio* he had once fought. Through 51–50 he backed the move to strip Caesar of his
command; when the Senate authorized him to defend the Republic, the rivalry ran to
**[[roman-civil-war-49-45bce|civil war]]** (49). Pompey **abandoned Italy** for the
East (a fateful choice), was checked at Dyrrachium, then **defeated at Pharsalus (48)**
despite superior numbers. He fled to **Egypt** — and was **murdered as he landed**, on
the orders of the boy-king Ptolemy XIII's ministers. Cicero's epitaph for his old
associate: "an honest, decent, serious man."

## Counterfactual / structural significance

Pompey's career is the proof that the Sullan order could not contain the forces
Sulla himself had unleashed. Every stage was an **exception granted to military
indispensability** — and each exception taught that the rules bent to whoever held
the legions. By restoring the tribunate he and Crassus reopened the instrument that
Caesar, Clodius, and the tribunes of the sixties would use to confer the next round
of **extraordinary commands** — beginning with his own
[[pompey-pirate-command-67bce|Mediterranean commands]] against the pirates and
Mithridates, whose [[pompeian-eastern-settlement-63bce|eastern settlement]] made him
briefly the unrivalled master of the Roman world.

## Historiography

Plutarch's *Pompey* and Sallust's *Histories* (the Spanish letters) are central;
Seager's *Pompey* underlies CAH IX's reconstruction. The degree of his early
"ambition" versus mere opportunism, and whether he and Crassus coerced their
consulships (Seager: they did not), are debated.
