# Narses

A Byzantine court eunuch and general who, in his seventies, led Justinian
I's final and decisive campaigns against the Ostrogoths in Italy, defeating
and killing King [[totila|Totila]] at the Battle of Taginae (552) and
crushing the last Gothic resistance under Teias at Mons Lactarius, ending
the [[gothic-war-535-554|Gothic War]].

## Role and Major Decisions

Originally a palace chamberlain with no prior major independent field
command, Narses was given supreme command in Italy after Belisarius's more
limited resources had failed to finish the war. Assembling and personally
funding a substantial army (including Lombard and Herul allied contingents),
he outmaneuvered and destroyed Totila's army at Taginae, then eliminated the
remaining Gothic forces at Mons Lactarius (552–553), effectively ending
organized Ostrogothic resistance in Italy. He subsequently served as
exarch (Byzantine governor) of Italy until his recall, amid Empress Sophia's
hostility, shortly before his death.

## Counterfactual Significance

Narses's victories completed the Byzantine reconquest of Italy that
Belisarius had been unable to secure permanently; the devastation of the
prolonged Gothic War under his and Belisarius's campaigns, however, left
Italy critically weakened and vulnerable to the Lombard invasion that
followed within years of Narses's death.

## Historiography

Narses's career is documented mainly through Procopius's *Wars* (for the
main campaigns) and later Byzantine and Lombard sources for his final years
and death; a later, likely apocryphal tradition blamed Narses's alleged
resentment at his recall for inviting the Lombard invasion of Italy, a claim
modern historians treat with considerable skepticism.
