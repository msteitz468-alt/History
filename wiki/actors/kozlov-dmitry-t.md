# Dmitry T. Kozlov

Soviet Red Army general (1896–1967), commander of the Crimean Front during the Kerch Peninsula campaign of winter–spring 1942. Appointed by Stalin on 28 January 1942 to unify the three Soviet armies that had landed at Kerch and Feodosiya into a single front tasked with destroying the German 11th Army under Manstein and reconquering the Crimea, Kozlov launched a succession of offensives (27 February, 13 March, 26 March, 9 April) against the German-Romanian Parpach line, all of which failed with heavy casualties.

Kozlov's position was further undermined by the presence of Stalin's political overseer Lev Mekhlis, whose interference historians have generally judged disastrous. When Manstein launched Operation Trappenjagd (May 1942), German air power under Wolfram von Richthofen struck Kozlov's headquarters within minutes of the opening attack, severing his communications and paralyzing the Crimean Front's response; the ensuing collapse cost the Red Army the Kerch Peninsula and tens of thousands of casualties. Kozlov was relieved of command, though — unlike Mekhlis, who bore the brunt of Stalin's anger — he continued to serve in lesser commands and staff positions for the remainder of the war.

## Significance

The Kerch disaster under Kozlov is a textbook case of operational-level paralysis produced by combined command dysfunction (political interference from Mekhlis) and decapitating enemy air attack on headquarters and communications.
