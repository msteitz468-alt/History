## Overview

The Mevlevi Order (Turkish: *Mevleviyye*; Arabic: *Mawlawiyya*) is the Sufi fraternity centered on the mystical legacy of Jalāluddīn Rūmī (1207–1273), headquartered in Konya (central Anatolia). It is the "only order in which the whirling movement has been elevated to a fully ritualized spiritual exercise" — the *samāʿ* or *sema* performed by the "*semazen*" (whirling dervishes). The order's name derives from *Mevlâna* ("Our Master"), the Turkish honorific for Rūmī.

## The Samāʿ Ritual

The whirling dance (*samāʿ*) is the Mevlevi's defining practice — "the earliest dance noted by the first European visitors to the convents of the Mevlevis." The ritual enacts Rūmī's theology of music and movement as spiritual ascension, grounded in his reading of the pre-eternal covenant (*yawm al-alast*):

> "A call reached Not-Being; Not-Being said: 'Yes [balā], I shall put my foot on that side, fresh and green and joyful!'" (D 1832)

The non-existent possibilities were called into being by the divine address — enraptured into a dance that became creation. Music and *samāʿ* re-enact this primordial event.

**Training**: The Mevlevi novice trained for **1001 days**, beginning with kitchen functions, then studying Rūmī's *Mathnawī* (correct recitation and interpretation), and finally learning the technique of the whirling dance. This rigorous curriculum distinguished the Mevlevis from more accessible popular orders.

## Social Position

Schimmel describes the Mevlevi order as "close to the house of the Ottoman emperors and, at the same time, the order of artists, inspiring music, poetry, and the fine arts." This dual court-and-artistic identity gave the order cultural prestige unlike any other: the music and poetry produced within its circles reached the highest levels of Ottoman classical culture.

The *sheikh* of the main convent in Konya — *Konya Dergâhı* — was known as the *Çelebi*, a hereditary position descended from Rūmī's family.

## Suppression and Revival

In 1925, Atatürk's decree abolishing dervish orders in Turkey closed all Mevlevi centers. Since **1954**, the order has been permitted to perform the *samāʿ* once annually — on the occasion of Rūmī's death anniversary, December 17 (*Şeb-i Arus*, "Wedding Night") in Konya, though not in the central sanctuary. The ceremony has since been performed internationally.

Modern Turkish poet Asaf Halet Çelebi (d. 1958) — "the last poet to genuinely interpret this feeling" of Mevlevi ecstasy, in Schimmel's view — wrote: "I whirl and I whirl / the skies whirl as well / roses blossom out of my face."

## Mevlûd-i Şerif (Süleyman Çelebi)

While not a specifically Mevlevi composition, the *Mevlȗd-i şerif* of Süleyman Çelebi of Bursa (d. 1419) — "a *mathnawī* in touchingly unsophisticated Turkish verse of the simplest possible meter, to which is added a sweet melody" — illustrates how Rūmī's cultural inheritance permeated Ottoman Turkish religious life. The poem, on Muhammad's birth, "is still recited on special occasions in many Turkish families."
