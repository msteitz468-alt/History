# Evagrius Scholasticus

The author of the **last of the classic Greek ecclesiastical histories** (c. 535/6–c. 594), a lawyer of
**Antioch** whose *[[whitby-evagrius-ecclesiastical-history|Ecclesiastical History]]* is the principal
continuous narrative of the Eastern Church — and much of the secular East — for the period **431–594**.

## Life

Born at **Epiphania** in Syria c. 535/6, Evagrius received an expensive education and became a
***scholasticus*** (advocate/lawyer) at **Antioch**, in the service of the patriarch **Gregory**, whom
he assisted and defended. He lived through the great calamities of his age in Syria — the recurrent
**plague** (which struck his own household repeatedly), Persian invasions, and the catastrophic
earthquakes of Antioch — and wrote his history in his late fifties, finishing c. **593/4**.

## The Work

His **Ecclesiastical History**, in **six books**, deliberately **continues the tradition of Eusebius,
Socrates, Sozomen and Theodoret**, beginning where Theodoret ended (the Council of Ephesus, 431) and
running to the twelfth year of the emperor **Maurice (594)**. Firmly **Chalcedonian**, it narrates the
long Christological controversies after **[[council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedon]]** but, unusually for the
genre, also incorporates a great deal of **secular history** — the reigns of the emperors from
Theodosius II to Maurice, the Persian wars, natural disasters — drawing on earlier secular historians
(Priscus, Procopius, John of Epiphania).

## Significance

Evagrius is the **indispensable Greek source** for the ecclesiastical and much of the political history
of the later fifth and sixth centuries in the East, and **the last representative** of the classical
Greek church-history genre before its long eclipse. His eyewitness account of the **plague at Antioch**
is a valuable independent witness, and his Chalcedonian narrative balances the Miaphysite Syriac
tradition of John of Ephesus and the Zuqnin chronicle.
