# Lucius Aemilius Paullus "Macedonicus"

The Roman general who won **Pydna** and ended the Macedonian kingdom — and the
natural father of **[[scipio-aemilianus|Scipio Aemilianus]]**. Treated in
[[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Ch. 9 (Derow), with his triumph and ethos in Ch. 6
(Astin).

## Role and career

Son of the consul killed at [[battle-of-cannae-216bce|Cannae]], Paullus reached a
second consulship in **168** and took command in the stalled
[[third-macedonian-war-171-168bce|Third Macedonian War]]. By a flanking march around
Olympus (led by Scipio Nasica) he forced **[[perseus-of-macedon|Perseus]]** from the
Elpeus and destroyed the Macedonian phalanx at **Pydna** (22 June 168). He
supervised the settlement that broke Macedon into **four republics**, and — on the
Senate's decree — let his army **sack 70 towns of Epirus**, enslaving 150,000, to
reward the soldiers' enforced discipline. His three-day triumph (167) was nearly
blocked by troops disgruntled at their (in fact large) donative.

His personal conduct became a Roman exemplum of restraint: he took **no share of the
booty**, a gesture his son Scipio Aemilianus would ostentatiously imitate after
Carthage. He brought the deported Achaeans (including [[polybius]]) to Italy, where
Polybius entered his sons' circle.

## Counterfactual / structural significance

Paullus is the executor of the harsh post-Pydna order in Greece. His combination of
battlefield decisiveness, settlement-engineering, and personal incorruptibility
made him a model of the Roman aristocrat at the height of the conquering Republic —
and, through his sons (one adopted into the Scipios, one into the Fabii) and his
patronage of Polybius, a node in the cultural Hellenization of the Roman elite.
