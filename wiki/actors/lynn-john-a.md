# John A. Lynn (1943– )

American military historian specializing in the institutional and operational history of early modern armies, particularly the French army of the Grand Siècle.

## Biography and Career
John Albert Lynn is Professor Emeritus of History at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. His major works focus on the practical realities of raising, supplying, commanding, and employing large standing forces in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

## Contribution to the Wiki
Primary source for [[sources/lynn-giant-of-the-grand-siecle-1997|Giant of the Grand Siècle (1997)]], the definitive study of the French army 1610–1715. Lynn provides the granular detail on army growth to ~420,000, multi-tiered administration, regimental economics and venality, recruitment and morale, siege-dominated positional warfare, and the complex relationship between that army and French state formation / absolutism.

His work directly qualifies and enriches the [[military-revolution]] debate (explicit engagement with Michael Roberts and [[parker-the-military-revolution-1988|Geoffrey Parker]]) and offers a major corrective to coercive "war made the state" models (Tilly et al.) by demonstrating distributed responsibility and the limits of centralization even at the height of Louis XIV's power.
