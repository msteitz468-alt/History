# Pan-German League

The most radical of Wilhelmine Germany's nationalist pressure groups —
small in membership but disproportionately influential, and an ideological
precursor of Nazism.

## Founding and Structure

Founded 1891 (as the General German League; renamed 1894) by the colonial
adventurer Carl Peters — forced out of the colonial service for mistreating
African bearers — and the young civil servant Alfred Hugenberg, later DNVP
leader. Chaired from ~1912 by Heinrich Class, whose pseudonymous manifesto
*If I Were the Kaiser* (1912) demanded stripping Jews of civil rights, banning
the SPD, restricting the franchise to the propertied, and a war of continental
conquest. Membership never reached 20,000 — far smaller than the allied Navy
League (300,000+) or Defence League — but elite networks, the right-wing
press, and the 1913 "Cartel of Productive Estates" alliance with the Agrarian
League gave it outsized reach.

## Program and Significance

Annexationist war aims (German-speaking Switzerland, the Netherlands, Belgium,
Luxembourg, Austria, plus colonial acquisitions), suppression of the SPD,
franchise restriction, and removal of "Jewish influence" from public life —
vulgarizing Nietzsche, Langbehn, Darwin, and Treitschke. Evans stresses these
groups were "a genuinely populist movement of political mobilization from
below," not elite manipulation — though with essentially no working-class
constituency. The League's 1913 memorandum (via retired General Konstantin von
Gebsattel), proposing anti-Jewish measures and a "coup d'état from above," was
rejected by Bethmann Hollweg and the Kaiser as impractical — though both
privately endorsed milder versions of the same prejudices (see
[[german-antisemitism]]).

## Decline

Sidelined from Wolfgang Kapp's mass-membership German Fatherland Party (1917)
as too extreme for broad appeal, the League "fell into sectarian obscurity
after 1918" (Evans). Its program — Anschluss, disenfranchisement of the Jews,
eastward conquest — was realized by the movement that superseded it.

## Historiography

Evans (2003) uses the League against manipulation-from-above (Wehler-school)
readings of Wilhelmine nationalism — relevant to the [[german-sonderweg]]
debate. Not yet ingested: Chickering's monographic treatment.
