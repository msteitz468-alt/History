# John Burgoyne (1722–1792)

British major general whose surrender at **[[battles-of-saratoga-1777|Saratoga]]** (17 October
1777) was the turning point of the **[[american-revolution|Revolution]]**. "Gentleman Johnny" — a
playwright and bon vivant as well as a soldier — devised and led the 1777 invasion south from
Canada down the Hudson corridor, intended to sever New England from the other colonies.

The plan unraveled through over-ambition and faulty coordination: [[howe-william|Howe]] turned
toward Philadelphia instead of marching to meet him, and the ministry (Germain) fatuously
believed both objectives achievable at once. Burgoyne's overland advance from Skenesborough
crawled at a mile a day against American obstruction; he "significantly overestimated … Loyalist
support," "mishandled his Indian allies" (whose departure after the Jane McCrea atrocity blinded
his scouting and inflamed New England militia), and outran his supply lines. Detaching a column
to Bennington cost him nearly 900 men. Checked at Freeman's Farm and broken at Bemis Heights —
where [[morgan-daniel|Morgan]]'s riflemen and [[arnold-benedict|Arnold]]'s aggression told —
Burgoyne surrendered ~5,900 men to [[gates-horatio|Gates]]. The victory brought **France into the
war**, transforming a colonial rebellion into a global conflict.
