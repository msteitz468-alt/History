# Enrico Corradini (1865–1931)

Italian nationalist writer, founder of the Italian Nationalist Association
(1910) and — with Barrès and Maurras — one of the carriers of the
"organic nationalism" pillar in [[sternhell-thesis|Sternhell's]] genealogy of
[[generic-fascism|fascism]]. He used the term "national socialism" in 1910
and coined its conceptual engine, the **proletarian nation**: "There are
nations that are in a condition of inferiority in relation to others, just as
there are classes... Italy is a proletarian nation: emigration demonstrates
this sufficiently. Italy is the proletarian of the world" (elaborated in *La
patria lontana*). The transposition of Marxist class struggle onto relations
between nations — "national imperialism and labor imperialism... the same
beyond these two variations" — was the bridge across which
[[revolutionary-syndicalism]] met nationalism.

Corradini theorized syndicalism as class economic solidarity and nationalism
as national economic solidarity, with the nation as "the largest functional
unit" and "primarily a moral factor"; he needed productionism to connect
nationalism to the masses. Writing in *Il tricolore* (with Mario Viana) and
alongside the syndicalists of *La lupa* (from October 1910), he championed
the Libyan War (1911) as the proletarian nation's war. Mass emigration —
draining the workforce while strengthening rivals — was his standing exhibit,
and a shared anxiety binding nationalists to syndicalists.
