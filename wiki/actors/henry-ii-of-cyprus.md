# Henry II of Cyprus

**Henry II**, king of Cyprus (1285–1324) and last crowned king of Jerusalem to hold Acre
until **[[fall-of-acre-1291|1291]]**. His long reign defines the first generation of Cyprus
as the sole major Latin outpost of the eastern Mediterranean after the mainland collapse.

## Accession and Acre

Crowned king of Cyprus June 1285 after his brother John's death. In June 1286 he recovered
Acre from the Angevin garrison (Sicilian Vespers had crippled Angevin expansion); crowned
king of Jerusalem at Tyre on 15 August 1286. He governed the mainland mainly through
lieutenants, visiting only twice after 1286: April–September 1289 (after Tripoli) and May
1291 with Cypriot reinforcements for Acre's final defence. Despite cowardice accusations,
[[edbury-kingdom-cyprus-crusades-1991|Edbury]] (following Hill, Richard, Prawer) finds his
personal conduct during the siege creditable; the problem was insufficient Christian
military strength. Cypriot forces (~200 knights as an upper limit) could not tilt the
balance.

## Sole outpost (1291–1306)

After 1291 Cyprus was the sole major western outpost (with [[cilician-armenian-kingdom|Cilician
Armenia]] the only other Christian state nearby). Henry relieved refugees, controlled bread
prices (1296), and recruited dispossessed knights. He styled himself *Jerusalem et Cypri
rex*, introduced the silver *gros* bearing the Cross of Jerusalem, and kept galley patrols
against the papal trade embargo — with limited effect while Famagusta–Syrian trade flourished.

Military initiatives: 1292 Alaya/Alexandria raid with Manuel Zaccaria; Ghazan's Syrian
campaigns 1299–1301 (Cypriot response always late or uncoordinated); Ruad (1302) ended
Cypriot-based efforts to regain the Holy Land. Writing in 1311, Henry favoured a direct
assault on Egypt from Cyprus.

## Amaury's coup (1306–10)

On **26 April 1306** brother Amaury, lord of Tyre, declared Henry too ill to rule and took
the title *gubernator et rector* with almost all the Ibelins and leading vassals — without
violence. Charges: insecurity, Genoese threat ignored, judicial delays of up to twenty
years, exclusive reliance on uncle Philip of Ibelin the seneschal. Only the queen-mother,
Philip, and cousin John Dampierre stood by Henry initially. After mediation he withdrew to
Strovolos with falcons. In February 1310 Amaury sent him into custody in Cilician Armenia
under King Oshin. On **5 June 1310** Amaury was murdered by household knight Simon of
Montolif (private homicide more likely than proven conspiracy). Famagusta, Paphos, Limassol
and Kyrenia declared for Henry; Hospitallers from Rhodes tipped the balance. Henry landed
at Famagusta **27 August 1310**. He imprisoned rather than executed opponents; dungeon deaths
of leaders followed in 1316; fief confiscations waited for Hugh IV in 1324.

## Later reign and assessment

Post-restoration: Genoese war (raids, internments; definitive settlement only 1329); strained
Armenia relations then 1322 aid when Mamluks overran Ayas; childless marriages (Maria of
Lusignan to James II of Aragon 1315; Constance of Sicily 1317 — reported impotent). Died
**31 March 1324** at Strovolos; nephew Hugh IV succeeded.

Edbury's judgment: despite personal shortcomings and the 1306 crisis, no Mamluk invasion
came; the dynasty survived short of civil war or removal; commercial prosperity unparalleled
in the island's history began in his reign.
