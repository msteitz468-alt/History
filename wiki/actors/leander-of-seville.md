# Leander of Seville

Bishop of Seville (c. 534–600/601) and elder brother and teacher of Isidore of Seville, whom he raised and educated before succeeding him as archbishop. Leander was the leading ecclesiastical architect of the Visigothic kingdom's conversion from Arian to Nicene (Catholic) Christianity under King Reccared, having spent time in Byzantine Constantinople (where he formed a friendship with the future Pope Gregory the Great) and worked for years to persuade the Visigothic elite toward orthodoxy.

Leander presided over the Third Council of Toledo (589), the landmark synod at which King Reccared and the Visigothic nobility formally renounced Arianism for Catholic orthodoxy, unifying the kingdom's Gothic and Hispano-Roman populations under a single confession and cementing the alliance between the Visigothic monarchy and the Catholic episcopate that shaped Spanish political culture for the remainder of the kingdom's existence.

## Significance

Leander's role at the Third Council of Toledo made the Visigothic conversion to Catholicism possible, ending a religious division between Gothic rulers and Hispano-Roman subjects that had persisted since the fifth century.
