# Shimazu Yoshihiro

Daimyo of the Shimazu clan of Satsuma domain in southern Kyushu, and a veteran commander of the invasions of Korea (1592–98) under Toyotoyomi Hideyoshi. At the [[battle-of-sekigahara-1600|Battle of Sekigahara]] (1600) he commanded a small Shimazu contingent nominally on the Western army's side but, disillusioned by poor coordination, largely withheld his forces from the fighting.

## Role

When the Western army collapsed, Yoshihiro executed one of the most celebrated tactical withdrawals in Japanese military history — the "Shimazu no Nokiguchi" (Shimazu breakthrough retreat) — driving his small force directly through the pursuing Eastern army's lines rather than fleeing around them, at heavy cost to his own men, and escaped to Satsuma. Remarkably, given Satsuma's remote, defensible geography and military reputation, Tokugawa Ieyasu chose to pardon the Shimazu rather than pursue a costly punitive campaign.

## Counterfactual Significance

The survival of Shimazu autonomy in Satsuma after Sekigahara is frequently cited as a structural exception within Tokugawa Japan, with long consequences: it was Satsuma's continued semi-autonomy and resentment of Tokugawa rule that fed directly into the domain's leading role, over two centuries later, in the movement that overthrew the shogunate in the Meiji Restoration.
