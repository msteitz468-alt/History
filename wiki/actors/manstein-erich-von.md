# Erich von Manstein

German field marshal, widely regarded as one of the most brilliant operational commanders of WWII. Key architect (with Guderian and others) of the 1940 sickle-cut plan through the Ardennes that defeated France. Later commanded in the Crimea (Sevastopol), led the brilliant 1943 Kharkov counteroffensive, and planned Operation Citadel (Kursk). Advocated elastic defense on the Eastern Front but clashed with Hitler over strategy and the "stand-fast" orders. Tried after the war; influential postwar memoirs.

See: [[guderian-heinz]], [[adolf-hitler]], [[world-war-ii-1939-1945]], [[operation-barbarossa-1941]], [[fall-of-france-1940]].

**From Murray & Millett, *A War to Be Won* (targeted extraction, ranges 1-2):**
- Manstein (planning/France): Suggested moving mechanized forces to Army Group A for Ardennes thrust; pestered OKH with Rundstedt to alter plan. (Ch. 3)
- Manstein (Barbarossa North): LVI Pz Corps reached Dvina in 4 days, captured bridges at Dvinsk. Urged continued drive but mishandled by dispersion on two axes. (Ch. 6)
- Manstein (Stalingrad relief): Selected for Army Group Don relief; sent optimistic report; urged breakout but Hitler refused and Paulus obeyed orders. (range2)
- Manstein (post-Stalingrad Kharkov): With SS divisions, launched major counterattack 20 Feb; closed 9 March despite mud; last major German offensive success on Eastern Front. (range2)
- Manstein (Zitadelle): Suggested encouraging Soviet attack then counterblow; proposed Kursk encirclement; wanted to continue despite Hitler cancellation for Sicily. (range2)
- Hitler (to Manstein): Flew to visit, blasted defensive attitude but Manstein surprised with readiness for counterattack. (range2)

- Manstein on Eastern Front (Zitadelle, Dnepr withdrawal): "Manstein had been the only commander on the Eastern Front to urge Hitler to continue Zitadelle... that his operations had gutted much of Soviet offensive capabilities. Thus, he moved a substantial portion of his armored reserves south..." "For Manstein and Army Group South, the retreat back to the Dnepr came too late. By waiting so long to make a sensible operational decision to retreat to more defensible positions, Hitler had produced two negative effects..." "On 25 March, after an all-day series of arguments, Manstein persuaded Hitler to order First Panzer Army to break out... " Dismissal: "On 30 March, Manstein and Kleist flew to Hitler’s headquarters... Hitler told Manstein that the time for great operational maneuvers had ended and Germany now needed commanders more capable of defending to the last man." **From Beevor *The Second World War* (targeted extraction):**
- Manstein’s *Sichelschnitt* plan reversed the 1914 Schlieffen right-hook: drew Allies into Belgium then cut through Ardennes/Meuse to coast. Hitler grabbed it after Manstein argued personally. (Range 1)
- Post-Stalingrad: Manstein extracted flexibility from Hitler; executed brilliant mobile counter-attack with 4th Panzer + II SS that retook Kharkov March 1943. (Range 2)
- Citadel: Advocated early May launch; Hitler nervous and delayed repeatedly despite Manstein. On 13 July (after Sicily), Hitler cancelled and diverted forces; Manstein protested but complied. (Range 2)
- Overall: One of the most brilliant operational minds; clashed with Hitler over strategy.
