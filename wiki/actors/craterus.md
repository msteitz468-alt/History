# Craterus

Macedonian general and close companion of Alexander the Great. Commanded the phalanx and other key forces; one of Alexander's most trusted and popular officers. After Alexander's death, played a leading role in the Wars of the Diadochi until killed in battle against Eumenes in 321 BCE.

## Role under Alexander

Rose through the ranks; commanded infantry and held senior positions in the later campaigns (India, etc.). Known for traditional Macedonian values and loyalty to the "old guard" against some of Alexander's orientalizing policies. At the time of Alexander's death, he was en route to Macedonia with veterans to replace Antipater.

## In the Wars of the Diadochi

Allied with Antipater against Perdiccas. Commanded forces in Asia Minor; defeated and killed by Eumenes of Cardia at the Battle of the Hellespont (or related actions in 321). His death removed a major stabilizing figure among the successors.

## Character and Significance

Ancient sources portray him as a solid, reliable commander beloved by the troops — the "Macedonian" counterweight to more ambitious or "Hellenizing" figures. His death illustrates the rapid fragmentation and personal rivalries that defined the early Hellenistic period.

## Historiography

Covered in sources on Alexander's companions and the Diadochi wars (Arrian, Diodorus via CAH and modern studies). Historians note his popularity and military competence but limited independent political vision compared to Ptolemy, Seleucus, or Antigonus. Collection has strong coverage in CAH volumes, Connolly on Greek/Roman warfare, and event pages on Lamian and Diadochi wars.
