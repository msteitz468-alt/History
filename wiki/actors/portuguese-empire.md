# Portuguese Empire

The Portuguese Empire was the earliest and, at its longest span, longest-lived of the European overseas empires, beginning with the capture of Ceuta in North Africa (1415) and the subsequent Atlantic and African voyages sponsored under Henry the Navigator, and reaching its first commercial-strategic peak with [[vasco-da-gama]]'s 1498 voyage around Africa to India and [[afonso-de-albuquerque]]'s early-16th-century seizure of key nodes — Goa, Malacca, Hormuz — that gave Portugal a fortified-trading-post network (the Estado da Índia) dominating Indian Ocean spice and textile trade routes rather than large territorial conquest.

In the Atlantic, Portugal pioneered the plantation-slave economy later copied across the Americas, developing sugar production in Madeira, the Azores, and above all Brazil (claimed 1500), fed by the Portuguese-organized transatlantic slave trade from West-Central African coastal outposts (Angola, São Tomé). Union with Spain (1580–1640) strained Portuguese overseas holdings, and the 17th century saw the Dutch and later English displace Portugal from most of its Asian trading network, leaving a residual empire concentrated on Brazil, Angola, Mozambique, Goa, and Macau.

Brazilian independence (1822) removed the empire's largest and richest possession; the 19th–20th century empire persisted chiefly in Africa (Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau, Cape Verde) and small Asian enclaves, fought prolonged colonial wars against independence movements from 1961, and was formally dissolved following the 1974 Carnation Revolution, with the handover of Macau to China in 1999 marking its final end.
