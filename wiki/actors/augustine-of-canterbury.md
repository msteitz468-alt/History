# Augustine of Canterbury (d. c. 604–609)

## Overview

**Augustine of Canterbury**, a monk of Gregory the Great's monastery of St Andrew on the Caelian, led the **Gregorian mission** sent by [[gregory-i|Pope Gregory I]] in 596–597 to convert the Anglo-Saxons. Received by **[[æthelberht-of-kent|Æthelberht of Kent]]** and Bertha, he established the see of **Canterbury**, which became the primatial centre of the English church.

## Role and Significance

Augustine's mission tied English Christianity to **Rome** rather than solely to the British/Irish traditions already present in the islands — a tension later negotiated at the [[synod-of-whitby-664|Synod of Whitby]] (664). Bede is the principal narrative source.
