## Overview

The **Achaean League** (*koinon* of the Achaeans) was the federal state that, in the
third century BCE, grew from a small ethnic confederacy on the north coast of the
Peloponnese into the dominant power of the peninsula — the great vehicle of Greek
**[[greek-federalism|federalism]]** in the Hellenistic age, and the political home of
[[polybius|Polybius]], who is our chief and admiring source. CAH VII.1 ch. 7 (Walbank)
traces its rise under **[[aratus-of-sicyon|Aratus of Sicyon]]**.

## Reconstitution and Structure

After lapsing in the fourth century, the League was **reconstituted from 280** (the
cities of Dyme, Patrae, Pharae, Tritaea, joined by Aegium in 275/4). In **255/4** it
replaced its two generals with a **single annual *strategos*** (not re-electable in
consecutive years) — a reform for efficiency with a safeguard against one-man
dominance. Its institutions, which [[polybius|Polybius]] praises as a model of
equality, free speech, and "genuine democracy":

- a **primary assembly** (*synodoi*, four times a year, open to men of military age),
  later supplemented by **summoned meetings** (*synkletoi*) for war, alliance, or
  communications from Macedon or Rome;
- a **council (*boule*)** of men aged 30+;
- federal magistrates — the general, ten *damiourgoi*, a secretary, hipparch, admiral.

The cities kept their own laws; common weights and coins followed the Aeginetan
standard (federal coinage only from c. 190). Walbank notes the democratic forms masked
an **office-holding class of landowners**, and the League socially favoured **stability
and the status quo** against land-redistribution or debt-cancellation — which set it
against the revolutionary Sparta of [[agis-iv|Agis IV]] and Cleomenes III.

## The Rise under Aratus

The turning point was **[[aratus-of-sicyon|Aratus']]** liberation of his native Sicyon
from tyranny (251) and his bold step of **uniting Dorian Sicyon to the (Achaean) League**
— beginning its transformation from an ethnic *koinon* into a pan-Peloponnesian body.
Funded by [[ptolemy-ii-philadelphus|Ptolemy II]] (who was made honorary *hegemon* of the
League, c. 243), Aratus reversed the balance of power in southern Greece by his
night-assault **capture of the Acrocorinth (243)** from [[antigonus-ii-gonatas|Antigonus
Gonatas]] — bringing Corinth, Megara, Troezen, and Epidaurus into the League.

## Significance

The Achaean League is the most successful experiment in Greek **federal union**, and the
chief native counterweight to [[antigonid-dynasty|Antigonid Macedon]] in the third
century. Its later history (in this volume, ch. 12) is paradoxical: to crush the social
revolution of [[cleomenes-iii|Cleomenes III]] of Sparta, Aratus called in the very
Macedonians he had spent his life expelling, joining Antigonus III Doson's **Hellenic
Symmachy** (224). It survived as the dominant Peloponnesian power until Rome destroyed it
in 146 BCE. See [[aratus-of-sicyon]], [[greek-federalism]], [[aetolian-league]].
