# Condoleezza Rice

American academic and diplomat who served as [[bush-george-w|George W. Bush]]'s National Security Advisor (2001–2005) and Secretary of State (2005–2009). A Soviet-affairs specialist and former Stanford provost, she was the first woman to hold the National Security Advisor post and the second woman (after Madeleine Albright) and first African-American woman to serve as Secretary of State.

## Role

As National Security Advisor, Rice was a central figure in the administration's response to the September 11 attacks and the decision to invade Iraq in 2003, supporting [[bush-george-w|Bush]]'s "Mission Accomplished" pivot toward democracy promotion. As Secretary of State she pursued the Six-Party Talks with North Korea, the US–India civil nuclear agreement, and diplomacy during the 2006 Israel–Hezbollah war and 2008 Russo-Georgian war.

## Counterfactual Significance

As a close, trusted adviser rather than an independent power center, Rice's absence would likely have altered the tenor rather than the substance of Bush-era foreign policy decisions, which were driven more by Cheney, Rumsfeld, and Bush himself.
