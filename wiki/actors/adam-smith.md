# Adam Smith

Scottish moral philosopher (1723–1790), the central figure of the **Scottish Enlightenment** and the
founder of modern **economics** — author of *[[smith-wealth-of-nations|The Wealth of Nations]]* (1776),
the foundational text of [[classical-political-economy|classical political economy]].

## Life and Work

A professor of moral philosophy at **Glasgow**, Smith first won fame with *The Theory of Moral
Sentiments* (1759), on sympathy and the social basis of morality. After years of travel and study he
published, in **1776**, *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations* — the work that
made him the founder of **political economy** as a discipline. He ended his life as a commissioner of
customs in Edinburgh.

## Significance

Smith provided the **intellectual foundation of market capitalism and economic liberalism**: the analysis
of the **division of labour** as the engine of productivity, the self-regulating market coordinated by
the "**invisible hand**," the critique of **mercantilism** and the case for free trade, and the labour
theory of value that later classical economists (Ricardo, Malthus, Marx) would build on and contest. His
ideas shaped the economic policy of the industrializing world and remain a touchstone in debates over
markets, government and wealth. (His thought is more nuanced than its later "free-market" caricature: the
*Wealth of Nations* must be read alongside the *Moral Sentiments* and his attention to justice and the
limits of self-interest.)
