## Role

Antigonus II "Gonatas" (c. 319–239 BCE), son of [[demetrius-i-poliorcetes|Demetrius
Poliorcetes]] and grandson of [[antigonus-i-monophthalmus|Antigonus I]], was the true
founder of the stable **[[antigonid-dynasty|Antigonid kingdom]]** of Macedon, which he
secured in 276 and ruled for nearly forty years. A man of culture and Stoic conviction
(a pupil of Zeno; he kept the philosopher Persaeus at his court at Pella), he gave
Macedon a long generation of stability after the chaos of the Successors.

## Decisions and Significance

CAH VII.1 (Will, ch. 4) covers his hard road to the throne. After Demetrius' fall
(288–283) Gonatas held only a fleet and the "Fetters of Greece" (Corinth, Chalcis,
Demetrias). He survived the apogee of [[lysimachus]], the murder of
[[seleucus-i-nicator|Seleucus]] by [[ptolemy-ceraunus|Ptolemy Ceraunus]], and a defeat
by Ceraunus — then seized his chance amid the **[[celtic-invasion-of-greece-279|Celtic
invasion]]**: in 277 he **annihilated a large Gaulish army by ambush near Lysimacheia**,
the only heavy defeat inflicted on the Celts in those years. This victory let him
present himself as a genuine *Soter* rather than a discredited pretender's son, and by
276 he was master of Macedon and Thessaly — fixing the three-dynasty pattern of the
Hellenistic world.

His long reign (treated in detail in [[walbank-cah-v7-1-1984|CAH VII.1]] ch. 7) faced
the recurring problem of holding Greece. He first had to survive **[[pyrrhus-of-epirus|Pyrrhus]]**,
who returned from Italy, seized Macedon (274), and was only removed by his death at Argos
(272). He then weathered the **[[chremonidean-war-267|Chremonidean War]]** (268–261, an
Athenian–Spartan–Ptolemaic coalition backed by Egyptian sea-power), capturing Athens and
defeating the Ptolemaic fleet at **Cos**. He ruled southern Greece through the "Fetters of
Greece" (Corinth, Chalcis, Demetrias), garrisons, and **client-tyrants** — earning a
reputation in Greece not as a liberator but as "the sponsor of tyrants" (which Walbank links
to the conspicuous *absence* of any ruler-cult for him). His later years were shaken by the
revolt of his nephew Alexander of Corinth and, decisively, by [[aratus-of-sicyon|Aratus']]
**capture of the Acrocorinth (243)** for the [[achaean-league|Achaean League]] — a blow he
never repaired, dying in the winter of 240/39. A pupil of Zeno, he reputedly called kingship
"a kind of glorious servitude."

## Significance

Gonatas converted his grandfather's dynastic claim and his father's fleeting kingship
into a durable institution. His Macedon was smaller and poorer than Egypt or the
Seleucid east but cohesive and strategically central; the dynasty he founded ruled
until Rome destroyed it at Pydna (168). His patronage of the Stoa made his court a
model of the philosopher-advised "glorious servitude" he is said to have called
kingship. See [[antigonid-dynasty]], [[hellenistic-monarchy]].
