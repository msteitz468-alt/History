# Saladin (Salah al-Din)

**Salah al-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub** (1137/38–1193), known in the West as **Saladin**, was
the Kurdish founder of the **Ayyubid dynasty** who united Muslim Egypt and Syria and
recaptured Jerusalem from the Franks. [[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] (R. Stephen
Humphreys, Ch. 23, "Zengids, Ayyubids and Seljuqs"; Hans Eberhard Mayer, Ch. 21) sets
his career within the wider unification of the Muslim Near East that doomed the
[[crusader-states|crusader states]].

## Rise

Saladin rose in the service of **Nur al-Din**, the Zengid ruler of Syria, as part of the
expeditions that intervened in the decaying **Fatimid caliphate of Egypt**. He became
**vizier of Egypt** (1169) and, on the last Fatimid caliph's death, **abolished the
Fatimid (Shi'ite) caliphate (1171)**, restoring Egypt to Sunni allegiance to the
[[abbasid-caliphate|Abbasids]]. On Nur al-Din's death (1174) he made himself master of
Egypt and, over the next decade, of Muslim **Syria** (Damascus, Aleppo) and Mesopotamia —
encircling the crusader states with a single great power for the first time and
proclaiming the **jihad** to recover Jerusalem.

## Hattin and Jerusalem

In 1187 Saladin annihilated the field army of the Kingdom of Jerusalem at the
**[[battle-of-hattin-1187|Battle of Hattin]]** and went on to take **Jerusalem** (October
1187) and nearly all the crusader strongholds. His capture of the Holy City provoked the
**Third Crusade (1189–92)**, in which he was matched by **Richard I of England**: the
crusaders recovered the coast (Acre) but not Jerusalem, and Saladin's negotiated truce
(1192) left him in possession of the city. He died at Damascus in 1193, his Ayyubid
dominions divided among his kin.

## Reputation

Saladin became a figure of legend on both sides — celebrated in the Islamic world as the
model *mujahid* who restored Jerusalem, and admired even in Latin Christendom (in Dante,
in chivalric romance) for his **magnanimity and chivalry**. He stands as the
counterpart and nemesis of the crusader states, and the founder of the Ayyubid order
that would govern Egypt and Syria into the thirteenth century (until the
[[crusades|Mamluks]]).
