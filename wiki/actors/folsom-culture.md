## Overview

Folsom is a Late Paleoindian archaeological culture defined by a distinctive fluted projectile point that succeeded [[clovis-culture|Clovis]] culture on the Great Plains and Rocky Mountains of North America. Like Clovis, it is defined primarily by its lithic technology. Folsom was first recognized in 1927 at the Folsom site, New Mexico, in direct association with bones of an extinct bison (*Bison antiquus*) — the discovery that originally established deep human antiquity in the Americas.

Folsom culture is notable for producing perhaps the finest fluted stone points in the prehistory of the Americas, rivaling or exceeding Clovis craftsmanship in precision. It flourished during the Younger Dryas cold spell (~10,950–9,750 BCE) and ended as the Holocene began.

## Defining Technology

Folsom points are bifacial and lanceolate in shape, fluted, and lacking barbs or a stem — like Clovis, but typically smaller and with longer, more refined flutes. They were made from high-grade materials, carefully chipped and ground along their lower edges to prevent the stone from cutting through the lashing used to haft them to spear shafts, foreshafts, or handles. The fluting of Folsom points is among the most technically demanding stone-knapping achievements in prehistory.

Like Clovis, Folsom represents a fluted-point tradition. Clovis and Folsom are the only two cultures in world prehistory known to have manufactured fluted projectile points.

## Chronology

Folsom culture dates to approximately 9,900–9,750 BCE (the text dates it as persisting "until around 9900 bce"), just before the Younger Dryas ended and the Holocene began. The onset of the Holocene (~9,750 BCE) disrupted monsoonal rain patterns on the Plains, reshaping environments and human adaptations. Folsom thus bridges the end of the Pleistocene and the very beginning of the Holocene.

## Subsistence and Economy

Folsom culture followed the extinction of mammoths and other Pleistocene megafauna associated with [[clovis-culture|Clovis]] times. Folsom hunters specialized in **bison**, particularly the now-extinct *Bison antiquus*, which was larger than modern bison. This specialized focus on bison herds made Folsom a **focal adaptation** — concentrated on a narrow prey range — in contrast to the more generalized Clovis toolkit.

Folsom people appear to have been highly mobile, following bison herds across the Great Plains. A rare Folsom structure at the Mountaineer site in Colorado (a foundation ring ~5 m in diameter) shows that shelters could house small groups beyond single nuclear families, but most settlement remains are ephemeral.

## Geographic Scope

Folsom is primarily a Great Plains and Rocky Mountain culture. Western Stemmed projectile points — not fluted — predominate along the Pacific Coast, where Clovis and Folsom points are very rare. This contrast reflects likely different origins: interior Paleoindians descending from Northern Paleoindians in Beringia through the ice-free corridor or across the Plains, versus coastal peoples who had arrived earlier via the Pacific coast.

## Decline and Transformation

Folsom culture ended around 9,750 BCE as the Holocene climate became established. The shift in monsoonal rain patterns disrupted Plains ecosystems. Paleoindians on the High Plains split into two adaptive modes:

1. **Foothills adaptation**: groups surviving in the Rocky Mountain foothills diversified their subsistence, exploiting plants and remaining fauna, transitioning toward Archaic patterns.
2. **Plains bison dependence**: groups on the open plains depended on bison herds. Bison kills declined sharply after ~6,000 BCE, and human habitation of the High Plains nearly ended between ~6,000 and ~3,000 BCE except for summer forays.

Fluting was dropped as a knapping technique in most regions after Folsom. Regional point types succeeded it — unfluted lanceolate types such as Plainview and Agate Basin (collectively called "Plano points") appear across the Plains; knappers elsewhere shifted to stemmed forms. These developments mark the transition into the Archaic period.

## Relationship to Clovis

Folsom derived directly from Clovis, representing regional differentiation and technological refinement. Clovis fluted points "diversified from standard Clovis forms to a variety of regional types," including Cumberland, Neponset, Nicholas, and Folsom. Folsom represents the persistence and intensification of the fluting tradition in a specific ecological context (Great Plains bison hunting) after Clovis culture had fragmented.

## Historiography

Folsom was the first Paleoindian culture to be recognized archaeologically, predating the recognition of Clovis itself. The 1927 discovery at Folsom, New Mexico — where a Folsom point was found embedded between the ribs of an extinct *Bison antiquus* — definitively established that humans had been in the Americas far longer than previously accepted by most scientists. This discovery preceded the formal recognition of Clovis culture, which came later from sites near Clovis, New Mexico.

Folsom points are among the most valued specimens in the antiquities market, and many modern fakes circulate — testament to both the aesthetic quality of the points and the difficulty of detecting skilled reproduction. Research on Folsom has focused on its relationship to Clovis (continuity vs. regional differentiation) and on bison hunting strategies.

**Dating methods**: Radiocarbon (calibrated). Dating of Folsom is generally well-established within its ~9,900–9,750 BCE range, though individual site dates vary.
