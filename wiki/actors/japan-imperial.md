# Imperial Japan

The Japanese state during its imperial era (1868–1945), from the Meiji Restoration's abolition of the shogunate through the militarist and expansionist period that ended in defeat and occupation. Under the 1889 Meiji Constitution, sovereignty resided formally in the emperor ([[actors/hirohito|Hirohito]] from 1926), while real authority shifted over time between civilian oligarchs, party governments, and — increasingly from the 1930s — the Army and Navy, which by law reported directly to the throne rather than through the civilian cabinet, giving the military effective veto power over government formation. Imperial Japan pursued continental expansion beginning with the annexation of Korea (1910) and accelerating after the 1931 Manchurian Incident, which produced the puppet state of Manchukuo and increasing international isolation (Japan withdrew from the League of Nations in 1933).

Full-scale war with China from 1937, alignment with Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy in the Tripartite Pact (1940), and the December 1941 attack on Pearl Harbor brought Imperial Japan into the wider Second World War, where it built and then lost a vast Pacific and Southeast Asian empire to Allied forces. The atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet declaration of war in August 1945 precipitated Japan's surrender; the postwar Allied occupation dismantled the imperial military and constitutional order, formally ending the imperial state with the 1947 constitution that reduced the emperor to a symbolic role.
