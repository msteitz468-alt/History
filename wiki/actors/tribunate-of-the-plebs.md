## Role and Background

The Tribunate of the Plebs was established, according to tradition, in 494 BCE following the plebeian order's first secession from Rome (a mass withdrawal in protest against patrician domination and debt bondage), as the central institutional achievement of the [[processes/struggle-of-the-orders|Struggle of the Orders]]. Initially two, later expanded to ten, tribunes were elected annually by the plebeian assembly and held sacrosanctitas — their persons were legally inviolable, and anyone who harmed a tribune could be killed with impunity.

## Powers and Evolution

Tribunes possessed the *ius intercessionis*, a veto that could block the actions of virtually any other magistrate, the Senate, or legislation, giving the office extraordinary defensive power over plebeian interests despite lacking independent command authority (*imperium*). Over the following two centuries the office was progressively opened to broader legislative initiative, culminating in the Lex Hortensia (287 BCE), which gave resolutions of the plebeian assembly (*plebiscita*) the full force of law binding on patricians as well as plebeians, effectively completing plebeian legal equality.

## Later History

In the late Republic the tribunate became a key vehicle for populist political action (the Gracchi, and later figures invoking tribunician authority against the Senate), contributing to the political violence that destabilized the Republic. Under Augustus, tribunician power (*tribunicia potestas*) was formally granted to and held permanently by the emperor himself, effectively absorbing the office's authority into the imperial constitutional settlement while the traditional tribunate persisted as a much-diminished honorific magistracy.
