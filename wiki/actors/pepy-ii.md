# Pepy II

**Pepy II** (Horus Netjerkhau; r. ~2278–2184 BCE) was the last great king of the
**6th Dynasty**, whose extraordinarily long reign closed the
[[actors/old-kingdom-egypt|Old Kingdom]] and shaded into its collapse.

## The longest reign in Egyptian history

Pepy II "inherited the throne at the age of 6," and his reign "of some ninety-four
years … was the longest in ancient Egypt." Its first half belonged to a still-functioning
state; "its second half probably was rather ineffective, as the forces that had been
insidiously eroding the theoretical foundations of the Egyptian state became apparent."

During his reign the administrators of the **Elephantine** nome (Harkhuf, Pepynakht
Heqaib, Sabni) led the great caravan expeditions into Nubia, bringing back incense,
ebony, ivory, "dancing dwarfs and exotic animals" — the famous letter of the boy-king
demanding the safe delivery of a dancing pygmy survives from this context. A settlement
flourished at **Ayn Asil** in the Dakhla Oasis. Yet the raiding scenes of Libyans and
Asiatics in his pyramid temple at South Saqqara are stereotyped copies of earlier reliefs
rather than records of real campaigns — a caution about reading royal monuments
literally.

## Toward collapse

After Pepy II came a brief, chaotic succession — Merenra II, the shadowy Queen
**Nitiqret** (2184–2181 BCE), and "some seventeen or more ephemeral kings" of Manetho's
7th and 8th Dynasties. "The fact that many potential royal successors were waiting in the
wings after Pepy II's exceptionally long reign probably contributed to the chaotic
situation that followed." His reign thus stands at the threshold of the
[[events/fall-of-old-kingdom-2160bce|Old Kingdom collapse]] and the
[[processes/first-intermediate-period|First Intermediate Period]].
