# Wampanoag

Southern New England Algonquian peoples including Pokanoket under Massasoit and later Metacom (King Philip). Central to Plymouth’s survival narrative and to [[king-philips-war-1675]] (Salisbury, CHNPA I.1 ch. 7). Communities endure in Massachusetts and beyond.

## Overview

### Contact, epidemic, alliance

English colonizers repeatedly intimidated Natives on Martha’s Vineyard, Cape Cod, and the Kennebec before permanent settlement. French long monopolized trade with Abenakis, Pawtuckets, Massachusetts, and Wampanoags. Epidemics **1616–19 killed ~90 percent** of these coastal Indians, exposing survivors to Europeans, [[micmac-mikmaq]], [[narragansett]], and [[pequot]]. Pokanoket Wampanoags under **Massasoit** welcomed Plymouth in 1621: corn and legitimacy for English in exchange for protection and a prominent place among SE New England Natives; Plymouth used coercion when extending alliance to Nausets and Massachusetts.

### Mid-century and King Philip’s War

Narragansett and Wampanoag burials in large cemeteries reflect high contact-induced mortality, with European goods and wampum solidifying alliances. By the 1670s debt land claims, criminal seizures, missionary village reduction, alcohol, and loss of outsider fur/wampum ties pressed expansion. Metacom led coalition resistance 1675–76; Mohawk intervention and English-allied Indians defeated the uprising. Coastal southern New England Natives reduced to scattered enclaves with cultural autonomy but restricted subsistence and political power.

### Later

Wampanoags appear among lower New England troops in Dummer’s War (1722–25). Fully subjugated eastern groups including some Wampanoag-related communities leaned Patriot in the Revolution (Salisbury notes Stockbridge Massachusetts Indians and Catawbas as American-supporting).

## Historiography

Thanksgiving mythology long occluded epidemic catastrophe and Metacom’s war. Salisbury, Jennings, Cronon re-center demography, land, and inter-Native politics. Name “King Philip” is colonial.
