# Totila

King of the Ostrogoths (541–552) who revived Gothic fortunes in Italy
against Byzantine reconquest, recapturing Rome twice, before being defeated
and killed by [[narses|Narses]] at the Battle of Taginae in 552, a defeat
that effectively ended the Ostrogothic kingdom.

## Role and Major Decisions

Taking the Gothic throne when Byzantine forces under [[belisarius|
Belisarius]] appeared close to completing the reconquest of Italy, Totila
rebuilt Gothic military strength, exploited Justinian's limited resources
and Belisarius's inadequate reinforcement, and retook Rome in 546 and again
in 550 after earlier Byzantine recapture. His pragmatic policies toward
Italo-Roman civilians and slaves (offering freedom to those who joined him)
broadened his base of support during the war's later phases. His defeat and
death at Taginae against Narses's much larger, better-resourced army in 552
marked the effective collapse of organized Gothic resistance.

## Counterfactual Significance

Totila's decade of resurgence demonstrated that the Gothic War, often
treated as effectively won after Belisarius's initial campaigns, remained a
genuinely contested, prolonged conflict; his death removed the last
capable Gothic leadership able to sustain resistance, and Ostrogothic Italy
did not survive his defeat by more than a year.

## Historiography

Totila's campaigns are documented primarily through Procopius's *Wars*,
whose portrayal (relatively favorable regarding Totila's treatment of
civilians, compared to his depiction of some Byzantine commanders) has
shaped modern assessments of the king's character; the prolonged,
destructive nature of the war under his resistance is considered a major
contributor to Italy's economic and demographic decline in the following
century.
