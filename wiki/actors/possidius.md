# Possidius

**Possidius** (c. 370 – after 437), bishop of Calama, a member of **[[augustine-of-hippo|Augustine's]]**
monastic household at Hippo, his lifelong friend, and the author of the *Vita Augustini* — the
sole contemporary biography of Augustine and, with the *Retractationes* and Possidius's own
*Indiculum* (a catalogue of Augustine's works), the foundation of Augustine's textual legacy.

A product of the Hippo "seminary," Possidius became bishop of Calama, where he was attacked by
Donatists (403) and later provoked a riot by breaking up a pagan procession (408). During the
**[[vandal-kingdom|Vandal]]** invasion he lost his see and took refuge with Augustine in
besieged Hippo — "we were all thrown together, with the terrifying judgements of God before
our very eyes" — and witnessed Augustine's death on 28 August 430. His *Vita* deliberately
emphasizes the daily, communal texture of Augustine's episcopate (the poverty rule, the table
verses against gossip, the forfeited wine-glass for swearing) rather than dramatic acts of
power — a gentler portrait than Paulinus's near-contemporary *Life of Ambrose*, and one Brown
reads as expressing the anxieties of Augustine's aging circle to preserve their ideals. He
preserved Augustine's Letter 228 (on why bishops must not abandon their flocks) within the
*Vita*, and was eventually driven from Calama by the Arian Vandal rulers of Carthage — an
irony, since he had once expelled Calama's Donatist bishop.
