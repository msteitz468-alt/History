# Theo van Gogh

Art dealer (Goupil & Cie) and younger brother of Vincent van Gogh (1857–1891). Theo provided the decisive financial, emotional, and professional support that enabled Vincent's brief career as a painter. After Vincent's death he attempted to secure his brother's reputation before his own collapse and death six months later.

## Narrative

Theo followed Vincent into the art trade but rose within Goupil while Vincent failed and was dismissed. From the early 1880s he sent regular stipends that kept Vincent alive and painting. Their correspondence (nearly 900 letters) constitutes one of the great documents of artistic self-understanding. Theo married Johanna ("Jo") Bonger in 1889; their son was named Vincent. He handled the practical side of Vincent's few sales and exhibitions. After the fatal shooting in Auvers, Theo rushed to the bedside, organized the funeral, and immediately began campaigning for recognition of Vincent's genius. His own health (advanced syphilis) collapsed under grief and guilt; he was institutionalized and died in January 1891. Jo later edited and published the letters, becoming the primary custodian of the Van Gogh legacy.

## Causal Analysis

Theo's steady employment and conventional success made him the family's reliable son; Vincent's "fanatic heart" made him the troubled opposite. Theo's decision to underwrite Vincent unconditionally was both fraternal love and an investment in an art he intuitively understood. The letters record Theo's repeated attempts to moderate Vincent's excesses (thick paint, speed, subject choice) while never withdrawing support. Theo's marriage and fatherhood added new pressures that coincided with Vincent's final crisis.

## Consequence Analysis

Without Theo's monthly allowance (150 francs and more) Vincent could not have painted the Arles and Saint-Rémy output. Theo's posthumous efforts, though cut short by his death, laid groundwork for Jo's later editions of the letters and exhibitions that established Vincent's reputation. The brothers' intertwined fates became a central myth of modern artistic dedication and sacrifice. Theo is buried beside Vincent in Auvers.

## Historiography

Naifeh & Smith (2011) give the fullest account of Theo's role, finances, health, and the psychology of his final months, using family papers and medical records. Earlier accounts often reduced Theo to "the brother who supported Vincent." Jo van Gogh-Bonger's 1914 edition of the letters made the correspondence public and shaped the image of the supportive sibling. Debates continue over the exact nature of Theo's illness and whether his breakdown was purely syphilitic or also psychological (guilt, identification with Vincent).
