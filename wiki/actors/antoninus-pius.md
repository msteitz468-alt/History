# Antoninus Pius (Titus Aurelius Fulvus Boionius Arrius Antoninus)

The **fourth of the "Five Good Emperors"** (138–161) — the mild, conscientious, stay-at-home
ruler whose long, peaceful reign was the **apogee of the Antonine "golden age,"** and whose chief
dynastic role was to bridge the succession from Hadrian to **[[marcus-aurelius|Marcus Aurelius]]**.
Treated in [[bowman-cah-v11-2000|CAH XI]] Ch. 3.

## A place-holder who endured

A wealthy senator of Gallo-Spanish descent with little military or provincial experience (one
proconsulship of Asia), Antoninus was chosen by **[[hadrian]]** — perhaps **precisely for his
lack of ambition** — as the "place-holder" to secure the eventual accession of Hadrian's favourite,
the young **Marcus** (whom Antoninus was required to adopt, with Lucius Verus). Hadrian "can
hardly have expected that Antoninus would survive a further twenty-three years," giving the empire
**over forty years of peace**. He earned the name **"Pius"** by forcing a reluctant Senate to
**deify Hadrian** (whom they hated), threatening that refusal would annul his own adoption.

## The mild reign

CAH XI quotes Marcus' famous portrait of his adoptive father in the *Meditations* — gentleness,
resolution after full investigation, no vainglory, love of work, the same friends kept, "a good
steward of the empire's resources," prudent and economical in shows and building, "interested in
what has to be done, not in the reputation he gets." Antoninus governed **without leaving Italy**,
through trusted long-serving deputies (the praetorian prefect Gavius Maximus), respected the
Senate, attended to **jurisprudence** (the jurist Salvius Julianus), and left a **large fiscal
surplus**. The famous **Roman Oration of Aelius Aristides** — its panegyric of an empire that had
"placed walls not around the city but around the empire," its army a "rampart enclosing the
world," war "only a memory" — captures the era's confident self-image.

## Limited military activity

Departing slightly from Hadrian's strict frontier policy, Antoninus advanced in **Britain**: his
governor Lollius Urbicus invaded southern Scotland and built the **Antonine Wall** (turf, between
Forth and Clyde, c. 142) — the occasion of Antoninus' only imperial salutation in 23 years —
though it was later abandoned and Hadrian's Wall reoccupied. He installed Roman nominees in
**Armenia** and on the Danube (the Quadi), suppressed a **Moorish uprising** and abortive
usurpations in Spain, and otherwise kept the peace.

## Significance

Antoninus' reign was the **high-water mark of the Pax Romana** — prosperous, stable, and
unwarlike — and its very tranquility left it thinly recorded. But his refusal to give Marcus any
**military or provincial training** (and his own reluctance to travel) bequeathed his philosopher-
successor an empire **unprepared for the storms to come**: within months of Antoninus' death
(161, his watchword "Equanimity") the **Parthian War** and then the **Marcomannic Wars** and the
**plague** broke over Marcus. He was the last emperor to die peacefully in possession of an empire
at the summit of its fortunes.
