# Andrew F. Krepinevich, Jr.

U.S. Army officer, West Point faculty member, and defense analyst best known for *The Army and Vietnam* (1986), a landmark institutional history arguing that the Army's rigid adherence to its conventional "Army Concept" was the central reason for its failure against the Vietnamese insurgency.

The book draws on Army archives, interviews, and personal experience to document how doctrine, training, personnel policies, and career incentives systematically favored big-unit conventional operations over the requirements of counterinsurgency. It remains a foundational text for understanding U.S. military adaptation (and non-adaptation) in Vietnam and influenced later debates on low-intensity conflict and doctrine.

Krepinevich went on to a prominent career in defense analysis, founding and directing the Center for Strategic and Budgetary Assessments (CSBA) and contributing to post-Cold War and post-9/11 strategic debates.
