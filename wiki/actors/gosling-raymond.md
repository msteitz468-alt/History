# Raymond Gosling

Raymond George Gosling (15 July 1926 – 18 May 2015) was an English physicist and molecular biologist who worked as a graduate student at King's College London in the Medical Research Council (MRC) Biophysics Unit.

## Role and Significance

Gosling was a central experimentalist in the structural analysis of DNA. Working under the supervision of Maurice Wilkins and later Rosalind Franklin, he was responsible for taking the highest-quality early X-ray diffraction photographs of DNA fibers, culminating in the creation of Photograph 51 in May 1952. His thesis and research contributions provided crucial data that enabled the discovery of the double-helical structure of DNA.

## Key Events and Activities

### Early X-ray Diffractions (1950)
In 1950, working under Maurice Wilkins, Gosling was assigned the task of analyzing the high-molecular-weight DNA fibers obtained from Rudolf Signer. Utilizing simple apparatus in the King's basement, Gosling obtained the first clear fiber diagrams showing an "X" pattern, indicating a regular, crystalline structure.

### Collaboration with Rosalind Franklin (1951–1953)
Upon Franklin's arrival in January 1951, Randall assigned Gosling to assist her. Together they reassembled and upgraded the X-ray equipment, implementing Werner Ehrenberg and Walter Spear's fine-focus tube and designing a tilting microcamera. Under Franklin's direction, Gosling performed the crucial hydration experiments, which allowed them to discover and control the transition between the A (dry) and B (wet) forms of DNA.

In May 1952, they exposed B-form DNA to obtain Photo 51. Despite its clarity, Randall's partition directed them to focus on the crystalline A-form. Gosling carried out the complex cylindrical Patterson calculations for the A-form. These calculations yielded a 14 Å repeat that suggested a non-helical structure, misleading Franklin into doubting the helix for the A-form.

### Sharing of Photo 51 (1953)
By January 1953, Franklin was preparing to leave King's for Birkbeck College. As Gosling was completing his doctoral thesis without his supervisor, he shared the B-form Photo 51 with Maurice Wilkins, who subsequently showed it to James Watson. Gosling later defended this decision, stating that "Maurice had a perfect right to that information."

Following Franklin's departure, Gosling finished his PhD at King's and went on to a successful career in medical biophysics.
