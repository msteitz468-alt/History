# Hongshan Culture

The **earliest known [[jade-age|Jade Age]] culture** of the [[east-asian-heartland-region|East
Asian Heartland]] (c. 3500–2500 BCE), in the **Near Northeast** — the Liaoning peninsula, the Liao
River valley, and eastern Inner Mongolia. From [[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook (2017)]].

## Jade and Ritual

Hongshan is defined by its sophisticated **worked jade**, above all the **"pig-dragon"** — a
C-shaped pendant with an animal head (read variously as pig, horse, or dragon) worn at the neck.
The culture demonstrates that an elaborate **ritual jade economy** preceded metallurgy in the
Heartland by over a millennium, the founding instance of the Jade Age pattern.

## Niuheliang

The ceremonial center at **Niuheliang** features **step-pyramid stone-lined elite tombs** and a
**"Goddess Temple"** — a multi-room structure with mural-painted walls and clay statues of heavy-set
(possibly pregnant) women, some **up to three times life-size**, with **blue-jade inlaid eyes**.
This monumental religious complex is unique in the Heartland and implies a ritual authority able to
command substantial collective labour.

## Economy and Decline

Hongshan combined **hunting, herding** (sheep, cattle, pigs, dogs, chickens), and **agriculture**,
and used a new **ox-drawn stone plow** more efficient than hoe tillage. Its decline after c. 2500
BCE is attributed partly to the **cooling and drying** of the Near Northeast as the Mid-Holocene
Climatic Optimum faded, undermining the agricultural surplus; the culture may have been displaced or
absorbed by the expanding [[longshan-culture|Longshan]] sphere.

## Significance

Hongshan establishes two themes Major & Cook stress: the **antiquity and primacy of jade ritual**
(see [[jade-age]]), and the **multi-regional** character of Heartland development — a major
ceremonial culture far to the northeast of the [[central-plains-china|Central Plains]], consistent
with the [[quxi-leixing|quxi leixing]] model and against a single Yellow River origin (see the
[[origins-chinese-civilization-monocentric-vs-multiregional|origins debate]]).
