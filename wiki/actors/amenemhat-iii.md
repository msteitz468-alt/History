# Amenemhat III

**Nimaatra Amenemhat III** (r. ~1831–1786 BCE), the only known son of
[[actors/senwosret-iii|Senusret III]], presided over the long, peaceful, prosperous reign
that is arguably the cultural peak of the [[actors/middle-kingdom-egypt|Middle Kingdom]]:
"consolidation of what had gone before appears to have been the hallmark of Amenemhat's
style of government."

## The Faiyum king

Amenemhat III is identified above all with the development of the **Faiyum**: colossal
quartzite seated statues at Biahmu (later seen by Herodotus), a great temple to the
crocodile-god **Sobek** at Crocodilopolis, and extensive land-reclamation works. He pushed
**Sinai turquoise and copper mining** onto a near-permanent footing (fifty-nine graffiti
attest his expeditions) and recorded exceptionally high **Nile floods** at the Nubian forts
of Kumma and Semna — followed by a sharp drop late in the reign that "must have had a
destabilizing effect on the economy."

## The Labyrinth and the late dynasty

His first pyramid at Dahshur cracked during construction and was abandoned; he was buried at
**Hawara**, whose vast mortuary temple — "a maze of rooms and corridors" — became famous to
Greek and Roman writers as the **Labyrinth** (described by Herodotus, Strabo, and Pliny).
The increased prominence of royal women (his probable daughter **Sobekneferu**, who would
later rule; the burials of queens at Dahshur) "suggests the increased status of royal women
in the late 12th Dynasty." His reign's large intake of **Asiatic workers** in the Delta,
the volume suggests, "may have encouraged the so-called Hyksos to settle in the Delta, thus
leading eventually to the collapse of native Egyptian rule" (see
[[processes/second-intermediate-period]], [[actors/hyksos]]). Later Egyptians worshipped him
as the Faiyum god **Lamares**.
