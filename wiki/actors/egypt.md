# Egypt (Modern State)

Modern Egypt emerged from nominal independence from Britain in 1922 (full sovereignty
came only gradually, with British forces remaining until 1956) as a constitutional
monarchy under the Muhammad Ali dynasty. This distinct modern nation-state is separate
from the wiki's ancient/classical Egypt pages (e.g. [[actors/ptolemaic-egypt]],
[[actors/new-kingdom-egypt]]), which cover earlier, unrelated political formations along
the same geography.

## The Republic and Nasser Era

The monarchy was overthrown in the 1952 Free Officers coup, led by a group of army
officers including [[gamal-abdel-nasser|Gamal Abdel Nasser]], who became president in 1954
and turned Egypt into the leading voice of pan-Arab nationalism and a founding member of
the Non-Aligned Movement. Nasser's nationalization of the Suez Canal (1956) triggered the
Suez Crisis, a diplomatic and military confrontation with Britain, France, and Israel that,
despite Egypt's military defeat, became a political victory for Nasser and accelerated
British and French imperial decline. Egypt suffered a heavier military defeat against
Israel in the 1967 Six-Day War, losing the Sinai Peninsula. Nasser's successor Anwar Sadat
later fought the 1973 October War and then reversed course diplomatically, signing the
Camp David Accords (1978) and a peace treaty with Israel (1979) — a move that isolated
Egypt within the Arab world but recovered the Sinai.
