# The Hohenstaufen Dynasty

The **Hohenstaufen** were a Swabian noble family that held the German (and, after 1155,
Holy Roman imperial) crown almost continuously from 1138 to 1254, and, through marriage
into the Norman line, the Kingdom of Sicily from 1194. The dynasty's greatest figure was
[[frederick-i-barbarossa|Frederick I "Barbarossa"]] (r. 1155–1190), who sought to
reassert direct imperial authority over the wealthy Lombard cities of northern Italy and
clashed repeatedly with the papacy and the Lombard League; his grandson Frederick II
(r. 1220–1250) built an unusually centralized, bureaucratic state in Sicily and continued
the dynasty's long struggle with the papacy over control of Italy.

## Decline

Papal opposition to Hohenstaufen power in Italy — the dynasty's simultaneous hold on
Germany and Sicily threatened to encircle the Papal States — culminated in Pope Innocent
IV's deposition of Frederick II (1245) and, after Frederick's death, papal sponsorship of
Charles of Anjou's conquest of Sicily. The last Hohenstaufen claimant, the teenage
Conradin, was defeated and executed at Naples in 1268, ending the male line and leaving a
power vacuum in Germany (the "Great Interregnum") and Italy that shaped the political
fragmentation of both regions for centuries.
