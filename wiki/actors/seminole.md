# Seminole

Florida multi-ethnic polity formed from Lower [[creek-muscogee|Creek]] migrants, African-descent communities, and post–Creek War Nativist refugees; fought the costliest Removal war. Green, CHNPA I.1 ch. 8.

## Overview

Origins: Lower Creek migrants to Spanish Florida from the early 18th century; augmented by escaped South Carolina/Georgia slaves (intermarried, enslaved, or autonomous allied communities) and defeated Creek civil-war Nativists after Horseshoe Bend (1814). Post-1812 Georgia planter raids for runaways. **First Seminole War 1818:** [[jackson-andrew]] led Army + Creek warriors into Florida; **1819** Spain sold Florida. **1823** treaty: peninsula reservation—unproductive, insufficient; settlers feared African-descent people among Seminoles.

## Second Seminole War and Removal

**9 May 1832** removal treaty with western-tour proviso; Fort Gibson document treated by U.S. as final. Required move onto western Creek Nation—alarmed refugees (retribution) and black Seminoles (enslavement by Creek slaveholders). **[[second-seminole-war-1835]]** (to 1842): U.S. **1,500** dead, **>$20 million**; unknown Seminole dead; ~**4,000** Seminoles and African-descent allies relocated via New Orleans/rivers to Fort Gibson; ~**500** remained southern Florida swamps. Mid-1850s Third Seminole War → **200** more west. **1856** purchased tract from Creeks; re-created Nation.

## Civil War

Deep division (like Creeks/Cherokees); 1866 reconstruction: lost all lands, received small parcel from Creeks.

## Counterfactual significance

Without black Seminole freedom stakes and fear of Creek enslavement, Removal resistance might have been shorter; Green ties Fort Gibson/Creek-host clause directly to war causation.

## Historiography

Mahon standard on Second Seminole War (Green’s bibliographic pointer). Full-fidelity war costs and remnant Florida population are load-bearing.
