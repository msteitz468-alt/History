# Fabius Maximus "Cunctator"

The Roman statesman and general who devised the **strategy of attrition** against
[[hannibal]] — the "delayer" (*Cunctator*). Treated in [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH
VIII]] Ch. 3 (Briscoe).

## Role and career

Appointed **dictator** after the disaster at Lake Trasimene (217), Fabius adopted
the policy of **shadowing Hannibal while refusing pitched battle** — keeping to the
hills, harassing, and denying the invader a decisive engagement on favourable
ground (see [[fabian-strategy]]). The policy was deeply unpopular: it meant letting
Hannibal ravage the *ager Falernus* unchecked, and the assembly took the
extraordinary step of equalising the *imperium* of his *magister equitum* **Minucius
Rufus** with his own. Minucius' near-disaster vindicated Fabius.

After [[battle-of-cannae-216bce|Cannae]] (216) the Fabian approach became Roman
orthodoxy until c. 210. Fabius held repeated consulships (he and his associates
dominated the colleges of 215–213), recaptured **Tarentum** (209), and in 205
**opposed [[scipio-africanus|Scipio's]] plan to invade Africa**, urging that
Hannibal be expelled from Italy first. He died in 203, before the victory at Zama.

## Counterfactual significance

Fabius embodies the structural-attrition answer to Hannibal's tactical genius:
his strategy converted Rome's reserves of manpower and allied loyalty into a
slow, grinding advantage that battlefield brilliance could not overcome. CAH VIII
treats him as head of a political grouping ("Fabian," overlapping the
"Fulvian") opposed to the Scipios' "forward strategy" — a reading resting on
contested prosopography. His name became the byword for cautious, delaying warfare
(Quintus Ennius: *unus homo nobis cunctando restituit rem*).

## Historiography

[[livy]] dramatizes the Fabius–Minucius and Fabius–Scipio conflicts; [[polybius]]
analyses the strategy in book III. The factional framework (Scullard, accepted in
part by Briscoe) makes Fabius a political as well as a strategic figure, but the
*gentes*-based reconstruction is debated. See [[second-punic-war-218-201bce]] and
[[fabian-strategy]].
