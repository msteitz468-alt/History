# Shawnee

Algonquian people repeatedly displaced and reconstituted across the Ohio Valley, Southeast, and Pennsylvania—Salisbury’s exemplar of refugee mobility inside [[eastern-woodlands-colonial-encounter-1600-1783]].

## Overview

### Seventeenth-century displacements

[[beaver-wars-17th-century]]: Iroquois drove most Shawnees (with Ottawas and other Algonquians) from Michigan/Ohio Valley; refugees to Wisconsin; some south. Savannah Shawnee refugees with “Goose Creek men” destroyed Westos by 1684, opening Southeast Indian–English trade. From 1707 many Savannahs moved to Pennsylvania; SC armed piedmont Indians (becoming [[catawba]]) against them; Shawnees enlisted Iroquois → ~seventy years intermittent Iroquois–Catawba warfare.

### Ohio “republicans” and imperial wars

Forced west from Delaware Valley with some [[lenape-delaware]]; rebuffed PA/Iroquois control; claimed Ohio ownership independent of French, English, Six Nations. 1747 joined anti-French attacks; later reassessed English land threat. Braddock 1755 alienated still-willing Shawnees; they attacked PA frontier. Easton 1758; [[pontiacs-war-1763]] participation. Fort Stanwix 1768 ceded Shawnee hunting lands via Iroquois never controlling them. Shawnee anti-Stanwix coalition blocked by Johnson and pro-English Iroquois. **Dunmore’s War 1774:** after Point Pleasant, humiliated Shawnees gave up Kentucky hunting rights for pledge English not settle north of Ohio. Revolution: increasing Shawnee joined Mingo raids; Bowman destroyed Shawnee villages 1779; devastated communities fought on with renewed determination.
