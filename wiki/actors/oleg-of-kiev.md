# Oleg of Kiev
## Biography / Description
**Oleg** (often called Oleg the Prophet or Oleg the Wise) was the first historical ruler of the Kievan state, reigning from approximately 882 CE until his death in 912 or 913 CE. According to the *Primary Chronicle*, Oleg was a Varangian commander and a relative of Rurik, who assumed the regency for Rurik’s young son, Igor. Oleg consolidated Scandinavian and Slavic control over the Dnieper River basin, laying the political foundations for [[kievan-rus|Kievan Rus′]].

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Unification of Kiev and Novgorod (882 CE):** Oleg led an army of Varangians, Slavs, and Finnic tribes south from Novgorod. He occupied Kiev, executed the local Varangian rulers Askold and Dir, and established Kiev as his capital, declaring it the "mother of Russian cities" (l. 727, 769).
* **Tribal Subjugation and Tribute:** Assisted by his retainers (*druzhina*), Oleg spread Kievan authority over neighboring East Slavic tribes, notably the Poliane, and began extracting regular tribute (*poliudie*). He faced bitter opposition from the Drevliane.
* **Campaign against Constantinople (907 CE):** Oleg launched a massive military expedition against the Byzantine Empire. According to Russian chronicles, he sailed to Constantinople and nailed his shield to the city gates, although Byzantine sources are silent on the campaign itself.
* **Russo-Byzantine Trade Treaty (911 CE):** Following his military success, Oleg concluded an exceptionally advantageous trade treaty with Byzantium, securing free merchant access and stipulating legal procedures for Russians in Constantinople.

## Historical Significance
Oleg's reign marks the transition of the Rus' from scattered, raiding bands into a organized, territorial state. By unifying Kiev and Novgorod, he secured the entire length of the vital trade route **"from the Varangians to the Greeks"** (l. 757). 

His treaty of 911 CE represented the first formal international recognition of the Kievan state and established highly developed commercial and legal norms. He was succeeded by Rurik's son, Igor.
