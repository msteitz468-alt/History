# João I of Kongo (Nzinga a Nkuwu)

**João I** (baptismal name; born Nzinga a Nkuwu, r. c. 1470–1509) was *manikongo* (king) of the [[kingdom-of-kongo]] at the time of first Portuguese contact (1483) and the first Kongo king to accept Christian baptism (1491). He is the father of [[afonso-i-kongo|Afonso I]], whose reign would be defined by the attempt to build a Christian Kongo state on the foundations his father laid.

## Portuguese Contact and Baptism

Portuguese navigators under Diogo Cão reached Kongo territory in **1483**, establishing the first direct contact between the kingdom and Europe. João I initially engaged diplomatically — sending Kongolese representatives to Portugal and receiving Portuguese missionaries. On **29 March 1491** he was baptized at [[mbanza-kongo]] as João I in a ceremony witnessed by Portuguese missionaries, taking his baptismal name from the reigning Portuguese king.

## Ambivalence and Death

João I's commitment to Christianity appears to have wavered. Missionary accounts suggest he returned to traditional practices toward the end of his life. On his death in 1509 the question of succession precipitated a crisis: his son Mvemba a Nzinga (the baptized Christian [[afonso-i-kongo|Afonso]]) defeated a pagan rival Mpanzu a Kitima to take the throne, establishing Christian rule on a firmer footing than his father had.
