# Robert H. Goddard

## Overview
Robert Hutchings Goddard was an American engineer, physicist, and inventor who is widely credited with creating and building the world's first liquid-fueled rocket, which he successfully launched in March 1926. While his work was initially met with scientific skepticism and limited funding, his collaboration with Charles Lindbergh secured the resources needed to establish his historic rocket testing facility in Roswell, New Mexico.

## Actions and Decisions
- **Lindbergh Collaboration (1929):** Hosted Charles Lindbergh, who visited his Worcester, Massachusetts lab after reading of his rocket tests, and convinced Lindbergh of the potential of liquid-fuel rocket propulsion.
- **Guggenheim Funding (1930):** Secured a $100,000 grant from the Daniel Guggenheim Fund through Lindbergh's direct advocacy, enabling him to relocate to Roswell, New Mexico, for large-scale flight tests.
- **Liquid-Fuel Testing in Roswell:** Conducted pioneering tests on gyroscopic control, multi-stage rockets, and fuel pumps, laying the physical foundation for modern spaceflight.
