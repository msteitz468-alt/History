# Jamuka (Jamuqa)

## Overview

**Jamuka** (Jamuqa) was the *anda* (sworn brother) and later principal steppe rival of [[genghis-khan|Temüjin]]. Weatherford stresses that “Jamuka was the only anda Temujin had in his life.” Of the Jadaran, he shared Temüjin’s youth, joined the Merkid rescue at Ong Khan’s request as Left (east) Wing, then sealed a third adult public *anda* ceremony—exchanged golden sashes and strong horses, slept under one blanket. For about a year and a half Temüjin followed him.

The split (mid-May 1181, after [[borte|Börte]]’s counsel) turned on kinship “bone” politics: Jamuka asserted higher white-bone herder status and ordered Temüjin to sheep/goats by the river while taking horses near the mountains. The night flight “evolved into two decades of warfare.” ~1190 Jamuka routed Temüjin after a cattle-raid killing pretext; the *Secret History* reports a captive leader’s head tied to a horse’s tail and seventy young captives boiled alive (Weatherford notes possible embellishment but retains the horror as driving sympathy to Temüjin). In 1201 (Year of the Cock) a *khuriltai* made Jamuka Gur-khan—title last held by Ong Khan’s uncle whom [[yesugei|Yesügei]] had helped overthrow—a public challenge to both [[togrul-ong-khan|Ong Khan]] and Temüjin.

## Role

- **Aristocratic foil.** Weatherford uses the rivalry to stage Temüjin’s rejection of inherited aristocratic privilege versus merit-and-loyalty following. After the Kereyid collapse Jamuka fled west to the Naiman; after Naiman defeat he disappeared into the forest as an outcast bandit. Weatherford’s thesis-framing: “In an odd reversal of fate, the once aristocratic Jamuka had been reduced to the same state of existence that the young Temujin had faced when his father died.”
- **End of the Jadaran opposition.** In 1205 (Year of the Ox), desperate followers seized him and delivered him to Temüjin. Temüjin valued loyalty above revenge: he executed the betrayers in front of Jamuka, then offered reunion—“Let us be companions….” Jamuka asked for aristocratic bloodless death and high burial to protect Temüjin’s seed. Legend: buried in the golden belt given at the *anda* oath. Weatherford flags that the *Secret History*’s lengthy confession “invite[s] suspicion regarding its accuracy.”
- **Sequence to 1206.** Weatherford’s milestone chain: Ong Khan fall 1203 → Naiman 1204 → Jamuka 1205 → *khuriltai*/title 1206 ([[mongol-unification-1206]]).

## Historiography

Almost entirely dependent on the *Secret History* (with later chronicle echoes). Vladimirtsov and others have read the split in class terms; Weatherford (2004) stresses personal and institutional styles and treats the death confession as literarily suspect. Dual capacity for friendship and enmity—formed before puberty with Jamuka and Börte—is presented as Temüjin’s defining trait.
