# Raphael Semmes

Commander of the CSS *Alabama* and the most famous Confederate commerce raider. Semmes turned the high seas into a major theater of the American Civil War and became, for much of the world, the living embodiment of the "piratical" character of the rebellion.

## Career and Cruises

A pre-war U.S. Navy officer, Semmes resigned to join the Confederacy. He commanded the CSS *Sumter*, then the *Alabama* (built in Britain). From 1862 to 1864 the *Alabama* captured or destroyed dozens of U.S. merchant vessels across the Atlantic, into the Indian Ocean, and around the Cape of Good Hope, touching ports on five continents (see Bonner's Appendix 1). Semmes burned prizes rather than attempting to bring them in, conducted himself with theatrical courtesy toward captured crews, and openly expressed anti-Black racism toward Black sailors and whalemen.

The *Alabama* was finally sunk by the USS *Kearsarge* off Cherbourg in June 1864. Semmes escaped, returned to the Confederacy, and ended the war as a rear admiral.

## International Significance (Bonner)

For anti-Confederate publicists the commerce raiders proved that a slaveholding republic would wage war on civilization's common highway. The destruction of unarmed merchant ships and the threat to the global insurance and shipping system turned "neutrality" into complicity for Britain and France. Semmes's cruises supplied the concrete evidence behind Sumner's "disturbers of the common highway, outlaws, and enemies of the human race" rhetoric. The *Alabama* claims after the war were a direct consequence.
