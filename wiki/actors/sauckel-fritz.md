# Fritz Sauckel (1894–1946)

**General Plenipotentiary for Labour Mobilization** from 21 March 1942 — the manager of Nazi Germany's slave-labor draft. Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: a plebeian Old Fighter (cabin boy, French POW 1914–19, lathe operator; Gauleiter of Thuringia 1927, where he Aryanized a major arms firm into his own Gustloff Foundation), allied to Bormann. His apparatus combined inducements with "coercion and terror" — armed recruitment commissions, hostage-taking of families, village reprisals, and "Operation Hay" (planned seizure of up to 50,000 children aged 10–14). He claimed 1.5 million+ new foreign workers by November 1942; nearly 8 million were in the Reich by autumn 1944 (see [[forced-labor-nazi-germany]]). His regulatory style paired minimal material improvement with terror ("hanged, shot!" for insubordination) justified instrumentally: "even a machine can only perform if I give it the fuel"; "flogged, half-starved and dead Russians do not mine coal for us." His running power-struggle with [[speer-albert|Speer]] over labor allocation exemplified the regime's polycracy. Hanged at Nuremberg, 16 October 1946.
