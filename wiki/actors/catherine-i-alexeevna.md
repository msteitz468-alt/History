# Empress Catherine I

## Biography / Description
**Empress Catherine I** (born Marta Skowrońska) was the second wife of Peter the Great and Empress of Russia from 1725 until her death in 1727 (L3032, L3096, L3122). A Lithuanian woman of low origin, she was captured by Russian forces during the Great Northern War, entered the household of Alexander Menshikov, and became Peter the Great's companion and wife (L3032). Peter married her officially in 1712 and crowned her as empress-consort in a special ceremony in 1724 (L3032, L3122). Following Peter the Great's death in 1725, she became the first woman to rule the Russian Empire.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Accession to the Throne (1725):** When Peter I died without designating an heir, the old nobility backed his grandson, young Peter (son of Alexis) (L3122). Catherine was supported by "the new men" of the Petrine reforms, led by Alexander Menshikov and Paul Iaguzhinsky (L3122). The issue was decided when the Preobrazhenskii and Semenovskii guard regiments staged a demonstration in her favor, securing her coronation (L3122).
* **Creation of the Supreme Secret Council (1726):** In February 1726, Catherine I created the Supreme Secret Council to deal with "matters of exceptional significance" (L3124). Consisting of six members led by Menshikov, the council became the de facto governing body of the state, departing from Peter the Great's collegiate administrative structure (L3124).
* **Succession Settlement:** Shortly before her death in May 1727, she named young Peter II as her successor and appointed the Supreme Secret Council to act as regent during his minority (L3124).

## Historical Significance
Catherine I's brief reign established the precedent of female rule in the Russian Empire and highlighted the political power of the guard regiments in resolving succession disputes (L3122). Her accession prevented a reaction by the old boyar nobility immediately following Peter the Great's death, securing the legacy of the Petrine reforms under the administration of the "new men" (L3122). Her creation of the Supreme Secret Council marked the beginning of an era of oligarchic court cliques that dominated the Russian government between 1725 and 1762 (L3098, L3124).
