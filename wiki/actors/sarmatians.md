# The Sarmatians
## Biography / Description
The **Sarmatians** were a confederation of nomadic, Iranian-speaking tribes originating from Central Asia who migrated westward and dominated the southern Russian steppe (the Pontic-Caspian steppe) from the late third century BCE to the early third century CE. According to Nicholas V. Riasanovsky, their social organization and culture were closely akin to the [[scythians|Scythians]] whom they defeated and replaced, although they introduced distinct military and social practices.

The Sarmatians were divided into several tribes, of which the **Alans** led in numbers and power. The modern Ossetians of the central Caucasus are their direct descendants.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Scythian Conquest (3rd century BCE):** The Sarmatians defeated the Scythians and took control of the steppe north of the Black Sea, establishing military dominance from the Danube to the Don.
* **Maintenance of Trade Routes:** Under Sarmatian rule, the major east-west trade route through the steppe remained open, facilitating commerce between Europe and Asia. The Greek geographer Strabo, writing in the first century CE, explicitly observed this economic continuity.
* **Adaptation to Regional Economy:** The Sarmatians successfully fit into the existing agricultural and commercial economy of southern Russia, maintaining peaceful, productive trade relations with the Greek Black Sea colonies (such as Olbia, Chersonesus, Panticapaeum, and Tanais) and avoiding their destruction in favor of mutual commercial benefit.

## Historical Significance
The Sarmatians represented the second major Iranian nomadic wave on the Pontic steppe, preserving the **Graeco-Iranian cultural synthesis** that characterized the pre-Slavic history of southern Russia. Rostovtzeff notes the "uninterrupted evolution of Iranian culture in South Russia through these centuries... which no German or Mongolian invaders were able to destroy" (quoted in Riasanovsky, l. 565-569).

Militarily, the Sarmatians contrasted with the light-cavalry Scythians by introducing heavy cavalry tactics, utilizing stirrups, heavy armor, lances, and long swords. Their political hegemony over the steppe was eventually shattered in the third century CE by the Goths, followed by the catastrophic invasion of the Huns around 370 CE.
