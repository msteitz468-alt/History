# Geoffrey Parker

**Geoffrey Parker** (b. 1943) is a British historian specializing in early modern European and world military history. He is best known for *The Military Revolution: Military Innovation and the Rise of the West, 1500–1800* (1988, based on the Lees Knowles Lectures), in which he revised and globalized Michael Roberts's 1955 thesis.

## Contribution to the Military Revolution Debate

Parker's key intervention reframed the "military revolution" (Roberts's 1560–1660 focus on tactics, army growth, and societal impact in Sweden and the Dutch Republic) around the **trace italienne** (angle-bastion fortification). The new artillery-resistant fortresses made quick victories rare and sieges the decisive form of war, requiring:

- Dramatically larger armies to besiege or garrison the new works.
- Vast increases in state fiscal and administrative capacity (the fiscal-military state).
- A naval dimension and the export of the system overseas.

He emphasized that the revolution was not only European but helped explain "the rise of the West" by giving Europeans a military edge in the Americas, Asia, and Africa between 1500 and 1800 — while also documenting the limits of that edge and parallel transformations in "gunpowder empires."

The book remains the central reference point for the debate; critics (notably Jeremy Black) argue the changes were incremental, late, and contingent, and that non-European military adaptations were equally significant.

## Historiography Note

Parker builds directly on Roberts while shifting weight from field tactics (volley fire, linear formations) to siege warfare and logistics as the primary drivers of army growth and state centralization. His global chapters test the thesis against the Ottoman, Mughal, Chinese, Japanese, and Southeast Asian cases and find the European advantage real but slower and more limited than often assumed before the later 18th century.
