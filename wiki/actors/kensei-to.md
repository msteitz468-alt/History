# Kenseito (Constitutional Government Party)

The **Kenseito** (憲政党, "Constitutional Government Party"), founded **22 June 1898** by the
merger of Japan's two main opposition parties — the **Jiyūtō** (Liberal Party, led by Itagaki
Taisuke) and the **Shimpōtō** (Progressive Party, led by Ōkuma Shigenobu) — formed the first
**party-based cabinet** in Japanese history. Its rapid collapse illustrated the structural
tensions between Diet parties and the genrō-dominated executive. From Donald Keene's
[[keene-emperor-of-japan-meiji-2002|*Emperor of Japan: Meiji and His World*]] (chapters 49–50).

## Founding and Context

By mid-1898, the Itō cabinet's tax bill had been defeated by a crushing margin (247 to 27),
and Itō had dissolved the Diet for the fifth time in less than ten years. The stalemate between
the imperial government and the elected House of Representatives had grown chronic. The merger
of the Jiyūtō and Shimpōtō was a response to this paralysis. At a gathering on **16 June**,
Ōkuma and Itagaki explained the urgent reasons for joining forces; both parties dissolved on
21 June and the Kenseito was formally constituted the following day.

The party's founding announcement stated: "It will soon be ten years since the promulgation of
the constitution and the opening of the Diet. During this period the Diet has been dissolved no
fewer than five times, and constitutional government has yet to bear fruit … the lingering evils
of the domain cliques are still frozen solidly." The new party's program demanded that **cabinets
be formed by a prime minister drawn from the strongest party** in the Diet, replacing the
prevailing practice by which the emperor selected prime ministers from Satsuma or Chōshū.

## The First Party Cabinet (1898)

Itō Hirobumi, concluding that the majority party could not be blocked, recommended Ōkuma and
Itagaki to [[emperor-meiji|Emperor Meiji]], who on **27 June** commanded them to form a cabinet.
The emperor was visibly dismayed: Matsukata later told friends he had never seen the emperor look
so sad. Ōkuma became both prime minister and foreign minister; Itagaki, home minister.

The cabinet lasted less than four months. Its collapse resulted not from opposition outside but
from irreconcilable factional conflict within:

- The **Ozaki Yukio affair**: The new Education Minister Ozaki, in an address on 22 August,
  made an offhand remark about the *possibility* of a republican Japan — immediately seized on
  by political enemies. The emperor, who had disliked Ozaki since 1887, commanded Ōkuma to
  dismiss him. Army Minister Katsura Tarō had deliberately amplified the scandal to destabilise
  the cabinet. Ozaki resigned; his replacement became another point of faction warfare.
- **Jiyū–Shimpō faction conflict**: Every cabinet appointment was contested between the two
  constituent factions. Itagaki pressed Ōkuma to surrender the Foreign Ministry; Ōkuma refused.
  On 29 October, Jiyūtō members voted to dissolve the existing Kenseito and re-form it excluding
  Shimpō members. The emperor sent Chamberlain Iwakura to Itagaki to ask him to remain, but
  could not resolve the impasse.

On 31 October 1898, Ōkuma resigned; his entire Shimpō-affiliated cabinet followed. Only the
non-party army and navy ministers remained. The genrō — led by Kuroda Kiyotaka, who had wanted
to end party government and had exploited the Ozaki affair accordingly — had prevailed.

## Significance

Keene presents the Kenseito experiment as revealing the structural contradiction of the Meiji
constitution: a *kenpō* modelled on Bismarck's Prussia created an elected legislature but kept
executive power in the hands of the genrō and service ministers, who could bring down any party
cabinet simply by withholding army and navy co-operation. The first party cabinet demonstrated
both the irresistible force of Diet majority politics and the fragility of inter-party coalition
in the face of genrō resistance and military veto.

After the Kenseito's dissolution, Itō Hirobumi — convinced that constitutional government
required a reliable party base — founded the **[[rikken-seiyukai-1900|Rikken Seiyūkai]]** in
September 1900, recruiting much of the old Kenseito membership.
