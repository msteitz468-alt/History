# Corinth (Archaic)

**Corinth** — astride the Isthmus, with a harbour on each gulf — was the great
commercial and naval power of Archaic Greece, pioneer of the warship and of the
exported fine pottery (Protocorinthian) that dominated Mediterranean markets, ruled
first by the **Bacchiad** oligarchy and then by the **Cypselid** tyranny.
[[boardman-cah-v3p3-1982|CAH Vol. III, Part 3]] (Ch. 42, N. G. L. Hammond) treats it.

## The Bacchiad commercial state

The closed, endogamous **Bacchiad** clan (some 200 households, intermarrying only among
themselves, electing one of their number *prytanis* each year) ruled Corinth and built
its power on its **geography** — the overland route between central Greece and the
Peloponnese, and the portage across the Isthmus between two seas. They seized the
**Megarid** (subjugating Megara c. 740, though it revolted under the Olympic victor
Orsippus), and projected Corinth's power outward by sea: trading to Ithaca, founding the
strong colonies of **[[greek-colonization|Corcyra]] and [[syracuse|Syracuse]] (c. 733)**,
and securing the straits at Rhium. Thucydides credited Corinth with **priority in
building warships** (the bronze-rammed **penteconter**; ships built even for Samos c.
704), the suppression of piracy, and revenue as "a centre of exchange." The Bacchiads
"provided suitable conditions for workshops" — the specialized potteries whose
**Protocorinthian** ware (the orientalizing leap from Geometric line to bold colour) was
exported across the west and influenced even Rhodes — while looking down least, of all
Greeks, on the aliens who did the actual trades.

## The Cypselid tyranny

The strains of this rapid commercialization split the Bacchiads, and in **657** —
soon after [[argos|Pheidon]]'s death (Pheidon had aided one faction) — **Cypselus**, a
Bacchiad by marriage and magistracy, seized sole power, founding the **Cypselid
[[greek-tyranny|tyranny]]** (c. 657–583). He and his son **Periander** (a reputed Sage,
later blackened with every crime) ran a powerful commercial-colonial state: they brought
**Corcyra** back into the fold, founded a ring of north-western colonies (**Epidamnus,
Leucas, Ambracia, Anactorium, Apollonia, Potidaea**) often under Cypselid sons as rulers,
drawing silver, timber, hides and trade-profits from the Adriatic and the route to the
west. They built lavishly (the diolkos ship-runway, the Fountain of Pirene's tunnels,
the Apollo temple), patronized poets (Arion, inventor of the dithyramb), styled
themselves *basileis*, and dedicated as "the Cypselidae" (the Chest of Cypselus, the gold
bowl from Heraclea). The tyranny fell c. **583**, the **Isthmian Games** founded in its
wake — Corinth thereafter an oligarchy and, after 582, an ally of [[sparta]].

## The restraining power in the League (CAH IV)

By the late sixth century Corinth was, after Sparta, **the most influential member of the
[[peloponnesian-league|Peloponnesian League]]** — "her authority in the League apparently
second only to that of its hegemon." [[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 6, Jeffery)
shows her shipping providing one of the **largest fleet contingents**, her grip on the
**Isthmus** controlling every League move northward, and her oligarchic government acting as
a **brake on Spartan adventurism**. Corinth was chosen to **arbitrate between Thebes and
Plataea** (519, ruling that any Boeotian city might leave the Boeotian League); twice she
**refused to follow [[cleomenes-i|Cleomenes]] against [[athens|Athens]]** (507/6), her
ambassador Socles famously opposing the Spartan plan to **restore Hippias** (c. 504); and
she sold Athens **twenty ships** for the "unheralded war" against [[aegina|Aegina]]. Her
consistent, unobtrusive favour to Athens reflected an oligarchy whose prosperity "depended
on the preservation of her key position" and so shunned extremes.

## Why Corinth mattered

- The **commercial-maritime engine** of the Greek mainland — first in warships, naval
  power and exported pottery (pre-eminent until Athens overtook it after 550), and a
  prolific colonizer that anchored the western and Adriatic trade routes.
- A textbook case of the **Archaic sequence** — Bacchiad oligarchy strained by new wealth
  into Cypselid [[greek-tyranny|tyranny]], then liberated into a Spartan-aligned oligarchy.
