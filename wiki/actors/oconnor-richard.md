# Richard O'Connor

British Army general (1889–1981), commander of the Western Desert Force during Operation Compass (December 1940 – February 1941), the first major British offensive of the North African campaign, in which his forces destroyed a much larger Italian army and advanced roughly 500 miles in ten weeks — one of the most lopsided operational successes of the early war. O'Connor was captured by German forces in April 1941 during the subsequent Axis counteroffensive under Rommel and spent over two years as a prisoner of war in Italy before escaping in 1943 following the Italian armistice.

Returning to active command, O'Connor led VIII Corps in Normandy in 1944, including during Operation Goodwood, the major British armored offensive south of Caen intended to draw German panzer reserves away from the American breakout sector and expand the Normandy bridgehead.

## Significance

O'Connor's Operation Compass victory remains a textbook case of operational-level exploitation against a numerically superior but poorly led opponent, while his later Normandy command under Montgomery illustrates the more attritional, set-piece character of the 1944 campaign compared to the war of maneuver he had commanded in the desert.
