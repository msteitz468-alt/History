# The Medes (Median Kingdom)

The **Medes** were the **Iranian-speaking people of the western Zagros and the
Iranian plateau** who, in coalition with the Chaldaean
[[nabopolassar|Nabopolassar]], **destroyed the [[neo-assyrian-empire|Neo-Assyrian
Empire]]** — sacking **Ashur (614)** and **[[fall-of-nineveh-612bce|Nineveh
(612)]]** — and became, for two generations, the dominant power of the Iranian
world before being absorbed by their Persian kinsmen.
[[boardman-cah-v3p2-1991|CAH Vol. III, Part 2]] (Chs. 22–25) traces them from the
margins of Assyrian campaign-records to the conquerors of Nineveh.

## From Zagros tribes to a kingdom

The Medes first appear in Assyrian records as a cluster of **Zagros tribes** raided
by [[tiglath-pileser-iii|Tiglath-pileser III]] (737) and Sargon II for horses and
tribute. Many Median chiefs became **sworn vassals** under [[esarhaddon|Esarhaddon]]
(the succession-oath tablets of 672 survive in Median copies), but the antagonist
**Kashtaritu** (often identified with Herodotus's **Phraortes**) attacked Assyrian
border-forts. Out of these fragmented chiefdoms a **kingdom** coalesced under
**Cyaxares (Umakishtar)** in the late seventh century — Herodotus credits him with
organizing the Median army by weapon-type — which "succeeded to Elamite power in
Iran" once [[ashurbanipal|Ashurbanipal's]] destruction of Elam (646) had removed
the older buffer.

## The destruction of Assyria

The decisive role. In **614** it was the **Medes, not the Babylonians**, who struck
the Assyrian heartland — capturing Tarbisu and **destroying Ashur**, the ancient
religious capital. On the battlefield Cyaxares made a **formal alliance with
[[nabopolassar|Nabopolassar]]** (reputedly sealed by the marriage of Nabopolassar's
son Nebuchadrezzar to a Median princess). In **612** the combined armies of
Cyaxares and Nabopolassar, with the **Ummanmanda** ("tribal hordes," including
[[scythians|Scythians]]), besieged and sacked **[[fall-of-nineveh-612bce|Nineveh]]**.
The Medes also extinguished **[[urartu|Urartu]]**, inheriting the highland north.

## The Median ascendancy and its fall

After 612 the Medes held the Iranian plateau, the Zagros, and the former Urartian
and Assyrian north (they garrisoned **Harran**; Nabonidus's mother lived under
Median control there). Median power reached deep into Anatolia, ending in the war
with **[[lydia|Lydia]]** halted by the "battle of the eclipse" (585) and the Halys
frontier. In **550** the Median king **Astyages** was overthrown by his vassal
**Cyrus of Anshan** — the rise of the [[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid Persian
Empire]], which absorbed Media and made it the senior partner-province of the new
order ("Medes and Persians").

## Liverani: was there a "Median empire"?

[[liverani-ancient-near-east-2014|Liverani (2014)]] is sceptical of the **"Median empire"** as
usually drawn. He rejects **Herodotus's** sequential dynasty (Deioces → Phraortes → Cyaxares),
which cannot be matched to contemporary Assyrian sources (Esarhaddon's list three *separate*
Median leaders) and reflects a Greek retrojection modelled on the Achaemenids. The Medes were
**politically fragmented** until **Cyaxares**, a charismatic anti-Assyrian leader heading an
unstable coalition rather than a true tribute-and-province empire — structurally closer to the
Mannaeans or Ellipi. Their rise was **driven by Assyrian pressure** (forcing tribal groups
into firmer polities), and he notes the paradox that the empire's collapse **damaged its own
periphery**: the central-Zagros cities that had prospered on Assyrian commerce reverted to a
tribal state. He treats Iranian ethnogenesis as gradual "Iranisation" (successive Indo-Iranian
waves, the first tied to the *maryannu* chariot), is cautious about Dumézil's tripartite
ideology ("largely debatable"), and places **Zoroaster** in the 7th century with Mazdaism
surfacing only in early Achaemenid sources. See [[ancient-near-east-historiography]].

## Significance

- `produced:` (with [[nabopolassar|Babylon]]) the fall of Assyria — the single most
  consequential act of the period for Near Eastern history.
- `enabled:` by [[ashurbanipal|Ashurbanipal's]] destruction of Elam, which removed
  the buffer shielding Assyria from the Iranian plateau.
- `produced:` the political framework — plateau kingdom, then "Medes and Persians" —
  that the [[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenids]] inherited and turned into the first
  world-empire.

## Historiography

The Medes are a **historiographical problem**: they left **no written records of
their own**, and almost everything is reconstructed from **Assyrian campaign-texts,
the Babylonian Chronicle (the Fall of Nineveh text), and Herodotus** — whose
schematic "Median Empire" of four kings (Deioces, Phraortes, Cyaxares, Astyages)
modern scholars treat with caution. Archaeology of the Median homeland (Hamadan,
Godin Tepe, Tepe Nush-i Jan) is thin, and how far a centralized "Median Empire"
ever existed beyond a tribal confederation remains debated.
