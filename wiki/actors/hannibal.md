# Hannibal Barca

The Carthaginian general who carried the [[second-punic-war-218-201bce|Second
Punic War]] into Italy and came nearer than anyone to breaking Roman power in the
peninsula. Eldest son of [[hamilcar-barca|Hamilcar Barca]]; treated in
[[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] Chs 2–3 (Scullard, Briscoe).

## Role and career

Hannibal grew up in his father's Spanish command (the boyhood **oath** at the
altar "never to be a friend of Rome," told later to Antiochus III). On the murder
of his brother-in-law Hasdrubal (221) the army acclaimed him commander, aged about
twenty-five, confirmed unanimously at Carthage. He completed the conquest of
central Spain to the Douro, then took **[[siege-of-saguntum-219bce|Saguntum]]**
(219), the occasion of war.

In 218 he marched from **[[new-carthage|New Carthage]]** across the Rhône and the
Alps into the Po valley, and won at the **Trebbia** (218), **Lake Trasimene**
(217), and overwhelmingly at **[[battle-of-cannae-216bce|Cannae]]** (216). Cannae
brought him the defection of much of southern Italy and Syracuse, but not the
Latins or central Italy. For the next decade he held the field undefeated in pitched
battle yet could not force a decision: Rome's [[fabian-strategy|attrition strategy]],
manpower reserves, and naval superiority slowly stripped away his Italian gains.
The destruction of his brother **Hasdrubal** at the **Metaurus** (207) sealed his
isolation in Bruttium. Recalled to Africa (203), he was defeated by
[[scipio-africanus|Scipio]] at **[[battle-of-zama-202bce|Zama]]** (202).

After the war he served as **suffete** and reformer at Carthage, then — pressed by
Roman and aristocratic enemies — fled east (195) to [[antiochus-iii-the-great|
Antiochus III]] and later to Bithynia, where he took poison (183/182) to avoid
surrender to Rome.

## Counterfactual significance

Hannibal is the strongest case in the wiki for a single actor's contingency. Without
his decision to invade Italy by land — and his tactical genius at Cannae — the war
that the Senate expected to fight in Spain would have been a different and lesser
conflict. Yet [[astin-cah-v8-1989|CAH VIII]] (Briscoe) stresses the **structural
limit** on his contingency: he could win battles but not detach the Roman alliance
system, whose cohesion was the decisive fact. His aim, Briscoe argues, was never to
destroy Rome but to impose a settlement recovering Sicily and Sardinia and checking
Roman expansion — a goal the treaty with [[philip-v]] (215) presupposed Rome would
survive to accept.

## Historiography

The pro-Barcid strand (Silenus, Sosylus, used by Polybius) and the anti-Barcid
strand (Fabius Pictor, blaming Hasdrubal's and Hannibal's ambition) shape every
source. [[polybius]] is generally favourable and analytical; [[livy]] dramatizes
and transmits the "cruelty of Hannibal" topos that Walbank shows to be largely
rhetorical. The **Alpine route** remains unresolved. See
[[causes-of-second-punic-war]].
