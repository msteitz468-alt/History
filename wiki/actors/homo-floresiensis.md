## Overview

*Homo floresiensis* — nicknamed "the Hobbit" — is a diminutive hominin
species known from the Liang Bua limestone cave on the island of Flores,
Indonesia. The type specimen, **LB1**, was discovered in 2003: an adult
standing roughly 1 meter tall with a brain volume of only about 380 cc — far
smaller than any other known member of the genus *Homo*, comparable in scale
to early australopithecines despite living far more recently. Its precise
dating, ancestry, and taxonomic status remain among the most debated
questions in human paleontology.

## Dating and Physical Characteristics

- LB1 and associated Liang Bua remains are dated to roughly **100,000–50,000
  BP**, making *H. floresiensis* a contemporary of both late *Homo erectus*
  populations in the region and early modern human dispersals through
  Southeast Asia.
- Extreme insular dwarfism (small body size, brain volume ~380 cc) is the
  leading explanation for its diminutive proportions — a pattern documented
  in other island mammal populations subject to restricted resources and the
  absence of large predators/competitors.
- Stone tools recovered at **Mata Menge**, also on Flores, date to
  ~800,000 BP — far older than the Liang Bua skeletal remains — implying
  hominins had already reached and colonized Flores, and by extension made a
  deliberate or accidental **open-water sea crossing**, roughly 800,000 years
  ago. This pushes back the date of the earliest known hominin seafaring
  significantly.

## Ancestry (Unresolved)

The species' ancestry is genuinely unresolved. Competing hypotheses include:
descent from a dwarfed local population of [[homo-erectus|*Homo erectus*]]
that reached Flores and shrank under island conditions over hundreds of
thousands of years; or descent from a more archaic, small-brained
pre-*erectus* lineage that reached Southeast Asia even earlier and has left
no other known trace. No ancient DNA has yet been successfully recovered
from Flores specimens (tropical conditions degrade DNA rapidly), so the
question is argued from morphology and stone-tool sequences alone.

## Significance (Counterfactual)

*Homo floresiensis* demonstrates that the genus *Homo* was more anatomically
and behaviorally diverse, and survived in isolated pockets far later, than
assumed before its 2003 discovery — coexisting with modern humans in the
region during the very period of the "Out of Africa 2" dispersal (see
[[../events/out-of-africa-2-60kbp|Out of Africa 2]]). The Mata Menge tools
also force reconsideration of how early hominins acquired the cognitive and
technological capacity for water crossings, a capability once assumed to
belong only to fully modern humans.

## Historiography

The 2003 discovery was immediately controversial: some researchers initially
argued LB1 was a modern human with a pathological condition (e.g.,
microcephaly) rather than a distinct species, though this view is now a
minority position. Its precise phylogenetic placement, exact extinction
date, and relationship to regional *Homo erectus* populations remain open
questions. Source: assigned background facts on *H. floresiensis* (Liang
Bua, LB1, Mata Menge); cross-reference with Fagan & Durrani (2020),
*World Prehistory: A Brief Introduction*, 10th ed., for the broader Southeast
Asian dispersal context.
