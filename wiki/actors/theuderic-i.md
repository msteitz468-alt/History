# Theuderic I

**Theuderic I** (c. 485–533) was the eldest son of **[[clovis]]**, born of an earlier
union before Clovis's marriage to [[clotild|Clotild]]. On Clovis's death in 511 he and
his three younger half-brothers — [[chlodomer|Chlodomer]], [[childebert-i|Childebert I]]
and [[chlothar-i|Chlothar I]] — "inherited his kingdom and divided it equally," Theuderic
taking the eastern, Rhineland portion centred on Reims and Metz (the nucleus of later
[[austrasia|Austrasia]]). He is the senior figure of the first post-Clovis generation in
**[[gregory-of-tours-history-of-the-franks-thorpe-1974|Gregory of Tours]]**.

## Career

Theuderic was already a grown man with a capable son, **Theudebert**, when his father
died. He defeated a Danish sea-raid (the earliest notice of a Scandinavian attack on
Frankish territory) by sending Theudebert against it: "The Danish King was killed, the
enemy fleet was beaten in a naval battle." His main wars were against **Thuringia** — he
first allied with King Hermanfrid against Hermanfrid's brother Baderic, then turned on
Hermanfrid himself, who died falling (or pushed) from the walls of Zülpich; Gregory, in a
characteristic admission of uncertainty, notes "who pushed him we shall never know."

When the men of **Clermont** in the Auvergne were rumoured ready to defect, Theuderic
"ravaged and destroyed the entire region." Gregory judges him a master of treachery — he
plotted to ambush and kill his brother Chlothar — concluding flatly that "Theuderic was
very good at this sort of trick." He died in the twenty-third year of his reign (533),
succeeded by Theudebert I.

## Counterfactual Significance

Theuderic established the eastern Frankish kingdom that would crystallise as Austrasia and
the dynastic heartland of [[brunhild|Brunhild]], [[childebert-ii|Childebert II]], and
ultimately the Pippinids. His line (through Theudebert and Theudebald) held the east until
its extinction in 555 allowed [[chlothar-i|Chlothar I]] to begin reuniting the realm.
