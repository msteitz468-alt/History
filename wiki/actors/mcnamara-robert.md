# Robert S. McNamara (1918–2009)

U.S. Secretary of Defense (1961–1968) under Presidents Kennedy and Johnson. Architect of much of the civilian-side management of the Vietnam War and a key figure in the application (and later disillusionment with) systems analysis and graduated response.

## Vietnam Role

McNamara oversaw the transition from advisory assistance to large-scale U.S. combat involvement. He initially supported the deployment of combat troops and the bombing campaign, while pushing for quantitative metrics (body counts, sortie rates, hamlet evaluation scores) to manage the war as a problem of resource allocation and cost-benefit calculation.

Over time he grew skeptical of the military's attrition strategy. By 1966–67 he was advocating caps on troop levels, greater emphasis on pacification, and limits on the air war. His internal doubts contributed to his departure from the Pentagon in early 1968.

In Krepinevich's account, McNamara and OSD Systems Analysis repeatedly clashed with the Army over whether the "Army Concept" approach was producing results, highlighting the gap between official progress reports and the lack of measurable control over the countryside.

**Ch. 6 specifics:** After July 1965 visit endorsed 44-battalion request and "The strategy for winning this stage of the war will be to take the offensive—to take and hold the initiative." "The number of US troops is too small to make a significant difference in the traditional 10-1 government-guerrilla formula, but it is not too small to make a significant difference in the kind of war which seems to be evolving in Vietnam—a 'Third Stage' or conventional war." By Oct 1966: "no reasonable way to bring the war to an end soon [enemy strategy of] attriting our national will." Pacification "a bad disappointment" that had "gone backward"; "the large-unit operations war... is largely irrelevant to pacification as long as we do not lose it." Recommended 470k cap and DMZ barrier. OSD Systems Analysis (Enthoven) used MACV's own data (ARCOV, after-action reports) to show 88% engagements enemy-initiated, declining battalion attacks, 90% incidents in 10% of country holding 80% population; recommended rejecting further troop requests and upgrading RVNAF. McNamara's DPMs and Program 4/5 decisions reflected these doubts despite JCS resistance.
