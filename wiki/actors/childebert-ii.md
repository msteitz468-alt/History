# Childebert II

**Childebert II** (570–595) was the son of **[[sigibert-i|Sigibert I]]** and
**[[brunhild|Brunhild]]** who, "barely five years old," was proclaimed king of
**[[austrasia|Austrasia]]** when his father was assassinated in 575. Through his uncle
**[[guntram|Guntram]]**'s adoption and the **Treaty of Andelot (587)** he became heir to
Burgundy as well, reuniting much of the Frankish east and centre under one line. He is a
central figure of **[[gregory-of-tours-history-of-the-franks-thorpe-1974|Gregory's]]** later
books, though always seen through his mother's regency and his uncle's tutelage.

## Career

Childebert "began to reign on Christmas Day" 575, snatched to safety as an infant by Duke
Gundovald (a namesake of the later pretender) and acclaimed in Austrasia. His minority was
dominated by **Brunhild** and contested by great nobles — Ursio rebuffed the queen with
"Stand back, woman!… his kingdom is under our control, not yours" — and by shifting
alliances: he broke with Guntram to ally with Chilperic, who named him sole heir, then
reconciled with Guntram. The emperor **Maurice** paid him 50,000 gold pieces to expel the
**Lombards** from Italy; Childebert took the money, made peace with the Lombards, and
ignored Maurice's demand for repayment, later sending armies into Italy that "achieved
nothing."

At **Andelot in 587** Guntram adopted him formally as heir, handing him his spear — "This
is a sign that I have handed the whole of my realm over to you" — and assigning him cities
including Meaux, Tours, Poitiers, and Albi. Childebert avenged his mother's enemies (ordering
the rebel Guntram Boso killed, crushing Ursio and Berthefried) and executed the conspirator
**Rauching**, who had plotted his assassination. He repeatedly survived assassins sent by
**[[fredegund|Fredegund]]** and presided over the treason trial of **Bishop Egidius of
Reims**. He died in 595, leaving his sons Theudebert II and Theuderic II.

## Counterfactual Significance

Childebert II's survival of a perilous minority, and the Andelot pact that made him
Guntram's heir, consolidated the Austrasian–Burgundian bloc against Neustria — the dynastic
configuration his mother Brunhild would defend for another two decades until her downfall.
His early death and the division of his realm between two young sons, however, reopened the
fratricidal pattern. See [[merovingian-franks]].
