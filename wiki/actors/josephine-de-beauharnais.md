# Josephine de Beauharnais

**Joséphine de Beauharnais** (1763–1814), born Marie Josèphe Rose Tascher de La Pagerie on Martinique, was the first wife of [[napoleon|Napoleon Bonaparte]] and Empress of the French (1804–1809). Her complex relationship with Napoleon — intense early passion on his side, her initial infidelity, later mutual affection, and eventual dynastic divorce — is central to understanding Napoleon’s personal life and dynastic calculations.

## Overview

Widow of Alexandre de Beauharnais (guillotined in the Terror), mother of Eugène and Hortense, Joséphine moved in Directory social circles. Napoleon met her in 1795 and married in 1796 shortly before departing for the Italian campaign. While he was in Egypt she had an affair with Hippolyte Charles; the revelation devastated Napoleon and contributed to his taking a mistress in Cairo. Upon his return they reconciled and enjoyed a decade of relative harmony despite his later infidelities. She was crowned Empress in 1804 at Notre-Dame.

## Key Developments

- **Marriage and early Empire.** Josephine provided social polish and connections; she accompanied Napoleon on campaigns when possible and acted as a moderating influence. Her children were integrated into the imperial family (Eugène commanded in Italy; Hortense married Louis Bonaparte).
- **Dynastic divorce (1809).** Despite deep affection, Napoleon divorced Josephine in 1809 to marry Marie Louise of Austria and secure a legitimate heir. The divorce was painful for both; they remained on friendly terms afterward. Josephine retired to Malmaison, where she died in 1814 shortly before Napoleon’s first abdication.
- **Legacy.** Through her children she remained connected to the Bonapartist story (Eugène’s descendants; Hortense’s son became Napoleon III). Napoleon’s letters reveal both the depth of his attachment and the political calculations that ended the marriage.
