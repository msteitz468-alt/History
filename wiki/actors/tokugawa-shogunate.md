# Tokugawa Shogunate

The Tokugawa shogunate (*Tokugawa bakufu*, or Edo bakufu) was the last of Japan's three warrior
governments — the feudal military regime founded by [[tokugawa-ieyasu|Tokugawa Ieyasu]] after his victory
at [[battle-of-sekigahara-1600|Sekigahara]] (1600) and his appointment as shōgun in **1603**. It ruled
Japan from Edo (modern Tokyo) for over 250 years, until the [[meiji-restoration-1868|Meiji Restoration]] of 1868.

Its power rested on the [[bakuhan-system|bakuhan system]] — direct rule of the shogunal domain plus
oversight of some 250 semi-autonomous *daimyō* domains (*han*) — disciplined by institutions such as
[[sankin-kotai|alternate attendance]] (*sankin-kōtai*) and the [[buke-shohatto|Laws for the Military
Houses]]. The regime imposed a rigid status order, suppressed Christianity, and from the 1630s closed the
country to most foreign contact (*sakoku*), inaugurating the long internal peace of the
[[edo-period|Edo period]].

## Foreign Relations: Inventing the "Pseudo-Subject" (Clulow & Mostert)

[[clulow-mostert-dutch-english-east-india-companies-2018|Clulow & Mostert (2018)]]
show the bakufu as an active, improvising shaper of East Asian diplomacy rather than a
passive isolationist. Fuyuko Matsukata argues it **invented a category of foreign
"pseudo-subjects"** — Dutch and Chinese merchants — to accept commercial relations
*without* state-level correspondence with foreign sovereigns. It distinguished
**Tsūshin-no-Kuni** (countries of diplomatic correspondence: Korea, Ryukyu) from
**Tsūshō-no-Kuni** (countries of commercial relations: China, Netherlands). The 1627
Nuyts embassy was refused because recognising the VOC governor-general as an envoy
would have implied he was an independent ruler; thereafter the [[dutch-east-india-company|VOC]]
stopped sending embassies and operated through merchants at Hirado, later Deshima,
reporting foreign news (*fūsetsugaki*) and providing intelligence rather than
diplomacy. The [[english-east-india-company|EIC]]'s 1673 *Return* mission failed the
same protocol test.

Adam Clulow's chapter adds the bakufu's tight control of subjects abroad: it issued
356 *shuinjō* (vermilion-seal passes) after 1604 but felt "no interest in either
regulating or protecting" Japanese once overseas — and in a **1621 edict** banned the
export of Japanese soldiers and weapons, ending the VOC's experiment with Japanese
mercenaries (see [[banda-conquest-1621]]).
