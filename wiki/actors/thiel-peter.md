## Role and Background

Peter Thiel co-founded the online payments company Confinity, which merged with [[actors/musk-elon|Elon Musk's]] X.com in 2000 to form PayPal, becoming its CEO; Thiel led the "coup" that removed Musk from PayPal's leadership shortly after the merger. He went on to co-found the data-analytics firm Palantir Technologies and to become the first outside investor in Facebook, later founding the venture capital firm Founders Fund.

## Later Influence

Beyond his business career, Thiel became an influential and unconventional figure in American conservative and libertarian politics, articulating skepticism toward mainstream tech-industry consensus in his book *Zero to One* (2014) and providing significant financial backing to a number of Republican political candidates, including J.D. Vance's Senate campaign.

## Significance

Thiel's trajectory from Silicon Valley co-founder to major venture financier and political donor exemplifies the growing overlap between technology-sector wealth and American political influence in the early twenty-first century.
