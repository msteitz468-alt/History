# Huron-Wendat

Northern Iroquoian confederacy of Georgian Bay / Lake Simcoe country (Wendat; colonial “Huron”). Linchpin of New France’s seventeenth-century fur economy until political destruction in the [[beaver-wars-17th-century]] (Salisbury, CHNPA I.1 ch. 7; Trigger). Descendants endure (e.g. Wendake/Lorette; diaspora communities).

## Overview

### French alliance and trade system

In 1609–10 Hurons with Montagnais and Algonquins routed [[mohawk]] with Champlain’s support. 1615 French and Hurons sealed alliance and attacked an Oneida or Onondaga village. Thereafter Hurons were “the linchpin of New France’s economy,” supplying beaver from Nipissings, Neutrals, Petuns, and Ottawas (Odawas) for metal, glass, cloth, and protection from Iroquois aggression. Having exhausted own beaver, they depended on northern thick pelts and intensified corn-for-meat exchanges inland.

### Epidemics and Jesuit condition

After English occupation of Canada (1629–32), French insisted Hurons accept Jesuits as price of reviving the central alliance. Epidemics culminating in massive smallpox 1639–40 cut population of ~21,000 in half. Many focused on Jesuit witchcraft but dependence on French for trade/protection restrained expulsion; trade volume stayed similar despite collapse → survivors more trade-dependent. From 1643 Christian Huron traders got special French treatment (higher prices, gun permission); over five years >50% of traders converted but <15% of total population Christian—deep divisions in consensus-oriented society.

### Destruction 1648–49 and diaspora

Iroquois full-scale war destroyed Hurons as a political entity winter 1648–9; conversions “part of the collective resignation.” Some stayed with Jesuits (Lorette near Quebec); many joined Hurons among the Iroquois (adoptees); others fled to Petun, Neutral, Erie—who were themselves soon dispersed. Huron Feast of the Dead rituals later spread among Great Lakes refugee villages (1660s–70s) facilitating multi-ethnic unity and French trade. Huron-Petuns/Wyandots appear again in eighteenth-century Detroit/Ohio politics (anti-French turn 1747; later alignments).

## Historiography

Trigger *The Children of Aataentsic* and *Natives and Newcomers* are foundational; Heidenreich on Huronia geography; Delage, Eccles, Jaenen, Dickason on New France–Huron relations. Jesuit *Relations* remain indispensable and biased primary sources. Population half-loss 1639–40 and 1648–49 political destruction are consensus facts; motives (beaver vs captives vs geopolitics) remain multi-causal.
