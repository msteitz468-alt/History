# James I of England (James VI of Scotland)

King of Scotland (as James VI, r. 1567–1625) and England (r. 1603–1625). Upon Elizabeth's death the Lord Chamberlain's Men received a royal patent and became the King's Men. James was a more consistent patron of the theatre than Elizabeth had been; court performances increased. He was interested in witchcraft (*Daemonologie*), divine-right kingship, and the union of the crowns. *Macbeth* (c. 1606) engages directly with Jacobean concerns about treason, equivocation, and Scottish kingship.

James's reign saw the company at the height of its institutional security and the writing of Shakespeare's late tragedies and romances. The king attended fewer public plays than masques, but the royal name conferred prestige and protection.
