# The Latin Empire of Constantinople and Frankish Greece

The **Latin empire of Constantinople** was the patchwork of crusader states created by the
**[[fourth-crusade-1204|Fourth Crusade]]** out of the conquered Byzantine lands ("Romania"
in western usage). [[abulafia-ncmh-v5-2000|NCMH V]] (David Jacoby, Ch. 16a) treats it as a
fragmentary, "tumultuous" feudal mosaic that "hastened the disintegration" of Byzantium —
and as the matrix of Venice's colonial empire and of a durable Frankish presence in Greece.

## A Feudal Mosaic

The March 1204 partition treaty between the "French" crusaders and **Doge Enrico Dandolo**
gave **Baldwin IX of Flanders** the imperial crown and a quarter of the empire, **Venice**
three-eighths, and the rest to the Frankish barons. Conquest was never completed, so Romania
became "a mosaic of political entities... within a complex and shifting web of vassal
relations":

- the **kingdom of Thessalonica** (Boniface of Montferrat);
- the **duchy of Athens** (Othon de La Roche);
- the **principality of Morea/Achaea** (the Villehardouins) in the Peloponnese — the most
  durable Frankish state, governed by the *Assizes of Romania*;
- the **duchy of the Archipelago** (Marco Sanudo) in the Cyclades;
- and Venice's island bases (Crete, Modon, Coron, Euboea/Negroponte).

Its government, defined in 1204, was "moulded along western feudal patterns," yet borrowed
Byzantine titles, ceremony, coinage and the fiscal system (via Greek officials). **Venice's**
position combined subordination and near-parity, with its *podestà* in Constantinople and a
Venetian patriarch.

## A Doomed Empire

The Latin empire "led a tumultuous life" for fifty-seven years, beset by the
**[[empire-of-nicaea|empire of Nicaea]]**, the despotate of **Epiros**, and the
**Vlacho-Bulgarian** kingdom. Disaster came at once: at **Adrianople (1205)** the Bulgarians
crushed the Latins and captured Emperor Baldwin. The empire survived only through "temporary
agreements and shifting alliances" and the rivalry of its neighbours, never acquiring
adequate resources or sustained western help. The hard-pressed **Baldwin II** pawned the
**Crown of Thorns** (redeemed by [[louis-ix-capetian|Louis IX]] for the Sainte-Chapelle) and
even his own son, and stripped lead from the palace roofs. In 1261 **Michael VIII Palaiologos**
of Nicaea retook Constantinople (see [[empire-of-nicaea]]), ending the empire — though Frankish
Morea, Athens and the Aegean lordships persisted, passing under the suzerainty of
[[charles-of-anjou|Charles of Anjou]] (Treaty of Viterbo, 1267).

## Significance

Jacoby stresses that the Latin conquest produced a "break on the political level" but
"accommodation and continuity in other spheres": a debased Greek peasantry (*paroikoi*
turned legally unfree villeins), an absorbed Greek elite (the *archontes*), and a vibrant
**Greek Church** that, looking to Nicaea, became "the focus and promoter of Greek ethnic
awareness." Economically the conquest **opened the Black Sea to western commerce** and bound
Romania into a triangular trade with Italy and the Levant dominated by [[republic-of-venice|Venice]]
and [[republic-of-genoa|Genoese]] and Florentine bankers — a lasting reorientation of the
Greek lands toward the Latin west.
