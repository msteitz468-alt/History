# Aimery of Lusignan

**Aimery (Amaury) of Lusignan**, brother of [[guy-of-lusignan|Guy]], first **crowned king**
of [[kingdom-of-cyprus|Cyprus]] and later king-consort of Jerusalem. He established the
Latin ecclesiastical hierarchy and the constitutional framework of the Lusignan monarchy.

## Path to the East

First of the brothers to leave Poitou (soon after the 1168 rebellion against Henry II); by
1174 a vassal of Baldwin IV (mid-thirteenth-century tradition: ransomed from Damascus by
King Amaury of Jerusalem). Constable of Jerusalem from c. 1180. He brought Guy east in
1180 and supported him through Acre (1189–91) and the Cyprus settlement.

## Crown and Latin Church (1195–97)

Within three years of Guy's death (~end 1194), Aimery secured (1) a Latin hierarchy and
(2) elevation of Cyprus to a kingdom. Celestine III's December 1196 bull created an
**archbishop of Nicosia** with suffragans at Paphos, Limassol and Famagusta — a structure
lasting until the Ottoman conquest. The first archbishop was Aimery's chancellor; Latin
clergy were seen as prerequisite for a coronation.

The royal title came from **Emperor Henry VI**: Aimery sent Renier of Jubail as ambassador;
Henry took homage by proxy and sent regalia (arrived April/May 1196). Coronation was
postponed hoping for the emperor's presence; instead imperial chancellor **Conrad of
Hildesheim** crowned Aimery in 1197. Motives (Edbury): prestige and hereditary security;
fear of Byzantine attack; an ally hostile to Constantinople; end of any idea that Cyprus
was a Jerusalem dependency; guarantee that Henry would not overturn a regime founded under
his enemy Richard.

## Dual monarchy (1197–1205)

In 1197 Aimery was reconciled with Henry of Champagne (marriage pact of sons and daughters;
remission of the remaining Cyprus purchase debt). After Henry's death (10 September 1197,
fall from a window), Isabella remarried Aimery mid-October; he became king-consort of
Jerusalem as well as king of Cyprus. The two realms remained linked only by the person of
the monarch — separate High Courts and chanceries. He used Cypriot forces at Beirut (1197)
and in a naval raid on Egypt (1204) but did not systematically plant Jerusalemites in
Cypriot fiefs. Truces with the Muslims were renewed 1198 and 1204. He forced Ralph of
Tiberias into exile after an alleged assassination plot; later lawyer-barons esteemed both
men for legal ability.

Aimery died **1 April 1205**; Isabella shortly after. Jerusalem passed to Maria of
Montferrat; Cyprus to Aimery's son **Hugh I** (by Eschiva of Ibelin), aged about nine —
opening the first royal minority.

## Counterfactual significance

Without Aimery's crown and Latin hierarchy, Cyprus might have remained a mere lordship under
Jerusalemite or English shadow, vulnerable to Byzantine recovery rhetoric. The 1196–97
settlement locked the island into the western imperial and papal systems and created the
institutional template the Lusignans kept for three centuries.
