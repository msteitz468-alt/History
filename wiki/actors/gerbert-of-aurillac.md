# Gerbert of Aurillac (Pope Sylvester II)

**Gerbert of Aurillac** (c. 945/946–1003) was the outstanding intellectual of the long tenth century and, as **Pope Sylvester II** (999–1003), the first French pope. [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]] (Leonardi, "Intellectual life") makes him, with **Abbo of Fleury**, the exemplary figure of the period's learning, and Nelson notes his influence as tutor and counsellor on the imperial programme of [[otto-iii|Otto III]].

## Life and Learning

Of humble Auvergnat origin, Gerbert was educated at the monastery of Aurillac and then in the **Spanish March** (Catalonia), where — at the edge of al-Andalus — he absorbed mathematics and astronomy beyond the standard Latin curriculum. He became master of the **cathedral school of Rheims**, the leading school north of the Alps, where he revitalised the **quadrivium** (arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music), reintroducing the **abacus**, the **armillary sphere**, and arguably knowledge of the **astrolabe** — sciences associated, in part, with Arabic learning transmitted through Iberia. Leonardi sets Gerbert alongside Abbo of Fleury as the men who "slowly added the quadrivium" to the trivium and joined the study of the sciences to the study of literature. His surviving **letter-collection** is a major source for the politics and intellectual networks of the age (it includes his anxious search for manuscripts "everywhere," noting the scribes "in the towns and in the countryside" of Italy).

## The New Kind of Scholar

Gerbert exemplifies what Leonardi identifies as the tenth century's novel **dissociation of the intellectual from the school**: a man of learning whose culture was a personal achievement pursued through his own library and study, not merely the institutional product of a *schola*. In this he stands with **Rather of Verona** and **Liudprand of Cremona** as a forerunner of the "modern intellectual."

## Politics: Rheims, Otto III, and the Papacy

Gerbert was deeply enmeshed in high politics. At **Rheims** he was caught in the disputed archiepiscopal succession (his rival Arnulf), bound up with the [[capetian-dynasty|Capetian]] accession of **Hugh Capet** in 987 (Gerbert drafted for the Rheims circle). He became tutor and adviser to **Otto III**, helping shape the emperor's eschatologically tinged **Renovatio Imperii Romanorum** — Nelson notes Otto, "strongly influenced by such learned tutors as Gerbert of Rheims," may have seen himself "summoning a new world of kings into existence." Otto made him **archbishop of Ravenna** (998) and then **pope as Sylvester II** (999), the name itself programmatic: as Sylvester I had stood beside Constantine, Sylvester II would stand beside a renewed Christian emperor. As pope he confirmed the new Christian kingships of the east — the royal title and church of **Stephen I of Hungary** and the metropolitan see of **Gniezno** for **Bolesław Chrobry** of Poland (1000) belong to the Otto III–Sylvester II axis.

## Significance

Gerbert links the strands of the long tenth century: the transmission of Arabic-mediated science into the Latin West, the revival of the quadrivium that fed the eleventh- and twelfth-century schools, and the imperial-papal ideology of the **[[ottonian-renaissance]]** and the *Renovatio*. His later, legendary reputation as a magician (a necromancer who owed his learning to the Saracens) is itself testimony to how exceptional his scientific knowledge appeared.
