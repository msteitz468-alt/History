# Empress Elizabeth

## Biography / Description
**Empress Elizabeth** (Elizaveta Petrovna) was the daughter of Peter the Great and Catherine I, who ruled as Empress of Russia from 1741 until her death in 1762 (L3122, L3198, L3218). In late 1741, she led the guard regiments in a palace revolution that deposed the infant Ivan VI and Anna Leopoldovna, ending the period of "foreign" German dominance (L3198). Elizabeth's reign is associated with a cultural revival, the expansion of gentry privileges, the introduction of French fashions and ideas, and the foundation of the first Russian university (L3216, L3646-3648, L3676).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Restoration of Petrine Institutions:** Elizabeth abolished the cabinet and restored the Governing Senate to its former authority, emphasizing a return to the administrative style of Peter the Great (L3204).
* **Abolition of Capital Punishment:** In a significant departure from contemporary European and Russian practice, Elizabeth abolished capital punishment in the empire (L3204-3206).
* **Cultural Expansion:** Supported by her favorites Ivan Shuvalov and Mikhail Lomonosov, she sponsored the creation of the University of Moscow in 1755 (L3676-3678). Her reign also witnessed the establishment of a native Russian theater under Fyodor Volkov in the 1750s, and the building of the Winter Palace (L3216, L3776).
* **Gentry Privileges and Serfdom:** Her government banned all but the gentry from owning serfs (1746), established the Gentry Bank to provide cheap credit, and allowed landlords to exile delinquent serfs to Siberia (1760), which further solidified the peasants' status as property (L3234-3236, L3244-3246).
* **Foreign Policy and Wars:** Guided by Chancellor Bestuzhev-Riumin, Russia fought Sweden in the War of 1741–1743 (Treaty of Abo), participated in the War of the Austrian Succession, and joined the Seven Years' War (1756–1763) against Frederick the Great of Prussia (L3258-3260, L3266-3270). Russian troops won major battles (Zorndorf, Kunersdorf) and briefly held Berlin in 1760, pushing Prussia to the brink of collapse before Elizabeth's death in January 1762 (L3270).

## Historical Significance
Elizabeth's reign brought internal stability to Russia after the turbulent successions of 1725–1740 (L3098). Her rule fostered the French cultural orientation of the Russian elite, shifting the focus of Westernization from technology to arts, fashions, and literature (L3216, L3646-3648). 

However, her personal extravagance drained the treasury, and her government's financial policies contributed to inflation (L3216). Under her administration, serfdom expanded in depth and severity, creating the social tensions that eventually exploded in the Pugachev Rebellion (L3216, L3244-3246).
