# Puzur-Inshushinak (Kutik-Inshushinak)

The last and greatest king of the **dynasty of Awan**, who rose from **governor (*ensí*)
of [[susa|Susa]]** to "**mighty king of Awan**" and "king of the four quarters,"
projecting [[elam|Elamite]] power deep into Babylonia at the end of the
[[akkadian-empire|Akkadian]] order (~2100 BCE). Securely dated by a synchronism: an Old
Babylonian copy of an inscription of **[[ur-nammu|Ur-Namma]]** of Ur names him as an
adversary — fixing him a full **900 years after** the [[proto-elamite|Proto-Elamite]]
script and ending the older view that made him a contemporary of [[naram-sin|Naram-Sin]].
[[potts-archaeology-of-elam-2016|Potts]] treats him as the first ruler to **deliberately
integrate highland and lowland** under one Elamite crown.

## Career and significance

His twelve preserved inscriptions show a **rising titulary**: from *ensí* of Susa and
military governor of the land of Elam, to *dannum* ("the mighty") and *lugal* ("king") of
Awan. In one text he claims to have **conquered eighty-one regions**; the Ur-Namma
material credits "Anshan" with subjugating Mesopotamian cities (Umma, Marad, Kazallu) and
places Awal, Kismar, Eshnunna, Tutub and Agade under his control — a brief Elamite
hegemony over the Diyala and central Tigris. He built extensively for the god
**Inshushinak** on the Susa acropolis (a canal, a statue, a copper-clad cedar gate-bolt
with daily sacrifices).

His monuments carry inscriptions in **both Akkadian and the indigenous Linear Elamite
script** — a gesture of national assertion. Linear Elamite (~30 known examples, mostly
from Susa) appears in his reign and **dies out with his defeat**; at heavily Akkadized
Susa, cuneiform proved more practicable. It remains **undeciphered** (over 40 of its ~103
signs are *hapax legomena*).

## Counterfactual significance

His father Shimpi'ishhuk is absent from the Awan king-list, so Puzur-Inshushinak may not
have been of Awanite blood — the "king of Awan" title perhaps a legitimating claim,
analogous to Mesopotamian rulers styling themselves "king of Kish." His bid for the
hegemony of Mesopotamia was **crushed by [[ur-nammu|Ur-Namma]]**, after which Elam fell
under the shadow of the [[third-dynasty-of-ur|Third Dynasty of Ur]]; had he consolidated,
the early-2nd-millennium balance of the Near East might have run through an Elamite rather
than a Sumerian centre. His career marks the **high-water mark of the Awan dynasty** and
the last flourish of an independent Elamite script before the long Akkadization of Susa.

## Historiography

Known almost entirely through his own inscriptions plus the Ur-Namma synchronism; his
ethnicity (Susian? highlander?) and the reality behind the "king of Awan" title are
debated. Whether his realm constitutes a "secondary state" in the anthropological sense
Potts finds unhelpful. The 900-year gap his redating opens between Proto-Elamite and
Linear Elamite is decisive against theories of continuous indigenous Elamite literacy.
