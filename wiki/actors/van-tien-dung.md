# Văn Tiến Dũng

Chief of the General Staff of the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) and the
field commander of the 1975 Ho Chi Minh Campaign that captured Saigon and
ended the Vietnam War.

## Role and Major Decisions

Dũng rose through PAVN's ranks from the anti-French resistance onward,
succeeding Võ Nguyên Giáp as chief of the general staff. He directed the
final campaign of the war, opening with the test operation at
[[phuoc-long-1974|Phước Long]] (1974–75), then the Central Highlands
offensive (Ban Mê Thuột, March 1975) that triggered the collapse of South
Vietnamese defenses, through to the encirclement and capture of Saigon on
30 April 1975. His memoir of the campaign, *Our Great Spring Victory*,
became a standard Vietnamese Communist account of the war's final year.

## Counterfactual Significance

Dũng's willingness to press the offensive rapidly after the unexpectedly
swift collapse at Ban Mê Thuột and [[xuan-loc-1975|Xuân Lộc]] — accelerating
the campaign timetable far beyond Hanoi's original two-year plan — is
credited with denying South Vietnamese and American planners the time to
organize any coordinated defense or diplomatic response.

## Historiography

Dũng's own memoir is a key Vietnamese-language primary source for the war's
final campaign but is also a work of Communist Party historiography, framing
events to emphasize inevitability and central planning; Western military
historians (drawing on captured PAVN documents and American intelligence
assessments) have used it alongside other sources to reconstruct the
campaign's actual, more improvised pace.
