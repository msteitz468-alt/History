# Jacob Baradaeus

**Jacob Baradaeus** (Jacob Barada‘us / Burd‘oyo, d. 578) was the Miaphysite bishop whose clandestine ordinations under Justinianic persecution rebuilt a separate hierarchy for the non-Chalcedonian church in Syria and beyond — giving the West Syrian community the later nickname **“Jacobite.”**

## Role

- Agent of [[post-chalcedonian-confessional-formation|confessional survival and institutionalization]] when imperial policy favored Chalcedon.
- Symbol of Miaphysite resilience; counterpart in social history to elite theologians such as [[severus-of-antioch|Severus of Antioch]].
- Part of the fractured Christian map that Arab conquerors inherited.

## Counterfactual significance

Absent Jacob’s ordination network, Miaphysite communal structure in the Roman East might have collapsed or been forced into deeper underground discontinuity; the durable “Jacobite” church of the early Islamic centuries would look very different.
