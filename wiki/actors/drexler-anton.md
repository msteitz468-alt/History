# Anton Drexler

## Overview

Anton Drexler (1884–1942) was a Munich locksmith and völkisch activist who founded the German Workers' Party (Deutsche Arbeiterpartei, DAP) in January 1919. The tiny group (initially ~40 members) was one of many radical nationalist circles in post-revolutionary Bavaria. Drexler recruited Adolf Hitler in September 1919 after hearing him speak at a DAP meeting in the Sterneckerbräu beerhall. Although Drexler was the nominal founder and "leader," Hitler rapidly became the dominant force through his oratorical talent. Drexler was sidelined as the party transformed into the NSDAP; he played no significant role after the early 1920s.

`preceded_by:` pre-1919 völkisch and nationalist workers' circles in Munich. `contributed_to:` [[actors/adolf-hitler]] entry into politics and [[events/hitler-dap-entry-1919]]. `part_of:` early Nazi movement formation.

## Formation and Role

Munich locksmith by trade. Active in völkisch politics amid the chaos of 1918–19 Bavarian revolution (Eisner government, Räterepublik, Freikorps "liberation"). The DAP was a minuscule, conspiratorial group emphasizing anti-Marxist, nationalist, and antisemitic "workers'" politics distinct from mainstream conservatives or the SPD.

On the evening Hitler first attended (as Reichswehr informant), Drexler was impressed by the newcomer's speaking ability. Quoting from memory in Kershaw: "Goodness, he’s got a gob. We could use him." Drexler personally invited Hitler to join; Hitler accepted and was issued membership card (photographed in sources). Hitler #555 (or sequential numbering in the tiny party).

Drexler quickly recognized Hitler's value as propagandist but could not control the direction once Hitler began dominating meetings and membership growth.

## Relationship to Hitler

Hitler joined as army observer, not ideological convert. Within weeks/months he became the party's star speaker and de facto leader. Drexler remained nominal chairman for a time but was marginalized. By 1920–21 the party (renamed NSDAP) was Hitler's vehicle. Drexler later attempted to resist the shift toward more explicit antisemitism or Hitler's personal control but lacked the following or charisma.

No evidence in Range 01 of deep personal friendship; Drexler served as the initial gatekeeper who "discovered" the agitator.

## Later Career and Death

After the 1923 putsch (in which he played no prominent role) and refoundation, Drexler faded. He lived until 1942, largely forgotten by the movement he had helped launch. Hitler rarely mentioned him in later years.

## Historiography

Kershaw (Range 01) presents Drexler as the accidental midwife of Hitler's political career. The DAP was "tiny"; Hitler's entry was the "discovery of a talent" moment that turned a failed artist/veteran into a professional agitator. Emphasizes structural context (post-1918 Munich radical right milieu) over Drexler's personal agency. Drexler is not portrayed as a significant thinker or leader in his own right — the party was a platform that Hitler seized.

Primary traces: DAP membership card (Bayerische Staatsbibliothek), contemporary police reports on Munich völkisch groups, Hitler's own later accounts (self-serving), Drexler's sparse memoirs (treated cautiously).
