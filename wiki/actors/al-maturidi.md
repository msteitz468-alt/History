# Al-Māturīdī

Abū Manṣūr al-Māturīdī (c. 853–944) of Samarqand, the founder of the **[[maturidism|Māturīdī]] school**
of Sunni theology — the Central Asian, Hanafī-rooted counterpart to the [[asharism|Ashʿarī]] school that
emerged in the same generation. Building on the theological tradition of Abū Ḥanīfa, he defended Sunni
doctrine with rational [[kalam]] argument, like [[al-ashari|al-Ashʿarī]], but differed on several points —
giving the unaided human intellect a larger role in knowing God and moral truth, and taking distinctive
positions on faith (*īmān*) and the divine attributes.

Long overshadowed in Western scholarship by the Ashʿarī school, Māturīdism became the dominant theology
of the Hanafī world — the Ottoman, Mughal, and Central Asian lands — and is, with Ashʿarism, one of the
two recognized schools of orthodox Sunni [[kalam]].
