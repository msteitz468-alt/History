# Lucius Sergius Catilina

The ruined patrician whose attempted *coup* gave its name to the
**[[catilinarian-conspiracy-63bce|Catilinarian conspiracy]]** of 63 — villain of
Sallust and Cicero, and a lightning-rod for the debt and despair of late-Republican
Italy. Treated in [[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]] Ch. 9 (Wiseman).

## Career

A **patrician** of an old but obscure house, Catiline had an "ugly past in the Sullan
years" — implicated in the proscriptions and the murder of **Marius Gratidianus**,
whose severed head he reportedly carried through Rome. As governor of **Africa** he
extorted heavily, dogged afterward by provincial complaints; a prosecution blocked his
consular bid for 65, and he was acquitted of extortion (65) and later of murder
(Caesar's court, 64). **Twice defeated** for the consulship (for 63, losing to
[[cicero]] and Antonius; for 62), and facing **bankruptcy and prosecution**, he turned
to revolution — championing the cause of the indebted ("only a poor man could
faithfully defend the poor") before launching the **[[catilinarian-conspiracy-63bce|conspiracy]]**.
He died fighting at **Pistoria** (62), refusing to survive defeat.

## Significance

Wiseman presents Catiline less as an arch-villain than as a symptom: the convergence
of **bankrupt noble ambition** and genuine **agrarian/debt distress** that the
Republic's politics could not relieve. The hostile tradition (Sallust, Cicero) made
him the type of the aristocratic demagogue-turned-traitor, obscuring how far the
Etrurian rising sprang from real peasant desperation.
