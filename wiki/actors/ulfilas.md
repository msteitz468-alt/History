# Ulfilas (Wulfila)

**Ulfilas** (c.311–83), part Greek and part Goth, was made an **Arian bishop at Constantinople (341)** with a mission to convert the Goths. He invented a **Gothic alphabet** and translated the Greek Bible into Gothic. Gothic-language scripture and services facilitated the **Arian conversion** of Visigoths and other German groups—shaping the confessional map of the fifth-century West.

## Mission and translation

Logan stresses that Ulfilas was himself an Arian Christian, bishop, and missionary to his fellow Goths. When Visigoths entered the empire (376) under convert **Fritigern**, Ulfilas was present; the subsequent disaster at [[battle-of-adrianople-378|Adrianople (378)]] fixed a Germanic military presence inside the empire that was already partly Christian in non-Nicene form.

> "he, in fact, was an Arian Christian and an Arian bishop and an Arian missionary to his fellow Goths."

## Consequences

- produced: Arian Gothic Christianity
- contributed_to: Catholic–Arian social divide in the western successor kingdoms (see [[arianism]])

Catholic conversions of Franks ([[clovis|Clovis]]) and later Visigoths ([[third-council-of-toledo-589|Toledo 589]]) reversed much of that division; Ostrogothic and Vandal Arianism ended with Justinianic reconquest or collapse.

## Counterfactual significance

Without a Gothic Bible and Arian episcopal network, Germanic kingdoms might have remained pagan longer or converted more directly to Nicene forms—changing the social fracture between Arian courts and Catholic Roman populations.

## Historiography

Details of Ulfilas’s creed and the precise content of his Bible (OT books allegedly omitted as too warlike is a later tradition) are debated; Logan’s account is a standard church-history outline.

## From Gibbon, Decline and Fall (1776–1788)

Attributed claims from [[sources/gibbon-decline-and-fall-1776]] (Ch. XXXVII on barbarian conversion).

- **Apostle of the Goths.** Ulfilas (Ulphilas) invented a Gothic alphabet and translated the Scriptures into Gothic/Teutonic: “Ulphilas, before he could frame his version, was obliged to compose a new alphabet of twenty-four letters.”
- **Kings suppressed.** Gibbon reports he suppressed the four books of Kings lest they irritate Barbarian ferocity.
- **Arian channel.** Fritigern adopted his faith; Athanaric persecuted converts; Ulfilas stands as the hinge by which the Goths entered the empire as Arians rather than Catholics—structuring later conflict with Catholic Romans and Franks.
