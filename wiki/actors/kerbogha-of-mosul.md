# Kerbogha (Karbugha) of Mosul

Atabeg of Mosul and leading Seljuq commander tasked with checking the western threat during the First Crusade. He assembled and led the major relief army that arrived at Antioch in June 1098 after the crusaders had captured the city.

## Role at Antioch (1098)

Following the death of Malik Shah and the ensuing Seljuq civil wars, Sultan Berkyaruk relied on Kerbogha to watch events in Syria. After Yaghi Siyan appealed for help, Kerbogha raised a large coalition force from Mosul, Jazira, and other territories. The army arrived after the crusaders had taken Antioch by treachery (2/3 June 1098), turning the crusaders into the besieged.

On 28 June 1098 Kerbogha's forces were defeated in a coordinated crusader sortie. Latin sources describe the crusader army marching out in a disciplined "complex formation" that exploited the fragility of Kerbogha's coalition. The defeat was decisive for the survival of the crusade and opened the road to Jerusalem.

Kerbogha's coalition suffered from the same political disunity that plagued other Seljuq responses: component emirs had their own agendas, and the army dissolved under pressure.

## Historiography / Sources

In the *Gesta Francorum* and other Latin accounts, Kerbogha appears as the great pagan threat providentially defeated. France (*Victory in the East*) treats him as a competent commander whose coalition was undermined by the same fragmentation that had allowed the crusaders to reach Antioch in the first place. His failure is presented as emblematic of the limits of Seljuq response in 1097–98 rather than personal incompetence.
