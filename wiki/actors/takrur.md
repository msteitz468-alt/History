# Takrur

**Takrur** was an early kingdom of the **middle Senegal valley** (Futa Toro), the homeland
of the **Tukulor (Toucouleur)** people and closely bound up with the origins of the
**[[fulani|Fulani]]**. Its outsized importance lies in religion and nomenclature: Takrur was
**one of the earliest Sudanese kingdoms to embrace Islam**, and "**Bilad al-Takrur**" became
the Arabic name for the **whole Western Sudan** — Egyptians even called the king of
[[mali-empire|Mali]] the "King of Takrur." Documented for the wiki through Nehemia Levtzion's
chapter in [[ajayi-crowder-history-west-africa-v1-1971|*History of West Africa* Vol. I]] (1971).

## Early Islam on the Senegal

Al-Bakri (1067–8) records that the people of Takrur "had been pagan and worshipped idols until
the reign of **War-Djabi** (War-Ndyay) son of Rabis," who "became Muslim, introduced Islamic
law, and enforced the religion upon his subjects" — dying in **432 AH (1040–41)**. By al-Bakri's
own day "the people of Takrur are Muslims." This makes Takrur, with Gao, among the **earliest
Islamised polities of the Sudan** — and unlike many Sahelian states, its conversion reached
beyond the court to the population, who "remained devoted to this faith." Takrur and its
tributary **Silla** waged war on the pagans between themselves and [[ghana-empire|Ghana]].

## The Almoravid alliance and the Fulani question

As fervent Muslims, the kings of Takrur allied with the **[[almoravids|Almoravids]]**: Labi son
of War-Djabi fought alongside the Almoravid leader Yahya b. 'Umar against the Juddala in 1056–7
— brethren in faith, and allies against the common adversary, pagan Ghana. Levtzion uses Takrur
to broach the **"elusive problem of Fulani origin"**: the Fulani speak the same language as the
Tukulor (related to Wolof and Sereer), and the likeliest reconstruction is that their ancestors
were **nomads who migrated into the Futa Toro**, where the differentiation of Toucouleur (settled)
and Fulani (pastoral) took place. The traditions of Takrur's successive dynasties (Dya'ogo, Manna,
Tondyon, Lam-Termes, Lam-Taga) record waves of invasion and a fragmenting Futa under pressure from
Berbers, Soninke, Malinke and the rising Wolof **Jolof** state.

## Significance

Takrur shows that **Islamisation of the Sudan began in the far west and reached society's roots
early** — and that the Senegal valley was the cradle of the Tukulor–Fulani peoples whose
descendants would later carry the [[west-african-jihads|jihads]] across West Africa. Its name,
generalised by Arab writers to the whole region, is why later Songhay askias could be invested as
**"caliph of Takrur"** (see [[songhay-empire]]).
