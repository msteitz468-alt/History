# The Akan States (Bono, Akwamu, Denkyira, Fante, Gonja)

The **Akan states** were the trading kingdoms of the forest and forest-fringe between the
Black Volta and the Gold Coast — the matrix from which **[[asante-empire|Asante]]** would
emerge as the greatest power. This page covers the major **non-Asante** polities: the
northern Akan (Bron) kingdom of **Bono**; the Mande-founded savannah kingdom of **Gonja**;
and the southern Akan forest kingdoms of **Akwamu**, **Denkyira**, and **Fante**.
Documented for the wiki through Ivor Wilks's chapter in
[[ajayi-crowder-history-west-africa-v1-1971|*History of West Africa* Vol. I]] (1971).

## The Akan world: matriclans and the gold trade

Southern Akan society is built on extended exogamous **matriclans (*abusua*)** — Oyoko,
Bretuo, Agona and others — used as a **device for integrating diverse groups** (often of
different origins) into a homogeneous culture, in marked contrast to the layered, estate-based
societies of the savannah. The dynamic of Akan history is the **gold trade** in two phases:
first the medieval **Dyula (Mande) trade** carrying gold north to the Sudanic markets
(stimulating production), then the **maritime trade** after the Portuguese built **Elmina
(1482)**, which re-routed gold to the coast, drew waves of immigration into the goldfields,
and bred new concentrations of political power. The later 17th–18th-century shift from **gold
to slaves** (with the West Indian sugar boom) then reshaped the same states.

## Gonja

**Gonja** (founded 16th c.) arose when a **Mande/Malian cavalry expedition** — sent south,
tradition says, over a collapse in the gold supply reaching Mali's markets — settled on the
Black Volta under **Nabaga**, expanding greatly under **Jakpa Lanta** (r. c. 1622–67). It
developed a three-estate society: the **Nbanya** princes (descendants of the Mande settlers),
the **nyamasi** commoners (subject peoples retaining their land-cults), and the **Kramo**
(Muslim Dyula, granted the imamates). Gonja became central to the **kola trade** between the
forest and the Sudan; its market **Salaga** grew into the major kola entrepôt of West Africa.

## Akwamu, Denkyira, Fante

- **Akwamu** (c. 1500–1730): founded by the **Abrade**, northern-Akan traders who settled
  behind Elmina and migrated east to behind Accra. As clients of the Accra kings they
  engrossed the gold trade at the **Abonse** market, then **seized power from Accra (1677)**
  and built an empire of **dual character** — a tightly-run *trade state* around Nyanaoase and
  a sprawling *tribute state* over Guan, Ewe, Ga and Adangme peoples. Bosman called its king
  richer "than all those we have hitherto described taken together." It **disintegrated in
  1730** under the strains of overexpansion, the corrosive shift to slave-raiding, and
  succession disputes — its lands divided among Akyem Abuakwa, Akyem Kotoku, and Akwapim.
- **Denkyira**: a gold-rich forest kingdom (capital Abankeseso) that dominated the western
  goldfields and the Twifo/Assin/Wassaw by the late 17th century — until its **total defeat by
  the rising Asante in 1699–1701**, after which many Denkyira were absorbed into Kumasi.
- **Fante**: a cluster of small coastal states ("republics," Bosman called them; "a sort of
  commonwealth," said Barbot), created largely by the **Borbor Fante** out of Bono after 1600,
  united loosely by the priests of **Mankessim**. The Fante became the **middlemen** of the
  English at Cape Coast (the Royal African Company HQ), brokering between Europeans and the
  inland Akan; their power grew with the **18th-c. slave trade**.

## Significance

The Akan states demonstrate the volume's thesis in the forest zone: **trade — first gold,
then slaves — drove state formation**, throwing up ever-larger systems (Akwamu, Denkyira →
Asante) that imposed a rule of law over wide areas to maximise commerce. They also show the
**bow-to-gun military transition** that reshaped Akan warfare and turned army commanders
(*kontihene*, *adontenhene*) into the agents of civil government. Their consolidation
**prepared the way for [[asante-empire|Asante]]**, which absorbed Denkyira's and others'
lands and peoples.
