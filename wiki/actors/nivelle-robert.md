# Robert Nivelle

French general. Rose through artillery commands; succeeded Pétain at Verdun; replaced Joffre as C-in-C in late 1916. Planned and executed the major spring 1917 offensive on the Chemin des Dames (Aisne) based on his "formula" for rupture and exploitation. The offensive's failure contributed to the French army mutinies of 1917; he was relieved in favor of Pétain.

**From Doughty** (Ch. 7, exclusive range): Rapid rise from colonel (Aug 1914) to C-in-C armies north/northeast (13 Dec 1916) in ~28 months. Artillery specialist who coordinated infantry/artillery effectively and developed rolling barrage. Verdun successes (capture of forts Douaumont and Vaux, esp. 15 Dec 1916) gave him reputation for swift, sure methods contrasting Somme "slow, costly" approach. "The experience is conclusive; our method has proved itself." (A Strategy of Decisive Battle, Early 1917 l 324)

Nivelle's "formula": extraordinarily high concentration of artillery "fired in depth throughout a carefully chosen zone" to devastate entire defensive zones and open corridors; infantry to "penetrate a defense in twenty-four to forty-eight hours and advance as deep as the enemy’s heavy artillery batteries in a single, continuous thrust"; heavy artillery "employed as if it were a 75-mm cannon"; after breakthrough "audacious lateral exploitation" to destroy remaining batteries and cut resupply, with additional forces flowing through to destroy reserves. "Objective: Total destruction of active enemy forces by maneuver and battle." "We now have the formula." Abandoned Joffre emphasis on "successive efforts in a methodical battle" for continuous thrusts; favored "penetration and breakthrough" over "rupturing." (l 324-325, 431)

Operational concept (Dec 1916-Jan 1917 letters): British and Northern Army Group to fix enemy in Noyon salient flanks; Central (later Reserve/Micheler) Army Group surprise attack on Aisne/Chemin des Dames to break through; "maneuver" forces through breach for decisive battle vs. reserves. "The success of our operations will thus depend essentially on the maneuver force." "The objective is the destruction of the principal mass of the enemy’s forces on the Western Front. This can be attained only by a decisive battle, delivered against the reserves of the adversary and followed by an intensive exploitation." Insisted on "violence, brutality, and speed." (l 325-327)

Planning and execution challenges: Secured British relief of 32 km front and Apr start at London conference (delayed from Feb); temporary unity of command at Calais (27 Feb) later revised. German withdrawal to Hindenburg Line (Feb-Mar) freed enemy reserves and strengthened Aisne; Nivelle adapted by adding Central AG "auxiliary" attack and reducing Northern role. Loose security forfeited surprise (open discussions with civilians; captured orders Feb and early Apr revealing Aisne offensive). Subordinates and Painlevé expressed grave doubts pre-offensive. Compiègne conference (6 Apr): Nivelle defended plan, offered resignation; compromise retained offensive with instruction to halt if no early rupture ("if the desired results are not attained after a brief period, we will not stubbornly continue the battle indefinitely as we did on the Somme"). (l 329-344)

Execution (main attack 16 Apr Reserve AG Micheler 5th/6th Armies on Chemin des Dames; 17 Apr Pétain Central): 5,350 guns in Micheler group fired >11M rounds, but weather, German air superiority, reverse-slope defenses, and logistics crippled prep. Infantry (e.g. II Colonial Corps) gained crest but fixed by intact MGs: "welcomed and fixed in place by the deadly fire of numerous machine guns that, located on the [reverse side of the] slope, outside the reach of our projectiles, have remained undamaged." "In less than an hour, the fighting is stabilized." Limited gains (Sixth left 6-7 km by 20 Apr, 5,300 prisoners); no breakthrough. By 20 Apr Nivelle shifted to "series of small operations." (l 349-353)

**Verdun 1916 (Doughty Ch. 6, Range 4):** Corps commander then commander of Second Army at Verdun (replaced Pétain May 1916 upon Pétain's promotion to Central Army Group). Noted for aggressive attitude; Joffre used him to prod Pétain ("the 'aggressive attitude' of General Nivelle"). During German June offensive (Fleury, Vaux sector) warned subordinates of German "infiltration" and "encirclement" tactics. Famous order during crisis after Fleury: "Comrades, they will not pass!" Directed counterattacks around Fleury (eight attacks in ten days with Mangin; heavy losses in regiments). Key role in planning and execution of successful Oct 24 (Douaumont) and Dec 15 (further advance) counteroffensives under rolling barrages and concentrated artillery (including naval/coastal 370/400-mm pieces). "In four hours, in a magnificent assault, you seized from a powerful enemy in a single blow the terrain northeast of Verdun..." These limited but decisive actions with light relative casualties (contrasted by politicians with Somme) contributed to his selection as Joffre's successor. See [[events/battle-of-verdun-1916]].


Outcome: 16-25 Apr est. 134,000 casualties (30k killed); "exceeded those of any month since November 1914." Morale collapse after high expectations. "The army was very fragile when it undertook the Nivelle offensive; it was even more fragile when the operation ended." Political leaders moved to replace him; 29 Apr Pétain appointed chief of staff. "the appointment of Pétain as chief of staff marked the beginning of new strategy and operational methods for France." (l 353-354)
