# Gideon Welles

**Summary page.** See Lincoln and Civil War pages for context.

Connecticut editor and politician, “Neptune” to Lincoln, and Secretary of the Navy throughout the war. Welles was an independent-minded cabinet member whose detailed diary provides one of the most valuable contemporary records of Lincoln’s leadership style, cabinet debates, and daily decision-making. He generally supported Lincoln’s measured approach while offering candid private assessments.

## Overview

Welles brought administrative competence and New England moral seriousness to the Navy Department. His diary is repeatedly cited for sharp observations of Lincoln, Stanton, Seward, Chase, and the shifting dynamics of the “team.” He remained in office under Johnson and later wrote historical essays that helped shape the early Lincoln legend.

## Key Developments

- Pre-war: Newspaper editor, postmaster, state politics; joined Republicans over slavery extension.
- 1861–1865: Built and directed the wartime navy; blockade strategy; ironclads (Monitor); cabinet participant in all major policy discussions.
- Post-war: Continued under Johnson; later historical writing; death 1878.

## Significance

Welles’s diary is a primary source of the first rank for the internal history of the Lincoln administration. Goodwin draws heavily on it for texture on Lincoln’s humor, patience, and command of detail.
