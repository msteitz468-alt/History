# Peter Jefferson

Virginia surveyor, planter, and colonial official (1708–1757), father of Thomas Jefferson. Patented land on the Rivanna River in Albemarle County in the 1730s–40s and built the family seat at Shadwell (named for the London parish of his own childhood), where Thomas Jefferson was born in 1743. Later built and moved the family to Tuckahoe, then back to Shadwell.

Peter Jefferson was among Virginia's leading surveyors, co-producing (with Joshua Fry) the influential 1751 Fry-Jefferson map of Virginia, one of the most accurate and widely used maps of the colony for decades. He served as a justice of the peace, county lieutenant, and burgess, representing the rising planter gentry of Virginia's Piedmont frontier. He died when Thomas Jefferson was fourteen, leaving him land and enslaved people as part of his inheritance.

## Significance

Peter Jefferson's surveying career and land acquisitions established the family's Piedmont landholdings and social standing that underwrote Thomas Jefferson's education and later political career.
