# Hermann Göring

Prominent Nazi leader, WWI flying ace, and head of the Luftwaffe. Designated Hitler's successor; built the air force and oversaw the Four-Year Plan for rearmament and autarky. Personally directed the Battle of Britain (failure damaged his prestige). Deeply involved in the economic exploitation of occupied territories and the early organization of the Holocaust (though rivalry with Himmler and others). Captured at Nuremberg; suicide before execution.

## Evans, *The Third Reich in Power* (2005)

The peacetime record: creator of the Prussian Gestapo (1933) and of SA
auxiliary-police terror; enrolled and then leashed the SA; handed the
Prussian political police to Himmler (20 April 1934) and directed the Berlin
killings of the [[night-of-the-long-knives-1934|Röhm purge]] in person.
Supreme over the [[four-year-plan-1936|Four-Year Plan]] from October 1936 —
"very little understanding of economics" (Evans) but decisive power: the
Hermann Göring Works, labour conscription decrees (22 June 1938), the
Foreign Currency Search Office under Heydrich (August 1936) weaponized
against Jewish firms. Antisemitic policy manager: chaired the 12 November
1938 conference after [[kristallnacht-1938|Kristallnacht]] ("I would have
preferred it if you had beaten 200 Jews to death and hadn't destroyed such
valuable property"), issued the billion-mark fine and expropriation decrees,
and the "privileged mixed marriage" rules (28 December 1938). In foreign
policy both hawk and dove: engineered the Anschluss by telephone ultimatum
(11 March 1938) coveting Austrian iron ore, then brokered Munich behind
Hitler's back; urged Hitler on 29 August 1939 not to "gamble everything"
("In my life, I've always put my whole stake on the table"). Corruption on a
pharaonic scale (Carinhall's 15m+ RM refit; the popular joke defined a "Gör"
as "the quantity of tin that one man can carry on his chest"). Source:
[[evans-third-reich-in-power-2005]]

## From Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)

His Dunkirk boast (the Luftwaffe would finish the trapped armies) helped persuade Hitler to halt the panzers; his air force then lost the [[battle-of-britain-1940|Battle of Britain]] on intelligence that undercounted British fighter losses by an order of magnitude. As economic overlord: the Hermann Göring Reich Works led the seizure of foreign industry; his 7 November 1941 guidelines set the murderous terms for Soviet labor ("Russians to arrange own food (cats, horses, etc.) . . . Range of punishment: from cutting food rations to execution by firing squad; generally nothing in between" — see [[forced-labor-nazi-germany]]); he repeated the Hunger Plan's arithmetic to Ciano (15 November 1941): "This year, 20-30 million people in Russia will starve." His 31 July 1941 commission to [[heydrich-reinhard|Heydrich]] authorized preparation of the "total solution of the Jewish question" (see [[wannsee-conference-1942]]). Losing the Todt succession to [[speer-albert|Speer]] (February 1942) and the Stalingrad airlift fiasco (300 tons/day promised, ~90 delivered; his 30 January 1943 Thermopylae broadcast comparing Sixth Army to Spartans who, Evans notes, were wiped out) completed his decline — by 1943 "weakened by heavy doses of morphine, to which he had now become addicted." Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## From Shirer, *The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich* (1960)

**Early career and Reichstag fire.** Shirer records Göring as a WWI ace, last commander of the Richthofen Fighter Squadron and Pour le Mérite holder, who joined the NSDAP in 1921 after meeting Hitler, became S.A. commander in 1922, and was wounded in the thigh at the Feldherrnhalle during the 1923 Beer Hall Putsch — given first aid by a Jewish bank proprietor and smuggled to Austria by his wife Carin von Kantzow. At the Reichstag fire scene (27 February 1933) Göring allegedly shouted to Gestapo chief Rudolf Diels: "This is the beginning of the Communist revolution!... Every Communist official must be shot, where he is found. Every Communist deputy must this very night be strung up." General Halder testified at Nuremberg that at a 1942 birthday luncheon Göring boasted, "The only one who really knows about the Reichstag is I, because I set it on fire!" — Shirer treats Nazi authorship of the fire as established "beyond a reasonable doubt" (Shirer 1960).

**Name origin of the Gestapo.** Göring intended "Secret Police Office" (Geheimes Polizei Amt/GPA) but avoided the acronym for resembling the Soviet GPU; an unnamed post-office clerk suggested "Geheime Staatspolizei" (GESTAPO), established for Prussia by Göring on 26 April 1933 (Shirer 1960).

**Spanish Civil War.** Göring's Nuremberg testimony (14 March 1946) on using Spain to test the Luftwaffe: "With the permission of the Fuehrer I sent a large part of my transport fleet and a number of experimental fighter units, bombers and antiaircraft guns... in order that the personnel, too, might gather a certain experience, I saw to it that there was a continuous flow" — see [[german-intervention-spanish-civil-war-1936]] (Shirer 1960).

**Anschluss.** Göring's minute-by-minute wiretapped phone calls (Forschungsamt transcripts, ~27 calls from 2:45 p.m., 11 March 1938) drove the coup: to Seyss-Inquart, "if the conditions are not accepted immediately, the troops which are already advancing to the frontier will march in tonight... Austria will cease to exist"; on President Miklas's refusal to yield, "Well, with fourteen children, a man has to stay put. Anyway, tell Seyss to take over." Göring personally dictated the fabricated "request for German aid" telegram to Keppler at 8:48 p.m. for Seyss-Inquart to send — Seyss-Inquart never actually sent it. Full detail on [[anschluss-1938]] (Shirer 1960).

**Munich crisis.** Shirer credits Göring — "appalled at war over an issue already conceded" (matching Evans's account above) — with brokering the Munich conference; the "Italian proposals" Mussolini tabled as his own were in fact drafted the day before in the German Foreign Office by Göring, Neurath, and Weizsäcker, behind Ribbentrop's back, and telephoned to Rome. See [[munich-agreement-1938]] (Shirer 1960).

**Kristallnacht.** At the 12 November 1938 meeting (already covered via Evans above), Shirer's version of the same exchange records Göring's reaction to the 5 million marks of broken-glass damage: "This cannot continue!... I wish you had killed two hundred Jews instead of destroying so many valuables!" — matching Evans's figure of 200 almost verbatim, cross-confirming the quotation across two independent source bases. Heydrich's reply, "Thirty-five were killed," and Göring's closing line on the billion-mark fine ("That will work. The swine won't commit another murder") are additional verbatim detail. See [[kristallnacht-1938]] (Shirer 1960).

**Nazi-Soviet Pact era.** At the Pact of Steel signing (22 May 1939), denied the Collar of the Annunziata given to Ribbentrop instead, Göring "made quite a scene." Göring used his private plane to send Swedish intermediary [[dahlerus-birger|Birger Dahlerus]] to London on 25 August 1939 seeking an "understanding" with Britain, maintaining his own back-channel to Halifax outside regular diplomatic channels, a fact kept from Ribbentrop. Dahlerus's Nuremberg testimony describes witnessing Göring's impassivity — "did not turn a hair" — during a bizarre Hitler outburst after midnight on 26–27 August. On hearing the British ultimatum translated (3 September, ~11 a.m.), Göring exclaimed to interpreter Schmidt: "If we lose this war, then God have mercy on us!" Around the same period Göring told British businessmen, via Dahlerus, "on his word of honor" that he would avert war, then boasted two days later of Luftwaffe air defenses: "The Ruhr will not be subjected to a single bomb. If an enemy bomber reaches the Ruhr, my name is not Hermann Goering: you can call me Meier!" — a boast Shirer notes he was "soon to rue" (Shirer 1960). See [[molotov-ribbentrop-pact-1939]] and the new page [[dahlerus-birger]].

**Barbarossa and the Hunger Plan.** Göring's Economic Staff East directive of 23 May 1941 ordered that surplus food from Russia's black-earth south not be diverted to feed industrial-region workers, who "will simply be left to starve"; Göring told Ciano some weeks after the invasion that "between twenty and thirty million persons will die of hunger in Russia... In the camps for Russian prisoners they have begun to eat each other" — evidence, per Shirer, that the starvation policy was openly discussed among top Nazis, not a secret horror discovered later. This matches and predates the 15 November 1941 figure already recorded via Evans above (Shirer 1960).

**Decline and fall of Mussolini.** [EYEWITNESS-adjacent via Ciano] Ciano's diary (2 February 1942) on Göring's Rome visit: "As usual he is bloated and overbearing... Goering talked of little else but the jewels he owned... he wore a great sable coat, something between what automobile drivers wore in 1906 and what a high-grade prostitute wears to the opera." Shirer's own gloss: "The corruption and corrosion of the Number Two man in the Third Reich was making steady progress" (Shirer 1960).

**Stalingrad eulogy.** Göring's 30 January 1943 broadcast (already noted via Evans above as a "Thermopylae" comparison) is given in full by Shirer: "A thousand years hence Germans will speak of this battle with reverence and awe... When you come to Germany, say that you have seen us lying at Stalingrad, as our honor and our leaders ordained that we should" (Shirer 1960).

**Final collapse.** On Hitler's birthday, 20 April 1945, Göring fled Berlin, believing Hitler would soon be dead and intending to succeed him. His telegram of 23 April, requesting authority to assume "total leadership of the Reich" under the 1941 succession decree if no reply came by 10 p.m., was intercepted and presented to Hitler by Bormann as a treasonous "ultimatum"; Hitler stripped Göring of all offices and had him arrested by the SS at Berchtesgaden before dawn on 24 April. Hitler's Political Testament formally expelled Göring from the party for "disloyalty" (Shirer 1960).

**Nuremberg.** [EYEWITNESS] Shirer attended the trial and described Göring in the dock as 80 lbs lighter, in a faded Luftwaffe uniform, "obviously pleased" to have the No. 1 dock position. Göring evaded hanging by swallowing smuggled poison two hours before his scheduled execution, 16 October 1946 (Shirer 1960).
