# Alboin

King of the [[lombards|Lombards]] (c. 560–572) who led his people from
Pannonia into Italy in 568 CE, founding the Lombard Kingdom of Italy that
would endure until Charlemagne's conquest in 774.

## Role and Major Decisions

Alboin first destroyed the rival Gepid kingdom (567), reportedly killing its
king Cunimund and taking his daughter Rosamund as wife, before leading the
Lombards' migration into a northern Italy weakened by decades of the
Gothic War between Byzantium and the Ostrogoths. The Lombard invasion of 568
permanently fractured Byzantine control of Italy, establishing a durable
Lombard kingdom in the north and a mosaic of Lombard duchies further south
that outlasted Byzantine efforts at reconquest.

## Counterfactual Significance

Alboin's invasion arrived at a moment of maximum Byzantine exhaustion after
the Gothic War; a Lombard failure to establish itself would likely have
allowed a more complete (if still contested) Byzantine consolidation of
Italy, altering the peninsula's fragmented political trajectory through the
early medieval period.

## Historiography

Alboin's career, including the vivid and possibly embellished account of his
murder in 572 (allegedly at Rosamund's instigation, in revenge for being
forced to drink from her father's skull), is known mainly through Paul the
Deacon's *History of the Lombards*, written some two centuries later and
drawing on a mix of Lombard oral tradition and earlier written sources whose
reliability for Alboin's actual reign is difficult to verify independently.
