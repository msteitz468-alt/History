# Udjahorresne

**Udjahorresne** (Uzahor-resenet) was a high Egyptian official of **Sais** — chief
physician, royal chancellor, and commander of the Saite navy — who **collaborated with the
Persian conquerors** of Egypt and left, on a green-basalt statue now in the Vatican, the
single most important Egyptian account of the conquest. He is the chief witness through
whom [[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 3g, Ray) rehabilitates **[[cambyses|Cambyses]]**
against the hostile Greek tradition.

## The arch-collaborator

Already a great man under **Amasis** and **Psammetichus III**, Udjahorresne went over to
**Cambyses** at the [[persian-conquest-of-egypt-525bce|conquest of 525]], and openly states
that the king advanced his career and listened to his advice. His **idealized hieroglyphic
autobiography** portrays Cambyses as ordering the **restoration of the temple of Neith at
Sais** and behaving with piety — even as it admits "the great misfortune which had befallen
the entire land" and that foreign soldiers had profaned the sacred precinct. Crucially, he
**drew up Cambyses' titulary as a regular Pharaoh** — making Cambyses the only Persian king
with a complete one, Mesuti-Re, "offspring of the sun-god." Under **Darius I** he was sent
back from Elam to Sais to revive the **"House of Life"** (the temple library and medical
school). Ray suggests he served Darius further as a **diplomat**.

## Significance: the psychology of assimilation

Ray reads Udjahorresne not merely as a careerist but as the agent of a **cultural
strategy**: by clothing the conqueror in pharaonic legitimacy, the Egyptian elite
**psychologically "denied" the unassimilable reality of conquest** and so secured "the
continuity of Egyptian civilization in its greatest crisis." This act — repeated in the
"Sesostris" legend of Darius and later in the Egyptianization of [[alexander-the-great|
Alexander]] — is why Ray compares Udjahorresne to the **biblical Ezra**, and why a
**posthumous cult** of him survived at Memphis two centuries later. He is the type-case of
how a sophisticated subject civilization tames its rulers from within.

## Causal Analysis

contributed_to: the **legitimation of Persian rule** in Egypt and the survival of Egyptian
religious institutions through the conquest; the **rehabilitation of Cambyses** in modern
scholarship.
caused_by: the Egyptian elite's mix of self-interest and cultural confidence under
[[persian-conquest-of-egypt-525bce|foreign conquest]] (see [[achaemenid-egypt]]).

## Historiography

Known almost entirely from his **own statue-autobiography** (Vatican) — an interested,
idealized source that nonetheless preserves precious detail, and that modern historians
(Posener, Lloyd, Ray) weigh against the Greek tradition to recover a more "statesmanlike"
Cambyses. A later romanticized Coptic conquest-tale features a wise counsellor "Bothor" who
may be Udjahorresne in disguise.
