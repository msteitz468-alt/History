August Wilhelm Antonius Neidhardt von Gneisenau (1760–1831) was a Prussian field marshal and key reformer alongside Scharnhorst. He served as chief of staff to Blücher in the Wars of Liberation, executing bold operational maneuvers (e.g., the march from the Elbe to the Saale in 1813 and the Wavre–Waterloo movement in 1815) that realized the Napoleonic principle of concentration for decisive battle while adapting it to coalition warfare.

## In Delbrück
Delbrück presents Gneisenau as the practical executor who, with Scharnhorst's institutional reforms and Clausewitz's theory, enabled Prussia to turn Napoleon's own methods against him. Emphasizes the operational boldness and the political-military integration of the reform era. See [[sources/delbruck-dawn-of-modern-warfare-vol-4-1985]].
