# Miles C. Dempsey (1896–1969)

British general. Commander of the British Second Army in Normandy under Montgomery's 21st Army Group.

## Role in Normandy (D'Este)

Dempsey was the operational army commander on the British side but operated with limited initiative because Montgomery frequently bypassed him to deal directly with corps commanders. He was the primary architect of Operation Goodwood, seeking to use the massed British armoured divisions to break the stalemate east of Caen. Loyal and competent, he left relatively few personal papers, making D'Este's use of his notes particularly valuable.

## Historiography

Often overshadowed by Montgomery. D'Este gives him significant credit for recognizing the need for a bold armoured stroke and for attempting to assert army-level command within the constraints of Montgomery's style.

## Normandy Role (D'Este)

Dempsey was the operational army commander but operated under tight Montgomery oversight (often bypassed). He conceived GOODWOOD as an all-armoured stroke east of the Orne to break the Caen stalemate, economise infantry (manpower crisis), and seize the Caen-Falaise Plain. "What I had in mind was to seize all the crossings of the Orne from Caen to Argentan." Post-battle verdict: tactically imperfect but strategically successful in pinning armour; "I was prepared to lose a couple of hundred tanks. So long as I didn’t lose men." Critical of 7th Armoured handling at Villers-Bocage ("a disgrace").
