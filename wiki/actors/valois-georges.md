# Georges Valois (1878–1945)

French ex-anarchosyndicalist who joined Action Française and served as the
broker of the Sorel–Maurras junction: signatory of the *La Cité française*
prospectus (1910), organizer (with [[berth-edouard|Berth]]) of the
[[cercle-proudhon|Cercle Proudhon]] (1911), speaker on syndicalist-
nationalist convergence at the Action française congress of December 1911
("the junction had already been made"). In 1925 he launched the **Faisceau**,
the first fascist party outside Italy — central evidence for
[[sternhell-thesis|Sternhell's]] claim that France was fascism's ideological
laboratory. Sternhell notes his Faisceau had no antisemitism at all — one of
his exhibits against equating fascism with Nazism. Griffin
([[griffin-nature-of-fascism-1991]]) concurs on the movement's authenticity
and adds detail: modeled on Mussolini (whom Valois had visited in 1923), it
fused Maurrassian integral nationalism with the Cercle Proudhon's
anarcho-syndicalism, proposed a Latin fascist bloc (France–Italy–Spain),
invited Jewish participation in the "New Age," peaked at ~60,000 members,
and dissolved in 1928 after Valois concluded fascism had no future in
France. Griffin rates Valois's pre-war synthesis proto-fascist while judging
the parent Action Française itself neo-conservative, not fascist (against
Nolte's *Three Faces of Fascism*).

Valois then broke with fascism and swung back left: his journal *Le Nouvel
Age* (from 1934) was anti-plutocratic and "leftist"; his 1935 application to
the SFIO (sponsored by Marceau Pivert) was accepted locally but rejected
federally, and the antifascist vigilance committee also refused him. He
joined the Resistance and died in Bergen-Belsen in 1945. His round trip —
syndicalism → integral nationalism → fascism → socialism → Resistance — makes
him a key individual case in [[fascism-and-the-political-left]].
