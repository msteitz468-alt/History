# Zenobia

**Septimia Zenobia** (Bat-Zabbai; c. 240–after 274) was the queen of **[[palmyra|Palmyra]]** who,
as regent for her son **Vaballathus**, built a short-lived **Palmyrene empire** spanning Syria, Egypt,
and much of Asia Minor at the height of the [[third-century-crisis|third-century crisis]]. Treated in
[[bowman-cah-v12-2005|CAH XII]] Ch. 2 (**John Drinkwater**).

## The Palmyrene ascendancy

Zenobia was the widow of **Odenathus**, the Palmyrene nobleman whom [[gallienus|Gallienus]] had made
*Corrector Totius Orientis* ("Marshal of the East") for holding the eastern frontier against Persia
after the capture of Valerian (260). When Odenathus was murdered in a family quarrel (267), Zenobia
ruled as regent. Exploiting the disorder of 270, she **took Egypt** (Rome's grain supply) and pushed
into Asia Minor as far as Ancyra, having her son styled *Augustus* and herself *Augusta* — while at
first carefully advertising a nominal **condominium** with Rome rather than open secession.

## Defeat

In 272 [[aurelian|Aurelian]] marched east, defeated the Palmyrene forces at **Antioch** and **Emesa**,
and besieged Palmyra, capturing Zenobia as she attempted to flee. A second revolt in 273 led Aurelian
to **destroy the city**. Zenobia was spared, displayed in his triumph of 274, and (in the tradition)
allowed to live out her life in Italy.

## Significance

The Palmyrene empire — like the contemporary [[gallic-empire|Gallic Empire]] in the West — illustrates
Drinkwater's key point about the crisis: even the great breakaway regimes advertised themselves as
**"Roman,"** and their long-term survival would have required their rulers to become emperors **in
Rome itself**. The prevailing tendency was **centripetal**: there was strikingly little genuine
separatism, even at the empire's lowest ebb. Zenobia's career is the most spectacular instance of
**local self-help** that, in the end, was reabsorbed into the empire.
