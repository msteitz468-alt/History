# Taliesin

Legendary and historical Welsh poet; historical Taliesin associated with court of Urien of Rheged (6th c.); the Taliesin tradition (poems in Llyfr Taliesin, later developments) embodies the figure of the inspired poet-seer. Central to Welsh literary identity and the Matter of Britain.

## Role and Major Phases
Vol V Contents: "Taliesin [1] the historical Taliesin 1652 [2] the Taliesin tradition 1653". Historical core 6th c. associated with Urien; tradition embodied in Llyfr Taliesin (includes Marwnad Cunedda, other awdlau).

## Key Events / Decisions (from range prose)
- Attribution and dúnad in Marwnad Cunedda: “the attribution to Taliesin in the first line of the extant text may be discarded, since the first word of the fourth line, kyfrwng ‘in between’, forms a dúnad (ring-style closing) with Marwnad Cunedda’s last word, Coeling ‘descendants of Coel’”.

## Additional Verbatim Claims from R05-Vol5-02 (Koch Vol V)

- **Llyfr Taliesin & Math lore**: "the four references in Llyfr Taliesin (‘The Book of Taliesin’ ... ) and the single reference to him in the triads (TYP no. 28). The poets of the 14th, 15th, and 16th centuries also seem to know of Math..." "In the mythological poem Kat Goªeu (Cad Goddau ‘The battle of the trees’) in Llyfr Taliesin, Modron is connected with the characters and magical events of the tale Math fab Mathonwy..." (Koch Vol V, Math fab Mathonwy entry).

- **Links to Urien / triads**: Modron "is the mother of Owain and Morfudd by Urien (TYP no. 70)"; cross-refs in further reading tie Llyfr Taliesin material to Urien, triads, Mabinogi, Arthurian. (Koch Vol V, Modron entry).

- **Broader tradition**: Taliesin corpus (historical + Llyfr Taliesin) preserves variant mythological and courtly lore; part of dense network with triads, genealogies, and Ulster Cycle / Mythological Cycle parallels in Insular literature.

## Counterfactual Significance
Without the Taliesin tradition, major loss to Welsh poetic self-understanding and transmission of early northern British material into medieval Welsh identity.
