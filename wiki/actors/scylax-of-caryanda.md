# Scylax of Caryanda

**Scylax of Caryanda** was a **Carian Greek navigator in the service of
[[achaemenid-empire|Darius I]]** who, c. 517 BCE, charted the course of the **Indus**
for the Persian king — the reconnaissance that preceded Darius's organization of the
**[[gandhara|Hindus satrapy]]** and the first securely recorded **sea-passage between
India and the Mediterranean world**. [[boardman-cah-v4-1988|CAH Vol. IV]] (Ch. 3d,
Bivar) treats his voyage as the hinge of Achaemenid expansion to the east.

## The voyage

Herodotus (IV.44) is the source: "Wishing to know where the River Indus discharges
into the sea, [Darius] sent with ships persons on whom he relied... and in particular
Scylax, a man of Caryanda." The expedition set out from **Caspatyrus in Pactyica** —
a corrupt name that Bivar restores as **Paskapyrus**, i.e. the region of **Peshawar**,
where the Kabul River is navigable a little above the city. From there Scylax sailed
**east** to the Indus confluence, down through the **Attock gorge** (whose towering
cliffs a fragment of his own account, preserved in Athenaeus, describes) into the
Punjab plain, and so to the **Indian Ocean**; then **west** across the sea, arriving
in the thirtieth month at the point from which an earlier Phoenician expedition had
been sent to circumnavigate Africa — i.e. the Red Sea / Egypt. "After they had made
the transit, Darius subjugated the Indians and made use of this sea."

## Significance

- **Enabler of empire.** The voyage opened the Indus to Persian power: Darius
  organized the province of **Hindus** (c. 515 BCE) on the strength of it, extending
  the [[achaemenid-empire|Achaemenid empire]] to its furthest eastern limit (see
  [[gandhara]]).
- **A landmark of Greek geography.** Scylax's report entered the Greek tradition
  through **Hecataeus of Miletus** and **Herodotus**, shaping the earliest Greek
  picture of India and the Indian Ocean. A later *Periplus* (a coastal survey of the
  Mediterranean and Black Sea) circulated under his name, though the surviving text is
  a 4th-century compilation, not his own work.
- **A Greek serving the Great King.** Scylax exemplifies the many **East Greeks who
  served Persia** in the generation before the [[ionian-revolt|Ionian Revolt]] —
  engineers, navigators, physicians and exiles moving freely through the empire, a
  reminder that "Greek and Barbarian" were entangled long before they collided in
  battle.

## Causal Analysis

contributed_to: Darius's conquest and organization of the **Hindus satrapy**; the
Greek geographical tradition of [[gandhara|India]] and the Indian Ocean.
enabled: the Achaemenid use of the Indus–Red Sea sea route (paired with Darius's
roughly contemporary completion of a **Nile–Red Sea canal**).

## Historiography

Scylax is known almost entirely from the single Herodotean notice and a few
fragments; the geography of his starting-point was long garbled by scribal corruption
(*Caspatyrus / Caspapyrus / Paskapyrus*), which Bivar resolves using a Sasanian-era
attestation of *Peshawar*. The voyage's reality is generally accepted, but its exact
route, duration ("thirty months"), and the relationship of the named *Periplus* to
the historical navigator remain debated.
