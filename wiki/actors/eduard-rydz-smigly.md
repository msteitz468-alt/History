# Edward Rydz-Śmigły

Marshal of Poland and, from 1935, Commander-in-Chief of the Polish Armed Forces, sharing de facto leadership of the Second Polish Republic with President Ignacy Mościcki after Piłsudski's death left a "colonels' regime" in charge. Rydz-Śmigły oversaw Polish military modernization efforts in the late 1930s that nonetheless left Poland's forces badly outmatched in mechanization and airpower against Germany. He directed Polish resistance to the German invasion of September 1939, but as the front collapsed under combined German pressure and, from 17 September, Soviet invasion from the east, he ordered a general withdrawal toward the Romanian Bridgehead and ultimately fled with the government into Romanian internment. He returned clandestinely to German-occupied Poland in 1941 under a false identity to join the underground resistance, and died in Warsaw later that year.

## Counterfactual Significance

Rydz-Śmigły's prewar "Plan Zachód" defensive strategy — attempting to hold Poland's entire long frontier rather than falling back to a defensible line — is widely judged by military historians to have contributed to the speed of the 1939 collapse; a different defensive posture might have prolonged, though probably not altered, the campaign's outcome.

## Historiography

Polish and Western historiography of the September 1939 campaign generally treats Poland's defeat as structurally overdetermined by the geography and force imbalance involved, while still debating whether Rydz-Śmigły's specific dispositions worsened an already dire strategic position.
