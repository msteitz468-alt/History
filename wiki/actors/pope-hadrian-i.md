# Pope Hadrian I (r. 772–795)

**Hadrian I** was pope throughout the formative quarter-century of
[[charlemagne|Charles the Great]]'s reign (772–795). His appeal against the Lombard king
[[desiderius]] precipitated the [[carolingian-conquest-of-lombard-italy-774|Frankish conquest
of Italy in 774]], and his pontificate established the working relationship between the
Carolingian monarchy and the Papacy that the [[coronation-of-charlemagne-800|imperial
coronation of 800]] would later crown. He died on 25 December 795 — five years to the day
before that coronation.

## Charles and Hadrian, per Nelson

[[sources/nelson-king-and-emperor-2019|Janet Nelson]] presents the relationship as warmer in
ideology than in practice:

- Charles's **Easter 774 visit to Rome** was entirely his own initiative; Hadrian was
  "thrown into great bewilderment and consternation" at the unannounced arrival of a Frankish
  army's king. Charles's devotion to St Peter was material and intense — the confessio "like
  a numinous oven," the donation-document left in it "like a battery on permanent charge."
- The **Donation of Quierzy** was "the very reverse of a watertight legal … document":
  Hadrian envisioned "whole territories," while Charles's [[missi-dominici|missi]] claimed
  only "estates within larger regions" — a structural mismatch from the start.
- After 774 Charles's agents played "a cat-and-mouse game with Hadrian," cultivating Spoleto
  and Ravenna at the pope's expense; Hadrian's letters of 775–6 are "near-hysterical," quoting
  the taunt that the Lombard conquest brought the papacy nothing: "not one of the promises
  made has been kept." By 776 Hadrian accepted "a more circumscribed but autonomous" position.
- Hadrian gave Charles the **Dionysio-Hadriana** canon-law collection with a bespoke
  dedicatory acrostic ("CARULO MAGNO REGI"), which Nelson argues had lasting impact, feeding
  the [[carolingian-capitularies|Admonitio generalis]] of 789.
