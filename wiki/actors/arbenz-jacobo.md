# Jacobo Árbenz

## Overview
Jacobo Árbenz, formerly Guatemala's defense minister, won the 1950 presidential election with 64% of the vote. In 1952 he enacted an agrarian reform law expropriating uncultivated United Fruit Company land — roughly 400,000 acres from the company, part of 1.4 million acres redistributed in total to about 100,000 campesino families by 1954, compensated at United Fruit's own undervalued declared tax value. He was overthrown by the CIA's Operation PBSUCCESS and resigned June 27, 1954.

## Key Decisions and Consequences
- The 1952 agrarian reform directly triggered United Fruit Company's lobbying campaign in Washington, which — through personal ties to the Eisenhower administration — helped produce the CIA-organized coup that removed him.
- His government suspended civil liberties and arrested hundreds, killing at least 75 suspected subversives (McCoy's figure), in its final weeks — a defensive response to the mounting coup pressure.

## Counterfactual Significance
Had the 1952 land reform survived, Guatemala's subsequent political trajectory — including the 1960–1996 civil war that killed an estimated 200,000 people — might have unfolded very differently, though McCoy is careful to present the coup-to-civil-war link as his own interpretive thesis rather than settled consensus (see [[mccoy-covert-empire-thesis]]). Árbenz's overthrow, alongside [[mossadegh-mohammad|Mosaddegh's]], became a template the CIA reused with much less success at the [[bay-of-pigs-1961|Bay of Pigs]].

## Historiography
McCoy's account of Árbenz draws on the CIA's own official history and the memoirs of E. Howard Hunt and David Atlee Phillips. Árbenz spent 72 days in the Mexican embassy after resigning, was humiliated by the Guatemalan military at the airport, and died years later in Mexico City, "possibly by suicide" (McCoy), after fifteen years of CIA surveillance and denial of the right to speak, work, or publish. Source: Alfred W. McCoy, *Cold War on Five Continents* (2024).
