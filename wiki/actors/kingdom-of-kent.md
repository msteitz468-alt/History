# Kingdom of Kent

**Kent** was the south-easternmost Anglo-Saxon kingdom, the first to convert to
Christianity and, under [[æthelberht-of-kent|Æthelberht]] (d. 616), the seat of the
earliest English overlordship recorded by Bede. Its wealth rested on cross-Channel trade
with Merovingian Gaul — to which it was bound by Æthelberht's
marriage to the Frankish princess Bertha — and its early law-code (the earliest surviving
in any Germanic vernacular) and the reception of the [[augustine-mission-597|Augustinian
mission]] in 597 made it the cradle of the English church, with [[canterbury|Canterbury]]
becoming the metropolitan see.

After Æthelberht's death a [[eadbald-of-kent|pagan reaction under Eadbald]] briefly checked
the new faith. Through the seventh and eighth centuries Kent's independent power waned as it
fell within the orbit first of Northumbrian and then of [[mercia|Mercian]] overlords;
[[offa-of-mercia|Offa]] ruled it directly for a time. West Saxon victory at the
[[battle-of-ellendun-825|Battle of Ellendun (825)]] brought it finally under
[[kingdom-of-wessex|Wessex]].

This is the network summary; for the dynasty's individual kings see
[[æthelberht-of-kent|Æthelberht]] and [[eadbald-of-kent|Eadbald]].
