# Napoleon Bonaparte

**Napoleon Bonaparte** (1769–1821) — First Consul (1799) and then **Emperor of the French**
(1804–14/15) — was the soldier-statesman who ended the [[french-revolution-1789|French
Revolution]], built a continental empire, and reshaped European law, administration, and
warfare. The 1907 *Cambridge Modern History* devotes its whole ninth volume to him as a figure
whose single personality dominated his age as few others "except those of Alexander the Great,
of Julius Caesar, and of Charlemagne" — and, unlike Charles V or Louis XIV, "the architect of
his own fortunes." From [[cambridge-modern-history-v9-napoleon|*The Cambridge Modern History*,
Vol. IX]] and [[sources/chandler-the-campaigns-of-napoleon-1966|*The Campaigns of Napoleon* (Chandler, 1966)]].

## Career

- **Rise.** A Corsican artillery officer who made his name in the Revolutionary Wars (Toulon
  1793, the Italian campaign 1796–97, Egypt 1798), Napoleon seized power in the **coup of 18
  Brumaire (1799)**, becoming First Consul and then **Emperor (1804)**.
- **The Napoleonic state.** He consolidated the Revolution's gains in a centralized,
  authoritarian order: the **Civil Code (Code Napoléon, 1804)** — equality before the law,
  property rights, secular civil law (but restored paternal authority and limited women's
  rights); the **Concordat (1801)** reconciling the Church to the state; the prefects, the Bank
  of France, the *lycées* and Imperial University, and a meritocratic service nobility.
- **Conquest and fall.** Through the **[[napoleonic-wars]]** he dominated Europe (Austerlitz
  1805, Jena 1806, Tilsit 1807), redrawing the map and spreading the Code and revolutionary
  institutions. Overreach — the **Continental System**, the **Peninsular War**, and the
  catastrophic **invasion of Russia (1812)** — broke his power; defeated in 1814 and again at
  **Waterloo (1815)**, he died in British exile on **St Helena (1821)**.

## Counterfactual significance

Napoleon is the archetypal test of the **Great Man vs. structural** question. He did not create
the Revolution or the levée en masse, but he gave them durable institutional form (the Code
shaped legal systems across Europe and Latin America) and, by conquest, **spread revolutionary
principles and provoked the nationalisms** — German, Spanish, Russian — that would define the
nineteenth century. His empire was fleeting; his administrative and legal legacy was permanent.

## Significance

Napoleon ended the revolutionary decade and fixed much of its work in lasting institutions,
exported the [[french-revolution-1789|Revolution]] across Europe by force, drove the
[[balance-of-power|balance of power]] to its great crisis, and — through the reaction to him —
helped birth modern **[[nationalism]]** and the Restoration order of 1815.
