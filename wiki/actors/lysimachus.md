## Role

Lysimachus (c. 360–281 BCE), a Macedonian bodyguard of [[alexander-the-great|Alexander]],
held **Thrace** through the [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323|wars of the Successors]] and rose
after [[battle-of-ipsus-301|Ipsus]] to rule a large kingdom astride the Straits — Thrace,
much of Anatolia, and eventually Macedon — before losing it all in a single battle.

## Decisions and Significance

Given Thrace at Babylon (323) — a frontier command, not a satrapy, detached from
[[antipater|Antipater's]] Europe — Lysimachus spent two decades in grinding war against
the Thracian and Getic tribes, which kept him peripheral to the early struggles though
he controlled the land-bridge between Macedon and Asia. He joined the coalitions against
both [[perdiccas]] and [[antigonus-i-monophthalmus|Antigonus]], took the royal title
in 305 in imitation of [[ptolemy-i-soter|Ptolemy]], and at **[[battle-of-ipsus-301|Ipsus]]**
(301) — with [[seleucus-i-nicator|Seleucus]] — crushed Antigonus, gaining Asia Minor to
the Taurus. He founded **Lysimacheia** on the Chersonese as his capital and for a time
(after 285) held Macedon itself, the high point of his power.

His end was swift: domestic tragedy (the judicial murder of his able son Agathocles,
under the influence of his wife [[arsinoe-ii|Arsinoe II]] — the dynastic "vipers' knot")
alienated his subjects and invited Seleucus' intervention. At
**[[battle-of-corupedium-281|Corupedium]]** (281) Lysimachus was defeated and killed;
his kingdom dissolved. His Anatolian treasure at Pergamum, left in the keeping of the
officer Philetaerus, became the seed of the later kingdom of [[pergamon|Pergamum]].

## Counterfactual Significance

Lysimachus shows the fragility of a kingdom built on personal authority and a single
field army: the most extensive realm of 281 evaporated at one battle and one dynastic
blunder. His career also seeds two later powers — Macedon (which passed via Ceraunus and
chaos to the [[antigonid-dynasty|Antigonids]]) and Pergamum (from his abandoned
treasure). See [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323]].
