# Theophilus of Edessa

A **Maronite Christian scholar and astrologer** (c. 695–785) at the **Abbasid court** of the caliph
**al-Mahdi**, and the author of a lost chronicle that became the **"common source" behind Greek, Syriac
and Arabic histories** of the early Islamic Near East — reconstructed in
**[[hoyland-theophilus-edessa-chronicle|Theophilus of Edessa's Chronicle]]**.

## Life and Work

Theophilus was a learned **Maronite** of Edessa who rose to be **court astrologer to al-Mahdi**
(r. 775–785), translating Greek works (including, it is said, Homer) and writing on astrology. His
**chronicle**, composed in Syriac and carrying events down to his own day (c. 750s), does not survive,
but its content was taken up independently by the Greek **Theophanes the Confessor**, the Arabic
**Agapius of Manbij**, and the Syriac **Dionysius of Tel-Mahre** — allowing modern scholars to
reconstruct it as the shared "**eastern source**."

## Significance

Theophilus matters as a **transmitter** across the language-frontiers of the early medieval Near East:
his chronicle fed historiography in three languages and is one of the earliest, best-informed **Christian
accounts of the Arab conquests and the Umayyad century**, written from inside the Caliphate and
independent of the Muslim tradition — and therefore a key control for historians of early Islam.
