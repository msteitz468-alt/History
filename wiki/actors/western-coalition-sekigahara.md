## Definition and Scope

The Western Coalition (*Nishi-gun*) was the coalition of daimyo assembled by [[ishida-mitsunari|Ishida Mitsunari]] in 1600 to oppose [[tokugawa-ieyasu|Tokugawa Ieyasu's]] consolidation of power following [[toyotomi-hideyoshi|Toyotomi Hideyoshi's]] death. It fought the [[eastern-coalition-sekigahara|Eastern Coalition]] at the [[battle-of-sekigahara-1600|Battle of Sekigahara]] on October 21, 1600 and was decisively defeated.

The coalition presented itself as defending the Toyotomi succession on behalf of the young Hideyori, though in practice it was held together by a combination of personal loyalty to Ishida's cause, opposition to Tokugawa dominance, and in several cases opportunism or coercion.

## Composition

**Nominal commander-in-chief**: Mori Terumoto (remained in Osaka; did not appear at the battle)

**Active Western commanders at Sekigahara**:
- Ishida Mitsunari — right wing, operational director
- Ukita Hideie — center (large division)
- Konishi Yukinaga — center
- Shimazu Yoshihiro and Toyohisa — left wing
- Otani Yoshitsugu — Nakasendo right, a leper but the Western army's most capable battlefield general
- Shima Sakon — Ishida's chief strategist; wounded early and forced to retire
- Hiratsuka Tameie, Toda Shigemasa, Akaza Naoyasu, Ogawa Suketada, Wakizaka Yasuharu, Kuchigi Mototsuna — right wing alongside Otani
- Kobayakawa Hideaki — Matsuo Hill (right flank; secretly in contact with Ieyasu)
- Nagatsuka Masaie, Ankokuji Eikei, Chosokabe Morichika, Kikkawa Hiroie, Mori Hidemoto — Nangu Hill (southwest; Kikkawa had agreed not to fight)
- Oda Hidenobu — held Gifu (fell before Sekigahara)

**Significant forces not present at the battle**:
- Mori Terumoto (30,000 men; remained in Osaka)
- Uesugi Kagekatsu (contained in the north by Date and Mogami)
- Western forces besieging Tanabe: 15,000 men tied down by Hosokawa Yusai
- Western forces besieging Otsu: 15,000 men under Tachibana Muneshige (arrived too late)
- Forces in Ise under Mori Hidemoto and Chosokabe (recalled but arrived too late)

## Internal Contradictions

The coalition was structurally compromised before the battle. Several key commanders had secretly agreed with Ieyasu not to fight or to actively assist the East:

- **Kikkawa Hiroie** (commanding Mori's vanguard on Nangu Hill) had arranged with Ieyasu to remain passive. He did nothing during the battle, blocking the rest of Mori's forces from engaging. When Shimazu's retreating troops reported the battle lost, the entire Nangu Hill force withdrew without having struck a blow.
- **Kobayakawa Hideaki** (Matsuo Hill) had been approached by Ieyasu before the battle. He remained passive even after Ishida's signal fire and multiple messengers. Only when Ieyasu ordered matchlock fire directed at his position did he attack — but he attacked Otani rather than the East, deciding the battle.
- **Wakizaka Yasuharu, Ogawa Suketada, and Kuchigi Mototsuna** (positioned by Otani on his own right flank as insurance against Kobayakawa) also turned against the Western army once Kobayakawa moved, completing the encirclement of Otani.

The coalition also lacked a unified command. Shimazu Yoshihiro refused all requests to support the center during the battle, maintaining that "each leader should fight his own battle." Mori Terumoto never left Osaka. Ankokuji Eikei, who had been the diplomatic agent who brought Mori into the coalition, proved to be "no soldier, but only a diplomat and schemer" and took no part in the fighting.

## Aftermath

The coalition's defeat resulted in catastrophic domain redistributions. The ninety smaller lords who had supported the West lost their domains entirely. The largest domain holders were severely reduced:
- Mori Terumoto: reduced from 1,205,000 to 369,000 koku (losing all western provinces except Suwo and Nagato)
- Uesugi Kagekatsu: transferred from Aizu (1,200,000 koku) to Yonezawa (300,000 koku)
- Satake: moved from Hitachi to Akita, losing 339,000 koku

Of the coalition's leaders, Ishida Mitsunari, Konishi Yukinaga, and Ankokuji Eikei were executed. Ukita Hideie was exiled. Shimazu was treated relatively leniently (allowed to retain his revenues) due to geography and the balance of power in Kyushu. Kobayakawa was rewarded for his defection with 574,000 koku in Bizen.
