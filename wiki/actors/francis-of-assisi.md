# Francis of Assisi (c. 1181–1226)

## Overview

**Francis of Assisi** founded the **[[franciscan-order|Order of Friars Minor]]**, the most influential expression of the early-thirteenth-century turn to **apostolic poverty** and itinerant preaching. From a merchant family in Assisi, his renunciation of wealth and embrace of a life “without property” answered the same lay hunger for evangelical simplicity that also fed [[waldensians|Waldensian]] and related dissent — but Francis stayed inside the church through papal approval under [[innocent-iii|Innocent III]].

## Role and Significance

Logan pairs the rise of the friars with the emergence of dissent: both sprang from a laity disturbed by clerical affluence and seeking a Christ who had “nowhere to lay his head.” Francis is the positive institutionalisation of that impulse; the [[mendicant-orders|mendicants]] became the church’s answer to heresy and urban pastoral need.

## From Logan, History of the Church in the Middle Ages (2013)

Born **1181** into a comfortable Assisi bourgeois family; fought for Assisi against Perugia **1202** (prisoner, ransomed, illness); abandoned a military expedition at Spoleto **1205** after a dream; lived as a solitary at **San Damiano** and in Umbrian caves, befriended lepers. On the feast of **St Matthias (24 February 1208)**, the gospel of Matthew 10:7–10 moved him to remove his shoes, don a rough cloak and knotted rope, and commit to literal gospel poverty and itinerant penance preaching — “like Waldès but without heresy taint.” His *Testament* later claimed: “The most high God directed me to live the life of the gospel. This I had written down in brief and simple words, and his holiness the pope confirmed it.”

By summer **1209** eight (then twelve) friars minor sought approval; in **1210** Francis walked barefoot to Rome. “Exactly what Innocent did is shrouded in mist” — oral encouragement or primitive-rule approval despite misgivings about non-possession. Whitsun **1217** chapter at the **Portiuncula** planned twelve provinces (half outside Italy). Francis sailed East **1219** for the Fifth Crusade and preached to the sultan under truce without converts; on return **1220** he resigned authority titles through illness. First Rule **1221**; Second Rule **1223** under Honorius codified “obedience, without property and in chastity” — seeds of later poverty disputes. He died at the Portiuncula **October 1226**, body marked by stigmata. Logan’s chapter thesis: “The voluntary poverty of Waldès and others like him was shared, in a strikingly similar way, by Francis of Assisi” — the papacy distinguished condemnation of some from institutionalization of others.
