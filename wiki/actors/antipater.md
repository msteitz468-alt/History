## Role

Antipater was the old Macedonian statesman whom [[alexander-the-great|Alexander]] left
as *strategos* — effective regent — of Macedon and Greece during the eastern campaign,
and after Alexander's death the most authoritative representative of dynastic
legitimacy among the [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323|Successors]]. A contemporary and loyal
adherent of [[philip-ii-of-macedon|Philip II]], he embodied prudence, wisdom, and
"unrelenting energy"; Will judges that without Antipater's vigilance in Europe
"Alexander's adventure would have been impossible."

## Decisions and Significance

- **The [[lamian-war-323|Lamian War]] (323–322)**: besieged in Lamia by the Greek
  revolt, he was rescued by Leonnatus and Craterus and, after the victories at Crannon
  and [[battle-of-amorgos|Amorgos]], crushed the rising — ending Athenian independence
  and imposing oligarchy.
- **Regent of the empire**: after [[perdiccas|Perdiccas']] death he was made regent
  (*epimeletes* of the kings) at **Triparadisus** (321) and withdrew the boy-kings to
  Macedon. Will sees this transfer of the empire's theoretical centre back to Europe —
  by a very old man with no sympathy for Asian affairs — as ominous: it accentuated the
  break between Macedon and the conquered East and let separatism run unchecked.
- **The fatal succession**: passing over his capable son [[cassander]] in favour of the
  older officer [[polyperchon]], Antipater (legally, he had no right to name a successor
  at all) `produced` the very civil war between Cassander and Polyperchon that consumed
  Macedon after his death in 319.

Antipater is the last figure of Philip's generation, and his death removed the one
restraint of personal authority and tradition on the marshals' ambitions. His daughters
(Phila, Nicaea, Eurydice) were woven through the dynastic marriage-politics of the age.
See [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323]].
