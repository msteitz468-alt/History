# Horatio Gates (1727–1806)

The American general whose career bracketed the war's greatest victory and one of its worst
defeats. A former British officer, Gates commanded the Northern Department in 1777 and presided
over the triumph at **[[battles-of-saratoga-1777|Saratoga]]**: he chose the strong defensive
position at Bemis Heights and "recognized that the moment called for steadiness rather than
swashbuckling," accepting [[burgoyne-john|Burgoyne]]'s surrender. (His public feud with
[[arnold-benedict|Benedict Arnold]], whose battlefield aggression he omitted from his report,
"redounded to little credit on either side.") The victory made Gates a rival to
[[washington-george|Washington]] for command.

Given the southern army in 1780, Gates marched it to ruin at **[[battle-of-camden-1780|Camden]]**
(August 1780): he placed raw militia opposite British regulars, watched his line collapse, and
fled 170 miles in three days. He was quickly replaced by [[greene-nathanael|Greene]]. The
*West Point History* judges he had been "promoted beyond what his experience merited."
