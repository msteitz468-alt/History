# György Dózsa (c. 1470–1514)

Szekler (Székely) border warrior and leader of the 1514 Hungarian "peasant crusade" that began as an anti-Ottoman expedition and became a major social revolt against the nobility.

## Role

Dózsa's personal military reputation (single combat victory over a Turkish champion) helped legitimate his leadership of the crusader host. Once the crusade turned inward, he became the symbolic head of a movement that fused religious war language with peasant grievances. His spectacular execution (mock "passion" or crucifixion) made him a lasting figure in Hungarian memory.

## Historiography

Housley uses Dózsa as a key example of the unstable boundary between external crusade and internal religious/social warfare on the Ottoman frontier. The episode shows how crusade institutions and rhetoric could be repurposed or inverted. National and Marxist historiographies have alternately celebrated or condemned him as social revolutionary or dangerous demagogue.
