# Erlitou Culture

The **Erlitou culture** (~1900–1500 BC) of the [[yiluo-basin|Yiluo basin]] in western Henan
is, in [[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's]] argument, the **first archaeologically
demonstrable state in China** — independent of whether it was the
[[xia-dynasty|Xia dynasty]]. It marks the threshold of the Chinese **Early Bronze Age** and
a qualitative break from the cyclical [[longshan-culture|Longshan]] chiefdoms that preceded
it. Named after the [[erlitou|Erlitou site]] (Yanshi), discovered in **1959**, the culture
is divided into four ceramic phases of roughly a century each; >200 sites are known from the
middle Yellow River to the middle Yangzi.

## Why Liu Calls It a State

Liu measures statehood by **settlement hierarchy and internal administrative
specialisation**, not by an artifact checklist (see [[state-formation]]). Erlitou qualifies
where Longshan polities did not:

- A **four-tier settlement hierarchy** with a **primate** rank-size curve: one major center,
  [[erlitou|Erlitou]] (**300 ha**, the largest of all contemporaries); two minor centers,
  Shaochai (60 ha) and Fucun (40 ha); ~11 large villages (15–25 ha); and many small
  villages. This degree of centralisation was "new to the Yellow River region."
- A **palace/temple complex** of 30+ rammed-earth structures, with attached **bronze-casting
  workshops** producing piece-mold ritual vessels — Erlitou is the **only** contemporary
  site to manufacture bronze ritual vessels, found only in its own elite burials. Control of
  the production and distribution of these sacred prestige goods "was the essence of the
  Erlitou ruling elite's political power."
- A core population of **18,000–30,000** in the city in Phase III, and **54,000–82,000** in
  the ~5,689 km² Yiluo core — an order of magnitude beyond any Longshan polity.

## Origins and Expansion

The Erlitou variant developed **locally** from the central-Henan Longshan through the
intermediate **Xinzhai phase**; the Dongxiafeng variant in southern Shanxi instead looks
like an **expansion** of the Erlitou culture from western Henan (local Longshan traditions
there largely vanish). From Phase II–IV, Erlitou established **outposts 100–500 km away** —
Dongxiafeng and Nanguan (near salt and copper), Donglongshan, Panlongcheng (Hubei copper) —
near strategic resources. A spike in projectile points in Phase III suggests this expansion
was **coercive**. Liu characterises the system as a **"world system"** with a dominant core
controlling prestige-goods production and a periphery supplying raw materials (metal ingots,
salt), and as a **[[territorial-state|territorial state]]** in Trigger's sense. Imported
Indian-Ocean cowries and Central-Asian-style artifacts reach its elite burials. Phase IV
shows clear decline before the site continued, much reduced, into the early Shang.

The transition from Longshan to Erlitou compresses ~700 sites to ~200, grows the largest
site from 50 to 300 ha, shifts from 2–3 settlement tiers to four, replaces competing
polities with one dominant center, and adds **bronze** to jade as a status marker.

## Historiography

The Erlitou=Xia identification is the central interpretive dispute of early Chinese
archaeology — see [[xia-dynasty-historicity]]. Liu's settlement-and-mortuary case for Erlitou
statehood is designed to be **robust to that question**: the city is a state whether or not
the Xia existed. The earliest unmistakable state level appears in **Erlitou Phase II (~1800
BC)** — later than the traditional ~2100 BC Xia founding. Cross-refs:
[[cambridge-history-of-ancient-china]], [[maisels-early-civilizations-of-the-old-world-1999]].

[[major-cook-ancient-china-2017|Major & Cook (2017)]] reinforce the agnostic reading: preferring
the neutral label **"Erlitou Period"** to "Xia," they note there is **no datable written reference
to the Xia before the fourth century BCE** and the name appears on **none** of the tens of
thousands of Shang oracle bones (see [[xia-dynasty-historicity]]). They add texture on Erlitou's
ritual culture: standardised bronze **[[oracle-bone-divination|ding and jue]]** ritual vessels;
**un-inscribed** pig/sheep oracle bones; turquoise-mosaic **dragon plaques** on the chests of the
elite dead; and an **archaeo-astronomical** signature — palace foundations oriented a few degrees
**west of north** (using the obsolescent polestar **Thuban**, vs. the Shang's eastward shift to
Kochab), and a **five-planet conjunction of 1953 BCE** that may have served as a legitimating omen.
**Source:** [[major-cook-ancient-china-2017]].

**Barnes (2015) adds:** bronze-casting began at Erlitou in **Phase II (~1750 BC)**, first in bivalve
stone moulds, then with the invented ceramic **piece-mould** — but the earliest piece-mould products
were **bells, not vessels**; vessels were cast only from Phase III, "changing forever the nature of
elite interaction on the Mainland" and making Erlitou "the first Bronze Age culture in the northern
Mainland." Almost all of its turquoise, jade and bronze was both produced and consumed on site, and
**no king's tomb has yet been found**. She treats the Erlitou=Xia identification as contested, not
established. See [[east-asian-bronze-metallurgy]]. **Source:** [[barnes-archaeology-east-asia-2015]].
