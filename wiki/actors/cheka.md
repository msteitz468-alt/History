# The Cheka
## Biography / Description
The **Cheka** (derived from the Russian abbreviation *ChK* for the All-Russian Extraordinary Commission to Combat Counter-Revolution, Speculation, and Sabotage) was the first Soviet state security and secret police organization. It was established on December 20, 1917, by a decree of the Council of People's Commissars and was headed by the Polish Bolshevik Felix Dzerzhinsky. Operating from its headquarters in Lubyanka, Moscow, the Cheka was created to defend the new Bolshevik regime against political opponents, spies, and economic saboteurs. It was dissolved in February 1922, with its functions transferred to the State Political Directorate (GPU).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **Establishment of Political Terror:** The Cheka was the primary instrument of the "Red Terror" launched in September 1918 following an assassination attempt on Lenin. It executed thousands of suspected counterrevolutionaries, striking miners, and rebellious peasants without trial.
- **Suppression of Uprisings:** The Cheka participated in crushing internal revolts against Bolshevik rule, including the Left Socialist Revolutionary uprising in July 1918, the Yaroslavl rebellion, and strikes in Petrograd.
- **Economic Regulation:** During the period of War Communism, the Cheka enforced grain requisitions and targeted black-market traders, wholesalers, and speculators.

## Historical Significance
The Cheka established the precedent of state security agencies operating outside the bounds of law and regular courts in the Soviet state. Riasanovsky highlights that from its inception, "the political police became a fundamental reality of Soviet life" (Line 5740). It created a pervasive climate of fear that enabled the consolidation of Bolshevik power. Its methods, personnel, and institutional culture laid the foundation for all subsequent Soviet state security organs, including the OGPU, NKVD, and KGB.
