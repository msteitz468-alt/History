# Seqenenra Taa

**Seqenenra Taa** (c.1560 BCE; now thought identical with Senakhtenra Taa) was the Theban
king of the **17th Dynasty** who appears to have begun the armed struggle against the
[[hyksos|Hyksos]] — and who died, on the forensic evidence, fighting it.

## The violently killed king

Seqenenra's mummy, recovered from the Deir el-Bahri royal cache, shows that he "died by
violence": a horizontal axe cut across the forehead, a shattered cheekbone, and a dagger
thrust to the back of the neck. Crucially, "the shape of the forehead wound is consistent
only with the use of an axe of Middle Bronze Age type, similar to those found at
[[places/avaris|Tell el-Dabʿa]]" — an Asiatic, not Egyptian, weapon. This is, in the
volume's words, "the most telling evidence so far that a major battle against the Hyksos
took place in Seqenenra's reign — one in which the king himself was brutally slaughtered."

## The quarrel of Apepi and Seqenenra

A 19th-Dynasty literary tale (composed ~350 years later, under Merenptah) preserves a
fictionalized **quarrel** between Seqenenra and the Hyksos king [[hyksos|Apepi]], opening
with Apepi's absurd complaint that the roaring of the Theban hippopotami was keeping him
awake at Avaris — a story that styles Seqenenra merely "the Prince of the Southern City"
against Apepi the King, capturing the political subordination the Thebans resented. During
his reign a forward military palace was built at **Deir el-Ballas**, stocked with weapons
and Nubian (Kerma) soldiers. His wife was the formidable Queen **Ahhotep**.
