# Sir Ivor Maxse (1862–1958)

Senior British Army officer and one of the leading tactical reformers in the BEF during the First World War. Commanded the 18th (Eastern) Division on the Somme; later played a major role in training and doctrine as Inspector-General of Training.

Griffith highlights Maxse’s papers and practical work as central to the dissemination of improved infantry tactics, small-unit all-arms coordination, and realistic training methods in 1917–18.

Linked to the evolution of platoon tactics (SS 143 era), training schools, and the broader argument that the BEF as an institution—not just isolated “élite” formations—drove tactical progress.
