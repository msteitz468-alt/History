# Mi'kmaq (Micmac)

Algonquian people of the Maritimes (Mi’kma’ki). Early and intensive partners in European fisheries and fur trade (Trigger & Swagerty; Salisbury).

## Overview

### Sixteenth–early seventeenth century

Dry fishery and Basque whaling brought Micmacs into regular contact; skin trade nearly from the start. By 1580s some bands described as “subtle” and “cruel”—possibly Native competition for European access. Built on French fur ties; used French shallops moving furs, wampum, European goods among Basque/Norman traders, Beothuks, Maliseets, Passamaquoddies, [[abenaki]]. Port Royal (1605) elevated local band and sagamore **Membertou**. Despite disease, economic dependence, and wholesale nominal Christian conversion, allies sought control of eastern Abenaki–European trade. Routed Abenakis at Chouacoet 1607 and again 1615 (Bashaba killed); “inspired fear among coastal Natives as far south as Cape Cod until shortly after the Massachusetts Bay colony was established in 1629.”

### Eighteenth century

After Utrecht (1713) ceded Acadia, Micmacs sought play-off but favored French (familiarity, missionaries, gift reciprocity vs English land-hunger). Attacked Canso 1720; full-scale war 1722 Cape Breton to Kennebec (with Abenakis). Successful harassing Nova Scotia; 1725 peace acknowledged British sovereignty and non-disturbance of settlers while Britain agreed not to disturb Native subsistence/religion. Revolution: Micmacs, Maliseets, Abenakis, Caughnawagas initially leaned rebel (French ties; British Canada failures) conditional on Patriot goods and victories.
