# Justin I

**Justin I** (c. 450–527; r. 518–527) was the Illyrian guards officer who succeeded [[anastasius-i|Anastasius I]] and opened the Justinianic dynasty — uncle and patron of [[justinian|Justinian]].

## Reign (Mitchell)

Mitchell’s Ch 4 treats Justin as the bridge from Anastasius’s surplus state to Justinian’s sole rule: Chalcedonian restoration in church politics, the rise of Justinian as co-architect of policy, and the setup for the law commissions and western ambitions that define the next reign. Detail is compressed relative to Justinian proper; the structural point is dynastic continuity and orthodoxy after Anastasius’s contested reputation.

## Counterfactual significance

Without Justin’s succession, Justinian’s path from *magister* influence to Augustus is blocked; the “Justinianic” legislative and reconquest program has no dynastic launchpad.

## Historiography

Often a footnote to Justinian; Mitchell keeps him as necessary prelude. Survey: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Mitchell 2015]].
