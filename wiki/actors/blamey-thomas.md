# Thomas Blamey

Australian soldier and the country's only Field Marshal, the senior Australian
commander of the Second World War.

## Overview

A staff officer in the First AIF (he served at Gallipoli and on the Western
Front), Blamey rose between the wars and commanded Australian forces in the
Middle East before becoming Commander-in-Chief of the Australian Military Forces
under the Allied command structure of General Douglas MacArthur during the
Pacific war. He directed Australian operations in the
[[kokoda-campaign-1942|Kokoda]] and Papuan campaigns of 1942 and the later
New Guinea and Borneo offensives within [[australia-world-war-2-1939|Australia's
World War 2]].

## Significance

Blamey personified both the strains of a junior partner fighting under American
operational command and the political tensions of high command: his relief of
subordinate commanders during the Kokoda crisis and his fraught relations with
the government and the press made him a controversial figure. He was promoted
Field Marshal in 1950, shortly before his death.
