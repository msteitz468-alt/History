# Brunhild (Brunechildis)

**Visigothic-born queen of [[austrasia|Austrasia]]** (and later regent of Austrasia and Burgundy),
whose decades-long struggle to uphold royal authority against the Frankish aristocracy — and her
murderous feud with the Neustrian queen [[fredegund|Fredegund]] — defined three generations of
Merovingian politics and ended in one of the most notorious executions of the early Middle Ages.

## Overview

Brunhild was a daughter of the Visigothic royal house who married **Sigibert I of Austrasia**. The
murder of her sister **Galswintha** — wife of the Neustrian king Chilperic, killed at the instigation
of [[fredegund|Fredegund]] — touched off "a three-generation feud that wrecked the Merovingian family
and ended only after the deaths of ten kings and the execution of Brunechildis by Chilperic's son
[[chlothar-ii|Chlothar]] in 613."

## A Romanising queen against the aristocracy

Brunhild "attempted to continue [the] Romanization" of Austrasian government — a centralising, fiscal
policy — "with the result that an increasing rift developed between the aristocracy and the monarchy."
After the death of her son Childebert II (596) she tried to rule both [[austrasia|Austrasia]] and
Burgundy as regent for her minor grandsons Theudebert II and Theuderic II. In **599** the Austrasian
aristocracy expelled her; she fled to Theuderic's Burgundy, where she was welcomed.

## Downfall

When Theuderic died, the Burgundian aristocracy betrayed her into the hands of [[chlothar-ii|Chlothar
II]]. Geary records her death in its full brutality: "she was tormented for three days with a diversity
of tortures," then "tied by her hair, one arm and one leg to the tail of an unbroken horse, and she was
cut to shreds by its hoofs." Chlothar's victory was the direct fruit of aristocratic cooperation — the
Austrasian leaders **Arnulf of Metz and Pippin of Herstal** (the future [[pippinids|Pippinids]])
invited him into the kingdom.

## Counterfactual significance

Brunhild personifies the central political struggle of the period: the last sustained Merovingian
attempt to maintain a **Romanising, centralised monarchy** against an aristocracy bent on autonomy. Her
defeat and the manner of her death marked the triumph of the magnates whose ascendancy would, within a
century, hollow out Merovingian kingship and raise the Pippinids — making her, in effect, the queen the
Carolingian future was built against.

## Historiography

The hostile tradition around Brunhild owes much to sources later shaped by Austrasian/Carolingian
sympathies; the gruesome execution narrative should be read with that bias in mind (compare the
manufactured image of the [[rois-faineants|rois fainéants]]). See [[geary-before-france-germany-1988]].
