# The Zapotec (Monte Albán)

## Overview

The **Zapotec** of the **Valley of Oaxaca** were, in [[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (Begun &
Brashler, Ch 20), the key evidence that Mesoamerican complexity did **not** descend from a
single Olmec "Mother Culture" but arose in several regions through interaction and trade. Their
great hilltop capital **Monte Albán** anchored the valley for over a millennium.

## Trade and Early Statehood

During the Preclassic, the Zapotec exchanged finished goods and prized **iron-ore "mirrors"**
(produced in Oaxaca, found in elite [[olmec|Olmec]] contexts on the Gulf Coast) for Olmec
shell and feathers. CWH IV concludes the two cultures **emerged independently** but
**influenced each other** through this interregional trade — the earliest clear case of trade
driving state development in Mesoamerica.

## San José Mogote and the Danzantes

The site of **San José Mogote**, in the valley's northern arm, yields some of the best
evidence for an early **martial elite**. Its **Danzantes** — figures carved in basalt, once
thought to be ritual dancers — are now read as **dismembered, mutilated captives**, attesting
to commonplace and intensifying **warfare**. Many bear an early **Zapotec blood glyph**,
pushing back the evidence for **non-Olmec writing systems** into the Preclassic — among the
earliest writing in the Americas. Power later consolidated at **Monte Albán**, which engaged
**[[teotihuacan|Teotihuacan]]** (probable marriage ties between the two dynasties) through the
Classic period.

## Significance

The Zapotec case is CWH IV's strongest argument for the **"Sister Culture"** reading of
Mesoamerican origins: independent, trade-linked centers co-creating a shared civilization. Their
early writing and calendar place them among the foundational literate societies of the
[[mesoamerican-state-formation|Mesoamerican sequence]].
