# Nguyen Van Minh

Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN) lieutenant general (1929–2000), commander of III Corps and the defense of An Loc during the North Vietnamese Easter Offensive of 1972. When NVA armor and infantry divisions besieged the provincial capital of An Loc from April to June 1972, Minh's III Corps, reinforced by ARVN airborne and ranger units and sustained almost entirely by American air power (B-52 strikes and resupply), held the town against sustained assault, denying North Vietnam a symbolically and strategically important breakthrough toward Saigon.

The successful defense of An Loc, alongside the parallel defenses of Kontum and Quang Tri during the same offensive, was cited by the Nixon administration as evidence that "Vietnamization" — the transfer of the war's ground combat burden to South Vietnamese forces — could succeed provided sufficient American air and logistical support remained available, a conclusion later undercut when that support was withdrawn after the 1973 Paris Peace Accords.

## Significance

The defense of An Loc under Minh's command became one of the most-cited examples of ARVN forces holding ground against a major conventional NVA offensive, though critically dependent on continued American air support that would not be available during the final 1975 offensive.
