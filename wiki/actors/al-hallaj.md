# Al-Ḥallāj

Abū al-Mughīth al-Ḥusayn ibn Manṣūr al-Ḥallāj (c. 858–922), the most famous martyr of [[sufism|Sufism]].
A disciple of the early masters of the [[early-sufism|formative period]], he broke with the prudent,
"sober" Sufism of his teacher [[junayd|Junayd]] by proclaiming the mystical experience openly — above all
in his celebrated cry **"anā al-ḥaqq"** ("I am the Truth/the Real"), understood as an expression of
[[fana-and-baqa|annihilation]] of the self in God. Tried for heresy amid the political tensions of
Abbasid Baghdad, he was imprisoned for years and **executed in 922**.

His death made him the archetype of the lover martyred for divine love — the central symbol of later
mystical poetry from [[rumi|Rūmī]] to the Persian, Turkish, and Indo-Muslim traditions — and the focus of
the enduring Sufi debate between ecstatic utterance (*shaṭḥ*) and sober restraint.
