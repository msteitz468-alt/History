## Overview

Augusto Pinochet was a Chilean general who commanded the army's Santiago garrison before becoming army commander-in-chief in August 1973, following the CIA-encouraged ouster of General Carlos Prats; a CIA file card from March 1972 had already noted him as "involved in coup preparations." He led the military coup of 11 September 1973 that overthrew President [[allende-salvador|Salvador Allende]] (see [[chile-coup-1973]]) and ruled Chile as head of a military dictatorship from 1973 to 1990, remaining commander-in-chief of the army until 1998 and then serving as senator for life. Under his rule, the secret police agency DINA, led by Colonel Manuel Contreras, carried out systematic repression and organized Operation Condor, a transnational assassination and intelligence network with counterparts in Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Paraguay, Peru, and Uruguay. Pinochet's regime also implemented a program of free-market economic restructuring designed by economists ("the Chicago Boys") trained under Milton Friedman, who visited Chile in March 1975 to recommend "shock treatment." A new constitution in 1980 created electoral districts favoring the right; Pinochet lost a 1988 plebiscite on his continued rule by 56% to 44% and stepped down in March 1990. He was arrested in London in 1998 on a Spanish extradition request from Judge Baltasar Garzón, and died in December 2006 facing roughly 300 pending criminal charges.

## Key Decisions

- Led the 11 September 1973 coup against Allende, coordinated with sustained US covert support.
- Authorized the post-coup repression apparatus (National Stadium detentions, the "Caravan of Death," DINA) and the formation of Operation Condor (1975).
- Adopted Chicago Boys neoliberal economic reforms.
- Accepted the result of the 1988 plebiscite and relinquished the presidency in 1990, though he retained military command.

## Counterfactual Significance

Pinochet's rise depended on a specific chain of US-backed interventions (the Schneider assassination, the ouster of Prats) that installed him as army commander shortly before the coup; a different Chilean general in that position might not have acted, or might have produced a different post-coup order. His 17-year rule set the template — both economic (Chicago Boys) and repressive (Operation Condor) — that shaped Chile's and the Southern Cone's subsequent political development.

## Historiography

Alfred W. McCoy's *[[mccoy-cold-war-five-continents-2024|Cold War on Five Continents]]* (2024) documents Pinochet's regime through declassified US records and Chilean truth-commission findings (1991 Rettig Commission: ~3,400 killed/disappeared; 2004 Valech Commission: 38,254 imprisoned and tortured). Kissinger's June 1976 remark to Pinochet — "You did a great service to the West in overthrowing Allende" — is cited by McCoy as evidence of continued US approval well after the coup itself.
