# William Kissam Vanderbilt

William Kissam "Willie K." Vanderbilt (1849–1920). Son of [[actors/vanderbilt-william-h|William H. Vanderbilt]]; husband then ex-husband of [[actors/vanderbilt-alva|Alva]]; father of [[actors/vanderbilt-consuelo|Consuelo]] and of [[actors/vanderbilt-harold-s|Harold Stirling Vanderbilt]]. Railroad heir, yacht owner, builder (with Alva) of the Petit Chateau at 660 Fifth Avenue.

## Narrative

Willie K. supplied the capital and architectural budget for Alva's social campaign. Cooper & Howe portray him as a pleasure-seeking sportsman often secondary to Alva's will: he kept a mistress (Nellie Neustretter figures in divorce gossip); his relationship with Alva collapsed into the 1895 divorce; he still funded the Marlborough settlement and walked Consuelo down the aisle after Alva banned most Vanderbilts from the ceremony. His son Harold later defended the America's Cup; Willie K. himself co-owned *Defender* in the 1895 Cup races.

## Historiography

Social histories often treat him as Alva's wallet; business histories place him among the third-generation railroad directors. Cooper & Howe are firmly in the social register.
