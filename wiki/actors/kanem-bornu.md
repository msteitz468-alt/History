# Kanem (and Kanem-Bornu)

**Kanem** — and its long-lived successor **Bornu** — was the great state of the **Lake Chad** basin and the
**central** pillar of the trans-Saharan world, the eastern counterpart to [[ghana-empire|Ghana]] and
[[mali-empire|Mali]] in the west. Documented for the wiki through the **UNESCO
[[gha-vol3-7th-11th-century|General History of Africa, Vol. III]]** (ch. 15, Lange, "The Chad region as a
crossroads"), Kanem controlled the desert road north to the **Fezzan** and the Mediterranean — and so one
of Africa's most enduring dynasties.

## Origins around Lake Chad

Kanem emerged (c. 9th century) among the **Zaghawa / Kanembu** peoples north-east of Lake Chad, at the
meeting point of the Saharan caravan routes and the Sudanic savanna. Its ruling **Sefuwa (Sayfawa)
dynasty** — which would reign, remarkably, for **about a thousand years** — built power on control of the
**central trans-Saharan trade**: gold, ivory, and above all **enslaved people** sent north across the
desert in exchange for horses, salt, copper, and manufactured goods. The early state contended with the
older **Sao** peoples of the lakeshore.

## Conversion to Islam

Kanem is a textbook case of the [[islamization-of-africa|Islamization of Africa]] **through trade and the
court**: in the **late eleventh century Mai Umme (Hummay)** adopted Islam, making Kanem one of the earliest
Sudanic states with a Muslim dynasty. Islam brought Arabic literacy, diplomatic ties to the wider Muslim
world (and the pilgrimage), and a court culture that the *mais* used to consolidate kingship — while older
sacral-kingship traditions persisted beneath the new faith.

## Kanem to Bornu

Pressure from the rival **Bulala** drove the Sefuwa in the 14th–15th centuries to shift their centre
**south-west of Lake Chad to Bornu**, where the dynasty re-founded itself and endured. **Kanem-Bornu**
reached its apogee under **Mai Idris Alooma** (late 16th c.), who imported firearms and Turkish military
advisers — a rare sub-Saharan power engaging the gunpowder age — before a long decline ended the Sefuwa
line in 1846.

## Significance

Kanem-Bornu is the **longest-lived state in African history** and the central African node of the
trans-Saharan system — proof that the Sahelian "empire of trade and Islam" pattern ran the full width of
the Sudan, not only in the gold-rich west.
