# The League of Corinth

## Overview

The **League of Corinth** (the "Hellenic League") was the federal organization through
which **[[philip-ii-of-macedon|Philip II]]** bound the Greek states after his victory at
**[[battle-of-chaeronea-338bce|Chaeronea]]** — the instrument that turned military
hegemony into a legal **Macedonian protectorate over Greece**.
[[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 15, Ellis) describes its constitution in detail.

## A new kind of Common Peace

Summoned to **Corinth** in winter/spring **337**, delegates of all the Greek states (all
but **[[sparta]]**, which stood aloof) ratified a **Common Peace** modelled on the
[[kings-peace-386bce|King's Peace]] of 386 — but differing in two decisive ways:
- its **guarantor was Philip**, "and not the barbarian King," its executor "a properly
  constituted general synod of all signatories";
- it **confirmed an existing state of peace** rather than imposing one, freezing the
  settlement Philip had arranged after Chaeronea.

Members were represented in the **synod** by votes "proportional to their achievable
military contribution"; each bound itself to **non-aggression** against the others, to
**uphold existing constitutions**, and to forswear internal revolution — "unlawful
execution, expulsion or confiscation, redistribution of land, debt cancellation, or
emancipation of slaves." The synod would arbitrate disputes, elect a commander, and levy
forces. A standing committee of *proedroi* was established and **Philip elected
*hegemon*** of the whole.

## The war on Persia

The League's **first act** was to resolve that "the Hellenes ought now to **punish Persia**
for the destruction of their sanctuaries 150 years earlier" — the
[[march-of-the-ten-thousand-401bce|panhellenist programme]] of Isocrates given legal form.
Ellis notes the realism beneath the romance: the stated aim of avenging Xerxes' impiety was
chiefly "a formal means of **activating the new Hellenic League**" for a war that promised
Macedon wealth, the army occupation, and the prestige of **liberating the Greeks of Asia
Minor**. The synod conferred full authority on its hegemon and the offensive was prepared —
only for Philip to be assassinated (336), after which the League confirmed
**[[alexander-the-great|Alexander]]** as his successor as hegemon.

## Significance

The League of Corinth is the **constitutional expression of the end of the free polis**: it
preserved the *forms* of city-state autonomy while subordinating all real power to the
Macedonian king. It became the framework through which Alexander controlled the Greek
mainland during his Asian campaigns, and the device by which his Persian war was waged "in
the name of the Greeks." Its collapse after Alexander's death (the **Lamian War**, 323–322)
confirmed that Greek freedom, in any meaningful sense, had ended at Chaeronea.

## Sparta's holdout and the Megalopolis revolt (331)

[[osborne-classical-greece-2000|Osborne (ed.), *Classical Greece 500–323 BC* (2000)]]
confirms that **[[sparta]] refused to join the League of Corinth** from the outset and
did not join the anti-Macedonian uprisings that followed Philip's assassination. Sparta
finally moved in **331**, when Persian naval successes in the Aegean (333/2) suggested
Alexander might yet be defeated and Spartan overtures for revolt gained traction; the
Macedonian regent **Antipater** crushed the rising in a battle at **Megalopolis (331)**
that left, on Osborne's figure, "some thousands" dead (a total of 5,300 is given in the
source but should be treated as an approximate order of magnitude rather than an exact
count). Athens notably did **not** join Sparta's 331 revolt — and, in a telling mirror,
when Athens itself rose after Alexander's death (323), neither Sparta, Arcadia, nor
Boeotia came to Athens's aid, underscoring how thoroughly Chaeronea and the League had
fragmented any capacity for coordinated Greek resistance.

## The Antigonid refounding (302)

The "Hellenic League" name and form were revived once more. [[walbank-cah-v7-1-1984|CAH
VII.1]] (Will, ch. 2) records that in **302**, during the [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323|war
of the Successors]], [[antigonus-i-monophthalmus|Antigonus I]] and
[[demetrius-i-poliorcetes|Demetrius Poliorcetes]] **refounded the League of Corinth** —
known from the partly-preserved foundation treaty found at Epidaurus (the officer
**Adeimantus of Lampsacus** served as one of its *proedroi*). Will stresses the reversed
emphasis from Philip's league of 338: where Philip's had been primarily a "common peace"
with the alliance secondary, the league of 302 was **above all a war-instrument against
[[cassander]]** and the Macedonian throne, with the common peace a distant goal. An
Antigonid garrison held the Acrocorinth for sixty years. The earlier "freedom of the
Greeks" propaganda of Antigonus (the Tyre manifesto of 315) belongs to the same
tradition of using Greek liberty as a weapon among the kings.
