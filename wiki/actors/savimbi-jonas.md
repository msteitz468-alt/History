# Jonas Savimbi

Founder and lifelong leader of **UNITA** (National Union for the Total Independence of Angola), and the principal US/South African surrogate in the [[angolan-civil-war-1975|Angolan civil war]] from 1975 until his death in 2002.

## Overview and Role

Per [[mccoy-cold-war-five-continents-2024|McCoy (2024)]], Savimbi broke from Holden Roberto's FNLA over its Bakongo-tribal focus and built UNITA on an Ovimbundu ethnic base in south-central Angola, having studied guerrilla warfare in China. He shifted sharply rightward in 1974 to win the backing of Henry Kissinger, and thereafter became the sole recipient of [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and apartheid South African support against the Soviet- and Cuban-backed MPLA. Cultivated by Washington's right during the Reagan era — styled by publicist Paul Manafort and received in the Oval Office in January 1986, with UN ambassador Jeane Kirkpatrick calling him "one of the few authentic heroes of our time" — he received TOW and Stinger missiles under the Reagan Doctrine after the 1985 repeal of the Clark Amendment.

## Significance

Savimbi kept the Angolan war going for 27 years. After losing the 1992 presidential election to the MPLA's José Eduardo dos Santos, he returned to war, financing UNITA through "blood diamonds" from Angola's northern provinces. He was killed by government forces in February 2002 (McCoy: 15 bullets); his death effectively ended the war. His career epitomizes the Cold War pattern by which superpower patronage sustained a proxy conflict long past any domestic logic — a framing developed on [[mccoy-covert-empire-thesis|McCoy's controversy page]].
