# Joachim of Fiore

Calabrian abbot, mystic, and apocalyptic theologian (c. 1135–1202), founder of the Florensian order after leaving the Cistercians. Best known for his Trinitarian philosophy of history, dividing time into three overlapping ages — of the Father (Old Testament law), the Son (the Church), and a coming Age of the Spirit, a period of contemplative, spiritual renewal he believed was imminent.

Joachim's prophetic historical schema, and his cryptic commentaries on Revelation, exerted enormous influence on later medieval religious enthusiasm, most notably on radical wings of the Franciscan movement (the "Spiritual Franciscans"), who read Francis of Assisi and his movement as heralds of the Age of the Spirit. Though Joachim himself was never condemned as a heretic in his lifetime (his Trinitarian doctrine was posthumously censured at the Fourth Lateran Council in 1215), his apocalyptic framework recurred across later medieval and early modern millenarian movements.

## Significance

Joachim's tripartite philosophy of history offered medieval Europe one of its most influential frameworks for interpreting historical time as progressive and eschatologically meaningful, shaping mendicant self-understanding and recurring in later revolutionary and utopian thought.
