## Overview

The Confederation of Indigenous Nationalities of Ecuador (CONAIE) is the most successful national indigenous confederation in South America as of the mid-1990s, uniting highland Quichua-speaking peoples under ECUARUNARI with Amazonian groups under CONFENIAE. Its formation in 1986 was the product of a decade of alliance-building between organisations that had independently emerged in the 1960s–1970s; the precursor body CONACNIE was created at Sucúa in 1980.

CONAIE's foundational insistence on the term "Nationalities" (*Nacionalidades*) rather than "peoples" or "communities" constituted an explicit political challenge to the Ecuadorian state, asserting that indigenous groups were not merely ethnic minorities but nations with self-governance rights.

## Formation and Predecessors

- **Shuar Federation (1961/1964)**: The first durable indigenous federation in Ecuador, formed in the Ecuadorian Amazon in response to highway construction threatening Shuar territory. By 1987 it managed 240 centres with 40,000 inhabitants. "The Shuar Federation became an example of what ethnic development and consolidation of territory could achieve."
- **ECUARUNARI (1972)**: "Ecuador Runacunapac Riccharimui" — "the awakening of the Indians of Ecuador" in Quichua. Founded in Tepeyac, Chimborazo Province, with support from progressive priests, especially Bishop Leónidas Proaño. Organised Quichua-speaking highland communities; Blanca Chancoso held the position of general secretary.
- **CONFENIAE (1980)**: Confederation of Indigenous Nationalities of the Ecuadorian Amazon, formed under Shuar leadership to coordinate Amazonian groups threatened by oil development.
- **CONACNIE → CONAIE (1980→1986)**: ECUARUNARI and CONFENIAE joined at Sucúa in October 1980 to form the Coordinating Council of Indigenous Nationalities of Ecuador (CONACNIE), renamed CONAIE in 1986.

## Key Events

### June 1990 Uprising (*Levantamiento*)

CONAIE led the first national indigenous uprising in Ecuador's republican history. Demonstrators took over Quito's Santo Domingo Church in the colonial centre; rural sympathisers blocked roads supplying the capital's food. Demands ranged from settlement of land disputes to Ministry of Health recognition of shamanic medicine. Commentators described it as "the Ethnic Earthquake." "Nothing of this magnitude had occurred since the days of the rebel Fernando Daquilema some 100 years before." The government sidestepped demands; IERAC processed land disputes slowly.

### June 1994 Uprising

A second national uprising, prompted by a rightist government's attempt to pass, without indigenous participation, an agricultural development law favouring landlords. Ten days of country-wide paralysis forced the government to negotiate in the presidential palace with a commission led by Luis Macas (Saraguro Indian) and Nina Pacari (indigenous lawyer). "The state had never before extended pomp and circumstance to indigenous persons."

## Programme and Ideology

CONAIE's platform combined:
- Settlement of land disputes and extension of communal land rights
- Bilingual intercultural education (secured a stronghold within the Ministry of Education)
- Multinational constitutional reforms (proposal submitted; partially rejected in 1990s; fully adopted in 2008 constitution)
- Recognition of indigenous medicine and health systems
- Opposition to neoliberal agricultural law

The insistence on "nationalities" rather than "communities" or "ethnic groups" reflected a deliberate theoretical position: that indigenous peoples had prior sovereignty, not merely minority rights within an existing nation-state. CONAIE abstained from partisan commitments while engaging the state on its own terms.

## Significance

Blanca Chancoso of ECUARUNARI summarised the transformation CONAIE represented: "Before, on the level of the unions or parties, Indians were never even mentioned. When there were events, they would speak for us, but it was out of the question for an Indian to be in the leadership." CONAIE changed that, ensuring that "today, since our Indian leaders have been present at various events to make their own speeches" the dynamics of representation had fundamentally shifted.

Xavier Albó concludes that CONAIE "has changed the whole footing of interethnic discourse in what was, not long ago, arguably the most retrograde Andean state."

## Counterfactual Significance

Without CONAIE, Ecuador's 1990s and 2000s constitutional transformations (culminating in the 2008 plurinational constitution) would not have had an organised constituency to drive them. The Shuar Federation's early success in building a self-governing model within existing legal frameworks was the template; CONAIE scaled it to the national level. Its absence would likely have left Ecuador's large indigenous majority as fragmented political clients of mestizo parties — as had been the case throughout the 1950s–1970s.
