# Joseph Stalin
## Biography / Description
Joseph Dzhugashvili, later known as Joseph Stalin, was born in 1879 in the small Georgian town of Gori near Tiflis (Tbilisi) to a humble shoemaker's family. He attended a Church school in Gori and then a theological seminary in Tiflis before being expelled in 1899 for revolutionary activities. He joined the Social Democrats and sided with the Bolsheviks in 1903. Between 1902 and 1913, he was arrested and exiled several times, repeatedly escaping. He was appointed General Secretary of the Bolshevik Party in 1922. Following Lenin's death in 1924, Stalin consolidated control over the party machinery, defeating rivals like Trotsky, Zinoviev, and Bukharin. He established an absolute personal dictatorship from 1928, leading the Soviet Union through industrialization, World War II, and the early years of the Cold War until his death on March 5, 1953.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **Socialism in One Country:** Stalin formulated the thesis that socialism could be built successfully within the borders of the USSR alone, without waiting for a world revolution, which became the ideological basis for the Five-Year Plans.
- **First Three Five-Year Plans (1928–1941):** Stalin launched rapid industrialization, focusing heavily on heavy industry, machine-building, and energy, turning the USSR into the world's second-largest industrial producer.
- **Forced Collectivization (1929):** Stalin ordered the rapid and coercive merging of individual peasant holdings into collective farms (kolkhozes) and state farms (sovkhozes). This process involved the liquidation and deportation of about five million "kulaks" and contributed to a catastrophic famine (especially in Ukraine).
- **The Great Purge (1936–1938):** Stalin unleashed a massive reign of terror (Ezhovshchina) that targeted the Old Bolshevik guard, the military leadership (including Marshal Tukhachevsky), and millions of citizens. It eliminated all potential political opposition and established absolute personal rule.
- **Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact (1939):** Stalin authorized a nonaggression treaty with Nazi Germany, which included a secret protocol partitioning Poland and the Baltic states, allowing the USSR to expand its borders before the German invasion.
- **World War II Leadership (1941–1945):** Despite initial military catastrophes due to unpreparedness and the purges of top generals, Stalin took personal military command as Generalissimo. He oversaw the mobilization of Soviet resources that defeated Nazi Germany in historic battles like Stalingrad and Kursk.
- **Post-War Expansion and Zhdanovshchina:** Stalin established a network of satellite communist regimes in Eastern Europe (the "Popular Democracies") and oversaw a rigid cultural and ideological consolidation at home led by Andrei Zhdanov.

## Historical Significance
Stalin was one of the most powerful and brutal dictators in world history. His regime transformed the Soviet Union from a backward agrarian society into an industrial and military superpower with nuclear capabilities. However, this was achieved at an extraordinary human cost, including millions of deaths from collectivization, famine, deportations, and political terror. Riasanovsky notes that Stalin lived in a paranoiac world of constant threat and wholesale conspiracy, blending fact and fantasy (Line 5917). Posthumously, his cult of personality was denounced by Nikita Khrushchev in 1956, initiating the process of de-Stalinization.
