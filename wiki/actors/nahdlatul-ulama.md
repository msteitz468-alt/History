## Overview

Nahdlatul Ulama (NU) is a traditionalist Sunni mass organization founded in 1926 in Indonesia, led by ulama and organized as a counterweight to Islamic modernism. It has been one of the largest Muslim organizations in the world and, unlike many Islamist rivals, has accepted [[pancasila]] as Indonesia's state ideology rather than pursuing an explicitly Islamic state.

## Role and Key Developments

NU actively recruited women into affiliated organizations from the 1950s–60s, with women in NU leadership roles campaigning ahead of Indonesia's 1955 elections. In 1965–66, NU's Ansor youth wing, alongside Muhammadiyah and rural ulama who framed communists as kuffar (infidels), participated in mass mobilization following the alleged September 30, 1965 coup, contributing to [[indonesian-mass-killings-1965]] in which between 500,000 and one million people — PKI members, their families, and ethnic Chinese — were killed over the following year. NU women were later mobilized in semi-military fashion against the PKI-linked Gerwani women's organization during the same period.

Under Suharto's New Order (1967–1998), NU — like other Islamic organizations — was pushed out of formal electoral politics but retained its social and religious base, continuing as the traditionalist pole against modernist and Islamist currents in Indonesian Islam.

## Counterfactual Significance

NU's willingness to accept Pancasila rather than demand an Islamic state, combined with its organizational depth, gave Indonesia's political Islam a durable moderate/traditionalist anchor that later distinguished Indonesian Islamic politics from more overtly Islamist trajectories elsewhere in Asia.

## Historiography

Formichi (2020) treats NU's role in the 1965–66 killings as a difficult but necessary part of the organization's history, alongside its longer-run role as a stabilizing traditionalist counterweight within Indonesian Islam.
