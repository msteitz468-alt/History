# Cimon

## Overview

**Cimon** (c. 510–450 BCE), son of **[[miltiades|Miltiades]]** the victor of
[[battle-of-marathon-490bce|Marathon]], was the dominant Athenian leader of the 470s and 460s
— the great commander of the [[delian-league|Delian League]] in its expansionist phase and
the champion of **cooperation with Sparta** at home. [[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH Vol. V]] (Chs.
3–4, Rhodes) makes his career the hinge between the Themistoclean and the democratic ages of
Athens.

## The League commander

With [[themistocles]] eclipsed after 480, Cimon led the League's campaigns: the capture of
**Eion** on the Strymon and of **Scyros** (whence, following a Delphic oracle, he brought back
the bones of the hero **Theseus** to Athens — a great prestige coup), the coercion of
**Carystus** and **Naxos**, and above all the double victory at the **Eurymedon** (c. 469),
which destroyed a Persian fleet and army and carried the League's reach to Pamphylia. He
financed public works (the south wall of the Acropolis, the Stoa Poikile) from the spoils and
cultivated a reputation for **aristocratic generosity**, keeping his estates open to his
fellow demesmen. His command closed with the **Thasian war** (465–463), after which he was
prosecuted (one accuser being the young Pericles) for allegedly taking a bribe not to attack
Macedon, and acquitted.

## Lakonism and fall

Cimon's politics were those of a **conservative aristocrat and friend of Sparta** (he named a
son **Lacedaemonius**). When the great earthquake and **Helot Revolt** of 464 led Sparta to
appeal for help, Cimon overrode [[ephialtes]]' objection and led **4,000 hoplites** to the
Peloponnese — only for the Spartans, fearing Athenian "daring and radicalism," to dismiss them
alone among the allies. The humiliation discredited his pro-Spartan line: in his absence
[[ephialtes|Ephialtes']] reform stripped the Areopagus, and on his return Cimon's attempt to
reverse it failed. He was **ostracized** (461) as *philolakon* and *misodemos* (pro-Spartan
and anti-democratic). Recalled at the end of his ten years (the story of an early recall after
Tanagra is doubtful), he led a last League expedition to **Cyprus**, where he **died besieging
Citium** (450); the campaign's success may have prompted the [[peace-of-callias-449bce|Peace
of Callias]].

## Significance

Cimon embodied the **first phase of empire** — the League as a still-shared anti-Persian
enterprise led by a hegemon — and the **last viable policy of Athenian–Spartan dualism**. His
fall in 461 marks the decisive turn: Athens abandoned the Spartan alliance, embraced
[[ephialtes|Ephialtes']] and [[pericles|Pericles']] democracy, and entered the
[[first-peloponnesian-war|First Peloponnesian War]]. His rivalry with [[themistocles]] over
the memory of the Persian Wars (Marathon vs. Salamis, army vs. fleet) shaped Athenian politics
for a generation.
