# The Luba and Lunda States

The **Luba** and **Lunda** kingdoms of the **southern savanna** (the Katanga/southern Congo region) were the
great state-building tradition of **Central Africa** — a cluster of related kingdoms that spread a model of
**sacred kingship** across a vast interior. Documented for the wiki through the **UNESCO
[[gha-vol5-16th-18th-century|General History of Africa, Vol. V]]** (ch. 20, Ndaywel è Nziem).

## The Luba model

The **Luba kingdom** (emerging c. late 16th century around the upper Lualaba) developed a distinctive and
influential system of **sacred kingship (*bulopwe*)** — divine royal blood, a court, esoteric political
associations (the *bambudye*), and the famous **lukasa memory boards** used to record political and
genealogical knowledge. This Luba model of statecraft was widely **emulated and exported** across the
southern savanna.

## The Lunda expansion

The **Lunda**, adopting and adapting Luba political ideas, built the larger and more expansive system. From
the core empire of the **Mwata Yamvo** (Mwaant Yaav) the Lunda projected power outward through a flexible
mechanism of **"perpetual kinship" and positional succession** that let conquered or allied chiefs be
incorporated into the royal genealogy. Lunda offshoots founded daughter-states across a huge area — most
importantly the **Kazembe** kingdom on the Luapula — knitting the interior into long-distance trade
networks reaching **both** the Atlantic (via Angola) and the Indian Ocean (via the Zambezi and the Swahili
coast).

## Trade and decline

The Luba-Lunda world prospered on **copper, salt, ivory, and — increasingly — the slave trade**, feeding
captives toward both coasts. In the nineteenth century this same commerce undid them: the **Chokwe**,
enriched by guns and the ivory/slave trade, and the advancing **Portuguese, Swahili-Arab, and Belgian**
frontiers fragmented the kingdoms before colonial conquest.

## Significance

The Luba and Lunda are the **central African counterpart** to the great Sudanic and forest states — proof
of sophisticated, expansive state-building in the equatorial interior, with an original political technology
(sacred kingship, positional succession, the lukasa) that shaped a vast region.
