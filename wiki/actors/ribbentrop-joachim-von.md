# Joachim von Ribbentrop

Nazi foreign-policy operator: head of the party's Dienststelle Ribbentrop,
negotiator of the Anglo-German Naval Agreement (1935), Ambassador to London
(1936–38), and Reich Foreign Minister from February 1938. A former champagne
salesman with purchased aristocratic credentials, he fed Hitler's conviction
after 1937 that Britain would not fight, and gave his name to the
[[molotov-ribbentrop-pact-1939|Nazi–Soviet Pact]]. Hanged at Nuremberg, 1946.

## Evans (2005)

A latecomer (joined the far right only in 1932 via the Herrenclub; "von" by
adoption — "von Ribbensnob" to London society), he ran a 150-strong personal
foreign-policy office outside the Foreign Ministry, negotiated the
Anglo-German Naval Agreement (18 June 1935 — Germany to 35% of British
tonnage, a triumph that broke the Stresa Front) and the Anti-Comintern Pact
(1936) behind the Foreign Office's back. His London embassy (1936–38) failed
publicly ("von Brickendrop"; the Nazi salute to George VI), and his counsel to
Hitler was contradictory — Britain would not fight, yet war with Britain was
ultimately inevitable — advice Evans calls bad but consequential. Foreign
Minister from February 1938 in the [[blomberg-fritsch-affair-1938|reshuffle]];
excluded from the Munich brokering (Goebbels: "he has a blind hatred of
England"); pushed hardest for the Soviet option and signed the
[[molotov-ribbentrop-pact-1939|Pact]] in Moscow in the early hours of
24 August 1939, then staged the midnight rejection scene with Henderson on
29 August as war came.

## Historiography

Michael Bloch (1992) and Weitz (1992) are the main biographies; universally
portrayed as vain and incompetent, though his influence on Hitler's
Britain-assessment in 1938–39 was real and consequential.
