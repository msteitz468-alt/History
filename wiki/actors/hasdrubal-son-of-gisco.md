# Hasdrubal son of Gisco

**Hasdrubal son of Gisco** was a Carthaginian aristocratic general of the
[[second-punic-war-218-201bce|Second Punic War]], active c. 213–202 BC — and, crucially,
**not a member of the [[barcid-dynasty|Barcid family]]**. Dexter Hoyos
([[hoyos-hannibals-dynasty-2003|*Hannibal's Dynasty*]]) makes him central to the
dynasty's story precisely because he was the rival power around whom a non-Barcid faction
coalesced: as Barcid military fortunes reversed after 211, Hasdrubal son of Gisco rose to
become "the first man in the state by far," and the Barcids were forced for the first time
to **share supremacy** with him rather than rule alone.

> **Disambiguation.** Four distinct Hasdrubals appear in this era: this one (son of
> Gisco); [[hasdrubal-the-fair|Hasdrubal the Fair]] (Hamilcar's son-in-law, d. 221);
> [[hasdrubal-barca|Hasdrubal Barca]] (Hannibal's brother, d. 207); and a minor
> "Hasdrubal the Bald" sent to Sardinia in 215.

## Career

Hasdrubal son of Gisco commanded in Spain from c. 214, acting independently of — and often
clashing with — the Barcid commanders [[hasdrubal-barca|Hasdrubal Barca]] and
[[mago-barca|Mago]]. He shared in the destruction of the elder Scipio brothers (211), but
the Punic commanders' failure to cooperate after that victory let the Roman position in
Spain recover. He was defeated by [[scipio-africanus|Scipio]] at **Ilipa (206)**, the
battle that effectively ended Carthaginian Spain.

Transferring the war effort to Africa, he became the leading figure of the Carthaginian
resistance to Scipio's invasion. To secure the decisive Numidian alliance he married his
daughter **Sophonisba to King Syphax** (204). His army was destroyed at the **Battle of
the Great Plains (203)**; discredited by repeated defeat, he was removed from command and,
per Appian, died in 202 — by suicide or at the hands of his enemies.

## Significance

For Hoyos, Hasdrubal son of Gisco is the measure of how far Barcid dominance had eroded by
the war's end: the family that had ruled Carthage alone for a generation now governed in
**coalition** with a rival aristocratic bloc. His marriage-diplomacy with Syphax also
shows the same Numidian-alliance statecraft the Barcids practised — and its failure, as
[[masinissa|Masinissa]] defected to Rome and Syphax was defeated, helped doom Carthage.

## Historiography

Reported entirely through the Roman tradition (Polybius, Livy, Appian); the circumstances
and even the fact of his death are uncertain, and army-size figures for the Great Plains
are treated skeptically by Hoyos.
