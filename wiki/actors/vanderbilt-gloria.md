# Gloria Vanderbilt

Gloria Laura Vanderbilt (1924–2019). Artist, actress, fashion designer, and memoirist; "poor little rich girl" of the [[events/gloria-vanderbilt-custody-trial-1934|1934 custody trial]]; mother of Anderson Cooper. Cooper & Howe present her as the hinge between the Gilded Age name and contemporary celebrity culture, and as the lived end of dynastic Vanderbilt wealth as inheritance.

## Narrative

Born to Reginald Claypoole Vanderbilt and Gloria Morgan Vanderbilt in 1924; Reggie died deep in debt, leaving a child trust (Cooper & Howe cite on the order of $5 million) that made little Gloria a prize. Her mother, still young, spent years in Europe with a household including nurse Emma "Dodo" Kieslich; after health crises and family intervention, aunt [[actors/whitney-gertrude-vanderbilt|Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney]] secured sole custody on 21 November 1934. The trial's sexual and class accusations made national spectacle.

Adult Gloria cycled through marriages (including an abusive first marriage to Pat DiCicco), careers in art and acting, and — in the 1970s–80s — a designer-jeans fortune that again ran through her hands. She married Wyatt Cooper; their sons included Anderson (b. 1967) and Carter (who died by suicide in 1988). Cooper & Howe, and Cooper's own memoir frame, describe childhood anxiety about money despite the name, modeling work, Studio 54 nights, and the absence of a dynastic trust fund. Gloria died at Beekman Place on 17 June 2019. Cooper's book is explicitly a reckoning after her death.

## Historiography

Layers: 1934 tabloids; Gloria's memoirs; Barbara Goldsmith's *Little Gloria... Happy at Last* (and the miniseries Gloria refused to watch); *The Rainbow Comes and Goes* (2016) with Anderson; Cooper & Howe 2021. Each has a stake. Distinguish trial-era press, self-fashioning, and descendant historiography. The "last Vanderbilt" framing is Cooper's thesis, not a genealogical fact (many Vanderbilt descendants live).
