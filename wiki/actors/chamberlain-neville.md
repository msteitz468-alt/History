# Neville Chamberlain

British Prime Minister (1937–1940). Architect of the Munich Agreement ("peace for our time") with Hitler. Pursued appeasement to buy time for rearmament while avoiding immediate war. After German invasion of Poland declared war; led early "Phoney War" period. Resigned after Norway debate and failures in France; replaced by Churchill. Died shortly after.

## Evans, *The Third Reich in Power* (2005)

The 1938–39 arc in Evans (ingested 2026-07-02): the first flight of his life
to Berchtesgaden (September 1938); Bad Godesberg's escalated demands; the
"far-away country... people of whom we know nothing" broadcast; Munich and
"peace for our time" — held sincerely, per Evans, as a fair and lasting
settlement including for the Czechs. Prague (March 1939) broke the premise:
his 17 March speech suspected Hitler sought "to dominate the world by
force," and the 31 March guarantee to Poland followed — though hedged with a
secret proviso against Polish "provocative or stupid obstinacy," still a
mixture of containment and appeasement. On 2 September 1939 his equivocating
Commons statement provoked the "Speak for England!" uproar; on 3 September:
"everything that I have worked for... has crashed into ruins." Source:
[[evans-third-reich-in-power-2005]]

## Steiner via Evans (2015)

Zara Steiner's assessment (relayed in [[evans-third-reich-history-memory-2015]],
ch. 16, attributed): "limited insight and imagination, repeatedly prone to
wishful thinking"; "hubristic ambitions and self-confidence." He misread
Munich (believing Hitler wanted only the Sudetenland), wrongly saw
Mussolini as a restraint on Hitler, rearmed from 1936 to deter rather than
prepare, and wrote to his sister as late as 23 July 1939 that Hitler had
concluded "the time is not ripe for the major war." "Rational to a fault,
Chamberlain could not imagine that anyone in his right mind... would
actually want war" — he was still seeking Mussolini's mediation after the
invasion of Poland began, until the Commons and cabinet forced the
ultimatum. Fuller debate at [[causes-of-the-second-world-war]].

## From Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)

The [[norwegian-campaign-1940|Norway debacle]] finished him: his Commons defense was "lame and unconvincing," the government's majority fell from 213 to 80 (Attlee: "Everywhere the story is 'too late'"), and he resigned on 10 May 1940 — "a broken man," dead within a year. Halifax declined the succession; Churchill took office the day the western offensive began. Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]
