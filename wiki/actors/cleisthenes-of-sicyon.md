# Cleisthenes of Sicyon

Orthagorid tyrant of Sicyon in the early sixth century BCE; grandfather of the Athenian Cleisthenes. [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] revises Herodotus’s famous story of insulting tribal names and treats Cleisthenes’ role in the [[events/first-sacred-war-c590bce|First Sacred War]] tradition as source-problematic.

## Tribal names (Herodotus 5.68)

Herodotus claims Cleisthenes gave derogatory pig/ass/swine names to three Dorian tribes and called his own *Arkhelaoi*; after sixty years Sicyonians reverted to Hylleis, Pamphyloi, Dymanes plus fourth Aigialeis.

Hall’s interpretation: the sixty-year/two-generation detail places civic reorganization at Sicyon around **500** (within Herodotus’s historical memory), when three *phylai* received standard Dorian names. **Hyatai, Orneatai, and Khoireatai** were likely genuine local tribal names (from toponyms) later scurrilously linked to animal words; *phylai* thus existed at Sicyon from at least early-sixth-century Cleisthenes’ reign. Fourth *phylê* Aigialeis may reflect incorporation of marginal land/population (cf. Achaean Aigialos; Pellene’s destruction).

“Orthagoras as cook’s son” and “stoner” insults against Cleisthenes may be post-fall fabrications. Cleisthenes married his daughter Agariste to Megacles the Alcmaeonid — linking Sicyonian and Athenian elite politics (and the Pheidon/Leokedes chronological tangle: [[actors/pheidon-of-argos]]).

## First Sacred War tradition

Later tradition places Sicyonians under Cleisthenes in the amphictyonic coalition against Cirrha (594–585 traditional). Hall notes chronological squeeze and fourth-century clustering of sources: do not treat participation as secure fact. See [[controversies/first-sacred-war-historicity]].

## Counterfactual significance

As a well-attested early-sixth-century tyrant, Cleisthenes indexes Orthagorid longevity and non-Athenian paths of tribal reorganization; the insult-name story is better read as later ethnography than as tyrannical eccentricity.
