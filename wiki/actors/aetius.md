# Flavius Aetius

**Flavius Aetius** (c. 390–454), son of Gaudentius and for a time a hostage among the [[huns|Huns]], was the dominating military figure of the mid-fifth-century West — *magister utriusque militiae* from 433 until his murder in 454.

## Rise and tenure (Mitchell)

In the 423–25 succession crisis Aetius brought Hunnic support for the usurper John; after a late arrival and clash near Ravenna, Galla Placidia bought the Huns off and Aetius took a senior Gallic command. From 433 he held the senior western generalship for twenty-one years, stabilizing Gaul and protecting Roman landowner interests despite the collapse of the old frontiers. Mitchell stresses that Aetius **depended on Hunnic support** and **made no attempt to prevent the growth of Hunnic power** on the Danube — the structural bargain that later produced [[attila|Attila]]’s western turn and the Catalaunian confrontation (451).

## Counterfactual significance

Without Aetius, mid-fifth-century Gaul lacks its principal Roman coordinator; the West’s survival into the 450s as a fiscal-military residual is harder to imagine. His reliance on Huns is the classic illustration of federate dependency in the fiscal-military “vicious circle” debate ([[causes-of-the-fall-of-the-western-roman-empire]]).

## Historiography

Often romanticized as “last of the Romans”; Mitchell’s account is structural (Hunnic dependency, Gallic landlord protection) rather than heroic. Complements [[heather-fall-roman-empire-2005|Heather 2005]] on the same decades. Survey: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Mitchell 2015]].
