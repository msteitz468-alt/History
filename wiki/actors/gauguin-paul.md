# Paul Gauguin

French painter (1848–1903). Stockbroker turned artist, key figure in Post-Impressionism and Synthetism. Brief but consequential collaboration and rupture with Vincent van Gogh in Arles (1888) marked a turning point for both.

## Narrative

Gauguin abandoned a successful career in finance after the 1882 crash to paint full time. He worked with Pissarro, exhibited with the Impressionists, then sought more "primitive" subjects in Martinique and Brittany (Pont-Aven). In October 1888 he accepted Vincent's invitation to the Yellow House in Arles. The two months of cohabitation produced intense conversations about color, symbolism, and the future of art, but ended in the ear incident and Gauguin's departure. Gauguin later claimed he had prevented Vincent from attacking him. He sailed for Tahiti in 1891, returned briefly to France, then lived permanently in the South Seas until his death in the Marquesas.

## Causal Analysis

Gauguin's self-mythologizing as a savage outsider and his search for "primitive" authenticity drove both the Brittany experiments and the Tahiti/Marquesas exile. The Arles stay offered money (Theo's stipend) and a potential ally in Vincent, but clashed with Vincent's need for a stable "studio of the south" and Gauguin's restlessness. The rupture crystallized differences: Vincent's attachment to observed reality and emotional directness versus Gauguin's turn to memory, imagination, and flat symbolic color.

## Consequence Analysis

The Arles crisis accelerated Vincent's institutionalization and contributed to his final period's intensity. Gauguin's later work (Vision after the Sermon, Where Do We Come From?, etc.) and his writings influenced Nabis, Fauves, and Picasso. His example of the artist as exotic exile became a powerful 20th-century archetype, though his personal conduct in Polynesia (exploitation, syphilis) remains controversial.

## Actors

- Brief cohabitant/foil: Vincent van Gogh
- Dealer/patron connection: Theo van Gogh
- Brittany associates: Émile Bernard, others of Pont-Aven

## Historiography

Naifeh & Smith provide detailed reconstruction of the Arles weeks from letters and later recollections (Gauguin's unreliable memoir). Gauguin's own *Noa Noa* and letters are primary but self-serving. Modern scholarship separates the artistic achievement from the colonial and personal record.
