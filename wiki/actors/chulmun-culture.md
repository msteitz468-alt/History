# Chulmun Culture

The **Chulmun** ("comb-marked" pottery) culture is [[barnes-archaeology-east-asia-2015|Barnes's]]
name for the Korean Peninsula's ceramic-using foraging-and-husbandry societies of roughly
**8000–1500 BC** — the Peninsular counterpart to the Japanese [[jomon-culture|Jomon]], and the
substrate from which the [[mumun-culture|Mumun]] (and so [[pen-insular-state-formation|Korean state
formation]]) emerged. She retains the local term deliberately, to keep the Peninsula distinct from the
Mainland "Neolithic."

## Sequence and the Post-Glacial Anomaly

Korea is a striking **anomaly**: there is virtually no evidence of human occupation during the first
~four post-glacial millennia after the Pleistocene/Holocene boundary. Sedentary Chulmun villages —
pit-buildings with internal storage pits and associated burials — appear on the southern Peninsula from
**~6500 BC**. Barnes (following Choe & Bale) divides the period into Incipient (8000–6000 BC), Early
(6000–3500 BC), Middle (3500–2000 BC) and Late (2000–1500/1000 BC). **3500 BC** marks a clear divide,
after which **chulmun pottery and millet cultivation** spread from the Neolithic cultures of the
Manchurian Basin; early Korean pottery itself probably diffused from the **Amur Basin**.

## Subsistence

Chulmun is an "in-between" economy, not primarily agricultural. Around **600 shellmounds** are known on
the Peninsula (about half Chulmun); over 40 shellfish species are attested, ~80% oysters. From Middle
Chulmun, **millets** from the Manchurian Basin were integrated into the husbanding regime, supporting
more permanent inland river-villages such as **Amsadong**, away from marine resources. The **dog**
(from Middle Chulmun) is the only clear domesticate; the status of pig is ambiguous. **Soybean**
achieved domesticate status in the Chulmun-to-Mumun transition — apparently a separate domestication
sequence from Middle Jomon. Isotope work shows a shift over time: marine-dominated diets at Incipient–
Middle shellmounds (Ando, Taepo, Tongsamdong) give way to terrestrial-protein dominance at Late Chulmun
western-Peninsula sites (Taejuk-ri, Konam-ri), perhaps as millet was incorporated.

Notably, the standard Jomon husbanded species (barnyard grass, shiso, burdock, bottle gourd, azuki) are
**as yet undocumented** in the Early Chulmun — an early divergence between the two Pen/Insular foraging
worlds despite their structural similarity.

## Significance

Chulmun matters as the demonstration of Barnes's [[pen-insular]] thesis on the Korean side: a society
that adopted domesticates without becoming agricultural, that lacked the chiefly ranking already
emerging on the Mainland, and that was reshaped only when the **Mumun rice package** arrived. Its end
(~1500–1300 BC) is defined not by collapse but by the spread of plain (Mumun) pottery and wet-rice
technology from Liaodong.
