# Sher Shah Suri

**Sher Shah Suri** (Farid Khan, c. 1486–1545) was the Afghan ruler who **expelled the
[[mughal-empire|Mughals]] from India** for fifteen years and, in a brief but brilliant reign,
built the administrative system that the restored Mughals would inherit and perfect. From the
[[cambridge-history-of-india-v4|Cambridge History of India, Vol. IV]].

## Rise and the defeat of the Mughals

Born Farid Khan into a family of Afghan service gentry in Bihar, he earned the title **Sher Khan**
("Lion Khan") and rose through the fractured politics of the post-Lodi Afghan north. Exploiting
the weakness of Babur's son **Humayun**, he defeated him at **Chausa (1539)** and decisively at
**Kanauj (1540)**, driving the second Mughal emperor into a long Persian exile and proclaiming
himself **Sher Shah**, founder of the **Sur dynasty**.

## The administrative blueprint

Though he ruled only five years (1540–45), Sher Shah's government became a model:
- **Land revenue.** He systematised assessment by **measuring the cultivated land** and fixing the
  state demand as a proportion (about one-third) of the average produce, recorded in writing for
  each cultivator (the *patta*/*qabuliyat* system) — reducing the arbitrariness of intermediaries.
  His revenue minister's methods influenced **Akbar's** later settlement (Todar Mal).
- **Currency.** He reformed the coinage, issuing the silver **rupee** and a standard copper coin —
  the basis of the monetary system that endured for centuries.
- **Communications.** He repaired and extended the great trunk road across north India (the
  ancestor of the **Grand Trunk Road**), planting it with **sarais** (rest-houses), wells, and
  shade trees, and organised a postal relay — both to speed trade and to bind the empire.
- He also built notably, his **tomb at Sasaram** being a masterpiece of Indo-Islamic architecture.

## Death and legacy

Sher Shah died in 1545 at the **siege of Kalinjar**; his successors proved incompetent, and within
a few years **Humayun reconquered** Delhi (1555), restoring the Mughals. His true significance is
**institutional**: as the wiki's [[delhi-sultanate]] and [[mughal-empire|Mughal]] pages note (after
Spear), Sher Shah supplied the administrative "blueprint" — land-revenue settlement, the rupee, the
road network — on which **Akbar** built the mature Mughal state. He is the bridge between the
[[delhi-sultanate|Sultanate]] and Mughal systems of government.
