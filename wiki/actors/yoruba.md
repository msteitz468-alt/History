## Role and Background

The Yoruba are a West African ethnolinguistic civilization centered on southwestern Nigeria and adjoining Benin, whose traditions trace cultural and religious origins to the city of Ile-Ife, renowned for its naturalistic terracotta and cast-bronze/brass sculptures (from roughly the twelfth–fifteenth centuries) that rank among the most technically sophisticated art traditions of the pre-colonial world. Yoruba civilization developed as a network of urbanized city-states rather than a single unified empire, sharing language, the Ifá divination system, and a common pantheon of *orisha* (deities) while remaining politically fragmented among numerous kingdoms.

## Political Structure

The largest and most powerful of these city-states, the [[actors/oyo-empire|Oyo Empire]], built a cavalry-based hegemony over much of Yorubaland and beyond from roughly the seventeenth to early nineteenth centuries; the neighboring [[actors/benin-kingdom|Benin Kingdom]], though ethnically Edo rather than Yoruba, shared closely related political and artistic traditions. Comparative archaeologist Bruce Trigger includes the Yoruba among his seven paradigm "early civilizations," noting their characteristic heterarchical city-state pattern — power shared between kings (*oba*) and councils of chiefs/lineage heads — and high urbanization rates, contrasting with more centralized territorial-state civilizations elsewhere.
