# Pippinids (Arnulfings)

**The Austrasian aristocratic dynasty of mayors of the palace** that rose across the seventh century
to dominate [[francia|Francia]], displaced the [[merovingian-franks|Merovingians]] in 751, and became
the **Carolingians**. The family is the connective tissue between Geary's Merovingian world and the
empire of Charlemagne.

## Origins: an alliance of two families

The Pippinid (or Arnulfing) line descended from two allied Austrasian families: that of **Pippin I the
Elder** and his grandson **Grimoald**, and that of **Arnulf of Metz** — joined by the marriage of
Grimoald's sister **Begga** to Arnulf's son **Ansegisel**. "The new family… would in time produce the
next royal dynasty, the Carolingians." Both Arnulf and Pippin had helped [[chlothar-ii|Chlothar II]]
destroy [[brunhild|Brunhild]] in 613; their descendants made [[austrasia|Austrasia]] their base.

## False start and sacred legitimation

A premature bid failed: c. 656 **Grimoald I**, mayor in Austrasia, had the boy-king Dagobert II tonsured
and exiled to Ireland and set his own son **Childebert** on the throne; the outraged Franks handed him
to the Neustrians, who executed him. The family rebuilt legitimacy through **sacred cult** — the
venerations of **Arnulf of Metz** (whose vita records no ancestry, making him a founder-saint) and
**Gertrudis of Nivelles** "provided a sacred legitimization of the family, in direct opposition to the
growing royal cult" of [[dagobert-i|Dagobert I]].

## Ascendancy: Pippin II and Tertry

**Pippin II of Herstal** made the family supreme. After the assassination of the Neustrian mayor Ebroin
(680) and the death of Wulfoald, Pippin led Austrasia; at the **[[battle-of-tertry-687|battle of Tertry
(687)]]** he defeated the Neustrian mayor Bercharius, gained control of King Theuderic III, and could
claim to be *princeps* — ruler of all Francia. He consolidated through a three-pronged strategy: merging
with Neustrian aristocratic families (his son Drogo married Bercharius's widow Anstrudis), manipulating
the royal court, and **seizing control of the church** diocese by diocese, beginning with St-Wandrille
and Jumièges on the lower Seine. Royal courts could still occasionally rule against the family (Drogo
lost a case before Childebert III in 697).

## Crisis and Charles Martel

Pippin II's death in **714** — his designated heir Grimoald II having just been assassinated at Liège —
left a disputed succession among Theudoald, the sons of Drogo, and **Charles** ("Martel"), Pippin's adult
son by a concubine, whom the widow Plectrude imprisoned. [[charles-martel|Charles Martel]] escaped,
defeated Plectrude (717) and the Neustrians at Soissons (718), and reconsolidated Francia city by city,
chiefly through **monastic and episcopal offices** — his cousin **Hugo** simultaneously held the sees of
Rouen, Bayeux, and Paris and the abbacies of St-Wandrille, St-Denis, and Jumièges. In doing so Martel
"destroy[ed] the religious basis on which had long rested the independent power of the Frankish
episcopate," transferring the monopoly of the sacred from bishops to monasteries.

## Culmination

The family's rise ended in the **[[pippinid-coup-751|coup of 751]]**, when [[pippin-iii|Pippin III]] was
elected and anointed king "on the command of" Pope Zachary — the first Frankish king made by ecclesiastical
ritual rather than Merovingian blood and long hair. The [[carolingian-empire|Carolingian]] dynasty had
arrived.

## Counterfactual significance

The Pippinids show how aristocratic families converted **control of land, kin networks, and above all the
church** into sovereign power. Their instrument was not the destruction of the monarchy but its capture —
and the reduction of the Merovingian king to the symbolic figure the [[rois-faineants|rois fainéants]]
legend would later mock.
