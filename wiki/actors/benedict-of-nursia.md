# Benedict of Nursia

**Benedict of Nursia** (c.480–c.550) founded **Monte Cassino**; his posthumous fame rests on the **Rule of Benedict**, which eventually became the predominant western monastic rule. Logan, following C. H. Lawrence and others, places Benedict historically somewhere between “patriarch of western monasticism” and “a minor Italian abbot,” while granting his organizational role and the later predominance of his Rule.

## Overview

Nearly the sole biographical narrative is **Gregory the Great’s** *Life* of Benedict (~593)—hagiographic and not a modern biography. Large sections of the Rule derive from the older **Rule of the Master**. On the Continent, mixed **Columban–Benedictine** houses later gave way to the moderate Benedictine Rule (see [[columbanus|Columbanus]]).

> "Benedict's historical place belongs somewhere between patriarch of western monasticism and a minor Italian abbot."

## Role and counterfactual significance

Without the Rule’s later institutional success (Carolingian promotion, Cluniac and Cistercian reform within Benedictine frameworks), western monasticism would have retained more of the diversity of late-antique and Irish rules. The person of Benedict is harder to recover than the text tradition that bears his name.

## Historiography

Logan’s judgment is explicitly intermediate: not pure “founder of the West,” not pure nonentity—organizational achievement plus later textual predominance. Source criticism of Gregory’s *Dialogues* and source-critical work on Rule of the Master / RB relationships are central modern debates.
