## Role and Background

Elihu B. Washburne represented Galena, Illinois in the US House of Representatives for nine terms (1853–1869) and became [[actors/grant-ulysses-s|Ulysses S. Grant's]] earliest and most consistent political patron, championing Grant's military appointments from the outbreak of the Civil War and steering his promotions through Congress.

## Later Career

Upon Grant's election as president, Washburne briefly served as Secretary of State (for a matter of days, largely an honorary gesture to a longtime ally) before becoming US Minister to France (1869–1877), the only foreign diplomat to remain in Paris throughout the Franco-Prussian War siege and the subsequent Paris Commune, using his position to protect German nationals and other foreigners in the city.

## Significance

Washburne's sponsorship was decisive in launching Grant's Civil War career, and his conduct as minister during the 1870–71 Paris crisis made him a notable diplomatic figure in his own right, remembered for steady conduct amid one of nineteenth-century Europe's most violent urban upheavals.
