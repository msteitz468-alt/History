# Oswald Pohl

**Oswald Pohl** (1892–1951) was the SS economic manager who, as head of the **SS Business Administration Main Office (WVHA)**, took control of the entire concentration-camp system in 1942 and drove its reorientation toward slave labour. He was, alongside [[eicke-theodor|Eicke]], one of the two men who built the KL — Eicke the disciplinarian, Pohl the economic empire-builder. Documented from [[wachsmann-kl-concentration-camps-2015|Wachsmann, *KL* (2015)]].

## The SS Economic Empire

A former navy paymaster recruited by Himmler in 1933, Pohl ran SS administration and finances, founding the flagship enterprise **DESt** (German Earth and Stone Works) and its lethal quarry camps Flossenbürg and Mauthausen to supply Speer's building program. In **March 1942** Himmler folded the Camp Inspectorate into Pohl's newly created WVHA (as Office Group D), keeping the camps out of the hands of rivals Speer and Sauckel. At his April 1942 commandants' conference Pohl announced his reign would be "all about economics" and prisoners would be worked to exhaustion — "this action must literally be exhausting."

## Slave Labour and Mass Murder

Pohl set the daily rental rates for prisoner labour to industry, presided over the explosion of the satellite-camp system after Hitler's September 1942 decision to let prisoners work in outside factories, and drove [[annihilation-through-labor|"annihilation through labour"]]. He ordered a **mortality-reduction campaign in 1943** (once prisoners became economically valuable) while simultaneously mandating minimum 11-hour workdays — a contradiction the WVHA never resolved. He was the "clearinghouse for all the booty" of the Holocaust, processing the plunder of Operation Reinhard, and personally toured Auschwitz repeatedly, approving its brothel and inspecting its killing complex. He observed human experiments (the Dachau high-altitude and freezing trials) at Himmler's side, and made a "breakneck tour" of the collapsing camps (including Bergen-Belsen) in March 1945.

## Trial and Legacy

At Nuremberg Pohl denied responsibility, claiming Glücks ran "the whole internal operation" — a defence Wachsmann rejects, since "his fingerprints were all over the major decisions." Arrested in May 1946, he was sentenced to death in the WVHA (Pohl) trial and hanged in 1951. His significance is as the man who fused the camps to the German war economy and the SS financial machine, making slave labour, plunder, and extermination a single administrative system.
