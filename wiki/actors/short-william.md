# William Short

William Short (1759–1849) was a Virginia planter's son, Jefferson's private secretary and chargé in Paris, and later U.S. diplomat; one of Jefferson's most trusted young protégés during the French years.

## Life and Career

Born in Surry County, Virginia; College of William and Mary graduate. Joined Jefferson in Paris 1786 as private secretary. Remained in France after Jefferson's departure in 1789 as chargé d'affaires, handling important negotiations (Dutch loans, etc.). Later minister to the Netherlands, Spain (where he negotiated but did not conclude a treaty), and Russia. Never achieved the high office he desired; spent later life in semi-retirement in France and America.

## Role with Jefferson

Short lived in Jefferson's household in Paris, handled much routine diplomacy and copying, accompanied on travels, and was treated almost as a son. Jefferson's letters to Short are among the most candid. Short's later career disappointments and his long attachment to a French woman (with whom he had children) are noted by Malone. Jefferson remained loyal but could not secure the appointments Short sought.

## Significance

Illustrates Jefferson's patronage style and the limits of transatlantic preferment in the early republic. Short's papers are an important supplementary source for the Paris years.
