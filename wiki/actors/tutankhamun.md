# Tutankhamun

The boy-king under whom Egypt **reversed [[akhenaten|Akhenaten's]] revolution** and
returned to orthodoxy — historically minor, but famous beyond all his dynasty for
the **chance survival of his intact tomb** (discovered 1922). Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]] (Ch. XIX, Aldred). Reign ~1361–1352 by
the volume's high chronology; modern dating ~1332–1323 BCE.

## The restoration

He came to the throne **little more than nine years old**, married to
Akhenaten's third daughter **Ankhesenpaaten**, after the brief shadowy reign of
**Smenkhkare**. Under his advisers — chiefly the vizier **Ay** (a former
Master of the Horse to Akhenaten) and the general [[horemheb|Horemheb]] — the court
**abandoned [[amarna|Akhetaten]]** for Memphis, restored the worship of
[[amun-priesthood|Amun]], and changed the royal pair's names from *-aten* to
*-amun*. His **Restoration Stela** (later usurped by Horemheb) is "exceptional in
Egyptian annals" for confessing the disaster of the previous reign — temples
neglected "from one end of the country to the other," prayers unanswered, foreign
ventures failed — and announcing the refounding of the cults, priesthoods and
endowments. The policy was a complete reversal of Atenism.

## Death and the succession crisis

He died young, perhaps from a head injury, leaving **no heir** (two mummified
foetuses, presumed his stillborn children, lay in his tomb). With him "the family of
Amosis... came to an end." His widow's extraordinary appeal to
[[shuppiluliuma-i|Šuppiluliuma]] for a Hittite prince to marry — the
[[zannanza-affair-1322bce|Zannanza affair]] — was the result; its failure left the
throne to the commoner-vizier **Ay**, then to [[horemheb|Horemheb]].

## Significance

Historically Tutankhamun matters as the **hinge of the return to orthodoxy** that
ended the Amarna experiment and as the trigger of the Zannanza affair. His
overwhelming **modern** fame rests on archaeology and accident: the only
substantially intact royal burial of the New Kingdom, whose "great wealth of golden
treasure" gives "an unparalleled conspectus of the applied arts of the period" — a
cautionary case of how the survival of evidence, not historical weight, shapes a
reputation.

## Historiography

His reign yields few historical monuments beyond the Restoration Stela; almost all
that is known comes from the **tomb (KV62)** and the forensic study of the royal
mummies (which links him by blood-group to Smenkhkare). His exact parentage —
son of Akhenaten? of [[amenhotep-iii|Amenophis III]] (which would require a long
co-regency)? — remains unresolved. See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
