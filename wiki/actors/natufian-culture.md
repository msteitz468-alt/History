# Natufian Culture

The Epipalaeolithic/"Mesolithic" culture of the Levant (~12,500–9,500 BCE), the
best-documented immediate precursor of [[early-agriculture-southwest-asia|Neolithic
agriculture in Southwest Asia]]. The Natufians built the first semi-permanent
villages of the Near East and harvested wild cereals intensively, standing at the
threshold between foraging and farming. This page is an anchor node; later
specialist sources will substantially enrich it.

## Founding and Definition

The Natufian was **named and defined by Dorothy Garrod** from her excavations at
the Wadi en-Natuf and the Mount Carmel caves, and her synthesis is the basis of
the treatment in [[edwards-cah-v1p1-1970|CAH Vol. I, Part 1]] (Ch. III). Garrod's
identification of a distinct, microlithic, sedentary culture immediately preceding
the Neolithic was a foundational move in Near Eastern prehistory — see the
historiography note on [[mesolithic]].

## Structural Features

- **Microlithic industry** with geometric forms; bone tools; the appearance of
  **sickle blades** bearing silica gloss, evidence of systematic harvesting of
  wild grasses (whether wild or incipiently cultivated cereals was already debated
  in 1970).
- **Semi-sedentism**: substantial dwellings and base camps, notably the Natufian
  levels at [[jericho|Jericho]], for which CAH cites early radiocarbon dates.
- Intensive exploitation of wild cereals and gazelle; storage; the demographic and
  social preconditions for the [[neolithic-revolution]].

## Relation to Neighbouring Cultures

CAH places the Natufian within a wider Epipalaeolithic Near Eastern mosaic: the
**Zarzian** of the Zagros (radiocarbon ~12,000 BP), which the volume judges older
than the Natufian, and the earlier **Baradostian** Upper Palaeolithic at
Shanidar and Zarzi. The onset of **Neothermal** (early Holocene)
conditions is the environmental backdrop — see
[[postglacial-environment-near-east]].

## Significance

The Natufian is the type case for the argument that **sedentism preceded
agriculture** rather than following it — a sequence central to
[[why-did-agriculture-begin]]. Its villages and harvesting economy make it the
proximate ancestor of the Pre-Pottery Neolithic of the Levant.

## Historiography

CAH I.1's Natufian data are now dated (pre-calibration radiocarbon; pre-Abu Hureyra,
pre-Ohalo). Treat the *culture-historical* framework and Garrod's foundational role
as the durable content here; absolute dates and the wild-vs-cultivated question are
superseded by later scholarship. See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]] on
reading the CAH's vintage.
