# Ivan III "the Great" and the Rise of Muscovy

Grand Prince of Moscow (r. 1462–1505), under whom the grand principality of Muscovy "achieved clear
dominance" in north-east Rus′ and laid the institutional and ideological foundations of the Russian
state. Treated by Nancy Shields Kollmann in [[allmand-ncmh-v7-1998|NCMH VII]] (Ch. 28), who calls the
fifteenth century "one of the most significant, and underappreciated, centuries in Russian history."

## The Gathering of the Lands

Moscow's rise rested less on geography than on the **means — institutional and symbolic — it devised
to consolidate authority and mobilise resources** (means that "endured for at least the next two
centuries"). After the brutal **dynastic war** of his father Vasilii II's reign (Vasilii was blinded
by rivals), Ivan III completed the absorption of the north-eastern principalities:

- **Novgorod.** The great merchant republic, ruled by a "myopic" boyar oligarchy, leaned toward
  Lithuania; after defeat at the **Shelon river (1471)** and a final campaign, Ivan **annexed
  Novgorod outright in 1478**, dismantling its government, deporting hundreds of boyar and merchant
  families, confiscating ~80% of seignorial property, and closing the Hanseatic *Kontor* (1494).
- **Tver** was conquered in **1485**; Viatka (1489), the Perm lands and the Urals followed, securing
  the fur-rich north. The rival **grand duchy of Lithuania** was pushed back as Orthodox Upper Oka
  princes defected to Moscow.

## The End of the "Mongol Yoke"

In **1480** the **Great Horde**, expecting Lithuanian aid that never came, confronted Moscow at the
**"stand on the Ugra river"** and withdrew without battle — an event "immortalised beyond all real
significance" as the throwing-off of the **Mongol yoke**, though, as Kollmann notes, effective Horde
authority over north-east Rus′ had already disintegrated decades earlier. The Horde was finally
destroyed in 1502.

## Autocracy and the Third Rome

Ivan's reign forged the symbolic apparatus of Muscovite **autocracy**: the gathering of "all Rus′"
under one sovereign (*gosudar′*), the elevation of the Moscow boyar elite, and a new imperial
ideology. His marriage in **1472 to Zoe (Sophia) Palaiologina**, niece of the last Byzantine emperor
[[fall-of-constantinople-1453|Constantine XI]], symbolically linked Moscow to fallen Byzantium; an
Orthodox Russia that had **rejected the Union of Florence** could present itself as heir to the Roman
Orthodox mantle — the seed of the **"Moscow, the Third Rome"** idea (though the Russian patriarchate
came only in 1589). The conditional land-tenure (*pomest′e*) introduced on confiscated Novgorod
estates became the basis of the service gentry.

## Significance

Ivan III is the founder of the unified Russian state — the "continuum that extended to the Russian
Empire, the Soviet Union and modern Russia." His gathering of the lands, defeat of the great rival
republics, and construction of an autocratic and imperial-Orthodox ideology set the pattern of
Russian governance for centuries.
