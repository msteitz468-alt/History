# Tecumseh (c. 1768–1813)

Shawnee war leader and pan-Indian organizer who, with his brother **Tenskwatawa** (the Prophet), rebuilt a western Native alliance against U.S. land cessions after [[treaty-of-greenville-1795]]. Killed at the Battle of the Thames (Ontario), October 1813. Michael D. Green, CHNPA I.1 ch. 8.

## Role and doctrine

Fallen Timbers / Greenville shattered Nativist–accommodationist cooperation; Nativists saw treaty chiefs corrupted by Harrison land cessions. Tenskwatawa began prophetic mission **1805** (personal/social repentance, ritual healing, spiritual power recovery). **1809** Tecumseh joined, challenging Fort Wayne and other Harrison treaties as invalid—unrepresentative unauthorized treaty chiefs. Doctrine: **God had given the land to all Indians; no small group could sell it**—same pan-Indian stand as the 1780s western alliance. Brothers set out to rebuild the alliance. Antecedent: Alexander McGillivray’s mid-1780s hosting of Shawnee/Wyandot/Mohawk/Oneida chiefs seeking southern support (Green).

## Tippecanoe and War of 1812

William Henry Harrison, fearing Northwest unity and southern talks (Tecumseh among Upper [[creek-muscogee|Creeks]] 1811), struck **Prophetstown** in Tecumseh’s absence: Sept 1811 ~1,000 men up the Wabash; **7 Nov** Tenskwatawa’s warriors attacked (rituals for invincibility failed); casualties roughly a draw but the prophet discredited; town burned. Tecumseh returned Jan 1812, rebuilt with British aid (Winnebago, Kickapoo, Potawatomi, Shawnee, Wyandot). War of 1812: British ally. **Oct 1813 Battle of the Thames:** Harrison scattered the force; **Tecumseh killed**—effectively ended the western alliance. Green: “Militant Nativist pan-Indianism had not worked.”

## Counterfactual significance

Without Tecumseh’s death and alliance collapse—combined with Horseshoe Bend’s destruction of Creek Nativists—Appalachian-crossing settlement would still have faced organized multi-regional military resistance for the first time since the 1760s (Green’s framing of the 1813–14 watershed).

## Historiography

Edmunds on Tecumseh/Shawnee Prophet (Green’s bibliographic guide); Dowd *Spirited Resistance*; Canadian memory cast Tecumseh as patriot ally (Washburn & Trigger intro historiography). Distinguish prophetic religious program (Tenskwatawa) from Tecumseh’s land-sovereignty diplomacy without collapsing them.
