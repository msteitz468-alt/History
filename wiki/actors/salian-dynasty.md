# The Salian Dynasty (Holy Roman Emperors, 1024–1125)

The **Salians** (Salier) were the German royal and imperial dynasty that succeeded the
[[ottonian-dynasty|Ottonians]] and ruled the [[holy-roman-empire|Holy Roman Empire]]
for a century, from the election of Conrad II in 1024 to the death of Henry V in 1125.
[[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] (Hanna Vollrath, Ch. 3, "The western empire under
the Salians") presents their century as falling into **two contrasting halves**: a
first phase of strong, customary kingship under Conrad II and Henry III, and a second
of profound crisis under Henry IV and Henry V, when the [[gregorian-reform|Gregorian
Reform]] collided with a Saxon revolt to produce a "crisis of medieval Germany."

## The Rulers

- **Conrad II (1024–1039)** — elected at Kamba after the childless death of the last
  Ottonian, Henry II; a kinsman of the old royal house (descended from Otto the Great's
  daughter). An illiterate (*idiota*) but effective ruler who consolidated the realm,
  acquired the [[kingdom-of-burgundy|kingdom of Burgundy]] (1032/34), and ruled "according
  to established customs."
- **Henry III (1039–1056)** — the apogee of the sacral imperial monarchy. A pious
  reformer, he **set aside three rival popes at the Synod of Sutri (1046)** and installed
  a line of German reforming popes (Leo IX) — paradoxically launching the church reform
  that would turn against his house. (See [[gregorian-reform]].)
- **[[henry-iv-holy-roman-emperor|Henry IV]] (1056–1105/6)** — a child of five at his
  accession; his long, stormy reign was consumed by the **Saxon wars** and the
  **[[investiture-contest|Investiture Contest]]** with [[gregory-vii|Gregory VII]]
  (Canossa, 1077; the antipope Clement III). Deposed by his own son.
- **Henry V (1105–1125)** — rebelled against and supplanted his father; continued the
  investiture struggle until settling it by the **Concordat of Worms (1122)**. He died
  childless, ending the dynasty; his English widow was the "Empress Maud" of the
  English civil war.

## Significance

The Salian century saw the German monarchy reach its sacral height under Henry III and
then suffer the gravest crisis of its medieval history under Henry IV — a crisis from
which, NCMH IV argues, German kingship emerged structurally **weakened**: the
Investiture Contest and the Saxon and princely revolts entrenched the power of the
**princes** and the elective character of the crown, setting Germany on a more
fragmented path than the consolidating monarchies of France and England. The Salians
were succeeded, after the brief reign of Lothar III, by the
[[frederick-i-barbarossa|Hohenstaufen]].
