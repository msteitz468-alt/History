# Jean-Étienne Valluy

French general who served as overall military commander in Indochina in 1946–1947. He bears direct responsibility for the orders that turned the Haiphong clash into a major bombardment and for the aggressive 1947 offensives (Léa, Ceinture) that failed to destroy the Viet Minh leadership or main forces.

## Role

As the senior French commander on the ground, Valluy recommended and executed large-scale initiatives in Hanoi and Annam. After the 20 November 1946 incident in Haiphong, he ordered Colonel Dèbes to “take maximum advantage” and seize complete control of the city. He launched Operation Léa (October 1947), the largest French colonial operation to date, aimed at capturing Ho and Giap.

## Outcome

The Haiphong bombardment succeeded in the narrow tactical sense but triggered nationwide war. Léa and the follow-on Ceinture operation failed to achieve their strategic objectives; the Viet Minh leadership escaped and the regular army avoided decisive battle. Valluy’s tenure illustrated both French conventional superiority and its inability to convert tactical successes into political victory.
