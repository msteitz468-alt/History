# Sacagawea (Sacajawea) (~1788 – after 1812, debated)

Shoshone (Agaidika band) teenager captured by Hidatsa raiders as a child; wife (or one of the wives) of French-Canadian trader/interpreter Toussaint Charbonneau. Joined the Corps of Discovery at the Mandan villages with Charbonneau and her infant son Jean-Baptiste ("Pahmpi").

**Role and agency** (grounded in Fehrman 2026 ranges): Born ~1788 Agaidika Shoshone (Lemhi area); family, buffalo hunts, menstrual hut; captured ~1800 at Three Forks by Hidatsa (“grabbed her, yanking her out of the water”); enslaved, learned Hidatsa, adapted, blue bead belt; Leaping Fish escape attempt; sold/gambled to Charbonneau (teen); close with Otter Woman; pregnant ~6 months on joining. At Fort Mandan: strategic silence + open-gate risk; ill Jan 1805 (possible complications); labor Feb 11 1805 (rattlesnake rattle); boy born (Pahmpi per Shoshone; Jean-Baptiste). 

Outbound: foraged (wild artichokes shared with Lewis); saved cargo in May 14 1805 pirogue (“leaned out the back and began scooping up supplies”; Lewis credited “equal fortitude and resolution with any person onboard”); recognized Three Forks as capture site (“This is the place”); identified Beaver’s Head landmark; sang/danced on sighting Shoshone horses; emotional reunion with brother Cameahwait (sobbed under blanket); translated council (gracious reply); learned most family dead; foraged/preserved in Rockies (refused horse meat per taboo); demanded to see ocean and whale at Clatsop (“she had traveled a long way with us to see the great waters… it very hard that she could not be permitted”); strapped Pahmpi in kohno. Assertive via Plains Sign Language (self-identified Shoshone/Snake).

**Spelling**: Source uses "Sacajawea"; modern scholarship often prefers "Sacagawea" or "Sakakawea." Page uses the latter for consistency with much academic usage while recording variants. She self-identified as Shoshone via signs.

**Later life**: Clark later cared for or claimed oversight of Jean-Baptiste. Sacagawea's own post-expedition life and death date are contested across Shoshone, Hidatsa, and documentary traditions (written sources ~1812; multiple oral histories claim decades longer among Shoshone or Comanche). 

**Historiography**: One of the most mythologized figures in U.S. history (suffragist symbol, "Indian guide," face on the dollar coin). Recent work and Fehrman (2026) recover her as a young mother exercising limited but real agency inside intersecting structures of captivity, marriage, and expedition. Oral vs. journals debates ongoing (Schultz stories vetted/skeptical for inconsistencies). See dedicated scholarship on Shoshone perspectives and the limits of the written record.
