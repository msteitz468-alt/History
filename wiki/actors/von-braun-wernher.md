# Wernher von Braun (1912–1977)

Per [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans (2008)]]: aristocratic engineering prodigy (PhD in applied physics on liquid-fuel rocketry), technical director at Peenemünde, Nazi Party member from 1937 and SS officer from 1940. His A-4/V-2 achieved the first successful long-range ballistic flight on 14 October 1942, and his film presentation convinced Hitler the rocket would be "the decisive weapon of the war." Production at the underground Mittelwerk ran on Dora camp labor that killed ~20,000 workers — more than the V-2 killed in action (see [[wonder-weapons-1943-1945]]). Himmler arrested him in March 1944 on fabricated sabotage-defeatism charges after he refused SS takeover of the program; Speer and Dornberger got him released — an episode Evans notes proved "convenient" for his postwar self-presentation as an apolitical engineer. Taken with 120 staff to the US in 1945 (El Paso, then Huntsville), he built America's missile arsenal and the Saturn V that carried Apollo to the Moon.

## Historiography

The moral accounting — Party and SS membership, direct awareness of Mittelwerk conditions, versus the arrest and the apolitical-technician defense — is the standard von Braun debate (Neufeld's biography is the reference); Operation Paperclip's laundering of such records belongs to Cold War historiography.
