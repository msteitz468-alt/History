# Sneferu

**Sneferu** (Horus Nebmaat, r. ~2613–2589 BCE) was the founder of the **4th Dynasty**
and the king under whom the true pyramid was perfected — the immediate predecessor of
[[actors/khufu|Khufu]] and the builder whose works set the scale of the
[[actors/old-kingdom-egypt|Old Kingdom]] pyramid age.

## The greatest builder of the Old Kingdom

"The sheer volume of material involved in Sneferu's building activities was greater
than that of any other ruler in the Old Kingdom." He built two pyramids at **Dahshur**
— the **Bent (Rhomboidal) Pyramid**, whose angle was altered in mid-construction
because of structural problems, and the **Red (North) Pyramid**, the first successful
true pyramid and his burial place — and probably completed a third at **Meidum**. He
also reshaped the **pyramid complex** itself, reorienting it east–west and adding the
pyramid temple, causeway, valley temple and satellite pyramid: "these architectural
innovations must have resulted directly from changes in the doctrine concerning the
king's afterlife."

## Expeditions and the state economy

Sneferu's reign shows the Old Kingdom state reaching abroad for resources. The
[[concepts/palermo-stone|Palermo Stone]] records **forty ships** bringing wood from
abroad and a Nubian campaign yielding "7,000 captives and 200,000 head of cattle" — a
raid so destructive it is associated with "the disappearance of the local culture known
as the A Group" in Lower Nubia (and the establishment of a southern outpost at
[[places/buhen|Buhen]]). His name also appears at the turquoise and copper mines of Wadi
Maghara in Sinai.

## Reputation

Later Egyptians remembered Sneferu as a **benign, approachable ruler** — *snefer* means
"to make beautiful" — a striking contrast with the despotic afterlife reputation of his
son Khufu. A scatter of small provincial step pyramids may belong to his cult.
