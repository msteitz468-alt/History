# István Tisza

Hungarian statesman who served twice as Prime Minister of Hungary (1903–1905, 1913–1917) within the Austro-Hungarian dual monarchy. In the [[july-crisis-1914|July Crisis]] of 1914 following the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand, Tisza was initially the leading voice within the Austro-Hungarian leadership opposing an immediate declaration of war on Serbia, fearing it would draw in Russia and destabilize the multi-ethnic empire — before ultimately acquiescing to the ultimatum under pressure from Vienna and Berlin.

## Role

Tisza was a staunch defender of Hungarian dominance within the dual monarchy's constitutional arrangement and of Magyar political control over the kingdom's minority nationalities, resisting broader electoral and nationality reforms even as the empire's structural strains deepened during the war. He was assassinated by mutinous soldiers in Budapest on 31 October 1918, amid the collapse of Austria-Hungary at the war's end.

## Counterfactual Significance

Tisza's initial resistance in July 1914 is cited by historians examining whether Austria-Hungary's decision for war was genuinely contested at the highest levels or effectively predetermined by Vienna's military and diplomatic leadership; his eventual capitulation removed the last significant internal check on the ultimatum to Serbia.
