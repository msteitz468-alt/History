# Gouverneur K. Warren

Union Army general and trained engineer, serving as chief topographical engineer of the Army of the Potomac at the Battle of Gettysburg (1863). On the second day of the battle, 2 July 1863, Warren recognized on his own initiative that Little Round Top — a hill anchoring the Union army's left flank — was undefended and about to be seized by advancing Confederate troops under James Longstreet; he moved quickly to divert nearby Union brigades (notably Strong Vincent's) onto the hill just before the Confederate assault arrived, an action credited by most historians of the battle as decisive in preventing a Union flank collapse. His prompt, unauthorized redeployment made him one of the most celebrated junior figures of the battle, later commemorated with a statue on Little Round Top itself.

Warren went on to corps command in the Army of the Potomac, but was controversially relieved of command by Philip Sheridan at the Battle of Five Forks in 1865 over perceived slowness in executing orders; Warren spent years afterward seeking a formal Army court of inquiry to clear his name, which ultimately vindicated him — the findings were published only months after his death in 1882.
