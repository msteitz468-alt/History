# Fiorello La Guardia

Mayor of New York City (1934–1945). La Guardia appointed Robert Moses to his most powerful combination of city posts (Parks Commissioner, Construction Coordinator) and worked with him on major New Deal-era projects including the West Side Improvement and numerous parks and playgrounds.

La Guardia repeatedly backed down from direct confrontations with Moses due to the latter's command of public opinion, the press (especially the *New York Times*), and control of federal funds and project momentum. He accommodated Moses' accumulation of titles and autonomy even when he personally wanted to curb him. The "Moses myth" made the elected mayor reluctant to challenge "a man who had to go to the electorate."

Key tensions included disputes over specific projects (e.g., Central Park Casino site) and Moses' methods, but the administration delivered enormous physical results with Moses as the dominant implementer.
