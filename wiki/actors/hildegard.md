# Hildegard, Queen of the Franks (758–783)

**Hildegard** was the most important wife of [[charlemagne|Charles the Great]] — married
in 771 following his repudiation of [[desiderius|Desiderius]]'s daughter — and the mother of
his heirs, including [[louis-the-pious|Louis the Pious]], Charles the Younger, and Pippin
of Italy. Of Alemannian/Agilolfing high nobility, she bore nine children in eleven years and
died at Thionville on **30 April 783, aged twenty-five**.

## A Dynastic Partner, Not an Adjunct

[[sources/nelson-king-and-emperor-2019|Janet Nelson]] presents Hildegard as an active
political agent, against the older tendency to treat Charles's wives as passive. Nelson also
rejects the modern category of *Friedelehe* ("a figment of … modern wishful thinking") that
had been used to diminish such unions:

- She travelled to the **siege of Pavia (774)**, "heavily pregnant" at sixteen; their
  daughter Adelhaid was born "near the high walls of Pavia" and died on the journey north.
  The whole court — nursery, chapel, writing-office — functioned at the siege camp.
- The **charter of 16 July 774** (Val Camonica/Sirmio) was issued *jointly with Hildegard*,
  her name placed where the deposed Lombard queen Ansa's had stood: "Hildegard stepped into
  Ansa's shoes."
- She **co-commissioned the Godescalc evangelistary (781)** — "Charles … along with his
  excellent wife Hildegard ordered this exceptional work."
- She shared the decision to **rename the son Carloman "Pippin"** at his Roman baptism (781),
  wanting "the most resonant Carolingian name" untainted by the dead brother Carloman: "She
  too saw matters from her own personal standpoint."

On her death the next day Charles granted an estate to St-Arnulf's "for the soul of our most
beloved wife, Hildegard the queen," with perpetual lights at her tomb. Paul the Deacon's
epitaph praised her "candour of soul and inner beauty" (*simplicitas animae, interiorque
decor*).
