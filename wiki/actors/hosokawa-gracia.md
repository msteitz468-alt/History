# Hosokawa Gracia

Japanese noblewoman, born Akechi Tama, daughter of the warlord Akechi Mitsuhide (assassin of Oda Nobunaga) and wife of the daimyo [[actors/hosokawa-tadaoki|Hosokawa Tadaoki]]. Secretly converted to Christianity around 1587 (taking the baptismal name Gracia) despite her husband's disapproval, at a time when Jesuit missionary activity was spreading among the Japanese nobility before Toyotomi Hideyoshi's and later Tokugawa restrictions on Christianity. In 1600, as Ishida Mitsunari's forces moved to seize noble hostages in Osaka to compel their husbands' loyalty ahead of the campaign culminating at Sekigahara, Gracia — her Christian faith forbidding suicide — chose to be killed by a retainer rather than be taken hostage or allow herself to be captured, an act that became one of the most cited episodes of loyalty and faith in the historiography of the Sekigahara campaign.
