# John H. Richardson

CIA station chief in Saigon (1962–1963) during the final year of the Ngo Dinh Diem government. Richardson had cultivated a close relationship with Diem's brother and chief adviser, Ngo Dinh Nhu, which put him at odds with the Kennedy administration's growing conviction — pressed by Ambassador Henry Cabot Lodge and journalists critical of the regime's handling of the Buddhist crisis — that Diem's government could not survive or was no longer a viable partner.

Richardson's perceived closeness to Nhu and skepticism toward regime change made him a liability once Washington shifted toward tacitly supporting a coup against Diem; he was recalled from Saigon in early October 1963, weeks before the coup of 1–2 November 1963 that overthrew and killed Diem and Nhu. His removal signaled the internal split within the U.S. government — State Department and White House officials favoring pressure on Diem versus CIA and military officials more invested in continuity — over Vietnam policy in 1963.

## Significance

Richardson's recall illustrates the bureaucratic infighting inside the Kennedy administration over Diem's fate in the months before the coup, and the CIA station's entanglement with the Ngo family that later drew scrutiny in postwar assessments of U.S. complicity in the coup.
