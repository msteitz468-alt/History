# Kelly DeVries

American medieval historian and historian of technology, specializing in late-medieval military history, particularly the role and tactics of infantry in the fourteenth century. Professor at Loyola University Maryland.

## Major Work Relevant to the Wiki

*Infantry Warfare in the Early Fourteenth Century: Discipline, Tactics, and Technology* (Boydell Press, Warfare in History, 1996). Landmark study reconstructing nineteen battles (1302–1347) to demonstrate the decisive importance of disciplined, primarily foot-fighting infantry forces using dense defensive formations on favourable terrain. Thesis directly challenges earlier cavalry-centric narratives (Oman et al.). Detailed case studies of Courtrai, Bannockburn, Crécy and others; emphasis on uniformity of infantry tactics across Europe and the secondary role of technology.

## Contribution

Provides the primary specialist source for high-resolution tactical analysis of the "infantry revival" battles that define the transition from High to Late Middle Ages in western European warfare. Supplies verbatim primary chronicle evidence, source criticism, and formation detail for the warfare hub (especially Courtrai and the missing Bannockburn template analysis).
