# Alexander H. Stephens

Vice President of the Confederate States of America (1861–1865) and author of the "Cornerstone Speech" (Savannah, Georgia, 21 March 1861), the single most effective piece of self-incriminating evidence used by the Confederacy's international enemies.

## The Cornerstone Speech

In a two-hour address before the Savannah Atheneum, Stephens declared that the new Southern government rested upon the "great truth" that "the negro is not equal to the white man; that slavery—subordination to the superior race—is his natural and normal condition." While the United States had been founded on a "sandy foundation" (the Declaration's equality premise), the Confederacy was built on the granite of scientific racism and would prove "the model nation of history."

The speech was telegraphed, reprinted worldwide, and immediately became the central exhibit in the case that the Confederacy was not a conventional nation but a "new power of a new species" whose explicit purpose was the perpetual subjugation of Black people and, by extension, a threat to the moral progress of civilization.

## International Impact

Frederick Douglass and Charles Sumner both seized on the speech. Decades later it remained the touchstone for arguments that the Confederacy had condemned itself by its own words. In the 21st century it has been brandished on the floor of the U.S. Senate (Carol Moseley Braun) and cited in debates over Confederate monuments and symbols.

## Historiography

Stephens later attempted to walk back or contextualize the speech; Lost Cause writers downplayed it. Bonner restores it to its central place as the clearest public articulation of the Confederate project at the moment of birth and the single most potent weapon in the global stigmatization campaign.
