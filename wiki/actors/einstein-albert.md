# Albert Einstein

**1879–1955**. German-born theoretical physicist whose special and general theories of relativity, 1905 light-quanta hypothesis, and other contributions redefined the foundations of physics and helped inaugurate the atomic age. Public intellectual, refugee from Nazism, advocate of world federalism and civil liberties. Icon of 20th-century science and the power of nonconformist imagination.

**Detailed analytic biography:** [[hubs/biographies/world-wars-era/einstein-albert|Albert Einstein — Detailed Biography]]

## Overview

Born in Ulm (1879) to a secular Jewish family of modest means in the electrical business, Einstein grew up in Munich and displayed early signs of intense visual and mathematical curiosity alongside a deep aversion to rote authority. Compass moment (age ~4–5) instilled lifelong devotion to field theories. Slow speech development and echolalia noted; rebellion against Munich Luitpold Gymnasium military tone and “Zwangs” led to departure (1894–95). Positive experience at Aarau cantonal school (Pestalozzi-inspired visual methods) and first light-beam thought experiment. Renounced German citizenship (1896); attended Zurich Polytechnic 1896–1900.

Unable to secure an academic post immediately after graduating near bottom of class (4.9 avg.), he worked as a patent examiner (third-class Technical Expert) in Bern from 1902 while producing, in his “miracle year” of 1905, four (plus a fifth) papers that laid foundations for both relativity and quantum mechanics. Key relationships: Mileva Mari (first wife, intellectual partner, mother of Lieserl/Hans Albert/Eduard); Michele Besso (sounding board); Olympia Academy (Solovine, Habicht). “Grossmann is doing his dissertation... I don’t know exactly what it is” (non-Euclidean geometry hint for later GR).

Over the next decade he moved through academic posts in Zurich, Prague, and back to Zurich before accepting a position in Berlin. There he completed the general theory of relativity (1915), whose confirmation during the 1919 solar eclipse made him an international celebrity. He received the 1921 Nobel Prize (for the photoelectric effect). In the 1920s and early 1930s he engaged in the famous debates with Niels Bohr over the completeness of quantum mechanics while pursuing a unified field theory.

The rise of the Nazis in 1933 forced his emigration. He settled at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, where he spent the rest of his life. In 1939 (Aug. 2) he actively participated in drafting and signed the letter to President Roosevelt alerting the United States to the possibility of nuclear weapons (the “Einstein–Szilard letter”; see [[events/einstein-szilard-letter-1939]]); he had no direct role in the Manhattan Project. After the war he became a prominent advocate of world government (“sole function of world government should be to have a monopoly over military power”), nuclear restraint (“no lasting secret”), and civil liberties, publicly resisting McCarthy-era investigations (“the current investigations are an incomparably greater danger...”). He signed the Russell-Einstein Manifesto shortly before his death in Princeton in 1955. Character note (1952): “I am not more gifted than anybody else. I am just more curious than the average person...” (AEA via Isaacson).

## Key Developments and Contributions

- **Formation**: Compass (field theory intuition); Aarau visual education and light-beam rider paradox; Zurich Polytechnic (Weber, Minkowski “lazy dog”); Olympia Academy (Hume, Mach, Poincaré, Spinoza).
- **1905 papers** (Bern patent office): “On a Heuristic Point of View Concerning the Production and Transformation of Light” (light quanta; revolutionary); molecular dimensions thesis (Avogadro number via viscosity); Brownian motion paper (statistical random walk proves atoms exist); “On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies” (special relativity; simultaneity relative; ether superfluous); E=mc² addendum. Letter to Habicht: “I promise you four papers... The first deals with radiation... very revolutionary.”
- **Early GR path (1907 onward)**: “Happiest thought” (falling elevator / equivalence of gravity and acceleration) while at patent office. “If a person falls freely, he will not feel his own weight.” Sketch in 1907 yearbook article; full development required Grossmann math collaboration.
- **General relativity (1907–1915)**: Equivalence principle → gravity as curvature of spacetime. Field equations published 1915. 1919 eclipse confirmation by Eddington.
- **Quantum debates**: Early contributions (Bose–Einstein statistics, 1924–25) followed by decades of resistance to the Copenhagen interpretation’s indeterminacy (“God does not play dice”).
- **Unified field theory**: Lifelong unsuccessful quest to subsume gravity and electromagnetism (and later quantum effects) into a single classical field framework.
- **Political and moral positions**: Early pacifism (revised by Hitler), support for Zionism as cultural refuge without state chauvinism, postwar “one-world” federalism, defense of individual conscience against authoritarianism.

## Personal Life

Married Mileva Mari (1903; divorced 1919); two sons (Hans Albert, Eduard); an illegitimate daughter Lieserl (fate unclear). Second marriage to cousin Elsa Einstein (1919). Longtime secretary and protector Helen Dukas lived with the household in Princeton. Close intellectual friendships included Michele Besso (sounding board for 1905 work), Paul Ehrenfest, and (despite disagreements) Niels Bohr and Max Born.

## Historiography Note

Prior to the full opening of the Einstein Papers Project archives and the systematic publication of correspondence, biographies relied heavily on Einstein’s own later recollections, the work of Philipp Frank, and limited letters. Isaacson (2007) and the CPAE editors (Buchwald et al.) made extensive use of the newly available personal letters (especially to Mileva and family), providing richer texture on the private life and the actual sequence of scientific thought. Ongoing work at the Einstein Papers Project continues to refine the documentary record.
