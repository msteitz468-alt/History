# Joseph E. Johnston

Confederate general known for a cautious, defensive strategic style, who
commanded Confederate forces against Sherman's advance in the Atlanta
Campaign (1864) before being replaced by Jefferson Davis for his perceived
excessive caution, and later commanded remaining Confederate forces in the
Carolinas that surrendered to Sherman in April 1865.

## Role and Major Decisions

Johnston conducted a methodical fighting withdrawal against Sherman's
numerically superior forces during the Atlanta Campaign, trading space for
time and preserving his army's cohesion — a strategy Jefferson Davis judged
inadequate to defend Atlanta, leading to Johnston's replacement by the more
aggressive John Bell Hood in July 1864, shortly before Atlanta fell anyway.
Restored to command in the war's final months, Johnston surrendered his
army to Sherman at Bennett Place, North Carolina, in April 1865, one of the
war's last major Confederate capitulations.

## Counterfactual Significance

Historians have long debated whether Johnston's cautious strategy, had it
been allowed to continue, might have prolonged Confederate resistance in
Georgia longer than Hood's more aggressive but costlier approach ultimately
achieved — though most agree Atlanta's fall was likely regardless of which
general commanded.

## Historiography

Johnston's rivalry with Jefferson Davis and his own postwar memoirs
(defending his strategic choices against critics) shaped a historiography in
which his caution has been read alternately as prudent economy of force or
as a critical failure of nerve at a decisive moment in the war.
