## Role

Aratus of Sicyon (271–213 BCE) was the dominant statesman of third-century Greece — the
man who built the [[achaean-league|Achaean League]] into the great power of the
Peloponnese and the lifelong adversary of [[antigonus-ii-gonatas|Antigonid Macedon]]. He
is also, through his thirty-book **[[hellenistic-historiography|*Memoirs*]]** (a
self-justifying apologia), a principal source for his own age, used by [[polybius|Polybius]]
and Plutarch.

## Decisions and Significance

- **The liberation of Sicyon (251)**: son of the murdered Sicyonian ruler Cleinias, he
  grew up in exile at Argos and, aged 20, seized his native city from its tyrant by a
  daring coup — then took the **far-reaching step of uniting Dorian Sicyon to the Achaean
  League**, beginning its transformation from a small ethnic *koinon* into a
  pan-Peloponnesian federation.
- **Ptolemaic paymaster**: rebuffed by [[antigonus-ii-gonatas|Antigonus]], he turned to
  [[ptolemy-ii-philadelphus|Ptolemy II]], who funded him (and was made honorary *hegemon*
  of the League) — the financial basis of his anti-Macedonian policy.
- **The capture of the Acrocorinth (243)**: his masterstroke — a night assault with 400
  men that took the great citadel from Antigonus (the Macedonian governor, the Stoic
  Persaeus, killed himself in shame). This **reversed the balance of power in southern
  Greece**, bringing Corinth, Megara, Troezen, and Epidaurus into the League.
- **The Cleomenean reversal**: a brilliant raider but a cautious general (he dismissed
  [[agis-iv|Agis IV's]] Spartan army in 241 partly from fear of its revolutionary
  example), Aratus met his match in the social-revolutionary king
  [[cleomenes-iii|Cleomenes III]] of Sparta. Unable to defeat him in the
  [[cleomenean-war-229|Cleomenean War]], and watching the League disintegrate, he **called
  in the Macedonians** — joining [[antigonus-iii-doson|Antigonus III Doson's]] Hellenic
  Symmachy (224) and surrendering the Acrocorinth he had famously taken (the great
  *volte-face* his *Memoirs* sought to justify). Doson then crushed Cleomenes at
  [[battle-of-sellasia-222|Sellasia]] (222), restoring Macedonian hegemony.

## Counterfactual Significance

Aratus embodies the possibilities and limits of third-century Greek independence: a man
who could outmanoeuvre kings by stealth and subsidy, and forge a genuine federal state,
yet who in the end preserved the social order of the propertied class by inviting Macedon
back into the Peloponnese — sacrificing the cause of Greek freedom to the cause of his
class. He served as Achaean general in alternate years for much of his life and died in
213 (poisoned, the tradition says, on the orders of Philip V). See [[achaean-league]],
[[hellenistic-historiography]].
