## Overview

Mannaea (Urartian **Mana**) was an Iron Age kingdom — originally a cluster of small kingdoms —
located **south of Lake Urmia** in modern Iranian Kurdistan, dating from the early first
millennium. Its strategic misfortune was geography: it lay directly between the two great rival
powers of the northern highlands, [[neo-assyrian-empire|Assyria]] and [[urartu|Urartu]], and
its history is largely the story of being fought over, conquered, and changing sides between
them. Its royal seat was probably the city of **Izirtu (Zirtu)**, and kingship appears to have
been hereditary (Bryce 2009: 443–4).

## History

When **Dayyan-Ashur**, commander-in-chief of [[neo-assyrian-empire|Shalmaneser III]],
campaigned in the region in 829, the Mannaean states were independent, though one ruler
(Udaku of Izirtu) may have exercised some hegemony. Assyria plundered but did not annex.
[[urartu|Urartu]] pursued a different policy: repeated invasions culminated in Urartian control
of the Urmia lowlands, probably in **801 under Minua**, continuing under **Argishti I**
(787–766). During Argishti's reign Mannaea broke free and unified into a single kingdom reaching
the lake shore — setting off a series of wars with Urartu until **Sarduri II** (765–733)
reimposed Urartian control, only for Mannaea to regain independence after a campaign by
[[neo-assyrian-empire|Tiglath-pileser III]] weakened Urartu.

Mannaea reached its peak in the late 8th century under **Iranzu**, who covered much of the Urmia
basin. Intimidated by Assyrian conquests, Iranzu submitted to [[sargon-ii|Sargon II]] and paid
tribute; in 719 Sargon intervened militarily to crush pro-Urartian governors threatening him.
On Iranzu's death Sargon installed his son **Aza**, but pro-Urartian governors (backed by the
Urartian king **Rusa I**) rebelled, killed Aza, and enthroned his brother **Ullusunu**, who
handed Rusa twenty-two fortresses. Sargon responded furiously, **storming Izirtu** and other
strongholds, flaying the rebel leader Bagdatti, then — when Ullusunu threw himself on his mercy
— forgiving him, restoring his throne and returning the lost fortresses; in 714 Ullusunu
supplied Sargon's army during the great campaign against Urartu.

The accord was short-lived; hostility (usually at Mannaea's expense) was the norm. In the 670s
Mannaea was beaten again after allying with the **Scythians**; in 660–659 King **Ahseri**'s
attempt to destroy an Assyrian force failed, and he was killed in an uprising. His son **Ualli**
made peace with **Ashurbanipal**, and the **Assyrian–Mannaean alliance proved lasting** — still
in force in the late 7th century, when the [[neo-babylonian-empire|Babylonian]] king
**Nabopolassar** (626–605) campaigned into Assyrian territory and defeated Assyria's Mannaean
allies.

## Significance

Mannaea is a valuable window onto the **Iron Age Iranian plateau** and the mechanics of the
Assyrian–Urartian rivalry: a buffer kingdom whose internal politics (pro-Assyrian vs.
pro-Urartian factions among its governors) were a direct function of great-power competition.
Its destruction came not as an enemy of Assyria but as its faithful ally, swept up in the
collapse of the Assyrian order.
