# Bogdan Khmelnitsky

## Biography / Description
**Bogdan Khmelnitsky** (Bohdan Khmelnytsky) was a Ukrainian Cossack noble and the Hetman (chief commander) of the Zaporozhian Cossack Host from 1648 until his death in 1657 (L2448-2450, L2458). In 1648, he launched a massive Ukrainian rebellion against Polish rule—known as the Ukrainian War of Liberation—uniting the Cossacks, Orthodox clergy, and peasantry (L2448-2450). 

To secure the rebellion against Polish counterattacks, he initiated the alliance with the Russian Tsardom, culminating in the **Union of Pereyaslav** in 1654, which transferred Ukrainian allegiance to the Russian Tsar (L2448-2452).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Coup and War of Liberation (1648):** Capitalizing on Cossack grievances, Khmelnitsky forged a military alliance with the Crimean Tatars and launched a massive rebellion (L2448-2450). He scored brilliant military victories over Polish forces, entering Kiev in triumph (L2448-2450).
* **Union of Pereyaslav (1654):** After two failed treaties with Poland, Khmelnitsky appealed to Moscow for protection (L2450). At the Pereyaslav Rada in January 1654, he and his assembly decided to transfer Ukrainian allegiance to Tsar Alexis of Russia (L2450-2452).
* **Autonomous Administration:** Under Khmelnitsky, Ukraine functioned as a virtually independent Cossack state, with its own military organization, elective offices, and administrative structures (L2442-2444).

## Historical Significance
Khmelnitsky's decisions permanently reshaped the geopolitics of Eastern Europe, leading directly to the decline of Poland-Lithuania and the dramatic rise of the Russian Empire (L2456-L2458). 

While the Union of Pereyaslav was viewed by the Russian state as unconditional integration, it was intended by the Cossacks as a conditional protectorate that preserved Ukrainian autonomy (L2450-2452). 

Following Khmelnitsky's death in 1657, Ukraine entered a period of civil war, partition, and devastation known as "the Ruin," during which the Russian government gradually dismantled Ukrainian autonomy, leading to centuries of subsequent conflict and the imposition of serfdom in the region (L2450-2454, L2458).
