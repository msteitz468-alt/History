# Julian of Eclanum

**Julian** (c. 386 – c. 455), bishop of Eclanum in southern Italy, the ablest and most
tenacious opponent of **[[augustine-of-hippo|Augustine]]** and the intellectual leader of the
**[[pelagianism|Pelagian]]** cause in its final phase.

> **Note:** Julian of Eclanum is a distinct person from the emperor **[[julian|Julian "the
> Apostate"]]** (331–363); the two must not be conflated.

Son of Bishop Memor (a friend of Paulinus of Nola) and married, as a priest, to the daughter
of Bishop Aemilius of Beneventum, Julian came from the civic-minded nobility of a formerly
pagan region and once sold his estates to relieve a famine. About 418, aged around thirty-two,
he led some eighteen Italian bishops in refusing to sign the condemnation of Pelagius and
Caelestius; driven from Italy in 419, he took refuge in the Greek East with Theodore of
Mopsuestia and spent nearly half his life in exile, dying in Sicily teaching Latin to the
children of a Pelagian family. His epitaph called him "Julian, the Catholic bishop."

Trained as a philosopher, Julian fought Augustine with logic and a jurist's conception of
divine justice. He defended creation, marriage, God's law, free will, and the goodness of the
sexual instinct (a neutral "sixth sense of the body"), and attacked Augustine's doctrine of
inherited guilt as making God "the persecutor of newborn children... who sends tiny babies to
eternal flames," a notion "hardly conceivable even among the barbarians." He charged Augustine
with covert **[[manichaeism|Manichaeism]]** and mocked him as "Patronus asinorum" and "the
African" (*Poenus*), framing the dispute as "a Punic War of the mind." Brown argues Julian's
Aristotelian defense of nature and reason anticipated by seven centuries the Christian humanism
of Aquinas — while conceding his charge of Manichaeism against Augustine was more good tactics
than fair analysis. Augustine dictated replies to "the importunate Julian" to the end of his
life; the *Opus Imperfectum* against him lay unfinished at his death in 430.
