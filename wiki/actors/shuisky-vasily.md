# Basil IV Shuisky (Tsar Vasily Shuisky)

## Biography / Description
Basil Shuisky was born in 1552 into the highly aristocratic Suzdal branch of the Rurikid dynasty. As a prominent boyar, Shuisky navigated the turbulent reigns of Ivan IV, Boris Godunov, and False Dmitry I. In 1591, Shuisky headed the official government commission investigating the death of Prince Dmitrii of Uglich, declaring the death accidental (Line 2171). However, in 1605 he politically reversed himself, declaring that the prince had escaped and was indeed the "tsar" (False Dmitry I) (Line 2253).

Shuisky soon turned on False Dmitry I, organizing the coup on May 26, 1606, in which the pretender was killed. Shuisky was subsequently proclaimed tsar as Basil IV by his faction and a Moscow crowd, ruling until he was deposed in July 1610 (Lines 2265-2271). Forced to take monastic vows, he was captured by the Poles and died in Polish captivity in 1612 (Line 2305).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **The Boyar "Conditions" (1606):** Upon his accession, Shuisky made specific promises to the boyars: he would not execute anyone without the boyar duma's approval, would not punish innocent family members of criminals, and would investigate all denunciations. This represented an early attempt to limit the arbitrary autocratic power of the tsar in favor of boyar security (Line 2271).
- **Suppression of the Bolotnikov Rebellion (1606-1607):** Shuisky faced a massive lower-class uprising led by Ivan Bolotnikov, which besieged Moscow. Relying on his nephew, Prince Michael Skopin-Shuisky, and exploiting divisions between the social rebels and the gentry factions, Shuisky's forces defeated Bolotnikov at Tula in 1607 (Lines 2285-2287).
- **Alliance with Sweden (1609):** In desperate need of military support against the Second False Dmitry (the Felon of Tushino), Shuisky signed a treaty with Sweden. In exchange for 6,000 Swedish troops commanded by Jakob De la Gardie, Shuisky abandoned Russian claims to Livonia and ceded a northern border district (Line 2301).
- **Klushino and Deposition (1610):** The Swedish alliance provoked King Sigismund III of Poland to declare war on Russia. Shuisky's brother, Dmitry Shuisky, led the Russian forces to a catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Klushino against the Polish commander Zolkiewski. Consequently, Shuisky was deposed by a coalition of clergy, boyars, and townspeople in July 1610, establishing the interregnum of the Seven Boyars (Line 2305).

## Historical Significance
Basil Shuisky's reign represented the peak of boyar reaction during the Time of Troubles. Lacking broad popular legitimacy, Shuisky was viewed as the tsar of a narrow oligarchical clique (Line 2281). His inability to restore order or unite the country led to the fragmentation of Russia into competing administrations (Moscow vs. the Tushino Camp of the Second False Dmitry) and forced him into a Swedish alliance that directly triggered formal military interventions by Poland and Sweden, bringing Russia to the brink of collapse (Lines 2301-2309).
