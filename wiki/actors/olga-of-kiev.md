# Olga of Kiev
## Biography / Description
**Saint Olga** was the regent of [[kievan-rus|Kievan Rus′]] from 945 to approximately 962 CE on behalf of her young son, Sviatoslav I, following the assassination of her husband, Prince Igor. She is one of the most prominent women in early Russian history, celebrated for her administrative capability, her ruthless suppression of the Drevliane rebellion (detailed in Primary Chronicle ll. 4174–4336: envoys buried alive, bathhouse burned, 5,000 at funeral feast; bird-fire siege of Iskorosten), and her conversion to Orthodox Christianity (baptism in Constantinople c. 955 as Helena, per ll. 4346–4410), for which she was canonized as the first Russian female saint. "She was the precursor of the Christian land."

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Regency and Igor's death (945):** With young Svyatoslav (tutor Asmund; commander Sveinald), receives Derevlian envoys (20, Mal prince) proposing marriage after they killed Igor ("like a wolf, crafty and ravening"). Outwardly agrees. Tricks first group: "carry us in our boat" into prepared deep trench outside Kiev hall; dropped in with boat; "Olga bent over and inquired whether they found the honor to their taste. They answered that it was worse than the death of Igor’." Buries alive. Second distinguished men: bathhouse prepared, locked, set afire from doors "so that the Derevlians were all burned to death." Third: funeral feast at Igor's tomb ("prepare great quantities of mead... that I may weep over his grave"); Derevlians drunk; followers massacre ("cut down five thousand of them"). Returns, prepares army (ll. 4174–4261).
* **Suppression of the Drevliane / Iskorosten (946):** With Svyatoslav, besieges Iskorosten (husband slain there). Svyatoslav spear barely clears horse ears (child). Message: submit or die of hunger. They offer honey/furs; Olga: "Give me three pigeons and three sparrows from each house." Ties sulphur + cloth; releases at night. "the dove-cotes, the coops, the porches, and the haymows were set on fire. There was not a house that was not consumed". Takes city; kills some elders, slaves others; remnant pay tribute. "She imposed upon them a heavy tribute, two parts of which went to Kiev, and the third to Olga in Vjshgorod; for Vishgorod was Olga’s city." Establishes laws/tribute/trading posts/hunting preserves "still" exist (ll. 4262–4336).
* **Novgorod reforms (947):** "Olga went to Novgorod, and along the Msta she established trading-posts and collected tribute. She also collected imposts and tribute along the Luga." "Her hunting-grounds, boundary posts, towns, and trading-posts still exist throughout the whole region, while her sleighs stand in Pskov to this day." Fowling preserves on Dnieper/Desna; village Ol'zhichi (ll. 4337–4345).
* **Baptism in Constantinople (c. 955/957):** Arrives Tsar’grad (Emperor Constantine son of Leo). Emperor "wondered at her intellect... worthy to reign with him". She insists he baptize first (still pagan). Baptized with Patriarch's aid; named Helena. Patriarch: "Blessed art thou among the women of Rus’, for thou hast loved the light... The sons of Rus’ shall bless thee to the last generation of thy descendants." Instructed in faith/prayer/fasting/alms/chastity. Emperor later proposes marriage; Olga: “How can you marry me, after yourself baptizing me and calling me your daughter? For among Christians that is unlawful.” Dismissed with gold/silver/silks/vases, still "his daughter". Requests Patriarch blessing for return; prays "My people and my son are heathen. May God protect me from all evil!" (ll. 4346–4410).
* **Efforts to Convert Sviatoslav:** "Olga dwelt with her son Svyatoslav, and she urged him to be baptized, but he would not listen... ‘How shall I alone accept another faith? My followers will laugh at that.’" Olga: "If you are converted, all your subjects will perforce follow your example." He follows heathen; "he was incensed at his mother". "notwithstanding, Olga loved her son Svyatoslav, and said, 'So be the will of God. If God wishes to have pity upon my kin and upon the land of Rus’, let him lead my son’s heart to return to God...' Thus saying, she prayed night and day for her son and for the people" (ll. 4411–4453). "Olga was the precursor of the Christian land" (eulogy at death 969).

## Historical Significance
Olga’s administrative reforms gave the Kievan state its first institutional and territorial stability, replacing loose tribal tribute with structured governance. 

Her conversion to Orthodox Christianity established the early Rurikid dynastic link to Byzantium, serving as a critical precursor to the formal adoption of Christianity by her grandson, Saint Vladimir, in 988 CE. She is remembered in Orthodox tradition as "equal to the apostles" (*apostoledishna*).
