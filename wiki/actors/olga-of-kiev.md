# Olga of Kiev
## Biography / Description
**Saint Olga** was the regent of [[kievan-rus|Kievan Rus′]] from 945 to approximately 962 CE on behalf of her young son, Sviatoslav I, following the assassination of her husband, Prince Igor. She is one of the most prominent women in early Russian history, celebrated for her administrative capability, her ruthless suppression of the Drevliane rebellion, and her conversion to Orthodox Christianity, for which she was canonized as the first Russian female saint.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Suppression of the Drevliane (945 CE):** Igor was murdered by the Drevliane while collecting excessive tribute in their land. Olga responded with a series of famously harsh retributions, including burying Drevliane ambassadors alive and burning their city of Iskorosten.
* **Taxation and Administrative Reforms:** Recognizing the cause of Igor's death, Olga replaced the arbitrary collection of tribute (*poliudie*) with a centralized system of fixed tax quotas and administrative centers (*pogosti*), laying the groundwork for internal stability.
* **Baptism in Constantinople (c. 957 CE):** Olga traveled to Constantinople, where she was received by the Byzantine Emperor Constantine Porphyrogenitus (who left a detailed record of her visit). She converted to Christianity, possibly in 954 or 955 CE, taking the Christian name Helena.
* **Efforts to Convert Sviatoslav:** While Olga attempted to convert her son Sviatoslav, he refused, fearing the loss of respect from his pagan retainers (*druzhina*). Consequently, Olga's conversion remained a personal choice and did not immediately Christianize the Kievan population.

## Historical Significance
Olga’s administrative reforms gave the Kievan state its first institutional and territorial stability, replacing loose tribal tribute with structured governance. 

Her conversion to Orthodox Christianity established the early Rurikid dynastic link to Byzantium, serving as a critical precursor to the formal adoption of Christianity by her grandson, Saint Vladimir, in 988 CE. She is remembered in Orthodox tradition as "equal to the apostles" (*apostoledishna*).
