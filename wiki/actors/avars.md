# Avars

**Avar khaganate in the Carpathian Basin (late 6th–early 9th century); nomadic confederation that dominated central Europe for over two centuries before destruction by Frankish and Bulgar forces.**

## Overview

The appearance of Avars in the Carpathian Basin in the last third of the sixth century heralded a new era of centralized rule that united the Carpathian Basin for almost a quarter of a millennium. This is not to say, however, that Avars were an ethnically homogeneous population. The core groups of inner and central Asian extraction were first allied with Byzantium... As Langobards left for Italy in 568, the consolidation of Avar power began. (range 07 cache, Hungary entry)

## Key Developments

The first sixty years of the Avar empire saw conflicts with Byzantium... Avars occupied the former Gepid capital of Sirmium in 582... Avars encouraged the settlement of northern Slavic allies around their empire... Amid confrontations and peace treaties, Avars extorted money and gold from Byzantium... an estimated 20 metric tons of Byzantine gold may have reached the Avar empire. In 626 Avar troops laid siege to Constantinople... (range 07)

After the defeat, Avars were confined to the Carpathian Basin. Their Slavic and Bulgar vassals also rebelled... Finds from high-status burials (Bócsa, Tépe, Kunágota, Kunbábony) show the power center shifted east... Large cemeteries... suggest ethnic changes took place peacefully... (range 07)

By the late seventh century... pressed metal fittings in men’s belt sets were replaced by molded... acanthus motifs gave way to the so-called “griffin and meander” motif... Gold objects in the Nagyszentmiklós hoard... reflect Byzantine and Sassanian influences, illustrating the rich universe of what is considered late Avar culture today. (range 07)

Frankish expansion... hit the Avar empire in 788. Military campaigns in 791 and 795, together with vicious infighting, weakened the Avars... an additional military thrust by Bulgar forces from the south in 804 destroyed their empire. (range 07)

## Capacity and Legacy

Avar power rested on nomadic military organization, extortion of Byzantine tribute (gold solidi), and alliances with/subjugation of Slavs and others. Rural settlements (e.g. 150 semi-subterranean houses at Kölked) and large cemeteries (50,000+ known burials) attest to prolonged presence and sedentism among commoners. Elite equestrian lifestyle persisted; agriculture dominant among mixed-ethnicity commoners in later phases. (range 07)

**caused_by:** westward movement of Asiatic nomadic groups after 558; alliance with Lombards against Gepids; Byzantine-Persian wars creating opportunities.

**produced:** buffer Slavic settlement; massive influx of Byzantine gold shaping elite material culture; reorganization of Carpathian Basin power.

**followed_by:** Hungarian (Magyar) conquest 895; Carolingian and Bulgar control of former Avar territories.

## Oxford Handbook of the Merovingian World (2020)

Matthias Hardt's chapter in the [[sources/effros-moreira-oxford-handbook-merovingian-world-2020|Oxford Handbook of the Merovingian World]] traces Avar contact with the Franks from their first appearance at the eastern frontier in 562. Sigibert I beat them that year and made *amicitia* with the khagan Baian, but was defeated and captured in 566, securing release only through gift-giving; the Avars settled the Danube–Tisza region in 568. They raided Thuringia in 596 and withdrew after receiving tribute from Queen [[brunhild|Brunhild]]. Their failed siege of Constantinople (626) triggered the internal upheavals — including the Slavic revolt under Samo — that reshaped the Frankish east (see [[early-slavs]]).
