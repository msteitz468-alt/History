# Indonesia

The sprawling archipelagic state that emerged from the **[[dutch-east-indies|Dutch East Indies]]** — the
Netherlands' most valuable colony, knit together by Dutch rule and a shared nationalist project. The
twentieth-century story runs from the rise of organized nationalism, through the [[japanese-occupation-southeast-asia|Japanese occupation]],
to the 1945 declaration of independence under [[sukarno]] and the armed national revolution (1945–49)
that forced Dutch recognition.

As the world's largest Muslim-majority nation and most populous Southeast Asian state, independent
Indonesia moved from Sukarno's "Guided Democracy" through the violent 1965–66 transition to Suharto's
"New Order," and is a founding pillar of [[asean-and-regionalism|ASEAN]].

## Pre-national Islamization

The archipelago's Muslim majority is the product of a centuries-long
[[islamization-of-southeast-asia|Islamization process]] predating the modern state by half a
millennium. Chiara Formichi ([[formichi-islam-and-asia-2020]]) traces Chinese Hanafi Muslim
diaspora communities established in the archipelago from 1407 (Palembang, Sambas) and spreading
through Java, and the [[aceh-sultanate|Aceh Sultanate]]'s rise as a major scholarly node — a
"verandah of Mecca" hosting Naqshbandi-trained returnees such as Abdu Ra'uf al-Sinkili under
Sultana Safiatuddin. Java's Islamization is credited traditionally to the **wali songo** ("nine
saints"), whose grave shrines became local pilgrimage sites; Formichi frames the durable fusion
of Islamic practice with retained Hindu-Buddhist court forms (as at Mataram) as **"contextual
orthodoxy"** rather than the "thin veneer" syncretism older scholarship alleged — see
[[syncretism-vs-authenticity-islam-in-asia]].
