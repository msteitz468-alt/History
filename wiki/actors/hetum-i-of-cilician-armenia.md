# Hetum I, King of Cilician Armenia (r. 1226–1269)

**Hetum I** is the pivotal ruler of the **[[cilician-armenian-kingdom|Cilician Armenian
Kingdom]]** and the architect of its most consequential decision: the **voluntary alliance
with the [[mongol-empire|Mongols]]**. Son of Constantine of Lampron of the Hetumid house,
he came to the throne through his forced marriage to **Queen Zabel** (Isabel), heiress of
[[levon-i-of-cilician-armenia|King Levon I]] — a union that fused the rival **Roupenid and
Hetumid** dynasties and ended a century and a half of internal rivalry. He was crowned at
Tarsus in **June 1226**.

## The Mongol Alliance — His Signature Decision

Hetum read the Mongol irruption not as a threat to be resisted but as an opportunity to be
harnessed. He first sent his brother **Sempad the Constable** to Karakorum (**1247–50**) to
accept Mongol suzerainty, then travelled **in person to the Great Khan Möngke in 1253** —
welcomed, in [[hayton-of-korikos|Hayton's]] telling, as the first ruler to come voluntarily
seeking peace and alliance. He laid **seven petitions** before the Khan that framed the
alliance: the Mongols' conversion to Christianity; perpetual peace with Christians; tax
exemption for churches and clergy; Mongol help to recover the Holy Land; the destruction
of Baghdad and the Abbasid caliphate; a standing right to call on nearby Mongol aid; and
the **restoration to Armenia of the lands taken from it**.

The alliance bore concrete fruit: **Hülagü's** Mongols sacked **Baghdad (February 1258)**,
killing the last Abbasid caliph; took **Aleppo (1260)**; and Hetum, riding with his
son-in-law **Bohemond VI of Antioch**, was rewarded with the return of western Cilician
territory the [[seljuk-empire|Seljuks]] had seized two decades earlier. This made Cilician
Armenia the most important Christian node in the abortive **[[mongol-latin-crusade-alliance|Mongol–Latin
crusade alliance]]**.

## The Mamluk Reprisal

The same alliance earned the enmity of the **[[mamluk-sultanate|Mamluks]]**, who had checked
the Mongols at Ayn Jalut (1260). After Hülagü's death (1265), Sultan **Baybars** struck
Cilicia in **1266** while Hetum was away seeking Mongol help: the Mamluks ravaged Sis,
Mamistra, Tarsus, Adana, and Ayas; Hetum's son **Toros was killed** and his son **Levon
captured** at the Syrian Gates. Hetum ransomed Levon at heavy cost. Broken by the disaster,
he **abdicated to Levon in 1269**, retired to a monastery, and **died in 1270**.

## Methods and Significance

Hetum advanced the kingdom's **Latinization** as deliberately as its eastern diplomacy: he
had Sempad translate the Frankish **Assizes of Antioch** into Armenian, giving the kingdom a
feudal law code on the Frankish model, and tightened marriage ties with Antioch and
[[cyprus|Cyprus]] (his sister Stephanie to Henry I of Cyprus; his daughter Sybille to
Bohemond VI). His struck coinage bore his own image on one face and the sultan's name in
Arabic on the other — a frank emblem of a small kingdom maneuvering among giants.

**Counterfactual significance:** Without Hetum's wager on the Mongols, Cilician Armenia
would likely have been absorbed by the Seljuks of Rum a generation earlier; with it, the
kingdom won a half-century reprieve and a place in the grand Mongol–Frankish anti-Mamluk
strategy — but also marked itself as the Mamluks' priority target, sealing the long
attritional decline that ended in 1375. [[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian]]
portrays him admiringly, even hagiographically, as a uniquely far-sighted statesman whose
vision the Latin West failed to exploit — a framing to weigh against its partisanship.

## Historiography

The fullest near-contemporary account is the chronicle of his nephew **[[hayton-of-korikos|Hayton
of Korikos]]** (*La Fleur des Histoires de la Terre d'Orient*, 1307), itself a pro-crusade,
pro-Mongol-alliance document. Hetum's reign is also reported by the Armenian chroniclers
Kirakos of Gandzak and Sempad the Constable (his own brother). Modern synthesis:
[[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian (2000)]].
