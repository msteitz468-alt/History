# Middle Kingdom Egypt

Egypt's second great age of centralized monarchy — the **Eleventh, Twelfth, and
Thirteenth Dynasties** (~2040–1650 BCE) — founded by the
[[reunification-of-egypt-2040bce|Theban reunification]] under
[[mentuhotpe-ii|Nebhepetre Mentuhotpe II]] and brought to its height by the
**Twelfth Dynasty**. Egyptians themselves regarded it, with the Old and New
Kingdoms, as one of the three classic epochs; its language (Middle Egyptian) became
the **classical idiom** and its literature the canon studied in every later
scribal school. Treated in [[edwards-cah-v1p2-1971|CAH Vol. I, Part 2]] (Chs. XX–XXI).

## The Twelfth Dynasty

The dynasty was founded by **Amenemhat I** (*Ammenemes I*, ~1991 BCE), the southern
vizier who had usurped the throne at the end of the Eleventh Dynasty. His acts set
the pattern for the whole dynasty:

- **A new capital, Itj-towy** ("Seizer-of-the-Two-Lands"), south of Memphis on the
  Upper/Lower Egypt boundary — a return to the Memphite orbit and to pyramid burial
  (at El-Lisht).
- **Amun of Thebes** elevated to the head of the pantheon, fused with the sun-god as
  **Amun-Re**, "King of the Gods."
- **The "Walls of the Ruler,"** a fortified line in the eastern Delta against
  Asiatic infiltration.
- **The co-regency system** — Amenemhat made his son **Senwosret I** co-regent in
  his 20th year; the device of father–son co-rule secured the succession through
  most of the dynasty. Amenemhat I was nonetheless **assassinated** in a palace
  conspiracy — the event behind two literary masterpieces, the *Instruction of
  Ammenemes I* and the *Story of Sinuhe*.

The line ran **[[actors/amenemhat-i|Amenemhat I]] → Senwosret I → Amenemhat II →
Senwosret II → [[senwosret-iii|Senwosret III]] → [[actors/amenemhat-iii|Amenemhat III]]
→ Amenemhat IV**, a remarkable sequence of able kings.

## Reconquest of Nubia

The dynasty's great imperial project was the **military occupation of Lower Nubia**,
begun under Senwosret I and consolidated by [[senwosret-iii|Senwosret III]], who
fixed the frontier at **Semna** above the Second Cataract and secured it with a
chain of massive **brick fortresses** (Buhen, Semna, Uronarti, Kumma) — among the
finest military architecture of the ancient world — to control the river, blockade
unauthorized traffic, and channel the gold of Kush. The garrisons' day-to-day life
survives in the **Semna Dispatches**.

## The Faiyum

The later kings turned the **Faiyum** depression into a showcase of land
reclamation. Beginning under Senwosret II and completed by **Amenemhat III**, dykes
and a barrage reduced the inflow to **Lake Moeris**, reclaiming ~17,000 acres of
farmland; Amenemhat III's pyramid and its vast adjoining complex at **Hawara** were
admired by Greek and Roman visitors as **"the Labyrinth."**

## Administration: the breaking of the nomarchs

[[mentuhotpe-ii|Mentuhotpe II]] and Amenemhat I had ruled *through* a restored
provincial nobility. The great **rock-tombs of Beni Hasan** show how wealthy and
quasi-royal those nomarchs became. [[senwosret-iii|Senwosret III]] ended it:
mid-reign the great provincial tombs simply **cease**, and government passes to
three regional departments (*waret*) of the central administration under the
vizier — accompanied by the rise of a literate **middle class** visible in the
flood of private votive stelae at Abydos.

## Western Asia: empire or influence?

The Twelfth Dynasty reopened intense relations with Syria-Palestine — resident
officials and royal gifts at **[[byblos|Byblos]]**, sphinxes at **Ugarit** and
**Qatna**, the Aegean-and-Mesopotamian **Tod Treasure**, the geographic detail of
the **Execration Texts**. Whether this amounts to an "empire" or to vigorous
diplomatic-commercial domination is debated (see [[middle-bronze-age]] and
[[byblos]]).

## Religion and the afterlife

The Middle Kingdom institutionalized the [[first-intermediate-period|"democratized"
afterlife]]: the **Coffin Texts** for commoners, the supremacy of **Osiris** and
his cult at **Abydos** (where the dead were "justified," *maa-kheru*), and the
crocodile-god **Sobk** of the Faiyum as a royal patron. It also produced the
**golden age of Egyptian secular literature** — *Sinuhe*, the *Eloquent Peasant*,
the *Shipwrecked Sailor*, the *Satire of the Trades*, and the great *Instructions*.

## Decline

After Amenemhat III the line ended with **Amenemhat IV** and the female pharaoh
**Sobekneferu** (the "virtual end of a great epoch"). CAH Vol. II.1 (Hayes) shows
the **Thirteenth Dynasty** (~1786–1633) was not the chaos once imagined: ~50–60
short-reigned, often non-hereditary kings (many "puppets" of a powerful line of
viziers, the family of **Ankhu**) still maintained a single central government from
Itj-towy, recorded Nile-levels at Semna, and were acknowledged as overlords at
[[byblos|Byblos]]. But royal instability, the secession of a parallel
**Fourteenth Dynasty** at Xois, and the swelling Asiatic population of the Delta
(one Theban official's household alone held 45 named "Asiatics") eroded it. The
occupation of Avaris (~1720) and then of Memphis (~1674) by the **[[hyksos|Hyksos]]**
broke the kingdom apart, opening the **[[second-intermediate-period|Second
Intermediate Period]]**.

## Kemp: the bureaucratic Utopia

For Barry Kemp ([[kemp-ancient-egypt-anatomy-2018|*Anatomy of a Civilization*]], Ch. 5) the
Middle Kingdom has a distinctive ideological character: it "was motivated by a vision... of
a **bureaucratic Utopia**," an unformulated drive to regulate economic life by arithmetic
and to impose order on the ground. Its signature is a programme of **planned, orthogonally
gridded towns** built on fresh ground — [[kahun|Kahun (Lahun)]],
[[wah-sut-south-abydos|Wah-sut at South Abydos]], Tell el-Dabʿa, Qasr el-Sagha, Middle
Kingdom Thebes — and the **[[buhen|Nubian fortress]]** network of the Second Cataract,
provisioned grain-stores as much as garrisons. At Kahun the rigid split between great
mansions and rows of small houses encodes a society "of distinct levels," in direct contrast
to the self-organized growth of New Kingdom [[amarna|Amarna]]. Kemp even links the gridded
settlement to the word *khenret* ("labour camp"), raising the possibility of a punitive
**forced-resettlement** policy after the First Intermediate Period. The whole is the
[[provider-state|provider state]] run by the [[egyptian-bureaucracy|bureaucratic mind]] at
its most assertive.

## Historiography

Documented by king-lists (the Turin Canon), regnal and co-regency dates, monuments,
and an unusually rich literature that doubles as **royal propaganda** (*Neferty*,
the *Instruction of Ammenemes I*). Chronology is conventional and debated at the
edges (the length of co-regencies). See [[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
The [[sources/shaw-oxford-history-ancient-egypt-2000|Oxford History]] (Callender) frames
the Middle Kingdom as the Egyptians' own **"classical" literary age** and stresses how much
its political ideology rests on the rhetorical contrast with the
[[controversies/first-intermediate-period-dark-age-debate|"chaos" of the preceding period]];
it also sets the dynasty's **co-regency and chronology** problems out explicitly (see
[[concepts/egyptian-dynastic-chronology]]).
