# Nicholas V (Tommaso Parentucelli; r. 1447–1455)

## Overview

**Nicholas V** (1447–55) is, in [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]]’s framing, the **first Renaissance pope**: restorer of Rome’s fabric, patron of Greek and Latin learning, and founder of the manuscript base of the **Vatican Library**.

## Role and Key Developments

He restored and enlarged the **Vatican Palace** (with **Fra Angelico**), repaired Rome’s fabric, and planned St Peter’s repairs. He patronized translations of historians, Aristotle from Greek, and Eastern Fathers. He acquired **over 1,000 manuscript books** (807 Latin, 353 Greek) forming the basis of the Vatican Library. Successor **Calixtus III** (1455–58) allegedly dismissed the collection as a waste of treasury.

Logan places him within the recovery: from Boniface VIII’s attack (1303) through Avignon, schism, and conciliar humiliation, the papacy was scarred—yet fifty years after Constance it had recovered constitutional supremacy, and “splendid” Renaissance popes would reign.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Nicholas’s collecting and building program, the cultural identity of the mid-fifteenth-century papacy—and the material base of the Vatican Library—would look different; humanism’s entry into the papal office would have waited for later pontiffs.

## Historiography

Logan’s brief is affirmative of humanist papal patronage as a landmark in the church’s attitude to secular learning; he does not dwell on political Italian wars of the period. Pastor’s *History of the Popes* is the classic older narrative frame.
