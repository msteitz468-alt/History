# Columbanus (St Columban)

Irish monk and missionary (c. 543–615), the most influential agent of **Irish monasticism on the
Continent**. Founder of Annegray, **Luxeuil**, Fontaines, and finally **Bobbio**, his career reshaped the
religious life of [[francia|Francia]] and, through the aristocratic families drawn to him, helped drive the
[[christianisation-of-the-frankish-aristocracy|Christianisation of the Frankish aristocracy]].

## Career on the Continent

Columbanus arrived in Gaul from the Irish/Scottish world **around 590**, was received by King
**Gunthchramn of Burgundy**, and established himself first at **Annegray** in the Vosges, then at
**Luxeuil**, adding a third house at **Fontaines**. He carried the distinctive marks of
[[merovingian-monasticism|Irish monasticism]]: extreme asceticism, *peregrinatio* (voluntary exile as a
spiritual discipline), and a monastic rather than diocesan model of the Church.

## Conflict with bishops and kings

The deepest source of friction was his **refusal to subordinate his monasteries to local bishops** —
contrary to Gallic custom — together with his celebration of **Easter by the Irish calendar**. In a step
"virtually unheard of in Gaul," Columbanus appealed directly to **Pope Gregory the Great** to keep his
Celtic usages; Gregory died before the appeal arrived. He was expelled from Burgundy after attacking Queen
[[brunhild|Brunhild]] and the polygyny of King Theuderic II; well received in [[neustria|Neustria]] and
[[austrasia|Austrasia]], he moved on to Alemannia and Bregenz and finally founded **Bobbio** in the Lombard
kingdom, where he died in **615**. After [[chlothar-ii|Chlothar II]]'s victory the king invited him back to
Luxeuil, but he was by then too old.

## Legacy: the Iro-Frankish rule

Columbanus's rule was eventually merged with that of **Benedict** into the **"Iro-Frankish" mixed rule**,
which preserved much of his monastic independence while tempering Irish ascetic extremes. Adopted on
aristocratic family estates across the Frankish north, it made Columbanian monasticism the vehicle through
which the lay aristocracy entered the Church on its own terms — founding family monasteries that doubled as
political centres and necropolises. Source: [[geary-before-france-germany-1988]].
