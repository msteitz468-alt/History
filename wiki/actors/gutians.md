# Gutians

A people from the Zagros mountains who gained political ascendancy in Mesopotamia during
and after the collapse of the [[akkadian-empire|Akkadian empire]] (later 22nd century BCE),
remembered in Mesopotamian tradition as the archetypal barbarian destroyers.

## Liverani's reading

[[liverani-ancient-near-east-2014|Liverani (2014)]] treats the lurid Mesopotamian
descriptions — "dragons of the mountains," "enemies of the gods," "the uncontrolled people"
who "damage land like locusts" and "have no fear of the gods" — as **ideological
constructs** (centre/periphery rhetoric; see [[ancient-near-east-historiography]]), not
accurate ethnography. In practice Gutian control was **lighter and less organised** than
Akkadian administration: they appointed some local *ensi* but left far less mark on rural
agriculture and on urban cult and administration, so southern Sumerian cities regained
autonomy relatively quickly. Their rule was **more solid in the north** (where they
substituted for the Akkadian dynasty, perhaps inheriting its structure) than in the south.
The "Gutian period" (~1 century) was blamed for trade disruption but may have meant only
limited tribute extraction from the Sumerian cities. It ended in a **single battle**:
**Utu-hegal of Uruk** (sole ruler of the "Fifth Dynasty of Uruk") defeated the Gutian king
**Tirigan**, who fled and was killed — the ease of collapse reflecting how shallowly the
Gutians had penetrated Sumer. This is the interlude in which local rulers such as
[[gudea|Gudea of Lagash]] flourished, just before the [[ur-nammu|Ur III]] reunification.

The "Curse of Akkad," which blames [[naram-sin|Naram-Sin's]] impiety and places the Gutian
invasions in his reign, is a later Neo-Sumerian composition and historically unreliable.
