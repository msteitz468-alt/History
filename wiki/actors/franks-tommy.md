# Tommy Franks

U.S. Army four-star general who commanded U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) from 2000 to 2003, directing the two major American campaigns that opened the post-9/11 "war on terror": the October 2001 invasion of Afghanistan that toppled the Taliban regime, and the March–April 2003 invasion of Iraq that overthrew Saddam Hussein. His Iraq campaign plan — a rapid, comparatively light-footprint armored drive on Baghdad rather than a Gulf War–style prolonged air campaign followed by a mass buildup — reflected Secretary of Defense Donald Rumsfeld's push for a smaller, faster force; the plan achieved a swift conventional victory but has been widely criticized in retrospect for under-provisioning for post-invasion occupation and stabilization, contributing to the insurgency that followed. Franks retired shortly after the fall of Baghdad and was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2004.
