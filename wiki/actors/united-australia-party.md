# United Australia Party (UAP)

The United Australia Party (UAP) was the dominant non-Labor federal political organisation in Australia from 1931 to 1945, when it was replaced by Robert Menzies' Liberal Party. It emerged from the Depression crisis as a fusion of the old Nationalist Party with "citizens' movement" groups and conservative businessmen who rallied around the defecting Labor minister [[lyons-joseph]].

## Origins

By May 1931 a new political force had been established "in place of the Nationalist Party, with Lyons as its parliamentary leader." The immediate precondition was the collapse of the Scullin Labor government under Depression pressures: Lyons had resigned from Scullin's Cabinet over the reappointment of the corrupt Edward Theodore and the government's departure from "sound finance." Businessmen in Melbourne had been "impressed by his recent successful campaign, as acting treasurer, to persuade Australians to subscribe to a domestic loan."

The UAP also absorbed the **All for Australia League** and similar "loyalist" and "citizen" organisations formed during the political and economic crisis of 1930–31 — bodies with "a social base in the urban middle class and rural Australia" that "professed to be above 'class' and 'party'."

## Political Character

The UAP governed in coalition with the Country Party after 1934. It was characterised by:
- **Financial conservatism**: implementation of the Premiers' Plan (expenditure cuts, tax increases, interest reduction on domestic bonds)
- **Imperial loyalty**: support for British appeasement of Germany and Japan; Singapore base strategy
- **Resistance to Keynesian planning**: only accepted Keynesian demand management "late in 1939, after the outbreak of World War 2"
- **Structural disunity**: accurately characterised as "an emergency government that outlived the emergency" — Lyons held it together by balancing factions

The UAP won comfortable electoral victories in 1931, 1934, and 1937. Its 1940 election gave it only a razor-thin majority (two independents), and it lost office in October 1941.

## Wartime Decline

Menzies led the UAP into the war but was forced to resign in August 1941. His Country Party successor Arthur Fadden lasted weeks before the two independent MPs switched to Labor. The UAP was in opposition from October 1941, increasingly discredited and disorganised. The Liberal Party replaced it in 1945 as a reinvigorated non-Labor alternative, with Menzies winning the 1949 election.

## Relationship to Previous Parties

The UAP was successor to the Nationalist Party (which had itself formed from Hughes' Liberals and conservatives during the 1916–17 conscription split). The Country Party remained a separate coalition partner throughout.
