# Severus of Antioch

**Severus of Antioch** (c. 465–538) was the leading Miaphysite theologian-patriarch of the early sixth century, patriarch of [[antioch|Antioch]] (512–518) until the Chalcedonian restoration under Justin I forced him into Egyptian exile. His writings (largely preserved in Syriac) defined non-Chalcedonian christology for the West Syrian tradition.

## Role

- Intellectual architect of post-Chalcedonian [[miaphysitism|Miaphysite]] identity.
- Pastoral and polemical leader whose audience included both learned and [[simple-believers|simple]] believers.
- Reference point for later Miaphysite schools and hierarchies ([[qenneshre]], [[jacob-baradaeus|Jacob Baradaeus]]’s network).

## Counterfactual significance

Without Severus, the theological self-definition of the Syriac Orthodox tradition would lack its most influential father; Justinianic and later reunion schemes would face a different doctrinal landscape.

## From Tannous (2018)

Severus is a core reference for Miaphysite identity formation and for pastoral communication across the literacy continuum (letters circulated with/without patristic proofs for different audiences). His works were revised/translated in the Qenneshre network ([[jacob-of-edessa]]). See [[post-chalcedonian-confessional-formation]].
