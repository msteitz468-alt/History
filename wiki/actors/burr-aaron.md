# Aaron Burr (1756–1836)

Revolutionary War officer, U.S. Senator (New York), third Vice President of the United States (1801–1805), and killer of **[[hamilton-alexander|Alexander Hamilton]]** in the 1804 Weehawken duel. Brilliant, opportunistic, and enigmatic New York politician whose career intersected Hamilton's at nearly every turn.

Burr was a grandson of theologian Jonathan Edwards. He rose quickly in the Revolution but clashed with superiors. As a lawyer and political operator in New York, he built the Tammany machine and helped engineer the 1800 Republican victory that tied him with Jefferson for the presidency (Hamilton's maneuvering in the House helped break the tie in Jefferson's favor). As Vice President he was marginalized.

The 1804 duel followed years of political and personal antagonism, culminating in Hamilton's opposition to Burr's gubernatorial bid and alleged remarks about Burr's character. After killing Hamilton, Burr's career collapsed; he was tried (and acquitted) for treason in the 1806–07 Burr Conspiracy (alleged plan to detach western territories or conquer Mexico). He spent years in Europe before returning to a quiet New York law practice.
