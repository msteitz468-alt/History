# Elizabeth I of England

Queen of England and Ireland (r. 1558–1603). Her long reign stabilized the Protestant settlement after the turbulent mid-Tudor years, defeated the Spanish Armada, and presided over a cultural flowering in which the commercial theatre flourished under regulated conditions. The Master of the Revels (a royal officer) licensed plays and companies; court performances were an important source of prestige and income for the players. Elizabeth's court was the ultimate audience for the companies Shakespeare worked with; her tastes and the political climate shaped what could be staged publicly.

Shakespeare's company performed at court; the Lord Chamberlain (Elizabeth's cousin and later the Earl of Pembroke's family) was the company's nominal patron. The queen's image as virgin, Protestant champion, and embodiment of the realm permeates the history plays and appears in more coded form elsewhere.
