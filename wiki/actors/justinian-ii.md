# Justinian II

**Justinian II** (669 – December 711 CE; r. 685–695 CE, 705–711 CE), known as *Rhinotmetos* ("the slit-nosed"), was the last emperor of the Heraclian dynasty. A highly ambitious, autocratically minded ruler, his reign was marked by military campaigns against the Arabs and Bulgars, significant administrative and fiscal reforms, and intense religious assertiveness. His harsh taxation and autocratic methods led to his deposition, mutilation, and exile in 695, but he staged a dramatic return to power in 705 with Bulgar help, ruling with terror until his execution in 711.

## First Reign and Reforms (685–695 CE)

Justinian II succeeded his father, Constantine IV, at the age of sixteen.
- **Foreign Policy**: He initially signed a highly favorable treaty with the Umayyad Caliph [[abd-al-malik|Abd al-Malik]] in 688, exploiting the [[second-fitna-680|Second Fitna]] to increase Byzantine control over Cyprus and Armenia. He campaigned successfully in the Balkans, breaking Avar-Slavic control in Macedonia.
- **Slavic Resettlements**: He forcibly resettled thousands of Slavic captives from the Balkans to the Theme of Opsikion in Anatolia, using them as agricultural laborers and a military recruit pool of 30,000 soldiers. However, many of these Slavs defected to the Arabs during the Battle of Sebastopolis (692), leading to a disastrous Byzantine defeat and the loss of Armenia.
- **Quinisext Council (692 CE)**: Justinian convoked the Quinisext Council (or Council in Trullo) to complete the work of the Fifth and Sixth Ecumenical Councils. The council passed 102 canons regulating church discipline and clerical morals, but many canons explicitly rejected Roman practices, leading to a clash with the Papacy. When Pope Sergius I refused to sign the decrees, Justinian ordered his arrest, but the Italian militia revolted and protected the Pope.

## Deposition, Exile, and Restoration (695–711 CE)

Justinian's aggressive fiscal policies, managed by his ministers Theodotos and Stephen, alienated the aristocracy and the populace. In 695, a rebellion led by the general Leontios deposed him. Justinian’s nose and tongue were slit (a traditional Byzantine practice designed to disqualify him from rule, hence his nickname *Rhinotmetos*), and he was exiled to Cherson in the Crimea.

During his ten-year exile, Justinian plotted his return:
- He escaped Cherson and took refuge with the Khazars, marrying the sister of the Khagan (who converted to Christianity as Theodora).
- He then allied with Tervel, the Khan of the Bulgars. In 705, Justinian marched on Constantinople with an army of Bulgars and Slavs, entered the city through an unused water conduit under the land walls, and deposed the usurper Tiberius III.

Justinian's second reign was a reign of terror. Obsessed with taking revenge on his enemies, he executed Leontios, Tiberius III, and hundreds of officers, while launching disastrous campaigns against Ravenna and Rome, further alienating the West. A military mutiny in Cherson led to a new revolt under Philippikos Bardanes. Justinian was captured and executed in December 711, ending the Heraclian dynasty.

## Significance

Justinian II's reign consolidated the **Theme System** and the militarization of the Byzantine administration. He was also the first emperor to depict the image of Christ on his gold coinage, styling himself as the *Servus Christi* ("Servant of Christ"), reinforcing the religious autocracy of the medieval Byzantine emperorship.
