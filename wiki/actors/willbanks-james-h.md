# James H. Willbanks

**James H. Willbanks** (b. 1947) is a retired US Army officer, military historian, and author specializing in the Vietnam War. He served 23 years in uniform, including as a young infantry officer advising South Vietnamese forces during the North Vietnamese Easter Offensive in 1972. After retirement he joined the faculty of the US Army Command and General Staff College (CGSC), where he served as Director of the Department of Military History and later as the George C. Marshall Chair of Military History (retired 2018). He has authored or edited twelve books on Vietnam.

## Major Contribution

*Vietnam: The Course of a Conflict* (Army University Press, 2018) collects eleven of his previously published article-length works that together trace the full military course of the American war in Vietnam. The chapters cover:
- The advisory effort and training of indigenous forces
- Introduction of Free World Military Assistance Forces
- Major conventional operations (e.g., Junction City)
- The 1968 Tet Offensive and Battle of Hue (with attention to intelligence failures on both sides)
- Pacification via CORDS and the Phoenix Program
- Vietnamization as an incomplete exit strategy
- Lam Son 719
- The Battle of An Loc (case study in ARVN performance during the 1972 Easter Offensive)
- The final 55 days and collapse of South Vietnam in 1975

The collection is grounded in US Army records, translated Vietnamese sources, and Willbanks' own experience as an advisor. It is particularly valuable for its operational and tactical detail suitable for warfare-hub analysis.

**Range 01 claims (advisory + Free World, exclusive):** Five-phase training history; post-1954 ARVN built as conventional mirror of US light infantry divisions for external invasion threat (Korea precedent), "the ARVN was structured and trained to meet a possible external attack by the PAVN, not the actual struggle against the National Liberation Front"; advisor shortages, cultural/linguistic barriers, short tours ("revolving door"), collective training collapse under ops tempo; post-1965 relegation to secondary; Vietnamization surge too late; "conscious decision to form... in the image of US forces" created fatal dependence ("forgotten how to walk"). "More Flags": >40 nations some aid; 5 direct military (ROK ~50k peak largest; Aus task force Jun 1965 under 173rd; etc.); US paid costs for several; Rusk/Johnson "other flags" for legitimacy; SEATO ineffective, bilateral pressure, Saigon ambivalent; "the war would remain America’s war." T.E. Lawrence lesson on indigenous ownership. Updates [[hubs/warfare/cold-war/vietnam-war/vietnam-war]], [[events/vietnam-war]], [[concepts/counterinsurgency]], [[concepts/army-concept]].
