# Pescennius Niger

Gaius Pescennius Niger (c. 135–194 CE) was governor of Syria and commander of its legions when the Praetorian Guard's auction of the throne to Didius Julianus following [[pertinax]]'s murder (193) discredited the succession. The eastern legions proclaimed Niger emperor, giving him control of Syria, Egypt, and much of Anatolia.

He was one of three rival claimants — alongside [[septimius-severus]] in the Balkans and Clodius Albinus in Britain — contesting the throne in the Year of the Five Emperors. Severus struck first at Rome, deposing Julianus, then turned east against Niger, defeating his forces at Cyzicus, Nicaea, and decisively at the Battle of Issus in 194. Niger fled toward Parthian territory but was captured and executed near Antioch; his head was displayed as a warning to the eastern provinces.

Niger's defeat let Severus consolidate the empire before turning against Albinus in the west (196–197), and prompted Severus's punitive measures against Syrian cities that had backed Niger, including elevating Laodicea and Tyre at Antioch's expense.
