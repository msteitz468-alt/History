# Cambyses II

Second Achaemenid Great King (r. 530–522 BCE), son and successor of Cyrus the Great. Conquered Egypt (525 BCE), incorporating it as a satrapy and adopting pharaonic titulature. His reign is marked in Greek sources (especially Herodotus) by tales of hubris, sacrilege in Egypt, and mental instability, contrasted with Cyrus' more measured image. Died (or was killed) during or after a campaign against a usurper (Gaumata / "False Smerdis"); succeeded after a period of turmoil by Darius I.

## Significance
Completed the initial rapid expansion of the Persian Empire into Africa; his Egyptian venture tested the limits of Achaemenid rule over ancient, culturally distinct kingdoms. Source of major historiographical controversy (Herodotus' reliability on Cambyses).
