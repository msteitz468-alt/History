# Taharqa

**Taharqa** (Taharqo; r. ~690–664 BCE) was the greatest and last effective ruler of the
Kushite **25th Dynasty** — a builder-king whose reign "was occupied by increasingly desperate
struggles to defend Egypt against Assyrian aggression."

## The Assyrian wars

As a young man Taharqa was present at the battle of Eltekeh (701 BCE) during the campaigns of
Sennacherib; a Dahshur stele records "a gruelling military exercise" he later staged across the
desert between Memphis and the Faiyum. His reign then collided with the expanding Assyrian
empire:

- **674 BCE** — [[esarhaddon|Esarhaddon's]] first invasion was repulsed.
- **671 BCE** — Esarhaddon took Memphis; Taharqa fled to Nubia, "leaving his wife and son as
  captives."
- **667 BCE** — Taharqa retook Egypt; [[ashurbanipal|Ashurbanipal]] invaded and drove him out
  again.

He died in Nubia in 664 BCE and was buried beneath a pyramid at **Nuri**, a new royal
necropolis opposite Gebel Barkal. The Assyrian onslaught culminated, under his successor
Tanutamani, in the [[events/sack-of-thebes-663bce|sack of Thebes (663 BCE)]].

## A Kushite pharaoh

Taharqa built prolifically (notably at Karnak and at Kawa in Nubia) and embodied Kushite royal
style — the **double uraeus** and the distinctive **cap crown** replacing the blue crown. His
prenomen "Khunefertemra" assimilated him to the Memphite god Nefertem. He ruled in the deeply
Egyptianizing idiom of the [[kush-meroe|Kushite state]], promoting **Memphis** and the cult of
Ptah (the **Shabaqo Stone** Memphite Theology belongs to this dynasty).
