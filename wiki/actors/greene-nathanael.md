# Nathanael Greene (1742–1786)

[[washington-george|Washington]]'s ablest subordinate and the architect of the American
recovery in the southern theater of the **[[american-revolution|Revolution]]**. A Rhode
Island Quaker (disowned for soldiering), Greene rose from militia private to major general.
He covered the retreat at [[battle-of-brandywine-1777|Brandywine]] ("saving the army from
total destruction"), reluctantly took over as **quartermaster general** in March 1778 and
overhauled the army's collapsing supply system, and led a division at
[[battle-of-monmouth-1778|Monmouth]].

## The southern campaign — losing battles, winning the war

Given the shattered southern command after [[battle-of-camden-1780|Camden]] (December 1780),
Greene waged the war's most sophisticated campaign of **attrition and maneuver**. He took the
"bold (and risky) step of dividing his forces," detaching [[morgan-daniel|Daniel Morgan]]
westward — which produced the tactical masterpiece at [[battle-of-cowpens-1781|Cowpens]] —
and sending Henry Lee to join the partisan Francis Marion. He drew [[cornwallis-charles|
Cornwallis]] into the exhausting "Race to the Dan," then turned and fought him to a Pyrrhic
standstill at [[battle-of-guilford-courthouse-1781|Guilford Courthouse]].

Greene grasped that "the Success of the War here, depends much upon Opinion & Appearances":
his mere presence with Continental troops kept rebel allegiance alive across the Carolinas.
He lost Guilford Courthouse, Hobkirk's Hill, and Eutaw Springs in succession — yet each British
"victory" was so costly, and so barren of political result, that the enemy was forced to
evacuate post after post until only Charleston and Savannah remained. The *West Point History*
(Conway) sums it: **"Greene had lost all the battles but won the campaign."**

## Counterfactual significance

Greene's operational artistry converted the disaster of 1780 into the strategic collapse of
Britain's "southern strategy," driving Cornwallis north to Virginia and, ultimately, to the
trap at [[siege-of-yorktown-1781|Yorktown]].
