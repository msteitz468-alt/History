# Hazael of Damascus

**Hazael** was the usurper-king of **Aram-Damascus** (c. 844–803 BCE) who made the
Aramaean kingdom the dominant power of the southern Levant in the later ninth century,
withstood [[neo-assyrian-empire|Assyria]], and reduced [[kingdom-of-israel|Israel]] to
a vassal. He is one of the best-attested rulers of the period — named in Assyrian
annals, in the Hebrew Bible, and on his own inscribed booty. [[boardman-cah-v3p1-1982|CAH
Vol. III, Part 1]] (Ch. 11, T. C. Mitchell, with the Assyrian frame from Ch. 6) traces
his career.

## Usurpation

Hazael seized the throne of Damascus around **844/841 BCE**, supplanting the king the
Assyrians call **Adad-idri** (the Hadadezer of the [[battle-of-qarqar-853bce|Qarqar]]
coalition). Shalmaneser III's annals call him pointedly "**son of a nobody**" — a
usurper without royal descent — and the Hebrew account (II Kings) makes him the agent
of the murder of his predecessor Ben-Hadad. His accession dissolved the old anti-
Assyrian coalition, whose cohesion had been a personal bond among its kings.

## War with Assyria

Hazael bore the brunt of Assyrian aggression alone. In **841 BCE** Shalmaneser III,
having beaten the coalition, marched on Damascus, defeated Hazael, and **besieged him
in his capital** — but could not take the city, ravaging its orchards and the Hauran
instead before withdrawing to the coast (where he took tribute from Tyre, Sidon, and
[[jehu-revolt-841bce|Jehu of Israel]]). Assyria then turned away for decades, and
Hazael was left the strongest king in the region until **Adad-nirari III** finally
brought Damascus to heel around 803 BCE.

## Domination of Israel

With Assyria preoccupied, Hazael fell on a Jehu-weakened [[kingdom-of-israel|Israel]]:
he "seized all the Israelite territory" east of the Jordan (the Transjordanian
tribes), and his pressure continued under Jehu's son Jehoahaz, reducing Israel to its
lowest ebb. He drove south as far as **Gath** in Philistia and extorted tribute from
[[kingdom-of-judah|Judah]]. Inscribed objects bearing his name — ivory and bronze
fittings, some looted and later recovered far afield — and the booty inscription "...
Hazael in the year ..." attest the reach of his power; the **Tel Dan stele**
(discovered after this volume) is now widely attributed to Hazael or his son and
famously refers to the "**House of David**."

## Significance

- `caused_by:` the collapse of the Damascus coalition and the Assyrian withdrawal
  after 841, which together opened a regional power vacuum.
- `produced:` the subjugation of [[kingdom-of-israel|Israel]]'s Transjordan and a
  generation of Aramaean supremacy in the southern Levant.
- `enabled:` (indirectly) the later **Israelite recovery** under Jeroboam II, once
  Assyria broke Damascus and removed Hazael's successors as a threat.
- `opposed_by:` [[neo-assyrian-empire|Shalmaneser III and Adad-nirari III]].

## Historiography

Hazael is a rare Iron Age figure documented from **three independent angles**:
Assyrian royal annals (hostile but datable), the Hebrew prophetic-historical narrative
(which casts him as Yahweh's scourge of Israel), and his own epigraphic remains. The
convergence makes him a fixed point in ninth-century Levantine chronology and a model
case of how Aramaean, Assyrian, and Israelite sources can be triangulated. The **Tel
Dan stele**, found in 1993, postdates this volume and has since sharpened the picture
of his wars with Israel and Judah.
