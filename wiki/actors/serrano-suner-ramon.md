# Ramón Serrano Suñer (1901–2003)

[[franco-francisco|Franco]]'s brother-in-law (the "cuñadísimo") and the chief architect of the FET single-party state — principal author of the April 1937 unification and the 1938–39 institutional legislation, Minister of the Interior (1938–40), then Foreign Minister (1940–42) during the regime's most pro-Axis phase. He accompanied Franco at [[hendaye-meeting-1940|Hendaye]] and negotiated with Hitler and Ribbentrop over Spain's price for entering the war. His fall in September 1942 (after the Begoña bombing incident) marked the turning point away from the Falangist-fascist ascendancy toward military-Catholic ministers and eventual neutrality.

## Significance

Payne (1987) treats Serrano as the nearest thing the regime had to a fascist statesman-ideologue and his dismissal as the key internal marker of defascistization — evidence for the [[concepts/para-fascism|para-fascist]] reading in which Franco used and then discarded fascist personnel as circumstances changed.

## Detail (Payne 1987)

A former CEDA deputy educated partly at Bologna, close friend of [[primo-de-rivera-jose-antonio|José Antonio]] since student days; both his brothers were killed in the Madrid Red terror before his February 1937 escape to the Nationalist zone. He drafted the unification decree (April 1937), the January 1938 administrative law (Article 16: "The Chief of State possesses the supreme power to dictate juridical norms of a general character"), and the rigid April 1938 press law. As Foreign Minister from October 1940 he was sent to Berlin (September 1940 — rejecting Ribbentrop's demand for a Canary Island naval base "with barely concealed indignation") and Berchtesgaden (November 1940, where Hitler conceded Spain's African claims could not be guaranteed); his own stated intention was "to enter the war at the very end, at the moment of the final victory, exactly as Russia did against Japan." "The most hated man in Spain" (per German intelligence) among generals and monarchists, he held more personal influence over Franco than anyone except later [[carrero-blanco-luis|Carrero Blanco]]. His fall (3 September 1942) followed the Begoña grenade attack on a Carlist crowd: Carrero warned Franco that firing the army ministers without Serrano would signal the FET had won — plus a society affair that was "about the nearest thing to a major sex scandal in the long and puritanical history of the regime." In September 1945 he privately urged Franco to dissolve the Falange and legitimize a monarchy by plebiscite. He lived to 2003, the great memoirist-witness of the regime's fascist phase (Payne leans heavily on his *Entre Hendaya y Gibraltar* and *Memorias*).
