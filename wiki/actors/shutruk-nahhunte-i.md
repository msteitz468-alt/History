# Shutruk-Nahhunte I

Founder-figure of the **Shutrukid dynasty** (Middle Elamite III, ~1190–1155 BCE), under
whom [[elam|Elam]] became, for a generation, the **dominant power of the Near East** at
the expense of the collapsing [[kassites|Kassite dynasty of Babylon]]. His standard
titulary — "beloved servant of Inshushinak, **king of Anshan and Susa**, enlarger of my
realm, protector of Elam" — marks the reassertion of the lowland–highland union.

## The sack of Babylon (~1158 BCE)

Shutruk-Nahhunte invaded Babylonia and **overthrew the Kassite king Zababa-shuma-iddina
in 1158 BCE**, exacting tribute (one text records 54 talents — ~1,620 kg — of silver from
Dur-Kurigalzu alone). His most famous act was carrying off to **[[susa|Susa]]** the great
monuments of Mesopotamian history as trophies — **the victory stele of
[[naram-sin|Naram-Sin]], the law-stele of [[hammurabi|Hammurabi]], and the Manishtushu
obelisk**, along with numerous Kassite *kudurrus*. **This is why those monuments were
excavated at Susa rather than in Babylonia** — a fact of first importance for the modern
recovery of Mesopotamian history. Out of piety he also gathered older monuments from
[[chogha-zanbil|Chogha Zanbil]] and other Iranian sites into Susa. He then installed his
eldest son **Kutir-Nahhunte** as military governor of Babylonia (an appointment, not an
abdication); Kutir-Nahhunte went on to remove the **statue of Marduk** from Esagila to
Susa — the "monstrous sin" later Babylonian texts never forgave.

## Counterfactual significance

The Shutrukid ascendancy exploited the **power vacuum of the [[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Late
Bronze Age collapse]]** — but note that Elam, like [[assyria-middle|Assyria]] and unlike
the Mediterranean powers, **prospered through** the upheavals rather than succumbing to
them; the collapse was largely an Anatolian–Aegean phenomenon. The Elamite high point
proved brief: within a generation Shutruk-Nahhunte's grandson Hutelutush-Inshushinak was
crushed by [[nebuchadnezzar-i|Nebuchadnezzar I]] (~1110 BCE), and Elam vanished from the
record for three centuries. The dynasty's signal legacy is therefore less territorial than
**curatorial** — by hoarding Mesopotamia's monuments at Susa, it inadvertently preserved
them. His successor and brother **[[shilhak-inshushinak-i|Shilhak-Inshushinak]]** pushed
Elamite arms even further west.

## Historiography

Known from 450+ inscribed bricks at Susa plus Babylonian chronicles and the later
Achaemenid-era "Kedor-Laomer" texts (which describe Kutir-Nahhunte's devastations). The
sequence and exact regnal dates of the Shutrukids rest on Babylonian/Kassite synchronisms
and remain approximate.
