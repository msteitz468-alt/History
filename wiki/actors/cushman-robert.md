# Robert E. Cushman Jr.

U.S. Marine Corps general who commanded III Marine Amphibious Force, responsible for the I Corps Tactical Zone of South Vietnam, from 1967 to 1969 — the period spanning the 1968 Tet Offensive and the Battle of Khe Sanh, where his headquarters directed the defense of the besieged combat base alongside General [[westmoreland-william|William Westmoreland]]'s MACV command. Before his Vietnam command he had served as Deputy Director of the CIA (1969–71, following his Vietnam tour) — reflecting close personal ties to Richard Nixon, whom he had served as military aide during Nixon's vice presidency. He later served as the 25th Commandant of the Marine Corps (1972–1975).

## Counterfactual Significance

As the senior Marine commander overseeing I Corps during Khe Sanh, Cushman's decisions on reinforcement and air support shaped the siege's outcome at a moment when Westmoreland feared a repeat of the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu; the successful relief (Operation Pegasus) became a significant, if contested, propaganda counterpoint to the political shock of Tet.

## Historiography

Khe Sanh historiography (e.g., Prados and Stubbe, *Valley of Decision*) generally credits the successful defense to combined-arms firepower and airlift rather than to any single commander's operational brilliance, situating Cushman's role within a broader command structure that included Westmoreland's MACV and Marine air assets.
