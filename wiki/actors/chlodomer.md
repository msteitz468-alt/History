# Chlodomer

**Chlodomer** (c. 495–524) was the second son of **[[clovis]]** and [[clotild|Clotild]],
king at **Orléans** after the partition of 511. His short reign and the gruesome fate of
his children are among the set-pieces of **[[gregory-of-tours-history-of-the-franks-thorpe-1974|Gregory's]]** Book III.

## Career

At the urging of his mother Clotild — who sought vengeance for her parents, killed by the
Burgundian king Gundobad — Chlodomer and his brothers invaded **Burgundy**. He captured
the Burgundian king **Sigismund** with his wife and sons; then, against the warning of
Abbot Avitus, "ordered them to be thrown down a well at Saint-Péravy-la-Colombe." Divine
retribution duly followed in Gregory's telling: in a second campaign Chlodomer outran his
own troops pursuing Sigismund's brother Godomar and was lured to his death by a false
Burgundian rallying-cry at **Vézeronce** (524) — "They hacked off his head, stuck it on a
stake and raised it in the air."

His widow Guntheuc was married by his brother [[chlothar-i|Chlothar I]], and his three
young sons were taken into the household of their grandmother Clotild. Two of them —
Theudovald (10) and Gunthar (7) — were then murdered by Chlothar with the connivance of
[[childebert-i|Childebert I]], in the notorious scene where Clotild was forced to choose
between the scissors and the sword and cried "I would rather see them dead than with their
hair cut short." The third boy, **Chlodovald** (St Cloud), escaped, cut his own hair, and
became a religious.

## Counterfactual Significance

The murder of Chlodomer's heirs let the surviving uncles absorb the Orléans kingdom — the
clearest early instance of the Merovingian pattern in which dividing the realm among sons
generated fratricidal consolidation. See [[merovingian-franks]].
