# Aghlabid Dynasty

The **Aghlabids** (800–909) were the **Sunni Arab dynasty of emirs who governed Ifriqiya** (modern Tunisia and
eastern Algeria) as nominal subordinates of the [[abbasid-caliphate|Abbasid]] caliphs of Baghdad, from their
capital at **[[qayrawan|Qayrawan]]** and the palace-city of **Raqqada**. Their overthrow by the
[[abu-abd-allah-al-shii|Ismaili da'wa]] in 909 opened the way for the [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid Caliphate]].

## Rule

Founded when **Ibrahim b. al-Aghlab** obtained hereditary governorship from the Abbasid caliph Harun al-Rashid
(800), the Aghlabids ran a prosperous, largely autonomous emirate: Qayrawan was the greatest city of Ifriqiya
and a centre of **Maliki** and **Hanafi** legal scholarship, and the dynasty patronised major building works.
From **827** they launched the long **[[muslim-conquest-of-sicily-827|conquest of Sicily]]** from the
Byzantines, a campaign of more than seventy years that made the central Mediterranean an Aghlabid–Byzantine
battleground.

## Fall

By the early 10th century the regime was undermined from within its own territory by the Ismaili mission of
[[abu-abd-allah-al-shii|Abu Abd Allah al-Shi'i]] among the [[berbers|Kutama Berbers]]. Aghlabid expeditions
against the movement from 902 failed to stop its advance; in **909** the Kutama took Qayrawan, the last emir
**Ziyadat Allah III** fled east, and the dynasty ended. The Fatimids inherited its administration, its Sicilian
war with Byzantium, and even household servants (among them the future [[jawdhar|Ustadh Jawdhar]]).

## Significance

The Aghlabids represent the pattern of **de facto independent provincial dynasties** that fragmented Abbasid
authority across the 9th-century Islamic world. Their fall is the necessary precondition of the Fatimid rise:
the Ismaili state was built on the Aghlabid foundation — its capital, its fisc, and its Sicilian frontier.
