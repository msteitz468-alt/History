# Philip Schuyler

Continental Army major general, prominent New York landowner and
politician, and later US Senator, whose daughter Elizabeth ("Eliza")
[[hamilton-eliza|Schuyler Hamilton]] married Alexander Hamilton, making
Schuyler father-in-law to one of the era's most influential Founding
Fathers.

## Role and Major Decisions

Schuyler organized and directed the Continental Army's Northern Department
during the early years of the American Revolutionary War, overseeing
preparations for the Saratoga campaign before being replaced by Horatio
Gates shortly before that campaign's decisive 1777 victory — a command
change that left Gates, rather than Schuyler, to claim credit for defeating
Burgoyne. Schuyler later served in the US Senate and remained an influential
figure in New York Federalist politics, closely allied with his son-in-law
Alexander Hamilton.

## Counterfactual Significance

Had Schuyler retained command through Saratoga, historians note he — not
Gates — would likely have received credit for one of the Revolution's most
consequential victories, a credit dispute that shaped both men's later
political fortunes.

## Historiography

Schuyler's reputation has been substantially reassessed in scholarship and
popular culture accompanying renewed interest in Alexander Hamilton's life;
earlier accounts often deferred to Gates's version of the Saratoga
campaign's credit, while more recent historians emphasize Schuyler's
logistical and organizational groundwork as essential to the eventual
victory.
