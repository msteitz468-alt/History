# Louis Loyseau de Grandmaison

French officer; head of the Operations Bureau of the General Staff. Delivered the famous February 1911 lectures on tactics that crystallized and propelled the "offensive à outrance" doctrine into the French army's official thinking.

## Key Contribution

In February 1911 lectures, Grandmaison criticized the "nearly complete atrophy" of the offensive spirit in the French army. His articulation of the offensive à outrance provided the immediate catalyst for doctrinal change.

Had General Victor Michel remained in a position of authority (as vice president of the Superior Council of War), his reservations about Grandmaison and the offensive doctrine might have limited its effects. Michel's ouster in July 1911 and Joseph Joffre's appointment removed that check. Joffre welcomed the ideas and used his new authority to develop the doctrine through maneuvers and map exercises, codifying it in the October 1913 regulations on large units ("The French army, returning to its traditions, accepts no law in the conduct of operations other than the offensive"; "Only the offensive yields positive results") and December 1913 regulations on smaller units.

## Relation to Broader Doctrine

Grandmaison's emphasis aligned with and amplified earlier influences (Ardant du Picq on moral ascendancy; Foch on firepower favoring the attacker; Bonnal on the decisiveness of the first battle of mass armies). The resulting regulations downplayed artillery preparation in favor of support during the assault, stressed the bayonet and infantry will, and rejected meaningful independent value for the defense.

`contributed_to:` Codification of offensive doctrine that shaped Plan XVII execution and early 1914 battles; high French casualties from unsupported or poorly prepared infantry attacks.
