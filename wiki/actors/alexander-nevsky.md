# Alexander Nevsky
## Biography / Description
**Alexander Yaroslavich**, known as **Alexander Nevsky** ("of the Neva"), was the Prince of Novgorod and later the Grand Prince of Vladimir (r. 1252–1263 CE). Born at a catastrophic time in Russian history—coinciding with the Mongol invasion and intense pressure from Western crusading orders—Alexander became Russia's most celebrated defender against Western invasions and its key diplomatic mediator with the Golden Horde.

He was later canonized by the Russian Orthodox Church for his role in preserving the Orthodox lands from both physical destruction by the Mongols and spiritual subjugation by Catholic Europe.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Battle of the Neva (1240 CE):** Alexander led a surprise attack against the advancing Swedish forces on the banks of the Neva River, securing a victory that halted Swedish expansion into northwestern Russia and earned him the surname "Nevsky."
* **Battle on the Ice (1242 CE):** Facing a systematic invasion by the Teutonic Knights (who had captured Pskov), Alexander mobilized a force and met the mailclad crusaders on the frozen Lake Peipus (Chud) on April 5, 1242. His tactical envelope of the German flank resulted in a complete rout, known in Russian tradition as the "massacre on the ice" (l. 1297).
* **Policy of Mongol Collaboration:** Alexander recognized that military resistance to the Golden Horde was hopeless. He adopted a policy of humble submission and cooperation, traveling repeatedly to Sarai and even to Karakorum. He became a favorite of the khan, who appointed him Grand Prince of Russia in 1252.
* **Suppression of Anti-Mongol Rebellions:** To prevent punitive Mongol expeditions that would lay waste to the remaining Russian cities, Alexander actively enforced Mongol taxation and suppressed local anti-Mongol uprisings, notably in Novgorod.

**Additional detail from Cambridge History of Russia Vol. I (Perrie ed. 2006, Martin ch. ll. 3709–3948):** On eve, NE defined by Riurikid and Church; Vladimir principality core (ll. 3717–3748). After invasion, succession: Iaroslav (d.1246); Sviatoslav division; Andrei 1249 after Karakorum; "Aleksandr returned to Vladimir in the company of a Tatar military force and evicted Andrei" 1252; Aleksandr Nevskii prince of Vladimir 1252–63 (ll. 3923–3927). Marked by co-operation: accompanied Tatar officials for 1257 Novgorod census; "inflicted punishment on Novgorod" for resistance; upon return "joined the Tatars to enforce the order to take the census in Novgorod" (ll. 3946–3948). Novgorod (beyond Vladimir borders) had accepted Vladimir princes since early 13th c.; controlled vast northern empire to Urals + Baltic trade (ll. 3942–3944). His youngest son Daniil founded Moscow branch (cross-ref). See [[golden-horde]], [[novgorod]], [[vladimir]], [[rurikid-dynasty]], [[moscow]].

## Historical Significance
Alexander Nevsky's double policy—military resistance to the Roman Catholic West and diplomatic appeasement of the Mongol East—profoundly shaped the historical trajectory of Russia. 

By crushing the Swedes and Teutonic Knights, he preserved Russia's northwestern borders and its Orthodox religious identity from Catholic crusader expansion. By cooperating with the Golden Horde, he stabilized the Russian lands, allowed economic recovery to begin, and saved Novgorod and other northern territories from the wholesale destruction that had leveled Kiev (l. 1301-1305). His youngest son, Daniel, founded the branch of Rurikids that would rule Moscow.
