# Al-Junayd of Baghdad

Abū al-Qāsim al-Junayd (c. 830–910), the foremost master of the [[early-sufism|formative period]] of
[[sufism|Sufism]] and the architect of its **"sober" (ṣaḥw)** tradition — the disciplined, law-abiding
mysticism that contained ecstatic experience within the bounds of the [[sharia]], as against the
"intoxicated" (*sukr*) tendency of Abū Yazīd al-Bisṭāmī and his own pupil [[al-hallaj|al-Ḥallāj]]. His
teaching on [[fana-and-baqa|annihilation and subsistence]], on *tawḥīd*, and on the primordial covenant
(*mīthāq*) — the soul's pre-eternal witnessing of God — became foundational for nearly all later Sufism.

Revered as "the master of the order" (*shaykh al-ṭāʾifa*), Junayd stands at the head of the spiritual
genealogies (*silsila*) of most later [[tariqa-orders|Sufi orders]], who traced their sober, sharia-bound
path back to him.
