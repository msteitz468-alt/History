# Robert M. Citino (1958– )

American military historian specializing in the German way of war from the early modern period through the Third Reich. Author of multiple volumes in the University Press of Kansas Modern War Studies series.

## Major Works and Contribution
- *Death of the Wehrmacht: The German Campaigns of 1942* (2007) — core operational study of the Wehrmacht's last classic *Bewegungskrieg* victories in spring 1942 (Crimea, Kharkov, Gazala/Tobruk) and the structural breakdown during the summer campaigns (Fall Blau and North African parallel operations). Argues that 1942 marked the effective end of the centuries-old Prussian-German tradition of operational-level maneuver, Kesselschlacht, and decentralized command initiative, even before the army's later physical destruction. Provides the interpretive backbone for 1942 turning points in the warfare hub.
- Contributed chapters to *The West Point History of World War II* (Rogers & Seidule eds.) on turning points, global war, and victory in Europe.
- Related works: *The German Way of War* (2005) and other volumes tracing Bewegungskrieg from the Great Elector through Frederick, Moltke, and into the world wars.
