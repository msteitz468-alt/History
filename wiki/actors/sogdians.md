# The Sogdians

## Overview

The **Sogdians** were the premier **merchant people of the Silk Roads**, an Iranian-speaking
population of the oasis city-states between the **Syr and Amu rivers** — **Samarkand,
Bukhara**, and their neighbors. In [[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (Liu, Ch 17) they are the
human connective tissue of the trans-Eurasian network: a trading **diaspora** linking China,
India, Persia, the steppe, and the Mediterranean, and the carriers of religion and culture
along the routes.

## A Commonwealth of Trading Cities

Sogdiana never formed a unified state — its walled cities, often at war with one another,
instead constituted "a commonwealth of culture and a uniquely Sogdian identity," bound by a
common **Sogdian language** (related to ancient Persian). Sitting at the knot of the
north–south and east–west routes, they welcomed travelers of every faith; their own funerary
practice shows them to have been predominantly **Zoroastrian**.

## Network-Builders and Religious Carriers

Sogdian traders, "donning their colorful, glittering silk robes," established diaspora
networks across the **[[silk-roads|Silk Roads]]** and served as the commercial **agents for
nomads** who obtained Chinese silk but lacked the skills to sell it. They were among the
**first missionaries of [[buddhism|Buddhism]] from India to China** — inscribing their names
and Buddhist devotions on boulders along the upper Indus — and later carried **Manichaeism**
into Central Asia and Tang China, while keeping their own Zoroastrian faith (attested by
sixth-century Sogdian merchant burials in China).

## Significance

The Sogdians embody CWH IV's "**networks**" — the argument that world history was driven not
only by states and empires but by the merchant diasporas that connected them. They sustained
Silk Roads commerce and cross-cultural religious transmission even between the rise and fall
of the great imperial patrons, until the **Islamic conquest of Central Asia** (8th c.) and
Turkic settlement gradually absorbed them.

## Historiography

The Sogdians are reconstructed from their own trade letters, inscriptions, tomb art in
China, and the records of the cultures they traded with. Their prominence has been
recovered by recent scholarship (de la Vaissière) as a corrective to empire-centered Silk
Roads narratives, complementing the [[silk-roads]] and [[trade-network-formation]] pages.

## A Secondary Role in the First Silk Roads Era (Benjamin 2018)

[[benjamin-empires-ancient-eurasia-2018|Benjamin]] (following de la Vaissière) stresses
that the Sogdians' dominance came **later**: during the [[silk-roads|First Silk Roads Era]]
they played only a **secondary role**, probably the same people [[zhang-qian|Zhang Qian]]
observed in **Dayuan (Ferghana) and Kangju**, whom the *Shiji* calls skillful at commerce,
haggling "over a fraction of a cent." Kushan coin finds are scarce in Sogdiana, so the
[[kushan-empire|Kushans]] likely never annexed it, though relations were cordial — and de
la Vaissière speculates Sogdian merchants migrated *into* the wealthier Kushan cities. It
was the **collapse of the Kushans** that opened the Sogdians' opportunity: they emerged as
the dominant overland intermediaries between Tang China and the
[[sasanian-empire|Sasanians]], the quintessential carriers of the **Second** Silk Roads Era
(see [[collapse-of-first-silk-roads-era-250]]).
