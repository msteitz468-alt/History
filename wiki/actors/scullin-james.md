# James Scullin

Labor Prime Minister of Australia (1929–1932), whose government was overwhelmed
by the onset of the Great Depression.

## Overview

Scullin led the [[australian-labour-movement|Labor Party]] to office in October
1929, days before the Wall Street crash. His government confronted the
[[australian-1930s-depression|Depression]] with little control over monetary
policy — the Commonwealth Bank Board and London creditors constrained its options
— and fractured over how to respond. The deflationary **Premiers' Plan** of 1931
(wage and pension cuts, budget austerity) split Labor three ways: the
right-wing defection of Joseph Lyons (who formed the [[united-australia-party]])
and the radical breakaway of the New South Wales premier Jack Lang
([[lang-jack]]).

## Significance

Scullin's premiership is a textbook case of a reformist government rendered
impotent by external financial dependence and institutional constraints on
policy. The party's disintegration handed government to Lyons and kept Labor out
of office until 1941.
