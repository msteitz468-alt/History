# Gnaeus Julius Agricola

Roman senator and general (40–93 CE), the **governor who carried Roman conquest to the far north of
Britain** — and the subject of the biography by his son-in-law **[[tacitus|Tacitus]]**, the
*[[tacitus-agricola-germany|Agricola]]*, through which he is almost entirely known.

## Career

Born at Forum Julii (Fréjus) in Gallia Narbonensis, Agricola served his military apprenticeship in
**[[roman-britain|Britain]]** under Suetonius Paulinus during the **Boudican revolt (60–61)**, then
rose through the *cursus honorum* under the Flavians: legionary legate in Britain, governor of
Aquitania, consul (77), and finally **governor of Britain (77–84)** — an unusually long tenure.

As governor he completed the subjugation of north Wales (Anglesey) and northern England, advanced into
**Caledonia (Scotland)**, planted forts, and in **84 CE won the battle of Mons Graupius** against a
confederation of Caledonian tribes led by the chieftain **Calgacus** — the northernmost pitched battle
of Roman Britain. His fleet circumnavigated Britain, confirming it an island. Recalled by **Domitian**
— out of jealousy, Tacitus claims — he retired to private life and died in 93, amid rumours (which
Tacitus reports but does not assert) of poisoning.

## Significance

Agricola matters less for what he permanently achieved — the Caledonian conquest was soon abandoned,
the frontier falling back to what would become **Hadrian's Wall** — than for the **text he generated**.
Through the *Agricola*, Tacitus made his father-in-law the exemplar of his central political argument:
that **a good man can serve honourably even under a bad emperor**, that integrity need not mean
futile martyrdom. Agricola is also the lens through which the *Agricola* preserves our fullest early
picture of Roman Britain and the most famous ancient critique of empire — the **Calgacus speech**
("they make a desert and call it peace"), which Tacitus, with deep irony, gives to Agricola's defeated
enemy.
