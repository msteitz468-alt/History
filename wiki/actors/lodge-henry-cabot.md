# Henry Cabot Lodge Jr.

U.S. Ambassador to South Vietnam (1963–1964 and 1965–1967). Prominent Republican, 1960 vice-presidential candidate (with Nixon), and longtime political rival of John F. Kennedy. Appointed by JFK in a surprise bipartisan move to replace Frederick Nolting in mid-1963.

## Role in the 1963 Coup

Lodge arrived in Saigon at the height of the Buddhist crisis. He quickly concluded that Diem and especially Nhu had to go. He became the primary channel for communications with the plotting South Vietnamese generals (via CIA's Lucien Conein). Washington gave him wide latitude; Kennedy's famous "green light" signals and the decision to leave final restraint authority with Lodge effectively made the ambassador the on-the-ground decider. Lodge feuded with MACV commander Paul Harkins and CIA station chief John Richardson; he was seen by some as operating independently.

## Personality and Style

Haughty, patrician, lone-wolf operator. Ignored or worked around Washington instructions when they conflicted with his views (e.g., resuming contact with Diem). After the coup he was briefly a 1964 Republican presidential contender.
