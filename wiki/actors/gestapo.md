# Gestapo (Geheime Staatspolizei)

The Secret State Police, created by [[goring-hermann|Göring]] in Prussia in 1933
and absorbed into [[himmler-heinrich|Himmler]]'s SS police empire by 1936. Its
core instrument was "protective custody" — indefinite detention without judicial
review, feeding the concentration camps. Modern research (Gellately, Mallmann/
Paul) shows it was a surprisingly small agency heavily dependent on voluntary
denunciations from the population — terror was targeted at defined enemy
categories while most "national comrades" experienced it only as background
threat.

## Evans (2005) — size, denunciation, and the limits of "self-policing"

The numbers behind the myth: 41 officers in Stettin, 44 in Bremen, 281 agents
for the 4-million-person Lower Rhine district (1937); of ~20,000 Gestapo
officers in 1939 only 3,000 were SS members — the staff were career policemen,
including chief Heinrich Müller, who had once called Hitler "an Austrian
draft-dodger" but was kept by Heydrich for his fanatical anti-Communism.
Fewer than 10% of cases came from its own investigations; the rest from
denunciations — of which study after study (Saarbrücken, Düsseldorf, Augsburg)
shows most were privately motivated or motiveless, and which were
statistically *rare* (Lippe: 292 denunciations to Party agencies across
1933–45; only 17,168 Malicious Gossip cases Reich-wide in 1937 against
contraventions "many hundred times greater"). Evans uses this to push back on
the "self-policing society" thesis (Gellately taken too far): denunciations
only mattered because the Gestapo's active pursuit of defined enemy
categories gave them teeth — the top-down terror was real. Its instruments:
"protective custody" without judicial review; re-arrest of released prisoners
and of Jews completing "race defilement" sentences (from March 1938) straight
into camps; interrogation beatings formalized in 1937 at "25 lashes with a
standard cane, administered before a doctor"; card indices of enemies
(Berlin HQ budget: 1m RM 1933 → 40m 1937); by 1938 agents embedded in
factories to catch "slackers" and undercover on KdF cruises.

## Evans (2015) — the same argument sharpened

Evans's British Academy lecture (in [[evans-third-reich-history-memory-2015]])
adds: 1937 staffing ~7,000 nationwide for 60+ million (the consent school's
favorite figure, which Evans relativizes rather than disputes); Koblenz/Trier
files show systematic paid *and* unpaid informer networks, about a third of
informers NSDAP-affiliated; most officers were Weimar-era police carryovers.
His structural point — the Gestapo was "only one small though important part"
of a "polymorphous, uncoordinated but pervasive system of control" (courts,
Special Courts, People's Court, prisons, Block Wardens, Labour Front, Hitler
Youth) — is developed at [[coercion-and-consent-nazi-germany]].

## Historiography

Gellately, *The Gestapo and German Society* (1990) reoriented the field from
omnipotent-police myth to denunciation-driven "self-policing society"; Evans
synthesizes this while insisting the terror was nonetheless real and central.
