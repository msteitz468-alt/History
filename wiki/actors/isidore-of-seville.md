# Isidore of Seville

Bishop, encyclopedist and "**the last scholar of the ancient world**" (c. 560–636), whose vast
compilations preserved a great part of classical learning for the Latin Middle Ages — above all the
*Etymologies*, the most widely used reference book of medieval Europe.

## Life

Of a prominent Hispano-Roman family of **Cartagena**, Isidore succeeded his brother **Leander** as
**bishop of Seville** (c. 600) in the now-Catholic **Visigothic kingdom** (after Reccared's conversion at
the **[[third-council-of-toledo-589|Third Council of Toledo]]**). He was the dominant churchman of his
age in Spain, presiding over the **Fourth Council of Toledo (633)**, which shaped the Visigothic church
and kingdom, and advising kings — his scientific work *On the Nature of Things* was written for King
**Sisebut**. He died at Seville in 636 and was later declared a Doctor of the Church.

## Works

Isidore was the supreme **encyclopedist and compiler** of late antiquity:

- the ***Etymologies* (*Etymologiae* / *Origines*)** — a twenty-book encyclopedia of all knowledge,
  organized by the origins of words, covering the seven liberal arts, medicine, law, theology, God and
  the angels, the human being, animals, the cosmos, geography, architecture, agriculture, war, ships,
  food and dress: the **reference book of the Middle Ages**, surviving in over a thousand manuscripts;
- ***On the Nature of Things* (*De natura rerum*)** — a treatise on cosmology, time and natural
  phenomena (now ingested as **[[kendall-wallis-isidore-of-seville|TTH 66]]**);
- the ***History of the Kings of the Goths*** — his patriotic history of Visigothic Spain (ingested in
  **[[wolf-conquerors-chroniclers-spain|Conquerors and Chroniclers of Early Medieval Spain]]**), framed
  by the celebrated *Laus Spaniae*;
- and works of theology, exegesis, chronology and grammar (the *Sententiae*, the *Chronica*, *On
  Illustrious Men*).

## Significance

Isidore stands at the **hinge between the ancient and medieval worlds**: by gathering and ordering the
fragments of classical knowledge into compendia, he made that knowledge transmissible to a Latin West
that could no longer read the originals. The *Etymologies* shaped medieval education, encyclopedism, and
the very habits of classification for nearly a millennium — for which, in 2000, he was (semi-officially)
proposed as the patron saint of the Internet.

## From Logan, History of the Church in the Middle Ages (2013)

- **Anti-Jewish policy and Isidore’s opposition (R1-032):** King Sisebut (612–20) forced Jews to choose baptism or banishment, creating crypto-Jews. Isidore of Seville opposed the order; Fourth Council of Toledo (633) overturned it temporarily, but later councils (Sixth 638, Eighth 653) renewed anti-Jewish canons—after the Catholic turn at [[third-council-of-toledo-589|Toledo III (589)]].
  - Quote: "King Sisebut (612–20) gave the Jews the choice between baptism and banishment."
- **Etymologies as medieval basic book (R1-033):** Isidore (d. 636) produced *Etymologies*, extant in over 1,000 manuscripts—a virtual encyclopedia called “the basic book of the entire Middle Ages”; Bede borrowed heavily for *De natura rerum*. Influence in Europe to the Reformation and longer in Spain.
  - Quote: "It has been called 'the basic book of the entire Middle Ages'."
