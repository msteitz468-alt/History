## Overview

*Australopithecus afarensis* is the best-known early bipedal hominid species,
living in eastern Africa (Ethiopia, Tanzania, possibly Chad) between about 4.0 and
3.0 million years ago (BP). It is represented by an unusually rich sample,
including the famous partial skeleton **"Lucy"** (Hadar, Ethiopia, 1974; ~3.18
mya) and the "First Family" assemblage of ~13 individuals. In
[[tattersall-world-from-beginnings-2008|Tattersall's]] framing it exemplifies the
"bipedal ape": habitually upright on the ground yet retaining an ape-sized brain
and substantial arboreal capability — proof that upright posture, not brain
expansion, came first in [[human-evolution]].

## Anatomy and Locomotion

- **Bipedal pelvis and knee:** broad, shallow, flaring pelvis and an inward-slanting
  femur with a right-angled knee surface — diagnostic of upright posture. See
  [[bipedalism-origins]].
- **Retained arboreal traits:** longer arms relative to legs, a funnel-shaped (apewise)
  rib cage, curved and elongated toe bones, small hip-joint surfaces — "have your
  cake and eat it too" locomotion, not as agile in trees as apes nor as efficient on
  the ground as later humans.
- **Marked sexual dimorphism:** Lucy stood ~3 ft and weighed perhaps 60 lb; males
  may have reached ~4 ft and ~100 lb. The size gap is typical of polygynous
  hominoids — a key objection to Owen Lovejoy's pair-bonding model of bipedal origins.
- **Dentition:** large cheek teeth with thick enamel (a shift toward tougher foods),
  but a still-projecting canine with a small upper diastema — a mosaic of ape and
  human traits.
- **Ape-grade development and cognition:** rapid, ape-like maturation; no evidence of
  stone toolmaking, which lay far in the future.

## Significance (Counterfactual)

Long treated as a plausible stem near the ancestry of later *Australopithecus*,
*Paranthropus*, and ultimately *Homo*, though Tattersall stresses that no specific
ancestor–descendant line can be confirmed (only general relationships are testable).
Its analytic importance is evidential rather than agentive: "Lucy" decisively
demonstrated **bipedalism before encephalization**, demolishing the brain-first
expectation that the [[tattersall-world-from-beginnings-2008|Piltdown]] hoax had
sustained, and anchoring the picture of the early hominid record as a bushy
[[punctuated-equilibria|radiation]] of experimental forms.

## Historiography

The locomotor debate — how much *A. afarensis* climbed vs. walked — remains
genuinely open, with some scholars emphasizing skeletal specializations for
bipedality and others the retained tree-living features; Tattersall reports an
emerging middle position. Whether the species is one variable taxon or several
(the central Chad jaw "*A. bahrelghazali*"; the splitter–lumper question) is also
unsettled. Dating rests on K/Ar and Ar/Ar of volcanic tuffs interbedded with the
fossils. Part of the African record where collection coverage is strong (archaeology
and paleoanthropology).
