# Kamose

**Kamose** ("Kamose the Brave"), the last king of the Theban **17th Dynasty**
(r. ~1555–1550 BCE), opened the war of liberation against the
[[hyksos|Hyksos]] that his successor [[ahmose-i|Ahmose I]] would complete — the
campaign that ended the [[processes/second-intermediate-period|Second Intermediate
Period]]. Only his third regnal year is attested, and he probably died soon after it.

## The war on two fronts

Kamose's opening address to his councillors frames the predicament of a divided Egypt:
"Why do I contemplate my strength while there is one Great Man in Avaris and another in
Kush, sitting united with an Asiatic and a Nubian while each man possesses his slice of
Egypt." In his year-3 campaign he moved north with army and fleet, sacked Nefrusi above
Cusae, and **blockaded [[places/avaris|Avaris]]** by water — plundering its territory but
not taking the city, since "Apepi refused to engage him." He also **intercepted a letter**
from the Hyksos king [[hyksos|Apepi]] to the [[kerma-kingdom|King of Kush]] (carried via the
Western Desert oases) and sent troops to Bahariya Oasis to cut that alliance — and retook
[[places/buhen|Buhen]] in Nubia.

The campaign was, the volume cautions, "far from definitive, perhaps no more than a raid,
given that the final destruction of Avaris did not take place until over twenty years
later." Its record survives on **two stelae at Karnak** and a near-contemporary
writing-board copy.

## Significance

Kamose marks the moment the Theban resistance went on the offensive against both the
Hyksos north and the Kushite south. His funerary cult endured into Ramesside times — one of
his Karnak stelae still stood over two centuries later. His exact parentage is unknown ("his
coffin carried no uraeus, emblem of royalty"), though tradition makes him the son of
[[actors/seqenenra-taa|Seqenenra Taa]] and elder brother of Ahmose.
