# Sundiata Keita

**Sundiata Keita** (also Sunjata, Mari Djata; c. 1217–c. 1255 by tradition) was the **founder of the
[[mali-empire|Mali Empire]]** — the Mande hero whose victory over the kingdom of Sosso united the Manding
chiefdoms into the greatest of the medieval West African states. He is known almost entirely through the
**oral epic** that bears his name (**[[niane-sundiata|*Sundiata*]]**), supplemented by the Arabic notices
of al-Umari and Ibn Khaldun; the line between the historical king and the legendary culture-hero cannot be
cleanly drawn.

## The traditional life

In the epic, Sundiata is the son of the Mande king **Maghan Kon Fatta** and **Sogolon** (the "buffalo
woman"), born crippled and mocked, who rises — literally, in the famous scene of rising on an iron rod — to
become a hunter and warrior. Exiled by a rival queen, he returns from the Sahel to lead a coalition against
**Soumaoro Kanté (Sumanguru)**, the **sorcerer-king of Sosso** who had conquered the Mande after the
collapse of [[ghana-empire|Wagadu/Ghana]]. Sundiata breaks Soumaoro's magic and army at the **Battle of
Krina (Kirina, c. 1235)**, then subdues the surrounding kingdoms.

## Founder of the state

Sundiata's enduring achievement, in the tradition, was not conquest but **constitution**: at the great
assembly of **Kurukan Fuga** (near Ka-ba) the clans recognized him as ***mansa*** (king of kings) and
agreed a division of offices, lands and rights among the lineages — a charter (the so-called "Kurukan Fuga
constitution") that the Mande remember as the foundation of their social order. He established the capital
at **Niani**, took control of the gold-and-salt arteries of the [[trans-saharan-trade|trans-Saharan
trade]], and founded the **Keita dynasty** that would culminate in [[mansa-musa|Mansa Musa]].

## The documentary cross-check (Ajayi & Crowder / Levtzion)

[[ajayi-crowder-history-west-africa-v1-1971|*History of West Africa* Vol. I]] (Levtzion, Ch. 4)
supplies the crucial point that **Sundiata is not only a legend**: the **Mari-Djata** who opens
Ibn Khaldun's chronicle of the kings of Mali — the great king who "overcame the Sosso, conquered
their land and wrested power from their hands" — should "undoubtedly be identified with the
Sundjata of the oral traditions." Whereas the authenticity of the *pre*-Sundiata Keita kings
cannot be established, **from Sundiata onward the dynasty is documented**. The intermediate
**Sosso** kingdom under **Sumanguru Kante**, absent from every other Arabic source, is likewise
known only through oral tradition but fits the documented sequence (Ghana → Sosso → Mali). Sundiata
is thus a rare case where the [[griot-oral-tradition|griot tradition]] and the external written
record **converge** on the same historical figure.

## Significance

Sundiata is the **archetypal African state-founder** and the central figure of Mande cultural memory — the
ancestor-hero whose taboos and deeds "still guide men," in the griot's words. His career marks the
**transfer of West African hegemony** from Ghana/Sosso to Mali, and the consolidation of the Mande world
that carried Islam, trade, and the *jeli* tradition across the western Sudan.
