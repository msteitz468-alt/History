# Russian Federation

The **Russian Federation** is the primary successor state to the Soviet Union following its dissolution in December 1991.

## Biography / Description
The Russian Federation emerged as an independent sovereign state following the collapse of the USSR. Under its first president, [[yeltsin-boris|Boris Yeltsin]], the country embarked on a rapid transition from a state-directed communist system to a market-based economy and democratic governance. 

## Key Developments (1991–1999)
* **Dissolution of the USSR:** Formally established in December 1991 following the Belovezha Accords, which dissolved the union structure.
* **[[economic-shock-therapy|Economic Shock Therapy]]:** A series of rapid free-market reforms launched in 1992, which led to hyperinflation, voucher privatization, and the concentration of national assets in the hands of a new class of oligarchs.
* **[[russian-constitutional-crisis-1993|1993 Constitutional Crisis]]:** A violent confrontation between President Yeltsin and the Russian parliament (Supreme Soviet), resulting in the shelling of the parliament building and the creation of a new constitution that heavily concentrated power in the executive branch.
* **[[first-chechen-war-1994|First Chechen War]] (1994–1996):** A disastrous military campaign to prevent the secession of Chechnya, ending in de facto Chechen autonomy.
* **[[russian-financial-crisis-1998|1998 Financial Crisis]]:** An economic default and ruble devaluation that wiped out the savings of the emerging middle class and led to the collapse of early market-reform coalitions.

## Historical Significance
The emergence of the Russian Federation marked the end of the Cold War and the ideological division of Europe. The early years of the federation were characterized by intense economic distress, high corruption, and political instability, which eventually paved the way for a reversion to more centralized, state-directed governance at the end of the decade.
