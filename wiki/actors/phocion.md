# Phocion

Phocion (c. 402–318 BCE) was an Athenian statesman and general, elected general an unprecedented 45 times, known in antiquity as "the Good" for his personal incorruptibility and austere lifestyle. A student of Plato and associate of Xenocrates, he combined a philosophical temperament with practical military competence, commanding Athenian forces in the Aegean and against Philip II's allies.

Politically he was a consistent advocate of accommodation with Macedon, opposing the anti-Macedonian policies of [[demosthenes-orator|Demosthenes]] and Hyperides. He judged that Athens lacked the military strength to resist Macedon and that provoking Philip or [[alexander-the-great]] risked the city's destruction — a position vindicated, in his view, by the [[lamian-war-323|Lamian War]] (323–322 BCE), the Greek uprising against Macedonian rule following Alexander's death, which he opposed and which ended in catastrophic Greek defeat.

After the Lamian War's failure, Antipater imposed an oligarchic, property-qualified constitution on Athens with Phocion as its chief guarantor, alienating the restored democratic faction. When [[antipater]] died in 319 BCE and [[polyperchon]] briefly reversed Macedonian policy toward Athens to court democratic support, Phocion — now identified with the discredited oligarchy — was condemned by an Athenian assembly and executed by hemlock in 318 BCE, a fate later compared by ancient writers to that of Socrates.
