# Túpac Amaru II (José Gabriel Condorcanqui)

José Gabriel Condorcanqui (c. 1738–1781), a *curaca* (hereditary Andean lord) of Tinta,
Surimana, and Tungasuca in the Cusco region and a prosperous muleteer, who claimed descent
from the last Inca and adopted the name **Túpac Amaru II** when he launched the great
Andean rebellion of 1780. He is the central figure of the
[[tupac-amaru-rebellion-1780|rebellion that bears his name]].

This is the concise summary; the rebellion is detailed on its own
[[tupac-amaru-rebellion-1780|event page]].

## Role and Significance

A literate, bicultural intermediary between the colonial order and Andean society, Túpac
Amaru II combined grievances over the *repartimiento*, the [[mita]], and [[bourbon-reforms|
Bourbon taxation]] with a claim to Inca legitimacy. His execution in Cusco in May 1781 —
and the subsequent ban on Inca symbols and the [[curaca|curaca]] nobility — made him a
lasting symbol of indigenous resistance, later invoked by independence leaders and by
twentieth-century movements from Peruvian *indigenismo* to Uruguayan and Andean radicalism.

## Counterfactual Significance

Had the rebellion succeeded — or had the Bourbon state responded with concession rather
than annihilation of the Inca nobility — the trajectory of late-colonial Andean society and
the relationship between indigenous identity and the later nation-states might have differed
substantially.
