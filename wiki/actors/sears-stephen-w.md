# Stephen W. Sears

Stephen W. Sears is a leading American Civil War historian and author of influential operational and narrative battle studies. A former editor at *American Heritage*, he is noted for deep research in primary sources—letters, diaries, journals, and especially the unique postwar veteran survey conducted by Ezra Carman and John Gould for the Antietam battlefield.

## Role and Major Works
Sears's books set a standard for single-battle and campaign studies used in staff-college-level analysis. *Landscape Turned Red* (1983) is his landmark treatment of the Maryland Campaign and the Battle of Antietam (17 September 1862), the bloodiest day in U.S. history. It combines high-level operational context (Lee's invasion decision, the Lost Order, South Mountain, deployment at Sharpsburg, McClellan's pursuit failure) with granular, human-scale accounts of the fighting.

Other major works include studies of Chancellorsville and Gettysburg that similarly blend command decision-making with the experience of the soldiers on the ground.

## Counterfactual Significance
Without Sears's synthesis of the Carman-Gould veteran material and his clear operational narrative, the wiki's understanding of Antietam would lack the rich eyewitness texture and the precise tracing of how Lee's divided forces and McClellan's caution shaped the bloodiest day and its larger consequences. His work anchors the "why the invasion ended where and how it did" analysis in the Long 19th Century warfare hub.

## Historiography Note
Sears represents the post-1960s wave of Civil War scholarship that moved beyond Lost Cause and "Great Captain" narratives toward primary-driven, multi-perspective operational history. His books are frequently paired with West Point-derived studies for their complementary strengths in primary voice versus map/OOB precision.
