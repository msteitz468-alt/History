# Alexander Radishchev

## Biography / Description
**Alexander Radishchev** was a Russian writer, government official, and social critic from the gentry class, who is historically regarded as the father of the Russian radical intelligentsia (L3744-3746, L3750). Educated at the University of Leipzig, he absorbed the egalitarian and radical ideas of the late European Enlightenment, particularly the works of Rousseau and Mably (L3744). 

Upon his return, he served in the Russian civil service (L3744). In 1790, he privately published his masterpiece, *Journey from Petersburg to Moscow*, a scathing expose of the horrors of Russian serfdom (L3744). Frightened by the French Revolution, Catherine the Great condemned the book, and Radishchev was arrested, sentenced to death, and subsequently exiled to Siberia (L3746).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* *The Journey from Petersburg to Moscow* (1790): Composed of twenty-odd chapters named after postal stations, the book presented a panorama of the cruelties of serfdom, describing serfs forced to work six days a week for their masters, the auctioning of family members, and arranged marriages (L3744).
* **Call for Reforms:** Beyond exposing individual abuses, Radishchev attacked Russian autocracy and administrative corruption (L3744). He advocated for the establishment of a Russian republic, proposed a detailed plan for the emancipation of the serfs, and suggested a corresponding land distribution settlement (L3744-3746).

## Historical Significance
Radishchev's work broke through the veneer of enlightened progressivism promoted by Catherine the Great (L3744). His arrest and exile marked the end of the state-led phase of Russian Enlightenment (L3746-3750). From 1790 onward, the Russian autocracy began to systematically censor and suppress the very Western ideas it had previously introduced (L3746-3750). 

Radishchev's *Journey* represents the birth of independent intellectual protest in Russia, serving as the ideological predecessor to the 19th-century revolutionary movements (L3750).
