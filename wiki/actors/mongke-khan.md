# Möngke Khan (Mongke)

## Overview

**Möngke** (Mongke; c. 1209–1259), eldest son of [[tolui|Tolui]] and [[sorghaghtani-beki|Sorghaghtani Beki]], was Great Khan (r. 1251–1259) who restored Toluid control after the chaotic regencies of [[toregene-khatun|Töregene]] and Oghul Ghaimish. Weatherford: he was “the last of Genghis Khan’s descendants to be acknowledged and accepted as Great Khan by the whole of the Mongol Empire”; under him the empire reached its greatest extent before the post-1259 fracture. He ordered the dual grand campaigns of his brothers [[kublai-khan|Khubilai]] (Southern Song) and [[hulegu|Hülegü]] (Assassins and Abbasids), culminating in the [[sack-of-baghdad-1258|fall of Baghdad (1258)]]. His death on 11 August 1259 precipitated the succession war between Khubilai and Ariq Böke that permanently fractured unified rule.

## Role

Administrative reformer and census-maker; enforcer of Chinggisid discipline after Ögödeid misrule. Self-description (Weatherford): “I follow the laws of my ancestors; I do not imitate other countries’ ways.” Serious, without Ögödei’s frivolity or Güyük’s recklessness; “almost alone among members of the Golden Family, avoided the destructive bind of alcoholism.”

## Key events and processes (Weatherford 2004)

- **Early command.** Co-commanded the European war effort with Batu; led southern force against Kipchak (1236); leadership role in the Kiev assault (winter 1240; city taken 6 December 1240).
- **1251 election.** Groundwork by Sorghaghtani and Batu: boycotted Issykul *khuriltai* 1250, then proclamation on the sacred homeland 1 July 1251 (age 43), with Berke’s 30,000-man guard. Week of feasting—Weatherford’s figures: 300 horses/oxen, 3,000 sheep, 2,000 wagons of *airak* daily.
- **Purge.** Ögödei/Güyük princes arrived mid-celebration; arrested as ruse for sneak attack. Advisers (mostly Muslims and Chinese) tortured with canes; two princes’ mouths stuffed with stones and dirt until death; seventy-seven people within or close to Ögödei’s lineage executed. Empire-wide inquisitors from China/Mongolia to Afghanistan, Persia, and Iraq; rooted out Chaghadaid and Ögödeid supporters; seized [[karakorum|Karakorum]]; survivors presented loyalty there. Then general amnesty for nonpolitical prisoners.
- **Tolui elevated.** 1252: retroactively awarded father Tolui the title Great Khan as *otchigin* heir to titles and homeland.
- **Karakorum as imperial capital.** Transformed Ögödeid family seat into imperial center; Guillaume Boucher (Parisian goldsmith captured at Belgrade) and fifty assistants built the Silver Tree—silver tree, golden serpents, pneumatic angel trumpet, four drinks (wine, black *airak*, rice wine, mead) for four directions.
- **Religion.** Christian wives and Caucasian loyalties briefly raised Christian profile. William of Rubruck (late 1253) described Christmas mass with Möngke and wife on a golden couch. Official audience 24 May 1254; ordered inter-religious debate before Christian, Muslim, and Buddhist judges under pain of death for “words of contention”; ended with singing, Koran recitation, Buddhist meditation, “everyone simply too drunk to continue.” Discharge message: “Just as God gave different fingers to the hand so has He given different ways to men”; letter to Louis IX—one Eternal God in heaven, one lord Genghis Khan Son of God on earth—submission or war (posthumous messianic rhetoric about Genghis noted as later accretion).
- **Administration.** Censuses of people, animals, orchards, farms—registers to Karakorum for taxes, soldiers, laborers, oversight. Paid Güyük’s merchant debts so business continued (Juvaini’s marvel). 1253 Department of Monetary Affairs to control/standardize paper money; *sukhe* silver ingot divided into 500 parts as universal measure; monetized taxes from China to Persia—infrastructure of the [[pax-mongolica|Pax Mongolica]].
- **1253 dual offensive.** Spring small *khuriltai*: Ögödei and Chagatai families crushed; Jochid west effectively independent via Sorghaghtani–Batu alliance. Hülegü Army of the Right (Baghdad, Damascus, Cairo); Khubilai Army of the Left (Sung); Möngke center, Ariq Böke as *otchigin*. Both headed out May 1253. Later stripped Khubilai of financial prerogatives over corruption, then publicly forgave him at Karakorum—tensions unresolved.
- **Death.** Fall 1257 *khuriltai*: Möngke would lead Sung campaign himself; left central administration and heir to Ariq Böke. Died 11 August 1259—Chinese: cholera; Persians: dysentery; others: battle arrow. “The death of Mongke Khan congealed the empire; advancement ceased.” Weatherford: he began but never finished a “Second Mongol World War”; it “flickered out.”

## Counterfactual significance

Möngke’s death without a settled succession is a classic fracture point: a longer reign might have delayed the 1260–64 civil war and the split into effectively independent khanates. Western limit after his death: Mamluk victory at Ayn Jalut, 3 September 1260.

## Historiography

Weatherford stresses the “warring queens” prelude and Sorghaghtani’s role elevating the Toluid line; core narrative also in Juvaini, Rashid al-Din, Rubruck, and modern syntheses ([[may-mongol-conquests-2012|May]], [[morgan-the-mongols|Morgan]]). Cause of death remains open across Chinese, Persian, and other traditions. Keep Weatherford’s “greatest extent / last universally acknowledged Great Khan” as attributed synthesis.
