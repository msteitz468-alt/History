# Otto III

**Otto III** (980–1002; king from 983, emperor from 996) was the most visionary and most controversial of the [[ottonian-dynasty|Ottonian]] emperors — the author of the **Renovatio Imperii Romanorum**, a programme to refound a Christian Roman empire centred on Rome itself. [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]] (Müller-Mertens) presents him as the culmination and the over-reach of Ottonian imperial ideology.

## Minority and Regency

Otto succeeded at the age of three on the sudden death of Otto II (983), amid the **Elbe Slav revolt** and a bid for the throne by **Henry the Quarrelsome**. The regencies of his Byzantine mother **Theophanu** (to 991) and grandmother **Adelaide** preserved the realm intact and continued Ottonian government — Theophanu even issuing diplomata as *Theophanius imperator augustus*. Otto received an exceptional education in **Latin and Greek**, fusing a Byzantine imperial sensibility (through Theophanu), an admiration for Charlemagne, and an intense ascetic religiosity.

## The Renovatio Imperii Romanorum

Crowned emperor in 996, Otto — guided by the chancellor Heribert, **Leo of Vercelli**, and above all **[[gerbert-of-aurillac|Gerbert of Aurillac]]** — pursued the **renewal of the Roman empire**. He:

- treated the **papacy as an imperial bishopric**, installing his kinsman Brun as **Gregory V** (the first German pope) and then Gerbert as **Sylvester II** — the name itself proclaiming Otto a new Constantine to Gerbert's new Sylvester;
- built an **imperial palace on the Palatine** in Rome, with **antique Roman court titles** and **Byzantine ceremonial**;
- sealed his diplomata with bulls reading ***renovatio imperii Romanorum***, adopting the ascetic formulae *servus Jesu Christi* and *servus apostolorum*;
- and **rejected the Donation of Constantine as a forgery**, refusing to ground imperial authority in papal grant.

## Gniezno and the New Christian Kingdoms

The Renovatio's most durable practical results lay in the east. On pilgrimage to the tomb of the martyred **St Adalbert of Prague** at **[[synod-of-gniezno-1000|Gniezno in 1000]]**, Otto founded a **Polish archbishopric**, concluded a friendship-pact with **[[boleslaw-i-chrobry|Bolesław Chrobry]]**, set his own diadem on Bolesław's head, and (in Thietmar's hostile words) made him "a lord instead of a tributary." He likewise sanctioned the new **Hungarian** church-province of Gran and sent **[[stephen-i-of-hungary|Stephen I]]** a royal crown. In Otto's vision these were not subject provinces but member-kingdoms of a renewed, Christian *imperium* — a "commonwealth" of kings under the emperor.

## Death and Assessment

A **Roman uprising** drove Otto and Sylvester from the city in 1001; he died near Rome in **January 1002, not yet twenty-two**, leaving no heir, the city unrecovered. His Renovatio was abandoned by his successor [[henry-ii-holy-roman-emperor|Henry II]] in favour of a German-focused kingship. Historians have read him as a brilliant idealist or a deluded dreamer; NCMH III stresses that the Renovatio, however short-lived in Rome, **permanently reshaped the political map of east-central Europe** by midwifing the Christian kingdoms of Poland and Hungary.
