# Yamhad (the Kingdom of Aleppo)

The dominant power of **northern Syria** in the [[middle-bronze-age|Middle Bronze
Age]] — the [[amorites|Amorite]] kingdom centred on **Aleppo** (Halab), whose kings
held a "**Great Kingship**" (*šarrūtum rabītum*) the Hittites later acknowledged as
equal to their own. Known mainly from the **[[mari|Mari]] letters** and the
**Alalakh tablets**, it is the western anchor of the Mari-age state system. Treated
in [[edwards-cah-v2p1-1973|CAH Vol. II, Part 1]] (Ch. I, Kupper).

## The first power of Syria

In the famous "balance-of-power" report from Mari, **Yarim-Lim I of Yamhad** ranks
*first* among the great kings — "twenty kings follow Yarim-Lim," more than follow
[[hammurabi|Hammurabi]], Rim-Sin, or Eshnunna (see [[interpolity-law]]). His prestige
rested on Aleppo's command of the **trade between Mesopotamia and the Mediterranean**:
caravans from Babylonia to Syria and Palestine had to pass through Yamhad's
territory, and the kingdom controlled the timber, the Cypriot copper, the
ivory-bearing elephants of north Syria, and the Aegean luxury goods that moved
through it. Yarim-Lim sheltered the exiled **[[zimri-lim|Zimri-Lim]]**, gave him a
daughter in marriage, and helped restore him to [[mari|Mari]].

## Alalakh and the structure of the kingdom

Yamhad ruled through **vassal kingdoms** — Carchemish, Qatna at times, and the
smaller **Alalakh** on the Orontes, which king **Abba-el** ceded to a junior branch
of the royal house (Yarim-Lim, brother of the next great king) after crushing a
revolt. The **Alalakh level VII tablets** document this vassal court and reveal a
society in which **[[hurrians|Hurrians]]** already held high office and whose
goddess **Hepat** had entered the official religion — the leading edge of the
Hurrian penetration of Syria that would, two centuries later, produce
[[hurrians|Mitanni]].

## The fall to the Hittites

Yamhad's "Great Kingship" was ended by the **[[hittites|Hittites]]**. **Hattusili I**
struck first at Alalakh (destroying level VII ~1620s), then enveloped Aleppo's
northern allies Urshu and Hashshum; his grandson **Mursili I** finally **destroyed
Aleppo itself** before marching on to sack [[fall-of-babylon-1595bce|Babylon]]. The
later **Treaty of Aleppo** (14th c.) recalls that it was Hattusili who first broke
"the great kingship of Aleppo." A weakened Yamhad survived as a lesser state,
increasingly Hurrianized, until it was absorbed into the Mitannian and then the
Hittite orbit.

## Significance

Yamhad shows that in the Mari age the **decisive weight of the state system lay in
Syria, not Babylonia** — a corrective to the Mesopotamia-centred narrative. It is
also the conduit through which the **[[hurrians|Hurrians]]** moved south into Syria,
and its fall is one limb of the same Hittite thrust that ended the
[[babylon-first-dynasty|First Dynasty of Babylon]] — linking the Anatolian, Syrian,
and Mesopotamian stories into a single ~1600 BCE upheaval.

## Historiography

Yamhad is reconstructed almost entirely from **other people's archives** — the Mari
letters (for Yarim-Lim's heyday), the Alalakh VII tablets (for the vassal court), and
the Hittite *res gestae* of Hattusili I from Boğazköy (for the fall) — never from
Aleppo itself, which lies under the modern city and is barely excavated. Its internal
chronology is pegged to the Babylonian and Hittite sequences. See
[[cambridge-ancient-history-methodology]].
