# Khurramites (Khurramiyya)

A religio-political movement rooted in the Caspian and Azerbaijani borderlands of the Abbasid Caliphate, blending elements of Mazdakite and older Iranian religious traditions with anti-Abbasid political revolt. The name derives from *khurram* ("joyful"), reflecting the movement's association (per hostile Arabic sources) with communal property and libertine practice, though modern scholarship treats such characterizations with caution as products of orthodox polemic.

The Khurramites reached their greatest political significance under **Babak al-Khurrami**, who led a major uprising against Abbasid authority in Arran (from 816–817 CE) that lasted over two decades and required a sustained campaign by the general Afshin to suppress, culminating in the fall of Babak's stronghold at al-Badhdh in 837 and his execution in 838. The revolt reflected both local resistance to centralized Abbasid taxation and administration and the persistence of pre-Islamic Iranian religious and social identity in the Caspian periphery.

## Significance

The Khurramite revolt was one of the most sustained internal challenges to Abbasid authority in the caliphate's first Islamic centuries, illustrating the limits of central control over Iran's peripheral, mountainous regions and the survival of non-orthodox religious movements under nominal Islamic rule.
