# J. P. "Jack" Morgan Jr. (1867–1943)

Senior partner of J.P. Morgan & Co. after the death of his father, John Pierpont Morgan Sr. Still the preeminent private banking house on Wall Street in 1929, though its relative dominance was eroding. Key participant in the organized support efforts during the crash.

## Role and Context

The House of Morgan remained a symbol of concentrated financial power. Partners including Thomas Lamont and George Whitney were directly involved in the crisis meetings and pool attempts. On Black Thursday Lamont (who had “inherited the mantle”) coordinated the $120 million bankers’ pool from the Morgan offices at 23 Wall Street; Whitney executed the key purchases on the floor. Rockefeller publicly backed “Morgan to Buy Stock.”

The firm navigated the crash and its regulatory aftermath. In 1933 Pecora hearings the partners revealed they paid no income tax in 1931–32 and maintained a “preferred list” of influential clients; Jack Morgan testified calmly while the revelations shocked the public and “displace[d] the Morgan influence.” The Whitney embezzlement scandal (1938) later implicated a Morgan partner connection and accelerated the shift away from the old Wall Street order.
