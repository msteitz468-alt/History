# Conon

## Overview

**Conon** (c. 444–c. 389 BCE) was the Athenian admiral who, in Persian service, **broke
Spartan sea-power at Cnidus (394)** and **rebuilt the Long Walls of Athens (393)** —
engineering, with Persian gold, the revival of Athens as a naval power barely a decade
after its fall. [[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 4, Seager) makes him the pivotal
figure of the [[corinthian-war-395bce|Corinthian War]] at sea.

## Career

Conon commanded at **Aegospotami (405)** and was one of the few to escape [[lysander|
Lysander's]] trap, taking refuge with **Evagoras of Salamis** in Cyprus. There he entered
the service of the **Persian King**, and by 397 was commanding a Persian fleet alongside
the satrap **Pharnabazus**. In **394**, off **Cnidus**, the two **annihilated the Spartan
navy** — the decisive event of the Corinthian War, ending Sparta's brief maritime empire
and emboldening the anti-Spartan coalition. Conon and Pharnabazus then toured the Aegean,
expelling Spartan **harmosts** while promising the cities autonomy (a policy Conon
recommended on the "cynical but practical" ground that openly proclaiming Persia's
imperial aims would provoke resistance — and might leave the cities easier to draw later
into a revived Athenian empire).

## The rebuilding of the Long Walls

In **393** Conon came to Athens, and with his crews and **Persian gold** rebuilt the
**Long Walls and the Piraeus fortifications** — Pharnabazus accepting that a stronger
Athens served Persia against Sparta. Hailed as "**liberator of the allies**," Conon
became the symbol of Athenian recovery; even where the credit was poetic licence
(Lysias' *Epitaphios* ascribes the walls "to the men of Phyle"), the achievement was
his. He financed the Athenian mercenary force at Corinth under **Iphicrates** and
schemed to bring **Dionysius I** of Syracuse and Evagoras into the anti-Spartan camp.

## Fall

Conon's position was always ambiguous — an Athenian using Persian money, suspected of
working to "restore the Athenian empire." When Sparta's envoy **Antalcidas** put exactly
this charge to the satrap **Tiribazus** in 392, Conon was **arrested**; he escaped to
Cyprus, where he died. His career embodies the central paradox of the period — that
**Athenian recovery depended wholly on Persian favour** — the same dependence the
[[kings-peace-386bce|King's Peace]] would soon exploit against Athens.

## Counterfactual significance

Without Conon's victory at Cnidus and the Persian-funded walls, the Athenian resurgence
that produced the **[[second-athenian-confederacy|Second Athenian Confederacy]]** is hard
to imagine. He is the agent through whom **Persian policy resurrected Athens** as a
counterweight to Sparta — and thus, indirectly, an author of the multipolar balance of
the fourth century.
