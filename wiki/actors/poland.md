# Poland

Poland is an East-Central European state whose political history begins with the Christianization of the [[piast-dynasty|Piast]] ruler Mieszko I in 966. Across the medieval and early modern periods it developed into one of Europe's largest polities, most notably as the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth (from 1569), a vast elective monarchy known for its unusually broad noble (szlachta) political rights and eventual paralysis under the liberum veto.

That state was progressively dismembered by Russia, Prussia, and Austria in the [[first-partition-of-poland-1772|First]], [[second-partition-of-poland-1793|Second]], and [[third-partition-of-poland-1795|Third Partitions]] (1772–1795), erasing Poland from the map for 123 years. It was restored as the Second Polish Republic after the First World War (1918), only to be invaded and partitioned again by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union in September 1939 ([[invasion-of-poland-1939]]), suffering occupation, the Holocaust on its soil, and the [[warsaw-uprising-1944|Warsaw Uprising]] (1944).

Postwar Poland was reconstituted with shifted borders under Soviet-aligned communist rule (the Polish People's Republic), a regime eventually challenged and ended by the Solidarity movement (see [[solidarity-poland]]) in 1989, after which Poland transitioned to liberal democracy, joining NATO (1999) and the European Union (2004).
