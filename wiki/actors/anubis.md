# Anubis

## Overview

**Anubis** (Egyptian *Inpw* or *Anpu*, Greek *Anubis*) is the jackal-headed god of mummification, embalming, cemeteries, and guide/protector of the dead in the Duat (underworld). As "Lord of the Sacred Land" and "He Who Is in the Place of Embalming," he oversees the preservation of the body (ensuring the ka and ba can reunite), the weighing of the heart in Osiris's judgment, and safe passage for the deceased. Often depicted as a black jackal or jackal-headed man (black symbolizing fertility of the Nile and the underworld, or the color of embalmed bodies).

He is one of the oldest Egyptian deities, with roots in predynastic jackal cults (guardians of necropoleis). In the *Book of the Dead*, he is central to funerary spells, vignettes (heart-weighing, embalming scenes), and protections.

## Role and Myth (from primary sources)

- **Myth**: Son of Osiris and Nephthys (or Ra and Nephthys in variants); raised by Isis. Key in Osiris myth: discovers and embalms the dismembered body, invents mummification to preserve it for resurrection. As "Opener of the Ways," he guides the dead.

- **Functions in afterlife**:
  - Embalming and mummification: Preserves the corpse; "Lord of the Mummy Wrappings."
  - Judgment: In Chapter 125 vignettes, he operates or tests the balance (heart vs. Ma'at's feather), often with Thoth recording.
  - Guide/protector: Leads the deceased through Duat gates, wards off dangers (snakes, demons).
  - Offerings: Receives funerary gifts; associated with "westerners" (the dead in the west, where sun sets).

- **Attributes**: Jackal head (or full jackal); black color; imiut fetish (skin on pole); scales in judgment; ankh and was-scepter.

- **Cult**: Cynopolis (Hardai); involved in royal and elite funerals. Later syncretized (e.g., Hermanubis in Greco-Roman).

## Historiography and Value

Primary: Pyramid Texts (Old Kingdom; Anubis in embalming spells for king), Coffin Texts, Book of the Dead papyri/vignettes (New Kingdom; e.g., Papyrus of Ani shows Anubis at scales), tomb reliefs (Deir el-Medina, Theban necropolis), canopic jars (sons of Horus under Anubis protection).

This source (Budge/Romer) provides the full spell corpus where Anubis is invoked for "not letting the heart be taken," guiding, protection. Romer's intro contextualizes his role in New Kingdom beliefs: practical magic for the dead, integration with Osiris solar theology. Budge emphasizes Anubis as "god of the dead" and embalmer.
