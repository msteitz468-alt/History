# Pius II (Aeneas Silvius Piccolomini; r. 1458–1464)

## Overview

**Pius II** (Aeneas Silvius Piccolomini)—humanist historian, romancer, poet, author of the *Commentaria* (*Memoirs of a Renaissance Pope*)—supported the **Council of Basel**, then defected to **[[eugenius-iv|Eugenius IV]]** (1442) and helped win Frederick of Germany to the papal side. As pope he took “**Pius**” from Virgil’s “*pius Aeneas*” and prioritized an Eastern campaign over artistic patronage.

## Career Arc

He delayed priestly orders (“I fear continence”), sired illegitimate children; after **1445** illness and conversion, ordained **1446**. His path from conciliar secretary to papal champion illustrates Logan’s larger story of Basel’s irrelevance: the best talent left for the papal Italian councils. With [[nicholas-v|Nicholas V]], he represents the mid-fifteenth-century landmark of **humanists as supreme pontiffs**.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Aeneas’s defection and diplomacy, German adherence to Eugenius—and thus the collapse of effective conciliarism—might have been slower; without his memoirs, Renaissance papal self-fashioning would lack a major primary voice.

## Historiography

Logan treats the humanist popes as a cultural turning point in the church’s attitude to secular learning, not as a simple “decline into worldliness” narrative. The *Commentaria* remain a principal primary source; Pastor and modern biographers read the conversion narrative against the earlier erotic and political writings.
