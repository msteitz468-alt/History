## Role

Eumenes of Cardia (c. 362–316 BCE) was the Greek secretary and archivist of
[[philip-ii-of-macedon|Philip II]] and [[alexander-the-great|Alexander]] who became,
improbably, one of the ablest generals of the [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323|Successors]] —
and the last to fight for the unity of the empire and the Argead dynasty. A Greek
(not a Macedonian) and a man of the pen, he was distrusted by the senior Macedonian
captains, and his assignment to the unconquered satrapy of Cappadocia at Babylon was,
Will suggests, a "poisoned chalice" meant to destroy him.

## Decisions and Significance

Installed in Cappadocia by [[perdiccas]] (322), Eumenes alone remained loyal to the
regent. After Perdiccas' death he was condemned by the army assembly, yet defeated and
killed Craterus in battle (321) and held much of Anatolia. Besieged at **Nora** by
[[antigonus-i-monophthalmus|Antigonus]], he escaped by a feigned alliance, then, as
[[polyperchon|Polyperchon's]] *strategos of Asia*, waged an astonishing campaign from
Asia Minor through Phoenicia into Iran (318–316). To bind his fractious Macedonian
troops and the rival satraps, he revived the **cult of Alexander** — convening his war
council before an empty throne bearing Alexander's diadem and insignia, in the name of
the dead king. Finally surrendered by his own men after the battle of Gabiene, he was
executed by Antigonus (316).

## Counterfactual Significance

Eumenes is, in Will's reading, "the last faithful follower of Alexander's ideas" —
though his loyalty to the dynasty was inseparable from the fact that, as a Greek
outsider, championing legitimacy was his only path to power. His defeat removed the
final obstacle to the satraps' open war for personal kingdoms, and left Antigonus to
take up the unitary cause for himself. The empty-throne device is a vivid early
instance of [[ruler-cult|Alexander's posthumous charisma]] as a political instrument.
See [[wars-of-the-diadochi-323]], [[hieronymus-of-cardia]] (his compatriot and the
historian who preserved his memory).
