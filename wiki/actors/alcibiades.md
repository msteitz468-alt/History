# Alcibiades

## Overview

**Alcibiades** (c. 452–404 BCE), the brilliant, mercurial, and untrustworthy Athenian
aristocrat — ward of [[pericles]], pupil of Socrates — was the dominant and most divisive
personality of the later [[peloponnesian-war|Peloponnesian War]]. [[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH
Vol. V]] (Chs. 10–11, A. Andrewes) draws him from Thucydides, whose verdict is that **personal
ambition predominated throughout**, though he twice allows that Alcibiades genuinely believed
in the policies he urged. "It has never been easy to sum him up."

## Career

- **The Argive policy (420–418)** — young but well-born, he engineered the anti-Spartan
  **Quadruple Alliance** (Argos, Elis, Mantinea, Athens), the gamble that ended at the
  Spartan victory of **Mantinea** (418). His collusion with Nicias to **ostracize
  Hyperbolus** (the last ostracism) damaged his standing. (Hanson: “Alcibiades could later brag that earlier, in 418, he had forced the issue so that ‘the Spartans had staked their all on one day at Mantinea.’” Engineered but “nowhere to be seen on the day”; small Athenian contingent lost 200 hoplites. Earlier fame from Delium 424 valor/saving kin during retreat after friendly-fire chaos.)

  From Kagan range: After Spartan alliance with Boeotia and demolition of Panactum weakened Nicias, Alcibiades urged Argive, Elean, and Mantinean ambassadors to ally with Athens. He tricked Spartan envoys (Leon, Philocharidas, Endius) into denying they had full powers before the assembly, then assailed their honesty, pushing Athens toward the alliance. The treaty was a 100-year mutual non-aggression and defensive pact. In 419 Alcibiades (reelected general) led a small force through the Peloponnesus to Argos, Mantinea, Elis, and Patrae, bringing Patrae into alliance and persuading it to build walls to the sea. He supported the Argive attack on Epidaurus to isolate Corinth. In 418, after the Argive truce with Agis, Alcibiades (arriving late with Athenians) complained the Argives had no right to truce without allies and pushed the coalition to capture Orchomenus in Arcadia, which entered the alliance. This "thwarted his Athenian rivals and gave new life to the quadruple alliance." The loss of Orchomenus led Spartans to blame Agis and appoint xymbouloi to constrain him.
- **Sicily and defection (415)** — chief advocate of the **[[sicilian-expedition|Sicilian
  Expedition]]**, he sailed as one of three generals, but was **recalled to face charges**
  over the mutilation of the Hermae and the profanation of the Mysteries. He **fled to
  Sparta** and advised the measures — a general for Syracuse, the fortification of **Decelea**
  — that ruined the expedition and Attica. From Kagan range: Even before first assembly Alcibiades “captured the imagination of the Athenian people, who ‘sat in groups drawing the map of Sicily and of the sea around it and the harbors of the island’ (Plutarch, Nicias 12).” He was “the most eager for the expedition... hoping to capture both Sicily and Carthage and, if he succeeded, to increase his own private wealth and reputation” (6.15.2-3). In debate: Sicily’s cities “seriously unstable,” diplomacy could win them and Sicels; Spartans “without hope or initiative” lacking fleet; must aid allies (“We must assist them, for we have given our oath” 6.18.1); empire maintained by active policy (“by always coming eagerly to the aid of those who called upon us” 6.18.2); victory brings “control of all Greece.” After Hermae/mysteries scandals and recall by Salaminia, he fled at Thurii. “In Athens he was convicted in absentia; with the others accused he was condemned to death, his property was confiscated, his name was inscribed on a stele of disgrace... and a reward of a talent was promised...” At Sparta (415/4) he adapted (“took vigorous bodily exercise and cold baths, let his hair grow long... ate the coarse bread and the black porridge”), then spoke: expedition aimed at “the entire island and more... subdue southern Italy, Carthage and its empire, and even far-off Iberia... use the vast resources... to attack the Peloponnesus itself, and ‘after that they would rule over the entire Hellenic people’ (6.90).” Urged immediate Spartan army to Sicily + “make war against the Athenians openly... build a permanent fort at Decelea in Attica.” Kagan: “It was so patently false: ‘The generals who are left will carry out the same plans...’ (6.91.1). It should have been inconceivable... that Nicias would pursue the grand design...” “That was the Alcibiades the Spartans saw before them, a defeated and hunted man who badly needed to convince them... We must marvel at his boldness and imagination and at the enormity of his bluff.” His earlier career: “He had not yet commanded an Athenian victory on land or sea, and all his plans had produced strategic failures.” Mantinea plan repeated 427-424 approach but expanded force “frighten[ed] the Greek cities... into neutrality or opposition.” “When the price of that expansion became clear at Rhegium he did not change his plan... That was the Alcibiades... before he attained the status of a legend.”
- **Persia and intrigue (412–411)** — alienating Sparta (and King Agis), he attached himself
  to the satrap **Tissaphernes**, intriguing to keep the two sides balanced and to engineer
  his own return; his promise of Persian money helped trigger the **oligarchic revolution of
  411**.
- **The Hellespont and return (411–407)** — recalled by the democratic fleet at **Samos**, he
  led it to brilliant victories, above all **Cyzicus** (410), reopening the grain route, and
  returned to Athens in **407** to a rapturous reception and supreme command.
- **Notium and final exile (406)** — when his helmsman lost ships at **Notium** in his
  absence, the old distrust revived; deposed, he withdrew to his castles in Thrace, warned the
  generals at **Aegospotami** in vain, and was murdered in exile in 404.

## From Kagan *The Peloponnesian War* (Range 8: return 407, Notium fall, Aegospotami advice)

**Triumphant return to [[places/athens]] (407) after Hellespont successes:** Victories at Abydos, Chalcedon (treaty with Pharnabazus yielding tribute + 20 talents indemnity), Selymbria (conspiracy, lenient terms), Byzantium (night entry via plot, "new policy of justness and conciliation"). "Even returning at the head of a victorious fleet, however, he was not completely secure... Athens was still full of his enemies of various political opinions: democrats who resented his slurs on popular government and were wary of his ambition, religious conservatives, patriots who had not forgotten his treason, and other ambitious politicians who feared his rivalry." Landed after seeing supporters; crowds "cheered and shouted their congratulations... crowning him with wreaths... 'not only restored its control of the sea but even brought victory over the enemy on land everywhere' (Plutarch, Alcibiades 32.4-5)." Cleared of charges, property restored, curses revoked, stelae thrown in sea; voted "general-in-chief (strategos autokrator) with command on land and sea." (ALCIBIADES RETURNS; THE RETURN OF ALCIBIADES)

**Ill-omened arrival and Eleusis master stroke:** "he had returned to [[places/athens]] on the day of the ceremony called Plynteria, in which the robes on the ancient wooden statue of Athena Polias were removed and washed... It was regarded as the unluckiest day of the year on which to undertake important actions... it seemed as if the goddess had not wished to welcome [[actors/alcibiades]] in a friendly manner but concealed herself from him and rejected him." Counter: "Alcibiades, with his keen sense of the spectacular gesture, recognized the chance to put an end to his religious problems with a single bold stroke. After consulting the relevant priests, he prepared to take part in the great procession in the traditional manner. Protected by his sentries and armed guard, he escorted the celebrants along the sacred route. They reached Eleusis without incident and returned on foot the same way. This spectacle, as an act of piety, helped disarm religious suspicions; as a demonstration of military daring and prowess it justified the extraordinary powers voted to him and raised the spirit of the Athenian army; politically it was a master stroke. No performance in the great propaganda competition between [[actors/alcibiades]] and [[actors/nicias]] could match it for timeliness or effect." (ALCIBIADES RETURNS)

**Notium disaster and deposition (406):** After Andros failure (left force but did not take island), sailed to Notium. Left "the bulk of his fleet there and sailed off to join Thrasybulus in the siege of Phocaea... The man he put in charge of the navy there in his absence was Antiochus, a petty officer, a helmsman or kybernetes and the pilot of [[actors/alcibiades]]’ own ship. This appointment, unique in the entire recorded history of the Athenian navy, has been assailed from antiquity to modern times." Gave order "Do not attack [[actors/lysander]]’s ships". Antiochus attacked anyway, mimicking Cyzicus without concealment; "[[actors/lysander]]... charged at the lead ship with three triremes of his own, sinking it and killing Antiochus... They lost twenty-two ships in a rout". "Alcibiades reached the scene of the battle three days later... the loss unavenged." "it was strategically an important defeat, reversing the momentum in the war... The Athenians would not soon recover their position in Ionia, nor would they take Andros." Additional failure at Cyme. Enemies (incl. "a bitter enemy of [[actors/alcibiades]], Thrasybulus son of Thraso") accused luxury cruise, debauchery, attacking allied Cyme, past treason, Persian collaboration to make him tyrant. "The Athenians appointed Conon to take command of the fleet at Samos, and [[actors/alcibiades]] again went into exile... he had prepared a safe harbor for himself in a fortified castle he had built on the Gallipoli Peninsula... and there he went." (THE BATTLE OF NOTIUM; THE FALL OF ALCIBIADES)

**Aegospotami (405) — rejected advice:** From his Thrace castle, "Riding down to the Athenian camp on horseback, he offered his advice and assistance. He urged the generals to move their base to Sestos for the obvious reasons, and announced that two Thracian kings had promised him an army with which to win the war." "The Athenian generals, however, had good reason to doubt that the forces promised by [[actors/alcibiades]] would appear if summoned... The renegade had also set unacceptable conditions for his help—namely, a share in the command of the Athenian forces... no Athenian general would have dared to yield any part of the command to an exile twice condemned by the Athenian people; still less were they inclined to accept the proposal of a man like [[actors/alcibiades]], fearing that 'if they were defeated they themselves would get the blame but that the credit for any success would go to [[actors/alcibiades]].' Instead, they told him that 'they were the generals now, and not he' (Diodorus 13.105.4), and then ordered him to leave." Fleet destroyed days later. (THE BATTLE OF AEGOSPOTAMI)

**Kagan judgment on liability:** "Many have judged this last departure of [[actors/alcibiades]] and his final removal from the command of the Athenian forces as a turning point in the last phase of the war, and as a disaster for [[places/athens]]." "the ablest commander in the campaigns in the Straits was not [[actors/alcibiades]] but Thrasybulus son of Lycus. As always, however, [[actors/alcibiades]]’ personal ambitions proved to be a severe liability, swelling the number of his enemies and the intensity of their hatred. The eagerness with which they waited to attack him compelled him to seek extraordinary accomplishments and make promises that could not be fulfilled in order to achieve and maintain a popularity that was his only security. This drove him to take risks that another general would have avoided and that were bound to bring disaster to [[places/athens]]. He was also a serious political liability, a divisive figure who evoked powerful feelings of admiration or dislike but never steady support from a large portion of the citizenry. He could not win a reliable majority to support his own policies, nor would he subordinate himself to another for the sake of [[places/athens]]. At the same time he could prevent anyone else from taking the lead... 'They yearn for him, they hate him, but they want to have him back' (Aristophanes, Frogs 1425). His disgrace also brought down such able friends as Thrasybulus and Theramenes, depriving [[places/athens]] of its most capable commanders when they were badly needed, which, in the end, may have been the most serious consequence of the Spartan victory at Notium." (THE FALL OF ALCIBIADES)

## Significance

Alcibiades embodies the **instability of the late democracy** — a leader of dazzling
ability the city "longed for and hated and wished to have" (Aristophanes) but could never
trust with the unfettered command Thucydides thought might have saved it (vi.15.4). His
defection turned the [[sicilian-expedition|Sicilian Expedition]] into a catastrophe and drew
**Persia and Sparta** together; his recall briefly revived Athenian fortunes; his fall sealed
them. He is the chief case study of the gap between individual brilliance and civic trust.
