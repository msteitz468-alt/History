# G. H. Hardy

G. H. (Godfrey Harold) Hardy (1877–1947) was an English mathematician and one of the leading analysts of his generation. He is best known to the wider public as the Cambridge mathematician who recognized the genius of Srinivasa Ramanujan, brought him to Trinity College, and collaborated with him during the First World War. Hardy was also a central figure in the “Hardy–Littlewood school” of analytic number theory and a powerful stylist of mathematical prose (*A Mathematician’s Apology*).

Hardy was the product of Winchester and Trinity. He hated the former, loved the latter, and developed an austere, aesthetic, almost religious devotion to pure mathematics combined with a lifelong atheism and a fastidious, sometimes cold, personal manner. He was a superb mentor and editor who prized elegance and clarity. His decision to answer Ramanujan’s 1913 letter and to arrange his journey to England was the decisive act of his life; their collaboration and Hardy’s subsequent guardianship of Ramanujan’s legacy are among the great stories of 20th-century mathematics.
