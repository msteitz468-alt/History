# Gorsedd of the Bards of the Isle of Britain

*(Welsh: Gorsedd Beirdd Ynys Prydain)*

The **Gorsedd of the Bards** is the Welsh bardic institution founded by [[actors/morganwg-iolo|Iolo Morganwg (Edward Williams)]] on Primrose Hill, London, in 1792. Presented by Iolo as the continuous survival of an ancient Druidic-bardic order, it was in fact his own invention, built on forged manuscripts and a fabricated Druidic theology. From its initial insertion into local *eisteddfodau* in south Wales in the 1810s, the Gorsedd grew through the nineteenth century into the governing body of the **National Eisteddfod**, the supreme cultural institution of modern Wales, and has functioned in that role ever since. It is among the most consequential legacies of any single forger in European cultural history.

## Origins and Founding

On 21 June 1792, Iolo Morganwg gathered a small group on Primrose Hill and conducted the first Gorsedd ceremony, using a ring of pebbles as a symbolic stone circle. The ritual he enacted centred on the unsheathing of a sword — held by both Bards and replaced sheathed as a sign of peace — and the declaration of the three grades of the ancient order: **Druids** (white), **Bards** (blue), and **Ovates** (green). The opening was made with an improvised prayer attributed by Iolo to 'Talhaiarn, the earliest known Welsh bard'; he had composed it himself. His motto — *Y Gwir yn erbyn y Byd* ('The Truth Against the World') — and his 'mystic sign' (the *nod cyfrin*, three rays diverging from a point) became the institution's permanent emblems.

Iolo subsequently planted the Gorsedd into regional gatherings in south Wales, including at Newbridge (later Pontypridd) in the Taff valley, where a natural rocking stone (*Yr Maen Chwyf*) provided a credible-looking ancient monument — though Hutton notes its attribution to the Druids derived from the fantasies of John Toland, not genuine antiquity.

## Stewardship of Taliesin Williams (1787–1847)

Following Iolo's death in 1826, his son **Taliesin Williams** (bardic name: Taliesin ab Iolo) took over the propagation of the institution. From his base at Merthyr Tydfil in the heart of the Welsh iron district, Taliesin published editions of Iolo's papers (*Cyfrenach y Beirdd*, 1829; *Coelbren y Beirdd*, 1840; *Iolo Manuscripts*, 1848), organised gorsedd ceremonies at the Pontypridd rocking stone, and forged an alliance with the Halls and Guests — the ironmaster families who replaced medieval Welsh princes as cultural patrons. At the Royal Cardiff Eisteddfod of 1834, attended by the Duchess of Kent and the future Queen Victoria, Taliesin won the bardic chair for poetry and presided over a gorsedd at the autumn equinox in which **Augusta Hall** (later Lady Llanover) was initiated as an Ovate.

## The Abergavenny Moment (1838) and Pan-Celtic Connections

At the Abergavenny eisteddfod of 1838, the Gorsedd for the first time processed alongside members of the [[actors/ancient-order-of-druids|Ancient Order of Druids]], formally linking the Welsh cultural movement with the English fraternal tradition. Crucially, one of the Bards initiated at this event was the **vicomte de la Villemarqué** of Brittany, who went on to lead the modern revival of Breton cultural nationalism. This initiated a pan-Celtic connection that culminated in 1899 when the Breton Gorsedd Llydaw was founded and sent its first delegation to the National Eisteddfod at Cardiff.

## Myfyr Morganwg and the Pontypridd Circle

A parallel line of development emerged at the Pontypridd rocking stone under **Evan Davies** (bardic name: **Myfyr Morganwg**, d. 1888). Around 1849–50, Myfyr constructed a monumental ritual complex around the stone — two concentric rings of megaliths with flanking avenues forming, as he believed, a snake passing through a circle — and claimed the title of Archdruid of the Isle of Britain in 1852. He developed Iolo's theology in a more explicitly syncretic direction, drawing on Hindu cosmology to create what Hutton describes as effectively 'an alternative religion'. His gorsedd at the rocking stone ran through the 1850s–1880s, and is notable as the setting at which the words and melody of 'Hen Wlad fy Nhadau' (the Welsh national anthem) were first performed publicly, after its composers were initiated as Ovates in 1850.

The flamboyant **Dr William Price** (1800–1893), though never formally part of Myfyr's circle, used the rocking stone for ceremonies and regarded himself as an Archdruid of the ancient British Druids; his successful legal defence of cremation in 1884 made him a permanent figure in British social history.

## The National Eisteddfod and Institutionalisation

The Gorsedd's absorption into a National Eisteddfod was achieved in stages. The clergyman **John Williams ab Ithel** organized the pioneering Llangollen Eisteddfod of 1858, which established the precedent for a national event and saw the first full use of coloured robes in the ceremony. A national governing committee was formed in 1860, and the First National Eisteddfod was held at Aberdare in 1861. After the committee collapsed in financial disorder (1868), the London Welsh (the refounded Cymmrodorion Society) revived the National Eisteddfod in 1880–81.

The Archdruidship passed in 1880 to **David Griffith** ('Clwydfardd'), a Denbigh clockmaker and Wesleyan lay preacher of tremendous physical energy. He brought the Gorsedd firmly under the authority of the revived national association, and presided at the gorsedd of Caernarvon in 1894 at which the Prince and Princess of Wales and their daughters were initiated as honorary Ovates.

## Ceremonial Elaboration, 1880s–1900s

The final decades of the century saw the Gorsedd acquire the ceremonial apparatus that has defined it ever since. In 1890, standard robes in the grade colours (Druids in white, Bards in blue, Ovates in green) were adopted for all members. These featured publicly for the first time at the 1894 Caernarvon gorsedd, their making funded by a group of aristocrats led by the Marquess of Bute. The ceremonial sword, crown, breastplate, and sceptre of the Archdruid were designed by **Hubert Herkomer**, Slade Professor of Fine Art at Oxford, for use from 1896. A purpose-designed Hirlas horn (for ceremonial drink), in African buffalo horn with silver dragons and semiprecious stones, was commissioned from Sir William Gascombe John RA in 1899. Permanent megalithic stone circles, as tall as a human, began to be left standing after eisteddfodau as monuments to Welsh nationalism.

By 1902 (Bangor National Eisteddfod), the Gorsedd had been given **supreme authority over the Eisteddfod movement** in its entirety: its consent was required for the holding of any eisteddfod, national or local, and it set the competition subjects for local events. Hutton summarises: 'It had become what Iolo had designed it to be, the central institution of Welsh culture.'

## Significance and Legacy

The Gorsedd of the Bards is the most enduring product of the British [[processes/druid-revival|Druid Revival]] and perhaps the most consequential cultural institution built on historical forgery in the modern world. Its triple-grade structure, its solstice-and-equinox ritual calendar, its prayer composed by Iolo Morganwg, its motto and mystic sign, and its permanent stone circles are now inseparable from modern Welsh cultural nationalism. Hutton's analysis underscores the irony: Iolo's inventions were more culturally productive than the most careful scholarship of his age, and proved impervious to exposure because the Welsh needed them to be true.

## Historiography

As noted above, the scholarly exposure of Iolo's forgeries was cumulative across the nineteenth century (Stephens 1848; Nash 1858) and largely complete by 1900, but had minimal effect on the Gorsedd's institutional position. Within Wales, defenders such as ab Ithel could invoke the authority of Elijah Waring's sympathetic 1850 memoir of Iolo, and Matthew Arnold's 1867 Oxford lectures gave respectable backing to the authenticity of the medieval Welsh texts on which Iolo had built. Full scholarly consensus on the extent of the forgeries emerged only in the twentieth century.
