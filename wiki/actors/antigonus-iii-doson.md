## Role

Antigonus III "Doson" (c. 263–221 BCE) was king of Macedon (229–221) — first as guardian
(*strategos*) for the boy Philip, then in his own right — who rescued the
[[antigonid-dynasty|Antigonid kingdom]] from crisis and, by his victory over
[[cleomenes-iii|Cleomenes III]], restored Macedonian hegemony over Greece. CAH VII.1 ch.
12 (Walbank) treats him as "a realist" of cautious, effective statecraft.

## Decisions and Significance

- **Restoring the kingdom (229)**: appointed guardian to the 8-year-old **Philip** (son
  of the late Demetrius II), he suppressed a Thessalian revolt, expelled the
  [[aetolian-league|Aetolian]] and Dardanian invaders, and consolidated the realm — then
  took the full royal title. He married Philip's mother but reputedly raised no children of
  his own, to safeguard Philip's succession.
- **The Carian expedition (227)**: a striking, if "curious and apparently irrelevant,"
  naval foray into Asia Minor — reviving dormant Antigonid maritime and Asian pretensions —
  abandoned when the Peloponnesian opportunity beckoned.
- **The [[cleomenean-war-229|Cleomenean War]] and the great *volte-face*** (224): when
  [[cleomenes-iii|Cleomenes III]] shattered the [[achaean-league|Achaean League]],
  [[aratus-of-sicyon|Aratus]] secretly appealed to Doson, who marched south in exchange for
  the **surrender of the Acrocorinth** — reversing decades of Achaean anti-Macedonian policy.
- **The Hellenic Symmachy (224)**: Doson founded a new **"League of Leagues"** (Achaeans,
  Macedonians, Thessalians, Epirotes, Acarnanians, Boeotians, Phocians) — an alliance, not
  a super-state, with a council (*synedrion*) he chaired as *hegemon*, designed to crush
  Sparta and (increasingly) to contain Aetolia. It was the framework of Antigonid power in
  Greece, inherited by Philip V.
- **[[battle-of-sellasia-222|Sellasia]] (222)**: with ~29,000 men he crushed Cleomenes,
  took Sparta, cancelled the revolution, and restored the ephorate — the high point of his
  reign.

## Death and Legacy

Recalled north to defeat an Illyrian/Dardanian invasion, Doson burst a blood vessel after
the battle and died (221), leaving the throne and the presidency of the Symmachy to the
teenage **[[philip-v|Philip V]]**, with a council of guardian-Friends. In a reign of barely
eight years he had "restored Macedonian morale and consolidated the realm" and "put
Macedonia back in her position of dominance" in Greece — though Athens was lost for good and
the Aetolians remained a threat. He was honoured across the Symmachy as "saviour and
benefactor." See [[antigonid-dynasty]], [[cleomenean-war-229]], [[battle-of-sellasia-222]].
