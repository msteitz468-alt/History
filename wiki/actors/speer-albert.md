# Albert Speer

German architect and later Reich Minister for Armaments and War Production (1942-1945). Centralized and rationalized German war production under Hitler, achieving significant output increases despite Allied bombing through dispersal, committees, and forced labor exploitation. Postwar convicted at Nuremberg; later reflected on the regime in memoirs.

See: [[processes/war-economies-wwii]], [[events/world-war-ii-1939-1945]], [[actors/adolf-hitler]].

**From West Point History Vol. 1 Range 5 (Overy):**
- Appointed Feb 1942. With Erhard Milch (Air Ministry), played key role in reducing waste and duplication. "not so much a production miracle as the final realization of what had up until now been a large but inefficiently mobilized war potential."
- Reforms included industrial dispersal to cope with bombing; output tripled 1941-44 but more might have been possible without 1944 bombing focus on oil/transport/chemicals.

## From Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)

Appointed Armaments Minister 8 February 1942 within hours of Fritz Todt's death in an unexplained (most probably accidental) plane crash at Rastenburg, outmaneuvering [[goring-hermann|Göring's]] grab for the portfolio; Hitler's decree of 21 March 1942 subordinated the economy to arms production under him. His rationalization — fixed-price contracts (inherited from Todt), industrialist self-responsibility, radical type-consolidation (tank types 18→7, lorry types 151→23, aircraft models 42→5; U-boat assembly 42→16 weeks) — yielded a claimed armaments-productivity index rise from 98 (1941) to 322 (July 1944). Evans's verdict: real in narrow terms, strategically futile (Allied:Axis GDP never below 2:1, over 3:1 by 1944), and from ~1944 Speer's effort "was directed not at managing the arms supply crisis but at disguising it." The memoirs' contrast between prior "chaos" and Speerian efficiency is "overdrawn" — Todt had begun the centralization, and the polycracy (Göring, [[sauckel-fritz|Sauckel]], Milch, the SS, Kehrl) persisted to the end. His dependence on camp and forced labor was knowing and continuous, from Flossenbürg/Mauthausen quarry stone as General Building Inspector to the [[forced-labor-nazi-germany|slave-labor economy]] of his ministry. Seriously ill January–May 1944, he nearly lost the portfolio to Himmler's and Sauckel's circling. Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## Dora, the Nero Order, and the memoirs — Evans (2008), Part 7

Speer sponsored von Braun's rocket program and, after the Peenemünde raid, built the underground Mittelwerk with Kammler using Dora camp labor — 20,000 of 60,000 forced laborers died there. His memoir claim to have been appaled by his 10 December 1943 visit is contradicted by his own file: a week later he wrote **congratulating** Kammler on a feat "that far exceeds anything ever done in Europe, and is unsurpassed even by American standards"; at Nuremberg he denied ever visiting a labor camp. He genuinely obstructed the Nero Order (touring fronts to countermand demolitions, dumping Gauleiters' explosives) after telling Hitler to his face the war was lost — then capitulating verbally to keep the authority he used to undermine it. His claimed February 1945 plan to gas the bunker Evans leaves as possibly "his own fantasies." At Nuremberg the "subtle and sophisticated mixture of self-exculpation and self-blame" (including the "palpable untruth" of ignorance of Auschwitz) bought him 20 years while [[sauckel-fritz|Sauckel]] hanged for the same labor empire; the celebrated memoirs were "less than honest." Died London, 1981. Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## Griffin (2007): Speer and Technocratic Modernism

*Modernism and Fascism* treats Speer as emblem of the Reich's technocratic modernism: the "Cathedrals of Light" (60 searchlights over the Berlin Olympic Stadium for Mussolini's visit, 28 October 1937) as a display of "Nazism's transformative technological and cultural power," anticipating by 70 years the Ground Zero memorial beams; his autobiography as one of the few first-hand windows into Nazi technocracy; wartime productivity rising after he replaced Todt as Armaments Minister (1942) on the back of forced and slave labour — conditions in the Harz V-2 tunnels "so grotesquely inhuman that even Albert Speer was appalled when he inspected the results of the implementation of his own orders in December 1943." The unbuilt Große Halle of Germania is Griffin's closing image of the modernist state's futility: "no more than an empty mausoleum for the still-born 'New German.'" Postwar coda: Speer's 1978 book dedication to Philip Johnson ("With sincere admiration of his most recent designs") as a fait divers of the modernism–fascism elective affinity. (Source: [[griffin-modernism-and-fascism-2007]].)
