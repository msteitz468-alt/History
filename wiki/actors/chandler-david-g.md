# David G. Chandler

David G. Chandler (1924–2004) was a British military historian and lecturer at the Royal Military Academy, Sandhurst. He is best known as the author of *The Campaigns of Napoleon* (1966), the definitive modern operational study of Napoleon's campaigns from his early career through the final defeat in 1815.

## The Campaigns of Napoleon (1966)

Chandler's masterwork combines exhaustive narrative reconstruction of the campaigns with a substantial analytical section ("Napoleon's Art of War") examining the Emperor's strategic concepts, grand tactics, doctrinal inheritance, and the evolution of the corps system and *manoeuvre sur les derrières*. The book draws on the *Correspondance de Napoléon*, contemporary memoirs, and detailed orders of battle. It remains the standard reference for the operational history of the Napoleonic period and a core source for the warfare hub's Napoleonic analyses.

Chandler emphasizes Napoleon's qualities as a commander (personal magnetism, intellectual range, capacity for work, mastery of grand tactics) alongside the defects and overreach that contributed to decline after 1806–07. The work includes extensive appendices with orders of battle and examples of key manoeuvres.
