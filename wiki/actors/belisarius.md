# Belisarius

**Flavius Belisarius** was the preeminent general of the Byzantine Empire under emperor [[justinian|Justinian I]]. He was the primary instrument of Justinian’s project to recover the western Roman provinces, leading the campaigns that destroyed the Vandal Kingdom of North Africa (533–534), recaptured Rome and Ravenna from the Ostrogoths, and briefly restored Mediterranean unity. His military career is detailed by his contemporary, the historian Procopius.

---

## Military Career

### Eastern Frontier: Dara and Callinicum (530–531)
Belisarius rose through the ranks of the guards (*bucellarii*) under Justinian. In 530, he won a major victory over a superior Persian force at the **Battle of Dara**, utilizing trench defenses and tactical cavalry counter-attacks. Although defeated at Callinicum in 531, he proved himself a capable commander.

### Suppression of the Nika Riots (532)
In Constantinople, Belisarius was tasked with ending the **[[nika-riots-532|Nika Riots]]**. Along with the general Mundus, he trapped the rioters inside the Hippodrome and executed approximately 30,000 citizens, securing Justinian's throne and clearing the way for his western campaigns.

### The Vandalic War (533–534)
Justinian appointed Belisarius to command a small force of 15,000 men to recover North Africa. Exploiting Vandal division and the absence of their fleet, Belisarius landed in North Africa, defeated Gelimer at **Ad Decimum** (533), and occupied Carthage. A final victory at **Tricamarum** ended Vandal rule. Belisarius returned to Constantinople in 534, where he was granted a formal Roman triumph—the last ever celebrated by a private citizen.

### The Gothic War in Italy (535–540)
Belisarius landed in Sicily in 535, took Naples, and entered Rome (536). The Ostrogothic king Witigis besieged Rome from 537 to 538. Despite starvation and plague, Belisarius held the city, using defensive tactics and cavalry sallies. 

In 540, Belisarius captured the Gothic capital of **Ravenna** by feigning acceptance of the Goths' offer to crown him Western Roman Emperor. However, Justinian grew suspicious of Belisarius's ambition and recalled him to Constantinople.

### Gothic Resurgence and Recall (544–548)
Following the outbreak of the **[[justinianic-plague-541|Justinianic Plague]]** and the rise of the Gothic king **[[totila|Totila]]**, Italy was lost again. Justinian sent Belisarius back in 544, but withheld funding and reinforcements. Unable to match Totila's mobile forces or pay his own troops, Belisarius could only watch as Totila sacked Rome. He was recalled for the final time in 548.

## Relationship with Justinian

The relationship between Belisarius and Justinian was characterized by deep imperial dependence and chronic suspicion:
*   **Loyalty vs. Suspicion:** Justinian relied heavily on Belisarius's military genius but feared he would use his popularity with the troops to seize the imperial throne. 
*   **Recall and Disgrace:** Justinian repeatedly starved Belisarius of resources during his campaigns. In 562, Belisarius was falsely accused of corruption and conspiracy against the emperor and was temporarily imprisoned, though he was restored to favor before his death in 565.
*   **The blind beggar legend:** The later medieval tradition that Justinian blinded Belisarius and forced him to beg on the streets of Constantinople is rejected by modern historians.
