# Martin V (Odo Colonna; r. 1417–1431)

## Overview

**Martin V** (Odo Colonna), elected at the [[council-of-constance-1414|Council of Constance]] on **11 November 1417** (feast of St Martin), ended the **[[great-schism-1378|Great Schism]]**. [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]]: “The schism was truly over.”

## Election

Electoral body: **23 cardinals** of three obediences plus **six delegates per nation**; two-thirds of cardinals and of each nation required. The body met **8 November 1417** and on 11 November elected Colonna, who took his name from the day’s saint. He made temporary **reform concordats** with secular rulers (not bishops), somewhat undermining episcopal authority. The council dissolved **22 April 1418** but essentially ended with the election.

## After Constance

Uneasy under a superior assembly that had mandated frequent councils (*Frequens*), Martin nonetheless summoned **Pavia** (23 April 1423); plague moved it to **Siena**. Only ~25 mitres at the postponed opening; little accomplished due to papal reluctance and poor attendance—practical bishops saw frequent councils as near-continuous parliament neglecting pastoral duties. Fathers set the next council for **Basel (1431)**. Martin died **20 February 1431**; cardinals elected **[[eugenius-iv|Eugenius IV]]** eleven days later.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Martin’s broadly acceptable election, triple schism might have continued; without his coolness toward *Frequens*, Basel’s later crisis might have arrived under different papal terms.

## Historiography

Logan rejects calling Constance “the end of the medieval papacy”: the 1418 papacy differed from 1378, but nations had questioned the identity of the pope more than the institution. Martin’s concordats and subsequent papal recovery of constitutional supremacy within ~50 years frame the fifteenth-century story.
