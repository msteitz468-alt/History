# Gebhard Leberecht von Blücher

Prussian Field Marshal nicknamed "Marschall Vorwärts" ("Marshal Forward") for his relentless offensive instincts during the Napoleonic Wars. Blücher commanded Prussian forces in the 1813–1814 campaigns that drove Napoleon back to France and led the Army of the Lower Rhine during the Waterloo campaign of 1815. Defeated but not destroyed by Napoleon at Ligny (16 June 1815), he kept his promise to [[gneisenau-neidhardt-von|Gneisenau]]'s and his own staff's plan of falling back parallel to Wellington's line rather than retreating east, and marched his battered army to Wellington's aid at Waterloo (18 June 1815), arriving in the late afternoon to strike the exhausted French right flank and turn the battle into a rout. He relied heavily on his brilliant chief of staff Gneisenau for operational planning, while himself supplying aggressive will and troop morale — a partnership historians credit as decisive to Prussian effectiveness in the campaign.

## Counterfactual Significance

Had Blücher honored a more cautious retreat after Ligny (as Napoleon expected), Wellington's army at Waterloo would very likely have been destroyed before Prussian relief arrived; his personal insistence on marching to the sound of the guns is treated by most accounts as the single most consequential command decision of the campaign.

## Historiography

Popular Anglophone narratives (echoing Wellington's own memoirs) sometimes underplay the scale of the Prussian contribution at Waterloo; recent Napoleonic-era historiography (e.g., Peter Hofschröer) has pushed back, emphasizing Prussian operational planning and the near-simultaneous, not merely reinforcing, nature of the Prussian assault.
