# Philipp Lenard

German physicist (1862–1947), winner of the 1905 Nobel Prize in Physics for his work on cathode rays, and later one of the most prominent scientific opponents of Albert Einstein and relativity theory. Lenard's early experimental work on the photoelectric effect helped lay groundwork later built upon by Einstein's 1905 explanation (for which Einstein himself received the 1921 Nobel Prize), a debt Lenard felt was insufficiently acknowledged.

Lenard became an early and vocal supporter of the Nazi party, embracing antisemitism and rejecting relativity as symptomatic of "Jewish physics" as opposed to what he termed "Deutsche Physik" (German/Aryan physics) — a movement he co-led with Johannes Stark to purge modern theoretical physics of Einstein's and other Jewish scientists' contributions from German science and education. His campaign, though scientifically discredited and ultimately unsuccessful in suppressing relativity and quantum theory within German physics, exemplifies the entanglement of Nazi racial ideology with attacks on modern science.

## Significance

Lenard's "Deutsche Physik" campaign against Einstein is a paradigmatic case of ideological science under Nazism, illustrating how personal scientific rivalry could be fused with and amplified by racial ideology.
