# Jayavarman VII

**Jayavarman VII** (r. c. 1181–c. 1218) was "arguably not only the greatest of all the Khmer kings but
also the greatest personage in Cambodian history" (Coe) — the **Mahayana Buddhist** ruler who rebuilt
the [[khmer-empire|Khmer Empire]] after catastrophe and remade **[[angkor|Angkor]]** in his own
image. Documented for the wiki through Michael D. Coe's
[[coe-angkor-khmer-civilization-2018|*Angkor and the Khmer Civilization*]] (2003).

## Reconquest and the Buddhist kingship

A prince who spent years in exile (partly in the Cham capital Vijaya), Jayavarman came to the throne
at about fifty, after the **Cham sack of Angkor in 1177** had plunged the empire into anarchy. In
**1181** he turned the tables, crushing the Chams in land battle and in a great naval engagement on
the Great Lake (an inscription speaks of a "lake of blood"), and was consecrated *chakravartin*.
Deeply influenced by his devout wife **Jayarajadevi** (and later her sister Indradevi), he was a
committed **Mahayana Buddhist** — and as such **dispensed with the Brahmin *purohita* and suspended
the Hindu [[khmer-empire|devaraja]] cult**, at least for his reign, centring royal religion instead on
the compassionate bodhisattva **Avalokiteshvara**.

## The great builder

Jayavarman launched "a building programme... truly unprecedented in its scope." He raised the walled
city of **Angkor Thom** ("Great Angkor"), with its monumental face-gates and, at its centre, the
**Bayon** — its dozens of serene **face-towers** bearing the smiling visage of Avalokiteshvara (widely
thought to be the king's own face), its galleries carved with the Cham war and scenes of everyday
life. He built the monastic "cities" of **Ta Prohm** and Preah Khan, the **Elephant Terrace**, his
**Jayatataka** baray with the island-temple **Neak Pean**, and knit the empire with **roads, bridges,
rest-houses, and 102 hospitals** — a famous concern for "the health and welfare of his subjects." His
portrait statues, depicting him in **meditation**, are among the masterpieces of Khmer art.

## Significance

Jayavarman VII is the apogee of [[khmer-empire|Khmer]] power and monument-building, and the figure who
gave Angkor its most iconic face. His **Mahayana Buddhist** kingship — welfare, public works, and the
bodhisattva-king replacing the Shaiva god-king — marks a distinctive moment in the religious history
of mainland Southeast Asia, even as it strained the empire's resources; after him the realm slowly
contracted and turned to **Theravada Buddhism**.
