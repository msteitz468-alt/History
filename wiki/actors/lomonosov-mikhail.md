# Mikhail Lomonosov

## Biography / Description
**Mikhail Lomonosov** was Russia's premier polymath, scientist, writer, grammarian, and poet of the 18th century (L3754-3756). Born into a free peasant family in the northern province of Archangel, he traveled to Moscow to receive an education, eventually studying at the Academy of Sciences and for five years in Germany at Marburg University (L3754-3756). 

Lomonosov was the Russian counterpart to the great encyclopedic scholars of the Western European Enlightenment (L3756). Known for his vast intellect, he made pioneering contributions to physics, chemistry, astronomy, grammar, and literature, leading Alexander Pushkin to famously describe him as "our first university" (L3754-3756).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Scientific Discoveries:** Lomonosov formulated the law of conservation of matter and energy decades before Antoine Lavoisier, discovered the atmosphere of Venus (1761), developed the theory of heat as a form of motion, and advanced the fields of electricity, geology, and physical chemistry (L3756-3758). He created the first physical chemistry course and textbook in 1751–1752 (L3756).
* **Moscow University (1755):** Together with Ivan Shuvalov, Lomonosov promoted and planned the establishment of the first Russian university in Moscow in 1755, which was modeled on advanced European universities (L3676-3678).
* **Linguistic Reform:** In 1755, he published the first comprehensive Russian Grammar, which helped regulate and establish the modern Russian literary language, separating it from archaic Church Slavonic (L3704-3706).
* **Poetic and Versification Reform:** Lomonosov, alongside Vasily Trediakovsky, developed the modern system of Russian syllabo-tonic versification and composed odes that became classics of Russian literature (L3708, L3714).

## Historical Significance
Lomonosov's achievements proved that Russians could master and advance Western science and scholarship within a single generation of Peter the Great's reforms (L3754-3756). 

However, his scientific discoveries were far ahead of his time in Russia; because he lacked immediate followers in the country, his work was largely forgotten until its rediscovery by 20th-century scientists (L3758-3760). In literature and grammar, his influence was immediate and lasting, laying the structural and linguistic foundations that enabled the Golden Age of Russian literature (L3702-3706).
