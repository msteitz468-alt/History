# Chu Huy Man

North Vietnamese (People's Army of Vietnam) general who commanded the B3 Front covering the Central Highlands of South Vietnam from 1965. In that capacity he directed PAVN regular divisions in the campaign that culminated in the Battle of the Ia Drang Valley (November 1965) — the first major direct clash between PAVN regulars and U.S. Army forces (the 1st Cavalry Division under [[westmoreland-william]]'s overall command), fought partly to test American airmobile tactics and doctrine. A veteran of the earlier war against the French, Chu Huy Man went on to a long senior career in PAVN political-military leadership, eventually rising to the Politburo of the Communist Party of Vietnam.

## Counterfactual Significance

Ia Drang's outcome — read by U.S. commanders as validating airmobile doctrine and by Hanoi as validating "grab them by the belt buckle" close-quarters tactics to negate American firepower — shaped both sides' subsequent operational approaches for the rest of the ground war; Chu Huy Man's Highlands command was central to testing that doctrine on the North Vietnamese side.

## Historiography

Harold Moore and Joseph Galloway's *We Were Soldiers Once... and Young* (1992) is the standard American narrative of Ia Drang; Vietnamese-language and PAVN institutional histories offer the fuller picture of Chu Huy Man's own planning and are less thoroughly represented in the collection.
