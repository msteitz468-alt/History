# Filippo Tommaso Marinetti

Founder of **Futurism** (Manifesto, 1909) — the most radical form of aesthetic modernism produced in Italy — and a founding member of Fascism. Marinetti is the standing exhibit in every debate about the relationship between modernism and fascism; this page follows the treatment in [[griffin-modernism-and-fascism-2007|Griffin, *Modernism and Fascism* (2007)]].

## Overview

The 1909 Futurist Manifesto declared: "We will glorify war – the world's only hygiene – militarism, patriotism, the destructive gesture of freedom-bringers, beautiful ideas worth dying for and the scorn of women." Griffin reads this not as an aberration but as "in tune with a diffuse undercurrent of modernist mythopoeia" treating war as ritual cultural catharsis; Emilio Gentile notes Marinetti's 1913 interventionist term "conflagration" invoked the Stoic myth of a palingenetic "great fire" from which "Great Italy" would rise.

## Futurism and the Birth of Fascism

In 1914–15 Marinetti called the war "the most beautiful Futurist poem that has ever seen the light of day," predicting it would "reinvigorate Italy." He and [[mussolini-benito|Mussolini]] held a joint interventionist meeting in Milan on 31 March 1915 and were arrested together in Rome the following month. On 23 March 1919 he was among the 118 founders of the Fasci di combattimento at San Sepolcro. His subsequent relationship with the regime "remained turbulent" (Griffin groups him with [[evola-julius|Evola]] among second-generation modernist fellow-travellers): the regime never adopted Futurism as official style, but Goebbels welcomed Marinetti and the Futurist *aeropittura* exhibition in Berlin in 1934, and Marinetti composed an ode to rayon as a metaphor of Italy's regeneration during the autarky drive.

## Counterfactual Significance

Griffin explicitly polices the causal chain: Paul Virilio's claim that "war is the world's only hygiene" led "to the shower block of Auschwitz-Birkenau" "has minimal empirical content" — "Not even the most ecstatic 'proto-fascist' prose of Filippo Marinetti or Ernst Jünger can be blamed for Fascism or Nazism, any more than Aleksandr Blok, Vladimir Mayakovsky, or Vladimir Tatlin can be held accountable for Stalin's Purges." Marinetti matters less as a cause of Fascism than as proof that Fascism was born *from* the modernist milieu — the paradox ("what led the founder of one of the most radical forms of aesthetic modernism to Mussolini's nationalism?") that Griffin's whole book dissolves.

## Historiography

Marinetti/Futurism is the single case study on which both Walter Benjamin's "aestheticization of politics" essay and Andrew Hewitt's *Fascist Modernism* rest — a narrowness Griffin criticizes. Emilio Gentile and Walter Adamson supply the standard accounts of the Florentine/Milanese avant-garde's role in Fascism's genesis; Claudio Fogu links Futurism and Gentile's actualism as sharing "the same intellectual horizon."
