# Qadi al-Nu'man

**Abu Hanifa al-Nu'man b. Muhammad**, known as **al-Qadi al-Nu'man** ("the Judge," c. 903–974), was the
**pre-eminent jurist, historian, and chief da'i of the early [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] state** — the man
who gave Ismaili Shi'ism its **[[imamate|legal code]]** and much of its historical record. He served all four
of the first Imam-caliphs, from [[al-mahdi-billah|al-Mahdi]] to [[al-muizz-li-din-allah|al-Mu'izz]].

## Career

Probably raised Ismaili (his father a convert from Sunni **Malikism**), al-Nu'man began under al-Mahdi as a
"collector and copyist of books," served al-Qa'im throughout his reign, and was made **chief judge of all
Ifriqiya** by [[al-mansur-fatimid|al-Mansur]] in 948 — "the first judge whom he appointed." Under al-Mu'izz he
became also the ***da'i al-du'at*** (chief missionary), uniting the judiciary and the da'wa and delivering the
weekly *majalis al-hikma* at the palace. He accompanied the dynasty to **Egypt** in 972.

## Works

Al-Nu'man was extraordinarily prolific. His major works, several still studied, include:
- ***Da'a'im al-Islam*** ("Pillars of Islam," 958–960), composed under al-Mu'izz's direction — the
  **foundational Fatimid legal code** and one of the first "civic constitutions" of the dynasty. It sets
  ***walaya*** (allegiance to the Imam) as the first of seven pillars, articulates the Ismaili theory of the
  imamate (the *ulu'l-amr*, "holders of authority"), distinguishes *iman* (faith) from *islam* (submission),
  and incorporates the **"Ahd of Ali,"** a governance charter stressing justice and the satisfaction of the
  common people.
- ***Iftitah al-da'wa*** ("Commencement of the Mission") — the principal narrative history of the da'wa, the
  rise of al-Mahdi, and the founding of the state, and a major source for the whole Fatimid rise.
- ***Kitab al-Idah*** (a vast collection of *ahl al-bayt* traditions) and the abridged ***Kitab al-Ikhtisar***
  (originally *Kitab al-Dinar*, named for its low copying cost).
- Esoteric-exegesis (***ta'wil***) works: *Asas al-ta'wil*, *Ta'wil al-shari'a*, *Ta'wil al-da'a'im*.
- ***al-Majalis wa'l-musayarat***, a memoir of his sessions with the Imam-caliphs and a key eyewitness source.

## Significance

Qadi al-Nu'man is to Fatimid Ismailism what the great school-founders were to Sunni law: he codified a
distinct Shi'i legal system centred on the living Imam's authority, and his histories remain the indispensable
insider sources — recovered by modern scholarship — for reconstructing the Fatimid rise against the hostile
Abbasid chronicle tradition.
