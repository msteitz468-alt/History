# The Kuomintang (Chinese Nationalist Party)

The **Kuomintang** (KMT, 国民党, "Nationalist Party") was the party that led the Chinese nationalist
revolution, reunified the country in 1928, and governed the [[republic-of-china-1912|Republic of
China]] until its defeat by the Communists in 1949. From the [[cambridge-history-of-china|Cambridge
History of China]] (Republican China volumes).

## From Sun Yat-sen to the Northern Expedition

The party grew from **Sun Yat-sen's** revolutionary organizations (the Tongmenghui, 1905) and was
founded as the Kuomintang in 1912; suppressed by **Yuan Shikai**, it regrouped in the south. In
**1923–24** Sun **reorganized the KMT on Leninist party lines with Soviet advice**, founded the
**Whampoa Military Academy** (commandant: **Chiang Kai-shek**), and formed the **First United Front**
with the new Chinese Communist Party. After Sun's death (1925), the KMT launched the **Northern
Expedition (1926–28)**, which broke the [[warlord-era|warlords]] and established a Nationalist
government at **Nanjing**.

## The Nanjing decade and the struggle with the Communists

In **1927** Chiang turned violently on his Communist allies (the Shanghai massacre), ending the
United Front and beginning a long civil war. The **"Nanjing decade" (1927–37)** brought real
state-building — administration, finance, infrastructure — under an increasingly **authoritarian,
militarized one-party state** built on Sun's **Three Principles of the People** (nationalism,
democracy, people's livelihood), though Chiang's control over China was always partial and the
regime was faction-ridden and dependent on urban and landlord elites.

## War, defeat, and Taiwan

The **Japanese invasion (1937–45)** devastated the Nationalist state even as it fought the main
weight of the war, while the Communists expanded in the countryside. In the renewed **Civil War
(1946–49)** the KMT, despite US support, was defeated and **retreated to Taiwan**, where it ruled the
Republic of China as a one-party state for decades before Taiwan's later democratization.

## Significance and historiography

The CHC's Republican volumes present the KMT as the bearer of China's first sustained attempt at
**modern nation-state building** — and debate why it failed on the mainland: corruption and factionalism,
over-reliance on military force and narrow social bases, the catastrophic Japanese war, or the
greater organizational and rural appeal of the Communists. Its rivalry with the
[[chinese-communist-revolution|CCP]] is the central thread of twentieth-century Chinese politics.
