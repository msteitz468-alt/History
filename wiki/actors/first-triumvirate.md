# The First Triumvirate

The private compact of the three most powerful men in Rome —
**[[julius-caesar|Caesar]]**, **[[pompey|Pompey]]**, and **[[crassus|Crassus]]** —
that overrode the Senate, dominated the 50s, and prepared the way for the
Republic's collapse into civil war. Not a formal office but an **informal political
alliance** (*amicitia*). Treated in [[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]] Chs. 9–10 (Wiseman).

## Formation (60 BCE)

The three were driven together by a common obstacle: **Cato and the optimate
establishment** had thwarted each of them. **Pompey** could not get his eastern
settlement ratified or his veterans settled; **Crassus** was denied the **publicani's**
tax rebate; **Caesar** was refused leave to stand for the consulship without
surrendering his triumph. Excluded by "a clique of nobles," they pooled their power
late in 60. [[cicero]], invited, declined — choosing to fight for the Republic "as
the doomed Hector for Troy."

## Operation

In Caesar's **consulship of 59** the alliance forced through its programme by popular
vote and mob force: **Pompey's *acta* ratified**, his **veterans' land law**, the
**publicani refunded**, and Caesar's **five-year Gallic command** secured. It was
cemented dynastically by Pompey's marriage to Caesar's daughter **Julia**.

By **56** the partners were fraying — Crassus and Pompey openly rivalrous, Caesar's
enemies threatening his command. Caesar summoned them to the conference of **Luca**,
where they renewed the deal: **Pompey and Crassus to hold a second consulship (55)**
and then five-year commands (Pompey in Spain, Crassus in Syria), Caesar's Gallic
command extended five more years. The empire was, in effect, to be **shared out among
the three** — and expanded for the glory and profit of each.

## Breakdown

Two deaths dissolved it. **Julia died in childbirth (54)**, severing the bond between
Caesar and Pompey; and **Crassus, craving military glory to match his partners, was
killed at [[battle-of-carrhae-53bce|Carrhae]] (53)**, removing the third party who
had balanced the other two. Pompey, now sole pre-eminent man, drifted toward the
**optimates** (the very *factio* he had once opposed), married into the Metelli
(Cornelia), and accepted a **sole consulship (52)**. The remaining rivalry of Caesar
and Pompey could no longer be mediated — and ran to civil war (Cycle 6).

## Significance

Wiseman presents the triumvirate as the symptom of a constitution "fast becoming
unworkable": the prizes of an empire doubled by Pompey and an electorate doubled by
the census of 70 could no longer be distributed by legitimate competition, so the
three strongest men simply **seized the distribution for themselves**. Its collapse
left no mechanism but war to resolve who would rule.
