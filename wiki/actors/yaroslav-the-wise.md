# Yaroslav the Wise
## Biography / Description
**Yaroslav I Vladimirovich**, known as **Yaroslav the Wise**, was the Grand Prince of Kiev from 1019 until his death in 1054 CE. His reign is universally recognized by historians as the zenith of political power, economic prosperity, and cultural achievement in [[kievan-rus|Kievan Rus′]]. 

Yaroslav was a builder, educator, and patron of the arts, who consolidated Christianity in Rus' and established legal and administrative frameworks that defined the East Slavic lands for centuries.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Civil War and Consolidation (1015–1019 CE):** Following the death of his father Vladimir I, Yaroslav waged a bitter war against his brother Sviatopolk "the Damned" (who killed their brothers Boris and Gleb). Yaroslav triumphed in 1019 with Novgorodian support. Per Primary Chronicle (lines 6680-6827): In Novgorod Yaroslav's Varangians abused locals; citizens killed them in marketplace; Yaroslav craftily killed chief men then regretted ("Alas for my beloved retainers"); gathered "one thousand Varangians and forty thousand other soldiers"; defeated Svyatopolk at Lyubech (after taunt calling Novgorodians "crooked-shanks, you carpenters?"); Svyatopolk fled to Lyakhs. 1018: Boleslav + Svyatopolk defeated Yaroslav at Bug; Boleslav took Cherven cities. 1019 Al'ta: Yaroslav invoked "The blood of my brother cries aloud to thee, oh Lord... My brethren, although ye be absent in the body, yet help me with your prayer"; "carnage was terrible, such as had never before occurred in Rus’"; Svyatopolk fled tormented ("a devil came upon him... died a miserable death"; tomb in wilderness "evil odor"; "new Abimelech"/Cain). Yaroslav "settled in Kiev... wiped away the sweat of his labors".
* **Division and sole rule (1026–1036):** Divided with Mstislav (Yaroslav west of Dnieper/Kiev; Mstislav east/Chernigov); "Strife and tumult ceased, and there was a great calm in the land" (lines 6919-6926). After Mstislav's death, sole ruler.
* **Defeat of the Pechenegs (1036/7):** Decisive victory outside Kiev; "the remnant of them disappeared from that day to this" (lines 6960-6976). Site later St. Sophia.
* **Building and culture:** "Yaroslav built the great citadel at Kiev, near which stands the Golden Gate. He founded also the metropolitan Church of St. Sophia, the Church of the Annunciation over the Golden Gate, and also the Monastery of St. George and the convent of St. Irene" (1037, lines 6980-6984). "Yaroslav loved religious establishments and was devoted to priests, especially to monks. He applied himself to books, and read them continually day and night. He assembled many scribes, and translated from Greek into Slavic." Famous agricultural metaphor: "His father Vladimir plowed and harrowed the soil when he enlightened Rus’ through baptism, while this prince sowed the hearts of the faithful with the written word, and we in turn reap the harvest by receiving the teaching of books" (lines 6987-6996). 1051: assembled bishops, appointed Hilarion (first Rus'? ) Metropolitan in St. Sophia (lines 7079-7080). Detailed account of Crypt Monastery origins under his reign (Hilarion crypt; Antonius from Athos; Theodosius adopts Studion rule).
* **Ecclesiastical Independence, law, campaigns:** See above. Credited Pravda Russkaia. Campaigns vs Chuds (founded Yur'ev), recaptured Cherven from Poland, Yam', Yatvingians, Lithuania, Mazovians. Sent son Vladimir vs Greece 1043 (storm, Vyshata captured, later peace; married sister to Kazimir, recovered 800 captives, lines 7036-7061). 1022 Mstislav's Rededya duel (but under Yaroslav's time).
* **Division of the Realm (1026–1036 CE):** After another conflict, Yaroslav divided the Rus' lands with his brother Mstislav the Brave of Tmutorokan, keeping the west of the Dnieper (with Kiev) and leaving the east (with Chernigov) to Mstislav. Upon Mstislav's death in 1036, Yaroslav became sole ruler.
* **Defeat of the Pechenegs (1037 CE):** Yaroslav scored a decisive victory over the invading Pechenegs outside the walls of Kiev, permanently breaking their military power and initiating a quarter-century of relative steppe peace.
* **Promulgation of the *Pravda Russkaia*:** Yaroslav is credited with codifying the first written laws of Rus', the *Russian Justice*, which established civil and criminal penalties, substituted fines for the death penalty, and regulated trade and property.
* **Ecclesiastical Independence:** In 1051, Yaroslav appointed **Hilarion** as the Metropolitan of Kiev, making him the first native Rus' metropolitan rather than a Greek appointee from Constantinople.
* **Building Program:** He rebuilt Kiev on a grand scale, founding the Cathedral of St. Sophia (1037 CE) and establishing schools and a large library.

## Historical Significance
Yaroslav's reign firmly established Kievan Rus' as a major power in medieval Europe. He used dynastic marriages to integrate the Rurikids with other European reigning houses: his daughters married the kings of France, Hungary, and Norway, and his sons married European princesses (l. 835). 

Upon his death in 1054, Yaroslav assigned separate principalities to his five sons under a rotating system of seniority. This arrangement quickly collapsed into internal division, initiating the Appanage period of political fragmentation.

## Legacy in the Primary Chronicle (successors' conflicts, ll. 9001–12000)
The Laurentian Text (Cross ed.) details the wars among Yaroslav's heirs and nephews in the 1090s: Svyatopolk (Izyaslav line), Vladimir Monomakh (Vsevolod line), Oleg (Svyatoslav line/"Gorislavich" context), David Igorovich, Vasil'ko and Volodar Rostislavichi, Mstislav Vladimirich. Key episodes grounded in the chronicle: 1096 battles around Chernigov/Starodub/Murom (Oleg kills Izyaslav Vladimirich Sept 6; Mstislav campaigns and wins at Kulachek); 1097 Lyubech congress attempting to stabilize by hereditary domains + Holy Cross oath; immediate blinding of Vasil'ko by Svyatopolk/David (Nov 5 at Belgorod; detailed narrative of treachery, "Such a crime as this has never been perpetrated in Rus’"); subsequent revenge wars, defeat of Hungarians by Bonyak+David, Uvetichi settlement (1100), and 1103 anti-Polovtsian coalition victory at Suten' (20 princes slain). Monomakh's appended Testament and Letter to Oleg reference these conflicts (Starodub siege, Izyaslav's death) and counsel against them.
