## Role and Background

Grigory Zinoviev was a leading Bolshevik revolutionary and one of [[actors/lenin-vladimir|Lenin's]] closest associates before 1917, later heading the Communist International (Comintern) from its founding in 1919 and serving on the Politburo. After Lenin's death he formed part of the ruling "Troika" with [[actors/stalin-joseph|Stalin]] and Lev Kamenev against [[actors/trotsky-leon|Trotsky]] in the mid-1920s succession struggle.

## Fall and Execution

Zinoviev's alliance with Stalin proved short-lived: once Trotsky was defeated and marginalized, Stalin turned on his former partners, and Zinoviev was expelled from the party, readmitted, and expelled again over the following years amid the escalating factional struggles of the late 1920s. He was arrested during the Great Purge and became the lead defendant in the first Moscow Show Trial (August 1936), convicted on fabricated charges of conspiracy and terrorism, and executed immediately afterward.

## Significance

Zinoviev's trajectory — from Lenin's inner circle to Comintern leadership to the first prominent victim of Stalin's show trials — makes him emblematic of the Bolshevik Old Guard's destruction in the purges of the 1930s, and the Zinoviev trial is regarded as the opening act of the Great Terror's public phase.
