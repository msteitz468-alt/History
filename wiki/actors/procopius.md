# Procopius

**Procopius of Caesarea** (b. c. 500 in Caesarea Maritima, d. c. 565) was the principal
historian of the age of [[actors/justinian|Justinian]] and the most important
contemporary witness to the **[[events/justinianic-plague-541|Justinianic Plague]]**.
A Greek-speaking provincial of the eastern Roman elite, he served as legal secretary
and adviser to the general [[actors/belisarius|Belisarius]], accompanying him on the
campaigns against the Vandals and the Goths.

## Works

Procopius wrote in three registers that historians read against one another:

- The ***Wars*** (*Bella*) — an eyewitness military history of Justinian's campaigns
  against Persia, the Vandals, and the Goths (the [[events/gothic-war-535-554|Gothic
  War]]). Its second book contains the famous extended description of the plague's
  arrival at [[places/constantinople|Constantinople]] in 542.
- The ***Buildings*** (*De Aedificiis*) — a panegyric cataloguing Justinian's
  construction program: he registers some **600 military sites** touched by the emperor
  in the Balkans and the heavily fortified Persian frontier, and praises the rebuilt
  [[places/constantinople|Hagia Sophia]] as a dome that "soars to a height to match the
  sky."
- The ***Secret History*** (*Anekdota*) — a venomous private invective depicting
  Justinian and the empress **Theodora** as wantonly depraved, "maybe even demons."

## Witness to the plague

Procopius is Kyle Harper's "star witness" for the first pandemic. He pinpointed the
outbreak's entry into the Mediterranean at **Pelusium** in Egypt in 541, described its
spread to the capital, and recorded its demographic devastation — including the claim
that Gothic-War Italy's Rome held only **500 inhabitants** by 547. His mortality figures
are rhetorically inflated but indispensable for reconstructing the scale of the
[[concepts/roman-climate-optimum|environmental and biological crisis]] of the sixth
century. See [[sources/harper-fate-of-rome-2017|Harper, *The Fate of Rome*]].
