# Anna Komnene

**Anna Komnene** (1083–1153) was the eldest daughter of Emperor [[alexios-i-komnenos|Alexios I Komnenos]] and the empress Eirene Doukaina, and the author of the *Alexiad* — the most important Byzantine account of the First Crusade and the principal literary source for Alexios I's reign.

## Life and Career

Born in the porphyra (the imperial birthing chamber), Anna held the title of porphyrogenneta and was initially betrothed to the future emperor Nikephoros Bryennios. After her father's death (1118), she and her husband supported a coup attempt to place Anna's brother-in-law on the throne against her brother John II Komnenos; it failed. She spent much of the rest of her life in a monastery, where she wrote the *Alexiad*.

## The *Alexiad* as Historical Source

The *Alexiad* (composed c. 1140s, in 15 books) is a biography of her father framed as high literary history in the Attic classical tradition. Its significance for historians:

- **Scope**: covers Alexios I's entire reign (1081–1118) including the Norman wars, the Pecheneg campaigns, theological controversies, and above all the First Crusade (1096–99).
- **Indispensability**: no other contemporary Byzantine narrative of the First Crusade survives at comparable length and analytical depth. It is the primary window into how Byzantine leadership experienced and managed the Crusader armies.
- **Bias**: Anna was defending her father's legacy after his death and in the context of her own political failure. She presents Alexios as consistently wise and far-sighted; the Crusaders are depicted as useful but unruly instruments, at best, and potential threats at worst. Her treatment of Bohemond is the most nuanced: she respected his ability and found him personally compelling even while recording his hostility to Alexios.
- **Terminology**: Anna used *Latinos* 97 times and *Keltos/Keltikos* 176 times (interchangeably) — the latter an archaism reflecting her preference for classical Greek ethnonyms. She used *Frankos* 20 times, primarily for Normans. Kazhdan notes that in her usage, "Franks" (Normans) are treated as partially "ours" — some served Alexios loyally — while "Latins" generically are dangerous aliens.
- **Literary achievement**: Anna's Greek is among the finest Byzantine prose of any period; she consciously modeled her style on Thucydides.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without the *Alexiad* the First Crusade would be known primarily from Latin sources, giving no direct Byzantine perspective on Alexios's strategy, the oath-taking controversies, or the fracture with Bohemond over Antioch. The *Alexiad* also preserves the only sustained Byzantine literary typology of Latins/Franks written by someone who observed Crusade leadership directly.
