# Rhydderch Hael

Rhydderch Hael ("the Generous," c. 550–614 CE) was a king of [[places/ystrad-clud|Ystrad Clud]] (Alt Clut / Strathclyde), the Brittonic-speaking kingdom of the Old North centered on Dumbarton Rock, known chiefly from later Welsh and Latin hagiographic and poetic traditions rather than contemporary documentary sources — his reign sits at the boundary between history and legend typical of sub-Roman British kingship.

He appears in the Welsh Triads and genealogies as one of the "Three Generous Ones" of Britain and as a combatant, alongside Urien of Rheged, at the semi-legendary Battle of Arfderydd (573), a decisive conflict among northern British kings sometimes linked in later tradition to the origin of the Myrddin/Merlin legend. He is also closely associated with Saint Kentigern (Mungo), whom hagiographic tradition credits Rhydderch with restoring to his see at Glasgow, making Rhydderch a patron figure in the Christianization of the Clyde region.

As with much of the "Old North" material, the historicity of specific episodes attributed to Rhydderch is debated, and his reign is known mainly through retrospective Welsh literary sources compiled centuries after his death; his significance lies chiefly in marking Ystrad Clud's role as a durable Brittonic Christian kingdom on the northern frontier of Anglo-Saxon expansion.
