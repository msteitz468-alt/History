# Plains Village Tradition

## Overview

**Plains Village** refers to a series of related cultural manifestations, dating from **A.D. 900 to 1850**, represented by sedentary settlements reflecting a **more stable agricultural economy** than that of the early Plains Woodland groups. Cordell & Smith (CHNPA I.1 ch. 4) place Plains Village in the post-A.D. 800 northern maize expansion, distinct from Mississippian ranked chiefdoms of the interior riverine South/East.

## Geographic Distribution

Along permanent Central Plains streams from the Dakotas to Texas. Shared features: multifamily lodges, regionally variable pottery, bison-scapula bone hoes, small triangular arrow points.

## Economy

Productive **eight-row corn** plus sunflower, bottle gourd, and beans probably of **eastern origin** — *part_of:* [[north-american-agricultural-transition]]; *preceded_by* Plains Woodland interaction with Hopewellian East (cord-marked pottery, mound/ossuary burial, copper/obsidian trade) that **lacked direct evidence of agricultural reliance**.

## Regional lineages (Cordell & Smith)

| Branch | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Central Plains tradition** (Upper Republican, Loup River, St. Helena, Nebraska) | Generally ancestral to Pawnees |
| **Dismal River** | Ancestral to Plains Apaches |
| **Middle Missouri** (Dakotas) | Developed into Mandan, Hidatsa, Arikara — larger semisubterranean rectangular houses (some >90 m²), grit-tempered pottery |
| **Southern Plains** | Wichita lineage via Great Bend/Norteño to Panhandle/Custer/Washita — increasing agricultural tools over time |
