# Eugenius IV (Gabriele Condulmer; r. 1431–1447)

## Overview

**Eugenius IV**, elected eleven days after [[martin-v|Martin V]]’s death (20 February 1431), fought and then submitted to the **Council of Basel**, later transferred the legitimate council line to **Ferrara–Florence**, and secured the East–West reunion bull ***Laetentur coeli*** (1439) at the **[[council-of-florence-1439|Council of Florence]]**.

## Basel Crisis

After poor opening attendance at Basel and legate Cardinal **Cesarini**’s arrival from Hussite crusade defeat, Eugenius **dissolved Basel** (18 December 1431) and called Bologna. The council refused, reaffirmed *Haec sancta*, and held that a pope cannot dissolve a council without its approval. On **15 December 1433** Eugenius abjectly declared Basel legitimate from the beginning and nullified his dissolution—“Pope Eugenius IV had surrendered” ([[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]]). The council then triumphantly repeated *Haec sancta*.

## Split and Florence

Basel’s reform energy drained into replacing the curia and micro-managing administration; reforming decrees targeted mainly pope/curia (“head” only). On **7 May 1437** a split vote: majority for Avignon, minority (including most mitres) for Italy. Eugenius summoned **Ferrara** (January 1438); the best left Basel; the rump deposed Eugenius and elected the lay duke of Savoy as **Felix V**. Felix withdrew 1442, reconciled 1449 as cardinal; effective [[conciliarism|conciliarism]] perished. At Florence, a ~700-strong Greek delegation under Emperor **John VIII Paleologus** negotiated union issues culminating in *Laetentur coeli* (6 July 1439).

## Counterfactual Significance

Eugenius’s survival of Basel—after abject 1433 submission—made papal monarchical recovery possible; a lasting Basel supremacy would have transformed Latin church constitution for the later fifteenth century.

## Historiography

Logan treats Basel as pinnacle and nadir of the Conciliar Movement; among agents of demise, “perhaps chief among them the Greeks” (reunion agenda drawing talent and prestige to the papal council). Joseph Gill’s work on Florence and Eugenius is Logan’s Further Reading anchor.
