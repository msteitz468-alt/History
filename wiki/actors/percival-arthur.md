# Arthur Percival

Lieutenant-General Arthur Ernest Percival (1887–1966) was the British Army officer commanding Malaya Command at the outbreak of the Pacific War in December 1941, coinciding with [[attack-on-pearl-harbor-1941]]. A decorated First World War veteran with staff experience in Ireland and pre-war planning postings in Malaya (where, ironically, he had co-authored assessments warning of a Japanese landing in the north and overland assault on Singapore), he was given operational command shortly before the invasion he had once anticipated.

Facing [[yamashita-tomoyuki|Yamashita Tomoyuki]]'s rapid overland offensive down the Malayan Peninsula, Percival's larger but under-trained, under-equipped, and poorly coordinated force was repeatedly outmaneuvered. Singapore's defenses, oriented primarily toward a seaborne attack, proved vulnerable to the northern land approach. On 15 February 1942, with water and ammunition supplies critical and the city's fall imminent, Percival surrendered Singapore and some 80,000 troops — the largest capitulation in British military history.

He spent the remainder of the war as a Japanese prisoner, later present at the Japanese surrender in 1945. His command has been the subject of extensive historiographical debate over the relative weight of British strategic neglect of the Far East, inadequate resources, and his own generalship in the disaster.
