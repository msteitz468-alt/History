# Caral

## Overview

**Caral**, in the Supe Valley some 120 km north of Lima, was a major kingdom by **2600 BCE** —
among the earliest complex polities in the Andes, and one of the earliest anywhere in the
Americas. Documented here through Brian Fagan and Nadia Durrani's *World Prehistory: A Brief
Introduction* (10th ed., 2020), Caral anchored a regional network of **17 associated centers**
in the Supe region.

## Economy and Monumental Construction

Unlike almost every other early complex society, Caral's economy was **not agricultural** in
the food-crop sense: subsistence was heavily anchovy-based, confirmed archaeologically via
desiccated feces recovered at the site. The one crop the Caral economy depended on was
**cotton**, grown not for food but to manufacture fishing nets — an industrial rather than
food crop, in keeping with the broader "cotton preceramic" pattern documented for the Peruvian
coast (see [[maritime-foundations-of-andean-civilization]]).

Despite this non-agricultural economic base, Caral supported substantial monumental
construction: **six large stone platforms**, the largest measuring 152 × 137 m and standing 18
m high. The site was abandoned between roughly **2000 and 1500 BCE**.

## "Mother Culture" Caution

As the earliest known complex Andean polity of its scale, Caral carries the same
historiographical risk already flagged for the [[olmec|Olmec]] in Mesoamerica and later for
[[chavin|Chavín]] in the Andes: the temptation to read an early, well-documented site as a
singular "mother culture" origin point for later civilization, when the wider evidence
(Aspero, El Paraíso, and other Norte Chico centers) points instead to a **regional network** of
roughly contemporary complex societies along the Peruvian coast.

## Significance

Caral is central evidence for the Preceramic phase of [[andean-state-formation]], and for the
broader **maritime foundations** debate over whether marine resources alone, or marine
resources in combination with cotton cultivation and coast–highland exchange, drove the
Andes' earliest complexity (see [[maritime-foundations-of-andean-civilization]]).
