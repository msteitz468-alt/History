# Dawenkou Culture

The **Dawenkou culture** (~4100–2600 BC) of the lower [[yellow-river-valley|Yellow River]] —
Shandong, northern Jiangsu, eastern Henan, and northern Anhui — is, in
[[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's]] account, the source of the **earliest burial
stratification in the entire Yellow River region** and the antecedent of the eastern
([[longshan-culture|Shandong Longshan]]) tradition. Where the Central Plains channelled
social energy into public architecture, the Dawenkou and its Longshan successors expressed
emerging hierarchy through **elaborate, richly furnished graves**.

## Mortuary Trajectory

At the Dawenkou type site (Tai'an), Phase I burials (~4100–4000 BC) are small and
near-egalitarian; by **Phase II–III (~4000–3700 BC)** a few tombs hold 67–106 grave goods,
built with **ercengtai ledges**, concentrating in one spatial group. Statistically the
differentiation is real and unprecedented: across 46 Phase II–III burials the coefficient
of variation for grave goods is **1.36** and the size/goods correlation **R²=0.48** —
stronger than any Yangshao or Peiligang site. High-status goods include **turtle shells,
ivory/bone cylinders, jade, black-pottery goblets, pig mandibles, and *dakouweng* vessels
bearing pictograms**. The most elaborate tomb (M10) held an adult **female**, so no fixed
gender hierarchy existed, "although in general men enjoyed higher social status." Underhill
finds rising numbers of status-marked graves and prestige-good diversity across the late
Dawenkou.

At the late-Dawenkou center **Yuchisi** (Anhui), high-status burials are distributed across
four residential groups — evidence that stratification had developed **within and between
competing kin groups**. Fung (2000) argues there was an ideal burial assemblage attainable
only by those with sufficient resources, so that **hierarchy emerged partly as a consequence
of mortuary ritual itself**.

## Significance and Movement

The middle–late Dawenkou (3500–2600 BC) was **more socially complex than the contemporary
late Yangshao/early Longshan** of the Central Plains. Its first walled settlements
(Xikangliu, Wangzhuang, Dantu) appear in this period. Dawenkou cultural elements — ceramics,
**cranial deformation, tooth extraction** — turn up at 40+ central-Henan sites during the
late Yangshao/early Longshan, marking **population movements** westward (coinciding with
marine transgression along the coast). These elements disappear from the Central Plains by
the late Longshan, when the Yellow River's course change reimposed a barrier between
Shandong and Henan. The Dawenkou hierarchical-burial tradition was **continued and enhanced**
by the Shandong Longshan before that whole elite system collapsed under the Yueshi culture.

## Historiography

Dawenkou mortuary data are the empirical core of the argument that **individual-oriented
ancestral cults** drove eastern social stratification (contrast the group-oriented Central
Plains). See [[settlement-archaeology]] for the CV/R² method, [[early-china-state-formation]]
for the regional model, and [[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Liu (2004)]].
