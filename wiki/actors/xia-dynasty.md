# Xia Dynasty

The **Xia** (traditionally ~2100–1600 BC) is recorded in later Chinese texts as the **first
dynasty** — founded by **Yu the Great**, who tamed the floods, defined the **Nine
Prefectures**, regulated land use, and pacified the "Ten Thousand States" through
redistribution (so the *Hanshu* of Ban Gu). Whether the Xia was a historical state, and
whether it corresponds to the archaeological [[erlitou-culture|Erlitou culture]], is one of
the central disputes of early Chinese studies — see [[xia-dynasty-historicity]].

## The Archaeological Question

Most Chinese archaeologists have reached a **consensus** that the [[erlitou|Erlitou site]]
was most likely the **capital of the late Xia**, and texts placing repeated Xia capital
relocations point to the core area of the Erlitou culture in central Henan. Many Western
Sinologists are skeptical of mapping a textually-attested dynasty onto a pottery horizon,
and some doubt the early Xia existed as a state at all.

[[liu-chinese-neolithic-2004|Li Liu's]] position threads the needle: the earliest
**state-level** society in the Erlitou sequence is recognisable only from **Erlitou Phase II
(~1800 BC)** — which does **not** match the traditional ~2100 BC Xia founding. So what is
called the "Xia" in its early period (late Longshan and Erlitou Phase I) **may not have been
a state**; the durable state appears later, archaeologically, as Erlitou. Liu therefore
argues Erlitou is the first state **regardless** of its relationship to the legendary Xia.

The neighbouring **Proto-Shang** (Xiaqiyuan culture) is held to be the Xia's contemporary
rival in northern Henan / southern Hebei — see [[shang-dynasty]].

## Counterfactual / Significance

If the Xia is essentially a **retrospective dynastic frame** later imposed on the Erlitou
polity, then the "first dynasty" of Chinese tradition is a textual construction built atop a
genuine archaeological state — a case where [[nationalism-and-archaeology|nationalist and
historiographic stakes]] shape interpretation. The flood-control charter myth of Yu also
echoes the real role of **Yellow River flooding** in the late-Longshan-to-Erlitou transition
(see [[early-china-state-formation]]).
