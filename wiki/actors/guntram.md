# Guntram

**Guntram** (Gunthchramn, c. 532–592) was a son of **[[chlothar-i|Chlothar I]]** who held
the kingdom of **Burgundy** (capital Orléans, then Chalon-sur-Saône) from the partition of
561. Surviving his brothers [[sigibert-i|Sigibert I]] and [[chilperic-i|Chilperic I]], he
became the senior figure and arbiter of the Frankish world in the 570s–580s. He is
**[[gregory-of-tours-history-of-the-franks-thorpe-1974|Gregory's]]** model of the **good
king** — pious, charitable, merciful — a portrait coloured by Gregory's approval of his
church-friendliness; he was later venerated as a saint.

## Career

Childless after his two sons died of disease, Guntram repeatedly acted as guardian of the
dynasty. He adopted his nephew **[[childebert-ii|Childebert II]]** at the Stone Bridge
meeting — "Let one single shield protect us both… and a single spear defend us" — and after
Chilperic's murder took the infant **[[chlothar-ii|Chlothar II]]** under protection too,
asking "three years at least in which to bring up these two nephews of mine." Deeply marked
by his brothers' violent deaths, he "was always surrounded by a band of guards," even in
church.

In **585** he suppressed the **[[gundovald-affair-585|Gundovald pretender]]**, raising "the
whole army of his realm" and pursuing the usurper to Comminges. He distrusted
[[brunhild|Brunhild]] (recalling that she "threatened to murder me") and openly named
[[fredegund|Fredegund]] as a would-be assassin, refusing friendship "to a woman who on more
than one occasion has sent her men to murder me." Yet he stood godfather to Fredegund's son
Lothar at Nanterre, insisting it did not break his treaty obligations. In **587** he
concluded the **Treaty of Andelot** with Childebert II and Brunhild, fixing mutual
inheritance — whichever king outlived the other without male heir "shall inherit the
kingdom of that other, in its entirety and in perpetuity" — and restoring Brunhild's
dower-cities. Gregory shows him refusing to sell bishoprics, restoring unjust seizures,
honouring the wills Chilperic had torn up, and giving lavishly to churches and the poor.

## Counterfactual Significance

Guntram's longevity and willingness to act as adoptive father to his nephews preserved the
dynasty through the crisis of the 580s. The Andelot inheritance pact is the legal hinge by
which Burgundy ultimately passed to Childebert II's Austrasian line; Guntram's stabilising
mediation is the main reason the civil wars did not destroy the Merovingian state outright.
Gregory's idealisation, however, makes him the explicit moral foil to Chilperic — a
construction as much as a record. See [[merovingian-franks]].
