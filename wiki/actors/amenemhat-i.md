# Amenemhat I

**Sehetepibra Amenemhat I** (r. ~1985–1956 BCE) founded the **12th Dynasty**, the great
age of the [[actors/middle-kingdom-egypt|Middle Kingdom]]. A man "from outside the royal
family" — son of a commoner named Senusret and a woman called Nefret — he was almost
certainly the same Amenemhat who had served as vizier to the last 11th-Dynasty king,
Mentuhotep IV.

## Legitimation and the move to Itjtawy

His accession was retrospectively legitimized by the **Prophecy of Neferty**, a text cast
as ancient prediction of a saviour-king "Ameny, the justified" who would arise from the
south, repel the Asiatics and build the "Walls-of-the-Ruler." Amenemhat moved the capital
from Thebes north to **Itjtawy** ("Amenemhat the seizer of the two lands"), an as-yet
undiscovered site near the Lisht necropolis in the Faiyum region — a strategic relocation
that also meant "the officials who served him at Itjtawy would have been entirely dependent
on the king rather than having their own power bases." He made the **individual town** the
focus of administration and personally appointed the nomarchs (see
[[concepts/egyptian-bureaucracy]]).

## Frontier policy and assassination

He fortified the eastern Delta with the **"Walls-of-the-Ruler"** against Asiatic incursion
and transformed Nubian policy "from the loose network of sporadic trading and quarrying
ventures that characterized the Old Kingdom to a new strategy of conquest and
colonization, principally with the aim of obtaining raw materials, especially gold."

Amenemhat I was **assassinated** — murdered in the night by members of his own bodyguard,
as recounted in the *Teaching of Amenemhat I* (probably composed early in the reign of his
son and co-regent [[actors/middle-kingdom-egypt|Senusret I]], who learned of the murder
while on campaign in Libya). The episode is the locus classicus of the disputed
12th-Dynasty **co-regency** question (see [[concepts/egyptian-dynastic-chronology]]).
