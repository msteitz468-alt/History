# Martin Niemöller

Lutheran pastor of Dahlem, co-founder of the Pastors' Emergency League (with
Bonhoeffer, September 1933; ~6,000 pastors by year's end) and the most famous
figure of the Confessing Church — and, per Evans, a far more ambiguous figure
than the later legend. A Great War U-boat commander who supported the Kapp
Putsch, founded a Free Corps unit, and voted Nazi in March 1933, he preached
in 1935 that the Jews were "eternally cursed" for the Crucifixion — while
arguing judgment belonged to God, not the state. His resistance was religious,
not political: defiant Dahlem sermons naming Goebbels, Rosenberg, and Gürtner
drew congregations of 1,500. Acquitted of the serious charges at his 1938
trial, he was rearrested at the prison gate on Hitler's personal order
(2 March 1938) and held as Hitler's "personal prisoner" in solitary at
Sachsenhausen — where guards forced him to announce himself as "the swine
Niemöller" — then Dachau from 1941. He offered to rejoin the navy in September
1939 (refused), repudiated his own antisemitism after witnessing the suffering
of Jewish inmates, and after the war authored the famous "First they came…"
confession — which, Evans notes, named Communists, trade unionists, and
socialists but in its classic formulations reflected his regret at the
narrowness of a purely religious opposition.

## Historiography

Benjamin Ziemann's biography (2019) has consolidated the critical reading
Evans anticipates: hero of conscience and lifelong nationalist whose
trajectory indicts the limits of church resistance.
