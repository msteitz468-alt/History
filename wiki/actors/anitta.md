## Overview

Anitta was a king of Kussara — a city-state probably located in the anti-Taurus region southeast of the Kızıl Irmak basin, possibly near modern Şar (Comana Cappadociae) — who is the earliest named ruler in Anatolian history to survive in a substantial written record. He is the reputed author of the **Anitta inscription** (CTH 1), the oldest text composed in the Nesite (proto-Hittite) language, preserved in three fragmentary copies apparently from an original stela set up at the gate of his capital. The earliest surviving version is a later Hittite Old Kingdom copy, made approximately 150 years or more after the original.

## Background and Dynasty

Anitta's father **Pithana** founded the Kussaran dynasty's military dominance. During the second phase of the [[old-assyrian-trade|Assyrian colony period]] (Kanesh Level Ib, roughly late 19th to mid-18th century BCE, middle chronology), Pithana struck first:

> "The king of Kussara came down from the town in great force and took Nesa in the night by storm. He seized the king of Nesa, but inflicted no harm on the inhabitants of Nesa. Instead, he made them his mothers and fathers." (Anitta inscript., CTH 1, lines 5–9)

The claim that he made the Nesans "his mothers and fathers" is unique in cuneiform literature. It may reflect propaganda of benevolence rather than literal ethnic kinship, though some scholars read into it ethnic affinities between Kussara and the Indo-European population of [[kanesh|Nesa/Kanesh]]. After the conquest Pithana relocated his dynastic seat to Nesa, placing the Kussaran dynasty at the administrative hub of the [[old-assyrian-trade|Assyrian merchant network]].

## Conquests

Anitta continued and vastly extended his father's campaigns. His first great sequence of operations subdued the kingdoms within and around the Kızıl Irmak (Marassantiya) basin:

- **Zalpa (Zalpuwa)** in or near the Pontic zone: conquered; its king Huzziya was brought in captivity to Nesa.
- **Kingdom of Hatti**: defeated; Piyusti, the king of Hatti, was a leading member of a hostile military alliance that Anitta broke.
- **Ullamma** in the south: subjugated.

By the end of this first series, Anitta controlled "all the lands which lay within or near the Kızıl Irmak basin — from Zalpa in the far north to Ullamma in the south" (Bryce, *Kingdom of the Hittites*, p. 37).

### The Destruction of Hattusa and the Curse

After breaking the Zalpa–Hatti alliance, Anitta placed Hattus(a) under siege. When the population was weakened by hunger, he took it "in a night assault, and destroyed it." He then pronounced the most famous curse in Anatolian history:

> "On its site I sowed weeds. May the Storm God strike down anyone who becomes king after me and resettles Hattusa." (Anitta inscript., lines 48–51)

This curse haunted later Hittite tradition. When [[hattusili-i|Hattusili I]] — the likely founder of the Hittite capital — eventually chose Hattusa as his permanent seat, he was knowingly defying Anitta's imprecation, a fact that later Hittite texts acknowledge.

### Southern Campaigns

Anitta next turned south. He waged two campaigns against **Salatiwara**, a city on the road connecting Wahsusana and Burushattum, destroying it on the second attempt and removing large quantities of silver, gold, and "40 teams of horse." He also built temples at Nesa and inaugurated his rule with a spectacular display of animals — "2 lions, 70 wild pigs, and 120 other beasts, including leopards, deer, and wild goats" — symbolizing the spoils of conquest.

### Campaign Against Purushanda

The final recorded campaign targeted **Purushanda** (Burushattum), the most powerful southern kingdom whose ruler bore the title *rubā'um rabī'um* ("Great King"), a title that outranked even the ruler of Ashur. When Anitta entered his territory the Purushandan king submitted without battle:

> "When I [—] went into battle, the Man of Purushanda brought gifts to me; he brought to me a throne of iron and a sceptre of iron as a gift. But when I returned to Nesa I took the Man of Purushanda with me. As soon as he enters the chamber, that man will sit before me on the right." (Anitta inscript., lines 73–9)

The gifts signified formal surrender. Anitta accorded the Purushandan king an honoured but subordinate position at Nesa — a practice later reflected in standard Hittite treatment of defeated rulers.

## The Anitta Dagger

Material confirmation of Anitta's palace at Nesa/Kanesh came from an inscribed dagger discovered in 1954 in the debris of a large building on the mound at Kültepe, bearing the inscription: "the palace of Anitta, the King." This physical artefact, combined with the textual evidence of the inscription, confirms [[kanesh|Nesa/Kültepe]] as Anitta's royal seat.

## Historical Significance

Anitta's empire — encompassing the Kızıl Irmak basin from Zalpa to Purushanda — was the first attested attempt to unify central Anatolia under a single political structure. It was, however, short-lived: "in probably less than a generation after Anitta's conquests, the colonies had come to an end" (Bryce, p. 39). The increased conflicts generated by his campaigns may paradoxically have accelerated the disintegration of the Anatolian kingdom system and ended the Assyrian merchant enterprises.

### Counterfactual Significance

Without Anitta, the political precedent of a Kızıl Irmak-basin unifying state may not have existed for later Hittite kings to build on. Conversely, his curse of [[hattusha|Hattusa]] shaped later Hittite political theology: the decision to site the Hittite capital precisely there required deliberate ideological confrontation with his legacy. The "throne of iron and a sceptre of iron" gifted by Purushanda is one of the earliest references to iron as a prestige metal in Anatolia.

- **caused_by**: [[old-assyrian-trade|Assyrian Colony Period]] — competition over control of the merchant network's hub drove Pithana's initial conquest of Nesa; Anitta inherited and exploited this position.

- **preceded_by**: Pithana's conquest of Nesa; Zalpa's destruction of Nesa during Level II.
- **followed_by**: [[hattusili-i|Hattusili I's]] Old Kingdom — the Hittite kingdom arose from the ruins of Anitta's collapsed empire.

## Historiography

The Anitta inscription is unique as the oldest text written in Nesite (proto-Hittite). Bryce notes that it was "once thought to have been written in Old Assyrian" but is now accepted as originally composed in Nesite. The surviving copies are Old Kingdom recensions made roughly 150 years after the events, raising questions of transmission and editorial shaping. The ethnic interpretations of Pithana's "mothers and fathers" formula have generated debate: most current scholarship cautions against reading literal ethnic solidarity into what may be standard diplomatic rhetoric. The dating of Anitta to the Level Ib colony phase (roughly c. 1800–1750 BCE, middle chronology) is broadly accepted but approximate. The location of Kussara itself remains unconfirmed archaeologically.
