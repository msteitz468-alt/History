# John Foster Dulles

U.S. Secretary of State 1953–1959 under Eisenhower. Architect of "massive retaliation," "brinkmanship," and the Southeast Asia Treaty Organization (SEATO). Central figure in the 1954 Dien Bien Phu crisis and Geneva Conference diplomacy.

## Role in Indochina

Dulles viewed the French war through the lens of global containment and the domino theory. He pushed for greater U.S. involvement but was constrained by congressional opinion, British reluctance, and Eisenhower's caution after Korea. During the siege he explored (with French and some British) the possibility of U.S. air strikes ("Operation Vulture") to relieve the garrison, but conditions (allied participation, congressional approval) were not met. At Geneva he refused to endorse the final accords, insisting the U.S. would not be bound by them, while privately working to build up a non-communist state south of the 17th parallel.

## Style and Influence

Moralistic, legalistic, and anti-communist in public rhetoric; pragmatic alliance manager in practice. Deeply suspicious of European colonialism yet committed to preventing communist gains in the decolonizing world. His handling of Indochina in 1954 set the pattern for subsequent U.S. commitment to South Vietnam.
