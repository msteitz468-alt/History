# Naḥmanides (Moses ben Naḥman / Ramban)

Prominent Catalan rabbi, biblical commentator, philosopher, and physician (1194–1270), known by the acronym Ramban and, in Latin scholarship, as Nahmanides. One of the foremost Jewish scholars of the medieval period, he authored an influential Torah commentary integrating rationalist, mystical (early kabbalistic), and traditional exegetical approaches, and served as a communal leader in Girona and Barcelona.

During the second Maimonidean Controversy of the 1230s, when anti-Maimonidean traditionalists in southern France sought to have Maimonides' philosophical works (especially the *Guide of the Perplexed*) condemned and banned across Jewish communities, Naḥmanides was called upon by the anti-Maimonideans to join their cause but instead wrote a carefully balanced diplomatic letter. He defended Maimonides' underlying purpose — arguing the *Guide* was meant to retain Jews at risk of losing their faith to philosophy, not to corrupt observant believers — while also arguing that it was inappropriate for one Jewish community to impose its religious-intellectual norms on another, since what served Jews in philosophically sophisticated Andalusia might not suit the more traditionalist communities of Provence. Late in life, Naḥmanides was compelled to participate in the 1263 Barcelona Disputation against the converted Jew Pablo Christiani before the royal court of Aragon, after which mounting pressure led him to emigrate to the Land of Israel, where he helped reestablish the Jewish community of Jerusalem before his death.

## Significance

Naḥmanides's mediating role in the Maimonidean Controversy exemplifies a sophisticated, pluralistic approach to intra-Jewish religious-intellectual conflict, respecting regional difference rather than seeking uniform doctrinal enforcement.
