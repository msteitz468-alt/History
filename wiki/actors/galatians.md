## Overview

The **Galatians** were the Celts (Gauls) who, after the
[[celtic-invasion-of-greece-279|invasion of Greece and Macedon]] (280–277), **crossed
into Asia Minor in 278/7** and settled in north-central Anatolia — the region thereafter
called **Galatia**. For a century they were a feared and disruptive presence, raiding and
extorting tribute from the Greek cities and dynasts, and serving as the most prized (and
turbulent) mercenaries of the [[hellenistic-world|Hellenistic]] armies. CAH VII.1 ch. 11
(Heinen) treats them as a key factor in the reshaping of Asia Minor.

## From Raiders to Settlers

Invited across the straits by **Nicomedes of Bithynia** (to use against a rival), the
Celts soon "seized their independence" and overran Asia Minor as complete migrating tribes
— the **Tolistoagii, Tectosages, and Trocmi** — with wives, children, and chattels. Cities
and dynasts often had no recourse but to pay them tribute. [[antiochus-i-soter|Antiochus
I]] won the celebrated **"elephant battle"** against them (c. 275–270), earning his cult
title *Soter* — but, Heinen notes (correcting older views), this did **not** end the
danger: the Galatians remained capable of large operations for decades, were decisive (as
mercenaries) at Antiochus Hierax's victory over Seleucus II at Ancyra (c. 240/39), and were
only gradually confined to their inland settlements — the Tolistoagii around Pessinus and
Gordium, the Tectosages around Ancyra, the Trocmi on the Halys.

## The Barbarian Foil of Pergamene Art

Their great antagonist was **[[pergamon|Pergamum]]**: **Attalus I** was the first to refuse
them tribute and to beat them in battle, and from his victories "the birth of Pergamene
art" — the **Dying Gaul** and the **Gaul killing himself and his wife**, and later the
Great Altar — in which the Galatians occupy "the place that had once been filled by the
Persians in the political thought and art of Athens." Heinen stresses the calculated irony:
the dynasts who posed as Greek champions against the Galatian barbarian (the Attalids, but
also Antiochus Hierax) freely took **Galatian mercenaries** into their own service.

## Significance

The Galatians are a vivid instance of the **nomadic/migratory shock** to the settled
Hellenistic order (compare the [[parthian-empire|Parni]] in the East), and of how the
"barbarian" served as an **ideological foil** for Hellenistic kingship and art. Their
hellenization began only in the second century; they remained a distinct people in Anatolia
into the Roman period (the "Galatians" of Paul's epistle). See
[[celtic-invasion-of-greece-279]], [[pergamon]], [[antiochus-i-soter]].
