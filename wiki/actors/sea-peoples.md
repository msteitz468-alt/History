# Sea Peoples

"Sea Peoples" is the modern collective name (after the Egyptian phrase for the
"foreign countries / peoples of the sea") for a series of seaborne and overland
coalitions of raiders and migrants who struck the eastern Mediterranean coastlands
in the decades around 1200 BCE. They are the most visible agent — cause, symptom,
or both — of the [[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]]. They are
treated here as a *movement* rather than a single state or people: the Egyptian
records name them as a confederation of distinct groups, and they appear to have
had no unified political organisation.

> **Chronology note.** CAH II.2 (chapter XXVIII) dates the climactic assault to
> [[ramesses-iii|Ramesses III]]'s Year 8 on the volume's high chronology
> (c. 1191 BCE); the conventional modern date is c. **1177 BCE**. Merneptah's
> earlier engagement is conventionally c. 1208 BCE.

## The Constituent Peoples

The Egyptian sources (the Great Karnak Inscription and "Israel" stele of
[[merneptah|Merneptah]]; the Medinet Habu reliefs and Papyrus Harris I of
[[ramesses-iii|Ramesses III]]) name a recurring but shifting roster:

| Egyptian name | Common identification (contested) |
|---|---|
| **Peleset** | [[philistines|Philistines]]; settled in the southern Levant |
| **Tjekker (Sikils)** | settled at Dor on the Levantine coast |
| **Sherden (Shardana)** | earlier serve as mercenaries in Egyptian armies; linked by some to Sardinia |
| **Shekelesh** | linked by some to Sicily (Sikels) |
| **Tursha (Teresh)** | linked by some to the Tyrrhenians / Etruscans |
| **Akawasha (Ekwesh)** | argued by some to be [[ahhiyawa-question|Achaeans / Aḫḫiyawa]] |
| **Lukka** | southwest Anatolia (later Lycia); appear already in the Amarna age |
| **Denyen (Danuna)** | linked variously to the Danaoi or to Cilician Adana |
| **Weshesh** | unidentified |

The Mediterranean "homeland" identifications (Sardinia, Sicily, Etruria) rest on
later name-resemblances and are disputed; they may reflect *destinations* of the
movements as much as origins. CAH II.2's western-Mediterranean chapter (Ch. XXXVII,
Daniel) takes up the **Sherden** case directly: of the two views — that they came
*from* Sardinia or *from* **Sardis** in Asia Minor and later gave their name to the
island — Daniel prefers the **Asia Minor origin**, seeing the Sherden as eastern
warrior-chiefs who, after raiding Egypt, may have founded or reinforced the
**[[western-mediterranean|Nuraghic civilization]] of Sardinia**. What the sources establish securely is that the
coalitions were ethnically mixed and moved with families, ox-carts, and ships —
i.e., they were migrations, not merely naval raids.

## Narrative of the Assaults

- **Merneptah, Year 5 (c. 1208 BCE).** A Libyan king, Meryey, invaded the western
  Delta allied with a coalition of northerners — Akawasha, Tursha, Lukka,
  Shekelesh, and Sherden. Merneptah defeated them in a six-hour battle, claiming
  thousands of dead and captives. This is the first appearance of the Sea Peoples
  as a named threat to Egypt, here as auxiliaries of a Libyan invasion.
- **Ramesses III, Year 8 (c. 1177 BCE).** The Medinet Habu inscriptions describe a
  coordinated land and sea offensive. An overland column moved down through Syria
  with families and wagons — Medinet Habu records that "no land could stand before
  their arms," naming the fall of Ḫatti, Qode (Cilicia), Carchemish, Arzawa, and
  [[cyprus|Alašiya]] in their path — while a fleet attacked by sea. Ramesses III
  defeated the land force in the Levant and trapped the fleet in the Nile mouths,
  where Egyptian archers on the banks and ships annihilated it. This is the
  decisive repulse; the reliefs are the single richest source for the appearance,
  equipment (feathered headdresses, horned helmets, oxcarts), and composition of
  the Sea Peoples.

After the defeat in Egypt, the surviving groups settled along the coasts they had
ravaged — most importantly the **Peleset as the [[philistines|Philistines]]** in
the southern Levantine pentapolis (Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod, Ekron, Gath), and the
Tjekker at Dor (attested still in the 11th-century *Report of Wenamun*).

## Causal Significance

- `contributed_to:` [[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]] — the Sea
  Peoples physically destroyed coastal centres ([[ugarit|Ugarit]] explicitly
  reports enemy ships before its fall) and severed the maritime trade that the
  palace economies depended on.
- `caused_by:` the same upheavals they helped spread — drought, famine, and the
  breakdown of the [[mycenaean-civilization|Aegean palace world]] that set
  populations in motion. The volume treats them as a prime mover; modern systems
  models treat them substantially as a *symptom* of prior collapse (see
  [[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|the collapse page's historiography]]).
- `produced:` the [[philistines|Philistine]] settlement and the wider
  reconfiguration of the Levantine coast.

## Historiography

The Sea Peoples are among the most contested topics in Bronze Age studies. CAH
II.2 (1975) presents them largely at face value as a migratory invasion that
brought down the empires. Subsequent scholarship has questioned almost every
element: whether they constituted a coherent phenomenon or an Egyptian rhetorical
construct; whether the Mediterranean homeland identifications have any value;
whether they caused the collapse or were displaced by it; and how much of the
Medinet Habu account is propaganda modelled on earlier triumphal templates rather
than a report of distinct events. The reliability of the inscriptions as event
history, and the cause/symptom question, are both treated on the
[[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age Collapse]] page. This actor page records
the figures and the named groups; it does not adjudicate the controversy.
