# John M. Schofield

Union general who commanded the Army of the Ohio during the Atlanta
Campaign and, most consequentially, defended Franklin, Tennessee against
John Bell Hood's disastrous frontal assault in November 1864.

## Role and Major Decisions

At the [[battle-of-franklin-1864|Battle of Franklin]], Schofield's forces
held a fortified line against repeated, costly Confederate assaults that
devastated Hood's Army of Tennessee's officer corps and combat power before
withdrawing in good order to Nashville, where [[george-h-thomas|George H.
Thomas]]'s subsequent victory completed the destruction of Hood's army.
Schofield's disciplined, orderly conduct of both the defense and the
withdrawal preserved his own force nearly intact while inflicting
disproportionate losses on the attacker.

## Counterfactual Significance

Schofield's stand at Franklin set up the conditions for Thomas's decisive
victory at Nashville weeks later; a less orderly defense or withdrawal could
have exposed his army to destruction in the field rather than allowing it to
reinforce Thomas.

## Historiography

Schofield's postwar career (including a stint as Secretary of War and
Commanding General of the US Army) gave him more influence over the war's
official record than many peers; historians generally credit his tactical
performance at Franklin highly, though his relationship with Thomas was
privately strained by rivalry over credit for the campaign's success.
