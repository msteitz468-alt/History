# CIDOB (Confederation of Indigenous Peoples of Bolivia)

The umbrella organization of Bolivia's lowland indigenous peoples, founded in 1982
(originally as the Central de Pueblos Indígenas del Oriente Boliviano) and uniting peoples
of the Amazonian and Chaco lowlands — Guaraní, Chiquitano, Mojeño, Ayoreo, and others. It
became the principal political voice of the eastern lowlands, distinct from the
highland Aymara-Quechua peasant and [[katarismo|Katarista]] movements.

This is the concise summary; CIDOB recurs in the twentieth-century chapters of
[[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-2|CHNPA III Part 2]].

## Role and Significance

- **The 1990 March for Territory and Dignity** — CIDOB led the landmark lowland march from
  the Beni to La Paz, putting indigenous territorial rights on the national agenda.
- **Plurinationalism** — CIDOB became a central actor in the mobilizations that culminated
  in Bolivia's 2009 plurinational constitution and the recognition of indigenous territories
  (TCOs).
