# John F. Kennedy

**Summary page.** John Fitzgerald Kennedy (1917–1963) was the 35th President of the United States (1961–1963). This page provides network-oriented summary; detailed treatment lives in the biography hub if warranted.

## Overview

Senator from Massachusetts, author of *Profiles in Courage*, narrow winner of 1960 presidential election against Richard Nixon. Selected Lyndon Johnson as running mate for Southern and congressional balance; privately acknowledged the ticket's dependence on Texas and LBJ's campaigning. As President, his legislative program (civil rights, tax cut) was largely stalled by the conservative coalition in Congress at the time of his death. His assassination on November 22, 1963 in Dallas triggered the transition that is the focus of Caro Vol. 4.

## Key Developments (LBJ intersection)

- 1960 primaries and convention: Defeated LBJ for nomination; offered and secured LBJ's acceptance of VP slot after tense "You Do It" dynamics and RFK pressure.
- As President: kept Johnson at arm's length; rejected power-sharing proposals; White House staff treated VP with open contempt.
- Domestic policy: Pushed civil rights bill and tax reduction that were bottled up until after Dallas.
- Foreign: Cuban Missile Crisis (VP reportedly excluded from final ExComm decision meeting); limited engagement on Vietnam.
- Dallas: Assassinated; LBJ sworn in aboard Air Force One with Jacqueline Kennedy present.

## Significance

The Kennedy presidency forms the immediate backdrop for LBJ's ascent. The personal and political relationship between JFK and LBJ, and especially the RFK-LBJ feud, shaped the transition and early Johnson presidency.
