# John F. Kennedy

**Summary page.** John Fitzgerald Kennedy (1917–1963) was the 35th President of the United States (1961–1963). This page provides network-oriented summary; detailed treatment lives in the biography hub if warranted.

## Overview

Senator from Massachusetts (1953–1960), author of *Profiles in Courage*, narrow winner of 1960 presidential election against Richard Nixon. Selected Lyndon Johnson as running mate for Southern and congressional balance. As President (1961–1963), pursued New Frontier agenda amid congressional gridlock; navigated Bay of Pigs, Cuban Missile Crisis, early Vietnam decisions, and civil rights push. Chronic back pain, Addison's disease, and other health issues were lifelong; private life marked by multiple infidelities and family pressures. Assassinated 22 November 1963 in Dallas. His character was in active evolution in his final months; detailed analytic treatment in the biography hub.

## Key Developments

- Early life and family (1917–1945): Youngest of nine; severe childhood illnesses; competitive family culture under patriarch Joseph P. and devout but emotionally distant mother Rose; PT-109 heroism in WWII.
- Senate and courtship (1946–1953): Elected to House then Senate 1952; met Jacqueline Bouvier; first Hyannis visit July 1952; marriage September 1953. Jackie viewed as political asset; relationship complex from outset.
- 1960 campaign and presidency: Defeated LBJ for nomination; LBJ as VP for balance. Bay of Pigs failure (1961); Cuban Missile Crisis (1962) — high-stakes ExComm deliberations, military push for strikes vs. caution over Berlin; early Vietnam policy and Diem coup.
- Private and secret life: Lifelong health struggles (crutches, Addison's, back); serial infidelities; father’s model of marriage influenced tolerance expectations. By 1963 showed signs of regret and desire for change ("I haven’t been the best husband"; planning vow renewal with Jackie for 1964).
- Assassination and unfinished life: Dallas 1963-11-22; agenda on civil rights, tax cut, Cold War, Vietnam left incomplete. Character evolution cut short at age 46. See [[events/assassination-of-john-f-kennedy-1963]] for the discrete event page and [[controversies/jfk-assassination-high-cabal-thesis]] for interpretive disputes.
- Vietnam policy and contested interpretations (attributed): Prouty (former Pentagon Focal Point liaison) asserts that Kennedy had committed via NSAM 263 (11 Oct 1963) to withdrawal of 1,000 advisers by year-end and broader disengagement, and that the immediate post-assassination reversal (NSAM 288 under LBJ) was structural rather than contingent. These claims are recorded as the author's thesis on the linked controversy page; they are used here only as attributed insider testimony from a participant in the national security bureaucracy. Cross-reference scholarly accounts for context.

## Significance

The Kennedy presidency forms the immediate backdrop for LBJ's ascent. The personal and political relationship between JFK and LBJ, and especially the RFK-LBJ feud, shaped the transition and early Johnson presidency. Taraborrelli provides the richest single-source treatment of the public/private/secret dimensions, family formation, health, infidelities, and late character evolution ("unfinished life").
