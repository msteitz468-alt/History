# Maximian

**Marcus Aurelius Valerius Maximianus** (*Herculius*; Augustus 286–305, d. 310) was [[diocletian|Diocletian]]’s western partner in the [[roman-tetrarchy|tetrarchy]] — the Herculian half of the Iovius/Herculius ideological pair.

## Career (Mitchell)

Made Augustus in 286; the panegyric of 291 celebrated *concordia* between the two emperors under Jupiter/Hercules roles. Usually based at Milan or Aquileia. In May 305 he retired with Diocletian when the first tetrarchy was dissolved. He was later drawn back into politics: after repeated claims to power, [[constantine-the-great|Constantine]] forced him to suicide near Massilia in 310. His dynastic link underwrote [[maxentius|Maxentius]]’s Italian regime.

## Counterfactual significance

Without Maximian’s western military partnership, Diocletian’s multi-front defense and the symbolic Iovius/Herculius ideology lack their western pillar; Maxentius’s claim to Italy loses its dynastic cover.

## Historiography

Panegyrics and Lactantius frame rival official versions; Mitchell uses them as evidence of tetrarchic ideology while cautioning on apologetic narrative. Survey: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Mitchell 2015]].
