# Horace (Quintus Horatius Flaccus)

Central poet of the [[augustus|Augustan]] age after [[virgil]]: author of *Satires*, *Epodes*, *Odes*, *Epistles*, and *Ars Poetica* — the model of Roman lyric, moralising hexameter, and literary criticism.

## Role

Born 65 BCE at Venusia; fought at **Philippi** on the Republican side; later entered the circle of **Maecenas** and received the Sabine farm that anchors his poetic persona. Lyne (OHCW) treats him within Augustan poetry and society: public forms redirected private Callimachean craft toward imperial peace and elite self-fashioning under the [[principate]]. Greek metres (especially Alcaic and Sapphic) are naturalized in Latin; moral commonplaces (*aurea mediocritas*, *carpe diem*) become cultural shorthand.

## OHCW (Lyne Ch. 24) — works and periods

Freedman’s son, Athens-educated; became near poet-laureate while Propertius, Tibullus, and [[ovid]] were equites — culmination of poets’ rise from low/client status to full-time professionals. *Epodes*/*Satires*: residual Republican libertarianism then commitment (Epode 9 on Actium; Sabine estate in Sat. 2.6). *Odes* 1–3 as “Roman Alcaeus” — public engage poetry plus leisure love/wine with proportion (vs elegists’ totalizing *otium*); “indirect” public method associates Augustus with Regulus (3.5) or Gigantomachy (3.4), avoiding invidious direct praise; love poems reflect demi-monde not upper-class adultery. After *Odes* 1–3, *Epistles* 1 turn to private ethics. Second Augustan period: *Carmen saeculare* (17 BCE Secular Games), *Odes* 4 panegyric under direct Augustus after Maecenas fades, *Epistle to Augustus* reasserts educative/immortalizing function and claims classic status while drawing a “veil of silence” over the love elegists (Ovid already the rage). Maecenas’ scale of gifts (Sabine farm) created moral pressure without Stalinist coercion.

## Significance

Horace supplied Rome — and later Europe — with a usable ethics of moderation, patronage, and poetic autonomy under monarchy. Without him the Augustan “settlement” lacks its lyric voice parallel to Virgil’s epic.

## Historiography
