# Dominican Order (Order of Preachers)

## Overview

The **Dominican Order** (Order of Preachers), founded by [[dominic-de-guzman|Dominic de Guzmán]] and confirmed by the papacy in 1216, was a **mendicant** order of learned preachers formed to combat heresy through doctrine and example. Dominicans quickly dominated chairs of theology at the [[medieval-university|universities]] and staffed much of the later medieval machinery of orthodoxy.

## From Logan, History of the Church in the Middle Ages (2013)

They were **friars not canons**: evangelical poverty by gifts and begging; white habit with black cloak — “**Black Friars**”; official name **Order of Preachers**. Innocent agreed in principle before [[fourth-lateran-council-1215|Fourth Lateran]] if they adopted an existing rule (Augustine + constitutions); **Honorius III** approved **1216** (further provisions 1220). On **15 August 1217** Dominic sent eleven friars from Toulouse (four Spain, seven Paris); others soon to Bologna. By **1221** five provinces (Spain, Provence, France, Lombardy, Tuscany) with projected expansion to England, Germany, Hungary, Scandinavia, Poland, Holy Land. English mission: **Gilbert de Fresney** and twelve landed Dover early August **1221**, established **Oxford** priory 15 August 1221. By **1256** about **13,000** Dominican friars. Priories attached to universities (Paris 1217, Bologna 1218, etc.); every priory a resident theologian; leading figures include **Richard Fishacre**, **Albertus Magnus**, **Thomas Aquinas**. **Gregory IX (1233)** appointed Dominicans as inquisitors in Languedoc — for Logan the instrument that, more than crusade, broke [[catharism|Catharism]]’s back by the later 1240s. Fourth Lateran’s ban on new rules was avoided by adopting Augustine (cf. Franciscans’ pre-council oral approval).
