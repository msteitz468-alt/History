# Ja'far al-Sadiq

Great-great-grandson of Ali ibn Abi Talib and Muhammad, recognized by Twelver and Ismaili Shia Islam as the sixth Imam, and one of the most influential legal and theological scholars of the early Islamic period across sectarian lines — the Ja'fari school of Islamic jurisprudence, still followed by Twelver Shia Muslims today, is named for him. He taught in Medina during the transitional decades spanning the late Umayyad and early Abbasid periods, a relatively tolerant window in which he could instruct students (reportedly including the future Sunni jurist Abu Hanifa) without direct political persecution, unlike several of his Alid relatives who led or were suspected of leading revolts. His theological legacy includes contributions to doctrines later systematized by his students and successors, including discussions of *bada'* (that God can alter apparent decrees in response to changed circumstances, reconciling foreknowledge with contingency) that became significant in later Shia theological debate.
