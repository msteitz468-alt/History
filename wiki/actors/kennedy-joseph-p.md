# Joseph P. Kennedy

**Summary page.** Joseph Patrick Kennedy (1888–1969) was a businessman, investor, and U.S. Ambassador to the United Kingdom (1938–1940), father of President John F. Kennedy and Robert F. Kennedy. Patriarch of the Kennedy political dynasty.

## Overview

Self-made millionaire through banking, film, real estate, and liquor interests (with controversial Prohibition-era allegations). Aggressive promoter of his sons' careers; installed campaign machinery for Jack's Senate and presidential runs. As Ambassador, isolationist stance drew criticism. Later incapacitated by stroke in 1961 but remained symbolic family head.

## Key Developments

- Patriarchal influence: "Failure was not an option"; individual letters to children; "aspirational" parenting; funded Senate/presidency machinery (Boston Post endorsement etc.); "Kennedys must win."
- Ambassador and business: Hollywood stock options, pre-crash stocks, post-Prohibition liquor (Dewar’s/Gordon’s); "Joe Kennedy was just too careful to be a bootlegger."
- Relationship with Jack: 1929 yacht Swanson incident (Jack age 12 jumped overboard, told to lie; "I hated him for that"); blocked Inga Arvad; shifted ambitions after Joe Jr. death 1944 ("If Jack really wanted to make a difference... go into politics"); 1961 stroke left him incapacitated ("Don’t let that be me... he took his soul. Just like Rosie").
- Family model: Rampant infidelities (Gloria Swanson, Janet DesRosiers 1948–56 with Rose accommodation for "freedom"); "Always be a supplicant, dear" (Rose); shaped Jack’s compartmentalization and tolerance expectations. Joe confessed to Jackie he "wished him pain" to build empathy.

## Significance

Dominant figure in the "Ties That Bind" and "Joe" sections of biographical treatments. His unpublished "Diplomatic Memoir" and personal papers at JFK Library are referenced sources. His model of marriage and power shaped the private dynamics explored in family-focused biographies.
