# Abu Abd Allah al-Shi'i

**Abu Abd Allah al-Shi'i** (d. 911) was the **[[ismaili-movement|Ismaili]] missionary (*[[dawa|da'i]]*)** whose
two decades of organising among the **[[berbers|Kutama Berbers]]** of Ifriqiya built the army and the movement
that overthrew the [[aghlabid-dynasty|Aghlabids]] and installed [[al-mahdi-billah|al-Mahdi]] as the first
[[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] Imam-caliph. He is the indispensable human engine of the Fatimid rise — and its
first great casualty.

## The mission among the Kutama

Originally from **Kufa** in Iraq, Abu Abd Allah trained in **Yemen** under the chief da'i **Ibn Hawshab**.
Around **893** a group of Kutama pilgrims met him in Mecca, were impressed by his learning, and invited him to
their homeland in the mountains of the lesser Kabylia (eastern Algeria). There he held *majalis al-hikma*
("sessions of wisdom"), preaching the virtues of Ali and the coming of the **[[mahdi]]**; audiences grew, many
Kutama themselves became da'is, and between 893 and 902 the community built a fortified refuge at **Ikjan** —
its ***dar al-hijra*** (abode of emigration), consciously modelled on the Prophet's hijra to Medina.

## Overthrow of the Aghlabids

From 902 the Aghlabid governors of Ifriqiya sent repeated expeditions against the movement, but it expanded
toward their capital **[[qayrawan|Qayrawan]]**. In **909** Abu Abd Allah's Kutama armies took Qayrawan,
forcing the last Aghlabid ruler to flee. Learning that the hidden Imam was under Aghlabid arrest at
**[[sijilmasa|Sijilmasa]]**, he marched the army roughly 1,000 km west, secured al-Mahdi's release, and — at
the *zuhur* outside Sijilmasa — dismounted and kissed the Imam's stirrup in tears. See
[[foundation-of-the-fatimid-caliphate-909]].

## Fall

Within two years the triumph curdled. Kutama chieftains — accustomed to mastery, and some disappointed that
al-Mahdi was not the militant *mahdi* they had expected — resented their marginalisation under the new
caliphal court; their grievances reached Abu Abd Allah and his brother **Abu'l-Abbas**, who chafed at sharing
power with former enemies. In **February 911** a conspiracy against al-Mahdi was uncovered; the two da'is were
convicted of treason and **executed**. Al-Mahdi personally led the funeral prayer — "May God have mercy on
you, Abu Abd Allah, and reward you in the next world for your earlier efforts" — and later Ismaili tradition
preserved his honour as the founder of the state.

## Counterfactual significance

Without Abu Abd Allah's twenty-year cultivation of the Kutama there is no Fatimid army and no 909; the imamate
would likely have remained a stateless underground. His execution set the dynasty's defining precedent: the
**Imam-caliph stood above the da'wa organisation** that created him. The tension he embodied — militant
missionary movement versus dynastic state — recurs across Ismaili history.
