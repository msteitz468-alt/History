# Lysander

## Overview

**Lysander** (d. 395 BCE) was the Spartan admiral who won the [[peloponnesian-war|
Peloponnesian War]] — the commander whose victory at **Aegospotami** (405) and siege of
Athens forced the city's surrender in **404**. [[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH Vol. V]] (Ch. 11,
Andrewes) presents him as "not only an able general but a ruthless politician determined to
build up a personal following in the cities accessible to him."

## Career

Of obscure origin, Lysander came to the Aegean command in **407** and at once forged the
decisive partnership with the King's son **Cyrus the Younger**, who funded the Spartan fleet
without reserve. His diplomatic skill (and Cyrus' money — pay raised to four obols a day) gave
Sparta the edge. His victory over the Athenians at **Notium** (406, in [[alcibiades|
Alcibiades']] absence) discredited his great rival. Replaced for a term by **Callicratidas**
(killed at Arginusae), Lysander was restored — the rule against a second admiralty evaded by
the title *epistoleus* — at the demand of the cities and Cyrus.

In **405** he outmanoeuvred the Athenians in the Hellespont: catching their fleet **beached
and unmanned at Aegospotami**, he destroyed it almost without a fight and executed the
Athenian prisoners. He then cleared the empire, sent every Athenian home to swell the number
the besieged city must feed, and **moored before the Piraeus** while Athens starved into
surrender. He sailed in to oversee the **demolition of the Long Walls**.

## Significance

Lysander **ended the Athenian empire** and made Sparta, briefly, the hegemon of Greece. His
method — alliance with **Persian gold** and the planting of pro-Spartan oligarchies
(*dekarchiai*) backed by garrisons — set the pattern for the resented Spartan supremacy of
the next generation, the "freedom" about which Sparta's admirers (Xenophon among them) would
be "adequately disillusioned." His personal ambition and ruthlessness make him the Spartan
counterpart to the very Athenian power he destroyed.
