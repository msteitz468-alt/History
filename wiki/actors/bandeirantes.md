# Bandeirantes

Armed expeditionary bands (*bandeiras*) from São Paulo and the Brazilian interior who, from
the late sixteenth through the early eighteenth century, penetrated deep into the South
American interior in search of indigenous slaves and, later, gold. The Paulista
bandeirantes were the principal agents of [[brazilian-indian-slavery]] in the south,
depopulating wide regions, destroying mission settlements (most notoriously the early
Guaraní [[reduccion|reductions]] of Guairá in the 1620s–30s), and pushing the effective
frontier of Portuguese America far west of the Tordesillas line.

This is the concise network summary; the bandeirantes recur in the Brazil and La Plata
chapters of [[cambridge-history-native-peoples-americas-v3-2|CHNPA III Part 2]].

## Role and Significance

- **Slave raiding** — bandeiras supplied indigenous labor to the Paulista and coastal
  Brazilian economy, accelerating the demographic collapse of interior peoples.
- **Territorial expansion** — by ranging far into Spanish-claimed territory, they shifted
  the practical boundary of Portuguese Brazil westward, a fact later ratified by treaty.
- **Antagonists of the missions** — bandeirante assaults forced the [[guarani|Guaraní]]
  reductions to relocate and to arm themselves, shaping the militarized mission province of
  Paraguay.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without the bandeiras, the Portuguese–Spanish interior frontier would likely have settled
much closer to the original line of demarcation, and many interior indigenous societies
would have faced colonial pressure decades later and on different terms.
