# The Grand Duchy of Lithuania

The **Grand Duchy of Lithuania** was the great power of the late-medieval Baltic and eastern
Europe — Europe's last **pagan** state, which grew into one of its largest, absorbing much of the
former Kievan Rus′ before uniting with Poland. [[jones-ncmh-v6-2000|NCMH VI]] (S.C. Rowell, Ch. 17;
Nancy Shields Kollmann, Ch. 19) makes it "the heart of 'Baltic Europe'."

## A Pagan Empire

By 1400 "Lithuanian rulers governed an empire which connected the Baltic world once more with the
Black Sea." Under **Gediminas** and his successors (Algirdas, Kęstutis), the Lithuanians expanded
eastward and southward over the fragmented, Mongol-dominated **[[rus-principalities|Rus′
principalities]]** — Polotsk, Smolensk, Kiev, Volhynia — becoming the chief rival of rising
**Moscow** for the inheritance of Kievan Rus′. Its ruling Baltic core remained **pagan**, while its
vast Rus′ population was **Orthodox**: a striking confessional and ethnic mosaic, in which the
Lithuanian princes ruled their Slav subjects largely through existing Rus′ institutions and law.

## The Teutonic Crusade and the Polish Union

Its paganism made Lithuania the target of the **[[teutonic-knights|Teutonic Order's]]** crusade —
the ***reysa***, the seasonal raid "to Prussia... or to Lithuania," which by the late fourteenth
century had become a fixture of the European chivalric calendar (Chaucer's Knight went "in Lettow...
and in Ruce"). To "raise a pagan prince from the font" was "a sign of highest chic."

The decisive turn came in 1385–1386. By the **Union of Krewo (1385)**, the Lithuanian grand duke
**Jogaila** married the Polish queen **Jadwiga**, accepted **Catholic baptism** (as **Władysław
II**), converted his people, and joined the two realms in a **dynastic union** — creating the vast
**Polish–Lithuanian** state that would dominate eastern Europe, dissolve the Teutonic crusade's
rationale, and crush the Order at **Tannenberg/Grunwald (1410)**.

## Significance

Lithuania's rise is one of the great untold stories of the late-medieval east: a Baltic pagan
polity that became, by absorbing Orthodox Rus′ and uniting with Catholic Poland, the largest state
in Europe and the principal counterweight both to the **Teutonic Order** on the Baltic and to
**Moscow** in the contest for the Rus′ heritage. The union of Krewo set the framework of
east-central European politics for four centuries.
