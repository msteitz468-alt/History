# Toghrul (Ong Khan / Wang Khan)

## Overview

**Toghrul** (To’oril / Torghil), known by the Chinese title **Ong Khan** (Wang Khan), ruled the powerful **Kereyid** confederacy between the Orkhon and the Tuul (Black Forest). Weatherford places him in a mid-12th-c. steppe triad: Kereyid center, Naiman west (Tayang Khan), Tatars east as Jurched vassals. Kereyid (and Naiman) Christianity via the Church of the East—wandering monks, *ger* sanctuaries, meat and alcohol compatible with nomad life—later fed European Prester John legends.

He was *anda* of [[yesugei|Yesügei]], who helped him overthrow his uncle the Gur-khan. After Yesügei’s death young Temüjin revived the bond (1178) by giving Börte’s black sable coat to Ong Khan and claiming ceremonial sonship. Ong Khan immediately agreed to raid the Merkid (own lingering feud; loot pretext), enabling the rescue of [[borte|Börte]] with [[jamuka|Jamuka]] as Left Wing. For years he played Temüjin and Jamuka against each other while generally siding with Temüjin; joint 1196 Tatar campaign with Jurched backing impressed Mongols with luxury plunder.

## Role and rupture (1203)

Weatherford: everyone knew Ong Khan was nearing the end of his career, but succession was open. In 1203 (Year of the Pig), a year after Temüjin’s Tatar victory, Temüjin requested marriage between Ong Khan’s daughter and eldest son [[jochi|Jochi]] as a public bid for favor over Jamuka. With urging from son Senggum, Ong Khan “haughtily refused”—treating Temüjin as a common upstart. Marco Polo later retold the tone (assuming Temüjin asked for himself): “I would sooner commit my daughter to the flames than give her to him as his wife.”

Regretting the refusal, Ong Khan lured Temüjin with a false wedding invitation modeled on Tatar trickery that had killed Yesügei. Temüjin learned of the plot a day’s ride out and fled east. After Baljuna and reassembly (relatives through [[hoelun|Hö’elün]] and Börte deserting the Kereyid), Temüjin’s “Lightning Advance” over a remote pass and three days’ fighting “swallowed” more than defeated Ong Khan’s army; deserters who had not harmed their former leader were accepted. Senggum died of thirst in the desert after servants abandoned him. Ong Khan, alone toward Naiman sanctuary, was killed at the border by a guard who did not believe the solitary old man was the renowned Kereyid khan (Mongol-circulated story). Mongol tradition: Naiman queen made offerings to the head on sacred white felt; Tayang, seeing the head “smile,” kicked and trampled it.

## Historiography

*Secret History* is primary; Weatherford (2004) narrates the betrayal arc at length (Ch. 3). Specialist care needed on dating Baljuna relative to the Ong Khan war. Christian Kereyid affiliations link to later Prester John discourse (Weatherford and Jackson with different weights). The Ong Khan war (1203) is the first major milestone in Weatherford’s path to [[mongol-unification-1206|1206]].
