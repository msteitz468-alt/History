## Overview

Yodogimi — known by various names including the Lady Yodo, Yodo-dono, and in post-death memorial names — was the principal consort of Toyotomi Hideyoshi and mother of Toyotomi Hideyori. She was a daughter of Azai Nagamasa and the niece of Oda Nobunaga through his sister Oichi. After Hideyoshi's death in 1598 she became the effective political authority of the Toyotomi house during Hideyori's minority, and retained that position through the Osaka campaigns of 1614–1615.

## Role in the Osaka Campaigns

Sadler characterizes Yodogimi as the real decision-maker at Osaka: "Hideyori is only a child, and it is his mother who is in command" (Hosokawa Tadaoki's assessment). Ieyasu recognized her as the critical psychological target, describing her as "imperious and ambitious" but "inclined to be narrow-minded from want of experience of the outside world as well as superstitious and emotional." He deliberately directed his diplomatic efforts against her rather than Hideyori.

During the Winter Campaign she made a notable theatrical gesture — dressing herself in armor with several ladies-in-waiting and going the rounds of the defensive posts — which Sadler says "the soldiers seem to have regarded with mixed feelings, and rather as a suggestion that they were hard up for leaders." More practically, the Tokugawa bombardment targeted her psychologically: a cannonball destroyed her tea-cabinet while she was taking morning tea, filling her ladies with panic and making her "very much inclined to pacific suggestions." A subsequent shot struck and splintered a pillar in her apartment, killing two ladies-in-waiting.

Ieyasu's diplomatic approach exploited her relationship with her sister — the lady known to Jesuit missionaries as Maria Kyogoku — sending female intermediaries (Acha-no-tsubone) to work through this channel. The Winter Campaign truce was effectively negotiated through these women rather than through Hideyori's male commanders.

She died by suicide in the burning keep at the fall of Osaka in June 1615.

## Significance

Yodogimi illustrates the structural position of high-born women in Sengoku and early Edo Japan: formally outside formal political authority but actually central to alliance networks, hostage diplomacy, and household governance of great lords. Her role as Hideyori's effective regent made her the natural target for Ieyasu's manipulation, and her susceptibility to the bombardment-and-diplomacy pressure of the Winter Campaign directly shaped the treaty that stripped Osaka's outer defenses.

Her sister, known both as Maria (Christian name given by Jesuit missionaries) and Jōkōin (Buddhist posthumous name), was the wife of Kyogoku Tadataka — one of Ieyasu's commanders. This family connection was the diplomatic channel Ieyasu exploited.
