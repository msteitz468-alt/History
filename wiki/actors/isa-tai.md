# Isa-tai

**Isa-tai** (also Isatai; name euphemized by whites as “rear end of a wolf,” “coyote droppings,” etc.—Gwynne: more accurate sexual/animal vulgarity, “wolf’s vulva” / “coyote vagina”) was a Quahadi Comanche prophet and medicine man whose **1873–74** revitalization message—promised immunity, purification, expulsion of whites, restoration of Comanche glory—helped assemble the multi-tribal coalition that attacked the hide-hunter post at [[events/second-adobe-walls-1874|Adobe Walls (27 June 1874)]]. The failure of his medicine there discredited millenarian leadership and cleared space for [[actors/parker-quanah|Quanah Parker]]’s more pragmatic wartime and reservation authority.

## Medicine and rise (winter–spring 1873–74)

About twenty-three, stocky, large head, bull neck. Claimed: miraculous healing; raising the dead; bullet immunity for self and others; “raised from his stomach a wagonload of cartridges”; four overnight ascents beyond the sun (Battey: sun-staring illusion plus slipping away); correctly predicted a comet’s vanish in five days; control of hail, lightning, thunder. Gwynne notes con-man/magician elements (appearing arrows, sleight of hand) alongside genuine following in a year of reservation misery, missing rations, horse theft, and patrol killings.

## Evangelism with Quanah and the Sun Dance

Vision: leave the white man’s road; final war. Recruited Comanches then Cheyenne, Kiowa, Arapaho. Revenge-raid tradition—both he and Quanah had lost relatives to army patrols (Hudson/Buell); Quanah long burned for father, mother, sister. Quanah’s pipe recruitment (quoted via Gwynne): “That time I pretty big man… If he smoke he go on war path… God kill him, he afraid.”

**May 1874:** Isa-tai did “something that no Comanche leader in history had ever done”—sent runners to all bands summoning them to a **Sun Dance** (not a Comanche tradition; no prior whole-tribe council; no single paraibo with such power). Gathered on the Red River west of the reservation (near present Texola): simplified practical dance, whiskey, feasting. Roughly half the tribe agreed to follow; Penatekas, most Nokonis (Horse Back), and many Yamparikas left under threat (kill horses / personal violence). Elders overruled young men’s plan to hit Texas settlements first → **first target: buffalo hunters at Adobe Walls.** Gwynne analogizes the concept to [[actors/buffalo-hump|Buffalo Hump]]’s 1840 vision expedition (Linnville / Plum Creek)—the tribe’s “second large-scale revenge raid against the white man.”

## Adobe Walls failure and discredit

**27 June 1874** (Gwynne’s closing date; one internal slip mentions May 26—prefer 27 June): ~250 warriors mainly Comanche/Cheyenne (few Kiowa; 22 Arapaho under Yellow Horse) under Quanah. Isa-tai rode naked, sage-stem cap, painted yellow for invulnerability (horse painted too). Plan: club ~28 sleeping men and one woman. Hunters were largely awake (Hanrahan’s fake ridgepole crack); sod walls would not burn; Big Fifty Sharps and thousands of rounds turned the approach into long-range slaughter. ~15 Indians killed, many more wounded; 3 whites early. Quanah wounded by ricochet; his horse shot at ~500 yards. **Isa-tai’s medicine failed:** Cheyenne whipped him; a neighbor’s horse was shot beside him; excuse that Cheyennes had skinned a skunk the day before. Whites later beheaded dead Indians and stuck heads on stakes.

Effects Gwynne stresses: shocking failure of prophetic immunity; wounding of Quanah; collapse of the multi-tribal millenarian project into the wider [[events/red-river-war-1874|Red River War]] under army converging columns.

## After the war

Still present and influential (without chiefly office) when Sturm found Quanah’s holdout camp in May 1875—“big heart… never gets mad”; left a pictograph for thirty men out hunting when the band started for Fort Sill. On reservation: later a rival of Quanah (antilease politics; protection rackets of his own; fistfight contexts with Quanah/Lone Wolf in Gwynne’s late chapters). Lived as living reminder that revitalization war without logistics against industrial firepower ended in discredit.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Isa-tai’s Sun Dance coalition, Adobe Walls might not have concentrated multi-tribal force—or might have hit settlements instead of hide men—but army offensive and buffalo slaughter were already structural. His failure mattered more as **negative precedent**: Quanah blocked Ghost Dance among Comanches/Kiowas partly from Adobe Walls/Isa-tai experience and channeled religious energy into peyote rather than another millenarian rising.

## Historiography

Known almost entirely through enemy and later ethnographic/reportorial filters (Battey; army; Gwynne’s synthesis). Name translations are colonial bowdlerizations of a sexual insult-name. Do not reduce him to “con man” alone—his following was real in 1874 crisis—or to pure prophet without noting sleight-of-hand claims in the same sources. Adobe Walls date: prefer 27 June 1874.
