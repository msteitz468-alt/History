# Thomas Müntzer (c. 1489–1525)

Radical Reformer, preacher, and leader in the German Peasants' War whose apocalyptic theology and calls for the "elect" to wield the sword against the godless influenced later sectarian holy war experiments (notably Münster).

## Role

Müntzer combined mystical, apocalyptic, and social-revolutionary ideas. He argued that the true Christians (the Elect) had a divine mandate to overthrow the ungodly powers. Captured after the Battle of Frankenhausen, he was executed. His movement provided a bridge between earlier Taborite-style radicalism and the 1534 Münster episode.

## Historiography (Housley context)

Housley references Müntzer in the context of sectarian apocalypticism as one driver of religious warfare's most extreme forms. Distinguishes him from the more stable (if still radical) evolution at Tábor.
