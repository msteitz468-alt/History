# Einsatzgruppen

The **Einsatzgruppen** ("task forces") were mobile killing units of the SS Security Police and SD, formed under [[heydrich-reinhard|Reinhard Heydrich]]'s RSHA. First deployed in the [[invasion-of-poland-1939|Polish campaign of 1939]] to murder the Polish intelligentsia, they were reorganized into four groups (A–D) for [[operation-barbarossa-1941|Operation Barbarossa]] in 1941, where — with Order Police battalions, Waffen-SS units, and local auxiliaries — they shot well over a million Jews, along with Communist officials, Roma, and psychiatric patients, in the occupied Soviet Union. Their escalation from shooting Jewish men to entire communities in summer–autumn 1941 marks the opening phase of [[the-holocaust|the Holocaust]]. Documented for the wiki from [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008|Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)]].

## Role and Significance

### Evans (2008): the killing operations of 1941–1942

Evans's *The Third Reich at War* reconstructs the escalation in the occupied Soviet territories in detail. From the first days of [[operation-barbarossa-1941|Barbarossa]], the four Task Forces (Einsatzgruppen A–D) followed the armies with Heydrich's orders to kill civilian resisters, Communist officials, and Jews. Heydrich also instructed commanders to incite deniable "self-cleansing" pogroms by local populations: Stahlecker (Task Force A) reported it was "important 'to create as firmly grounded and provable for posterity the fact that the liberated population took the toughest measures against the Bolshevik and Jewish enemy on its own initiative, without any direction from the German end being recognizable.'" In Kovno on 25–27 June 1941 Lithuanian nationalists killed some 3,800 Jews, including victims publicly beaten to death with clubs before cheering crowds — witnessed and reported by Wehrmacht Lieutenant-Colonel von Bischoffshausen, whose general refused to intervene. But Evans stresses the pogrom strategy largely failed: "only a tiny minority" of locals participated, and Task Force C concluded that "a decided antisemitism on a racial or spiritual basis is . . . alien to the population." The units then did the killing themselves, aided by auxiliary police.

**The escalation to total genocide (July–August 1941).** Initially the units shot Jewish men of military age under security pretexts (Garsden, 24 June 1941: 200 men and one woman shot by Tilsit border police; Himmler and Heydrich visited and approved on 30 June). After Hitler's 16 July declaration that partisan war "gives us the possibility of exterminating anything opposing us" and his 17 July decrees giving [[himmler-heinrich|Himmler]] control of security in the East, Himmler reinforced the theater with two SS cavalry brigades (~13,000 men) and, in repeated verbal orders during July–August, extended the killing to women and children. His early-August order — "all Jewish men must be shot. Drive Jewish women into the marshes" — produced the notorious Pripet marshes report of 12 August 1941 that the marshes "were not deep enough for them to sink in"; SS officers concluded the women must be shot too. Jeckeln's brigade killed 23,600 men, women, and children at Kamenets-Podolsk in three days in late August; at **Babi Yar** outside Kiev on 29–30 September 1941, Task Force C units and Ukrainian police shot 33,771 Jews (the figure from the unit's own report), the victims made to lie face-down on the bodies of those already killed. Evans records the shooter Kurt Werner's testimony verbatim and notes the massacre of children at Byelaya Tserkov (19–22 August 1941), ordered by Field Marshal von Reichenau over the protest of staff officer Helmuth Groscurth. Officers who refused to participate (e.g., Erwin Schulz, Task Unit 5) were transferred without career damage; "the great majority took part willingly," motives mixing antisemitism, conformity, and greed — victims' property was looted everywhere.

**Scale and documentation.** By their own running reports: Task Force A ~230,000 Jews killed by end of January 1942; B 91,000 by late February; C ~75,000 by 20 October 1941; D ~92,000 by 8 April 1942. Evans judges that around **half a million Jews had been shot by the Task Forces and associated military and paramilitary groups by the end of 1941**, and ~1.3 million by war's end including police units and allied militias. The reports circulated widely: from 1 August 1941 Gestapo chief Müller ordered them forwarded to Hitler; "Event Report 128" went out in fifty-five copies, countersigned by twenty-two Foreign Office officials — Evans's point being that knowledge of the massacres saturated the senior Party and state administration. Himmler's "Report No. 51" (29 December 1942), read by Hitler, recorded 363,211 "Jews executed" in southern Russia, Ukraine, and Bialystok in August–November 1942 alone, under the heading "the fight against bandits."

**Command structure.** Evans's analytic conclusion: "There was no one specific, precise order; Hitler set the overall parameters of action, Himmler interpreted them, and the SS officers on the ground, with his encouragement, used their initiative" — the uneven local timing of the transition to killing women and children shows the latitude, while Himmler's inspection tours and reinforcements show the central drive. The psychological strain of face-to-face shooting (suicides, alcoholism among the men, per Höss's recollection) was itself an argument used for the transition to gassing — gas vans were delivered to all four Task Forces by the end of 1941.

Evans also treats the parallel and partly independent Romanian killings under Antonescu (Iaşi pogrom June 1941; Odessa October 1941, where 19,000 Jews were machine-gunned in harbor sheds which were then set on fire; the Bogdanovka–Domanovka–Akmecetka camp massacres, December 1941, with ~43,000 shot and thousands burned or deliberately starved): 280,000–380,000 Jews killed by Romanian forces, "the largest number murdered by any independent European country during the Second World War apart from Germany." Task Force D under Ohlendorf worked alongside and criticized the Romanians' killings as insufficiently systematic. In Serbia, army reprisal policy under General Böhme (100 hostages shot per German dead, Jewish men included wholesale) crossed, in Evans's phrase, "the line that separated military reprisals, however excessive, from gratuitous mass murder" — over 9,000 Jews, Roma, and civilians shot by army units in two weeks of October 1941; by August 1942 the SS declared Serbia the first country where the "Jewish question" was "solved," the women and children having been gassed in a van shuttling through Belgrade to Avala by May 1942.

## Historiography

Evans (2008) synthesizes the post-1990s archival consensus (Soviet-bloc archives) on the Einsatzgruppen and embeds them in the functionalist-leaning "process, not single decision" account of the genesis of [[the-holocaust|the Final Solution]] — see the decision-debate summary on [[wannsee-conference-1942]]. The units' own Event Reports (Ereignismeldungen) are the core primary source; Evans notes their figures may include exaggeration or double-counting but exclude many army and militia killings, so the aggregate estimate stands.
