# Brasidas

## Overview

**Brasidas** (d. 422 BCE) was the ablest and most enterprising Spartan commander of the
[[archidamian-war|Archidamian War]] — the man who carried the war into the **Thraceward
region** of the [[delian-league|Athenian empire]] and stripped Athens of **Amphipolis**.
[[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH Vol. V]] (Ch. 9, Lewis) presents him as the exception to Spartan
"sloth" — "the first person in the war to be praised in Sparta" — whose energy outran anything
"dreamed of or thought desirable" by the cautious "first men" at home.

## Early roles (from Kagan Range 9001-13000)

- At Corcyra (427): served as xymboulos (adviser) to navarch Alcidas with Peloponnesian fleet; “The next day Brasidas urged Alcidas to attack the city while the Corcyraeans were confused and frightened, but the timid navarch refused, and the delay was fatal: news that an Athenian fleet of sixty ships under Eurymedon son of Thucles was on its way.”
- At Pylos/Sphacteria (425): “conspicuous bravery of Brasidas, who was overcome by his wounds” during the fighting on the island.

## The Thracian campaign

After early exploits (the defence of **Methone** in 431, the relief of **Megara** in 424),
Brasidas led a small army — **700 liberated helots** and 1,000 Peloponnesian mercenaries —
on a daring **long march by land** to Thrace, a route Athens had thought impossible. There he
exploited the one part of the empire "not protected by sea." His method was **persuasion as
much as force**: proclaiming Sparta the **liberator of Greece** and pledging autonomy, he won
over **Acanthus**, **Stagira**, and others by their own vote, and then, in a winter night
march, forced the Strymon bridge to take the great prize of **Amphipolis** (424) — whose
failed Athenian defender was the historian **[[thucydides]]**, exiled in consequence. Revolt
"became fashionable"; Torone and Scione followed.

**Megara relief (424)**: While gathering forces near Corinth for the northern expedition, Brasidas heard of the Athenian assault on Megara. He sent to Boeotia for reinforcements and led 300 troops to the city. The Megarians refused entry at first due to internal factions fearing civil war or oligarchic restoration. After the Athenians withdrew behind Nisaea's walls, Brasidas was admitted. The democratic plotters fled; oligarchic exiles returned and established a narrow regime. "Henceforth Megara was a loyal ally of Sparta and an even more bitter enemy of Athens."

**Capture of Amphipolis (early December 424)**: Brasidas marched through a dark and snowy night to Argilus (which immediately rebelled). He seized the critical bridge over the Strymon by surprise, capturing the land outside the walls and many prisoners. Rather than storm the walled city immediately (risking heavy casualties with a small force), he relied on treachery and offered generous terms: residents could stay with equal rights and property or leave freely within five days. "Compared to what they had feared the proclamation seemed just." The city surrendered before Thucydides arrived from Thasos with seven ships; Thucydides saved Eion but was too late for Amphipolis and was later exiled.

**Battle of Amphipolis (422)**: Brasidas anticipated Cleon's move and positioned his main force with Clearidas at the Thracian (northern) gate while he took 150 elite men to the southern gate. He used a decoy sacrifice and northern deployment to lure Cleon into beginning a withdrawal southward. When the Athenian left wing advanced and the right wing's complicated maneuver created disorder and exposed the unshielded side, Brasidas "burst from the southern gates on the run and struck the Athenian center." Clearidas then attacked the flank. About 600 Athenians killed (including Cleon); only seven Spartans, among them Brasidas mortally wounded. "He lived long enough to learn he had won his final battle." The Amphipolitans later buried him inside the city facing the agora, adopted him as founder, and held annual athletic contests and sacrifices in his honor.

## Death and legacy

Brasidas' successes embarrassed the Spartan peace party, which arranged a one-year armistice
(423) he partly ignored. In **422** the Athenian **[[cleon]]** came north to recover the
losses; outside **Amphipolis**, Brasidas caught Cleon's army in a sudden sally and routed it
— but was **mortally wounded** in the moment of victory. The Amphipolitans buried him with
**heroic honours** as the city's new founder. The **simultaneous deaths of Brasidas and
Cleon** removed the leading war-advocates on both sides and made the **Peace of Nicias**
(421) irresistible.

## Significance

(Hanson Ch.5, range ~638-646): Brasidas (423 Illyria) stressed Greek "regular order" vs barbarian "mobs" — hoplite interdependence as dividend of Hellenic civilization. At Amphipolis (422) not regular phalanx battle (parataxis) but confused sally/rout; Cleon + Brasidas both killed, enabling Peace of Nicias. (Grounded range lines ~1211-1223.)

Brasidas demonstrated that the Athenian empire was **vulnerable by land** and that Sparta's
"liberation of Greece" could be made a real instrument of war. His campaign was the most
imaginative Peloponnesian initiative of the Archidamian War, and his death — like Cleon's —
was "universally treated as symbolic." Thucydides, his defeated opponent, gives him an
unusually admiring portrait.
