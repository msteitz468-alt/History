# Louis the German (r. 843–876)

## Overview

**Louis the German** (*Ludovicus Germanicus*; c. 806–876), a younger son of [[louis-the-pious]],
became the first king of **East Francia** — the political ancestor of the medieval German kingdom — at
the [[treaty-of-verdun-843]]. For more than three decades he ruled the German-speaking lands east of the
Rhine, integrating Bavaria, Alemannia, Saxony, and Thuringia and managing a long, turbulent Slavic and
Moravian frontier. In [[mckitterick-ncmh-v2-1995|NCMH II]] (Fried, Ch. 5) he is the central figure of the
East and Middle kingdoms.

## From Verdun to the Eastern Kingdom

Allied with his half-brother [[charles-the-bald]] against [[lothar-i|Lothar]], Louis fought at **Fontenoy**
(841) and swore the **Strasbourg Oaths** with Charles in 842 — the oath sworn by Louis's followers in Old
High German, by Charles's in early Romance, a famous marker of the linguistic divergence of the future
Germany and France. At **Verdun** (843) he received the lands east of the Rhine plus the bridgehead cities
of Mainz, Speyer, and Worms. He repeatedly intervened westward — most boldly invading West Francia in 858
at the invitation of disaffected magnates, a bid that failed — but his lasting work was the consolidation
of a coherent East Frankish realm.

## The Eastern Frontier

Louis's kingdom faced outward onto the Slavic world: the Sorbs, the Bohemians, and above all
**Great Moravia** under Rastislav and Svatopluk, which he sought repeatedly to subdue and which became the
arena of the Cyrillo-Methodian mission (see slavs and bulgars). He governed the marches through
frontier commanders and used sub-kingdoms to position his sons, notably Karlmann on the Bavarian/Carinthian
frontier.

## Succession and Significance

At his death (876) Louis divided East Francia among his three sons (Karlmann, Louis the Younger, Charles the
Fat). Charles the Fat would briefly reunite almost the whole Carolingian empire (885) before his deposition
in 887 by Karlmann's illegitimate son **Arnulf of Carinthia** — after which the empire fragmented for good
(888). Louis the German's enduring achievement was to give the East Frankish/German kingdom its territorial
and dynastic foundation.

## Historiography

NCMH II (Fried) reframes Louis away from the older "founder of Germany" teleology, stressing that East
Francia in his lifetime was a Frankish kingdom, not yet a German nation. The eastern sources (the *Annals of
Fulda*) are the counterpart to the western *Annals of St-Bertin*, and the contrast between the two annal
traditions is itself evidence of the political and cultural separation underway after 843.
