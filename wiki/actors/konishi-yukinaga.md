# Konishi Yukinaga

Japanese daimyo and Christian convert (baptized Augustin/Dom Agostinho, c. 1555–1600), one of the principal commanders of the Japanese invasions of Korea (1592–1598) under Toyotomi Hideyoshi, where he led the initial landing force and later conducted much of the diplomatic negotiation with Ming China and Korea — often working at cross-purposes with his rival commander Kato Kiyomasa.

Following Hideyoshi's death, Konishi allied with Ishida Mitsunari's Western Army against Tokugawa Ieyasu. At the Battle of Sekigahara (21 October 1600), he held the center of the Western line alongside Ukita Hideie; when the Western Army collapsed after Kobayakawa Hideaki's defection, Konishi's forces broke and he fled. As a Christian, he refused to commit ritual suicide (seppuku) and was instead captured, then publicly executed alongside Ishida Mitsunari and Ankokuji Ekei in Kyoto.

## Significance

Konishi's career and death illustrate both the prominence of Christian daimyo in late 16th-century Japan and the decisive, winner-take-all consequences of choosing the losing side at Sekigahara, which established Tokugawa hegemony for the next two and a half centuries.
