# Martin Eberhard

American engineer and entrepreneur who co-founded Tesla Motors in July 2003 with Marc Tarpenning, serving as the company's founding CEO. Eberhard, who had previously founded the e-book reader company NuvoMedia, drove Tesla's initial engineering direction and its founding thesis — that a premium, high-performance electric sports car (the Tesla Roadster, based on a Lotus Elise chassis) could establish electric vehicles as desirable rather than merely dutiful. Elon Musk joined as chairman and lead investor in Tesla's 2004 Series A round. As Roadster development ran over budget and behind schedule, Eberhard was removed from day-to-day operational leadership in 2007 and left the company in 2008; he subsequently had a public dispute — including a lawsuit, later settled — with Musk over founder credit and characterization of his departure, with Musk asserting his own claim to co-founder status alongside Eberhard, Tarpenning, and others.

## Counterfactual Significance

Eberhard's founding engineering choices (a lithium-ion battery pack rather than lead-acid, a sports-car halo product rather than an economy vehicle) set Tesla's technical trajectory before Musk's later chairmanship and eventual CEO role reshaped the company's public identity and capital strategy.

## Historiography

Popular business-press narratives (and Musk's own public statements) have tended to center Musk as Tesla's founder; more detailed corporate histories (e.g., journalistic accounts of the founder dispute) restore Eberhard and Tarpenning's originating role.
