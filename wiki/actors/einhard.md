# Einhard (c. 770–840)

**Einhard** was a Frankish courtier, scholar, and lay abbot, raised at Fulda and brought
into [[charlemagne|Charles the Great]]'s palace school at Aachen, where he became a trusted
agent (he carried the [[hubs/biographies/early-middle-ages/charlemagne|Divisio Regnorum of 806]]
to Rome for the pope's subscription). He is best known as Charles's biographer: the author
of the ***Vita Karoli Magni***, the most influential life of the emperor and a foundational
text of the [[carolingian-renaissance]].

## The Vita Karoli and Its Biases

Per [[sources/nelson-king-and-emperor-2019|Janet Nelson]], the *Vita Karoli* must be read
critically rather than taken as a transparent record:

- **Date and motive.** Einhard wrote it "early 829," and Nelson argues his purpose was
  political: he "decided … to recover some of his old influence" after the 828 crisis at
  [[louis-the-pious|Louis the Pious]]'s court — not from disinterested piety.
- **Suetonian model.** The *Vita* is structurally indebted to Suetonius's *Lives of the
  Caesars*. Einhard's claim that Charles died "in the seventy-second year of his age" was a
  fabricated parallel to Suetonius's Augustus — the true figure was "not quite sixty-six"
  (Charles born 2 April 748, not 742).
- **The coronation topos.** Einhard's celebrated claim that Charles "would never have
  entered the church that day … if he had known the pope's plan beforehand" is, Nelson
  argues, a literary topos of humility; the [[coronation-of-charlemagne-800|coronation of 800]]
  was in fact carefully co-planned.

Nelson nonetheless defends the *Vita* as usable: "all biographies are authorial constructs …
both [Einhard and Notker] transmit, beneath liberal sprinklings of ninth-century spice,
nutritious grains of historicity."
