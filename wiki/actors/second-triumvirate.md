# The Second Triumvirate (43 BCE)

The legally-constituted three-man dictatorship — **Octavian**, **Mark Antony**, and
**Lepidus** — formed to avenge Caesar and crush the Republic, which it did through
the **proscriptions** and (at Philippi) the destruction of the "liberators." Unlike
the [[first-triumvirate|first]], a **formal magistracy**: *triumviri rei publicae
constituendae* ("to reorganize the state"). Treated in [[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]]
Ch. 12 (Rawson); its career proper belongs to CAH X.

## The road to the triumvirate (44–43)

After the **[[julius-caesar-assassination-44bce|Ides]]**, the contest for Caesar's
inheritance pitted **Antony** (the surviving consul, controlling Caesar's papers and
treasure) against the **nineteen-year-old Octavian** (Caesar's adopted heir), who
won over the **veterans and two Macedonian legions** with lavish bounties. The Senate,
guided by **[[cicero|Cicero's]] Philippics**, tried to use Octavian against Antony,
granting him *imperium*; at the **war of Mutina (43)** Antony was defeated and
declared a public enemy — but **both consuls (Hirtius and Pansa) died**, leaving
Octavian in command of their armies.

Octavian then turned on his sponsors: refused the consulship, he **marched on Rome
(a second crossing of the Rubicon)**, took it, and was elected consul at nineteen.
Meanwhile **Lepidus** and the western governors' armies went over to Antony. The three
Caesarians met near Bononia and, in November **43**, had the **Lex Titia** make them
**triumvirs for five years**, dividing the West among them (Octavian the junior
partner: Africa, Sicily, Sardinia).

## The proscriptions

Desperate for money and land for their troops, the triumvirs revived **Sulla's
device**: an edict declaring Caesar's *clemency* a failure and listing the proscribed —
reportedly **300 senators and 2,000 equites**, their property confiscated and rewards
paid to killers. The most famous victim was **[[cicero]]**, listed by Antony (his
*Philippics'* target) and killed at Caieta on **7 December 43** — "he died bravely."
Eighteen of Italy's richest towns were marked for veteran settlement, throwing Italy
into turmoil for years.

## Significance

The Second Triumvirate completed what Caesar's murder had failed to prevent: the
**armed liquidation of the Republican governing class** and the open rule of military
dynasts. Rawson's theme is "Caesar's ghost" — it was loyalty to the dead dictator, his
precedent of vast donatives, and the wealth he had amassed that drove the dynasts and
their veterans to overturn the Senate. The triumvirs' war against **Brutus and
Cassius** ended at **[[battle-of-philippi-42bce|Philippi]] (42)**, after which the
**East** fell to [[mark-antony|Antony]] and **Italy and the West** to
[[augustus|Octavian]]. Their partnership frayed through the 30s (the Perusine War 41–40;
the pacts of Brundisium, Misenum, and Tarentum), and the **elimination of Sextus
Pompeius and the deposition of Lepidus (36)** left only two. The final rivalry ran to
**[[battle-of-actium-31bce|Actium]] (31)** and the
**[[death-of-antony-and-cleopatra-30bce|fall of Alexandria]] (30)**, leaving Octavian
sole master — the founder of the Principate as **[[augustus|Augustus]]** (CAH X).
