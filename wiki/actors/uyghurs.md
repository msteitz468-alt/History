## Overview

The Uyghurs are a Turkic Muslim ethnic group of the Tarim Basin and greater Xinjiang
in western China, historically and religiously distinct from the Han-descended,
Sinophone [[islam-in-china|Hui]] Muslim population of China's interior provinces (see
[[islam-in-china]]). Islamization of the Tarim Basin's Turkic population proceeded
via the [[qarakhanid-khanate]] from the tenth century onward, and the Uyghur
population has remained overwhelmingly Muslim for centuries since. The distinction
between "Hui" (Chinese Muslims of the inner provinces) and the Turkic Muslims of the
Tarim Basin sharpened under Qing-era territorial consolidation of the region in the
eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

## Role

Because their Muslim identity is bound up with a distinct Turkic ethnicity and
homeland rather than assimilation into Han Chinese cultural forms (in contrast to
the Hui), Uyghurs have historically occupied an ambiguous position within the
Chinese state — simultaneously governed as imperial/state subjects and treated as
ethnically and religiously "other." In the twentieth and twenty-first centuries this
tension has escalated sharply. Formichi documents halal food practice in Xinjiang
becoming an explicit site of ethnic contestation — halal-labeled establishments
serving the Uyghur population, distinct from establishments serving pork and alcohol
to Han Chinese migrants — even as the Chinese state has simultaneously pursued
integration into the global halal export market (projected at US$280 billion by
2030) while banning halal advertising within Xinjiang itself, explicitly to curb
what state discourse frames as Uyghur "Islamization" and "radicalization." Formichi
states plainly that contemporary PRC national politics "has dictated the
segregation, suppression, and incarceration of Western China's Muslims" — a direct
reference to the mass internment and surveillance apparatus built in Xinjiang in the
2010s–2020s. Chinese social media has also hosted an organized "halalification"
(*qing zhen fa hua*) backlash movement calling for boycotts of halal products and
delivery services, reflecting broader popular hostility toward visible Muslim
identity markers.

## Key Events and Processes

- Islamization of the Tarim Basin under the Qarakhanids, from the 10th century
- Qing-era sharpening of the Hui/Turkic-Muslim distinction, 18th–19th centuries
- Contemporary Chinese state segregation, suppression, and mass incarceration of
  Uyghur Muslims in Xinjiang (21st century) — a policy the wiki records at full
  fidelity per its collection-coverage and controversy protocols; no dedicated event

## Counterfactual Significance

The Uyghur case is a live instance of a recurring wiki theme — Islam surviving as a
minority identity under a non-Muslim state — but distinguished from most other cases
in the wiki by the scale and severity of contemporary state suppression, which
Formichi treats as ongoing rather than historical.

## Historiography

Formichi's account (2020) is necessarily contemporary and drawn from recent
reporting and scholarship on PRC ethnic and religious policy; she treats the
"segregation, suppression, and incarceration" of Xinjiang's Muslims as an
established fact of national politics rather than a contested claim, while also
tracing the deeper history (Qarakhanid conversion, Qing consolidation, Republican
and Communist-era administration) that produced the Hui/Uyghur distinction now at
the center of that repression. The wiki records this material without softening per
the atrocity/persecution-documentation standard in its ingest protocol.
