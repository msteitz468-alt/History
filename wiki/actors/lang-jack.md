# Jack Lang

Premier of New South Wales (1925–1927, 1930–1932) and the most radical Labor
leader of the Depression years.

## Overview

During the [[australian-1930s-depression|Depression]] Lang rejected the
deflationary orthodoxy of the [[scullin-james|Scullin]] federal government and
the Premiers' Plan, proposing instead that Australia default on or defer interest
payments to British bondholders (the "Lang Plan"). His confrontation with the
Commonwealth and with the bondholders precipitated a constitutional crisis: in
May 1932 the NSW Governor, Sir Philip Game, dismissed Lang's government for
directing officials to defy Commonwealth law — a notable precedent later invoked
in discussions of the 1975 [[dismissal-1975|Dismissal]].

## Significance

"Langism" split the Labor movement and became a lasting symbol of radical
populist resistance to financial orthodoxy. Lang's dismissal is one of only two
occasions a Crown representative has removed an elected Australian government.
