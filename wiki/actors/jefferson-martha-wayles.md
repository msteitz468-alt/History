# Martha Wayles Skelton Jefferson

Martha Wayles Skelton Jefferson (1748–1782) was the wife of Thomas Jefferson and the mistress of Monticello. Daughter of John Wayles, a prominent Virginia lawyer, planter, and slave trader, and Martha Eppes. Widowed young after a brief first marriage to Bathurst Skelton (one son who died in infancy). Married Thomas Jefferson on New Year's Day, 1772. The marriage brought Jefferson substantial additional land and slaves through the Wayles inheritance (1773–74), including the notorious Poplar Forest tract and the human property that complicated Jefferson's relationship to slavery for the rest of his life.

Contemporary descriptions portray her as small, graceful, with auburn hair and a beautiful singing voice; she played the harpsichord and was an accomplished hostess. The couple had six children together; only daughters Martha ("Patsy") Randolph and Mary ("Maria") Eppes survived to adulthood. Jefferson described the marriage as one of deep happiness and intellectual companionship.

Her health deteriorated after the births and during the stresses of the Revolution and Jefferson's governorship. She died at Monticello on September 6, 1782, at age 33, after the birth of their last child. Jefferson was devastated; he never remarried and long afterward could not bear to hear her name mentioned. He destroyed most of their correspondence. Her death marked a permanent emotional divide in his life.
