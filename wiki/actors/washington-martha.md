# Martha Washington (1731–1802)

Born Martha Dandridge; married Daniel Parke Custis (d. 1757), inheriting one of the largest fortunes in Virginia (including ~17,000 acres and hundreds of enslaved people); widowed with two surviving children (Jacky and Patsy). Married **[[washington-george|George Washington]]** in January 1759 at her estate; the union brought him wealth, social elevation, and management of the enlarged Mount Vernon holdings. 

During the Revolutionary War she spent winters at camp with her husband (Valley Forge, Morristown, etc.), boosting morale and providing domestic stability for the commander and his officers. As First Lady (1789–1797) she presided over the presidential households in New York and Philadelphia with dignity and hospitality, establishing precedents for the role while enduring the strains of public life and separation from Mount Vernon.

## Character and Role

A capable plantation manager during her husband's long absences; pragmatic, devout, and reserved. Their marriage combined practical partnership with genuine affection. She supported Washington's decision to free his slaves in his will (effective after her death; she chose to free them in 1800/1801). Chernow emphasizes her as essential ballast to Washington's public image and private equilibrium.
