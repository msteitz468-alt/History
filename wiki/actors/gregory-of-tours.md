# Gregory of Tours

**Gregory of Tours** (Georgius Florentius Gregorius) was the Bishop of Tours (r. 573–594 CE) and the preeminent historian of Merovingian Gaul. His primary work, the ***Decem Libri Historiarum*** (Ten Books of Histories, commonly known as the *History of the Franks*), is the single most important narrative source for the transition from Roman Gaul to the Merovingian successor kingdoms.

---

## Life and Office

Gregory was born in Clermont-Ferrand to a wealthy, distinguished Gallo-Roman senatorial family. Nearly all of his ancestors on both sides had been bishops, including his great-uncle Nicetius of Lyon and his uncle Gallus of Clermont. 

In 573, Gregory was elected Bishop of Tours, succeeding his cousin Eufronius. As bishop of Tours, Gregory was the guardian of the shrine of **St. Martin of Tours**, the most popular pilgrimage site and political sanctuary in Merovingian Gaul. This office placed him at the center of Merovingian dynastic struggles, particularly in his conflicts with King **Chilperic I** (whom Gregory dubbed "the Nero and Herod of our time").

---

## Works and Historiographical Method

Gregory’s output was large, consisting of historical and hagiographical writings:
*   ***Decem Libri Historiarum* (Ten Books of Histories):** A universal history starting from the creation of the world, focusing on the history of Gaul from the conversion of Clovis (c. 508) to Gregory's own time. 
*   **Hagiographies:** Wrote extensively on the miracles of St. Martin (*De Virtutibus Sancti Martini*) and other saints, promoting the power of holy relics as local sources of healing and protection.

### Theological and Moral Framing
In *The New Cambridge Medieval History, Vol. 1* (Ch. 8), **Raymond Van Dam** analyzes Gregory's historiographical approach:
1.  **Theological Determinism:** Gregory did not write modern, objective political history. He framed all historical events within a strict providential and moral schema. The success of a Merovingian king was determined not by military or economic strategy, but by their piety, their support of the Catholic Church, and their respect for the sanctuary and relics of St. Martin.
2.  **Episcopal Bias:** Gregory wrote from the perspective of the Gallo-Roman senatorial episcopate. He championed the authority of bishops as the true defenders of cities and the moral guides of the Frankish kings.
3.  **Constructed "Franks":** Gregory was central to constructing the narrative of a unified "Frankish" identity centered on the orthodox conversion of **Clovis**. He contrasted this with the "heretical" Arian Visigoths and Burgundians, justifying Frankish territorial expansion as a holy war.
