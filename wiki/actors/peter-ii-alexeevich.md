# Tsar Peter II

## Biography / Description
**Tsar Peter II** (Peter Alexeevich) was the grandson of Peter the Great and the son of Tsarevich Alexis (who died in prison in 1718) (L3032, L3122). Born in 1715, he was the last direct male member of the Romanov dynasty (L3132). He ascended the throne in May 1727 at the age of eleven, following the death of Empress Catherine I, and died of smallpox in January 1730 before reaching his fifteenth birthday (L3126, L3132).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Overthrow of Menshikov (1727):** Upon his accession, the young emperor fell under the control of Alexander Menshikov, who moved Peter to his residence and betrothed him to his daughter (L3126). Resenting Menshikov's dominance, Peter II allied with young Prince Ivan Dolgoruky (L3126). The Dolgoruky family arrested Menshikov in 1727, exiling the favorite to northern Siberia and replacing him in government (L3126-3128).
* **Dolgoruky Dominance:** The Dolgoruky family secured two seats on the Supreme Secret Council and arranged Peter II's betrothal to a Princess Dolgorukaia in late 1729, though the marriage was aborted due to his sudden death (L3128-3132).
* **Peasant Conscription Restrictions:** During his reign, serfs were prohibited from volunteering for military service to escape their bondage, reflecting the growing power of the gentry over the peasantry (L3244).

## Historical Significance
Peter II's reign was characterized by court intrigue and a temporary aristocratic reaction against the Petrine reforms, symbolized by the exile of Menshikov (Peter the Great's closest collaborator) and the rise of the old Dolgoruky family (L3126-3128). His death in 1730 without a designated heir or natural successor brought the male line of the Romanov dynasty to an end, triggering a major constitutional crisis resolved by the accession of Empress Anne (L3132-3134).
