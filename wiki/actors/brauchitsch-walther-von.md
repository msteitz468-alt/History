# Walther von Brauchitsch

German field marshal; Commander-in-Chief of the Army (OKH) 1938-1941. Oversaw planning and early execution of Operation Barbarossa. Clashed repeatedly with Hitler over strategy (Moscow priority vs. economic flanks) and was dismissed after the failure before Moscow in late 1941. Replaced by Hitler himself as head of the army.

## Role in Barbarossa

As head of the army high command, worked with Halder on operational plans. Advocated for Moscow as the decisive objective. Unable to persuade Hitler, who diverted forces to Kiev and other axes. "Neither Brauchitsch nor Halder could persuade him [Hitler]. Nor could Fedor von Bock..."

## Dismissal and Aftermath

After the Soviet counteroffensive of December 1941 and German setbacks, Hitler dismissed several generals, including Brauchitsch. "In the aftermath, Hitler dismissed a number of his generals, including Brauchitsch. He now took command of the army personally."

## Historiography

Portrayed as a professional soldier caught between traditional General Staff thinking and Hitler's ideological/strategic interference. Sources from West Point Vol. 1 (Megargee chapter) and Murray-Millett highlight the command friction that contributed to German overextension and ultimate failure in the East. Limited independent agency once Hitler assumed direct control.
