# Shilhak-Inshushinak

The conquering builder-king of the **Shutrukid dynasty** (~1150–1120 BCE), "chosen
brother" and successor of Kutir-Nahhunte by [[elam|Elam's]] characteristic **fraternal
succession**. He left the **largest corpus of royal building inscriptions** of any
Elamite king and pushed Elamite arms to their greatest western extent before the dynasty's
fall.

## Significance

He **restored temples in at least twenty cities** across the realm — from
**[[susa|Susa]]** (stelae, door-sockets, a moulded brick façade of a bull-man guarding a
sacred date-palm) to Deh-e Now, Bushehr/Liyan on the Gulf, and sites in the Zagros as far
as the Nuzi region. His great limestone stele invokes "gods of the heavens, gods of Elam,
gods of Susa" for the lives of his wife **Nahhunte-Utu** (sister-wife of both himself and
Kutir-Nahhunte) and their nine children. A **campaign stele** enumerates conquests
including **Arrapha (Kirkuk) and Nuzi** — cities in the normal sphere of
[[assyria-middle|Assyria]] and Babylonia — exploiting "the power vacuum created by the
collapse of the Kassite dynasty," following a route that anticipated the later Achaemenid
royal road through Der to the Diyala.

To him is owed the enigmatic **Sit Shamshi ("bronze sunrise")** — a cast bronze tableau
of two nude crouching figures amid stepped structures, basins and trees, inscribed "I have
made a bronze sunrise," interpreted as a dawn ablution ritual or funerary ceremony, and
"one of the most evocative finds ever made at Susa."

## Counterfactual significance

Shilhak-Inshushinak's reign is the **apex of Middle Elamite military reach**, yet it rests
on the transient opening left by the [[bronze-age-collapse-1200bce|Bronze Age collapse]]
of the Kassites. His successor and son **Hutelutush-Inshushinak** was defeated by
[[nebuchadnezzar-i|Nebuchadnezzar I]] (~1110 BCE), and Elam fell silent for ~300 years.
The reach Shilhak achieved exemplifies Potts's *courte durée* Elam — spectacular but
structurally fragile expansion that left **monuments rather than durable empire**. His vast
inscriptional output is the single richest documentary base for any Elamite reign.
