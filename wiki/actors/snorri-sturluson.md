# Snorri Sturluson

Icelandic chieftain, lawspeaker, poet, and historian (c. 1178/9–1241), the towering
figure of Old Norse letters. A leading member of the **Sturlungar** family, he twice
held the office of lawspeaker at the [[thing-assembly|Althing]] and ran a great
[[icelandic-free-state|chieftaincy]] (*ríki*) from his farm at **Reykholt**, a key
centre of scribal activity. Caught in the feuds of the Sturlung Age and in Norwegian
royal politics, he was killed at Reykholt in 1241 on the implicit authority of King
[[hakon-hakonarson|Hákon Hákonarson]].

## Works and Significance

Snorri is credited with three foundational works of
[[saga-literature|Old Norse literature]]:
- **Heimskringla** (c. 1220–1241), a connected history of the kings of Norway from
  mythical times to Sverre, uniquely integrating biographies, speeches, skaldic
  verses, and hagiography into a causal narrative — so authoritative that it
  displaced earlier kings' sagas, which survive only through later compilations
  based on it (e.g. *Flateyjarbók*).
- the **Prose Edda** (*Snorra Edda*), a textbook for skalds and an encyclopaedia of
  pre-Christian [[norse-paganism|mythology]] (Gylfaginning, Skáldskaparmál,
  Háttatal) with a euhemeristic prologue — the single most important source for
  Norse myth.
- probably **Egils saga**.

His systematisation of skaldic theory and his canonical histories make him the
principal mediator of Viking-Age tradition to posterity. See
[[helle-cambridge-history-scandinavia-v1-2003]].
