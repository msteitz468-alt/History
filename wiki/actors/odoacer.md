# Odoacer

**Odoacer** (Odoacar; c. 433–493), son of Attila’s legate Ediko and a Scirian mother, was the barbarian officer declared king in Italy on 23 August 476 who deposed Romulus Augustulus — the conventional end-date of the western [[roman-empire|Roman Empire]].

## 476 and the settlement with the East (Mitchell)

A former bodyguard of Ricimer, Odoacer was proclaimed by mixed barbarian troops in Italy, killed Orestes within a fortnight, and banished Romulus to a bay-of-Naples estate (*Anon. Val.* 8.37–8; Jordanes). Coinciding with Basiliscus’s coup against Zeno, he had Romulus send a senatorial embassy proposing that **no second western emperor was required** and that Odoacer should rule as *patricius*. Zeno preferred recognizing Julius Nepos as emperor with Odoacer as *patricius* (Malchus fr. 14). After Nepos’s murder (480), Odoacer brought relative stability to Italy and Dalmatia until [[theoderic-the-great|Theoderic]]’s Ostrogothic conquest (ended 493).

## Counterfactual significance

476 is symbolic rather than the sole structural break (Africa 439 and the fiscal spiral matter more — see [[causes-of-the-fall-of-the-western-roman-empire]]); still, Odoacer’s refusal to create a new western Augustus crystallized the West as a post-imperial kingdom under eastern theoretical suzerainty.

## Historiography

Traditional “fall of Rome” date; Mitchell embeds Odoacer in eastern diplomacy and Italian federate politics rather than treating 476 as unexplained catastrophe. Event page: [[fall-of-the-western-roman-empire-476]]. Survey: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Mitchell 2015]].
