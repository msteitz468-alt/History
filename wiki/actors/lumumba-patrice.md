# Patrice Lumumba

## Overview
Patrice Lumumba, leader of the Mouvement National Congolais (which won 33 of 137 seats), became the first prime minister of an independent Congo on June 30, 1960. He was dismissed by President Kasavubu on September 5, 1960 but won a parliamentary confidence vote nonetheless; he was ousted in [[mobutu-sese-seko|Mobutu]]'s CIA-backed coup on September 14, 1960. Captured while fleeing to Stanleyville on November 27, 1960, he was flown to Katanga on Mobutu's orders on January 17, 1961 and executed by a firing squad led by a Belgian police officer the same day.

On Congo's independence day, Lumumba delivered a defiant rebuttal to King Baudouin's speech: "We were subjected to contempt, insults, and blows that we endured morning, noon, and evening, because we are Negroes... Long live the independent and sovereign Congo."

## Key Decisions and Consequences
- His independence-day speech, and his broader refusal to defer to Belgian and Western interests, made him a target for removal within weeks of taking office; CIA Director Allen Dulles cabled on August 26, 1960 that Lumumba's removal was "a high priority," with a $100,000 discretionary fund allocated to station chief Larry Devlin.
- CIA scientist Sidney Gottlieb personally delivered botulinum-poisoned toothpaste to Devlin for a planned assassination attempt, per an order Devlin's own memoir attributes to President Eisenhower ("Yes, President Eisenhower has requested"). Devlin did not use the poison and did not personally execute Lumumba, though he explored other assassination plots; this is a single-source memoir claim and should be treated with appropriate caution.

## Counterfactual Significance
Lumumba's survival might have produced a very different Congo — potentially non-aligned or Soviet-leaning rather than the Mobutu client-state that emerged, with its decades of kleptocratic rule. McCoy frames Lumumba's removal as the opening phase of a broader CIA-tracked four-phase Congo operation; see [[congo-crisis-1960]].

## Historiography
McCoy's account relies heavily on Larry Devlin's own memoir, including verbatim reconstructed dialogue — a source that, while directly involved, is uncorroborated on some of its most serious claims (the alleged presidential assassination order). Source: Alfred W. McCoy, *Cold War on Five Continents* (2024).
