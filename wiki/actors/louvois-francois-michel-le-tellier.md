François Michel Le Tellier, Marquis de Louvois (1641–1691), was Louis XIV's Secretary of State for War and the principal creator of the first true large-scale standing army in Europe. Building on his father Michel Le Tellier's foundations, Louvois professionalized recruitment (including the militia and foreign regiments), introduced uniforms and standardized equipment, established magazines and ration depots, created the intendant system for field administration, and regularized pay and regimental organization. The result was a permanent force of ~400,000 men at peak, capable of sustained multi-front operations.

## Significance
Louvois represents the shift from ad-hoc mercenary bands to state-controlled, permanently embodied forces funded by centralized taxation — the institutional prerequisite for the "period of standing armies" analyzed by Delbrück. His administrative reforms enabled both the scale of Louis XIV's wars and the tactical continuity that produced the linear drills and battalion guns of the eighteenth century.

## In Delbrück Vol. IV
Delbrück devotes substantial attention (Book III) to the French standing-army reforms under the Le Telliers as the model against which the Prussian and other developments are measured. Emphasis on the political and fiscal bases of military power. Grounded in [[sources/delbruck-dawn-of-modern-warfare-vol-4-1985]].
