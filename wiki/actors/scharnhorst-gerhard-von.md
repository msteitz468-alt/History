Gerhard Johann David von Scharnhorst (1755–1813) was a Hanoverian-born Prussian general and the chief architect of the Prussian military reforms after the catastrophic defeats of 1806. He introduced universal military service (in principle), the Krümpersystem for expanding the army under French restrictions, modern staff education, and tactical innovations including skirmisher doctrine and combined-arms flexibility. Mentor to Gneisenau and Clausewitz.

## Reforms
Scharnhorst sought to fuse the disciplined linear traditions of the Frederican army with the new French national and skirmish-based methods. Key measures: opening the officer corps to talent, establishing the War Academy, and preparing the moral and institutional base for the 1813 uprising. His emphasis on education and staff work created the foundation of the Prussian/German general staff tradition.

## In Delbrück Vol. IV
Delbrück credits Scharnhorst (with Gneisenau) as the figure who understood and adapted Napoleonic strategy and tactics to Prussia, enabling the Wars of Liberation. Detailed in the final chapters on the transition to national armies and the critique of pure maneuver strategy. Primary grounding in [[sources/delbruck-dawn-of-modern-warfare-vol-4-1985]].
