# Amenhotep I

**Amenhotep I** (r. ~1525–1504 BCE), son of [[ahmose-i|Ahmose I]], was the second king of
the [[new-kingdom-egypt|18th Dynasty]]. By the end of his reign the dynasty's defining
features were in place: devotion to **Amun of Karnak**, military expansion into Nubia for
material reward, the closed nuclear royal family, and an administration centred on Elkab,
Edfu, and Thebes.

## Reign

He completed the **Nubian campaigns** begun by his father, pushing south of the Second
Cataract around year 8 (monuments at Sai Island and a stele at Aniba record gold and products
delivered). He reopened the Sinai turquoise mines and the Gebel el-Silsila sandstone
quarries, and built actively at **Karnak** — a great limestone gateway, a stone enclosure
with chapels around the Middle Kingdom court, and a jubilee chapel — in a style so closely
emulating Senusret I "that some blocks have been difficult to assign to the proper ruler."
No certain tomb or funerary temple has been identified, and despite a twenty-year reign no
offspring is securely known.

## Deification

Amenhotep I is unusual among Egyptian kings: "soon after his death, both he and his mother
[[ahmose-i|Ahmose-Nefertari]] were deified and worshipped at Thebes," above all as the
**patron deities of [[places/deir-el-medina|Deir el-Medina]]**, the village of the royal-tomb
workmen, throughout the New Kingdom. A month of the calendar was named for him.
