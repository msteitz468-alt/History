# Ḫattušili III (Hittite king)

The Hittite Great King who **usurped the throne from his nephew** and then secured
his shaky legitimacy through the famous **[[egyptian-hittite-treaty-1259bce|peace
treaty with [[ramesses-ii|Ramesses II]]]]**. Author of a remarkable
self-justifying **"Apology,"** and husband of the formidable queen **Puduḫepa**.
Brother of [[muwatalli-ii|Muwatalli II]]. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]] (Ch. XXIV, Goetze). Spelled
**Khattushilish** in the source; reign ~1286–1265 (volume) / ~1267–1237 BCE.

## The usurpation

As [[muwatalli-ii|Muwatalli II's]] viceroy of the north (king of Ḫakpiš and Nerik,
which he recovered from the Kaska), Ḫattušili had accumulated extraordinary power.
When Muwatalli died, the throne passed to his son by a concubine, **Urḫi-Teshub**
(who took the throne-name **Muršili III**). Mutual suspicion grew; when Urḫi-Teshub
stripped Ḫattušili of his offices, he **revolted, defeated and deposed his nephew**,
and exiled him "across the sea" to Alashiya ([[cyprus|Cyprus]]). His **"Apology"** —
a kind of royal autobiography blaming the conflict entirely on the young king and
casting himself as the loyal, divinely-favoured servant of Ištar (Šaušga) — is a
landmark of ancient political self-justification.

## The treaty and the balance of power

His usurpation made **international legitimacy** urgent. Sixteen years after
[[battle-of-qadesh-1274bce|Qadesh]], in Ramesses II's Year 21 (1259 BCE), he and
Ramesses sealed the **[[egyptian-hittite-treaty-1259bce|"Eternal Treaty"]]** of
parity and mutual defence — cemented later by giving his daughter to Ramesses in
marriage. The driving fear behind it was **Assyria**: Ḫattušili haughtily rejected
[[tukulti-ninurta-i|Adad-nirari I's]] claim to "brotherhood" ("were you and I born of
one mother?") and built a **defensive alliance with Kassite Babylonia**
(Kadashman-Turgu) to threaten Assyria's rear. Throughout, his queen **Puduḫepa**
acted as a co-ruler — state documents in both their names, queen-to-queen
correspondence with Egypt.

## Significance

Ḫattušili III stabilized Hatti after a succession crisis and presided over the
**diplomatic high-water mark of the Late Bronze great-powers system** — the
Egyptian peace and the Babylonian alliance. But the **rise of Assyria** he so feared
would, under his son Tudhaliya IV and grandsons, press Hatti toward the
collapse of ~1200. His career is a prime exhibit of **dynastic instability and
legitimacy-by-diplomacy**.

## Bryce: the usurper's *Apology* and the diplomat-king

[[sources/bryce-kingdom-of-the-hittites-2005|Bryce (2005)]] makes Hattusili the wiki's central
case of **legitimacy manufactured after the fact**. The chief source for his life, the
**[[urhi-teshub|Apology of Hattusili]]** (CTH 81), is — Bryce warns — "largely self-laudatory
and self-justificatory," a usurper's brief: a sickly child dedicated to the goddess **Ishtar**,
who "took me by the hand," he rose as his brother [[muwatalli-ii|Muwattalli's]] viceroy of the
north (famously recapturing the holy city of **Nerik**), loyally enthroned his nephew
[[urhi-teshub|Urhi-Teshub]] — "Out of esteem for my brother, I did no evil against him" — and
"submitted for seven years" until the king stripped his powers, whereupon a "divine command"
sanctioned his coup. Bryce notes the coup lacked wholehearted support, and that Hattusili
explicitly barred Muwattalli's line from the succession. In power, "the predominant image of
Hattusili … is that of diplomat and conciliator": he reigned ~25 years into his seventies,
worked the great-power system (Babylon, the prickly correspondence with [[assyria-middle|Assyria]]),
and crowned it with the **[[egyptian-hittite-treaty-1259bce|Silver Treaty]]** with
[[ramesses-ii|Ramesses II]] — whose true prize was Egyptian recognition of his legitimacy. His
gravest error, Bryce argues, was the **honourable exile** of Urhi-Teshub, who escaped to Egypt and
became a standing destabiliser; with his Kizzuwatnan queen **[[puduhepa|Puduhepa]]** as an
unprecedented co-ruler, he managed an empire he never expanded.
