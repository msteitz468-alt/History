# Robert Maheu

Robert A. Maheu (1917–2008) was a former FBI agent and operator of a private investigative and public-relations firm who became Howard Hughes' most visible lieutenant and front man during the critical 1966–1970 Las Vegas phase. Maheu handled day-to-day operations of the newly acquired casinos and hotels, served as the public face of the "Hughes Nevada Operation," conducted political and business missions on Hughes' behalf, and later clashed with the Mormon inner circle over control.

## Role and Relationship with Hughes

Maheu was selected for his discretion, operational skill, and ability to operate in the gray areas Hughes preferred. Hughes communicated with him largely by memo and telephone from sealed suites; Maheu executed in the real world. For a time he wielded enormous apparent authority in Nevada and Washington.

The relationship ultimately soured. According to Barlett & Steele and Maheu's own later accounts, the Mormon aides (led by Bill Gay) gradually supplanted Maheu, leading to his ouster, bitter lawsuits, and Maheu's public break with the Hughes organization. Maheu's memoir and interviews became important sources for the later Hughes story.

## Significance

Maheu exemplifies the proxy system Hughes increasingly relied upon as his personal involvement diminished. His tenure covers the height of the Las Vegas spending and PR campaign and the beginning of the internal conspiracies and power struggles that marked the empire's final phase. His post-Hughes testimony and writings provide one of the richer aide-level views of Hughes' methods and decline.
