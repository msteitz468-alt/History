# Angelos Dynasty

Byzantine imperial dynasty (1185–1204) that came to power when Isaac II Angelos led the popular uprising that overthrew Andronikos I Komnenos, ending the Komnenian line and its program of centralized restoration. The Angeloi presided over a marked weakening of central Byzantine authority, marred by court intrigue, fiscal mismanagement, and the loss of provincial control to local magnates and the resurgent Bulgarian state.

## Role

Isaac II was himself deposed and blinded in 1195 by his brother Alexios III; the dynasty's final crisis came when Isaac's son Alexios IV, seeking Western military help to restore his father, invited the Fourth Crusade to Constantinople in 1203 — a decision that led directly to the crusaders' sack of the city in 1204 and the dynasty's extinction.

## Counterfactual Significance

The Angeloi's internal succession struggles and courting of Latin military power are treated by historians as a critical structural failure that converted the Fourth Crusade from a diverted expedition into the catastrophic [[fourth-crusade-1204|sack of Constantinople]], a rupture from which the Byzantine state never fully recovered even after the 1261 restoration.
