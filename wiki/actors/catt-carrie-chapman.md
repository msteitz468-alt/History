# Carrie Chapman Catt (1859–1947)

President of the National American Woman Suffrage Association (NAWSA); architect of the "Winning Plan" combining state campaigns with federal-amendment pressure. Successor tradition to [[anthony-susan-b|Susan B. Anthony]] and [[stanton-elizabeth-cady|Elizabeth Cady Stanton]]; tactical rival and sometime partner of [[paul-alice|Alice Paul]]'s more confrontational NWP.

## Role in the Wilson years

Catt cultivated access to [[wilson-woodrow|Wilson]] and congressional leaders, betting that wartime loyalty and organization would convert the president. Cox documents both her persistence and her frustration at Wilson's long refusal to treat the Anthony Amendment as a party or war measure — until political arithmetic and NWP pressure forced a shift. After ratification she founded the League of Women Voters.

## Historiography

Often framed as the "respectable" moderate against Paul's militants; Cox stresses that both wings faced the same structural opponent in Wilsonian and southern Democratic resistance, and that Catt's access did not equal influence until very late.
