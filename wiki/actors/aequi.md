# Aequi

An ancient Italic people inhabiting the mountainous Apennine country east of
Rome, among the Roman Republic's most persistent early adversaries during
the fifth and fourth centuries BCE, frequently allied with the neighbouring
Volsci against Rome and the Latin League.

## Role and Significance

Roman tradition preserves numerous wars against the Aequi, most famously the
legendary campaign of 458 BCE in which the dictator Cincinnatus was said to
have been summoned from his farm to relieve a Roman army trapped by the
Aequi at Mount Algidus — a story later celebrated as an exemplar of Roman
civic virtue. Sustained Roman and Latin pressure gradually reduced Aequian
power through the fourth century BCE; Rome finally subdued them decisively
around 304 BCE, after which they disappear as an independent political
force and were absorbed into Rome's expanding system of alliance and
citizenship in central Italy.

## Counterfactual Significance

The prolonged Aequian (and allied Volscian) wars occupied much of early
Republican Rome's military energy for over a century, shaping the
development of Roman military institutions and its alliance system with the
Latins well before Rome confronted more powerful adversaries such as the
Samnites and Carthage.

## Historiography

Nearly all detailed information on the Aequi derives from later Roman
historians (chiefly Livy and Dionysius of Halicarnassus), writing centuries
after the events they describe and shaping episodes such as the Cincinnatus
story into moral exempla; archaeological evidence for the historical Aequi
themselves is comparatively sparse, and modern scholarship treats much of
the detailed narrative with caution.
