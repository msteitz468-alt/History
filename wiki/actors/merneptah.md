# Merneptah

The thirteenth son and aged successor of [[ramesses-ii|Ramesses II]] — the pharaoh
who beat off the **first great wave of the [[unknown|Sea Peoples]]** and whose
**Israel Stela** carries the earliest known mention of **Israel**. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]] (Ch. XXIII, Faulkner). Reign 1236–1223
(volume) / ~1213–1203 BCE.

## Egypt on the defensive

Already over fifty at his accession, Merneptah inherited a slackened frontier. In
**Year 5** a coalition of **Libyans** (Libu, Meshwesh, Kehek) allied with "**peoples
of the sea**" — **Sherden, Sheklesh, Lukka, Tursha and Akawasha** (the last perhaps
the [[ahhiyawa-question|Aḫḫiyawa/Achaeans]]) — invaded the western Delta to
**settle**, bringing their families and goods. Merneptah, having reorganized the
army, met and **routed them at Pi-yer** in a six-hour battle. These are, in
Gardiner's phrase, the "forerunners of the great migratory movement about to descend
on Egypt and Palestine" — the first surge of the [[unknown|Bronze Age Collapse]].

## The Israel Stela

His **"Israel Stela"** celebrates the Libyan victory and a suppressed revolt in
Palestine: *"Ascalon is carried off, Gezer is captured, Yenoam is made non-existent,
**Israel is waste and has no seed**, Khor has become a widow because of Egypt."* The
mention of **Israel** — written with the determinative for a *people*, not a
land/state — is the **only occurrence in any Egyptian text** and the earliest
attestation of the name, placing an "Israel" already in Canaan by ~1207 BCE. It
overturned the old assumption that Merneptah was the **pharaoh of the Exodus**; "the
fact remains that there is no positive evidence relating to the date of the Exodus"
(see Section 4). The stela's "**Khatti is at peace**" confirms the
[[egyptian-hittite-treaty-1259bce|Egyptian–Hittite peace]] still held — Egypt even
shipped grain to relieve a Hittite famine.

## Significance

Merneptah marks the **turn from Egyptian offence to defence**: the empire is now
fending off migration rather than projecting power. His reign yields two
landmark documents — the first **Sea-Peoples** invasion record and the first
**"Israel"** inscription — that make it pivotal for both the
[[unknown|Bronze Age Collapse]] and the debate over **Israelite origins**. His death
opened the **chaotic end of the Nineteenth Dynasty** (Amenmesse, Sethos II, Siptah,
Queen Tewosret, and the powerful chancellor **Bay**).
