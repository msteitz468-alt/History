# Madame Ngo Dinh Nhu (Tran Le Xuan)

Wife of Ngo Dinh Nhu, de facto First Lady of South Vietnam 1955–1963, and a powerful political figure in her own right. Known in the Western press as the "Dragon Lady." Member of the National Assembly, commander of the Women's Paramilitary Corps and Solidarity Movement, and outspoken defender of the Diem regime's Catholic-influenced moral and political order.

## Public Role and Controversies

Madame Nhu was a polarizing public voice: she defended the regime's crackdowns, mocked the Buddhist protesters (famously commenting on the self-immolation of Thich Quang Duc), and pushed legislation enforcing "traditional" morality (e.g., bans on dancing, beauty contests, and "immoral" influences). Her flamboyant style, pistol range photos, and combative interviews made her a lightning rod for US journalists and officials who saw the Ngo family as the main obstacle to reform and broader legitimacy.

## After the Coup

In exile after the November 1963 coup. Lived in France, Italy, and the United States at various times. Remained unrepentant about the regime until her death in 2011.
