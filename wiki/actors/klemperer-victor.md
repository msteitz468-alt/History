# Victor Klemperer

German-Jewish philologist whose diaries are among the most important
first-person witnesses to Weimar Germany and the Third Reich — used by Evans
as a running primary source across the trilogy, and already cited on multiple
pages of this wiki (hyperinflation, persecution, *LTI* language analysis).

## Life

Son of a Reform-synagogue preacher; baptized Protestant; married the
non-Jewish pianist Eva Schlemmer in 1906. Academic start at Naples; served on
the Western Front, invalided 1916, then army censorship; supported the 1914
war as "just." Professor of French literature at Dresden Technical University
from 1920, where he remained without advancement — which he partly attributed
to antisemitism ("the liberal ones always have two Jews already and don't
take a third," diary, 26 Dec 1926). His diary credo: "Just collect life.
Always collect. Impressions, knowledge, reading, events, everything. And
don't ask why or what for."

## The Witness

Politically a conservative, nostalgic for Bismarckian Germany, but permanently
alienated from the Nationalists by their antisemitism ("My inclination to the
right has suffered greatly... as a result of permanent antisemitism," March
1920). On Hindenburg's 1925 election: "Fascism everywhere." August 1932:
"Hitler before the gates – or who else? And what will become of me, the
Jewish professor?" Evans notes that in the Weimar years Klemperer "did not
suffer unduly from antisemitism... did not experience any violence" — formal
Jewish freedom and equality exceeded the pre-1914 order — which makes his
post-1933 record of incremental persecution the more analytically valuable.
His survival in Dresden (protected by his marriage) and postwar career in the
DDR are covered where his diaries are cited on [[the-holocaust]] and
[[persecution-of-german-jews-1933-1939]].
