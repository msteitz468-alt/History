# Petrograd Soviet of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies

The **Petrograd Soviet of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies** was a representative council established in the Russian capital during the February Revolution of 1917. It served as the primary rival and partner of the Provisional Government in the "dual power" structure of 1917, ultimately becoming the institutional vehicle through which the Bolsheviks seized power in the October Revolution.

## Biography / Description

The Petrograd Soviet was formed on March 12, 1917, modeled on the St. Petersburg Soviet of the Revolution of 1905. It was composed of delegates elected directly by the factory workers and the soldiers of the capital's military garrison. Initially dominated by moderate socialists—Mensheviks and Socialist Revolutionaries (SRs)—who believed that Russia was undergoing a bourgeois revolution and was not ready for socialism, the Soviet did not attempt to seize state power directly but sought to monitor and influence the Provisional Government.

The Soviet enjoyed immediate, massive support among the restless working class and the garrison troops, giving it greater de facto power than the Provisional Government. In September 1917, following the Kornilov affair and the radicalization of the masses, the Bolsheviks won a majority in the Soviet, and Leon Trotsky was elected its chairman. In October 1917, the Soviet created the **Military Revolutionary Committee**, which executed the armed overthrow of the Provisional Government.

## Key Decisions and Achievements

### Order No. 1 (March 14, 1917)
- On March 14, 1917, the Soviet issued **Order No. 1** to the troops of the Petrograd military district. 
- The order directed military units to elect soldiers' committees, send representatives to the Soviet, and obey only those orders of the military command that did not contradict the Soviet's decisions. It also declared that weapons were to remain under the control of the soldiers' committees and abolished traditional officers' privileges.
- Order No. 1 played a central role in the rapid demoralization, loss of discipline, and eventual collapse of the Russian army during World War I.

### The Congress of Soviets
The Petrograd Soviet took the lead in organizing national representation. In June 1917, the **First All-Russian Congress of Soviets** met in Petrograd, representing over 350 local soviets. The Congress elected an executive committee that served as the supreme soviet body in Russia, although the Petrograd Soviet remained the most politically active.

### The Military Revolutionary Committee (MRC)
In October 1917, the Soviet established the MRC, ostensibly to defend Petrograd against German advance and counter-revolutionary plots. Under Trotsky's direction, the MRC assumed control of the capital's garrison, armed the Red Guards, and systematically occupied strategic points in Petrograd to overthrow the Provisional Government on November 7–8, 1917.

## Historical Significance

The Petrograd Soviet's historical significance lies in its role as a highly democratic, direct-representation council that challenged the traditional state authority. In the "dual power" dynamic of 1917, the Soviet possessed the popular mandate and the physical force (control of the garrison and workers), while the Provisional Government possessed only the nominal administrative authority. By issuing Order No. 1, the Soviet fatally weakened the army. When the Bolsheviks captured the Soviet's leadership in the autumn of 1917, they transformed it into the institutional base for their dictatorship, using the slogan "All power to the Soviets" to legitimize their coup.
