# Tiglath-pileser III

**Tiglath-pileser III** (reigned 744–727 BCE) was the **maker of the Neo-Assyrian
Empire in its imperial form** — the king whose reign Grayson calls "a brilliant
beginning to a new and final era in the history of the Neo-Assyrian empire."
Where his predecessors had raided, he **annexed and administered**; the
[[neo-assyrian-empire|empire]] that ruled the Near East for the next century was
essentially his creation. He is the biblical **Pul** (II Kings 15:19).
[[boardman-cah-v3p2-1991|CAH Vol. III, Part 2]] (Ch. 22, A. K. Grayson) is the
authority.

## Accession

Almost certainly a **usurper**. He makes only an "amorphous claim to ancient
Assyrian lineage" rather than naming his father, and Grayson concludes he "took
advantage of the chaotic times to stage a coup d'état" in 745, ending the
decline of the [[neo-assyrian-empire#Decline into the Interval|"Interval"]]
(782–745) when over-mighty governors and a resurgent [[urartu|Urartu]] had
shrunk Assyria. He campaigned **every year of his reign** save one.

## The war with Urartu

His central foreign achievement was breaking [[urartu|Urartu]], which during the
Interval had "grown at Assyria's expense to be the greatest state in south-west
Asia." In **743** he shattered an anti-Assyrian coalition (Arpad, Urartu, Melid,
Gurgum, Kummukhu) and routed **Sarduri II** personally in Kummukhu; Arpad fell
after a three-year siege (740) and became a province. A daring thrust in **735**
carried the Assyrian army through Urartu to besiege the capital **Tushpa (Van)**
— the city held, but Urartu's western power was broken.

## The conquest of the West

Tiglath-pileser pushed Assyrian control to the **Egyptian border** for the first
time. He took **Gaza** (734), set a statue at the "Brook of Egypt," and created a
trading-post (*bit kari*) there. When **Rezin of Damascus** organized a revolt
(supported by Tyre, [[kingdom-of-israel|Israel under Pekah]], and Arab queens),
Tiglath-pileser crushed it: **Damascus fell in 732**, was made a province, and
Rezin was killed. Pekah of Israel, an ally of Rezin, was defeated and then
murdered in a coup by **Hoshea**, who became an Assyrian vassal. A long roll of
tributaries — Ammon, Moab, Ashkelon, [[kingdom-of-judah|Judah (Jehoahaz/Ahaz)]],
Edom, Gaza, Tyre, Arvad — marks the new reach. This is the Syro-Ephraimite
crisis of Isaiah 7.

## Babylonia

"The fortunes of Assyria depended upon her relations with Babylonia." Five
months after his accession he raided the south to prop up **Nabonassar** (a
treaty obligation); when a Chaldaean, **Mukin-zeri**, seized Babylon, Tiglath-
pileser besieged him and in **729 personally took the Babylonian crown** —
beginning the Sargonid policy of the Assyrian king holding the **dual monarchy**
of Assyria and Babylon. He noted in passing the submission, in 729, of the
Chaldaean **[[neo-babylonian-empire|Merodach-baladan]]**, who would become
Assyria's nemesis under his successors.

## The instruments of empire

Grayson credits him with the reforms that made the empire durable, taken up in
[[assyrian-mass-deportation|mass deportation]] and the army:

- **Systematic two-way deportation.** Earlier kings moved conquered peoples
  mainly *to* Assyria as labour; Tiglath-pileser **exchanged whole populations
  between regions** to break the roots of rebellion — the policy that destroyed
  [[fall-of-samaria-722bce|Samaria]]'s nationhood and reshaped the Near Eastern
  ethnic map.
- A reorganized, better-equipped **standing army** and a tightened **provincial
  system** with governors and fixed tribute.
- The practice of leaving the **crown prince** in charge of the empire while the
  king campaigned.

## Significance

- `produced:` the imperial structure inherited by [[sargon-ii|Sargon II]],
  [[sennacherib|Sennacherib]], [[esarhaddon|Esarhaddon]], and
  [[ashurbanipal|Ashurbanipal]] — see [[neo-assyrian-empire]].
- `produced:` the destruction of Aram-Damascus and the vassalage of the Levantine
  states, the precondition for the [[fall-of-samaria-722bce|fall of Samaria]].
- Counterfactual weight: without his coup and reforms the Assyrian "empire" might
  have remained the insecure, raid-based hegemony it had been — Grayson stresses
  that real Assyrian *control* begins only with him.

## Historiography

Known from **royal annals** (badly mutilated in places), the **Eponym Chronicle**,
the **Babylonian Chronicle**, the **Nimrud letters**, and biblical synchronisms
(II Kings 15–16; Isaiah 7). His palace reliefs at Calah were looted in antiquity
by [[esarhaddon|Esarhaddon]], leaving the visual record fragmentary. The
fragmentary annals make the *order* of his campaigns a standing problem.
