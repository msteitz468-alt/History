# Avignon Papacy (1309–1377)

## Overview

The **Avignon Papacy** is the nearly seven-decade residence of the bishops of Rome at **Avignon** on the Rhône (from Clement V, 1309, until Gregory XI's return to Rome in 1377). Logan stresses that the common label "Babylonian Captivity" is polemical and misleading: the popes were not prisoners, and the period saw major **curial, fiscal, and administrative development**. The return to Rome, however, precipitated the **[[great-schism-1378|Great Schism]]** (1378–1417).

## Role and Significance

Avignon made the papacy the most systematically organised absentee episcopal court in medieval Europe, raised enduring questions of French influence, and left a palace and bureaucracy whose legitimacy crises fed [[conciliarism]].
