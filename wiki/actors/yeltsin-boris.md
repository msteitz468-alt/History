# Boris Yeltsin
## Biography / Description
Boris Nikolaevich Yeltsin was born in 1931 in Butka, Sverdlovsk (now Yekaterinburg) region, to a poor peasant family. He trained as an engineer and became a long-time Party administrator, serving as the first secretary (boss) of the Sverdlovsk region. In 1985, Gorbachev brought him to Moscow to head the Moscow Party organization. However, his outspoken criticism of the slow pace of perestroika led to his dramatic dismissal in November 1987. Yeltsin pivoted to independent politics, resigning from the Communist Party in July 1990 and building a populist base. He was elected President of the Russian Soviet Federated Socialist Republic (RSFSR) in a landslide on June 12, 1991. Following his resistance to the August Coup and the subsequent dissolution of the Soviet Union, Yeltsin became the first President of the independent Russian Federation, serving until his sudden resignation on December 31, 1999. He died on April 23, 2007.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **Defeat of the August Coup (1991):** Yeltsin led the popular resistance against the hardline communist coup, famously rallying crowds from atop a tank outside the Russian parliament building ("the White House"), which catapulted him to supreme political prominence.
- **Dissolution of the USSR (1991):** In December 1991, Yeltsin met with the leaders of Ukraine and Belarus to sign the Belovezha Accords, declaring the Soviet Union dissolved and establishing the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS), which forced Gorbachev's resignation.
- **Economic Shock Therapy (1992):** Under advisors like Yegor Gaidar and Anatoly Chubais, Yeltsin launched rapid market reforms, including price liberalization, the lifting of trade barriers, and a sweeping voucher privatization scheme that transferred state assets to private hands, creating a new class of "oligarchs."
- **1993 Constitutional Crisis:** Yeltsin clashed with the Russian parliament (the Supreme Soviet led by Khasbulatov and Rutskoy) over presidential authority. In September 1993, he unconstitutionally dissolved parliament; when legislators resisted and launched armed rebellions, Yeltsin ordered the army to shell the parliament building, killing over a hundred people.
- **First Chechen War (1994–1996):** To prevent Chechnya's secession, Yeltsin ordered a military invasion. The war proved to be a disaster, exposing the appalling condition of the Russian army and resulting in massive civilian casualties (such as the Samashki massacre) before a peace pact was signed by Aleksandr Lebed in 1996.
- **1996 Presidential Election:** Despite an approval rating of single digits, widespread economic misery, and poor health, Yeltsin won re-election against communist challenger Gennady Zyuganov, heavily aided by media control and financial backing from the oligarchs.
- **1998 Financial Crisis:** Following years of borrowing and economic stagnation, the Russian government defaulted on its debt and devalued the ruble, wiping out savings and leading to the collapse of Yeltsin's economic programs.

## Historical Significance
Yeltsin was the founding father of the modern Russian Federation, overseeing its transition from a communist command economy to a capitalist, democratic state. He successfully dismantled the monopoly of the Communist Party and established basic freedoms of speech and press. However, his tenure was plagued by severe economic decline, hyperinflation, rampant corruption, the rise of organized crime (mafias), and the concentration of wealth in the hands of the oligarchs. Riasanovsky notes that while Yeltsin had a sincere desire to reform Russia, his career-long political manner remained that of an "authoritarian Communist boss" who repeatedly bypassed constitutional boundaries (Lines 7299–7301).
