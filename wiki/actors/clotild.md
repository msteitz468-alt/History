# Clotild

**Clotild** (c. 474–545) was the Burgundian princess who became the wife of
**[[clovis]]** and, in **[[gregory-of-tours-history-of-the-franks-thorpe-1974|Gregory of
Tours']]** telling, the human instrument of his conversion to Catholic Christianity — the
hinge on which the orthodox self-definition of the Franks turns. She should be
distinguished from the later royal nun **Clotild, daughter of Charibert**, who led the
[[gundovald-affair-585|sixth-century]] revolt at Radegund's convent of the Holy Cross
(treated on [[radegund-of-poitiers]] and [[merovingian-monasticism]]).

## Life

Clotild was the daughter of the Burgundian king Chilperic, whom — Gregory reports — his
brother Gundobad had murdered, drowning Chilperic's wife "after tying a stone round her
neck" and exiling the daughters. Clovis sought her hand and "was delighted when he saw
Clotild and made her his wife." A Catholic among an Arian people, she repeatedly pressed
her pagan husband to abandon his idols: "The gods whom you worship are no good… You ought
instead to worship Him who created… heaven and earth." When their first baptised son,
Ingomer, "died in his white robes," Clovis reproached her faith; she gave thanks rather
than despair, and when the second son Chlodomer sickened she prayed and "the baby
recovered."

It was Clotild who "ordered Saint Remigius… to be summoned in secret" to instruct Clovis,
preparing the conversion that followed his battlefield vow against the Alemanni. After
Clovis's death she "came to live in Tours" and "served as a religious in the church of
Saint Martin," but remained a dynastic actor: she urged her sons into the **Burgundian
war** to avenge her parents, and was the anguished centre of the murder of
[[chlodomer|Chlodomer]]'s sons, forced to choose between the scissors and the sword and
crying "I would rather see them dead than with their hair cut short." Her prayers to St
Martin are credited by Gregory with repeatedly saving her son [[chlothar-i|Chlothar I]]
from his brothers.

## Counterfactual Significance

Gregory's narrative makes Clotild the proximate cause of the single most consequential act
in Frankish history — Clovis's Catholic baptism, which aligned the Franks with the Gallo-
Roman episcopate against the Arian Goths and Burgundians (see
[[christianisation-of-the-frankish-aristocracy]]). Modern scholarship
([[geary-before-france-germany-1988|Geary]]) reads the conversion as a collective political
act rather than a wife's persuasion, treating Gregory's domestic drama as hagiographic
construction; but the *image* of Clotild as Catholic queen-maker shaped the dynasty's
self-understanding for centuries.
