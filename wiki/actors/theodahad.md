## Role and Background

Theodahad was a nephew of the Ostrogothic king Theoderic the Great, ruling as a regional magnate in Tuscany, known more for his interest in Platonic philosophy and Roman literary culture than for military ability. When his cousin Amalasuntha, Theoderic's daughter and regent for her young son Athalaric, sought a co-ruler to shore up her authority after Athalaric's death in 534, she elevated Theodahad to the throne — a decision that proved fatal to her.

## Reign and Downfall

Theodahad had Amalasuntha deposed and murdered in 535, an act Emperor Justinian used as pretext to launch the Byzantine reconquest of Italy that opened the [[events/gothic-war-535-554|Gothic War]]. As [[actors/belisarius|Belisarius]] swiftly overran Sicily and southern Italy, Theodahad's passivity and perceived incompetence discredited him with the Gothic army, which deposed him in 536 in favor of the more forceful [[actors/witigis|Witigis]]; Theodahad was killed shortly afterward while attempting to flee.

## Significance

Theodahad's brief and disastrous reign — precipitating the murder that gave Justinian his casus belli, then collapsing under military pressure — marks the point at which Ostrogothic Italy's internal instability opened the door to two decades of devastating war that ultimately destroyed the kingdom.
