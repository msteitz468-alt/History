# Dwight D. Eisenhower (1890–1969)

Supreme Commander Allied Expeditionary Force (SHAEF) for Overlord and the campaign in Northwest Europe. Earlier commander of Torch and Mediterranean theater.

## Role in Normandy (from D'Este and related sources)

Eisenhower bore ultimate responsibility for the success of the invasion and the cohesion of the Allied coalition. He made the final decision on the D-Day date despite weather risks. He enforced unity of command and generally avoided direct interference in Montgomery's operational conduct, even when frustrated by the slow progress around Caen. His relationship with Montgomery was complex — supportive in public, privately critical of the pace and of Montgomery's postwar claims.

He played a key role in the broader strategic decisions (e.g., Broad Front vs. narrow thrust later in 1944) and in managing the air commanders and the inter-Allied political dimension.

## Historiography

Often portrayed as the great coalition manager rather than a battlefield general. D'Este and other operational historians credit him with holding the alliance together under extreme pressure while noting his occasional frustration with ground command arrangements. His postwar memoirs present a balanced (if sometimes sanitized) view.
