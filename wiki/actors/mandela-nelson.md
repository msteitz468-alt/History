# Nelson Mandela

**Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela** (18 July 1918 – 5 December 2013), also known by his clan name **Madiba**, was a South African anti-apartheid revolutionary, political leader, and statesman. He served as President of South Africa from 1994 to 1999, the first to be elected in a fully representative democratic election. He had previously been a central figure in the African National Congress (ANC), co-founder of its armed wing Umkhonto we Sizwe, and the world’s most prominent political prisoner for 27 years (1964–1990).

Mandela’s life spanned the height of apartheid, the Cold War confrontation over southern Africa, and the negotiated transition to non-racial democracy. He is widely regarded as the preeminent symbol of the global anti-apartheid struggle and of reconciliation after racial tyranny.

## Summary

Born into a Thembu royal family in the Eastern Cape village of Mvezo (raised in Qunu), Mandela was given the English name Nelson at school. After mission education and a brief period at Fort Hare, he moved to Johannesburg, qualified as a lawyer, and entered politics through the ANC Youth League alongside Walter Sisulu and Oliver Tambo. He rose to national prominence during the 1952 Defiance Campaign, endured the marathon Treason Trial (1956–61), and after the 1960 Sharpeville massacre and the banning of the ANC helped launch the armed struggle (Umkhonto we Sizwe). Captured in 1962, he was the leading defendant in the 1963–64 Rivonia Trial, where he delivered the “I am prepared to die” statement. Sentenced to life imprisonment, he served most of it on Robben Island (prisoner 466/64), later Pollsmoor and Victor Verster.

From the mid-1980s he conducted secret talks with the apartheid government while the mass movement and international sanctions rendered the country ungovernable. Released on 11 February 1990, he led the ANC through the CODESA negotiations, managed the turbulent transition (including tensions with Winnie Madikizela-Mandela), and in 1994 became president in the Government of National Unity. His presidency emphasized national reconciliation (symbolized by the 1995 Rugby World Cup), the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, and the entrenchment of constitutional democracy, while structural economic change proved limited. He stepped down after one term, married Graça Machel in 1998, and devoted his retirement to HIV/AIDS advocacy, conflict mediation (notably Burundi), and philanthropy. He died at his home in Houghton, Johannesburg, aged 95.

## Summary (continued)

From mid-1982 he was held at Pollsmoor (with Sisulu, Mhlaba, Mlangeni; Kathrada later) in improved conditions (roof garden, 900 plants in oil drums, proper beds, library, terrace) before transfer to Victor Verster (Dec 1988). The 1976 Soweto uprising and “FREE MANDELA!” campaign transformed him into a global symbol. He initiated secret contacts with the government (1985 Coetsee hospital visit; Savernake 3-hour talk with sherry “like nectar”; regular secret committee with Barnard 1988) while maintaining the public hard line (“Only free men can negotiate”). Released 11 February 1990, he led the ANC through CODESA negotiations marked by violence (Reef war, third-force suspicions, Inkathagate, Boipatong 45 dead, Bisho 28 dead), public rupture with de Klerk, and personal crisis over Winnie’s convictions and affair. He extracted the Record of Understanding (Sept 1992) via direct pressure and the Ramaphosa–Meyer bilateral channel, advancing the interim Government of National Unity and majority-rule framework. Winnie issues forced separation (Apr 1992, with generous public tribute) and 1996 divorce (court testimony: “I was the loneliest man”). 

In the GNU (1994–1999) he prioritised reconciliation (Springboks jersey at 1995 Rugby World Cup final for “national fusion”; magnanimity to de Klerk, Botha, Viljoen, Coetsee). He proposed the Truth and Reconciliation Commission to address “festering sore[s]” via amnesty-for-truth. Early governance faced “culture of entitlement,” slow delivery, strikes, and crime. Winnie scandals as deputy minister led to her dismissal. De Klerk departed 1996 citing declining influence. Mandela stepped down after one term, married Graça Machel in 1998 (“I’m in love with a remarkable lady... blooming like a flower”), and in retirement made AIDS advocacy a priority, publicly breaking with Mbeki’s denialism (2001–02 Cameron meeting, prize award the day before Mbeki’s address, Achmat “HIV-POSITIVE” T-shirt, NEC mauling). He announced full public retirement in 2004 at 85. Died 5 December 2013.
