# Adolf Hitler

Austrian-born German politician who led the Nazi Party and became Chancellor (1933) then Führer of Germany. Architect of the Third Reich, aggressive expansion leading to World War II, and the Holocaust. His decisions drove German strategy from early victories (Blitzkrieg, 1939-1941) to catastrophic overreach (Barbarossa, Stalingrad, Ardennes). Committed suicide in Berlin as the war ended.

**Early career (Paxton & Hessler 2011)**: Hitler's Vienna years were
cushioned by his mother's gifts and inheritance, contrary to the poverty
narrative Hitler himself constructed in *Mein Kampf*; he emigrated to
Munich in part to avoid the Austrian draft, then served four years in the
Great War as a runner, was twice wounded, won the Iron Cross First Class,
was gassed in 1918, and claimed to have experienced a political vision
during a period of temporary blindness. In 1919 the army assigned him,
undercover, to investigate the German Workers' Party (DAP), founded in
March 1918 by a Munich locksmith; he joined instead, becoming card number
555, and went full-time with the party from April 1920. The party newspaper
*Völkischer Beobachter* was purchased with funding split half from Röhm
and Epp and half from two wealthy women patrons. Paxton and Hessler read
the Twenty-Five Points (February 1920) as small-creditor grievance
politics rather than socialism — "a better guide to lower-middle-class
grievances than to later Nazi action." Source:
[[paxton-europe-twentieth-century-2011]].

**Contemporary reading (Flynn 1944, attributed)**: [[flynn-as-we-go-marching-1944|Flynn]]'s
mid-war analysis treats Hitler as the sixth member of Drexler's crackpot German
Workers' Party (sent by the army to surveil it, joining instead) whose only
fixed convictions were army-worship and scapegoating of socialists and Jews for
1918; the socialist-confiscatory economic planks of the 1920 Twenty-Five Points
were discarded once Thyssen and Rhenish industry began funding him ("Do you
think I am crazy enough to ruin Germany's greatest industry?"). Flynn's
signature claim — "public opinion had molded him": Hitler located and rode
existing German currents rather than imposing a doctrine — and his
counterfactual that blocking Hitler personally would not have prevented a
German fascism (Röhm, Strasser, Papen, Schleicher, "some general") are
attributed positions, as is his argument that Hitler's war on the churches was
a monopoly claim over youth indoctrination rather than religious animus.

**Payne's account (1995)**: [[payne-history-of-fascism-1995|Payne]] traces
the trajectory precisely: assiduous prewar Vienna reader of the Ostara
Society's occult-racist Ariosophy; army "political observer" who joined
Drexler's DAP in September 1919 as member fifty-five; propaganda director,
then uncontested Führer by July 1921; the Twenty-Five Points (Feb 1920 —
"No Jew can be a member of the nation," nationalization of 51 percent of
large corporations) as the founding program; the Beer Hall Putsch as "the
very opposite of the March on Rome"; Landsberg's *Mein Kampf* fixing the
race-and-space (Lebensraum) core while deliberately concealing the full
revolutionary goals; the Führerprinzip formalized at the 1925 refounding.
Payne reads Hitler as revolutionary rather than opportunist — his own words:
National Socialism "is more even than a religion; it is the will to create a
new man" — who inverted the Stalinist sequence, requiring foreign conquest
*before* full internal racial revolution, within a self-imposed window of
"scarcely more than a decade." On the war: Hitler "had no intention of
starting a world war in 1939"; the decision to widen it "was made in London
and Paris," but the war's revolutionary racial character and the gamble of
declaring war on the US in December 1941 ("the most serious of Hitler's
blunders") were his. The Holocaust, in Payne's judgment, "served no military
purpose and in fact weakened the German war effort" — the primacy of
ideology over utility ([[the-holocaust]]). This reading contrasts with
Flynn's opportunist portrait above; both are attributed.

**Griffin's account (1991)**: [[griffin-nature-of-fascism-1991|Griffin]]
sides with Payne against the pure-opportunist reading, but locates the
difference in temperament and praxis rather than doctrine: the NSDAP's 1920
program was ideologically unoriginal völkisch fare — "what made this party
different was that its rising star, Hitler, had no illusions about the chasm
which separated wishful thinking from reality" ("an idea is of no value, as
long as the wish does not turn into action"); "it is this determination that
marked Hitler out temperamentally as a fascist rather than a proto-fascist."
Griffin treats Hitler's palingenetic conviction as psychologically genuine —
Fest ("the concept of a great turning point in the history of the world"),
Rauschning ("the prophet of the rebirth of man in a new form... 'It is even
more than a religion: it is the will to create mankind anew'"), and the
political testament dictated hours before suicide ("the seed will inevitably
grow once again... which will lead to the glorious rebirth of the National
Socialist Movement") — "once again palingenetic myth had shown its power to
make death seem a rite de passage to a new life." Contrast with Mussolini:
armed with a myth that translated readily into strategy, Hitler showed
"little of the hesitancy or aimlessness" of the duce, made no Lateran-style
compromises, and ruthlessly suppressed rival factions. See
[[palingenetic-ultranationalism]].

**Evans on the peacetime dictator (2005)**:
[[evans-third-reich-in-power-2005|Evans]] documents the mechanics of rule
1933–39. Working style: a "Bohemian" irregular schedule, avoidance of
paperwork, problems left to "mature" (Speer) — but Evans rejects the
merely-reactive picture: in the domains Hitler cared about (foreign policy,
war, racial policy, visual art/architecture) he laid down the parameters
personally, and "it was without question Hitler, personally, who drove
Germany towards war from the moment he became Reich Chancellor" (8 February
1933: all job creation judged by rearmament; February 1934: living-space
within ~8 years). Personal decisions Evans documents across the volume: the
merger of chancellorship and presidency at Hindenburg's death and the
personal army oath (August 1934); the tempo of antisemitic policy throttled
and released from the top (Olympic pause; the 13 September 1937 restart; the
9 November 1938 pogrom instruction and 10 November stop decree; the veto of
badge and ghetto in December 1938); the sterilization of Black German
children on his authority alone (1937); the retroactive death penalty for
the motorway robbers; the assumption of supreme military command
([[blomberg-fritsch-affair-1938]]). Evans's synthesis is intentionalist with
qualifications — Hitler sometimes followed (Göring on Austria) and sometimes
yielded (Munich, which left him feeling "cheated of the war") — and ends with
self-mythologization: "a sleepwalker guided by Providence," whose first
serious miscalculation was Britain's declaration of war. Finances: the
renounced salary was propaganda; Mein Kampf royalties (1.2m RM in 1933),
stamp royalties, the business "donation," and a written-off 400,000-RM tax
bill made him rich, and his position above the law seeded the regime's
systemic corruption.

**Evans, *The Coming of the Third Reich* (2003) — formation and 1933
(attributed):** the Vienna education in [[schonerer-georg-von|Schönerer]]'s
doctrine and [[lueger-karl|Lueger]]'s technique; the abstract-to-visceral turn
of his antisemitism at war's end ([[hitler-pasewalk-1918]]); the September
1919 "antisemitism of reason" letter ("the removal of the Jews altogether");
the escalating exterminationist rhetoric of 1920–23 ("when we get our hands on
power: 'Then God have mercy on you!'"); and the artist-politician's cultural
vendetta — modernism as "morbid excrescences of insane and degenerate men,"
personal removal of Nolde paintings from Goebbels's house, and the reply to
Carl Bosch's protest at the scientific purges: Germany "could get on for
another hundred years without any physics or chemistry at all." Evans also
documents the pattern of Hitler restraining anti-Jewish "individual actions"
(10 March 1933) only where they threatened economic or diplomatic interests —
never on principle. See [[mein-kampf]] for Evans's anti-"blueprint" reading.

**Evans, *The Third Reich in History and Memory* (2015) — person-level findings (attributed):**

- **Health and sanity (ch. 9, reviewing Eberle & Neumann, *Was Hitler
  Ill?*, endorsing Redlich 1998).** The long catalogue of medical
  speculation — Jewish ancestry, syphilis, psychosis, Machtan's
  homosexuality thesis, genital mutilation legends — is debunked point by
  point. Documented facts: 1918 mustard-gas blindness; benign vocal polyp
  (removed 1935, again Nov 1944); chronic irritable bowel; Parkinsonian
  tremor and shuffling gait from 1941 (briefly stopped by the Stauffenberg
  bomb blast); severe dental decay (Blaschke's 50 kg of gold "quite
  possibly" including camp victims' dental gold). Dr. Morell
  ("Reichsspritzenmeister" — ~28 pills/day by war's end) was a qualified
  physician within period standards, not the cause of Hitler's decline;
  Vitamultin "may have included methamphetamine" (the manic July 1943
  Mussolini meeting) but no addiction is evidenced. Evans's verdict:
  Hitler was "sane according to any reasonable definition of the term,
  and fully responsible for his actions" — against the self-exculpatory
  postwar German myth of the mad hypnotist and his dupes.
- **Eva Braun and the private-sphere alibi (ch. 10, reviewing
  Görtemaker).** The first scholarly Braun biography disproves the
  entourage's claim that Hitler's private world was sealed off from
  politics: Braun (Hoffmann's studio assistant from 1929; two suicide
  attempts, 1932 and 1935, that cemented the relationship) became the
  Berghof's de facto hostess, followed the war closely, and in the final
  phase reinforced Hitler's refusal to admit defeat — Evans: she
  "undoubtedly contributed to hundreds of thousands of deaths in the last
  weeks of the war." Bunker marriage 29 April 1945; joint suicide 30
  April.
- **Berlin (ch. 5, reviewing Friedrich).** Hitler never reconciled with
  actual Berlin ("turned into a pigsty by the Jews"; flew in during the
  Kapp Putsch in a false beard, too late) — he preferred Munich, and his
  post-1933 plan was to replace Berlin with an imagined "Germania," not
  to embrace it.
- **War decisions (chs. 18–21, reviewing Kershaw and Kennedy).**
  Kershaw's *Fateful Choices*: the Barbarossa decision was "strategically
  forced upon him" (beat Stalin's buildup and American entry); the
  declaration of war on the US was "not fateful after all" given the
  undeclared Atlantic war. On extermination: no single order exists, but
  the day after declaring war on the US Hitler told Nazi leaders "the
  world war is here. The annihilation of the Jews must be the necessary
  consequence" — "the decision had clearly been made, and it had been
  made by Hitler" (Kershaw/Evans). Evans partly defends the Kiev
  diversion of August 1941 (grain, Crimean airbases vs. Romanian oil) as
  not irrational, while noting the underlying "Jewish-Bolshevik clique"
  collapse fantasy was. Hitler's Darwinist administrative style (rival
  appointees, the Me262 fiasco) crippled war production; he ordered Kiev
  razed and its people starved ("Kiev must starve").

Source: [[evans-third-reich-history-memory-2015]]

## Decline, withdrawal, and the radicalized home front — Evans, *The Third Reich at War* (2008)

From 1942 Hitler withdrew from the public: 9 speeches in 1940, 2 in 1943, effectively none in person after 24 February 1944 (Himmler read the 1944 Munich anniversary speech). ECGs from 1941 showed progressive coronary sclerosis; chronic stomach cramps from 1943; a worsening left-side tremor and stooped gait symptomatic of Parkinson's, acknowledged even by Theo Morell ("the Reich Master of Injections" — Göring), who had him on 28+ pills a day. Speer's train anecdote (7 November 1942) — Hitler ordering the blinds drawn on a wagon of wounded soldiers — stands for the sealed-off command style: he refused to visit the front or the bombed cities, bullied the General Staff, and insisted the map divisions were at full strength. Governance fragmented around the vacuum: the Committee of Three died of Goebbels's and Speer's hostility; Himmler took the Interior Ministry (August 1943); Bormann ("Secretary of the Leader," April 1943) controlled access; 42 Gauleiter Reich Defence Commissioners fed a new radicalized localism; Ley's Block Wardens surveilled households monthly. Wartime justice radicalized in parallel: 40+ capital offences by 1940, 16,560 death sentences over the Reich's life (15,896 of them wartime; the People's Court alone over 5,000), Hitler personally phoning the Justice Ministry to demand prisoners be "shot while trying to escape," and the Thierack–Himmler deal of 18 September 1942 handing 20,000+ "asocial" prisoners to the SS for extermination through labor at Mauthausen. Also documented: Georg Elser's lone Bürgerbräukeller bomb (8 November 1939) missing him by ~20 minutes — read by Hitler as Providence. Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## Downfall — Evans (2008), Part 7

After the Bulge failed he told his adjutant: "I know the war is lost . . . Most of all, I'd like to put a bullet through my own head," but "We'll not capitulate. Never. We can go down. But we'll take a world with us." Physically he was, in Speer's words, "shrivelling up like an old man"; the March 1945 **Nero Order** commanded scorched earth inside Germany with an openly Social-Darwinist rationale — the nation "had failed in the struggle for the survival of the fittest" — and was sabotaged in practice by [[speer-albert|Speer]] and sympathetic commanders. Roosevelt's death (12 April) briefly revived the Frederick-the-Great fantasy Goebbels fed him from Carlyle. The **Political Testament** (29 April 1945) is, in Evans's words, a scarcely veiled confession-boast of the genocide: "the real people to blame for this murderous struggle would be: the Jews! . . . the real guilty parties would also have to expiate their guilt, even if by more humane means," closing with the charge to "resist pitilessly the world-poisoner of all peoples, international Jewry." He married Eva Braun in the bunker in the early hours of 29 April and shot himself on 30 April 1945; the burned remains were identified by Soviet investigators through his dental bridges. Source: [[evans-third-reich-at-war-2008]]

## Griffin (2007): Hitler as Political Modernist

*Modernism and Fascism* reframes Hitler as the paradigmatic **programmatic modernist** of the right: "Though it has nothing aesthetically modernist in style or form, *Mein Kampf* has claims to be one of the most important manifestos of political modernism of the twentieth century." Its ch. 10 diagnosis of the diseased Volkskörper (materialism, "mammonization," syphilis, Cubism/Dadaism as "morbid excrescences of insane and degenerate men") is the standard modernist decadence-catalogue; its remedy is Osborne-style reactionary modernism verbatim: "The meaning and purpose of revolutions is not to tear down the whole building, but to remove what is bad or unsuitable and to continue building on the sound spot that has been laid bare." Griffin's supporting texture: Hitler as "the ultimate Nazi modernizer" who refused to let Nazism become a völkisch religion ("no mystical gloom of a cathedral, but the brightness and light of a room or hall"); his 1937 House of German Art speech proclaiming "The new age of today is at work on a new human type" while damning modernist painters as "prehistoric daubers" — the building itself boasting gas-fired heating, air-conditioning, and an air-raid shelter ("an alternative modernity... an alternative modernism"); his endorsement of steel, iron, glass, and concrete; his enthusiasm for television and the cine camera; his Wagner passion and Autobahn patronage. On religion, Griffin follows Ryback ("Hitler was the classic apostate") against Steigmann-Gall's positive-Christianity thesis; Kershaw's "working towards the Führer" and Stevens' shamanic-leader analysis (Nazism's full parodic religious apparatus, "its Promised Land (the East)") supply the charisma mechanics. Michaud highlights the founders/bearers/destroyers-of-culture triad and Hitler's invocation of Christianity's "fanatical intolerance" as licence for culture-destruction — and thereby for destroying a people's "moral right to exist." The Zitelmann "modernizer" controversy is resolved via Fritzsche: Hitler was a modernist, not a modernizer — the racial purge was not a side-effect but the core. (Source: [[griffin-modernism-and-fascism-2007]].)

## Payne (1980): the movement and the seizure of power

From [[payne-fascism-comparison-definition-1980]] (chs. 3–4): by 1925–26 the NSDAP was "incontestably the 'Hitler movement,'" subordinated to the Führer myth-cult "in a manner incomprehensible to Italian Fascism," whereas Fascism grew up around and partly against Mussolini. After the Beer-Hall failure Hitler learned what Mussolini had intuited: "a multiclass nationalist movement must come to power legally or not at all," via coalition with right-wing nationalists. The Twenty-Five Points' partial collectivism was never repudiated (the land-expropriation point dropped by 1928); the blue-collar "leftist tactic" was abandoned 1927–28 for genuine multiclass mobilization; "National Socialism means peace" was the 1932 slogan and antisemitic propaganda was toned down in the mass-electoral phase — "Hitler did not campaign on a platform of beginning World War II in 1939 and cremating Jews." Membership data (Payne's Table 3): one-third blue-collar at the seizure of power, over half non-capital-owning employees; the SA two-thirds blue-collar in 1932–34; heavy voting support came from rural farmers, small-town middle classes, and the urban *upper* classes, not disproportionately the urban Mittelstand — generational revolt "seems to explain the Nazi Party membership as well as any other single factor." On the seizure of power Payne weighs accident, agent, and frustrated-nationalism theories (see [[theories-of-fascism]]), preferring the last while conceding timing and luck: the Nazi vote was falling by late 1932, and with better German leadership "Hitler might well have been resisted one more year." Hitler's own view of generic fascism was "more firm, practical, and consistent" than Mussolini's: from the March on Rome he held that Fascism and National Socialism were "historical equivalents in their respective countries," making Italy Germany's natural ally (hence conceding Alto Adige) — while refusing the "fascist" label for Nazism itself, whose core was race; by 1928 the NSDAP was among the nationalist groups subsidized by the Italian state.

## Shirer (1960): Mein Kampf — economics, income, and the blueprint claim

From the Eher Verlag royalty statements seized in 1945 (Shirer 1960, ch. 4): sales of 9,473 (1925) declining to 3,015 (1928), rising with the party's fortunes to 54,086 (1930) and 90,351 (1932), then one million copies in 1933 — with the royalty rate raised from 10 to 15 per cent from 1 January 1933, giving Hitler over a million marks that year, "the most prosperous author in Germany and for the first time a millionaire"; six million copies sold by 1940. Royalties were his chief income from 1925. Editorial helpers: Rudolf Hess (dictation), Father Bernhard Stempfle, and Josef Czerny, who scrubbed embarrassing passages from the second printing. On strategy, Hitler's own retrospective (table talk, 3–4 Feb 1942): "Without my imprisonment Mein Kampf would never have been written… It's from this time, too, that my conviction dates… that we could no longer win power by force." Shirer's blueprint argument — that the book publicly announced the eastern empire ("If we speak of soil in Europe today, we can primarily have in mind only Russia and her vassal border states"; see [[lebensraum]]), the Führerprinzip state, and the racial program, so that "no one can accuse him of not putting down in writing exactly the kind of Germany he intended to make" — remains the classic intentionalist statement. Shirer, who heard Hitler speak on the "folkish state" dozens of times, admitted he "never clearly understood" what it meant, and doubted Hitler ever defined it. Source: [[shirer-rise-fall-third-reich-1960]]
