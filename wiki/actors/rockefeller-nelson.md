# Nelson A. Rockefeller

Nelson Aldrich Rockefeller (1908–1979) was the most politically prominent grandson of [[actors/rockefeller-john-d|John D. Rockefeller, Sr.]] and the second of the six children of [[actors/rockefeller-john-d-jr|John D. Rockefeller, Jr.]] and Abby Aldrich. Named for his maternal grandfather, Senator Nelson Aldrich, he was the one flamboyant publicity-seeker among Junior's disciplined children — as a Dartmouth undergraduate he wrote an honors thesis (coached by William O. Inglis) defending [[actors/standard-oil|Standard Oil]] as having treated competitors "with extreme fairness." He became a four-term Governor of New York (1959–1973), an architect of mid-century infrastructure and university expansion, repeated contender for the Republican presidential nomination, and the 41st Vice President of the United States (1974–1977) under Gerald Ford. In [[sources/caro-the-power-broker-1974|Robert Caro's *The Power Broker*]] he appears as the governor who finally broke Robert Moses's hold on New York's public authorities.

This page exists primarily as a network node linking the Rockefeller dynasty across the [[sources/chernow-titan-rockefeller-1998|Chernow *Titan*]] and Caro sources; his full career sits outside the present ingest's biographical core (which centers on his grandfather).
