# Göktürk Khaganate

A Turkic steppe empire founded in 552 CE by Bumin Qaghan after the Göktürks'
revolt against their Rouran overlords, at its height controlling a vast arc
of Central Asia from Manchuria to the Black Sea steppe. The first great
imperial power to unify most of the Turkic-speaking steppe peoples, it
formalized the *khaganate* as a political model that later steppe empires
(including the later Turkic and Mongol states) would inherit.

## Role and Significance

The Göktürks allied with the Sasanian Empire to destroy the Hephthalite
("White Hun") confederation in the 550s–560s, then split into rival Eastern
and Western khaganates whose rivalry, alliance-seeking with Byzantium and
Persia, and control of Silk Road oases made them central actors in
sixth-to-eighth-century Eurasian diplomacy. Internal succession conflicts
and Tang Chinese military pressure fragmented Göktürk power repeatedly; a
Second Göktürk Khaganate (682–744) briefly restored unity before falling to
the Uyghur-led steppe coalition that succeeded it.

## Counterfactual Significance

Göktürk destruction of Hephthalite power reshaped the balance between Persia,
Byzantium, and the steppe for a century; Göktürk control (and later loss) of
the Silk Road's central Asian corridor directly affected the flow of trade
and diplomacy between Byzantium, Persia, and Tang China.

## Historiography

Göktürk history is reconstructed from Chinese dynastic histories, Byzantine
diplomatic accounts (notably Menander Protector), and the Göktürks' own
Orkhon runic inscriptions (eighth century) — among the earliest native
Turkic-language sources. The collection's coverage of Central Asian steppe
history in this period is thin relative to its strength on Byzantium,
Persia, and China individually.
