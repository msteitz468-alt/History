# The Yuezhi

## Overview

The **Yuezhi** were a pastoral-nomadic confederation whose forced westward migration
in the second century BCE is, in [[benjamin-empires-ancient-eurasia-2018|Benjamin's]]
account, the chain-reaction that *opened* the [[silk-roads|First Silk Roads Era]]. Driven
from the Gansu Corridor by the [[xiongnu]], they conquered Hellenistic [[bactria|Bactria]],
declined a Han alliance carried by [[zhang-qian|Zhang Qian]], and — three or four
generations later — reconstituted themselves as the [[kushan-empire|Kushan Empire]], one
of the four great powers of the era. Their ancestors were part of the Bronze Age
Indo-European-speaking pastoral migrations from the Caucasus into Inner Asia; Benjamin
tentatively links the oldest Tarim Mummies (c. 1800 BCE) to them, though the
ethno-linguistic identification is unresolved. They spoke Tocharian (an Indo-European
branch) before their Kushan descendants adopted Bactrian.

## Role and Significance

Before [[modu-chanyu|Modu's]] rise the Yuezhi were the dominant steppe power on China's
northwest frontier, fielding **100,000 mounted archers** and treating the Xiongnu "with
disdain"; they grew wealthy trading **jade, horses, camels, and composite bows** with the
[[zhou-dynasty|Zhou]] (named in the 7th-century-BCE *Yi Zhou Shu* and *Guanzi*). The
**counterfactual significance** is the book's: had the Yuezhi accepted Zhang Qian's
proposal c. 128 BCE and returned east to fight the Xiongnu, "there would have been no
Kushan Empire" — and arguably no First Silk Roads Era. Their conquest of Bactria is also
a **historiographical milestone**: the first event in world history recorded
simultaneously by Western (Greco-Roman) and Eastern (Chinese) historians.

## The Migration (c. 166 – c. 130 BCE)

- **c. 166 BCE** — the Xiongnu under Modu inflict a final, decisive defeat; the Yuezhi
  king is killed and his skull made into a Xiongnu drinking cup. `caused_by: [[xiongnu]]`
- **First stage** — c. 1,900 km northwest into the **Ili River Valley** near Lake Issyk
  Kul, where they settle for ~30 years until a Wusun–Xiongnu attack c. 133 BCE routs them.
- **Second stage** (c. 133–130 BCE) — west through the Ferghana Valley and **Kangju**
  (modern Uzbekistan), then south to the river valleys draining into the **Amu Darya
  (Oxus)**.
- **c. 130 BCE** — they defeat resident Saka and take control of **Greco-Bactria** south
  of the Oxus, ruling (as nomads typically did, and as the [[genghis-khan|Mongols]] would
  1,300 years later) **through subordinate local chiefs**. The displacement set off a
  domino effect pushing Saka groups into Seistan and the Indus/Punjab.

## From Confederation to Empire

By the early-to-mid first century BCE the Yuezhi had split — for unknown reasons — into
**five princedoms** (*xihou*/*yabghu*) across Bactria, becoming semi-sedentary, urbanized,
and even minting coins in imitation of Greco-Bactrian issues. The graves at **Tilya-tepe**
("Golden Hill," excavated 1978), with ~20,000 syncretic gold artifacts and a denarius of
Tiberius, may belong to one princedom (identification contested). In the mid-first
century CE the ruler of the **Guishuang** princedom, [[kujula-kadphises|Kujula Kadphises]],
reunified the five and founded the Kushan Empire — see [[kushan-empire]].

## Historiography

The Yuezhi left **no written records of their own**; their history is reconstructed
entirely from Chinese ([[han-dynasty|Shiji, Han Shu]]), Tibetan, Persian, and
Greco-Roman sources, plus archaeology (Khalchayan, Tilya-tepe). [CONTROVERSY] Their
linguistic/ethnic identity, the cause of the five-fold split, the locations of the five
*xihou*, and the Tilya-tepe identifications all remain disputed among specialists. The
[[xiongnu]]→Yuezhi→[[kushan-empire|Kushan]] sequence is the spine of Benjamin's argument
that **nomadic intermediaries**, not just agrarian empires, made the Silk Roads.
