# The Almohads (al-Muwahhidun)

The **Almohads** (Arabic *al-muwahhidun*, "those who affirm the unity [of God]") were
the second Berber empire to rule the Maghreb and Muslim Iberia, succeeding the
[[almoravids|Almoravids]] in the mid-twelfth century. [[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]]
(Hugh Kennedy, Ch. 16) contrasts them with their predecessors as a movement of
**religious** as well as military leadership.

## Origins and Empire

The movement was founded among the **Masmuda Berbers** of the Atlas by **Ibn Tumart**
(d. c. 1130), a charismatic reformer who proclaimed himself the *mahdi* and preached a
rigorous doctrine of divine unity (*tawhid*) against what he condemned as the
anthropomorphism and laxity of the Almoravids. Under his successor **Abd al-Mu'min**,
the Almohads conquered the Almoravid Maghreb — taking **Marrakesh in 1147** — and built
an empire stretching from Tripolitania to the Atlantic, the first to unite the whole of
North Africa west of Egypt under a single indigenous power.

## Rule in al-Andalus

Crossing into Spain after the collapse of Almoravid authority and a second taifa
period, the Almohads reunited al-Andalus under their rule and defeated Castile at
**Alarcos (1195)**, a serious Christian reverse. But a coalition of the Iberian
Christian kingdoms, summoned as a [[crusades|crusade]] by the papacy, decisively broke
Almohad power at **Las Navas de Tolosa (1212)** — the turning point that opened the
great thirteenth-century [[reconquista|reconquests]] (Córdoba 1236, Seville 1248). The
Almohad empire then fragmented, surviving in Morocco until the Marinids took Marrakesh
in 1269.

## Culture

Despite their rigorism, the Almohad period saw a high point of Andalusi intellectual
life: the philosopher **Ibn Rushd (Averroes, 1126–98)** and the physician-philosopher
**Ibn Tufayl** served the Almohad court, and Averroes' Aristotelian commentaries —
written in Almohad Córdoba and Seville — would shape the Latin
[[twelfth-century-renaissance|universities]]. The Almohads were also patrons of
monumental architecture (the Giralda of Seville, the Kutubiyya of Marrakesh).

## Significance

The Almohads were the last great Berber empire to dominate Iberia; their defeat at Las
Navas de Tolosa (1212) marked the irreversible turning of the [[reconquista|Reconquista]]
in the Christians' favour and the beginning of the end of al-Andalus.
