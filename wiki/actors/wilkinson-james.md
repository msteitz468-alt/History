## Role and Background

James Wilkinson served as senior Commanding General of the United States Army for much of the period from the 1790s to the 1810s while, unknown to his superiors until long afterward, secretly drawing a pension as "Agent 13" of the Spanish crown, feeding Madrid intelligence and advocating policies favorable to Spanish interests in the trans-Appalachian West.

## Burr Conspiracy (1806–1807)

Wilkinson was [[actors/burr-aaron|Aaron Burr's]] principal co-conspirator in the ambiguous scheme — variously alleged to involve detaching western territory, invading Spanish Mexico, or both — that unraveled in 1806. When the plan's exposure threatened his own position, Wilkinson abruptly turned against Burr, alerting [[actors/jefferson-thomas|President Jefferson]] and becoming the chief prosecution witness at Burr's 1807 treason trial, testimony [[actors/john-marshall|Chief Justice Marshall's]] court treated with considerable skepticism.

## Significance

Wilkinson's decades-long double game as a senior US officer secretly in Spanish pay, followed by his self-serving betrayal of Burr, made him one of early American history's most persistently ambiguous and distrusted senior military figures, though his Spanish connections remained unproven in his lifetime and were only conclusively documented from Spanish archives long after his death.
