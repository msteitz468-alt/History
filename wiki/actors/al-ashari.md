# Al-Ashʿarī

Abū al-Ḥasan al-Ashʿarī (874–936), the founder of the **[[asharism|Ashʿarī]] school** of Sunni theology
and the most influential figure in classical [[kalam]]. Trained as a [[mutazilism|Muʿtazilite]], he
famously broke with the school around the age of forty and turned its own rationalist dialectical methods
to the defense of traditionalist Sunni doctrine — affirming the reality of God's attributes, the
uncreated [[quran|Qur'an]], and the vision of God in the hereafter, while accepting them "without asking
how" (*bilā kayfa*).

By using reasoned argument to defend revealed doctrine, al-Ashʿarī forged a middle path between the
rationalism of the Muʿtazila and the anti-rationalism of the strict traditionists — a synthesis that,
through followers like [[al-baqillani|al-Bāqillānī]], [[al-juwayni|al-Juwaynī]], and [[al-ghazali|al-Ghazālī]],
became the dominant theological orientation of Sunni Islam. (In the east, the parallel
[[al-maturidi|Māturīdī]] school played a comparable role.)
