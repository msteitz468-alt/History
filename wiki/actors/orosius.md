# Paulus Orosius

A Spanish priest and disciple of **Augustine** (c. 375–after 418), author of the
*[[fear-orosius-history-against-pagans|Seven Books of History against the Pagans]]* — the **first
Christian universal history** and the most widely read history-book of the Middle Ages.

## Life

Born probably at **Braga** (Bracara) in Roman Gallaecia (north-west Spain), Orosius was a young priest
who fled the barbarian incursions into Spain and reached North Africa c. 414, attaching himself to
**Augustine of Hippo**. Augustine sent him east to **Jerome** in Palestine (415), where he became
embroiled in the controversies over **Pelagianism** and **Priscillianism/Origenism** (writing his
*Commonitorium* and a *Liber apologeticus*). He attended the synod of Jerusalem (415) and returned to
the West; nothing certain is known of him after c. 418.

## The Work

At **Augustine's request** — as a documentary supplement to the *City of God* — Orosius wrote the
*Historiae adversus paganos* (c. 417), a universal history from the Flood to his own day. Its purpose
was **apologetic**: to refute the pagan charge that the abandonment of the old gods, and Christianity,
had brought disaster on Rome (above all the **[[sack-of-rome-410|sack of 410]]**). Orosius answered by
piling up the catastrophes of the **pre-Christian past** to show that earlier ages were far bloodier,
and by reading history as **providentially guided** toward the Christian Roman empire.

## Significance

Orosius created the template of **[[christian-providential-historiography|Christian providential
history]]** — universal in scope, theological in meaning, organized around the four world-empires and
the divine plan. Though Augustine's own theology was subtler and less triumphalist, it was **Orosius's
optimistic, providential synthesis** that medieval Europe absorbed: his *Histories* survived in some
250 manuscripts, were translated (into Old English under King Alfred, and into Arabic), and supplied the
Middle Ages with much of its picture of ancient history and geography.
