# Theoderic the Great

King of the **Ostrogoths** (r. 493–526) and ruler of **Italy**, the most powerful and "Roman" of the
barbarian successor kings — a man who governed the old western heartland for a generation with a continuity
of Roman administration that made his reign a byword for prosperity, yet whose **Arianism** and Gothic
kingship left him unassimilable to the Roman order he preserved. Treated in [[cameron-cah-v14-2000|CAH XIV]]
Ch. 5 (**Ian Wood**) and Ch. 3 (Cameron).

## From the Balkans to Italy

Theoderic the **Amal** emerged from forty years of confused Gothic politics in the eastern Balkans, where a
very mixed Germanic population had subsisted under rival leaders — including his rival **Theoderic Strabo**.
Only after Strabo's son Recitach was murdered (an act Theoderic arranged for the emperor **Zeno**) were the
various groups first united under one leader; the migration to Italy and four years of war then forged them
into a coherent, ethnically self-conscious people. Unable to secure a stable position in imperial service,
Theoderic was **persuaded by Zeno (in 488) to take his confederacy to Italy to dispossess [[odoacer|Odoacer]]**.

Forcing his way in (August 489), he defeated Odoacer repeatedly but could not take **Ravenna**, blockading
it until **493**, when a treaty of joint rule was agreed — whereupon Theoderic, once admitted, promptly
**murdered Odoacer and his family** at a banquet. The eastern emperor **Anastasius I** recognized him as
ruler of Italy around 497.

## The Ostrogothic kingdom of Italy

Theoderic's rule was marked by **remarkable continuity with the Roman past**. Families who had served
Odoacer kept their offices; the **senate** flourished and senators staffed the administration (the quaestor
**Cassiodorus** its great voice, **Boethius** consul and *magister officiorum*). He undertook a **programme
of public works** — aqueducts, baths, walls, palaces — restored the **corn dole** to the poor of Rome, made
a formal imperial-style *adventus* into Rome (500), and gave Italy a security and prosperity "previously
unmatched in the fifth century." Tax evasion aside, Ostrogothic Italy earned a reputation for plenty.

Abroad he built a **western-Mediterranean "co-prosperity sphere,"** binding the other Germanic kingdoms to
the Amals by marriage and arms: his sister married the **[[vandal-kingdom|Vandal]]** king Thrasamund; the
**Visigoths** of Spain came under his hegemony (he installed his grandson Amalaric after their defeat at
**Vouillé in 507**, and annexed Provence); the Thuringians, Heruls and Alamans were drawn into his orbit.
The fragility of this system showed when **Clovis's Franks** and imperial diplomacy combined against him.

## Boethius, succession, and the end

Not all Romans accepted permanent rule by "an Arian Germanic soldier." Such sentiment surfaced in the
charges against **Boethius**, accused (524) of shielding senators in treasonable correspondence with
Constantinople; he was condemned and **executed (524)**, along with his father-in-law **Symmachus** — the
episode immortalized by Boethius's *Consolation of Philosophy* and later imperial propaganda (though Wood
finds no evidence it alienated the aristocracy at the time).

Lacking a son, Theoderic married his daughter **Amalasuintha** to **Eutharic**, who predeceased him; at his
**death in 526** the throne passed to his ten-year-old grandson **Athalaric** under Amalasuintha's regency.
The minority unravelled: Athalaric died (534), Amalasuintha was forced to share power with **Theodahad**,
who had her imprisoned and murdered — giving [[justinian|Justinian]] the *casus belli* for **[[belisarius|Belisarius's]]**
invasion (535). The kingdom resisted heroically under **Vitigis**, then **Totila (Baduila)**, but was
destroyed in the long, ruinous **Gothic War** (Narses's victories at Busta Gallorum and Mons Lactarius,
552–553). No king was chosen after Teias; the Ostrogoths vanished as a people.

## Significance

Theoderic is the paradigm of the **Romano-barbarian king**: he preserved the Roman state in Italy more fully
than any contemporary, ruling as Zeno's nominal viceroy while in substance an independent monarch. His reign
shows both the **viability of the successor kingdoms** as continuations of Rome and their structural limit —
the unbridged divide between an **Arian, Gothic army** and a Catholic, Roman civilian society. His death and
the disputed succession opened Italy to Justinian's reconquest, whose devastation (and the **[[fall-of-the-western-roman-empire-476|Lombard]]**
invasion of 568) ended the prosperity he had built.

## Gibbon, *Decline and Fall* (1776–1788) — Ch. XXXIX

[[sources/gibbon-decline-and-fall-1776|Gibbon]] treats the ~50 years between Western fall and [[justinian|Justinian]] as faintly marked in the East, while Italy “revived and flourished under the government of a Gothic king, who might have deserved a statue among the best and bravest of the ancient Romans.”

- **Birth and education:** Fourteenth Amali in lineal descent; born near Vienna two years after Attila’s death; boy-hostage at Constantinople under Leo, yet remained illiterate — “a rude mark was contrived to represent the signature of the illiterate king of Italy.”
- **Conquest of Italy:** Entire-people migration; after battles and nearly three-year siege of Ravenna, “Odoacer, in the midst of a solemn banquet, was stabbed by the hand, or at least by the command, of his rival,” with massacre of mercenaries — crime Gibbon excuses as necessary to public felicity.
- **Land partition:** One-third of Italian lands to Gothic soldiers — “the sole injustice of his life,” justified by conquest and Odoacer’s example; Goths multiplied to ~200,000 men; freemen’s lands tax-exempt; Italians kept arts of peace, Goths the service of war.
- **Civil reign:** Rare Barbarian who “sheathed his sword in the pride of victory”; ~33 years of civil government; Gothic sovereignty from Sicily to the Danube, Sirmium to the Atlantic; maintained the Western balance until Clovis overthrew it.
- **Roman disguise:** Declined Imperial name, purple, and diadem yet assumed full prerogative; civil administration reserved to Italians — “It had been the object of Augustus to conceal the introduction of monarchy; it was the policy of Theodoric to disguise the reign of a Barbarian.”
- **Toleration and late crisis:** Arian indifference protected Catholics and Jews; late jealousy of Justinian’s anti-Arian law drove him to the brink of persecuting Catholics and to condemn [[boethius|Boethius]] and Symmachus — “the life of Theodoric was too long.”
- **Death legend:** After the executions, vision of Symmachus’s face in a fish-head; repentance to Elpidius; death by dysentery at Ravenna; hermit legend of his soul plunged into Lipari’s volcano.

## From Mitchell, Later Roman Empire (2015)

Attributed claims from [[sources/mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Stephen Mitchell, *A History of the Later Roman Empire* (2015)]].

- **Zeno redirects Theoderic to Italy; murder of Odoacer 493.** In 488 Zeno agreed Theoderic should take his Ostrogoths to overthrow Odoacer; warfare from Aug 488 through Adda (11 Aug 490) and a three-year Ravenna siege; dual-rule agreement brokered by the city’s bishop; “On March 5 493, Theoderic entered the city; and on the Ides of March he murdered Odoacar with his own hands. He was to rule Italy from Ravenna until 526.” Related: [[odoacer|Odoacer]].
- **Eastern insider, imperial manner without imperial title.** Zeno’s “son in arms,” *MVM praesentalis* and patricius 476, consul 484, Flavius name; Anastasius 497 returns *ornamenta palatii* — regal not imperial. Conduct imperial in manner (Rome visit 500; grain, walls) but avoids emperor title; Cassiodorus *Variae* and most inscriptions style him *rex*; Terracina Appian Way text calling him perpetual Augustus is “exaggerated aberration.” Procopius: “Although in name Theoderic was a usurper (*tyrannos*), yet in fact he was as truly an emperor as any who have distinguished themselves in this office.”
- **Roman law framework, not a Germanic code.** “It is revealing that neither Theoderic nor his successors issued a code of laws in their own name, as the other Germanic kingdoms did… They worked entirely within the Roman framework of government, using Roman officials, mostly senior members of the Senate, and operating with Roman law.” Kingdom conceived as part of the Roman Empire subordinate in protocol to the East — “glory of the Goths is to be guardians of civilisation” (*Var.* 9.14). Amal antiquity myth-historicized; real genealogy only to grandfather Valamir.
- **Gothic legitimacy claim vs Justinianic realpolitik (538).** During the 538 armistice Goths argued Zeno had charged consul/patricius Theoderic to oust usurper Odoacer and rule Italy lawfully; Anastasius had legitimized Theoderic in 509. Belisarius retorted that Theoderic failed to restore Italy to the emperor — Mitchell calls this “crudely swept aside by Justinianic realpolitik.” Related: [[justinian|Justinian]]; [[gothic-war-535-554|Gothic War]].
- **Dynastic diplomacy that failed at Vouillé.** Marriage to Clovis’s sister Audofleda; daughters to Alaric II and Sigismund; niece Amalaberga to Herminafrid; Boethius tasked with water-clock for Gundobad and cithara-player for Clovis. 506 flurry of letters failed to prevent Vouillé 507: “Neither the letters nor the diplomacy of the Ostrogothic envoys who carried them availed.”
