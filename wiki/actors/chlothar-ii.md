# Chlothar II

**The Merovingian king who reunited [[francia|Francia]] in 613** and, with his son
[[dagobert-i|Dagobert I]], presided over "the most peaceful, prosperous, and significant period of
Frankish history since the reign of [[clovis|Clovis]]." His reign marks the settlement between crown
and aristocracy on which the seventh-century kingdom rested.

## Reunification (613)

Son of Chilperic I and [[fredegund|Fredegund]], Chlothar ended the three-generation feud by defeating
and executing [[brunhild|Brunhild]] in 613 — a victory won only because the Burgundian and Austrasian
aristocracies, led by **Arnulf of Metz and Pippin of Herstal**, invited him in. He rewarded them at
once: **Warnachar** was named *maior domus* of Burgundy for life, on Chlothar's solemn oath never to
remove him; **Rado** was made *maior domus* of [[austrasia|Austrasia]].

## The Edict of Paris (614)

Chlothar issued a **24-article edict** promising to respect the traditional rights of the aristocracy,
the church, and the people, correcting abuses that had grown up during the wars. It guaranteed
**episcopal elections by clergy and people** with royal confirmation, forbade bishops from naming their
own successors, reserved clerical cases to ecclesiastical courts, corrected unjust tax rolls, and barred
royal tax collectors from infringing immunities. Its famous clause — "no judge from one province or
region shall be appointed in another" — was not, Geary argues, a revolutionary surrender of royal power
but a confirmation of "the intensely local character of Francia."

## Decisions and their mechanics

| Decision | Intended outcome | Actual effect |
|---|---|---|
| Execute Brunhild (613) | End the feud, win aristocratic backing | Reunified the kingdom; bound crown to magnate support |
| Mayoralties for life (Warnachar, Rado) | Reward and bind regional aristocracies | Entrenched the office of *maior domus* — the seed of later Pippinid power |
| Edict of Paris (614) | Restore order by guaranteeing customary rights | Stabilised the realm; institutionalised local, aristocratic, and episcopal autonomy |

## Counterfactual significance

Chlothar's settlement bought a generation of peace, but the price — hereditary, life-tenured mayoralties
and confirmed local autonomy — built the very machinery the [[pippinids|Pippinids]] would later use to
eclipse his dynasty. He is thus both the restorer of Merovingian unity and an unwitting architect of its
decline.

## Historiography

The reign is comparatively well served by the *Chronicle of Fredegar*; the Edict of Paris survives as a
key document of the Frankish "constitution." Later Carolingian-shaped sources colour the surrounding
narrative. See [[geary-before-france-germany-1988]].
