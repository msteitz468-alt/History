# Mikhail Gorbachev
## Biography / Description
Mikhail Sergeevich Gorbachev was born in 1931 in Privolnoye, near Stavropol, to a peasant family of mixed Russian and Ukrainian background. He studied law at Moscow State University, joining the Communist Party in 1952. He rose through the regional Party apparatus in Stavropol, eventually becoming a protégé of KGB chief Yuri Andropov and entering the Politburo. Following Andropov's and Chernenko's short tenures, Gorbachev was elected General Secretary of the Communist Party on March 11, 1985, at the age of 54. He sought to revitalize the stagnant Soviet state through radical reforms, but instead unleashed forces that led to the collapse of Eastern European communism and the Soviet Union itself. He resigned as President of the USSR on December 25, 1991, marking the formal end of the Soviet state. He died on August 30, 2022.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **Perestroika (Restructuring):** Gorbachev launched an ambitious program to modernize the Soviet economy, initially focusing on a "speed-up" (uskorenie) and labor discipline (such as the 1985 anti-alcohol campaign) before moving toward marketization, enterprise autonomy, and limited private business.
- **Glasnost (Openness):** To overcome bureaucratic inertia, Gorbachev permitted unprecedented freedom of speech and press. This led to a re-examination of Soviet history (including the crimes of Stalinism) and allowed citizens to openly criticize the government.
- **Democratic Reforms:** In 1989, Gorbachev established the Congress of People's Deputies, introducing competitive elections. In March 1990, he oversaw the abolition of Article 6 of the Soviet Constitution, ending the Communist Party's monopoly on political power, and was elected President of the USSR.
- **Non-intervention in Eastern Europe (1989):** Gorbachev decided against using military force to defend communist regimes in the Warsaw Pact, allowing the peaceful collapse of communism in Poland, Czechoslovakia, East Germany, Hungary, Romania, and Bulgaria, and accepting German unification.
- **Ending the Cold War:** Gorbachev established a close working relationship with US President Ronald Reagan and signed historic treaties to reduce nuclear arms. He also ordered the withdrawal of Soviet troops from Afghanistan in 1988–1989.
- **Union Treaty and the Coup (1991):** To prevent the disintegration of the USSR from rising nationalisms, Gorbachev negotiated a new Union Treaty. This prompted a hardline communist coup in August 1991, which collapsed within three days but fatally weakened his authority.

## Historical Significance
Gorbachev was one of the most consequential political figures of the twentieth century, awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1990. While his intention was to save and modernize the Soviet socialist system, his reforms destabilized it. Riasanovsky notes that Gorbachev repeatedly obtained results opposite to those he intended, severely underestimating the explosive power of nationalism in the Baltic and Transcaucasian republics (Line 7021, 7059). He remains a highly controversial figure: praised internationally for ending the Cold War without bloodshed, but blamed by many in Russia for the economic collapse and the loss of superpower status.
