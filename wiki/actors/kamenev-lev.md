# Lev Kamenev

Bolshevik revolutionary and senior Soviet official (1883–1936), a close early associate of Lenin who returned to Russia with him and Stalin in 1917 and served on the Military Revolutionary Committee during the October seizure of power. In the power struggle following Lenin's illness and death, Kamenev formed the "troika" with Stalin and Grigory Zinoviev (1922–1925) that sidelined Trotsky, before Kamenev and Zinoviev themselves broke with Stalin to form the "New Opposition," which was defeated at the 14th Party Congress (December 1925).

Kamenev was repeatedly expelled from and readmitted to the party through the late 1920s as factional struggles continued. He became a central defendant in Stalin's Great Purge show trials, tried alongside Zinoviev in the first Moscow Trial (August 1936) on fabricated charges of conspiracy and terrorism, and was executed shortly after conviction. A July 1928 note of a private conversation between Kamenev and Nikolai Bukharin, in which Bukharin denounced Stalin as an "unprincipled intriguer," was later obtained and weaponized by Stalin against the "Right Opposition."

## Significance

Kamenev's career traces the arc from Lenin's inner circle to victim of Stalin's purges, illustrating how the "troika" and factional alliances that helped Stalin consolidate power were themselves consumed by it.
