# Henry II (Holy Roman Emperor)

**Henry II** (973–1024; king from 1002, emperor from 1014), the last [[ottonian-dynasty|Ottonian]], was the Bavarian-line Liudolfing who turned the Reich away from Otto III's Roman dream and back toward a German-centred kingship. [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]] (Müller-Mertens) frames his reign under the slogan he himself adopted: ***renovatio regni Francorum*** — renewal of the *kingdom of the Franks* north of the Alps, not Otto III's *renovatio imperii Romanorum*.

## Accession

When Otto III died childless (1002), Henry — duke of Bavaria, great-grandson of Henry I — was the only surviving male Liudolfing. He won the crown against the rival candidacies of Hermann II of Swabia and Margrave Ekkehard of Meissen (the latter killed in a feud), securing recognition region by region through a perambulation of the realm. He regarded his accession providentially: his "Henrician" line, excluded from the throne in 936, had been **exalted by God after long humiliation**.

## A German-Focused Kingship

Henry reversed the orientation of Ottonian policy:

- **Italy and Rome:** his three Italian expeditions were short (he took the Lombard crown in 1004 against the anti-king **Arduin of Ivrea** and the imperial crown in 1014), and he scrupulously **refrained from interfering in Roman affairs**, reverting to Otto I's policy of balancing the Italian magnates.
- **The east:** he abandoned the long Ottonian effort to reconquer the lands of the Liutizi, and in a move that scandalised Saxon churchmen (Thietmar of Merseburg, Brun of Querfurt) **allied with the pagan Liutizi against the Christian** [[boleslaw-i-chrobry|Bolesław Chrobry]] of Poland. Three wars (1004–18) failed to dislodge Bolesław from the Lausitz and Milzener lands (conceded as fiefs at the **Peace of Bautzen, 1018**) but did prevent Poland's absorption of Bohemia and preserved Ottonian overlordship there.

## The Reichskirche at Its Apogee

Henry brought the **[[reichskirche|imperial church system]]** to its height: in his reign roughly two-thirds of episcopal vacancies were filled from the **royal chapel**; he invested candidates even against cathedral chapters' wishes and sometimes deleted free-election clauses from privileges. He extended immunities, regalian *bannus*, and whole counties to bishoprics, and promoted **monastic reform** (the Gorze observance) as an instrument of royal control — an "imperial monasticism." His central foundation was the new bishopric of **Bamberg (1007)**, richly endowed as a node of royal lordship on the north–south axis linking Saxony through Franconia to Italy, and his own burial place.

## Itinerant Government and the *Regnum Teutonicum*

Müller-Mertens shows Henry intensified the **itineracy** of kingship — shorter, more frequent stays across more of the realm — drawing the once-distant southern duchies (Bavaria, Swabia, Alsace) into "close zones" and curbing the quasi-regal ethnic dukedoms. By his reign contemporaries in Venice and southern Italy were calling him ***rex Teutonicorum*** — the first ruler perceived in his own lifetime as a "king of the Germans," even as he also claimed the *imperium Romanorum*.

## Death and Canonisation

Henry died childless in 1024 and was buried at Bamberg; the Ottonian line ended and the **Salians** succeeded. A cult grew at his tomb, leading to his **canonisation (1146)** and that of his wife **Kunigunde (1200)** — Henry remains the **only sainted German emperor**.
