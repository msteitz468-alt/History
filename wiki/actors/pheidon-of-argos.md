# Pheidon of Argos

Argive ruler used by [[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] Excursus I as a **cautionary tale** in reconstructing Archaic “big-men” and tyrants: Herodotus, Ephorus, Pausanias, and Olympic traditions conflict on date, deeds, and even genre of power. “Ultimately, there is not much to salvage here.”

## Role / traditional notices

- **Aristotle:** places Pheidon among tyrants arising from kingship, before mid-seventh-century *dêmos*-champions like Cypselus.
- **Herodotus (6.127.3):** father of Agariste’s suitor Leokedes (early sixth-century context); introducer of Peloponnesian measures; usurper of Olympic administration.
- **Ephorus (Strabo 8.3.33):** tenth from Temenos (~third quarter of eighth century); Temenid legacy recovered; measures/weights/coinage; forced Olympics.
- **Pausanias 6.22.2:** eighth Olympiad (748) with Pisatans.
- **Modern scholars** often emend to 28th Olympiad (668) with Hysiae 669 — without direct ancient authority.
- Other dates: Theopompus mid-ninth; Parian Marble 895/4; Plutarch/Eusebius early eighth. Notices span some **three centuries**.

## Why no single date works

Any chosen date excludes other activities:

- A tenth-generation Temenid Pheidon cannot mint earliest Aeginetan silver (turtles not before the sixth century); Herodotus mentions **measures**, not coinage.
- The Agariste-suitors list may be a chronological Who’s Who rather than a synchronized court.
- Early-sixth-century Argive hegemony ill fits Cypselid Corinth, Prokles of Epidaurus, and [[actors/cleisthenes-of-sicyon|Cleisthenes of Sicyon]]; Leokedes-as-suitor is hard to reconcile with Cleisthenes’ anti-Argive cult policy.

Hall: “It is not a question of filtering out less reliable information to reach a solid core of historical fact because every single one of our literary notices for Pheidon has its flaws.”

## Against a Pheidonian Peloponnesian empire

Hall rejects early Argive Peloponnesian “empire” under Pheidon:

- Argive Plain communities keep independence into the sixth century (Tiryns *damos*/*aliaia*; Mycenae *damiourgos* ~525; boards of Argive *damiourgoi* ~575–550 leave little room for a sixth-century autocrat).
- Eighth-century Argive population ~2,500 (~500 fighters) cannot sustain wide hegemony.
- Asine destruction ~720–710 under Eratos, not Pheidon.
- Mid-sixth-century hero-shrine of the Seven against Thebes in the Argive agora is ideological assertion of Argive primacy as Sparta rose over Tegea/Thyrea — context for inventing former dominance and Pheidon’s role.

## “Pheidonian measures” and the name

Aristotelian *Ath. Pol.* 10.1–2: Pheidonian measures smaller than Solon’s (mina raised 70→100 drachmas). The name Pheidon means “miser/stingy”; Hall asks whether association with “sparing” measures is coincidence and why commercially less-oriented Argos (vs Corinth/Aegina) set a Peloponnesian standard. Aristis of Kleonai names a father Pheidon ~560 (ML 9) — real name, weak support for historical Argive metrological role.

## Modern Hysiae package

Modern mid-seventh dating links supposed Olympic usurpation (emended Pausanias 28th Olympiad 668) with Argive victory at Hysiae (Paus. 2.24.7: 669) and sometimes Second Messenian War revolt — yet Pausanias does not name Pheidon at Hysiae; no independent testimony. Often folded into [[controversies/hoplite-revolution-debate]] narratives.

## Counterfactual significance

As a **secure agent** for causal history, largely unusable. As a **tradition**, Pheidon indexes later Argive claims to primacy, metrological folklore, and the difficulty of writing Archaic biography from composite notices. Related analytical frame: [[concepts/basileus-and-big-man]], [[concepts/greek-tyranny]].

## Historiography

[[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall, *Archaic Greek World* (2014)]] Excursus I — do not build a firm political narrative from the composite. Chronological charts that pick one notice and discard others systematically understate the problem.
