# Leonidas I

Agid king of Sparta (r. c. 489–480 BCE). Led the Greek rearguard of ~7,000 (including 300 Spartans and Thespians) at the Battle of Thermopylae (480 BCE) against Xerxes' massive Persian invasion force. Held the pass for three days, inflicting heavy casualties and buying time for the Greek fleet and evacuation preparations. Killed with his men in the final stand after a betrayal revealed the mountain path. Icon of Spartan courage, discipline, and sacrifice ("molon labe").

See: [[battle-of-thermopylae-480bce]], [[sparta]], [[greco-persian-wars-499bce]], [[classical-antiquity]].

## Hall (2014) — light touch

[[sources/hall-history-archaic-greek-world-2014|Hall (2014)]] stresses that Thermopylae was a **total defeat** despite Spartan death-and-glory propaganda centered on Leonidas and the 300: after the Anopaia path betrayal he kept 300 Spartans, suspect Thebans, and volunteer Thespians; delay and Persian casualties still mattered operationally for the reduced force that later sacked Boeotia and Attica. The mirage of unflinching Spartan constancy (see [[concepts/spartan-mirage|Spartan mirage]]) was already shaping how Thermopylae was remembered.
