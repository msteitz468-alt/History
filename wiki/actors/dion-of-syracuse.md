# Dion of Syracuse

## Overview

**Dion** (c. 408–354 BCE), brother-in-law and minister of **[[dionysius-i|Dionysius I]]**
and the close friend and disciple of **[[plato]]**, is the central figure of the
**Platonic experiment in Sicilian politics** — the man who tried to turn the Syracusan
tyranny into the rule of philosophy, liberated Syracuse from
**[[dionysius-ii|Dionysius II]]**, and was destroyed by the forces he unleashed.
[[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol. VI]] (Ch. 13, Westlake) tells his story largely through
Plutarch's *Dion* (drawing on Timaeus) and the Platonic *Letters*.

## The philosopher-king project

A man of high birth, wealth and Platonic conviction, Dion sought to educate the young
**[[dionysius-ii|Dionysius II]]** into the **philosopher-king** of Plato's *Republic*,
bringing **[[plato]]** himself to Syracuse. The attempt foundered on the suspicion and
dissipation of Dionysius, who **banished Dion** (366) and confiscated his great fortune.

## The liberation and its failure

In **357** Dion returned from exile with a **small mercenary force** and, to popular
acclaim, **drove Dionysius from Syracuse** — an astonishing feat that briefly made him
master of the city. But the liberation curdled. Dion's **aristocratic, doctrinaire**
manner alienated the *demos* that had welcomed him; accused of aiming at a tyranny of his
own, he grew autocratic and was drawn into faction and bloodshed. In **354** he was
**assassinated** by his own associate **Callippus**, an Athenian — and Sicily collapsed
into the decade of anarchy from which only **[[timoleon]]** would rescue it.

## Significance

Dion's career is the **practical test — and refutation — of Plato's political philosophy**.
The attempt to impose the rule of the wise from above, first by converting a tyrant and
then by force, ended in autocracy and murder. For Plato it was a personal tragedy (the
*Seventh Letter* is in part its apologia); for the wiki it is the clearest case of
[[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH VI]]'s thesis that **"much of Plato's political experience is
Sicilian experience."** Dion's failure left the field to Timoleon's very different,
republican solution.
