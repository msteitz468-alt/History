# White Huns (Hephthalites)

A nomadic or semi-nomadic confederation, of disputed ethnic and linguistic
origin (Iranian- and possibly Turkic-influenced groupings figure in the
scholarship), that dominated Central Asia, Bactria, and parts of northern
India from roughly the mid-fifth to mid-sixth centuries CE. Called
"White Huns" by Byzantine sources (to distinguish them from the Huns of
Attila, with whom they had no direct political connection), and Hephthalites
in Greek and Sasanian sources.

## Role and Significance

The Hephthalites inflicted severe, repeated defeats on the Sasanian Empire,
killing the shah Peroz I in battle (484 CE) and extracting tribute for
decades afterward — a humiliation that weakened Sasanian prestige and
finances at a critical period. They also invaded northwestern India,
contributing to the decline of the Gupta Empire. Their power was broken in
the 550s–560s by a Sasanian-Göktürk alliance, which partitioned Hephthalite
territory between the two victors.

## Counterfactual Significance

Sustained Hephthalite pressure diverted Sasanian military and financial
resources for generations, shaping Sasanian relations with Byzantium and
contributing to the Sasanians' later willingness to seek alliance with the
Göktürks against a common enemy.

## Historiography

Hephthalite history is reconstructed almost entirely from outside
sources — Sasanian, Byzantine, Chinese, and Indian — since no substantial
Hephthalite textual corpus survives; their ethnic and linguistic
classification remains genuinely disputed among specialists, reflecting the
broader thinness of the collection's Central Asian steppe coverage in this
period.
