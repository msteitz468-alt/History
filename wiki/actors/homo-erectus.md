## Overview

*Homo erectus* is the long-lived East Asian hominid, the **first really ancient
hominid ever discovered** ("Java Man," Trinil, 1890s; later "Peking Man,"
Zhoukoudian). In [[tattersall-world-from-beginnings-2008|Tattersall's]] reading it
is **not** a worldwide "stage" between australopiths and Neanderthals but a *local*
East Asian species that evolved after an [[homo-ergaster|*ergaster*]]-like ancestor
reached Asia in the first [[out-of-africa-1-1800kbp|diaspora]]. On the early dates
it had the longest run of any known hominid species — from ~1.8 mya to as late as
~40,000 BP on Java, plausibly ended by the arrival of *Homo sapiens*.

## Character

- A successful, persistent Asian form with a brain larger than *ergaster*'s but a
  low, long braincase, heavy brow, and projecting face.
- Associated in East Asia with relatively conservative tool kits; notably, Acheulean
  handaxes barely cross the **Movius Line** into eastern Asia.
- Most Javan specimens date ~1.0 mya–700 kya; one sample is dated as young as
  ~40,000 BP — close to the inferred arrival of modern humans in the archipelago.

## Significance (Counterfactual)

Its survival to ~40 kya makes it one of several non-*sapiens* hominids (with the
[[neanderthals]] and *Homo floresiensis*) that vanished around the time modern
humans spread — evidence Tattersall uses for replacement of archaic hominids by
*Homo sapiens* (see [[modern-human-origins-debate]]). Removing it from the "main
line" of descent is part of the larger move from a linear to a bushy
[[human-evolution|hominid phylogeny]].

## Historiography

The chief dispute is taxonomic: "lumpers" treat *H. erectus* as a single, vastly
distributed and variable species absorbing African [[homo-ergaster|*ergaster*]] and
even some European material; Tattersall and other "splitters" restrict it to East
Asia. The traditional reading of *erectus* as a universal evolutionary stage is what
this source most directly rejects. Multiregionalists invoke Javan and Chinese
*erectus* as direct ancestors of living Australasians and East Asians — a claim
rejected here (see [[origins-chinese-civilization-monocentric-vs-multiregional]]).
Dating on Java has itself been contested.
