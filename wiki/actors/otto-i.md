# Otto I "the Great"

**Otto I** (912–973, king of east Francia from 936, emperor from 962), the second [[ottonian-dynasty|Liudolfing]] king, is the central figure of the tenth-century Latin West: he revived the western empire, broke Magyar raiding for good, and built the institutional structure of the Reich. [[reuter-ncmh-v3-1999|NCMH III]] (Müller-Mertens) treats his reign as the hinge of the period.

## Accession and the Break with Consensual Kingship

Crowned and anointed at **Aachen in 936**, Otto pointedly took possession of **Charlemagne's throne**, signalling continuity with the Carolingian imperial centre. Unlike his father **Henry I**, who had ruled as first-among-equals through friendship-pacts, Otto asserted an **arbitrary, sacral kingship "by the grace of God,"** demanding submission rather than negotiating *amicitiae*. This provoked a decade of revolts (936–941) led by members of his own family — his half-brother **Thankmar** (killed 938), his brother **Henry**, and later his son **Liudolf** (953–954) — backed by aggrieved dukes. Otto rode them out, suppressing the Liudolf revolt only as the **Magyars** invaded in 954, and thereafter bound the duchies to the dynasty by **marriage and the appointment of kinsmen** (his brother Brun as archbishop-duke of Lotharingia).

## Lechfeld and Eastern Expansion

In 955 Otto met a great Magyar invasion besieging Augsburg and **annihilated it at the [[battle-of-lechfeld-955|Battle of the Lechfeld]]** (10 August), ending the nomadic raids that had terrorised Latin Europe for two generations; days later he crushed the **Abodrites** at the Recknitz. He pressed direct lordship over the **Polabian and Sorbian Slavs** east of the Elbe through the great marches of **Hermann Billung** and **Gero** and the **burgward** system (see [[elbe-slavs]]), and anchored eastern mission on the new archbishopric of **Magdeburg** (achieved 967/968 against the resistance of Mainz).

## Empire and the Reichskirche

Drawn into Italy by the appeal of the imprisoned queen **Adelaide** (whom he married) and then by **Pope John XII**, Otto conquered the kingdom of Italy and on **2 February [[coronation-of-otto-i-962|962]] received the imperial crown in Rome** — reviving the western empire after nearly four decades. He compelled the Romans to swear never to elect a pope without imperial consent, deposing John XII and installing his own popes. At home, building on his brother Brun, he forged the **[[reichskirche|imperial church system]]**: bishoprics and royal abbeys staffed from the royal chapel, holding regalian rights and rendering military and administrative service to the crown. His marriage of his son **Otto II to the Byzantine princess Theophanu** (972) won eastern recognition of his imperial title.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Otto, the survival of a unitary east Frankish realm — by no means assured c. 936 — and the revival of a *western* empire independent of Constantinople are both in doubt. Lechfeld converted the Magyars from existential raiders into a settling, soon-Christianising kingdom (see [[hungary-arpad|Hungary]]); the 962 coronation created the German-Italian imperial nexus that defined Central European politics for centuries; and the Reichskirche set the monarchy and the church on the collision course of the Investiture Contest.
