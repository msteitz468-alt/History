# Dominic de Guzmán (c. 1170–1221)

## Overview

**Dominic de Guzmán**, a Castilian canon, founded the **[[dominican-order|Order of Preachers]]** (Dominicans) in response to the failure of conventional clerical preaching against [[catharism|Cathar]] dualism in Languedoc. His order combined **mendicant poverty** with rigorous study and orthodox preaching — the intellectual counterpart to Franciscan popular evangelism.

## From Logan, History of the Church in the Middle Ages (2013)

Unlike [[francis-of-assisi|Francis]], Dominic was **well educated** in liberal arts and theology and already a **cleric in holy orders** when he entered public view (c.1171–1221). From **1206**, with bishop **Diego of Osma**, he adopted Cathar-like simplicity — barefoot preaching and public debates in Languedoc (Servian; two weeks at Montréal with some 150 reconversions) under a plan approved by [[innocent-iii|Innocent III]]. He founded a house for women converts (proto-Dominican nuns). Logan finds **no evidence** of Dominic’s involvement in the “tragic” [[albigensian-crusade-1209|Albigensian Crusade]], though he was friendly with **Simon de Montfort**’s family and continued preaching during the war’s turbulence. In April **1215** the bishop of Toulouse recognized Dominic’s preaching community; approaching Innocent just before [[fourth-lateran-council-1215|Fourth Lateran]], he received agreement in principle if an existing rule were adopted — **Rule of St Augustine** plus constitutions; **Honorius III** approved the order in **1216**.
