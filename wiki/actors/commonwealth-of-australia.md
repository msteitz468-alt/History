# Commonwealth of Australia

The federal nation-state inaugurated on 1 January 1901 by the union of six
self-governing British colonies, enjoying exclusive possession of an island
continent. Created by [[australian-federation-1901|Federation]], it combined a
constitutional monarchy, a bicameral legislature elected on a common franchise,
responsible executive government, and a written constitution dividing powers
between the Commonwealth and the six States.

## Overview

In 1901 the Commonwealth held 3.8 million people (0.23% of world population),
rising to 22.8 million by 2012. Government was both advanced and durable: the
1901 Constitution remains substantially unaltered, making Australia one of the
oldest continuous democracies. The early Commonwealth parliament built the
institutions of the [[australian-settlement]] — protective tariff, the
Arbitration Court and a "fair and reasonable" male wage, the old-age pension and
maternity benefit — while enacting the [[white-australia-policy]] that excluded
Asian and Pacific Island labour and denied the vote and welfare to
[[aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people]].

## Governing Structure

Constitutional monarchy with the British (later Australian) Crown as head of
state, represented by a Governor-General; a House of Representatives (preferential
voting from 1918, compulsory enrolment 1911, compulsory voting 1924) and an
elected Senate representing the States; High Court as constitutional arbiter.
A resilient two-party alignment (Labor vs. the Coalition of Liberal and
Country/National parties) consolidated over the century despite repeated splits.

## Economic Base

A resource-and-commodity export economy — wool, wheat, then minerals and energy —
overlaid by a protected manufacturing sector that expanded in the first half of
the century, peaked, and declined from the 1980s as protection was dismantled.
Services grew to commanding dominance. Per-capita income by the 2000s stood near
six times the 1901 level.

## Mechanisms of Change

External shocks repeatedly reshaped the state: World War 1 (60,000 dead),
the 1930s Depression, World War 2 (38,000 dead, total mobilisation), and the
1970s collapse of protection and Keynesian management. Each crisis extended
state capacity yet left the two-party system and federal framework intact.

## Historiography

Debated whether the founding arrangements constituted a lasting "Australian
Settlement" (Paul Kelly) condemning the nation to "insular mediocrity," or a
contested, repeatedly renegotiated exercise in nation building (the view of
several Vol. 2 contributors). See [[australian-settlement]].
