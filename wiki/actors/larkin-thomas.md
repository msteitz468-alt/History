# Thomas B. Larkin (1890–1968)

U.S. Army general and key logistics commander in the Mediterranean Theater during World War II. Played a central role in establishing and running the Services of Supply / base section system that supported Torch, the Tunisian campaign, and subsequent operations. Commanded Center Task Force SOS during the invasion; later commanded Mediterranean Base Section, then SOS NATOUSA.

## Background and Expertise

Commissioned from West Point 1915, entered Army Corps of Engineers. Served in Punitive Expedition in Mexico 1916 and with AEF in World War I (silver star for building bridge across Marne under heavy fire). Extensive civil engineering: district engineer for flood control at Vicksburg (1929–1933); headed construction of Fort Peck Dam (1933–1936); officer in charge of third locks project at Panama Canal (1939–1942). This background "had a direct connection with the demands of operating a theater-support organization."

## Role in Operation Torch and Theater Establishment

Commanded support forces for Center Task Force (Oran/Arzew landings). As Brigadier General, "Brigadier General Thomas Larkin commanded the Center Task Force SOS." Expected to operate independently under task force control initially, with possible later merger into theater SOS under AFHQ.

Following landings: Mediterranean Base Section (BS) arose November 10, 1942 with HQ at Oran; Larkin commanded. "The Mediterranean BS assumed responsibility for support of all forces in II Corps (the Center Task Force) as well as support of US forces operating in the area of the British First Army." Handled port operations (Oran), depots, civil affairs, construction, transportation, and coordination with Navy.

By early 1943: promoted to Major General; left Mediterranean BS to command the new SOS NATOUSA (activated ~Feb 12-15, 1943), an intermediate headquarters between theater and base sections. "The SOS had the responsibility to direct and overwatch all sustainment activities for the entire North African theater." Colonel Edmond Leavey assumed command of Mediterranean BS.

Larkin "held a slightly different interpretation" from Hughes on command: AFHQ intended SOS should have "command of all US Army supply and maintenance activities in the theater." Base sections should report direct to SOS. Tensions with AFHQ and communications zone command "lasted well into 1944." The SOS "handled the detailed planning of immediate support operations, the management of supplies, and the execution of all sustainment actions."

## Key Contributions and Context from Novice Phase

Oversaw initial response to beach/port chaos (mixed supplies, insufficient trucks, untrained labor). Supported rail reconstruction (locomotives shipped disassembled; reassembly in 76 hours by shop battalions) and TUP vehicle assembly lines at Oran (143 2.5-ton trucks in one day). Managed growing demands including French rearmament ("the French had more than 300,000 men under arms" requiring full US outfitting).

Larkin "sent a clear message to AFHQ: the SOS needed adequate resourcing, or future operations and the lives of thousands of soldiers would be at risk." His leadership exemplified the "personal interest in caring for the forces they supported" by base section and SOS commanders. Dworak credits such service force leaders with turning the theater from "novice" disarray after Torch into a coherent base capable of sustaining the final drive in Tunisia and follow-on campaigns.

## Range 02 Contributions (Husky Mounting / Italy Transition)
Larkin-era SOS structures enabled the "journeyman" phase. Eastern BS executed Husky mounting "while ... deeply involved in supporting the Allied drive across Tunisia." Depot at Mateur; "This was the first time the North African theater would mount a sizeable assault using its own forces". Loaded "77,520 personnel, 17,165 vehicles, and more than 12,000 tons" by July 5. "the SOS decided to consolidate ... within the Mediterranean BS" post-Husky; centralized G4 truck control.

Island BS (Sep 1 under Sears) and Peninsula BS (Pence Oct 2) formalized admin separation: "with the activation of the Island BS, the Seventh Army was now free to focus on any follow-on missions." "One lesson ... SOS personnel needed to arrive early ... and establish a formal base section as quickly as possible."

Naples port innovation (under theater sustainment system): Germans destroyed 69 of 72 berths; Allies built "piers over them"; 6th Port achieved "5,380 long tons" first day, >2.375M tons in 6 months. "Within thirty days ... sufficient throughput capability to support both the Fifth and Eighth Armies". "trusted officers, such as Tom Larkin" freed commanders from admin burden as theater matured.
