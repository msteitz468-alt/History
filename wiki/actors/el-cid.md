# El Cid (Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar)

**Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar** (c. 1043–1099), known as **El Cid** (from Arabic *al-sayyid*,
"the lord") and *El Campeador* ("the battler"), was the most famous warrior of the
eleventh-century [[reconquista|Reconquista]] and the emblematic figure of the era's
frontier society. [[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] (Simon Barton, Ch. 6, "Spain in the
eleventh century") presents his career as a paradigm of the fluid, mercenary,
**cross-confessional** warfare of the *taifa* age.

## Career

A Castilian noble who rose in the service of King Sancho II and then **Alfonso VI** of
[[christian-kingdoms-of-spain|León-Castile]], Rodrigo fell from royal favour and was
**exiled** (1081). Like many frontier warriors he then served Muslim paymasters,
fighting for years in the service of the *taifa* king of **Saragossa** against both
Christian and Muslim enemies — a vivid illustration that the eleventh-century frontier
was not a simple line of religious confrontation. After the [[almoravids|Almoravid]]
invasion (1086) transformed the conflict, he carved out an **independent lordship at
Valencia**, which he conquered in **1094** and ruled until his death in 1099 (the city
fell back to the Almoravids in 1102).

## Legend and Significance

Rodrigo's deeds were quickly mythologised: the Latin *Historia Roderici* and, above all,
the great Castilian epic **_Cantar de Mio Cid_** (c. 1200) recast him as the ideal
loyal vassal and crusading hero, a foundational figure of Spanish national legend. The
historical Cid, by contrast, exemplifies the **opportunistic, multi-confessional**
reality of the *taifa*-era frontier — a self-made warlord who served Muslim and
Christian masters alike. He stands at the intersection of the [[reconquista]], the
[[almoravids|Almoravid]] intervention, and the rise of [[chanson-de-geste|vernacular
epic]].
