# Daniel Morgan (1736–1802)

The most gifted American battlefield tactician of the **[[american-revolution|Revolution]]** and
the victor of **[[battle-of-cowpens-1781|Cowpens]]**. The "Old Wagoneer" — a six-foot-six former
teamster who had served the British army in the Seven Years' War — Morgan commanded a Virginia
rifle company at the [[siege-of-boston-1775|siege of Boston]] (1775), was captured in
[[arnold-benedict|Arnold]]'s assault on Quebec, and after exchange led the **11th Virginia
riflemen**, whose marksmanship "played a significant role halting the initial British advance"
at [[battles-of-saratoga-1777|Saratoga]] (his men killed General Simon Fraser).

## Cowpens

Detached by [[greene-nathanael|Greene]] with ~700 men into the South Carolina backcountry and
pursued by [[cornwallis-charles|Cornwallis]]'s cavalry under Banastre Tarleton, Morgan turned at
Hannah's Cowpens on 17 January 1781 and fought one of history's cleanest **double envelopments**.
His **three-line defense** — skirmishers, then militia ordered to fire two volleys and withdraw,
then Continentals on the rise — absorbed and channeled Tarleton's impetuous charge; a feigned
withdrawal pivoted into a devastating counterattack while American cavalry struck the flanks and
rear. Tarleton's force was nearly annihilated; Congress voted Morgan a medal. The full
staff-college analysis is at
**[[hubs/warfare/age-of-expansion/american-revolutionary-war/battle-of-cowpens|Cowpens — Tactical Analysis]]**.

Ill health forced Morgan to leave the army the following month.
