# Arminius (Cheruscan war-leader)

The noble of the **Cherusci** who organised the German coalition that destroyed Varus' three
legions at the **[[battle-of-teutoburg-forest-9ce|Teutoburg Forest]]** in **AD 9** — the man
who, more than any other, fixed the Rhine as the limit of Roman Germany. Treated in
[[bowman-cah-v10-1996|CAH X]], Ch. 4 (Gruen).

## Career

A young warrior "from the ruling house of the Cherusci," Arminius had been made a **Roman
citizen** and served as an officer in the **auxilia** — knowledge of the Roman army he turned
against it. Exploiting the resentment provoked by the legate **Varus'** premature imposition
of taxes and jurisdiction, and the tying-down of Rome's main forces in the **Pannonian
revolt**, he **lulled Varus into trust and lured him into ambush**, annihilating three
legions over several days in September AD 9.

He continued to lead German resistance against the retaliatory campaigns of **Germanicus**
(AD 14–16), which claimed victories but achieved little of substance. His later career (a war
with **Maroboduus** of the Marcomanni, and his death around AD 21 at the hands of his own
kinsmen, who distrusted his power) belongs to the reign of Tiberius.

## Significance

Tacitus' famous verdict made Arminius *liberator Germaniae* — "the liberator of Germany."
Gruen cautions that the phrase **does not imply Rome had ever truly annexed the land as a
province**; the Germans had been "intimidated rather than subdued." But the practical effect
of his victory endured: it **broke the Roman experiment of occupation between Rhine and
Elbe** and helped settle the long northern frontier of the empire on the Rhine. In the modern
era (especially 19th-century Germany) Arminius — "Hermann" — became a potent nationalist
symbol.
