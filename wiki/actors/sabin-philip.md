# Philip Sabin

Philip Sabin is a modern military historian specializing in ancient Greek and Roman warfare. He is the developer of a systematic quantitative and simulation-based model for reconstructing "lost battles" — engagements whose ancient sources are thin, contradictory, or ambiguous.

## Background and Approach

Sabin has long been active in the historical wargaming community (Society of Ancients) and has iteratively developed and play-tested his model since 1993. He co-edited the two-volume *Cambridge History of Greek and Roman Warfare*. His approach combines close reading of ancient texts (Polybius, Livy, Xenophon, Caesar, Arrian, etc.) with comparative statistical analysis across dozens of battles and the use of purpose-designed conflict simulations to test the military plausibility of alternative reconstructions.

The work is explicitly synthetic and "grand tactical" rather than soldier's-eye "face of battle." It draws inspiration from quantitative logistics modeling (Engels) and operational analysis while remaining accessible and grounded in the practical constraints of pre-gunpowder armies (human, equine, and elephantine capabilities).

## Contribution via *Lost Battles*

- Builds a generic "model" of ancient land battle using a 5×4 zone grid, unit activations, command points, troop quality distinctions (veteran/average/levy), "spent" status, and a composite Fighting Value (FV) metric.
- Employs the model to refight ~35 named battles, generate dynamic reconstructions, and adjudicate between conflicting source interpretations by reference to patterns across the corpus.
- Emphasizes the four-dimensional interaction of force, space, time, and command.
- Highlights that raw numbers matter less than troop quality, reputation, and generalship; that frontages scale with the square root of army size; that combat is typically attritional and prolonged until one side breaks, after which pursuit produces the decisive slaughter; and that time (the "battlefield clock") is critical to understanding manoeuvres such as double envelopments.

Key quote grounding the project:
> "I will push this process of synthesis even further by building up a generic model of ancient battle in a more analytically rigorous way than in previous surveys of the topic. I will then use this model to cast new light on each individual battle in turn..."
