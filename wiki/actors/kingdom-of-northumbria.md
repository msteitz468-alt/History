# Kingdom of Northumbria

**Northumbria** (the land "north of the Humber") was formed by the union of the two
Anglian kingdoms of [[bernicia|Bernicia]] (the north, centred on Bamburgh) and
[[deira|Deira]] (the south, centred on York). Under [[edwin-of-northumbria|Eadwine]]
(d. 633), [[oswald-of-northumbria|Oswald]] (d. 642) and [[oswiu-of-northumbria|Oswiu]]
(d. 670) it was the dominant power in seventh-century Britain, exercising overlordship far
to the south and north and, after the [[synod-of-whitby-664|Synod of Whitby (664)]],
aligning its church with Rome.

Northumbrian expansion was checked by [[mercia|Mercia]] — [[penda-of-mercia|Penda]] killed
both Eadwine and Oswald in battle before Oswiu destroyed him at the
[[battle-of-the-winwaed-655|Winwæd (655)]] — and by the Picts, who annihilated
[[ecgfrith-of-northumbria|Ecgfrith's]] army at [[battle-of-nechtansmere-685|Nechtansmere
(685)]], ending the northern hegemony. The eighth century brought a cultural golden age
(Bede, Wilfrid, the Lindisfarne school) amid dynastic instability, before the Viking
capture of York in 867 ended the independent kingdom.
