# Stilicho

The **half-Vandal, half-Roman generalissimo** (*magister utriusque militiae*) who governed the western
empire as guardian of the child emperor **Honorius** from 395 to 408 — the dominant figure of the West in
the years after [[theodosius-i|Theodosius I]]. Treated in [[cameron-cah-v13-1998|CAH XIII]] Ch. 4
(**R. C. Blockley**).

## The Theodosian regent

On Theodosius' death (395), Stilicho — married to Theodosius' niece Serena — claimed the guardianship of
**both** young emperors, but his insistence on the **unity of the empire** was emphatically rejected by
the eastern court, and his career was consumed by friction with Constantinople and by the problem of
**[[alaric|Alaric]]**, whom he repeatedly fought but never destroyed (and at times tried to employ).
His greatest success was the annihilation of **Radagaisus'** invasion of Italy (406); but the crossing of
the Rhine by the Vandals, Sueves and Alans (406/7), the usurpation of Constantine III in Gaul, and his
payment of **4,000 pounds of gold to Alaric** (408) eroded his standing.

## Fall

The faction of **Olympius** exploited the army's anti-barbarian mood; at Ticinum the troops massacred
Stilicho's supporters, and — refusing to let his barbarian soldiers resist — Stilicho took sanctuary and
was **executed at Ravenna (22 August 408)**. The ensuing massacre of the families of barbarian federates
drove some 30,000 of them to join Alaric — directly precipitating the march on Rome.

## Significance

Blockley defends Stilicho against the charges of treason and incompetence, locating his real failures in
**policy**: his insistence on imperial unity proved divisive, his **reliance on barbarian federates**
inhibited the rebuilding of a Roman army and ultimately destroyed the western governing consensus, and
his prioritizing of the **defence of Italy and the emperor at Ravenna** led to the loss of the northern
provinces. "In a very real sense the failure of Stilicho was the failure of Theodosius" — the
working-out of the Theodosian legacy of federate dependence and dynastic guardianship. His death removed
the one figure who might have managed Alaric, and the sack of Rome followed within two years.

## From Gibbon, Decline and Fall (1776–1788)

Attributed claims from [[sources/gibbon-decline-and-fall-1776]] (principally Chs. XXIX–XXXI); Gibbon’s judgments and Claudian-colored narrative, not modern consensus.

- **Origin and trust.** Gibbon presents Stilicho as of Vandal father (cavalry under Valens), risen to master-general of the West, married to Serena (Theodosius’s adopted niece); dying Theodosius “recommended to Stilicho the care of his sons, and of the republic.” Claudian is the chief literary witness and must be discounted as panegyrist.
- **Rufinus.** Ordered back from the East, Stilicho publicly obeyed yet left Eastern troops under Gainas to murder Rufinus before Arcadius at the Hebdomon—a deed that, “in the execution, perhaps, of justice, violated every law of nature and society.”
- **Defense of Italy.** Gibbon credits Stilicho with chastising Alaric in Greece (then forced to retire by Arcadian rivals), defeating Alaric at Pollentia/Verona, and destroying Radagaisus before Florence; “the valor and conduct which he afterwards displayed, in the defence of Italy, against the arms of Alaric and Radagaisus, may justify the fame of his early achievements.”
- **Subsidy of 4,000 pounds of gold.** Stilicho obtained reluctant senatorial approval of a “subsidy” for Alaric; Lampadius alone dissented: “This is not a treaty of peace, but of servitude.”
- **Fall (408).** Olympius (under a “mask of Christian piety”) turned Honorius against him with the tale of Eucherius’s diadem; friends massacred at Pavia; Stilicho refused to unleash barbarian confederates on Italians; dragged from a Ravenna church and beheaded by Heraclian—“with a firmness not unworthy of the last of the Roman generals.”
- **Verdict.** Services “great and manifest,” alleged crimes “obscure”; “The pride and power of Stilicho constituted his real guilt”; “it is the last humiliation of the character of Honorius, that posterity has not condescended to reproach him with his base ingratitude.”

## From Mitchell, Later Roman Empire (2015)

Attributed claims from [[sources/mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Stephen Mitchell, *A History of the Later Roman Empire* (2015)]].

- **Using Alaric to secure Illyricum.** After holding Alaric at Pollentia and Verona (402), Stilicho changed tactics and used the Goths as allies to secure Illyricum for the West; Alaric received *magister utriusque militiae* for Illyricum from the western court, not Arcadius. Later Stilicho cowed the Senate into paying Alaric 4,000 pounds of gold, arguing Alaric had stayed in Epirus by arrangement with Honorius to detach Illyricum from the East. Lampadius quoted Cicero that such payment bought “not peace but servitude.”
- **Fall (408) and massacre of barbarian families.** After Arcadius’s death Olympius alleged Stilicho aimed to set son Eucherius on the eastern throne; mutiny at Ticinum and Roman/barbarian polarization led to Stilicho’s seizure and killing at Ravenna. Honorius’s loyalists then massacred thousands of barbarians quartered in Italy, including women and children; “Up to thirty thousand of the survivors sought protection and redress by joining Alaric.”
