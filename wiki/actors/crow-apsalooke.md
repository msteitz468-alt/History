# Crow (Apsáalooke)

The **Crows** (Apsáalooke; Siouan, split from Hidatsas) obtained horses via Shoshones and Flatheads probably in the **1740s**, which may have been a factor in permanently migrating to the Upper Yellowstone; they brought horses to Mandan and Hidatsa villages. Peoples closest to horse sources (Comanches on the Spanish frontier; Crows near Plateau traders) were better supplied than northerly Crees and Assiniboines—five horses made a Cree wealthy while twenty-five meant little for Crow/Comanche (Fowler).

## Overview

Teton [[actors/lakota|Lakota]] expansion contested Crow ranges: by **1825** Crows driven west to Powder/Yellowstone; smallpox/cholera 1840s and pressure from Laramie pushed them further, allying with Shoshones, Flatheads, and Americans. Fort Cass served Crow robe trade. After Louisiana Purchase American trappers entered Crow country; Crows eventually accepted Americans who helped against Tetons, while Blackfeet/Gros Ventres repelled Americans.

In Montana, Crows, Blackfeet groups, Gros Ventres, and some Assiniboines faced Teton contest for buffalo ground; removed from overland routes, they saw **Tetons as worse than Americans**. Crows eventually **scouted for the Army against the Sioux** to defend remaining hunting territory and signed an **1868 treaty** ceding most land and accepting a reservation on the remainder. Crow scouts also figure in the multi-nation scout force (~400 from six nations) that made Northern Cheyenne/Lakota evasion harder after [[events/battle-of-little-bighorn-1876|Little Bighorn]].

## Role and Structure

- **Military:** horse-rich nomads; long war with Teton expansion; U.S. alliance as survival strategy.
- **Political:** 1851 Fort Laramie party; 1868 cession and reservation.
- **Economic:** horse trade to Missouri villages; robe trade (Fort Cass); Army scout pay/prestige.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Crow–U.S. military alliance, Army operations against the Sioux in Montana would have been weaker; without horse-enabled Yellowstone migration, Hidatsa–Crow separation geography would differ. Teton expansion and buffalo decline remained structural constraints.

## Historiography

Fowler frames Crow scouting as rational defense of hunting territory, not mere “collaboration.” Do not collapse Crow with Lakota under “Sioux wars” without naming opposing indigenous interests.
