# Vladimir Lenin
## Biography / Description
Vladimir Ulianov, better known by his pseudonym N. Lenin, was born in 1870 in Simbirsk on the Volga to an intellectual family (his father was a school inspector). He became a radical early in life, influenced by the execution of his eldest brother Alexander in 1887 for participating in a plot to assassinate Alexander III. He studied law at the University of Kazan, became a Marxist, and suffered imprisonment in 1896 followed by three years of Siberian exile. In 1900, he participated in publishing the Social Democratic newspaper *The Spark* (*Iskra*) abroad, and in 1903 he led the Bolshevik faction during the split of the Russian Social Democratic Labor Party. He masterminded the Bolshevik seizure of power in the October Revolution of 1917 and led the Soviet state as Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars until his death on January 21, 1924, following a series of debilitating strokes starting in May 1922.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **Bolshevik Party Structure (1903):** Lenin insisted on a tightly knit body of dedicated professional revolutionaries with clear lines of command and military discipline, which split the Social Democrats into Bolsheviks and Mensheviks.
- **October Revolution (1917):** Lenin decided that his party could stage a successful revolution in Russia in 1917, drafting the *April Theses* to advocate transforming the bourgeois revolution into a socialist one, supported by poor peasants.
- **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (1918):** Against strong opposition within his own party (including Bukharin and Trotsky), Lenin insisted on accepting Germany's harsh peace terms to save the fledgling Soviet state, stating it was necessary to sacrifice territory to consolidate power.
- **War Communism (1918–1921):** Lenin oversaw the radical socialization of the economy, introducing grain requisitions (prodrazvyorstka), nationalization of industry, and the suppression of political opponents via the Cheka.
- **New Economic Policy (1921):** Following the Kronstadt rebellion and widespread peasant unrest, Lenin made the realistic compromise to retreat to a mixed economy, replacing food levies with a tax in kind and permitting small-scale private trade.

## Historical Significance
Lenin was the principal founder of the Soviet state and the primary theoretical architect of Marxism-Leninism, adapting classical Marxist theory to Russian conditions. He recognized the revolutionary potential of the peasantry in an underdeveloped capitalist state (the theory of the "weakest link" in the imperialist chain) and championed the role of a vanguard party. Riasanovsky notes that Lenin "combined high intelligence, an ability for acute theoretical thinking, and practical sense to become a great Marxist 'realist'" (Line 5715). Unlike Stalin, his authority within the party rested on his personality, intellect, and achievements rather than terror. In his final testament, Lenin warned the party against Stalin's rising power and rude character, though his warning went unheeded.
