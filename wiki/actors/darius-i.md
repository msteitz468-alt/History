# Darius I (Darius the Great)

Darius I (Old Persian *Dārayavauš*, "he who holds firm the good"; etymologized in the Behistun vocabulary as "the possessor" from dar "to hold") seized the Achaemenid throne in 522 BCE after the death of Cambyses II. According to his own first-person account in the Behistun inscription (see [[behistun-inscription]]), he killed the Magian Gaumata (who had dispossessed Cambyses of Persia and Media and impersonated Bardiya/Smerdis, son of Cyrus, suppressing the ayadana and religious observances), restored what the usurper had destroyed, and suppressed nine "liar-kings" in rebellions (Elam, Babylon, Media, Sagartia, Margiana/Thatagush, etc.) via 19 battles in one year "by the favor of Ahuramazda." All opposition is framed as drauga ("the Lie"); Darius upholds arta (truth/order).

He is remembered less for new conquests than for the administrative and ideological system he imposed: satrapies (khshathrapa), Royal Road, taxation, garrisons, qanats, and the king as upholder of *arta* against *drauga* with Ahuramazda's favor. The Behistun text itself is the primary vehicle (genealogy, restoration of worship, province lists, building activities). A copy reached Elephantine.

His reign saw the empire reach its greatest extent (Indus satrapy via Scylax; Bactria as apanage; eastern provinces attested with Arian etymologies) and the first major collision with the Greek world (Marathon 490 BCE). He campaigned personally against the Saka (Sakukha/Skunkha with pointed cap on the relief; Tigrakhuda western Saka; paradraya European Scythians beyond the sea in the Hellespont expedition).

The Behistun inscription (with 19th-c. vocabulary and commentary) is his own primary testimony, correcting Greek accounts (Herodotus has two Magi; Ctesias uses Spendadates). Key conspirators include Gobryas (Gaubruwa, son of Mardonius; later Susiana expedition; high noble, married to Darius's sister per Herodotus). Provinces: Gadara/Gandhara (Indus), Thatagush (Hindu Kush), Asagarta (Media-dependent), Athura (Assyria), Khwarizm (Oxus), etc. Arian colonization inferences from native names.

## Briant's Reading

[[sources/briant-from-cyrus-to-alexander-2002|Briant]] treats the [[behistun-inscription|
Behistun]] account as **self-justifying autobiography**, not history. Darius was not a son of a
king — Herodotus recalls he was "not yet of any particular importance" (*idiotes*) before 522 —
and his eight-king genealogy is internally incoherent (his father and grandfather were alive and
never reigned). The term **"Achaemenid"** acquires its political sense only with Darius, who uses
it narrowly of his own patriline and retrojects it onto [[cyrus-the-great|Cyrus]] via forged
[[pasargadae|Pasargadae]] inscriptions. He suppresses the seven conspirators' agency, claiming
sole credit. The satrapy was **not** his creation — it existed under Cyrus and Cambyses; his real
innovations were fixing tribute amounts (the first published [[achaemenid-tribute-economy|tribute]]
schedule, c. 518) and the great [[achaemenid-royal-ideology|building-and-ideology]] program at Susa
and [[persepolis|Persepolis]]. In Egypt he codified Egyptian law (a 16-year commission, 519–503),
dug the Nile–Red Sea canal, and built the Hibis temple — signalling pharaonic continuity even as his
own inscriptions stressed conquest.
