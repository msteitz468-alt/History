# The Berbers (Imazighen / Libyco-Berbers)

The **Berbers** (self-designation **Imazighen**, "free people"; **Amazigh** sing.) are the **indigenous
peoples of North Africa** west of Egypt and across the northern Sahara — the continuous human substratum
beneath every external power from the Pharaohs to the Arabs. Documented for the wiki through the **UNESCO
[[gha-vol2-ancient-civilizations|General History of Africa, Vol. II]]** (chs. 17–20), which insists on the
**African agency** of these "Libyco-Berbers," too often treated as a mere backdrop to Carthage and Rome.

## The Libyans in antiquity

The earliest records are Egyptian: the **Tehenou** (from the Predynastic period and the reliefs of Sahure,
c. 2500 BCE) and later the paler-skinned **Temehou** appear as the Pharaohs' western neighbours — the people
the Greeks called **Libyans**. They were diverse: GHA distinguishes coastal **Mauri** (Mauretania) and
**Numidians**, plateau **Getulians**, and Saharan groups (Pharusii, Garamantes and the dark-skinned
"Ethiopians" of the oases) — a mixed population, neither simply "white" nor "black," whose physical types
ranged from Mediterranean to Nilotic to (in the desert) ancestors of the Fulani and Toubou.

## Libyco-Berber states

Far from stateless, the Berbers built **kingdoms** on Carthage's frontier:

- **Numidia**, united under **Masinissa** (r. c. 202–148 BCE) — who allied with Rome against Carthage in
  the Punic Wars, sedentarized his people around towns and agriculture, and forged the most powerful
  indigenous North African state before Rome; later contested in the **[[causes-of-second-punic-war|Punic]]**
  and Jugurthine wars (his grandson **Jugurtha** fought Rome 112–106 BCE).
- **Mauretania** (modern Morocco/western Algeria), a client kingdom (notably under **Juba II**) annexed by
  Rome in 40 CE.
- The **[[garamantes|Garamantes]]** of the Fezzan — a Saharan Berber civilization living off
  *foggara*-irrigated oasis agriculture and controlling early trans-Saharan routes.

## Carthage, Rome, and beyond

Berbers supplied **Carthage's** armies (the famous Numidian cavalry) and farmed its hinterland; under
**[[roman-africa|Rome]]** their lands became the empire's granary and a heartland of **Latin Christianity**
(Tertullian, Cyprian, Augustine of Hippo — and the **Donatist** schism, in part an expression of Berber and
rural resistance to the Romanized coastal elite). Never fully assimilated, Berber language and identity
survived Vandal, Byzantine and ultimately Arab conquest; the **Amazigh** peoples (Tuareg, Kabyle, Riffian,
Shilha and others) persist across the Maghrib and Sahara to this day.

## Significance

The Berbers are the **indigenous spine of North African history** and a corrective to accounts that reduce
the region to a stage for Phoenicians, Romans, and Arabs. As masters of the desert margins they were also
the **carriers of the [[trans-saharan-trade|trans-Saharan trade]]** that, after the spread of the camel
(end of the 1st c. BCE), linked the Mediterranean to West Africa.
