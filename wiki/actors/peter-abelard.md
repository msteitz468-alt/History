# Peter Abelard (c. 1079–1142)

## Overview

**Peter Abelard** was the most celebrated — and controversial — master of the early twelfth-century **Paris schools**. His dialectical method (*Sic et non*), theological daring, and tragic relationship with **Heloise** made him Logan's first "twelfth-century profile": a window onto the world of the schools that would become the [[medieval-university|university]]. Condemned at Soissons (1121) and again under pressure from **Bernard of Clairvaux** at Sens (1141), he died at Cluny.

## From Logan, History of the Church in the Middle Ages (2013)

### Memory and sources

Logan holds that every generation rediscovers Abelard under competing lenses — rebellious malcontent, seducer, paranoid, seminal scholar, unhappy monk — and that he is “almost inevitably and probably unfairly” remembered chiefly for Heloise, with moral judgments on his conduct toward her almost always negative. Principal narrative source: eight letters beginning with the autobiographical *Historia calamitatum* plus Heloise correspondence. A minority question authenticity (earliest of nine Latin MSS c.150 years after alleged 1130s composition; intervening silence near-total; some suspect Abelard wrote the whole dossier including Heloise’s letters); major life events are otherwise attested.

### Origins and schools career

Born c.1079 at **Le Pallet** (southern Brittany near Nantes), eldest son of a knightly family with Poitevin roots (probably not ethnically Breton), destined for knighthood, he chose student life. By his departure c.1093, learning had largely shifted from monastic to **urban cathedral schools**; students followed famous masters. He studied logic with **Roscelin** (Loches/Tours), reached Notre-Dame Paris c.1100 under **William of Champeaux**, clashed as a ~21-year-old, founded rival schools at Melun then Corbeil, and c.1105 suffered a probable mental breakdown. Against William’s Realism he developed a **moderate Nominalism**: universals lack external real existence yet are more than *flatus vocis* — meaningful mental constructs/signs of abstracted features (ultimate basis in divine ideas). He taught c.1109–1113 at **Mont-Ste-Geneviève**; in 1113 studied divinity under **Anselm of Laon**, cut classes, presumed to lecture on the Bible, was silenced, and made enemies of **Alberic of Rheims** and **Lotulf of Lombardy**; 1114 master at Notre-Dame.

### Heloise, castration, monastic years

As Notre-Dame canon he lodged with senior canon **Fulbert** and tutored Fulbert’s learned teenage niece **Heloise**; pregnancy and birth of son **Astrolabe** (1118 crisis) led to a secret marriage (career self-interest, not celibacy law — he was not a priest); Heloise warned “We shall both be destroyed.” After the secret leaked he sent her to **Argenteuil** in habit short of the veil; Fulbert’s kinsmen **castrated** him in his bedroom — news that spread across Christendom. He entered **St Denis**, pressed Heloise to take the veil, denounced worldly monks, and taught from a dependent priory; students likened the mutilated master to Origen. Between 1118 and 1121 he wrote *Theologia* and popularized the term “**theology**”; at **Soissons 1121** Alberic and Lotulf secured a heresy charge (allegedly teaching three gods); he was forced to burn his book — an injury he ranked worse than castration — though he retained another copy.

After offending St Denis over the patron saint’s identity he lived as a hermit near Troyes, founded the **Paraclete** oratory, attracted students, and composed *Sic et non* (contradictions in theological sources resolved by reason — his best-known scholarly work). Elected abbot of **St Gildas** (Brittany) c.1125 (probably then ordained), he claimed monks tried to poison the altar wine; after Argenteuil nuns’ expulsion 1129 he gave them the Paraclete with Heloise as abbess. After the *Historia calamitatum* (c.1131) reached Heloise, her letters claim marital and emotional right (famously preferring to be called his whore than the emperor’s wife); Abelard replied that his love had been lust and counselled prayer — Logan notes readings of cold piety vs rare honesty.

### Bernard, Sens, death

Logan frames **Bernard of Clairvaux** (most powerful western ecclesiastic of the 1130s–40s) as accepting mysteries by faith alone versus Abelard’s reason applied to God as fullness of faith — personal and profound collision, Abelard vanquished. At **Sens (June 1141)**, before Louis VII and a throng of bishops, Bernard prosecuted nineteen allegedly heretical propositions; he had pre-dined the bishops; Abelard said “I appeal to the pope” and left; the council condemned propositions but not his person. En route to Rome he was received at **Cluny** by **Peter the Venerable**; **Innocent II** (dependent on Bernard) condemned him and (one account) burned his books in St Peter’s; he died **21 April 1142** (~63) at Chalon-sur-Saône after reconciliation and absolution; body taken to the Paraclete; Heloise buried beside him ~22 years later; graves disturbed in the Revolution; now Père Lachaise. Logan’s final tragedy: Abelard remembered more for Heloise than as one of the greatest twelfth-century scholars. (Logan also notes **Celestine III** as a Sens defender of Abelard and friend of [[thomas-becket|Becket]] — linking the three profiles into the Innocent age.)
