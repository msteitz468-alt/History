# Christopher Duffy

**Christopher Duffy** (b. 1937) is a leading British military historian specializing in the armies and warfare of early modern and eighteenth-century Europe, with particular expertise on Frederick the Great, siegecraft, and the social and experiential dimensions of old-regime armies.

## Career and Approach
Long associated with the Department of War Studies at the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst. His work combines deep archival research, especially in German, Austrian, French, and Russian sources, with a focus on the lived reality of soldiers and officers rather than purely operational narrative or great captains. He is noted for restoring the "face of battle" and institutional texture to the study of the Age of Reason and the military revolution.

## Major Works (in the collection or referenced)
- *Siege Warfare: The Fortress in the Early Modern World 1494–1660* (1979) and its sequel *The Fortress in the Age of Vauban and Frederick the Great* — foundational on the trace italienne and the dominance of siege in early modern war.
- *Frederick the Great: A Military Life* (1985) — standard operational and personal study.
- **The Military Experience in the Age of Reason** (1987) — the present source; a comparative social and cultural anatomy of eighteenth-century armies (Prussia, Austria, France, Russia, Britain and others), covering officer and private soldier life, motivation, honour, drill, campaign, battle, and siege, with attention to national differences and the subsequent "death of a memory" of these forces.
