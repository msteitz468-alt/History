# Piye (Piankhy)

**Piye** (also rendered Piankhy; r. ~747–716 BCE), son of Kashta, was the Kushite king who led
the first great Nubian conquest of Egypt, founding the **25th Dynasty**. His campaign is
recorded on his triumphal stele from **Gebel Barkal**, one of the finest royal narratives of the
[[processes/third-intermediate-period|Third Intermediate Period]].

## The conquest of c.730 BCE

Provoked by the rapid northward expansion of **Tefnakht of Sais**, Piye marched down the Nile.
Thebes submitted without a struggle; he relieved Herakleopolis, captured Hermopolis, and took
**Memphis by storm** — yet "with conspicuous reverence for the religious traditions of Egypt"
he protected its temples from looting. His stele preserves the famous snapshot of Egypt's
fragmentation c.730 BCE: rival kings at Bubastis, Leontopolis, Hermopolis, and Herakleopolis,
plus a "Prince of the West" at Sais and several Great Chiefs of the Ma.

Strikingly, after his victory **"Piy returned to Nubia, leaving the political situation in
Egypt virtually unchanged"** — the local dynasts kept their thrones as his nominal vassals. His
sister **Amenirdis I** had already been installed as
[[concepts/gods-wife-of-amun|God's Wife of Amun]] at Thebes, giving Kush a religious foothold
there.

## A Kushite-Egyptian king

Piye embodied the deep **Egyptianization** of the Kushite elite (see [[kush-meroe]]): the cult
of Amun was central to Kushite kingship. He was buried at **el-Kurru** "in a tomb of strongly
Egyptian character, with a pyramid superstructure" and shabti-figures — but with his chariot
horses interred nearby, "evidently a distinctively Kushite practice." In Kush the throne often
passed to a **brother** rather than a son.
