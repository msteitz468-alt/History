# The Visconti of Milan

The **Visconti** were the dynasty that ruled **Milan** from the late thirteenth century and built
the largest **[[signoria|signorial]]** state in northern Italy — the most powerful of the
fourteenth-century lordships that grew out of the [[commune-movement|communes]].
[[jones-ncmh-v6-2000|NCMH VI]] (John Law, Ch. 13a) treats their rise as the exemplary case of the
consolidation of the territorial signoria.

## The Rise

Archbishop **Ottone Visconti** seized Milan from the Guelf della Torre at **Desio (1277)**;
**Matteo Visconti** entrenched the family's lordship as imperial vicar under
[[holy-roman-empire-late-medieval|Henry VII]]. Across the fourteenth century the Visconti expanded
from Milan across **Lombardy and beyond**, absorbing rival communes and signorie and turning a
personal lordship into a **hereditary regional state** with a court, bureaucracy and standing
finance.

## Giangaleazzo and the Duchy

The state reached its height under **Giangaleazzo Visconti** (d. 1402), who purchased the title of
**Duke of Milan from the Emperor (1395)**, conquered much of northern and central Italy (Verona,
Padua, Pisa, Bologna), and seemed poised to dominate the whole peninsula — checked only by his
sudden death in 1402 and the resistance of republican **[[florence|Florence]]**, against whose
"tyranny" the Florentine humanists defined their civic liberty. The duchy fragmented after him but
the Visconti recovered, ruling until the line failed in 1447 (succeeded by the **Sforza**).

## Significance

The Visconti are the great example of the fourteenth-century transformation of the Italian
[[signoria|signoria]] into a **dynastic territorial principality** — the model, with the Este,
Gonzaga and Scaligeri, of the Renaissance state, and the principal antagonist of the surviving
republics ([[florence|Florence]], [[republic-of-venice|Venice]]) in the contest for northern Italy.
