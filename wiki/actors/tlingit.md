# Tlingit

Northernmost major Northwest Coast people of the Alaska panhandle; Na-Dene language related to interior Athapaskan—possibly indicating comparatively recent coastal arrival. Yakutat Bay is usually the northern boundary of the [[pacific-northwest-coast]] culture area.

## Role and overview

Complex hierarchical coastal society within the wider maritime Northwest Coast pattern (seasonal round, ranked leadership by birth/wealth/ability with consent). Participated in long-distance coastal trade; under the fur trade, Tlingits acted as armed middlemen rather than coerced hunters, trading at prices Fisher cites as **3–5×** those obtained from Aleuts under Russian tribute/compulsory labor.

## Key episodes (Fisher, CHNPA I.2)

- **Russian American Company and Sitka:** New Archangel founded in Tlingit territory **1799**; by **1800** three-quarters of company sea-otter pelts came from Sitka; colonial capital **1808**.
- **Military resistance:** Unlike sparsely populated Aleuts reduced to virtual serfdom, Tlingits were more numerous, warlike, and environmentally rich; well armed from American trade; hide/slat armor. In **1802** a force of ~600 under **Kotlean** destroyed New Archangel—20 Russians and 130 Aleuts killed, 3,000 furs lost. Rebuilt **1804**; Yakutat destroyed **1805**; threats **1809**, **1813**; attack as late as **1855** on a capital of ~1,000. Hostility highlights the **relative absence of comparable violence** south of Russian territory during the fur-trade era.
- **Economic adaptation:** Potato cultivation and sales; by 1840s large potato sales at New Archangel (Russian staple plus export surplus)—paralleling [[haida]] diversification.
- **Religion:** Russian Orthodox among Tlingits from mid-1830s (greater toleration of Native customs, language learning, Native clergy in principle); influence always weak—baptisms often commercial/political; after Alaska sale **1867** Orthodox status declined as other denominations arrived.

## Later political organization

Alaska Native Brotherhood founded **1912** among Tlingit/Haida/Tsimshian (English-language; anti-segregation)—early intertribal organization (Hoxie, CHNPA I.2 ch. 12).

## Counterfactual significance

Successful armed denial of Russian labor control preserved Tlingit autonomy through the fur-trade century and forced Russians into more limited, trade-oriented relations—contrasting Aleut outcomes and shaping the northern boundary of Fisher’s low-violence coastal fur-trade zone.

## Historiography

Fisher uses Tlingit–Russian conflict as the foil for “fatal impact” overstatements south of Russian America and as a case where disease timing (hard hit late 1830s after long intervals since earlier epidemics) supports **rebuild-interval** demography rather than continuous depopulation. Demographic magnitude claims for the coast generally require caution (well-informed guesses; local variation).
