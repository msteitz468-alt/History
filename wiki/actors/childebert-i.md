# Childebert I

**Childebert I** (c. 496–558) was the third son of **[[clovis]]** and [[clotild|Clotild]],
king at **Paris** after the partition of 511. In **[[gregory-of-tours-history-of-the-franks-thorpe-1974|Gregory's]]** account he is a restless, conspiring figure who repeatedly schemed against his brother [[chlothar-i|Chlothar I]] and "every time he conspired against Lothar he came off worse in the end."

## Career

Childebert invaded **Spain** to aid his sister Clotild, ill-treated by her Arian Visigothic
husband Amalaric; she died on the return journey and "was carried to Paris" to be buried
beside Clovis. From Spain he carried off rich church plate — "sixty chalices, fifteen
patens and twenty Gospel bindings" — but, notably, donated it to the Church rather than
melting it down. With Chlothar he engineered the murder of [[chlodomer|Chlodomer]]'s
children, dispatching Arcadius to Clotild "with a pair of scissors in one hand and a naked
sword in the other."

Childless, he tried to secure the succession by adopting his nephew Theudebert ("I have no
children of my own… I should like to adopt you as my son"). He fell ill and died in Paris
in 558; Chlothar promptly seized his kingdom and exiled his widow Ultrogotha and two
daughters.

## Counterfactual Significance

Childebert's childless death was the hinge that let Chlothar I reunite the whole Frankish
realm in 558. As founder of the Parisian royal church of St Vincent (later Saint-Germain-
des-Prés), he also fixed Paris in the dynasty's sacral geography. See [[francia]].
