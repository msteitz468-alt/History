# The Aviz Dynasty (Portugal, 1385–1495)

The second Portuguese royal house, founded by **João I** in 1385, under which Portugal launched the
European **age of overseas expansion**. Treated by A.H. de Oliveira Marques in [[allmand-ncmh-v7-1998|
NCMH VII]] (Ch. 24c). Marques takes the Portuguese "fifteenth century" to run from the **capture of
Ceuta (1415)** — the start of Moroccan and Atlantic expansion — and frames the period as one of slow
demographic recovery, growing crown authority, and the opening of the wider world.

## Foundation and Character

The dynasty was born of the crisis of 1383–85: **João**, bastard of Avis, secured the throne against
Castile at the decisive battle of **Aljubarrota (1385)**, with the support of the towns and the rising
gentry. The Aviz monarchy rested on a partnership between crown, **municipal councils** and a
service nobility, and on the *cortes* (which lost influence as the century wore on). Its great kings:

- **João I** (1385–1433) and **Duarte** (1433–38): consolidation, and the opening of **Moroccan
  expansion** (Ceuta, 1415).
- The **Infante Dom Henrique** ("Henry the Navigator") and the regency of the Infante Pedro: the
  patronage of the **[[age-of-discovery|Atlantic voyages]]** — the Madeira and Azores colonies, and
  the West African coast.
- **Afonso V** (1448–81), "the African," whose reign saw aristocratic resurgence and costly Moroccan
  campaigns.
- **João II** (1481–95), under whom "centralist government... became almost absolute, as king and
  state merged together": he broke the great nobility (the executions of the dukes of Braganza and
  Viseu), and drove the **[[age-of-discovery|exploration of the African coast to the Cape of Good
  Hope]]** (Cão, Dias 1488) toward the sea route to India.

## Significance

The Aviz dynasty is the **launching-pad of European overseas empire**. Crown patronage turned the
chivalric, crusading and commercial impulses of the Portuguese nobility outward — to Morocco, the
Atlantic islands, the West African gold and slave trade, and ultimately the **Carreira da Índia**
(da Gama, 1498, under João's successor Manuel I). Portugal thus pioneered the
[[age-of-discovery|age of discovery]] and the [[atlantic-slave-trade|Atlantic slave trade]], a small
kingdom on the ocean's edge that "came from behind" to open the routes that reshaped the world.
