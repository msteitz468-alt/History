# Dracon

**Dracon** (Draco) was an Athenian lawgiver traditionally credited with issuing Athens's
first written law code, c. 621/620 BCE, at a time of aristocratic factional violence and
demands from the wider citizenry for known, publicly posted law rather than
oral judgments controlled by aristocratic magistrates. His code was proverbial for its
severity — many offenses, including relatively minor property crimes, reportedly carried
the death penalty — giving rise to the English adjective "draconian." Dracon's code was
largely superseded a generation later by [[solon|Solon's]] reforms (594/3 BCE), which
retained Dracon's homicide law (distinguishing intentional from unintentional killing)
but replaced most of the rest. Little is known of Dracon as a person; his significance
lies in the codification itself — a step toward the rule of law that later Athenian
democracy built upon — as reported chiefly by Aristotle's *Athenian Constitution* and
later antiquarian tradition.
