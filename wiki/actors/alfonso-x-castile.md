# Alfonso X of Castile ("El Sabio")

King of Castile and León (1252–1284), known as "el Sabio" ("the Wise" or
"the Learned"), whose court sponsored an extraordinary program of legal
codification, historical writing, and scientific translation that made
Castilian Spanish, rather than Latin, a major literary and scholarly
language for the first time.

## Role and Major Decisions

Alfonso commissioned the *Siete Partidas*, an ambitious legal code that
became foundational to Spanish and later Latin American law; the *Estoria
de España* and *General Estoria*, pioneering vernacular historical works;
and the *Cantigas de Santa Maria*, a major vernacular poetic and musical
collection. His court's Toledo translation workshop rendered Arabic and
Hebrew scientific and astronomical texts into Castilian rather than Latin,
producing the influential *Alfonsine Tables* of planetary astronomy. His
reign was politically troubled, including a failed bid for the title of
Holy Roman Emperor and a succession crisis with his son Sancho that
overshadowed his final years.

## Counterfactual Significance

Alfonso's deliberate choice of Castilian over Latin as the language of law,
history, and science is credited by literary historians as a pivotal moment
establishing Castilian as a prestige literary language, accelerating the
broader medieval European shift toward vernacular literatures.

## Historiography

Alfonso is unusually well documented through his own commissioned corpus of
texts, though these works' function as instruments of royal legitimation
(and his troubled succession crisis) complicate any straightforward reading
of them as neutral scholarship; his significance for the history of Iberian
vernacular literature is broadly uncontested even where assessments of his
political reign remain more mixed.
