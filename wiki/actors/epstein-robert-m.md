# Robert M. Epstein

American military historian and professor at the U.S. Army’s School of Advanced Military Studies (SAMS) at Fort Leavenworth. Author of *Napoleon’s Last Victory and the Emergence of Modern War* (1994), which analyzes the 1809 Franco-Austrian War as the moment when the Napoleonic system reached maturity and displayed characteristics of modern operational warfare.

Epstein emphasizes the full operational use of the corps system, distributed maneuver (dispersed movement followed by concentration), the integration of multiple theaters (Germany and Italy), and the transition away from 18th-century cordon and limited-war approaches. The work argues that Wagram (and the 1809 campaign more broadly) marks a key step toward 19th-century mass, industrial-era warfare while still within the Napoleonic framework.

The book includes detailed campaign reconstructions, orders of battle (appendices), and explicit comparisons to later conflicts.
