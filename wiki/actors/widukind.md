# Widukind

**Widukind** was the principal leader of Saxon resistance to [[charlemagne|Charles the
Great]]'s conquest and forced Christianisation of Saxony (see [[saxon-wars-772-804]]). A
Westphalian noble, he became the symbolic head of the pagan Saxon cause through the great
revolts of the late 770s and 780s, before his submission and baptism at Attigny in 785.

## The Baptism of 785

[[sources/nelson-king-and-emperor-2019|Janet Nelson]] stresses that the reconciliation was
**Charles's initiative**, conducted through the same diplomatic mechanics he had used with
[[tassilo-iii|Tassilo of Bavaria]] in 781: Charles "sent envoys and offered hostages … to
secure Widukind's trust," and then **stood as godfather**, raising Widukind from the font.
The *Annales regni Francorum* closed the episode with the famous verdict "**And then all
Saxony was subjugated**" — a claim Nelson notes "was written before the 795 revolt disproved
it," since Saxon resistance continued for nearly two more decades.

Widukind disappears from the contemporary record after his baptism; his later prominence as
a Saxon and (after canonisation legends) quasi-sainted national figure belongs to medieval
and modern mythmaking rather than to the eighth-century sources.
