# The Second Athenian Confederacy

## Overview

The **Second Athenian Confederacy** (or Second Athenian League) was the naval alliance
[[athens]] founded in **378/7 BCE** to resist the [[spartan-hegemony|Spartan hegemony]],
proclaiming itself the champion of the "**freedom and autonomy**" principle of the
[[kings-peace-386bce|King's Peace]] that Sparta was abusing. [[lewis-cah-v6-1994|CAH Vol.
VI]] (Ch. 6, Seager) treats it as the institutional core of the Athenian resurgence — and
as a deliberate, advertised **repudiation of the abuses of the fifth-century
[[delian-league|empire]]**.

## Foundation and the Decree of Aristoteles

The nucleus formed early in **378** — the first members being **Chios, Byzantium, Rhodes
and Mytilene** — around a common council, the **synedrion**, sitting at Athens in which
each member had **one vote** and Athens was **hegemon**. After the **Sphodrias raid** (a
Spartan harmost's botched attempt on the Piraeus) drove Athens into open war and alliance
with Thebes, the league was given its charter in the **Decree of Aristoteles** (Feb/Mar
**377**; Tod no. 123), inscribed on stone with the members' names appended. Its terms
were designed to reassure Greeks who still distrusted Athenian motives:

- Membership open to "anyone, Greek or barbarian, islander or mainlander, **who is not a
  subject of the King**" — so as not to repudiate the King's Peace or provoke Persia.
- Each member to keep **whatever constitution it wished**, and to receive **no garrison,
  no governor (*archon*), and pay no tribute** — the precise opposite of the
  [[delian-league|Delian League's]] later practice.
- **Athens renounced all property holdings** (cleruchies, private land) in allied
  territory, and barred Athenians from acquiring any in future; disputes to be heard not
  in Athenian courts but in the **allied synedrion**.
- A mutual-defence guarantee: an attack on any member to be met by the combined forces.

## Growth and limits

The decree "served its purpose well": Euboea (which had suffered badly from fifth-century
cleruchies) and many others joined, reaching a total variously given as **70–75**
(Diodorus, Aeschines), though the inscribed list names no more than ~58. **Thebes**
joined as a member — a fact that soon embarrassed Athens, since a Theban recovery of
Boeotia would itself destroy the "freedom and autonomy" the league existed to protect.
The contribution members paid was carefully **not** called *phoros* ("tribute") but
*syntaxeis* ("contributions"). The peace of **375** explicitly recognized Athenian naval
hegemony.

## Significance

The Confederacy made Athens again a great power and the maritime counterweight to Sparta
and Thebes — the institutional expression of the multipolar fourth century. Yet its
guarantees eroded as Athens, under financial pressure, drifted back toward imperial
habits (cleruchies, *syntaxeis*, interference), provoking the **Social War (357–355)** in
which its strongest members revolted. It limped on until the rise of Macedon; after
**Chaeronea (338)** Philip dissolved it. Its founding charter remains the clearest
ancient statement of how a hegemonic league might bind itself **against** the abuses of
empire.
