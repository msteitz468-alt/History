# Charles M. Schwab

Steel executive (1862–1939). Began as a laborer at the Edgar Thomson Works; rose rapidly through talent for operations and sales. Became a key lieutenant to Andrew Carnegie and later president of Carnegie Steel. Played a central role in the 1901 negotiations that sold the company to J.P. Morgan's syndicate; Morgan then made him the first president of U.S. Steel. Left to build Bethlehem Steel into a major competitor. Schwab was known for personal charisma, technical knowledge, and ability to motivate workers and close deals.
