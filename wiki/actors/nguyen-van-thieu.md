# Nguyen Van Thieu

President of the Republic of Vietnam (elected 1967; re-elected). Key figure in final phase of Vietnam War. See [[events/fall-of-saigon-1975]], [[events/vietnam-war]], [[hubs/warfare/cold-war/vietnam-war/vietnam-war]].

## Overview

Military officer (ARVN) who rose through coups to lead RVN 1965–1975. Ruled during Vietnamization, Paris Accords (1973), and final collapse (1975). Decisions on defense posture and withdrawals central to 1975 outcome per operational sources.

## Role and Key Events (from Willbanks Ch. 11, Range 04 exclusive)

- Post-Paris: "When the cease-fire went into effect, the South Vietnamese controlled most of the populated parts of South Vietnam, but PAVN forces occupied..." Thieu complained to Nixon (Apr 1973) of violations; found Nixon "preoccupied." Ordered ARVN attacks for territorial advantage; "cease-fire war" heavy casualties.
- Aid cuts after Nixon resignation (Aug 1974): "Thieu was relegated to fighting a 'poor man’s war.'" $700M appropriation devastating.
- Phuoc Long loss (Dec 1974–Jan 1975): In shock; "His indecision during the battle for Phuoc Long had resulted in the loss of a large number of soldiers and equipment..." "still in a state of shock over the loss of an entire province."
- Ban Me Thuot / truncation (11 Mar 1975): "Given our present strength and capabilities, we certainly cannot hold and defend all the territory we want." Drew "light at the top, heavy on the bottom" phase lines. MR III/IV "untouchable heartland." Ordered II Corps withdraw from Highlands "to reoccupy Ban Me Thuot at all costs" despite no reinforcements.
- I Corps: Pulled Airborne to Saigon; "yo-yo" orders to Truong on Hue vs Da Nang ("It was like a yo-yo"). "Thieu’s inability to deal with the mounting crisis would have disastrous results."
- Final: Resigned 21 Apr 1975 after congressional aid denial. Blamed US, Montagnards, defeatism, foreign radio in 4 Apr speech. "One wonders whether US commitments can be trusted..."

## Counterfactual Significance

Per source: Earlier or better-executed truncation might have preserved core forces ("Thieu’s decision was not a bad one from a purely military standpoint... if applied earlier, may have worked"). But under fire + poor execution + aid cuts produced corps-level disintegration. Without Thieu's conflicting orders and shock-induced indecision, I/II Corps might have conducted orderly withdrawals preserving more combat power for Saigon defense. US aid denial (Congress) was decisive external factor; Thieu's leadership amplified internal collapse.

## Historiography

Balanced on ARVN (valiant units existed; leadership and family concerns decisive in routs). Vietnamese sources (Vien, Phu, Dung via translators) used.
