# John F. Reynolds

Union major general (1820–1863), commander of the Army of the Potomac's I Corps and, at the time of his death, effectively field commander of the Union forces converging on Gettysburg. A West Point graduate and veteran of the Mexican-American War, Reynolds was widely respected and had reportedly been offered command of the Army of the Potomac before Gettysburg but declined, citing political interference from Washington.

On the morning of 1 July 1863, Reynolds rode ahead of his corps to assess the ground west of Gettysburg, committed his infantry to reinforce Buford's dismounted cavalry along McPherson's Ridge, and was killed almost immediately by a sharpshooter's bullet while directing troops into position. His decision to fight west of town, and his selection of the high ground (Cemetery Hill and Ridge) as the position to fall back to, shaped the battle's entire subsequent geography.

## Significance

Reynolds's death removed one of the Union army's most capable and trusted corps commanders at the opening moment of the war's largest battle, but his tactical decisions on Gettysburg's first day — committing to defend the ridge lines and anchoring the eventual Union position — proved decisive to the battle's outcome.
