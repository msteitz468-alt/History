# Eratosthenes of Cyrene

**Eratosthenes of Cyrene** (c. 276–194 BCE) — mathematician, astronomer, chronographer, literary critic, and poet — was the outstanding polymath of the [[museum-library-of-alexandria|Alexandrian Museum and Library]]. He coined *philologos* ("lover of learning") for his profession and was nicknamed **"Beta"** in antiquity: second-best in each field, first in none, despite brilliance across many. [[thonemann-hellenistic-age-2016|Thonemann]] makes him the case study for Hellenistic intellectual culture (*The Hellenistic Age*, ch. 4).

## Scientific Geography

Scientific geography was, in Thonemann's phrase, Eratosthenes's **single-handed creation**, via *On the Measurement of the Earth* and the *Geographika*.

**Earth circumference.** Assuming a spherical earth, co-longitude of Alexandria and Syene, and parallel solar rays: at summer-solstice noon a gnomon at Syene (on the Tropic of Cancer) cast no shadow, while Alexandria's showed **7.2°** (1/50 of a circle). With the Alexandria–Syene distance estimated at **5,000 stades**, circumference = 50 × 5,000 = **250,000 stades**. At ~184.98 m per stade this is about **46,245 km**, roughly **16%** above the true polar circumference (40,008.6 km). The method is elegant and correct in principle.

**Geographika.** Eratosthenes first described the earth's surface as continuous geometric space divided by latitude and longitude (against earlier linear *periegesis* journey-accounts). The inhabited temperate zone from India to Spain spanned only about a third of the earth's circumference. He denied that Europe/Asia/Africa were **objective** continental divisions: the traditional three-continent scheme, split by narrow straits, was an arbitrary construct born of early Greeks' limited Aegean perspective; the inhabited world was a single whole, and the categories "European Greeks" vs "Asiatic barbarians" had no geographic basis. Homeric geography he dismissed as wholly imaginary: "You will find the scene of Odysseus' wanderings when you track down the cobbler who sewed up the sack of the winds."

## Chronography

In the *Chronographiai* he ordered Greek chronology from the best documentary sources: Olympic victors to **776/5 BCE**, early Spartan king-lists another three centuries to **1104/3**, Trojan War dated **1184/3**, and Greek history from Troy to Alexander's death (**323**) divided into ten epochs. The Olympiad-based chronological spine was adopted by virtually all later Greek historians and is still depended on in certain respects.

## Philology and Mathematics

He wrote a twelve-volume *On Old Comedy* on rare vocabulary and dialect in fifth-century Attic comedy. Mathematical survivals include a short cube-duplication treatise addressed to a Ptolemy (text variously associated with Ptolemy III or IV — mild source inconsistency) with elegiac epigram, and a summary of the **"sieve"** algorithm for primes. "Beta" is a fair verdict for pure mathematical standing beside [[archimedes|Archimedes]], who addressed the *Cattle Problem* and the *Method* to him as friend and admirer.

## Ptolemaic Context

Thonemann stresses patronage: measurement was anchored to the northern and southern limits of Ptolemaic Egypt (Alexandria–Syene); alone among major third-century dynasties the Ptolemies held territories in Europe, Asia, and Africa, likely colouring the denial of continental divisions; geometric land-survey for Egyptian taxation echoes geometric world-mapping; Eratosthenes was a royal appointee housed and fed in the palace. Contemporaneously with Ashoka's mid-third-century inscriptions, he was mapping the world at the Museum some **3,350 miles** west of Pataliputra — Thonemann's emblem of Hellenistic geographic scale (see [[ashoka]], [[hellenistic-world]]).

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Eratosthenes, Greek geography remains a genre of traveller-itineraries and Homeric speculation; with him it becomes a geometric science whose continent-scepticism and lat/long grid reframe how the Hellenistic world could think about itself as one inhabited space. The chronographic spine he built is infrastructure for almost all later Greek historical writing.

## Historiography

Primary works lost; reconstruction via Strabo and later geographers/chronographers. Modern treatment: [[thonemann-hellenistic-age-2016]]; institutional setting in [[museum-library-of-alexandria]] and [[hellenistic-science]] (Lloyd in CAH VII.1 ch. 9). Do not confuse the librarian-scholar with other Eratosthenes-named figures; and do not merge "Aristarchus" the Homeric editor (Samothrace, c. 150) with Aristarchus of Samos the astronomer.
