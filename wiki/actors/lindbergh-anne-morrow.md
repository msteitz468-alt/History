# Anne Morrow Lindbergh

## Overview
Anne Morrow Lindbergh was an acclaimed American author, poet, and aviator. As the daughter of diplomat Dwight Morrow, she married Charles Lindbergh in 1929, subsequently serving as his co-pilot, navigator, and radio operator on pioneering global survey flights. In addition to her aviation achievements, she was a prolific writer whose works, such as *Gift from the Sea*, *North to the Orient*, and *Listen! the Wind*, captured the philosophy of flight and the inner life of women.

## Actions and Decisions
- **Pioneering Survey Flights (1931–1933):** Navigated and operated the radio for Charles Lindbergh on major route-mapping flights, including the 1931 Great Circle flight to China and the 1933 30,000-mile North and South Atlantic surveys.
- **Hubbard Medal Award (1934):** Received the Hubbard Medal from the National Geographic Society for her contributions to geographic exploration and radio communication in aviation.
- **Writing Career:** Pushed by her husband to lock herself in the library at Long Barn, she completed *Listen! the Wind* (1938), establishing herself as an independent literary voice.
- **European Exile (1935):** Relocated with Charles to England and France to protect their family from relentless media harassment following the trial of Bruno Richard Hauptmann.
