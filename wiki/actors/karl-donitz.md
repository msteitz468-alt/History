# Karl Dönitz

Grand Admiral of the German Kriegsmarine (1891–1980), architect of Germany's U-boat ("wolf pack") strategy in the Battle of the Atlantic and commander-in-chief of the German Navy from 1943. A First World War U-boat officer, Dönitz rebuilt and doctrinally reshaped the submarine arm in the interwar years and directed its operations against Allied shipping until the tide turned decisively against U-boats in 1943.

In his political testament, dictated in the Berlin bunker shortly before his suicide on 30 April 1945, Adolf Hitler named Dönitz — rather than Hermann Göring or Heinrich Himmler — as his successor as head of state (President), reflecting Dönitz's demonstrated loyalty and lack of independent political ambition. Dönitz led the brief Flensburg Government for three weeks, presiding over the final German surrender (Reims, 7 May; Karlshorst, 8/9 May 1945) while using the interval chiefly to evacuate as many German soldiers and civilians as possible westward, away from the advancing Red Army. He was tried at Nuremberg and convicted of war crimes (principally waging aggressive war and the unrestricted submarine campaign), serving ten years.

## Significance

Dönitz's improbable succession to head of state — a loyal military technocrat rather than a senior Nazi party figure — reflected Hitler's final calculation that only military obedience, not ideological zeal, could manage the collapse.
