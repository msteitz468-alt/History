# Sara Delano Roosevelt

Sara Ann Delano Roosevelt (1854–1941) was the mother of Franklin D. Roosevelt and, for most of his life, the dominant maternal figure in his household — a formidable and wealthy scion of the Delano merchant-trading fortune who married James Roosevelt (a widower twice her age) in 1880 and devoted herself to her only child's upbringing and advancement.

She financed and directed much of Franklin's early formation — his education at Groton under Endicott Peabody, his entry into Harvard and New York society, and his 1910 entry into politics — and remained a controlling presence within the Roosevelt household at Hyde Park and in New York even after his 1905 marriage to Eleanor Roosevelt, with whom she had a long, difficult relationship over authority in the household and the raising of Franklin and Eleanor's children.

Her wealth and social standing gave Franklin a financial cushion and social credibility valuable to his early political career, and biographers (notably Jean Edward Smith) treat her formative influence — for both its advantages and its psychological costs — as significant to understanding Franklin's character, his polio-era resilience, and his complicated domestic life. She died in September 1941, shortly before the United States entered the Second World War.
