# Mencius (Mengzi)

The "**Second Sage**" of the Confucian tradition (Mengzi, 391–308 BCE) — after Confucius its most
important thinker — who developed Confucian ethics into a powerful doctrine of the **innate goodness of
human nature** and of **benevolent government**, recorded in the book that bears his name (now ingested
as **[[mencius-ivanhoe|Mencius]]**, trans. P. J. Ivanhoe / Irene Bloom).

## Life

A native of the small state of **Zou**, Mencius lived in the violent **[[warring-states-period-475-bce|Warring
States period]]**, when the [[zhou-dynasty|Zhou]] king ruled in name only and rival lords styled
themselves "king." Like Confucius before him, he travelled among the courts — notably **King Hui of
Liang** and **King Xuan of Qi** — pressing rulers to govern humanely, and, finding no lasting patron,
retired to teach.

## Thought

- **Human nature is good.** Mencius's signature doctrine, argued against **Gaozi** (who held nature
  morally neutral): all people are born with **"four sprouts" (beginnings)** of virtue — the heart of
  **compassion** (the root of *ren*, humaneness), of **shame** (*yi*, righteousness), of **deference**
  (*li*, propriety), and of **right-and-wrong** (*zhi*, wisdom). Famously, anyone seeing a child about
  to fall into a well feels instant alarm — proof of innate moral feeling. Virtue is the **cultivation**
  of these sprouts, like tending plants.
- **Benevolent government (*ren zheng*).** A true king wins the realm not by force but by **caring for
  the people's welfare** — secure livelihood, light taxes, education; "the people are the most precious;
  the ruler is the lightest."
- **The right of rebellion.** Extending the **[[mandate-of-heaven|Mandate of Heaven]]**, Mencius held
  that a ruler who becomes a tyrant **forfeits the Mandate** and may rightly be removed — the killing of
  the tyrant Zhòu was the execution of "a mere fellow," not regicide.
- **The cultivation of the self** and the "flood-like *qi*" nourished by righteousness.

## Significance

Mencius gave Confucianism its **optimistic theory of human nature** and its boldest **political
ethics** — the people's primacy and the conditional legitimacy of rulers. Canonized in the Song as one
of the **Four Books**, the *Mencius* became, with the [[confucius-analects-watson|Analects]], a core
text of Chinese education and thought, and the great counter-pole to **Xunzi's** view that human nature
is bad and to the **[[han-feizi-watson|Legalist]]** reliance on law and punishment.
