# Rudolf Höss

**Rudolf Höss** (1901–1947) was the longest-serving commandant of [[auschwitz|Auschwitz]] and the single most important operational manager of mass murder in the KL system — the man who oversaw the invention of the Auschwitz gas chamber and the running of the largest killing site of [[the-holocaust|the Holocaust]]. His remarkably detailed postwar memoir, written in a Kraków cell, is a central and self-serving primary source. Documented from [[wachsmann-kl-concentration-camps-2015|Wachsmann, *KL* (2015)]].

## Career

A convicted Weimar-era political murderer, Höss followed the classic Camp SS careerist path through [[dachau|Dachau]] (from 1934) and [[sachsenhausen|Sachsenhausen]] before being made commandant of the new Auschwitz camp in 1940. He approved the **first experimental gassings with Zyklon B** in autumn 1941 (Soviet POWs and sick prisoners), relocated the killing to the crematorium morgue — "the prototype of the death factory" — and briefed [[adolf-hitler|Adolf]] Eichmann on the method. Under his command Auschwitz became the hybrid labour/death camp at the centre of the Final Solution, and Himmler personally promoted him during his July 1942 visit. Höss visited Chełmno and Treblinka to refine cremation and gassing, competing with Globocnik to be "the greatest mass murderer." In November 1943 he moved to WVHA Office D I as Pohl's "main troubleshooter," returning to oversee the murder of Hungarian Jews in 1944 and to monitor the death-march evacuations in 1945.

## Character and Sources

Höss embodied Wachsmann's picture of the perpetrator who normalized genocide as administration: he attended gassings to model "cold and heartless" toughness for his men, kept his family in an idyllic villa beside the camp (his wife called him the "special commissioner for the extermination of Jews"), and wrote of the dying Soviet POWs as "no longer human beings." Captured in 1946 disguised as a farmhand named "Franz Lang," he testified at Nuremberg, wrote his memoir and analytical portraits of the SS, was tried in Poland, and was **hanged at Auschwitz in April 1947**. Wachsmann treats his writings as invaluable but consistently self-exculpating.

## Counterfactual Significance

Höss is the case study in how a mid-level functionary's organizational competence — not ideological frenzy alone — made industrialized mass murder work. His memoir also makes him, uniquely, a perpetrator whose inner rationalizations are documented at length, a key to the historiography of the "ordinary" génocidaire.
