# Jacqueline Kennedy

**Summary page.** Jacqueline Lee Bouvier Kennedy (later Onassis, 1929–1994) was First Lady of the United States (1961–1963), wife of President John F. Kennedy. This page provides the network summary; detailed treatment of her role and perspective appears through the JFK biography and related pages.

## Overview

Aristocratic, cultured, and reserved, Jacqueline Bouvier married the ambitious Senator John F. Kennedy in 1953. She brought elegance, languages, and social polish that complemented his political drive. As First Lady she restored the White House interiors, promoted the arts, and curated the public image of "Camelot." Privately the marriage faced severe strains from JFK's chronic health issues, infidelities, and the demands of political life; the loss of their infant son Patrick in August 1963 and the president's assassination three months later marked a traumatic close.

## Key Developments

- Courtship and marriage (1952–53): Introduced via Martha Bartlett; first Hyannis Port visit July 1952 (Jackie viewed as "perfect asset" politically; not yet lovers; "it was work"); wedding Sept 12 1953 Newport (best man Bobby; Joe: "a good calculation").
- Early strains (1953–56): separate vacations; Arabella stillbirth 1956 (Jack delayed return from yacht); Gunilla/Joan affairs exposure; Jackie slapped Jack ("too much bad blood"); Joe’s $100k deal; she set "rules of engagement" ("No more lies... I had to choose").
- 1963 evolution and closeness: Patrick’s birth/death ("I haven’t been the best husband... by painful, I mean shameful"); "After everything you’ve been through, you really do love him... I do"; planned private 1964 vow renewal at Hammersmith ("it’s we who made him"); affectionate anniversary; last night together before Dallas.
- Dallas and aftermath: Present in motorcade; Parkland rituals (kissed foot and lips; "His mouth was so beautiful"); "What a waste. All of it. Just a waste" (crumpled note); 1964 oral history key primary source on marriage and crises.
