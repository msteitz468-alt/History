# Maria Cosway

Maria Cosway (1760–1838) was an Italian-English painter, musician, and educator; the most significant romantic and sentimental attachment in Thomas Jefferson's life after the death of his wife Martha.

## Life and Career

Born Maria Luisa Caterina Cecilia Hadfield in Florence to an English father and Italian mother. Trained as a painter; exhibited in London. Married the Anglo-Italian painter Richard Cosway in 1781 (a marriage of convenience that left her artistically and emotionally frustrated). In Paris in 1786 she met Jefferson; their intense but chaste or semi-chaste relationship ("sentimental adventure") produced famous correspondence, including Jefferson's "Dialogue between the Head and the Heart" (Oct 1786). She visited Jefferson in Paris, they toured, exchanged letters after her return to England and his to America. Later in life she founded a girls' school in Italy and became a religious figure.

## Relationship with Jefferson

The 1786 affair (while Jefferson was minister) was one of the richest private episodes of his European years. Malone treats it as revealing Jefferson's capacity for intense feeling, his conflict between reason and emotion, and his continuing grief for Martha. The relationship remained within bounds of 18th-century sensibility; they never lived together. Jefferson's famous letter balancing head (duty, reason) against heart (affection, beauty) is a key self-revelation.

## Significance

Humanizes Jefferson during his Paris "Minister of Enlightenment" phase. Shows the limits of his emotional reserve and the role of cultivated European women in his social and aesthetic life.
