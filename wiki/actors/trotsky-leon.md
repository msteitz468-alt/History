# Leon Trotsky
## Biography / Description
Leon Trotsky (born Lev Bronstein) was born in 1879. A brilliant orator, writer, and organizer, he early became a prominent Marxist revolutionary, active in the 1905 Russian Revolution where he chaired the St. Petersburg Soviet. Initially taking an independent stance between the Bolshevik and Menshevik factions, he joined the Bolsheviks in 1917. He played a central role in masterminding the October Revolution and served as the first Soviet Commissar for Foreign Affairs, negotiating the peace talks at Brest-Litovsk. As Commissar of War during the Russian Civil War (1918–1920), he built the Red Army from scratch and successfully defended the Soviet regime. Following Lenin's death, he lost the succession struggle to Joseph Stalin. He was expelled from the Party, exiled from the USSR in 1929, and ultimately assassinated on Stalin's orders in Mexico in August 1940.

## Key Decisions and Achievements
- **October Revolution (1917):** Trotsky chaired the Petrograd Soviet and the Military Revolutionary Committee, directly organizing the armed insurrection that overthrew the Provisional Government.
- **Brest-Litovsk Negotiations (1918):** As Commissar for Foreign Affairs, Trotsky led the Soviet peace delegation, famously declaring a policy of "no war, no peace" to delay signing the humiliating treaty in hopes of a German revolution, before eventually resigning the post.
- **Creation of the Red Army (1918–1920):** Trotsky organized and disciplined the Red Army, utilizing former tsarist officers (military specialists) and establishing political commissars to oversee them. His strict leadership and mobile train-based command were decisive in defeating the White armies.
- **Left Opposition (1923–1927):** Trotsky championed the Left Opposition within the party, advocating for rapid industrialization, the collectivization of agriculture, support for international revolution, and a return to inner-party democracy against Stalin's bureaucratic centralization.

## Historical Significance
Trotsky was one of the foremost figures of the Russian Revolution and early Soviet state, second only to Lenin. His theory of **Permanent Revolution** argued that a socialist revolution in a backward nation like Russia could only survive if it triggered socialist revolutions in advanced Western countries. This stood in direct contrast to Stalin's theory of "Socialism in One Country." Riasanovsky notes that Stalin managed to defeat Trotsky by exploiting his superior control over the Party's organizational apparatus (Line 5851). Trotsky's exile and subsequent writings (such as *The Revolution Betrayed*) laid the foundation for Trotskyism, a major Marxist current critical of Soviet bureaucracy and Stalinism.
