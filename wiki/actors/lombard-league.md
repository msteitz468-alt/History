# The Lombard League

The **Lombard League** (*Lega Lombarda*) was the alliance of north-Italian
[[commune-movement|city-communes]] formed in **1167** to resist the attempt of
[[frederick-i-barbarossa|Frederick Barbarossa]] to reimpose direct imperial rule on
Lombardy. [[luscombe-ncmh-v4-2004|NCMH IV]] treats it as the decisive expression of
communal resistance to the empire.

## Formation and Victory

After Barbarossa asserted the imperial *regalia* over the cities at the Diet of
Roncaglia (1158) and razed **Milan** (1162), the affronted communes — Milan, Cremona,
Brescia, Bergamo, Mantua, Venice, and others — bound themselves by oath into the League,
with the backing of **Pope Alexander III** (against whom Barbarossa was simultaneously
supporting imperial antipopes). The League rebuilt Milan, founded the fortress-town of
**Alessandria** (named in the pope's honour), and in **1176** routed Barbarossa's army at
the **[[battle-of-legnano-1176|Battle of Legnano]]**. The ensuing **Peace of Venice
(1177)** reconciled emperor and pope, and the **Peace of Constance (1183)** recognised
the cities' **rights of self-government** (the election of their own consuls, jurisdiction
over their *contado*) in return for formal acknowledgement of imperial overlordship.

## Significance

The Lombard League's success secured the **practical independence of the Italian
communes** and marked the failure of the medieval empire's bid to make its authority
real in Italy — a turning point with long consequences for both German and Italian
history. It was revived in the thirteenth century against Frederick II, and stands as a
landmark example of the medieval **sworn league** of self-governing cities.
