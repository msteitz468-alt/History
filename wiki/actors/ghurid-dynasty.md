## Overview

The Ghurid dynasty was a Persianate Turco-Afghan dynasty centered on Ghur
(central Afghanistan) that supplanted the [[ghaznavid-dynasty]] in 1186 and, under
Mu'izz ad-Din Muhammad of Ghor (1149–1206), extended Islamic rule decisively into
northern India, laying the direct groundwork for the [[delhi-sultanate]].

## Role

Where the earlier Ghaznavid raids under [[mahmud-of-ghazni]] were largely
plunder-driven winter expeditions rather than territorial settlement, the Ghurid
takeover of the Ghaznavid domains in 1186 opened a phase in which "Islam stretched
significantly further across India" (Formichi). Muhammad of Ghor's general Qutb
al-Din Aybak (1150–1210) entered Delhi in 1193. When Muhammad of Ghor was
assassinated shortly after (1206), Aybak proclaimed his own autonomy in Delhi,
founding the Mamluk dynasty — the first of the five dynasties that together
constitute the Delhi Sultanate.

## Key Events and Processes

- Ghurid conquest of the Ghaznavid domains, 1186
- Ghurid entry into Delhi under Qutb al-Din Aybak, 1193
- Death of Mu'izz ad-Din Muhammad of Ghor, 1206, and Aybak's founding of the Delhi
  Sultanate's Mamluk dynasty

## Counterfactual Significance

The Ghurid conquest, not the earlier Ghaznavid raids, is the proximate cause of
permanent Muslim political rule in northern India; without Muhammad of Ghor's 1186
victory and Aybak's 1193 entry into Delhi, the establishment of the Delhi Sultanate
in its historical 1206 form would not follow from the Ghaznavid legacy alone.

## Historiography

Formichi draws a clear distinction, following Barry Flood and others, between
Ghaznavid-era raiding — oriented toward plunder and prestige vis-à-vis the Abbasid
Caliphate rather than proselytism — and the Ghurid conquest, which produced lasting
territorial control and dynastic succession in India. This distinction bears on the
broader historiographical debate over jihad-as-conquest versus jihad-as-proselytism
in the Ghaznavid/Ghurid period.
