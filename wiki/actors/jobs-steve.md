# Steve Jobs

**Steve Paul Jobs** (24 February 1955 – 5 October 2011) was an American entrepreneur, industrial designer, and business magnate. He was the co-founder, chairman, and CEO of Apple Inc.; founder of NeXT Inc.; and a major figure in the acquisition and growth of Pixar. Jobs is widely recognized as the driving force behind the personal computer revolution (Apple II, Macintosh), the digital music era (iPod + iTunes), the smartphone (iPhone), and the tablet (iPad), as well as the integration of hardware, software, and content into closed, consumer-focused systems.

## Summary

Born in San Francisco to unwed graduate students Joanne Schieble and Abdulfattah Jandali, Jobs was adopted and raised by Paul and Clara Jobs in Mountain View and Los Altos, California. He met Steve Wozniak while still in high school; together they built and sold the Apple I (1976) from a garage and incorporated Apple Computer. The Apple II (1977) became the first mass-market personal computer. After a visit to Xerox PARC, Jobs drove the development of the Macintosh (launched 1984) with its graphical user interface, mouse, and insistence on end-to-end integration.

Power struggles with CEO John Sculley led to Jobs’s ouster from Apple in 1985. He founded NeXT (workstations aimed at education and creative markets) and acquired Pixar from Lucasfilm. Pixar’s *Toy Story* (1995) was the first fully computer-animated feature film; the company’s subsequent successes and 2006 Disney acquisition made Jobs a billionaire independent of Apple. In 1997 Apple purchased NeXT, returning Jobs as interim CEO (“iCEO”). He simplified Apple’s product line, restored profitability, launched the “Think Different” campaign, and forged a transformative design partnership with Jonathan Ive.

The iMac (1998) revived consumer interest; the iPod (2001) and iTunes Store (2003) upended the music industry with a closed ecosystem. The iPhone (2007) redefined mobile computing and communications; the iPad (2010) opened the post-PC tablet era. Jobs maintained tight control over design, retail (Apple Stores), and software platforms while battling pancreatic cancer (diagnosed 2003, recurrences 2008–2011). He stepped down as CEO in August 2011 and died two months later at his home in Palo Alto.

## Summary (continued)

Jobs’s leadership style combined visionary product focus with a “reality distortion field” that could inspire extraordinary effort or alienate colleagues. He insisted on simplicity, premium materials, and the marriage of technology with liberal-arts aesthetics. His closed-system philosophy (no licensing of Mac OS, tight iTunes/iPhone/iPad control) contrasted with Microsoft’s platform approach and the open hacker ethos of the Homebrew Computer Club era. Apple under Jobs achieved high margins with a small market share by controlling the entire user experience.

Personal life included early relationship with Chrisann Brennan (daughter Lisa born 1978; initially denied paternity), Zen training under Kobun Chino, a 1991 marriage to Laurene Powell, and three children with her (Reed, Erin, Eve). He reconciled with Lisa later in life. His 2005 Stanford commencement address distilled his philosophy around death, intuition, and connecting the dots backward.

## Historiography Note

Isaacson’s 2011 biography (based on direct access and extensive interviews) is the central narrative source for the wiki’s treatment of Jobs. It is supplemented by participant accounts (Wozniak’s *iWoz*, Ive studio recollections, Catmull’s *Creativity, Inc.*, various oral histories) and contemporary reporting. Debates persist over the degree of Jobs’s individual genius versus team contributions, the ethics of his management methods, the long-term viability of his closed-ecosystem model, and the balance between his personal flaws and the scale of his impact on consumer technology and design.
