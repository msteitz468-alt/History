# Edward Bates

**Summary page.** See Lincoln pages for cabinet context.

Missouri elder statesman, distinguished lawyer, former Whig congressman, and one of Lincoln’s three major 1860 rivals. Served as Attorney General 1861–1864. Initially viewed Lincoln as a well-meaning but inexperienced prairie lawyer; eventually concluded he was “very near being a perfect man.” Provided conservative, legal ballast in the cabinet and important opinions on the legal basis for presidential actions.

## Overview

Bates represented the conservative, border-state, old-Whig element of the Republican coalition. His unpublished diary and letters to his wife Julia offer a warm domestic counterpoint to the more ambitious rivals. Though he sought the nomination, he accepted the Attorney General post and became one of the steadiest supporters of Lincoln’s leadership. He resigned in late 1864 for family and health reasons.

## Key Developments

- Pre-1860: Virginia birth, move to Missouri, long legal and political career, brief service in Congress, respected elder.
- 1860: Dark-horse contender; supported Lincoln after nomination.
- 1861–1864: Attorney General; legal advice on habeas, blockade, emancipation; growing admiration for Lincoln.
- Post-cabinet: Return to family in St. Louis; death 1869.

## Significance

Bates’s transformation from skeptic to admirer is one of the clearest demonstrations of Lincoln’s effect on those who worked closely with him. His presence helped anchor the cabinet’s more conservative wing and reassured border-state Unionists.
