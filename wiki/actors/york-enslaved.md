# York (c. 1770s – after 1815)

Enslaved by William Clark; body servant, hunter, and full participant in the Corps of Discovery. Clark's "black servant" in the journals. York was a large, strong man, an excellent hunter and swimmer, and a source of fascination and diplomatic leverage among Native nations who had never seen a Black man.

**Agency on the journey**: Multiple journals record York hunting, scouting, and interacting independently with Native people. Some tribes treated him with special curiosity or respect. He appears to have used the expedition's unique circumstances to assert greater autonomy than his legal status allowed at home.

**Aftermath**: Clark eventually granted York his freedom (timing and terms debated). York reportedly returned to Kentucky, then lived in Indiana or further west; later accounts (including Clark family tradition and some oral histories) are inconsistent. He remains one of the least documented yet most symbolically powerful members of the expedition.

**Historiography**: Long overlooked or romanticized. Fehrman (2026) and recent studies restore him as a complex actor whose skills mattered and whose experience exposes the racial contradictions of Jeffersonian expansion. Primary sources: Clark's and other journals; limited post-expedition records.
