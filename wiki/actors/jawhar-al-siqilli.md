# Jawhar al-Siqilli

**Abu'l-Hasan Jawhar al-Siqilli** ("the Sicilian"; also *Jawhar al-Qa'id*, "the Commander," and *Jawhar
al-Katib*, "the Scribe"; c. 924–992) was the **general of [[al-muizz-li-din-allah|al-Mu'izz]]** who reconquered
the western Maghrib, **conquered Egypt in 969**, and founded **[[cairo|Cairo]]** and **al-Azhar**. A road in
modern Cairo still bears his name.

## From slave-scribe to commander

Of **Slavic (*Saqlabi*)** origin, Jawhar was brought as a slave to Ifriqiya and at about 24 entered the service
of [[al-mansur-fatimid|al-Mansur]] as a personal secretary (*katib*), accompanying him on the campaigns against
[[abu-yazid-al-nukkari|Abu Yazid]] and being manumitted afterward as a "Client of the Commander of the
Faithful." Under al-Mu'izz he headed the chancery, then rose to military command: between **958 and 960** he led
the Kutama army that recovered the western North African lands as far as the **Atlantic**, after which he was
titled **al-Qa'id** ("the Commander").

## The conquest of Egypt and the founding of Cairo

Al-Mu'izz entrusted him with the **[[fatimid-conquest-of-egypt-969|Egyptian expedition]]**: at the head of a
reported 100,000 troops he set out in **February 968**, entered Egypt in **May 969**, and secured **Fustat**
under the celebrated ***aman*** — a written guarantee, signed by Jawhar on al-Mu'izz's terms, protecting the
lives, property, and religious practice of Muslims, Christians, and Jews. He founded a new royal city north of
Fustat that same evening — first called al-Mansuriyya, later renamed **al-Qahira al-Mu'izziya (Cairo)** — laid
out as a square grid echoing [[mahdiyya|Mahdiyya]] and [[mansuriyya|Mansuriyya]], with the gates **Bab al-Futuh**
and **Bab Zuwayla** and the **Bayn al-Qasrayn** ceremonial square. In its south-eastern quarter he founded the
congregational mosque of **al-Azhar** ("the Radiant"), whose foundation plaque named its builder "his servant
Jawhar al-Katib al-Siqilli."

## Governor of Egypt

For four years (**969–973**) Jawhar ruled Egypt as governor, preparing the ground for al-Mu'izz's arrival:
restoring order, reforming coinage, and building the administration of the new capital before the Imam-caliph
took up residence.

## Counterfactual significance

Jawhar is the executor of the Fatimid pivot to Egypt. The deliberately generous, legally-framed *aman* he
negotiated — rather than a sack — made the conquest durable and set the tolerant tone of Fatimid Cairo; the
city and university he founded outlasted the dynasty by a millennium.
