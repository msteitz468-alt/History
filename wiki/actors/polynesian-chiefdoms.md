# Polynesian Chiefdoms and Archaic States

## Overview

The **hereditary chiefdoms of Polynesia** were, in [[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (McNiven,
Ch 22), "the most formal and stratified polities and ranked societies developed in Oceania" —
and, in their largest forms, **archaic states** ruled by divine kings. They emerged from the
shared ancestral society of **Hawaiki** (Tonga/Samoa) and, given the shallow time-depth of
Polynesian origins (<1,000 years), retained strong family resemblances across thirty-odd
island societies.

## Structure: Mana and the Chief

Polynesian communities were organized around the **chief's extended household**, with
commoner households, religious structures, and agricultural gardens. Paramount chiefs were
leaders of spiritual power — **mana** — held to descend **directly from the gods**. Ceremonial
life, including large-scale **tribute offerings**, centered on these chiefs. CWH IV notes a
broad correlation between **socio-political stratification and population size**: atoll
chiefdoms numbered a few hundred people, while the great stratified paramountcies of Tonga,
Samoa, Hawaii, and the Society Islands governed **tens of thousands**.

## The Great Paramountcies

- **The Tongan maritime empire:** centered on Tongatapu chiefs exacting **tribute across ~800
  km² of sea**, with ~30,000–40,000 people. Its monumental **langi** (chiefly tombs) used cut
  stone blocks weighing up to **50 tonnes**, and a canoe-wharf complex moved over 100,000 m³ of
  fill — all dating to ~500–600 BP.
- **Hawaiian divine kingship:** four island paramountcies (~200,000 people total) were welded
  into a single empire under **Kamehameha I** in the early nineteenth century — the process
  Patrick Kirch frames as **"how chiefs became kings."** Vast stone agricultural terraces,
  irrigation, fishponds, and temple complexes (**heiau**) attest to chiefly control of labor
  and resources.
- **[[rapa-nui|Rapa Nui]] (Easter Island):** chiefs ruled hundreds of coastal districts
  (*kainga*), erecting the monumental **moai** statues (up to 80 tonnes) on stone platforms
  (*ahu*).
- **Maori New Zealand:** ~100,000 people at contact, with chiefly militarization expressed in
  some **6,000 fortified, terraced hill-villages (*pā*)**, especially within the last 400 years.

## Significance

The Polynesian paramountcies are CWH IV's prime Oceanic example of **indigenous
[[state-formation|state formation]]** — the independent rise of divine kingship, monumental
construction, and tributary hierarchy on remote islands, achieved without metallurgy, writing,
or draft animals. They were the "worlds encountered by James Cook" in the late eighteenth
century. Their governance — **specialized, institutionalized, hierarchical, hereditary** —
contrasts sharply with the achieved, non-institutionalized leadership of
[[aboriginal-australians|Aboriginal Australia]].
