# Gustavus Adolphus (1594–1632)

King of Sweden (1611–1632) and the central figure of the seventeenth-century
**[[military-revolution|military revolution]]** — "the Lion of the North." Intervening in the
**[[thirty-years-war-1618|Thirty Years' War]]** on the Protestant side in 1630, he forged an army
whose **combined-arms tactics, mobile field artillery, disciplined volley fire by shallow linear
formations, and integration of cavalry shock with infantry firepower** set the template for modern
European warfare. He destroyed Tilly's Imperial army at **[[hubs/warfare/early-modern/thirty-years-war/battle-of-breitenfeld|Breitenfeld]]** (1631) and won, but was killed at,
**[[hubs/warfare/early-modern/thirty-years-war/battle-of-lutzen|Lützen]]** (1632).

His reforms — standardized lighter artillery, salvo fire, smaller and more flexible tactical units,
and a measure of national/standing organization — are treated by Doughty & Gruber as the hinge of
the military revolution. See the [[hubs/warfare/early-modern/thirty-years-war/thirty-years-war|Thirty
Years' War hub]] for the operational analysis.
