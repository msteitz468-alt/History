# Nikolai Bukharin

Leading Bolshevik theorist, longtime editor of *Pravda*, and (by the mid-1920s) the party's foremost defender of continuing Lenin's New Economic Policy — favoring gradual, market-mediated growth and an alliance with the peasantry ("enrich yourselves," in the popular gloss of his position) over forced industrialization. Bukharin allied with Stalin against the Left Opposition (Trotsky, Zinoviev, Kamenev) in the mid-1920s, only to be outmaneuvered himself once Stalin adopted forced collectivization and crash industrialization from 1928–29, branding Bukharin's caution "Right Deviationism." Removed from the Politburo in 1929, he was later arrested in the Great Purge and was the central defendant of the last major Moscow Show Trial (the Trial of the Twenty-One, March 1938), confessing under pressure to fantastical charges of conspiracy and espionage before being executed. He was formally rehabilitated by the Soviet state in 1988.

## Counterfactual Significance

Bukharin's NEP-continuation program represented the clearest alternative path for Soviet development on offer in the 1920s; his defeat marks the point at which the USSR committed irreversibly to forced collectivization and crash industrialization under Stalin's personal control.

## Historiography

Stephen Cohen's *Bukharin and the Bolshevik Revolution* (1973) remains the standard sympathetic biography and did much to popularize Bukharin as a "road not taken"; Kotkin's *Stalin* volumes situate his fall within Stalin's broader consolidation of personal power rather than a purely doctrinal dispute.
