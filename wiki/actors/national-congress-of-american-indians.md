# National Congress of American Indians

National pan-Indian organization of tribes and individuals that Washburn (CHNPA I.2 ch. 15) describes as the body “closest to official tribal-government spokesman,” contrasting with the more radical [[american-indian-movement]].

## Role

Twentieth-century pan-Indian organizations phrased demands for all Indians rather than single tribes; Washburn pairs NCAI with AIM (and Canadian counterparts: Assembly of First Nations, Métis National Council, Inuit Committee on National Issues) as more capable than individual tribes of presenting “the Native” case to non-Natives. NCAI participated with Ada Deer’s DRUMS and friendly anthropologists in achieving the Menominee Restoration Act of 1973, which one scholar called a signal of the official end of [[termination-policy]].

Vine Deloria Jr. served as NCAI executive director in 1964 before *Custer Died for Your Sins* (1969) defined a generation’s activist intellectual posture (Washburn’s scholarly-renaissance section).

President Joseph B. DeLaCruz, while criticizing Reagan-era policies, conceded: “Our dependency upon outside assistance is our Achilles heel,” urging tribes to use resource economic power—an admission Washburn cites in his dependency/market-initiative argument under [[tribal-self-determination]].

## Counterfactual significance

Without a tribal-government-aligned national voice, Washburn’s dual structure of “official” vs radical pan-Indian politics collapses; NCAI is the institutional hinge between IRA-era tribal governments and federal self-determination policy.
