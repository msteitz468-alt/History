# Stepan Razin

## Biography / Description
**Stepan Razin** (Stenka Razin) was a Don Cossack military commander and the leader of the largest social rebellion in 17th-century Muscovite Russia (L2426-2428). Razin first rose to prominence as a daring freebooter, leading Cossack raids down the Volga and along the Caspian Sea coast into Persia (L2426). 

In the spring of 1770, he launched an ambitious mass rebellion along the Volga River, calling for the overthrow of landlords, boyars, and central government officials (L2426-2428). He was captured by loyal Cossack authorities in 1771 and sent to Moscow, where he was publicly executed (L2428).

## Key Decisions and Achievements
* **Volga Campaign (1670):** Razin marched up the Volga River at the head of a growing army, seizing towns like Astrakhan, Tsaritsyn, Saratov, and Samara (L2428). In each captured city, he established Cossack-style self-government and executed local gentry and state officials (L2426).
* **Mass Mobilization:** Razin issued manifestoes promising freedom and land, attracting support from Russian serfs, runaway peasants, slaves, soldiers, and native Volga tribes (such as Chuvash, Mordva, and Mari) (L2428). His forces reached Simbirsk, growing to approximately 20,000 men (L2428).
* **Defeat at Simbirsk:** Despite the size of the rebellion, the lack of organization and military discipline among the rebels led to their defeat by regular Muscovite troops, who used Western-trained regiments to break Razin's lines at Simbirsk in late 1670 (L2428).

## Historical Significance
Stepan Razin became a legendary folk hero in Russian culture, commemorated in countless popular songs and tales (L2426). His rebellion demonstrated the deep social discontent produced by the final codification of serfdom in the Ulozhenie of 1649 (L2426, L2536). The threat of Razin-style anarchy reinforced the alliance between the Russian autocracy and the service gentry, setting the pattern of state repression and peasant resistance that persisted into the 18th and 19th centuries (L2428, L2528-2530).
