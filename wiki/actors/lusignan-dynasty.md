# The Lusignan Dynasty

The **Lusignans**, a Poitevin crusading family, ruled **[[cyprus|Cyprus]]** for nearly three
centuries (1192–1489) and at various points held the crowns of **Jerusalem** and
**[[cilician-armenian-kingdom|Armenia]]** as well — the three eventually combined in their
coat of arms. Their Cypriot kingdom was the durable Latin survivor of the crusading age, and
its dense dynastic entanglement with Cilician Armenia is a principal thread of the
**[[armenian-latin-integration|Armenian–Latin integration]]**.

## Cyprus

**[[guy-of-lusignan|Guy of Lusignan]]**, the dispossessed king of Jerusalem (after
[[battle-of-hattin-1187|Hattin, 1187]]), bought the lordship of Cyprus in 1192 from the
Templars — who had bought it from Richard I after the
[[richard-conquest-of-cyprus-1191|Third Crusade conquest (1191)]] from Isaac Komnenos.
Brother **[[aimery-of-lusignan|Aimery (1194/96–1205)]]** secured a royal crown from Emperor
Henry VI and erected a Latin hierarchy (Nicosia + three suffragans) — a model Levon I of
Armenia followed almost simultaneously. Succession: Hugh I (1205–18) → Henry I (1218–53) →
Hugh II (1253–67) → Hugh III of Antioch-Lusignan (1267–84, dual king of Jerusalem) → John →
**[[henry-ii-of-cyprus|Henry II]]** (1285–1324) → Hugh IV (1324–59) →
**[[peter-i-of-cyprus|Peter I]]** (1359–69). The house weathered the
[[war-of-the-lombards-1228|War of the Lombards]] against Frederick II, [[fall-of-acre-1291|Acre
(1291)]], Amaury of Tyre's coup (1306–10), Peter's [[sack-of-alexandria-1365|Alexandria
crusade (1365)]], and the [[genoese-invasion-of-cyprus-1373|Genoese climacteric (1373–74)]].
Full political narrative: [[kingdom-of-cyprus]]; standard monograph:
[[edbury-kingdom-cyprus-crusades-1991|Edbury (1991)]].

## Links with Cilician Armenia

Cyprus and Cilician Armenia intermarried repeatedly and sheltered each other's exiles:
Armenian refugees from the Mamluk assaults of the 1320s–30s settled permanently on Cyprus
(founding monasteries such as **Sourp Magar**); **Henry I** married Hetum I's sister
Stephanie; **Levon I** of Armenia married Amaury's sister Sybille (1210). After the
assassination of **Levon IV** (1341) and the collapse of the Roupenid–Hetumid line, the
Cypriot Lusignan house took the **Armenian throne** itself — Constantine II (Guy de
Lusignan), Constantine III, Constantine IV, and finally the half-Lusignan **Levon V**, the
last king, deposed at the [[fall-of-cilician-armenia-1375|fall of Sis (1375)]]. On Levon V's
death in **1393**, **James I** of Cyprus assumed the empty Armenian crown, formally uniting
the three titles.

## Decline

Lusignan Cyprus passed to **Venice** in 1489 (the abdication of Caterina Cornaro), and the
island fell to the **Ottomans** in 1571.

## Historiography

The Cyprus–Cilicia connection is treated at length in
[[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian (2000)]], who frames the two kingdoms'
histories as "specifically interdependent" — and argues, from a pro-Armenian standpoint, that
Lusignan enforcement of Latin rites helped fracture and doom Cilician Armenia. The political
history of the Cypriot house itself is anchored by
[[edbury-kingdom-cyprus-crusades-1991|Edbury, *The Kingdom of Cyprus and the Crusades*
(1991)]], reappraising Hill in light of post-1950s crusade scholarship (Riley-Smith, Richard,
Hamilton).
