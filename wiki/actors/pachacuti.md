# Pachacuti

**Pachacuti Inca Yupanqui** (Quechua *Pachakutiq*, "earth-shaker" / "cataclysm"; r. c. 1438–1471)
is, in the standard chronicle tradition, the ninth Sapa Inca and the founder of the imperial
[[inca-empire|Inca state (Tawantinsuyu)]]. He transformed a regional chiefdom centered on
[[cuzco|Cuzco]] into the largest empire of the pre-Columbian Americas.

## Role and key decisions

- **The Chanca war.** The conventional origin narrative has the young prince repelling an
  invasion by the rival Chanca confederation after his father Viracocha Inca and the heir
  Urco fled — the victory that legitimated his seizure of power and launched Inca expansion.
- **The rebuilding of Cuzco.** Pachacuti is credited with the monumental replanning of
  [[cuzco|Cuzco]]'s ~40-hectare ceremonial core (reportedly in the shape of a puma) and the
  founding of the Coricancha sun temple as the empire's ritual heart.
- **Imperial institutions.** His reign is associated with the consolidation of the systems
  that defined Inca rule: the [[qhapaq-nan|royal road network]], state storehouses, the
  *mit'a* labor levy, and — crucially — **[[split-inheritance-and-royal-mummies|split
  inheritance]]**, whereby a dead ruler's *panaca* retained his estates and venerated mummy,
  forcing each successor to conquer new lands of his own. This institution made perpetual
  expansion structurally necessary and is part of the deeper
  [[andean-state-formation|Andean state-formation]] dynamic.

## Counterfactual significance

The chronicle figure of Pachacuti compresses what was probably a longer, more collective
process of Inca expansion into a single founder-reformer. Archaeology suggests Inca control of
the Cuzco basin was consolidating by the early 1300s, before the conventional Pachacuti
narrative. Whether or not a single architect existed, the institutional package attributed to
him — above all split inheritance — supplied the engine of relentless conquest that built, and
ultimately overstrained, the empire that fell to [[spanish-conquest-of-peru-1532|Pizarro in
1532]]. Treated in [[daltroy-incas-2004|D'Altroy's *The Incas*]].
