# Cymru Fydd ("Wales To Be")

A Welsh nationalist political movement founded in 1886 within the Welsh Liberal Party, campaigning chiefly for Welsh home rule (on the Irish model), disestablishment of the Church of England in Wales, and greater recognition of Welsh cultural and linguistic distinctiveness. A young David Lloyd George was among its most prominent early advocates. The movement attempted in the mid-1890s to fuse with the North and South Wales Liberal Federations into a single national Welsh Liberal organization, but collapsed in January 1896 after a hostile reception in Newport exposed a deep regional split — industrial, heavily anglicized south-east Wales resisted subordination to a movement seen as representing rural, Welsh-speaking north and west Wales.

## Counterfactual Significance

Cymru Fydd's failure to fuse Welsh Liberalism into a unified nationalist vehicle meant Wales did not develop an Irish Home Rule-style party machine in this period; Welsh political nationalism would not find comparably organized expression again until the founding of Plaid Cymru in 1925.

## Historiography

Standard Welsh political historiography (K. O. Morgan) treats the 1896 Newport split as exposing the underlying industrial/rural and anglicized/Welsh-speaking divides within Wales that a purely cultural nationalism could not paper over.
