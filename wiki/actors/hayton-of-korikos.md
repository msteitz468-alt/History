# Hayton of Korikos (Het'um the Historian)

**Hayton (Het'um) of Korikos** was an Armenian prince of the Hetumid house, lord of the
coastal castle of **Korikos**, nephew of King **[[hetum-i-of-cilician-armenia|Hetum I]]**,
and the author of the most influential medieval Western account of the Mongols and the
crusading East. He embodies the **[[armenian-latin-integration|Armenian–Latin integration]]**
the [[cilician-armenian-kingdom|Cilician kingdom]] rested on: an Armenian noble who became a
Premonstratensian canon in Cyprus, a client of the **papal curia**, and a propagandist for a
new crusade in alliance with the **[[mongol-empire|Mongols]]**.

## La Fleur des Histoires de la Terre d'Orient

Hayton dictated his chronicle — *La Fleur des Histoires de la Terre d'Orient* (the "Flower of
the Histories of the East") — to the scribe **Nicolas Falcon at Poitiers in 1307**, who
rendered it into Latin for **Pope Clement V**. Its four books cover the geography of Asia,
the rulers of Asia, Mongol history, and — in Book IV — a **proposal for a new crusade**
(*Passage to the Holy Land*) coordinated with the Ilkhanid Mongols against the
[[mamluk-sultanate|Mamluks]]. Widely translated (German 1534, Italian 1559, Dutch 1563; Tudor
English versions *A Lytell Cronycle* and *The Floure of Histories*, c. 1515–20, printed by
Pynson), it shaped Western crusading imagination for two centuries.

## Political Role and Reliability

Hayton was a partisan actor as well as a historian. [[ghazarian-armenian-kingdom-cilicia-2000|Ghazarian]]
suspects him of intrigue against King **Hetum II** — a possible hand in Sempad's usurpation
and in the 1306 rebellion of Amaury of Tyre against Henry II of Cyprus — and notes he offered
the curia 10,000 florins to recognize Amaury. He appears as *"Haytonus dux generalis"* at the
Council of Adana (1314). As a source his chronicle is therefore **doubly partisan**: pro-Mongol,
pro-crusade, and pro-union with Rome. It is invaluable for the **terms and significance of
[[hetum-i-of-cilician-armenia|Hetum I's]] Mongol alliance** but must be read as advocacy.
