# Nikolai Yezhov (Ezhov)

Nikolai Ivanovich Yezhov (also transliterated Ezhov) was head of the NKVD from September 1936 to November 1938. He presided over the height of the Great Terror (Ezhovshchina), including the Moscow show trials, the military purge, mass operations against "former kulaks, criminals, and other anti-Soviet elements," and the quota system of arrests and executions.

Yezhov was himself arrested, tried, and executed in 1940 after being replaced by Beria. He became the scapegoat for the worst excesses while Stalin remained above the apparatus.

Key figure in analyses of how terror became institutionalized statecraft under Stalin.
