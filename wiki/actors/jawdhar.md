# Ustadh Jawdhar

**Ustadh Jawdhar** ("the Teacher," a euphemistic title for a eunuch; d. 973) was the **most senior
administrator and most trusted confidant of the first four [[fatimid-caliphate|Fatimid]] Imam-caliphs** — a
figure whose career maps the growth of the Fatimid state from [[mahdiyya|Mahdiyya]] to the eve of the move to
Egypt.

## Career

Of **Slavic (*Saqlabi*)** origin, Jawdhar was among the household slaves the last [[aghlabid-dynasty|Aghlabid]]
governor left behind when he fled in 909; still a youth, he was taken into [[al-mahdi-billah|al-Mahdi]]'s
service. Over more than sixty years he served al-Mahdi, [[al-qaim-bi-amr-allah|al-Qa'im]] (who entrusted him
with the secret of the succession), [[al-mansur-fatimid|al-Mansur]] (who made him overseer of the treasury and
the state ***tiraz*** textile factories, and left him guarding state affairs during the Abu Yazid war), and
[[al-muizz-li-din-allah|al-Mu'izz]], under whom he became chief administrator — advising on the navy, coastal
defence, grievances against officials, trade, and diplomacy. He accompanied the dynasty toward Egypt.

## The Sirat al-Ustadh Jawdhar

Decades later Jawdhar's private secretary **Mansur al-Azizi** composed the ***Sirat al-Ustadh Jawdhar*** ("Life
of Ustadh Jawdhar"), part biography and part **archive of 86 letters and conversations**, mainly between
al-Mu'izz and Jawdhar. It is one of the richest surviving windows onto the Fatimid court and everyday
administration — prized by modern historians precisely because it preserves internal documentary material
rather than later hostile chronicle.

## Significance

Jawdhar exemplifies the trusted **servant-administrator** on whom the Fatimid state relied — loyalty to the
Imam-caliph above tribe or faction — and his secretary's *Sira* is a key documentary source for the reign of
al-Mu'izz and the mechanics of the early caliphate.
