# German Labour Front (Deutsche Arbeitsfront) and Strength Through Joy

Founded 10 May 1933 on the ruins of the trade unions (dissolved 2 May, the day
after the co-opted "Day of National Labour"), under **Robert Ley** — the Nazi
Party's largest mass organization, and with its leisure subsidiary **Strength
Through Joy (Kraft durch Freude, KdF)** the regime's main instrument for
managing the working class. Based on Evans, *The Third Reich in Power* (2005),
ch. 5.

## Structure and labour regime

Collective bargaining was replaced by twelve state-appointed Trustees of
Labour (May 1933); the Law for the Ordering of National Labour (20 January
1934) made the employer "plant leader" and workers his "retinue." Ley to
Siemens workers, 1 November 1933: "We are all soldiers of labour, amongst whom
some command and the others obey." Work-books (1934/35) enabled state
direction of labour; Göring's Decree on the Duty of Service (22 June 1938)
allowed outright labour conscription — over a million workers drafted into
munitions and West Wall projects by the war. Nominal hourly wages in 1939
still stood at 98% of the 1932 level; real industrial wages passed 1928 only
in 1939, while hours rose past 50/week and deductions (Labour Front dues, KdF,
collections) could take up to 30% of pay. Evans's judgment: workers'
acquiescence was transactional — steady jobs after the Depression — not
ideological conversion; informal "go-slow" resistance was widespread by
1937–38 but never organized, with [[gestapo]] agents embedded on factory
floors at employers' request.

## Strength Through Joy

Founded 27 November 1933 (model: the Italian Dopolavoro). By 1939: 7,000
staff, 135,000 volunteers, automatic membership giving ~35 million members.
Package tourism grew from 400,000 (1934) to 1.7 million (1937) plus ~7 million
weekend excursions; eight owned cruise ships (one-class by design) carried
140,000 passengers in 1939; the 20,000-bed Prora resort on Rügen (begun 1936)
was never finished. Gestapo/SD monitoring of the cruises documented the
scheme's ideological failure — party bigwigs monopolizing cabins (the Madeira
cruise was nicknamed the *Bonzenfahrt*), a 1935 Norway cruise with only 10%
workers, mass drunkenness — yet KdF was genuinely popular: of 42,000 Siemens
workers polled in 1934, 28,500 had never before had a holiday away from
Berlin. Its "Beauty of Labour" department drove workplace improvements in
~34,000 firms — frequently paid for by the workers' own unpaid "voluntary"
labour. The KdF-car (Volkswagen) savings scheme collected 110 million RM from
270,000 subscribers by end-1939 (eventually 340,000); not one received a car —
Evans calls the scheme a device for financing rearmament through workers'
overtime.

## Ley and corruption

Ley (b. 1890; WWI pilot with a frontal-lobe injury; chemist at Bayer;
antisemitic newspaper publisher; Reich Organization Leader from December 1932)
presided over corruption Evans documents as institutional: confiscated union
funds, multiple Labour-Front-staffed villas, an entailed estate, a 1-million-RM
personal gift from Hitler (1940); construction chief Anton Karl paid over
580,000 RM in bribes in 1936–37; a Sopade agent judged "the corruption in the
Labour Front is vast, and the general standard of morals correspondingly
low." Ley's public drunkenness (replaced mid-visit by Göring as host to the
Windsors, 1937) was tolerated by Hitler in exchange for loyalty. Ley's second
wife Inge, an alcoholic after years of violent rows, shot herself in December
1942.

## Historiography

Timothy Mason's work on the working class under Nazism frames the debate
Evans engages (workers' latent opposition vs. integration); Shelley Baranowski
(*Strength Through Joy*, 2004) covers the tourism apparatus.
