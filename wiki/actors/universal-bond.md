# Universal Bond of the Sons of Men

The **Universal Bond of the Sons of Men** (later formally styled **An Druidh Uileach Braithreachas**, Gaelic for "the Universal Druid Brotherhood"; also known as "The Druid Order" and "The Most Ancient Order of Druids") is the first distinctively twentieth-century Druid order, founded by [[actors/reid-george-watson|George Watson MacGregor Reid]] in London c. 1910 and first brought to public notice at [[places/stonehenge|Stonehenge]] at Midsummer 1912. It is the central vehicle for the twentieth-century [[processes/druid-revival|Druid Revival]] in England, and the organization that established the tradition of robed Druids celebrating the summer solstice sunrise at Stonehenge.

## Origins and Character

The Universal Bond grew from Reid's **British Nature Cure Association** (founded 1906) and its universalist theology, which sought to synthesize Christianity, Buddhism, Islam, Zoroastrianism and other traditions into a single religion of nature, brotherhood and social reform. Its liturgy was drafted by Reid and drew on Hermetic Golden Dawn ritual forms. Women were admitted on theoretically equal terms — unusual among Druid orders of the period.

The order was distinctly syncretic and politically radical: Reid was a former trade-union organiser and parliamentary candidate who proclaimed the "Brotherhood of Man" as Druidry's core ideal and attacked poverty and oppression in his ceremonial speeches. The evening addresses at the "Double Circle" (a double Bronze Age burial mound on Lady Glenconner's land south of Stonehenge) drew crowds of up to three thousand and mixed socialist apocalypticism with dissenting Christian idiom.

The Bond's name was unstable and multiple for years: it appeared variously as "the Universal Bond," "the Sacred Bond of the Sons of Men," "the Universalist Church" and "The Holy Brotherhood of the Elect." Only in 1918, after years of growing Druid identity through the Stonehenge confrontations, did it settle on the Gaelic form *An Druidh Uileach Braithreachas*.

## At Stonehenge: The Ceremonial Record

- **1912**: Midsummer debut of five members; press photographers invited; identified as "sun-worshippers" and universalists rather than Druids.
- **1913–1915**: Annual confrontations with the landowner Sir Edmund Antrobus and then (after Stonehenge passed to Cecil Chubb) growing success; Reid adopted the title of "chief of all the Druids of England" in 1915.
- **1916**: First year of full access under Chubb; four ceremonies; up to thirty members in attendance.
- **1918**: Five services; first use of the Gaelic name; white robes with crimson hoods adopted (modelled on the Ancient Order of Druids).
- **1919–1924**: Acrimonious conflict with the Office of Works over ceremony schedules, admission fees, and the right to inter cremated ashes; the 1924 national controversy united British archaeologists in protest; permission for ash interment withdrawn. A fragment of a probable Druid ash-bottle was found by Richard Atkinson at the North Station Stone in 1956 and reburied.
- **1928–1932**: Smooth relations under an amiable new custodian; fifty-plus members; the standard "Rite of the Golden Dawn" ceremony (confession of faith at the Hele Stone → communion at the Altar Stone → oak-leaf burning → gong-sounding) was established.
- **1932**: Reid withdrew permanently after a dispute over sale of the Bond's magazine.
- **1938–1946**: The breakaway **Ancient Order of Druid Hermetists** (AODH) of G. W. Smith held ceremonies quietly during the war, including at the Double Circle.

## Post-1946 Schisms

Reid's death in August 1946 produced two immediate successor groups:

1. **Arthur Peacock's Universalist Church (British Circle)** — the non-Druidic heir; Peacock became the last Universalist minister in Britain before switching to Unitarianism in 1951.
2. **G. W. Smith's AODH**, which renamed itself "the Druid Universal Bond" and took over the ceremonial programme at Stonehenge, holding the dawn solstice position from 1949.

In December 1946 **Robert MacGregor-Reid** (the founder's son) gathered a rival faction at Bristol, proclaimed the Leamington Spa lodge the "Mother Lodge," and invented a founding lineage from John Toland (1717) and Stukeley. After Smith's death in 1954 Robert became the unchallenged successor and from 1955 commanded the dawn ceremony. He built bridges with Gerald Gardner (who attended his founding meeting in 1946 and went on to found Wicca), Lewis Spence, the Goursez of Brittany, and occultist allies. His initiation rites were explicitly based on Hermetic Golden Dawn forms; the order added equinox ceremonies at Tower Hill from 1956, and introduced the chanting of "awen" (creative inspiration in Welsh tradition) at Stonehenge in the late 1950s.

Robert's death in January 1964 produced the final schism: **Thomas Maughan** won a leadership election by a tiny margin; **Philip Ross Nichols** seceded to found the **Order of Bards, Ovates and Druids** (OBOD), which celebrated on Parliament Hill rather than Stonehenge and dissolved on Nichols's death (1975) before revival under Philip Carr-Gomm (1988). Maughan's Universal Bond continued Stonehenge ceremonies through the 1960s–70s (during which English Heritage took over the site and a growing Stonehenge Free Festival complicating access), until **English Heritage banned all access at the summer solstice in 1985**. The order thereafter arranged special access at other dates and "slipped into obscurity." It continues to exist.

## Fabricated History and Invented Tradition

A defining feature of the Universal Bond through all its phases was what Hutton calls "wholesale invention of history" escalating with each political challenge. Successive mythical histories claimed: the Bond had protected Stonehenge since 1643 (the date on an antique table in the Clapham temple); delegates had come annually since Tudor times; Stukeley had been chief in 1720, with John Aubrey, Toland, Iolo Morganwg, Charles Dickens and the Duke of Kent all members; the Bond had inspired the Scientific Revolution through a "Mount Haemus Grove" founded at Oxford in 1245; and every eighteenth- and nineteenth-century Druid society had been an offshoot of the Universal Bond. Robert MacGregor-Reid in the 1950s elaborated this into a complete pseudo-prehistory rooted in Atlantis mythology and Iolo Morganwg. Hutton characterises this as "a study in the invention of tradition, and a micro-history of how a modern religion can develop and progressively create and recreate a past."

## Significance

The Universal Bond matters to the history of the Druid Revival for four reasons identified by Hutton: (1) it established the Stonehenge midsummer ceremony as the signature public act of modern Druidry; (2) it triggered, in 1924, the first coordinated professional-archaeological campaign to claim exclusive interpretive authority over prehistoric monuments, setting the terms of conflict for decades; (3) it stimulated Stuart Piggott's 1968 book *The Druids* — the defining scholarly demolition of modern Druidry — through its occupation of the Stonehenge dawn ceremony in 1964; (4) it created the lineage from which OBOD descended, making it the grandparent of the largest Druid order of the late twentieth century.
