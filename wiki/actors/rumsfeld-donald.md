# Donald Rumsfeld

American politician and businessman who served as US Secretary of Defense
under two presidents — the youngest ever to hold the post (under Gerald
Ford, 1975–1977) and, decades later, the oldest (under
[[bush-george-w|George W. Bush]], 2001–2006) — during which he oversaw the
Afghanistan invasion (2001) and the Iraq War (2003) and its contested
post-invasion planning.

## Role and Major Decisions

As Bush's Defense Secretary, Rumsfeld championed a lighter, faster
"transformation" of the US military and pushed a rapid, relatively
small-footprint invasion force for the 2003 Iraq War; the subsequent lack of
sufficient troops for post-invasion stabilization, alongside decisions such
as the disbandment of the Iraqi army, is widely blamed by historians and
military officers for the insurgency that followed. Persistent controversy
over detainee treatment policies (including at Abu Ghraib and Guantánamo
Bay) further shaped his tenure, and he resigned in 2006 amid mounting
criticism of the war's conduct.

## Counterfactual Significance

Historians and retired officers have argued that a larger initial
occupation force and a different approach to de-Ba'athification might have
substantially reduced the scale of the Iraqi insurgency, making Rumsfeld's
planning decisions a frequently cited hinge point in the war's trajectory.

## Historiography

Rumsfeld remains a deeply contested figure: defenders credit his push for a
leaner, more mobile military doctrine, while critics (including many former
military leaders) hold his Iraq War planning primarily responsible for the
conflict's prolonged and costly aftermath — a dispute that remains largely
unresolved in the historical literature.
