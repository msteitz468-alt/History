# Aristides ("the Just")

## Overview

**Aristides** (c. 530–c. 468 BCE), surnamed **"the Just"** (*ho dikaios*), was the Athenian
statesman who drew up the **first tribute assessment** of the [[delian-league|Delian League]]
— the act that organized the alliance Athens would turn into an empire.
[[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH Vol. V]] (Ch. 3, Rhodes) treats the assessment as foundational; his
proverbial integrity became, in the moralizing tradition, the foil to [[themistocles|
Themistocles']] cunning.

## Career

A commander at [[battle-of-marathon-490bce|Marathon]] and rival of Themistocles, Aristides was
**ostracized in 482** (the famous *ostrakon* on which he wrote his own name for an illiterate
voter who was simply tired of hearing him called "the Just" belongs to this episode). Recalled
under the amnesty before Xerxes' invasion, he commanded the Athenian hoplites at
[[battle-of-plataea-479bce|Plataea]] (479). When leadership of the anti-Persian war passed to
Athens (478/7), Aristides was entrusted with **fixing each member's contribution** — deciding
which states would furnish ships and which would pay *phoros*, and setting the famous total of
**460 talents**. His reputation for fairness made him the ideal officer for an assessment that
had to be accepted as equitable; later tradition contrasted his honest poverty with the
profits of empire. After this he disappears from public life and died, by repute, poor.

## Significance

Aristides is remembered less for battles than for the **institutional founding** of the
League's finances — the machinery of [[delian-league|tribute]] that became the economic engine
of [[athenian-democracy|Athenian democracy]]. His by-name fixed a permanent ideal of the
incorruptible statesman against which later Athenian politicians were measured.
