# Bernard Law Montgomery (1887–1976)

British field marshal. Victor of El Alamein. Commanded Eighth Army in Sicily during Operation Husky.

Montgomery played the dominant role in reshaping the Husky plan. He rejected dispersed landings as too risky, insisted on concentrating the main effort on the southeast coast under Eighth Army, and effectively sidelined Patton's Seventh Army to a flank-protection role. D'Este is sharply critical of the resulting plan as overly cautious and responsible for the frontal battle of attrition on the Catania plain.

Montgomery later complained (privately) about the failure to cut off the German evacuation and recognized the "bitter" quality of the victory, but his post-war accounts and the official history are more positive.

## Sicily Role (D'Este)

- Imposed the final invasion plan after deeming earlier versions a "dog's breakfast."
- Directed the Eighth Army assault on the east.
- Fought the prolonged and costly battle for the Primosole Bridge and the Catania plain.
- Advocated (too late or ineffectively) for stronger action against the Messina evacuation routes.
- Maintained the "hero of Alamein" persona and high public profile.

## Historiography

Heroic figure in British memory. D'Este and other revisionist accounts emphasize how his dominance of planning produced a lopsided, conservative plan that failed to exploit Allied material superiority for a decisive result. His relationship with Patton is often mythologized as personal rivalry; the deeper issue was structural coalition command under a weak Army Group commander (Alexander).
