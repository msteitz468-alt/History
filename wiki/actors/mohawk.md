# Mohawk (Kanien'kehá:ka)

Easternmost nation of the [[haudenosaunee-confederacy]] (“Keepers of the Eastern Door”). Salisbury (CHNPA I.1 ch. 7) makes them the crucial conduit of Dutch guns, wampum, and later English power into Five/Six Nations politics.

## Overview

### Early seventeenth century

Mohawks continued attacks on Montagnais trading with French at Tadoussac. Quebec (1608) strengthened Montagnais; in 1609 and 1610 Champlain and armed French enabled Montagnais, Algonquins, and [[huron-wendat]] to rout the usually feared Mohawks. Henry Hudson’s 1609 Dutch expedition opened the Hudson; after Fort Orange (1624), Mohawks warred Mahicans for direct access and by 1630 routed them—thereafter free Dutch trade including guns for other Iroquois. Dutch attempt to emulate French military role failed in 1615 (traders captured with Mohawk–Mahican expedition vs Susquehannocks).

### Beaver Wars and wampum

Mohawk elevation gave the Five Nations unrivaled manufactures and southern New England wampum (after [[pequot-war-1637]], Narragansetts primary source). Mohawks attacked Algonquin/Huron pelt convoys to Montreal, diverted goods to Fort Orange, and took captives (most adopted). They distributed wampum throughout the Confederacy. After 1639 Dutch monopoly end, weapons flow escalated to full-scale war culminating in Huron destruction 1648–49.

### Mid-century setbacks and Covenant Chain

1664 large Mohawk force repulsed at a Mahican village; English seized New Netherland cutting Dutch arms; French expeditions twice raided Mohawk country 1666, burning villages; Mohawks joined French peace 1667 after western Iroquois (1665). During [[king-philips-war-1675]], New York–armed Mohawks attacked multi-colony Algonquian conclaves (Jan 1676)—turning point of the war. 1677 Covenant Chain treaties placed Mohawks/Mahicans as formal brokers with New England colonies.

### Eighteenth century and Revolution

Few Mohawks under William Johnson joined British Montreal attempts in the 1740s–50s while most Iroquois resisted enlistment. In the Revolution most Mohawks followed **Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea)**, English-educated Johnson protégé, for the British; after 1777 many fled as refugees near Montreal and Niagara. Haldimand grants settled Mohawks and allied groups permanently in Ontario (Bay of Quinte; Grand River) by 1785.

## Historiography

Colonial sources overstate both Mohawk terror and English control; ethnohistory stresses middleman strategy, mourning-war demography, and faction (pro-French Canadian Mohawk towns vs Albany orientation). See Richter; Salisbury ch. 7.
