# Christopher Marlowe

Playwright, poet, and (likely) government agent (1564–1593). The dominant figure in the London theatre when Shakespeare arrived. *Tamburlaine* (Parts 1 and 2) revolutionized English drama with its mighty line, overreaching heroes, and spectacular violence. *Doctor Faustus*, *Edward II*, *The Jew of Malta*, and *Dido* established a new tragic and historical mode. Marlowe was killed at 29 in a Deptford tavern brawl (officially a dispute over a bill, with political overtones given his espionage connections and the Baines note accusing him of atheism and treason).

For Shakespeare and his generation, Marlowe was the immediate predecessor and rival to be absorbed and surpassed. The Henry VI plays, *Titus*, and *Richard III* show clear Marlovian influence; later work moves beyond it. Ackroyd treats the shadow of Marlowe's early death and the "mighty line" as part of the enabling pressure on the young Shakespeare.
