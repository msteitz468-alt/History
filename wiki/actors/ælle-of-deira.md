## Overview

Ælle was the king of [[deira|Deira]] who founded or re-founded the Deiran royal dynasty in the late sixth century and was the father of [[edwin-of-northumbria|Eadwine]], the future overlord of the Angles. He also fathered Acha, who married [[æthelfrith-of-bernicia|Æthelfrith]], thereby forging a dynastic link between the two rival Anglian houses of Deira and Bernicia that would shape Northumbrian politics for a generation.

## Chronology

The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle A gives Ælle an accession year of 560, a reign of thirty years, and a death in 588, but Kirby regards this scheme as unreliable. The Chronicle's Deiran chronology was effectively derived backward from Bernician dates, yielding figures that conflict with Bede's evidence that Ælle may still have been alive when Augustine landed in 597 (HE II, 14). Kirby's preferred reconstruction anchors the chronology on Eadwine's birth: "Aelle's son, Eadwine, was 47 years old when he was killed (HE II, 20), so that he was born c. 586/7, which would seem to confirm the likelihood that Aelle belongs to the late rather than the mid-sixth century." Kirby infers Ælle's accession from Æthelfrith's annexation of Deira in 604 or 605 (after twelve years in Bernicia alone) and a Deiran Aethelric who ruled the kingdom between Ælle and Æthelfrith's takeover, placing Aethelric's reign at 599–604/5 and Ælle's accession somewhere between 569 and 572. Neither extreme of a 28- or 30-year reign is inherently credible given the unreliability of round numbers.

Ælle had a brother called Aelfric (HE III, 1) and a successor, Aethelric, who may have been another brother. The two Aethelrics — the Deiran one who succeeded Ælle and the Bernician one who was Æthelfrith's father — were sometimes confused by later chroniclers but were, Kirby argues, quite separate individuals.

## Political Significance

Like the Deirans generally, Ælle's people "included communities whose ancestors were established in Britain in the earliest days of Germanic settlement." Deira occupied the more settled and agriculturally prosperous zone between the Humber and the Tees, in contrast to the militarised Bernician frontier zone to the north. After an obscure preceding dynasty (ending with Soemil), the Deirans re-emerge into recorded history under what may have been a new dynasty with Ælle.

Ælle's daughter Acha married Æthelfrith, and this marriage was either a prelude to or consolidation of Bernician domination of Deira. Æthelfrith subsequently expelled Eadwine, Ælle's son, and Eadwine's long exile before reclaiming northern overlordship was one of the formative episodes of early Northumbrian history.

## Counterfactual Significance

Without Ælle's dynastic connection to Æthelfrith through Acha, the Bernician takeover of Deira would have had a different character. The intermarriage gave Oswald and Oswiu (sons of Æthelfrith and Acha) a legitimising Deiran bloodline that facilitated their own acceptance as kings of the northern Angles after Eadwine's fall.

## Historiography

Kirby treats Ælle's dating as fundamentally unreliable because the Chronicle A's scheme for Deiran chronology was controlled by its (equally uncertain) figures for the Bernician Æthelfrith. Round numbers (thirty-year reign) inspire scepticism. The calculation from Eadwine's birth year provides the best positive check: if Eadwine was born c. 586/7, his father's reign falls in the late rather than mid-sixth century. Bede's statement that Ælle was alive when Augustine came in 597 (HE II, 14) is consistent with Kirby's reconstruction.
