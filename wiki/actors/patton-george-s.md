# George S. Patton, Jr. (1885–1945)

US Army general. Commanded Western Task Force during Operation Torch (French Morocco landings) and later II Corps in Tunisia (after Kasserine) and Seventh Army in the invasion of Sicily (Operation Husky). Known for aggressive leadership, emphasis on discipline and offensive spirit, and rapid exploitation.

## Role in Operation Torch (from Dworak)

Commanded Western Task Force (~44,760 men: 3rd and 9th Infantry Divisions, 2nd Armored Division). Landings at Safi, Fedala, Mehdia, Port Lyautey, Casablanca area (Nov 8, 1942). Task force support under Brig. Gen. Arthur Wilson. Patton provided minimal guidance on logistics to his G4, Col. Walter Muller: "I don’t know anything about logistics. You keep me out of trouble."

Personally involved in clearing beaches: spent "eighteen hours on the beaches outside Casablanca trying to motivate the troops to clear the beaches." On D+1 (Nov 9) wrote in diary "The beach was a mess and the officers were doing nothing." Attack toward Casablanca on D+1 stalled in afternoon due to lack of food, ammunition, and transport despite light opposition; "Anderson’s units had run out of the food, water, and fuel needed to proceed toward their objective. The Jeeps and small cargo trucks that accompanied the force were proving inadequate for the task."

The Western Task Force suffered massive landing craft losses (242 of 378 damaged/destroyed at Fedala sector alone, nearly all non-combat causes) and port chaos. By Nov 16 a "continuous pile of miscellaneous supplies on the piers of Casablanca that averaged 10 feet high, 20 feet at the base, and approximately 700 yards in length." Docks "in really fine shape" only by Dec 23 (D+45). Patton later criticized the lack of integrated SOS planning "beyond the landing of troops on the beaches."

## Role in Tunisia and Sicily (from D'Este and related)

Commanded II Corps in Tunisia (after Kasserine) and Seventh Army in the invasion of Sicily (Operation Husky). Known for aggressive leadership, emphasis on discipline and offensive spirit, and rapid exploitation (notably the "left hook" to Palermo).

In Sicily, Patton chafed under Alexander's orders that kept Seventh Army in a supporting role while Montgomery's Eighth Army made the main effort. He secured permission for an independent advance westward that captured Palermo (first major European city taken by US forces in WWII). D'Este portrays him as the most dynamic senior Allied commander in the campaign, contrasting with Alexander's caution and Montgomery's methodical approach.

Later commanded Third Army in Normandy breakout and across Europe. Controversial figure: brilliant tactician and operational commander; disciplinary incidents (soldier-slapping) damaged his reputation.

## Key Actions in Sicily (per D'Este)

- Relieved Fredendall and restored combat effectiveness and morale to II Corps in Tunisia.
- Demanded and received the 1st Infantry Division ("Big Red One") for Husky.
- Aggressively pushed for westward maneuver once the beachhead was secure.
- Captured Palermo 22–23 July 1943.

## Historiography

Official accounts and popular memory emphasize the Patton–Montgomery "rivalry." D'Este argues the real story was more about Alexander's failure to impose a coherent plan and the structural problems of coalition command than personal feuding. Patton's operational contribution in Sicily is seen as under-appreciated relative to his later fame and controversies.
