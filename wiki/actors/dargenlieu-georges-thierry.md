# Georges Thierry d'Argenlieu

French admiral and High Commissioner for Indochina (1945–1947). A former Carmelite monk (“the Bloody Monk” to his left-wing critics), he exercised enormous power in Indochina, often without close consultation with Paris, and consistently pushed for a hard-line military solution against the Viet Minh.

## Role

Appointed by de Gaulle, d’Argenlieu viewed the preservation of French sovereignty in Indochina as non-negotiable. He repeatedly blocked or undermined negotiated settlements (including the March 1946 Ho–Sainteny accords). He supported and encouraged the aggressive exploitation of the Haiphong Incident and the decision to force the Viet Minh into open war.

## Key Actions and Character

- Instructed Valluy that French troops must respond with “decisive forceful action” if hostilities resumed.
- After the Haiphong bombardment, congratulated Valluy: “We will never retreat or surrender.”
- Operated with significant autonomy; French government in Paris was sometimes surprised by actions taken in his name.
- Combined religious/mystical language with uncompromising colonial defense.

## Legacy in the War

His tenure is widely seen as having made a negotiated modus vivendi impossible and having helped ignite the full-scale war that France was poorly prepared to win. He was replaced in 1947 by Émile Bollaert as part of a belated French attempt to pursue political solutions.
