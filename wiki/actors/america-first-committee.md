# America First Committee

## Overview
The America First Committee (AFC) was the foremost United States non-interventionist pressure group opposing American entry into World War II. Founded in September 1940, it grew to over 800,000 members across the nation and was led by General Robert E. Wood. Charles Lindbergh was the committee's most prominent and controversial speaker, delivering anti-interventionist addresses until the group dissolved immediately following the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor.

## Actions and Decisions
- **Anti-Interventionist Campaigning (1940–1941):** Organized massive public rallies and radio addresses arguing that the United States had the geographic capacity to defend itself without entering the European war.
- **Des Moines Speech (Sept 11, 1941):** Sponsored the rally where Charles Lindbergh delivered his infamous speech identifying the British, the Roosevelt administration, and Jews as the forces pushing the U.S. toward war, leading to a major public backlash.
- **Dissolution (Dec 10, 1941):** Voted to dissolve the organization immediately following the U.S. declaration of war against Japan, pledging its support to the war effort.
