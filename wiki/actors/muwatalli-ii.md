# Muwatalli II (Hittite king)

The Hittite Great King who **fought [[ramesses-ii|Ramesses II]] to a standstill at
[[battle-of-qadesh-1274bce|Qadesh]]** and held the Hittite hold on Syria at its
furthest reach. Son of [[mursili-ii|Muršili II]]. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]] (Chs. XXI(a) & XXIV, Goetze). Spelled
**Muwatallish** in the source; reign ~1320–1294 (volume) / ~1295–1272 BCE.

## Qadesh

When the rising [[new-kingdom-egypt|Nineteenth Dynasty]] ([[sethos-i|Sethos I]],
then Ramesses II) moved to recover Syria — and **Amurru** defected to Egypt —
Muwatalli prepared for the inevitable trial of strength. He assembled a vast
coalition "from the ends of the sea to the land of Khatti" ([[mitanni|Mitanni]],
Arzawa, [[ugarit|Ugarit]], the Anatolian vassals) and concealed it **behind the city
of Qadesh**. In Ramesses' Year 5 (~1274 BCE) his chariotry ambushed and scattered the
strung-out Egyptian columns in the **[[battle-of-qadesh-1274bce|battle of Qadesh]]**.
Though the day ended a tactical draw, Muwatalli **held the field, pursued the
Egyptians to Damascus, and kept Qadesh and recovered Amurru** (deposing Benteshina) —
a strategic Hittite success.

## Reign

To concentrate force for the Egyptian war, Muwatalli took the drastic step of
**moving the Hittite capital south from [[hattusha|Hattusha]] to Tarḫuntašša**,
carrying off the state gods and the royal dead, and leaving his powerful brother
**[[hattusili-iii|Ḫattušili]]** as viceroy of the northern frontier (king of
Ḫakpiš and Nerik) — a concentration of power that would let Ḫattušili usurp the
throne from Muwatalli's son after his death. Meanwhile **Assyria** under Adad-nirari
I exploited his preoccupation to overrun [[mitanni|Khanigalbat]] up to the Euphrates.

## Significance

Muwatalli kept the Hittite empire at its **maximum southern extent** and checked
Egypt's last serious bid for Syria — making possible the
[[egyptian-hittite-treaty-1259bce|peace treaty]] his usurping brother would sign. But
his removal of the capital and elevation of Ḫattušili sowed the **dynastic conflict**
(Urḫi-Teshub vs. Ḫattušili III) that weakened Hatti in its last century.
