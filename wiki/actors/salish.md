# Salish Peoples

Umbrella page for **Coast Salish** and **Interior Salish** peoples of the Northwest (Fisher and related CHNPA material treat them as dialect/regional clusters rather than a single polity). Future work may split coast/interior pages; this summary keeps Fisher’s free-text distinction.

## Geographic placement

- **Coast Salish dialects:** lower BC coast (east side of Vancouver Island and adjacent mainland) and Washington coast down to the Columbia River; **Tillamook** (Salishan) lived south of the Columbia on the Oregon coast.
- **Interior Salish:** east of coast mountains—Thompson, Shuswap, and related groups in lake/river valleys; part of Fisher’s less prolific, more mobile interior pattern.
- After Europeans arrived, [[kwakwaka-wakw]] pushed Salish as far south as **Cape Mudge on Quadra Island**.

## Social structure (Fisher contrast with northern coast)

Among Coast Salishes, social divisions were **less clearly defined** than among northern groups such as [[haida]]/Tsimshian; kinship tended to be **patrilineal**; **clans did not exist**. Coast Salish art differed and, to many European beholders, seemed less impressive than Haida art—a judgment Fisher records as outsider bias rather than a measure of cultural importance.

## Key processes

- **Fur-trade era:** Participated in coastal and interior exchange; less often named as the classic middleman polities ([[chinook]], Maquinna’s Moachat, Legaic’s Tsimshian) but fully within Native-controlled trade.
- **Land and resources:** Interior Salish (Thompson, Shuswap) among groups for whom land/water held spiritual personhood (rocks/water as souls of myth-age people); Sproat’s observation that some Indians “die if they lose their land” applies region-wide. Placer mining, fencing, and water monopolies hit interior livelihood; commercial canning from late 1860s competed with coastal “when the tide was out the table was laid” abundance.
- **Douglas policy:** 1850 Songhee treaties near Fort Victoria—title surrender, reserves at traditional villages/cultivated fields, hunt unoccupied land, fish as before—then treaty practice largely abandoned for want of compensation funds.
- **1880s losses:** Interior Salishes of eastern Washington among peoples who lost territory through imposed land-sale agreements in the 1880s (Hoxie: part of >70 million acres continental decade losses).
- **Religious adaptation:** Indian Shaker Church founded by **John Slocum**, Squaxin (Puget Sound Coast Salish), 1881—rose-from-dead narrative, healing, shaking trances akin to Coast Salish guardian-spirit healing; expanded to Oregon and BC; legally constituted 1892, incorporated WA 1910.

## Counterfactual significance

Coast Salish demographic weight around Georgia Strait / Puget Sound put them at the epicenter of settler urbanization (Victoria, Seattle); Interior Salish held strategic valley corridors. Umbrella diversity cautions against treating “Salish” as one political actor.

## Historiography

Anthropological literature long coast-biased; Interior Salish less served by general ethnographies at Fisher’s writing. This page’s umbrella slug is a Stage-1 scaffold choice—split if density warrants.
