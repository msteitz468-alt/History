## Role and Background

Winfield Scott Hancock, nicknamed "Hancock the Superb" by [[actors/mcclellan-george-b|George McClellan]] early in the war for his battlefield bearing, commanded the Union II Corps through most of the Army of the Potomac's major campaigns from the Peninsula through Appomattox.

## Gettysburg

At the [[hubs/warfare/long-19th-century/american-civil-war/battle-of-gettysburg-1863|Battle of Gettysburg]], Hancock played a central role on all three days: he organized the defense of Cemetery Hill on July 1 after the collapse of the Union I and XI Corps, and on July 3 his II Corps position on Cemetery Ridge bore the brunt of Pickett's Charge, during which Hancock was severely wounded while directing the repulse, remaining on the field despite his injury.

## Later Career

Hancock recovered to serve through the war's remainder and later became the 1880 Democratic presidential nominee, narrowly losing to James Garfield.

## Significance

Hancock's steady, aggressive corps leadership at Gettysburg — particularly his role in stabilizing the Union line on July 1 and anchoring the defense against Pickett's Charge — made him one of the war's most consistently effective Union corps commanders.
