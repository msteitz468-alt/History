# Gaius Marius

The *novus homo* from Arpinum who held an unprecedented **seven consulships**, saved
Italy from the northern tribes, and transformed the Roman army — the first of the
great military dynasts whose careers undermined the Republic. Treated in
[[crook-cah-v9-1992|CAH IX]] Chs 2–3 (Lintott).

## Role and career

Of **equestrian** family, Marius rose through military service (Numantia under Scipio
Aemilianus), the tribunate (119, a ballot law), and the praetorship. As a legate of
**Metellus** in the [[jugurthine-war-112-105bce|Jugurthine War]], he agitated against
his commander and — backed by **equestrian businessmen** and a wide popular canvass —
won the **consulship of 107** as a new man, the assignment of Numidia overriding the
Senate's procedure. He ended the war (though Sulla's diplomacy secured Jugurtha's
betrayal).

Recalled to face the **Cimbri and Teutones** after the disaster at Arausio (105),
Marius was elected to **successive consulships (104–100)** — contrary to the *cursus*
rules — and destroyed the **Teutones at Aquae Sextiae (102)** and the **Cimbri at
Vercellae (101)**. To raise his armies he enrolled the propertyless **capite censi**
(see [[marian-army-reforms]]). His sixth consulship (100) entangled him with the
radical tribune **Saturninus** (whose laws settled Marius' veterans); when Saturninus'
violence forced the Senate to pass the *[[senatus-consultum-ultimum|SCU]]*, Marius —
choosing order over his ally — suppressed and abandoned him.

### Ruin and the murderous return (88–86)

Marius' final years were a catastrophe. Coveting the **[[first-mithridatic-war-89-85bce|Mithridatic command]]**,
he allied with the tribune **Sulpicius** to strip it from **[[sulla|Sulla]]** by
popular vote (88) — provoking Sulla's **march on Rome**. Declared a *hostis* and
hunted out of Italy, Marius fled to Africa (the "ruins of Carthage"). When **Cinna**
was driven from Rome later in 87, Marius returned with a force of freed slaves (the
**Bardyaei**) and Italian troops, and the two **besieged and took Rome**, unleashing
a **bloody purge** of Sullan and senatorial enemies — Octavius (the first consul to
be beheaded at the rostra), the Caesars, the orator M. Antonius, Catulus. He
attained at last the **seventh consulship** "foretold" him (86) — and **died within
a fortnight**, embittered, probably of pneumonia. Sulla later had his remains
disinterred and scattered. His name was carried on by his nephew **Marius
Gratidianus** and his son, both killed in [[sullan-civil-war-83-82bce|Sulla's civil
war]].

## Counterfactual / structural significance

Marius is the hinge between the *popularis* tribunes and the warlords: his
people-granted commands (a precedent ending with Caesar, Pompey, and Crassus) and his
opening of the legions to the **capite censi** created the **professional, general-
loyal army** that made civil war possible. CAH IX is careful, however, to deny that
Marius or his contemporaries foresaw this: the army became an instrument of civil war
only after the Social War's bloodletting, not automatically through his recruitment.

## Historiography

Marius' reputation "never recovered" from the hostility of his enemies, above all
Sulla, and of Rutilius Rufus and Posidonius; **Sallust's *Jugurtha*** is the fullest
source for his rise, **Plutarch's *Life*** for the German wars. See
[[marian-army-reforms]], [[optimates-populares]].
