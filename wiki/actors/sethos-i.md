# Sethos I (Seti I)

The second king of the [[new-kingdom-egypt|Nineteenth Dynasty]] — the soldier-pharaoh
who **restored Egypt's empire in Asia** after the Amarna collapse and set the stage
for his son [[ramesses-ii|Ramesses II]]. His Horus-name, **"Repeater-of-Births"**
(inaugurator of a renaissance), announced his programme. Treated in
[[edwards-cah-v2p2-1975|CAH Vol. II, Part 2]] (Ch. XXIII, Faulkner). Reign 1318–1304
(volume) / ~1294–1279 BCE.

## Restorer of the empire

Son of the brief founder **Ramesses I** (Horemheb's vizier Pramesse), Sethos
inherited an Egypt whose "prestige had fallen low during the Amarna episode." His
first campaign (recorded on the Karnak reliefs and a Beth-shan stela) **re-secured
Palestine** — relieving the besieged Egyptian fortress of **Beth-shan**, taking
Gaza, Acre and Tyre, and chastising the ʿApiru and the Shasu. Later campaigns drove
north against **Qadesh and Amurru** and a **Hittite army**, briefly recovering Qatna
and Tunip, but he could not hold north Syria; he concluded a **treaty with
[[muwatalli-ii|Muwatalli II]]** that left the frontier south of Qadesh. (That the
Hittites kept Qadesh is why Ramesses II had to fight the
[[battle-of-qadesh-1274bce|battle]] there a generation later.)

## Builder and restorer

At home Sethos completed Horemheb's work of repairing the Amarna damage — restoring
hacked-out names of **[[amun-priesthood|Amun]]** with the modest note "Restoration of
the monument which King Sethos made." His monuments are among the finest in Egypt:
much of the **Karnak hypostyle hall**, his **funerary temple at Qurna**, the
**finest tomb in the Valley of the Kings** (KV17), and above all the exquisite
**temple of Osiris at Abydos** — with its **Abydos King List** (Menes to Sethos), a
key document for Egyptian chronology — and the enigmatic subterranean **"Osireion"**
cenotaph.

## Significance

Sethos I is the **architect of the Ramesside restoration** — the king who turned the
post-Amarna recovery into a genuine imperial revival and a flowering of art (his
Abydos reliefs are the high-water mark of Nineteenth-Dynasty craftsmanship). His
reign is the necessary prelude to the long contest of [[ramesses-ii|Ramesses II]]
with the Hittites.
