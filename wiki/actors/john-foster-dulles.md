# John Foster Dulles

U.S. Secretary of State (1953–1959). He was deeply involved in the 1954 Dien Bien Phu crisis, the U.S. decision not to intervene directly, and the subsequent construction of the Southeast Asia Treaty Organization (SEATO) and the commitment to the Diem regime after Geneva.

## Role

Dulles viewed Indochina through a rigid Cold War lens. He pushed for greater U.S. involvement while being constrained by Eisenhower’s caution, congressional opinion, and British reluctance. At Geneva he refused to endorse the final accords. He later became the architect of the U.S. policy of building South Vietnam as a bulwark against communism.
