# Cleon

## Overview

**Cleon** (d. 422 BCE) was the leading Athenian politician of the [[archidamian-war|
Archidamian War]] after [[pericles|Pericles']] death — the most prominent of the new,
**non-aristocratic demagogues** the [[athenian-democracy|democracy]] threw up, and the most
forceful advocate of the war. [[lewis-cah-v5-1992|CAH Vol. V]] (Ch. 9, Lewis) draws him from
a hostile tradition — Thucydides ("the most violent of the citizens") and Aristophanes, who
pilloried him — but credits his real impact on the war.

## Career

Of a tanning family (not the old aristocracy), Cleon made his name by **vehemence in speech
and manner** and a hold over the *demos*. He first appears opposing Pericles. 

**Kagan (2003)** details the class shift: "Cleon belonged to a new class of politicians in Athens: not aristocrats but rich men whose wealth came from trade and manufacturing, rather than from the traditional source, land. Such occupations were considered base and unworthy by the aristocratic code..." Aristophanes mocked him as tanner "whose voice 'roared like a torrent' and sounded like a scalded pig’s." Thucydides: "the most violent of the citizens" (3.36.6); "harsh and bullying." Aristotle: "seems to have corrupted the people more than anyone by his attacks; he was the first to shout while speaking in the assembly, first to use abusive language there, first to hitch up his skirts [and move about] while addressing the people..." (Range 2: PERICLES UNDER FIRE; Ch. 7)

His recorded
acts run through the central war years:

- **The Mytilenean Debate (427)** — after Mytilene's revolt, Cleon carried a decree to
  **execute the whole male population**; the Assembly reversed itself the next day (Diodotus
  arguing expediency over vengeance), but the episode shows both his ferocity and the
  volatility of the democracy.
- **Pylos and Sphacteria (425)** — Cleon, having taunted the generals, was sent to finish the
  blockade and, with Demosthenes, **captured the 120 Spartiates** trapped on Sphacteria — the
  war's greatest Athenian success, which forced Sparta to **sue for peace**. From Kagan range: detailed use of light-armed swarms (Demosthenes); “The promise of Cleon, mad as it was,” Thucydides remarks, “was fulfilled” (4.39.3). “In the eyes of the Greeks it was the most unexpected event in the war” (4.40). Athenians “showered their gratitude on the hero of the hour, Cleon... with the assembly voting him the highest honors in the state, furnishing meals at the state’s expense in the Prytaneum... providing him with front seats at the theater.” “From midsummer 425 at least until the spring of 424, when he was elected general, Cleon was supreme in Athens.”
- **New assessment and empire policy**: Cleon’s hand in raising tribute to 1,460 talents (over 3x prior); “tough and efficient collection... including some regions that had not paid in some time and others, like the island of Melos, that had never contributed.” “reflect Cleon s determination to restore the empire to its full size, to govern it with a tight rein, and to draw from it the greatest revenue possible.”
- **The rejected peace** — Cleon **pressed the Assembly to refuse** the Spartan terms and to
  raise its demands (the return of Nisaea, Pegae, Achaea, Troezen) — the chance Thucydides
  thought it a grave mistake to miss. He also proposed the **massacre of Scione**. In Kagan: moderates would have accepted alliance recognizing empire; Cleon argued for tangible gains (Megara, Boeotia) first, as peace on promises alone insecure. Athenians followed aggressive counsel.
- **Amphipolis (422)** — Cleon took the northern command against **[[brasidas]]**. He retook Torone by a coordinated land-sea assault (capturing the Spartan commander Pasitelidas), recovered Galepsus and other towns, and allied with Perdiccas and the Odomantians. At Amphipolis he positioned for reconnaissance and awaited Thracian reinforcements to encircle the city. When Brasidas' northern decoy prompted a withdrawal, a complicated left-wing maneuver created disorder on the vulnerable right. Brasidas struck the center; Clearidas the flank. "The Athenians, amazed by his daring and terrified by their own disorder, turned and ran." Cleon "fled immediately" but was killed by a Myrcinian peltast's spear from a distance while in the dangerous rear of the retreating right wing (not struck in the back). "We should not doubt his courage any more than his countrymen did." About 600 Athenians killed. He and his men were buried in the Cerameicus among the state's honored war dead. Cleon's earlier successes (Sphacteria, Torone, Galepsus) refute charges of incompetence.

## Significance

Cleon embodies the **second generation of the democracy** — the rise of leaders "vulgar both
in origin and in manner" (the sources' verdict) after the aristocratic founders. His
**death, simultaneous with Brasidas'**, removed the chief war-advocate on the Athenian side
and made the **Peace of Nicias** possible. Whether he was a reckless demagogue or an
effective war-leader the democracy needed remains coloured by the hostility of the only
sources (Thucydides and comedy) — a caution flagged in [[classical-greek-historiography]].
