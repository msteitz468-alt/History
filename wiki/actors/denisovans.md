## Overview

The Denisovans are an archaic human population identified in 2010 by Svante
Pääbo's team at the Max Planck Institute, initially from mitochondrial DNA
extracted from a single finger bone found at **Denisova Cave**, in the Altai
region of Siberia; a full genome was reconstructed from the same find within
the year. Unlike the Neanderthals, no substantial skeletal record has yet
been recovered — the Denisovans are known almost entirely through ancient
DNA. Per Fagan & Durrani (2020), they are treated as a species genuinely
distinct from both Neanderthals and *Homo sapiens*, despite recurring
interbreeding among all three populations — "hybridization was probably a
feature of human evolution throughout most of the past 7 Ma."

## Origin and Range

- **Divergence**: Denisovans split from the Neanderthal lineage roughly
  ~400,000 BP; the common ancestor of Denisovans, Neanderthals, and *Homo
  sapiens* is now placed earlier than ~750,000 BP.
- **Known range**: Denisova Cave, Altai Siberia, occupied by the population
  from roughly ~200,000 BP to as late as ~50,000 BP (based on later tooth
  finds at the site). Some Chinese fossils dated 350,000–60,000 BP may
  represent Denisovans, but no skull has yet been recovered to confirm this.
- **Interbreeding**: genomic evidence shows Denisovans interbred with both
  Neanderthals and with an as-yet-unidentified archaic hominin lineage, in
  addition to modern humans.

## The Melanesian Puzzle

Modern Melanesians and Papua New Guineans carry an unusually high proportion
of Denisovan ancestry — on average ~4.8% of their genomes — far more than any
population near Siberia itself. This is a genuine puzzle: how Denisovan
genetic material reached Island Southeast Asia/Melanesia from an Altai source
population thousands of kilometers away. David Reich's research suggests the
Denisovans themselves may have physically migrated and colonized parts of
Asia and the Pacific rim, rather than the ancestry arriving purely through
intermediary human populations carrying admixed DNA.

## Significance (Counterfactual)

The Denisovans are a reminder that the "Out of Africa" replacement model
requires substantial modification: modern human dispersal into Asia and the
Pacific involved absorbing genetic material from at least two distinct
archaic populations (Neanderthals and Denisovans), and possibly a third
unidentified lineage. Their existence was invisible to science until ancient
DNA techniques matured after 2010 — a single finger bone rewrote the
population history of Asia and the Pacific. See [[neanderthals]] and
[[../periods/behavioral-modernity|Behavioral Modernity]].

## Historiography

Denisovan research is entirely a product of 21st-century ancient-DNA
methods; the near-total absence of diagnostic skeletal remains means the
population's physical appearance remains essentially unknown, and its
taxonomic status (full species vs. subspecies) is argued largely from
genomic divergence rather than morphology. The mechanism behind the
Melanesian ancestry signal (direct Denisovan migration vs. more complex
population dynamics) remains actively debated. Source: Fagan & Durrani (2020),
*World Prehistory: A Brief Introduction*, 10th ed., citing the 2010 Pääbo
team discovery.
