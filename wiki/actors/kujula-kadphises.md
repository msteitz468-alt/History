# Kujula Kadphises

## Overview

**Kujula Kadphises** (r. c. 25–85 CE) was the founder of the [[kushan-empire|Kushan
Empire]]: the ruler of the **Guishuang** princedom who reunified the five
[[yuezhi|Yuezhi]] *xihou* of [[bactria|Bactria]] under his sole leadership and forged them
into one of the four great powers of the [[silk-roads|First Silk Roads Era]]. The 1995
**Rabatak Inscription** names him as Kanishka's great-grandfather, anchoring the dynastic
line ([[kanishka|Kanishka]] → Vima Kadphises → Vima Takhtu → Kujula).

## Rise and Decisions

- Named as a **prince at the court of the Indo-Parthian Gondophares** in an inscription at
  Takht-i Bahi dated **45 CE** — placing him in regional politics before his own rise; the
  *Hou Han Shu* says he was "more than eighty years old when he died," implying a very long
  reign.
- **Reunified the five Yuezhi princedoms** in the mid-first century CE — possibly
  catalyzed by Gondophares' conquest of the Kabul Valley.
- **Crossed the Hindu Kush**, evicted Gondophares from Kabul, and pushed south into
  **Kashmir and the Swat Valley**, adopting an Indianized Kharosthi-script title:
  *Maharaja Rajarajasa Devaputra Kujula Kara Kadphises* ("Great King of Kings, Son of a
  Divine Being").
- **Eclectic coinage** reflecting an emergent multicultural state: one copper tetradrachm
  series copies a **bust of [[augustus|Augustus]]** on the obverse with a curule chair
  reverse — material evidence of indirect Roman influence reaching Central Asia.

## Significance

Under Kujula and his immediate successors the Kushans came to control most of the major
east–west and north–south Central Asian trade routes, including the corridors to the
northwest Indian ports via the Indus. Numismatist **Joe Cribb** (1984) showed direct
Kushan control of **Khotan and [[kashgar|Kashgar]]** in the Tarim Basin at points in the
first century CE — possibly permitted by the Han general Ban Chao in exchange for Kushan
help against the [[xiongnu]]. Direct evidence for tolls or fees is lacking, but Kushan
wealth implies substantial revenue from trade transit.

## Historiography

Kujula's reign is reconstructed almost entirely from **coins**, scattered **inscriptions**
(Takht-i Bahi, Rabatak), and the Chinese [[han-dynasty|*Hou Han Shu*]]. His successors
**Vima Takhtu** (the "Soter Megas" long known only from coins until Rabatak confirmed him)
and **Vima Kadphises** standardized the copper coinage and struck the first Kushan gold —
see [[kushan-empire]].
