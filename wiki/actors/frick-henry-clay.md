# Henry Clay Frick

Industrialist (1849–1919). Built a dominant position in coke (essential fuel for steelmaking) in western Pennsylvania through the H. C. Frick Coke Company. Entered partnership with Andrew Carnegie; Frick's coke interests were integrated into Carnegie Steel. As managing partner (especially while Carnegie was abroad), Frick directed the hard-line response to the Homestead strike of 1892, including the Pinkerton operation. Later broke with Carnegie over business and personal matters. After the 1901 sale to U.S. Steel, Frick became a major art collector; his New York mansion is now the Frick Collection.

Frick was the operational counterpoint to Carnegie's public persona—more ruthless in labor matters, focused on day-to-day control of production. The Homestead violence occurred under his direct authority.
