# Raymond Spruance

U.S. Navy admiral. Commanded U.S. carrier forces at the Battle of Midway (decisive victory despite being outnumbered). Led Fifth Fleet in the Central Pacific campaign (Gilbert, Marshall, Mariana Islands, Philippine Sea "Marianas Turkey Shoot," Iwo Jima). Known for cool judgment, meticulous planning, and effective delegation. Later ambassador to the Philippines.

See: [[world-war-ii-1939-1945]], [[battle-of-midway-1942]], [[nimitz-chester]].

"Admiral Nimitz, as commander of the Pacific Ocean Areas Theater..." Nimitz (with subordinates) questioned Formosa; unanimously favored Iwo Jima/Okinawa for siege bases. "Recovering the Third Fleet from Halsey, Spruance—trusted by all except the carrier admirals—would command the joint task forces built from the Fifth Fleet in both operations." MacArthur approved Nimitz plan (to sink Formosa option). On Okinawa: "Only Spruance and Nimitz had the authority to order Buckner to change his plan, and once again the admirals shrank from conflict with the army." Spruance lost flagship; Mitscher shifted flags multiple times under kamikazes. Fifth Fleet heavy losses (64 ships sunk/heavily damaged; carriers Bunker Hill, Franklin, etc.) but maintained sea control. Nimitz signed surrender for US on Missouri (Halsey’s flagship); MacArthur presided. **From West Point WWII Vol 1 (range 4 extraction - Pacific Pearl to Midway context):**
- **Spruance (Task Force 16 actions at Midway, ~5089–5095):** “...they were followed by strike aircraft from the Yorktown, launched at seven o’clock a.m. One hour later, Admiral Raymond Spruance’s Task Force Sixteen, organized around the carriers Hornet and Enterprise, sent its planes into the air—a risky move, since the Japanese flattops were barely within strike range.”
- **Supporting attacks and futility of early phases (~5095–5100):** “During this interval, Army Air Forces B-26s flying from Midway’s still-operational runways attacked the Japanese carrier force, as did U.S. Navy torpedo planes. Around eight o’clock, B-17s and sixteen U.S. Marine dive-bombers from Midway plus two torpedo squadrons from the carrier Hornet launched high-level bombing, dive-bombing, and low-level torpedo attacks against the enemy carriers. These were futile despite great bravery and loss of life. Torpedo Squadron Eight, for example, lost all fifteen of its aircraft, and half the marine planes were shot down, mostly by veteran Japanese pilots in swift, high-performance Zero fighters.”
- **Critical window and outcome (10:20–10:30 a.m. dive-bomber success, ~6040–6050):** “Between 10:20 a.m. and 10:30 a.m., U.S. Navy dive-bomber strikes left three Japanese carriers blazing wrecks. Planes from the surviving carrier, Hiryu, exacted a measure of revenge early that afternoon with two attacks that crippled the Yorktown with two 550-pound bombs and three torpedo hits. A Japanese submarine would sink the stricken carrier the next day. A few hours later, around 5:00 p.m., Hiryu suffered catastrophic damage from four 1,000-pound bomb hits on her forward deck.” (Also: “Lucky timing was a major factor in the dramatic success of the American dive-bombers...” “Without centralized command and control, the Japanese CAP pilots had no sense of the overall battle. Instead of an aerial umbrella over the carriers, the CAP was a cheesecloth that left the big ships exposed to attack.”)
