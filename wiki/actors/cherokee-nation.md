# Cherokee Nation

Southeastern Iroquoian nation; among the principal targets of U.S. Removal; rebuilt in [[indian-territory]] after the [[trail-of-tears-1838]]. Colonial-era and Mississippian antecedents appear in other CHNPA chapters; this page integrates Michael D. Green, CHNPA I.1 ch. 8 (1780–1880), with colonial context from Salisbury (ch. 7).

## Overview

Few eastern nations suffered more in the Revolutionary War: summer 1776 attacks on backcountry settlements produced retaliatory invasions by Virginia and the Carolinas. Soldiers burned **some fifty towns** and tens of thousands of bushels of stored corn and beans; cut or burned hundreds of acres of fields and orchards; killed or confiscated several hundred livestock. Thousands fled into the forested Appalachians. Treaties of 1777 cost most land east of the Blue Ridge; Green: Cherokee society in the 1780s was “on the brink of disintegration.” Some **500 families** from Lower Towns crossed mountains to Chickamauga Creek / Tennessee River (present Chattanooga) under **Dragging Canoe**—center of opposition into the mid-1790s.

Hopewell (1785–86) recognized wartime cessions plus a small additional tract while guaranteeing boundaries (denounced by Georgia and North Carolina). Squatter cycle: Holston 1791, Tellico 1798; almost annual U.S.–Cherokee negotiations 1797–1806. Colonial background (Salisbury): Cherokee–English trade; Yamasee War alliance choices; 1759–61 Cherokee War and 1761 British burning of homes/crops; 1776–77 massive cessions after town-burning.

## Civilization peak and constitutional sovereignty

Widely recognized as most “civilized” southern nation under the [[us-civilization-program]]: ~**14,000** (1826), **16,542** (1835). Federal tools/livestock/artisans; Moravian school 1804; ABCFM from 1817 (eight schools, 882 students over 16 years). Women still primary agriculturalists except slaveowning families (**7.4%** in 1835); plows nearly equal farms by end 1820s; farms 1835 avg **14 acres**. Men: livestock (end 1820s: 7,600 horses, 22,000 cattle, ~42,000 hogs, >2,500 sheep); turnpikes, ferries, stores, taverns, mills. 1835: >1,000 read English; **4,000** read Cherokee. **Sequoyah** 85-syllable syllabary **1821**; *Cherokee Phoenix* weekly bilingual **1828** at [[new-echota]]. Political: National Council; 1808 mounted police; 1810 disallow clan vengeance; 1820 eight districts; 1821 Supreme Court; **1827 constitution** → **1828** republican government (judiciary, bicameral legislature, elected Principal Chief)—proclaimed sovereignty. Purpose of southern Indian nationalism: **stave off removal**.

## Removal crisis

Jackson’s 1829 message aimed pointedly at the Cherokee constitutional republic. Corn Tassel (1830); *Cherokee Nation v. Georgia* (1831) “domestic dependent nation”; **[[worcester-v-georgia-1832]]** nullified Georgia jurisdiction—unenforced. Treaty of New Echota Dec 1835 (unauthorized Treaty Party ~100 signers); Senate ratified despite national opposition. **[[trail-of-tears-1838]]:** gunpoint roundups, stockades (many died of exposure/hunger/thirst), winter 1838–39 National Party ~**15,000** under John Ross—“trail where we cried.” Treaty Party ~6,000 with Old Settlers earlier; Boudinot/Ridges killed summer 1839; seven-year civil strife; **1846** reunion. **Eastern Band** of Cherokees in western NC held 1817/1819 allotments exempt from 1835 treaty.

## Indian Territory and Civil War

1840s–50s agricultural surplus, slavery/cotton, missions; Joseph Vann purchased a steamboat. Civil War: Cherokee Nation a crossroads of conflict; deep north/south factional division; 1866 reconstruction treaty sold Kansas holdings, abolished slavery, granted railroad rights of way—corporate penetration by 1880s.

## Counterfactual significance

Without Cherokee constitutional nationalism and literacy, Removal might have lacked its most publicized legal and missionary contest—but Georgia land hunger and Jacksonian non-enforcement suggest structural drivers would still have targeted Cherokee land. Without Treaty Party signature of New Echota, forced removal might have required even more naked military legislation; Green shows Senate ratified despite clear lack of national authority.
