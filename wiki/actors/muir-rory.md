# Rory Muir

Australian historian specializing in the Napoleonic Wars. Best known for two major works: the operational study *Britain and the Defeat of Napoleon, 1807–1815* (Yale, 1996) and the tactical and experiential analysis *Tactics and the Experience of Battle in the Age of Napoleon* (Yale, 1998).

Muir's approach to the latter emphasizes primary eyewitness accounts (heavily British memoirs, letters, and diaries, supplemented by French material) over drill manuals and theoretical treatises. He explores the practical realities of combat for artillery, light infantry, line infantry, and cavalry; the mechanics of command at general, staff, and regimental levels; and the psychological and emotional dimensions of battle — the "eve of battle," morale and cohesion under fire, attitudes and feelings, victory or defeat, and the aftermath (plundering, wounded, prisoners, emotional aftermath).

His work complements operational studies such as Chandler's *The Campaigns of Napoleon* by supplying the battalion- and regimental-level reality and the human dimension of what actually occurred on Napoleonic battlefields, drawing on the unusually rich British first-hand literature for the period.

## Key Contributions

- Detailed reconstruction of how the different arms interacted in combat.
- Emphasis on morale, cohesion, and the limits of drill and obedience under fire.
- Balanced treatment of British and French experience (with acknowledgment of source bias).
- Analysis of the role of generals, ADCs, staff, and regimental officers in sustaining units.
- Description of post-battle realities: plunder, treatment of wounded and prisoners, emotional reactions of victors and vanquished.
