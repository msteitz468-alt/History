# Mago Barca

**Mago** was the youngest of the three sons of [[hamilcar-barca|Hamilcar Barca]] and the
youngest of the [[barcid-dynasty|Barcid]] brothers, serving throughout the
[[second-punic-war-218-201bce|Second Punic War]] as one of [[hannibal|Hannibal]]'s most
trusted subordinate commanders alongside his brother [[hasdrubal-barca|Hasdrubal]].

## Role and Career

Mago accompanied Hannibal on the invasion of Italy and commanded in several of the early
victories, most famously at [[battle-of-cannae-216bce|Cannae]] (216 BC), where he shared
in the double-envelopment that destroyed the Roman army; he was then sent to Carthage to
report the victory and press for reinforcement. He subsequently campaigned in Spain with
Hasdrubal against the Romans, and after the collapse of the Punic position in Spain
(206 BC) he was tasked with opening a new front in Italy from the north-west. Landing in
**Liguria** (205 BC), he sought to rally Gauls and Ligurians against Rome, but was
defeated and severely wounded. Recalled to Africa with Hannibal in the war's final phase,
he **died of his wounds at sea in 203 BC**.

## Significance

Mago exemplifies the Barcid reliance on a **kinship network of commanders** — Hoyos's
point that the dynasty governed and fought through family and trusted associates rather
than a bureaucratic command structure. The simultaneous loss of both younger brothers
(Hasdrubal at the Metaurus in 207, Mago in 203) stripped Hannibal of the lieutenants on
whom the family's strategy depended and marks the practical end of the dynasty's military
reach.

## Historiography

As with the other lesser Barcids, Mago is reported almost entirely through the Roman
tradition (Polybius, Livy), and the details of his Ligurian campaign and death are
comparatively sparse.
