# Charles Cornwallis, 2nd Earl Cornwallis (1738–1805)

The most aggressive British field commander of the **[[american-revolution|American
Revolution]]** — "a naturally aggressive general, who pushed himself and his men hard" — and
the one whose surrender ended it. He pursued [[washington-george|Washington]] across New
Jersey in 1776 (famously boasting he would "bag the Old Fox in the morning" before Washington
slipped away to [[battle-of-princeton-1777|Princeton]]), commanded the flanking column at
[[battle-of-brandywine-1777|Brandywine]], and led the rear guard at
[[battle-of-monmouth-1778|Monmouth]].

## Southern command and the road to Yorktown

Given independent command in the South in 1780, Cornwallis crushed [[gates-horatio|Gates]] at
[[battle-of-camden-1780|Camden]] but found his victories barren — "galled that his victory
achieved so little." After [[morgan-daniel|Morgan]] destroyed [[battle-of-cowpens-1781|
Tarleton's force at Cowpens]], Cornwallis burned his own baggage to chase
[[greene-nathanael|Greene]] in the "Race to the Dan," won a ruinous Pyrrhic victory at
[[battle-of-guilford-courthouse-1781|Guilford Courthouse]] (losing nearly a quarter of his
men), and limped to Wilmington. Abandoning the Carolinas, he marched into Virginia and — on
[[clinton-henry|Clinton]]'s instruction to secure a naval anchorage — dug in at
[[siege-of-yorktown-1781|Yorktown]].

There the aggressive Cornwallis turned strangely **passive**: with the French fleet closing,
he "remained remarkably passive in the face of a mounting crisis" and let himself be sealed in.
Trapped by land and sea, he surrendered on 19 October 1781, feigning illness to avoid the
ceremony and sending General Charles O'Hara to give up his sword.

After the war Cornwallis rebuilt his career as Governor-General of India and Lord Lieutenant
of Ireland — a reminder that Yorktown ended a war, not a career.
