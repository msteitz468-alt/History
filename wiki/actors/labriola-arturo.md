# Arturo Labriola (1873–1959)

Neapolitan economist and founder of Italian [[revolutionary-syndicalism]]:
leader of the PSI's Milanese revolutionary splinter (end 1902) and founder of
*Avanguardia socialista* (December 1902), where the young
[[mussolini-benito|Mussolini]] wrote. Not to be confused with the Marxist
philosopher Antonio Labriola. Drawing on the neoclassical economists (Walras,
Jevons, Pareto), Labriola and Enrico Leone carried out the **revision of
Marxist economics** at the heart of the Sorelian current: surplus value as
"an automatic phenomenon... of the normal conditions of the labor market,"
not of capitalist production; rebellion aimed at capitalism's "hierarchical
organization," not its economic principle — "socialism should develop and
apply on a still larger scale the economic principles of capitalism." He
co-founded the free-trade Lega antiprotezionista (1904), coined "A class
liberalism! That's what syndicalism is!" (1905), and argued Italy's
proletariat was too undeveloped for revolution.

On the national question he moved early: contesting Hervé's antimilitarism
from 1907, distinguishing a "cultural nationalism, supported by the
syndicalists" from "the nationalism of the contractors," theorizing the
"striking army," supporting the **Libyan War** ("probably the most important
and serious move in favor of the Mezzogiorno") and telling workers "Let the
bourgeoisie teach the proletariat how really to fight." Yet he left
revolutionary syndicalism after the Libyan War, sat in parliament as an
independent socialist, served as Giolitti's **minister of labor (1920)**
during the factory occupations, **opposed fascism**, went into exile, and
returned only in 1935 — reading the Ethiopian War as continuous with the
Libyan expedition's "labor imperialism." With Leone, he is Sternhell's prime
example of the "economists" who "prudently retired from the scene" while the
pure Sorelians founded fascism — see [[fascism-and-the-political-left]].
