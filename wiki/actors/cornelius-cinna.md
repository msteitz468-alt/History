# Lucius Cornelius Cinna

## Overview
Lucius Cornelius Cinna (d. 84 BCE) was the patrician *popularis* leader who
dominated Rome from **87 to 84 BCE** — the so-called **"Cinnan domination"** —
in the interval between [[sulla|Sulla's]] departure for the East and his return.
Holding the consulship four years running (87–84), Cinna ran the state through
controlled elections while Sulla was declared a public enemy in absentia.

## Role and Significance
Elected consul for 87 alongside the optimate Gnaeus Octavius, Cinna had first been
made to swear an oath — sealed by casting a stone and accepting exile as its
forfeit — not to disturb Sulla's settlement. He broke it almost at once by reviving
the drive to distribute the newly enfranchised Italians across all thirty-five
tribes (the programme of the murdered tribune Sulpicius). Driven from Rome and
stripped of his citizenship by Octavius, Cinna:
- raised **more than ten legions** from the Italian communities hungry for full
  suffrage (Tibur, Praeneste, Nola) and allied with **Carbo**, **Sertorius**, and
  the returning [[marius|Marius]];
- besieged and took Rome in 87, whereupon Marius unleashed a **five-day terror**
  against his enemies (the consul Octavius, the orator Marcus Antonius, Catulus,
  and others were killed) — which Cinna and Sertorius ended by massacring Marius's
  own bodyguard of freed slaves;
- after Marius's death in January 86, governed largely alone, passing debt-relief
  (three-quarters of debts cancelled) and coinage-standardization measures;
- was **stoned and killed by his own mutinous troops** at Ancona in early 84 while
  trying to ferry an army across the Adriatic to intercept Sulla before he reached
  Italy.

## Counterfactual Significance
Cinna demonstrated that a determined faction leader could simply **hold the state
by force for years** through iterated consulships and controlled elections — a
warlord regime in republican dress that prefigures the dictatorships of Sulla and
Caesar. His broken oath is, in [[duncan-storm-before-the-storm-2017|Duncan's]]
telling, a signal instance of the [[mos-maiorum|erosion of the mos maiorum]]: the
sacred oath had become just another discarded norm.

## Historiography
Cinna is oddly obscure for so dominant a figure — the sources (Appian, Plutarch,
Velleius Paterculus) are hostile and thin on his origins and administration, in
part because the victorious Sullan tradition had every reason to blacken the
"Cinnan domination." His actual governing record is therefore hard to reconstruct
behind the label of tyranny.
