# Muhammad Ali's Egypt

**Muhammad Ali** (r. 1805–48) and his dynasty made nineteenth-century **Egypt** the foremost
**modernizing power** in Africa and the Islamic world — an autonomous, militarized, would-be industrial
state that nearly broke free of the Ottoman Empire. Documented for the wiki through the **UNESCO
[[gha-vol6-19th-century|General History of Africa, Vol. VI]]** (ch. 13, Abdel-Malek, "The renaissance of
Egypt").

## Seizing and consolidating power

An Albanian officer in the Ottoman army sent to expel the French (whose 1798 invasion under Napoleon had
shattered the old order), **Muhammad Ali** maneuvered himself into the governorship in **1805** and secured
his rule by **massacring the Mamluk beys (1811)** — ending the caste that had dominated Egypt for
centuries. He then built a **centralized state** on the foundation of state-controlled agriculture.

## Defensive modernization

GHA VI presents Muhammad Ali's reign as a programme of **"defensive modernization"** — reform from above to
match European power:

- a **conscript army** trained on European lines, the first modern army in the region;
- **state monopolies** and a forced shift to **long-staple cotton** as a cash export, tying Egypt to the
  world market;
- **factories, schools, translation bureaus, a navy, and educational missions** to Europe.

His armies conquered the **Sudan (1820–21)** (founding Khartoum and the Turco-Egyptian regime), intervened
in Arabia and Greece, and in the **Egyptian-Ottoman wars (1831–40)** twice defeated the Sultan and overran
Syria — until the **European powers (Britain foremost) intervened in 1840** to force his withdrawal,
capping his ambitions but securing his family's **hereditary rule** over Egypt.

## Toward 1882

Under his successors (Said and especially **Ismail**), Egypt pursued grand modernization — the **Suez Canal
(1869)**, railways, Cairo's rebuilding — but at the cost of ruinous **European debt**, which led to
Anglo-French financial control, the nationalist Urabi revolt, and the **British occupation of 1882**, the
foundation of colonial Egypt.

## Significance

Muhammad Ali's Egypt is the great African and Middle Eastern case of **autonomous, state-led modernization**
in the face of European power — a project that transformed Egypt, conquered the Sudan, and nearly
dismembered the Ottoman Empire, before debt and European intervention turned modernization into dependency.
