# Premonstratensian Order (Norbertines)

The **Premonstratensians** (or **Norbertines**), founded by **St Norbert** at **Prémontré**, adopted a **more severe** form of Augustinian canonical life — in simplicity and austerity resembling the [[cistercian-order|Cistercians]] in their withdrawal, while remaining canons under a form of the [[augustinian-canons|Rule of St Augustine]]. [[logan-history-of-the-church-in-the-middle-ages-2013|Logan]] groups them with other twelfth-century “new orders” that initially combined reform austerity with pastoral or apostolic aims.

## Expansion

By the **middle of the thirteenth century** the order had about **500 houses** from **Ireland to Palestine**. In England the first house was **Newhouse** (c. **1143**). Logan notes that after early preaching energy, Premonstratensians tended to turn **inward** by the late twelfth century — part of his wider judgement that nearly all new orders (except the [[carthusian-order|Carthusians]]) diverted from early ideals.

## Related forms

Closely associated in Logan’s chapter with other Augustinian variants: the **Victorines** of St Victor, Paris (founded **1108** by William of Champeaux; Hugh of St Victor and strong liturgy/scholarship; nearly two score French houses by end of twelfth century).
