# Australian Imperial Force

The all-volunteer expeditionary army raised by the
[[commonwealth-of-australia|Commonwealth of Australia]] for overseas service in
the two world wars. The First AIF (1914–1921) fought at
[[gallipoli-campaign-1915|Gallipoli]] and on the
[[western-front-australia-wwi|Western Front]]; the Second AIF (1939–1947) fought
in the Middle East, the Mediterranean, and the Pacific within
[[australia-world-war-2-1939|Australia's World War 2]].

## Overview

Because conscription for overseas service was twice rejected at referendum (see
[[conscription-referendums-1916]]), the First AIF remained wholly voluntary
throughout the Great War — a point of national pride central to the
[[anzac-legend]]. From 1918 the Australian Corps was commanded by
[[monash-john|John Monash]], whose set-piece offensives in the Hundred Days made
the Corps one of the most effective formations on the Western Front. Of some
330,000 who served overseas in WWI, around 60,000 died. The Second AIF, in which
[[blamey-thomas|Thomas Blamey]] rose to Commander-in-Chief, served from North
Africa and Greece to the [[kokoda-campaign-1942|Kokoda]] and New Guinea campaigns.

## Significance

The AIF is the institutional core of Australia's national military memory: the
volunteer ethos, the "digger" identity, and the commemoration of sacrifice on
foreign soil that the [[anzac-legend]] enshrined and that recurs in the
twentieth-century "history anxiety."
