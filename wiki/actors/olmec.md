# The Olmec

## Overview

The **Olmec** of the Gulf Coast (Veracruz and Tabasco, the "Olmec Heartland") were, in
[[benjamin-cwh-v4-2015|CWH IV]] (Begun & Brashler, Ch 20), "one of the first recognizable
cultural horizons to emerge" in **Mesoamerica** — the earliest society in the Americas to
display the markers of complexity, and the source of cultural forms that recur across
two-and-a-half millennia of Mesoamerican civilization.

## The Heartland Centers

Olmec power shifted successively among monumental **ceremonial centers**. **San Lorenzo**,
on the Rio Chiquito, peaked **1200–900 BCE** — "the first significant administrative center
in Mexico," a hilltop site with at least ten **colossal basalt heads** (single blocks
transported great distances, evidence of elites able to command labor). After San Lorenzo's
decline, **La Venta** (on the Tonalá River) dominated **c. 900–400 BCE**. The Olmec built the
first **interregional trade networks**, exchanging Gulf Coast shell and feathers for finished
goods and prized **iron-ore "mirrors"** from the nascent [[zapotec|Zapotec]] of Oaxaca.

## The Olmec Legacy

CWH IV credits the Olmec — whether by origination or through interaction — with the first
appearance of pan-Mesoamerican institutions: the **[[mesoamerican-calendar|calendar system]]**,
early pan-Mesoamerican **deities** and **bloodletting ritual**, and the **Mesoamerican
ball game**. These life-ways recur from the Olmec down to the Mexica (Aztecs).

## Mother Culture or Sister Culture? (Historiography)

The Olmec's classification as a full **state** is contested, and so is their role. The
**"Mother Culture"** school sees the Olmec as the singular primogenitor of later
Mesoamerican civilization; the **"Sister Culture"** school (drawing on Oaxaca evidence) sees
parallel, mutually influencing developments across several Preclassic regions. CWH IV leans
toward the latter — emphasizing **interregional trade and intercultural relations** as the
engine of complexity — while granting the Olmec an undeniable, lasting imprint. The debate
often turns on the spread of a shared **Olmec art style**. See [[mesoamerican-state-formation]].
