# Licinius

**Valerius Licinianus Licinius** (Augustus 308–324, d. 325) was [[galerius|Galerius]]’s protégé who ruled the East (and much of the Balkans) as Constantine’s co-emperor until their final war.

## Career (Mitchell)

Made Augustus at Carnuntum (308) with Galerius. In 313 he defeated Maximinus Daia at Adrianople; at Milan (February 313) he sealed a pact with [[constantine-the-great|Constantine]] by marrying Constantine’s sister Constantia — the context of the so-called [[edict-of-milan-313|“Edict of Milan”]] tradition on religious toleration. Wars with Constantine followed (Cibalae and near Adrianople, 316–17; final campaign 324 at Adrianople and Chrysopolis on the Bosporus). Licinius surrendered at Nicomedia; by spring 325 he and his son had been put to death at Thessalonica.

## Counterfactual significance

Licinius is the last durable eastern partner before Constantine’s sole rule; without him the 313 settlement and the delayed Christianization of eastern government look different.

## Historiography

Constantinian sources after 324 rewrite Licinius as persecutor/traitor; Mitchell tracks the military-political sequence without accepting the full black legend. Survey: [[mitchell-later-roman-empire-2015|Mitchell 2015]].
